# Batch Summaries

## Volume 01, Batch 0001 — Chapters 1–10

### Story Progress

A routine Saint Orra relay failure becomes an unresolved conflict between an obsolete bridge permission and current medical service claims. Jonas Mercer proves competent with ordinary tools, attended bypasses, and local witnesses, but the opening force is his growing inability to treat a technically successful route as a complete answer.

The batch separates three leads that must not be collapsed prematurely:

1. The bridge reset is physical work plus diagnosis. It activates only a limited Fault-reader view and creates no civic claim.
2. The unlisted shuttle is an independent live service route. Its people and immediate care needs are real even though the route and the old-canal shelter are absent from ordinary city records.
3. The Old Ward’s empty root holder field is a maintenance fact during uneven renewal, not a hidden person or request for Jonas to become its bearer.

### Midpoint

The apparent unauthorized access is not sabotage. Pia Santos, a child with a hand-marked route token, a driver, and Alma Reyes are using a route held by local practices the city map does not recognize. Every immediate disposition is completed without exposing the passenger list or turning Jonas into the route’s holder.

### Climax and Cost

Oren Pell’s valid closure forces the old-canal residents to relocate to Bellow Market. The pump inspection then keeps the unsafe line closed despite a live maintenance practice. In the final attended-service test, existing bearers keep one ambulance lane, the clinic entrance, and the shelter-to-clinic route within a 90-minute attended window (10:00–11:30). Ruth Nwosu’s duty ends at 11:00 with no one to hand the key to, because the local record never carried her as a holder, so she stays to the expiry as a named witness and pays thirty unpaid minutes for it while Mina keeps the paper and corridor. The shelter vehicle reaches the clinic at 10:55; that ends its run and handoff leg, not the route authorization, which closes at 11:30. The crew isolates a pressure fault on the failed side of the south span and closes every route at expiry. The 38-minute shelter delay is recorded as three causes, not two: the permission conflict, the physically stopped side service gate, and Jonas’s own scope. He priced the failure at the 9:58 round, could not re-book Selah’s local claim, and chose not to spend a shelter with no water on a taxi line. The service works for the window; the underlying conflict remains.

### Character Change

- Jonas moves from wanting a clean record and a useful credential toward accepting that a safe shift and an honest report do not make him legitimate. In Chapter 10 he writes a line into an official report that works against him: he read the risk, priced it, and left the run on a failed span anyway.
- Sanaa remains professionally independent but stops treating Jonas’s silence as agreement after he discloses the 43-minute night-transfer cost.
- Tomas refuses an undocumented shared-node repair and places a visible responsibility bond on the next agenda.
- Pia acts for herself and the immediate passengers without becoming Jonas’s apprentice or Lowglass’s sole spokesperson.
- Oren remains a valid local safety official whose rules protect real hazards while imposing real displacement.
- Selah Mora establishes the consent-limited custody practice that protects residents from Jonas’s curiosity.

### Power and Continuity State

Jonas ends Stage 1. Fault reading shows relationships between active faults and terms but not intent; safe reading requires a physical anchor and trusted witness and causes bounded sensory bleed. He has no root access, bearer authority, civic mending power, seam mark, or personal claim. The Chapter 2 gate-use suspension remains active but is narrowed to attended maintenance after a named bearer initiates the route; Jonas cannot initiate or approve gate use alone.

Selah’s sealed log contains the full route and resident identities. Each resident controls their own entry; Selah’s access is limited to completed return-of-care and immediate safety follow-up; the record expires at the next route review; later use requires resident consent. The NDC holds only a redacted custody reference.

Every temporary route in the batch ran on municipal procedure rather than Jonas’s ability: the bridge attended bypass, the clinic emergency maintenance card, the attended escort procedure, and the separately inspected Assembly Emergency Service Test. That last clause also binds its own witnesses: inside a running test a named witness stays to the expiry or hands the duty to a named successor, which is why Ruth Nwosu works unpaid past 11:00. The Chapter 6 union transport record Jonas did sign covers transport only; it is not the responsibility bond Tomas blocks on in Chapters 9 and 10.

### Handoff

The old-canal shelter and pump remain closed for physical reasons. The bridge and clinic conflict remains open. CivicCore has made no direct offer. Lowglass’s civic history, Evan’s counter-amendment and second signature, the public administrator title, and the blank-bearer request remain unspent.

The next batch must place Tomas’s visible responsibility bond before the first bounded civic repair at the west-clinic gate. Ruth Nwosu remains the existing bearer, Sanaa a separate informed witness, and Jonas the issuer with a named maintenance claim. The repair’s fallback must create a visible seam mark, a real patient-transfer cost, and an active claim before the separate Lowglass route work begins in Chapters 19–20.

## Volume 01, Batch 0002 — Chapters 11–20

### Story Progress

Jonas signs a visible responsibility bond, restores one missing precedence field at the Saint Orra west-clinic care entrance, and watches the repair fail socially before he has finished being proud of it. The term works exactly as written: it opens for the issuer and for nobody else, and a booked cardiac slot is released because the person who fixed the door was asleep. A second, separate amendment later removes the issuer from the fallback and prints *term unchanged, issuer unchanged, claim unchanged*. The cost of the first repair is entered in five records and discharged by none of them.

The batch also moves the work outward without letting it explain itself. A Reclamation Season maintenance review of the plate’s Continuity Project series requires the history of a chain that runs off human maps at two lowland nodes. Jonas finds the address by four empty records, refuses to open Selah Mora’s sealed log to get there faster, and ends up on the Weir being told what the barrier will cost and that he is not the person who gets to choose.

### Midpoint

Chapter 15 is the reversal. **Lorna Vessey, sixty-one**, is refused a transfer at 15:22 on the third day with a verified claim, an assigned bed, a named receiving nurse and a booked cardiac slot, because the fallback schedule names a man who is asleep. The slot is released at 15:41; the next available date is the fourteenth. Sanaa refuses to call the case an emergency to save it. Jonas opens the door anyway, in knowing breach of his suspension. The repair is technically correct and socially harmful, and nobody shouts at him.

### Climax and Cost

Chapter 17 is the batch climax. Six people argue about what *available* means and write a roster with a named, published gap they refuse to fill with Jonas; the gap is then tested in the corridor and costs a real patient two hours. At 14:00 Jonas makes a second bounded edit at the same plate, with the evening porter on the probe, removing the issuer from the fallback. The plate refuses to pretend anything has been settled: **Term unchanged. Issuer unchanged. Claim unchanged.** The seam mark extends past his wrist to below the elbow and his right hand will not close. The first released slot and the first fall on the floor remain on the record, and the term is still six days long with three and a half of them gone.

Two costs in that chapter are left visibly unpaid on the page rather than quietly settled. Selah Mora's return-of-care hours carry the line **retainer requested in writing of Saint Orra west: not yet agreed**, which she insists on reading aloud and keeping until the money exists. Tomas's fourth prerequisite — a written rule for when two people on one rota both refuse — is asked once and answered in the worst available way, so it stays open.

### Character Change

- Jonas moves from “fix first, explain later” to signing a document that lets other people stop him, to naming four bearers instead of himself, and to accepting a debt he cannot pay quickly. He changes one scheduling decision on the record rather than repeating the Chapter 10 omission, and he is caught out by a twenty-six-year-old porter for a breach of his own that he had not noticed.
- Sanaa refuses to certify a working door, accepts a fallback clause that will one day call her in a resuscitation room, and says out loud that he has made himself the last available answer again.
- Ruth Nwosu signs the term, adds a refusal clause in her own words, names her own unpaid hours as a defect in the record, and refuses to be his convenience.
- Tomas turns his bond into a stop-work mechanism and refuses every form that would make a category responsible. He also leaves one question unanswered on purpose: what happens when two people on the same rota both refuse.
- Selah Mora names her price in writing, refuses Jonas's version of it, and keeps the words *not yet agreed* on the board until the hospital pays.
- Pia sets four conditions, refuses him the tool roll, and keeps her people’s decisions their own.
- Nell Ardent arrives as the Vessey family’s lawyer, not Jonas’s ally, and solves nothing.
- Bo Trimble, seventeen, is right about the pipe, is not on the tool rota, and tells Jonas not to make a story out of the district.

### Power and Continuity State

Jonas ends Stage 2 at the limit of one hinge and one local rule. Two civic edits occurred, both at the west-clinic care-entrance plate, both with a named witness, both bounded, neither touching the root or another node. He is issuer, never bearer. He carries a visible seam mark, a numb right hand, an unpaid maintenance claim, a public issuer line, and an open registrar's question about two solo shift acknowledgements. The Chapter 2 gate-use suspension is still live and was breached twice on the page.

CivicCore has made no offer. The bridge, pump, and old-canal shelter are unchanged. Lowglass's history, the community-watch term's origin, the Quiet Cut, Evan's counter-amendment and second signature, Iona's preservation, the public administrator title, and the blank-bearer request all remain unspent.

### Handoff

The next batch must begin in Pia's Lowglass service work and the flood barrier on the Weir, with the clinic gate running on a local renewable term in Jonas's absence and one published gap nobody has closed. Three things are owed before that work starts: the registrar's answer to Jonas's solo acknowledgements, which is not his to write; the unsigned Bellow Market retainer, which only the west site administration can settle; and Tomas's unanswered question about two people on one rota both refusing, which no speech closes. The residents' three-way disagreement about recognition is unresolved and must stay unresolved unless the work earns a decision. Selah's sealed log stays sealed. The full barrier repair, the Quiet Cut, the civic erasure, and any Evan fragment belong to Chapters 21–27, which the outline treats as the second half of its Lowglass section; Chapters 28–30 open the contractor's-bargain section and may set it up but must not spend it.

## Volume 01, Batch 0003 — Chapters 21–30

### Story Progress

The batch takes the Weir from a jammed flap gate to a real, witnessed, bounded repair, and then takes the consequence of that repair away from the Weir and into the city's own machinery. Jonas returns on the Thursday with a coupling and a gasket, fits it to a boy of seventeen's specification while a man who is on the tool rota holds the wrench, and cannot solve the rota. Over four working days inside a tide table, six people from the street cut a rotten sill out of a concrete chamber at the bottom of a lowland dip, put a new beam and a new pintle in, revet two metres of bank with four tonnes of stone, and find under the frame a lead service seal stamped eleven years ago and, under that, paint on the concrete saying **DO NOT CLOSE ON THE EBB. CLOSE ON THE FLOOD** — which is the century-old operating error behind the boil. The barrier stands and will not hold a surge. Nobody is on the bank for the next flood, and there is no name on the line for it.

Underneath that work runs the clinic gate, and the gate runs it. The registrar's question is answered in public and does not go the way Jonas wants: one acknowledgement is a breach, one is ratified, the department takes the claim, he keeps the issuer line and loses the cabinet and his appointment review is deferred. He writes six words into a form — **non-civic plant** — that will, if the review accepts them, take the Weir out of scope on the first of April, and he writes them on the fourteenth of March, in eleven minutes, because the alternative is a hole in a box that will be traced back to the records supervisor.

The batch's second civic act is the one that matters: Jonas mends one field at the Weir stand and does not get to be the issuer. Tomas stops the work by telephone because the bond is named to one hinge; Bea drives ninety minutes and stops it again because Jonas has written a name into a holder line; the term is issued under **the holder on the rota, keeper M. Doré**, and the renewal clause says the issuer cannot renew it. Then the clause he wrote himself — *fourteen days, or the completion of the barrier work, whichever is sooner* — expires four days before anyone looks at it, and the consequence is not a line on a map. It is a tap that opens to anybody with a stopcock and runs brown into nine houses, because when the rota does not answer, the mains wins.

### Midpoint

Chapter 25. The first time a bounded civic term is mended at a hinge Jonas did not issue, with a holder who can refuse, a witness who states the cost, and a claim that is somebody else's and unpaid. The holder is a rota, not a person, and the first thing the new term does is let a man refuse his slot in writing on a board and take ten minutes off the water.

### Climax and Cost

Chapter 28. The Weir puts a sheet of paper on the Old Ward public board — the first thing from that street in a civic record in thirty-two years — and within two hours and eleven minutes the department lodges a classification that will close both lowland nodes at the review. The pressure comes with a name on it, and the name is Jonas's, and it is the name he put there himself six days earlier. Oren Pell, the man who closed the old-canal shelter, tells him the cure and then tells him the Weir will not help him write it. The week's costs: a child with a fever and no water, thirty-one hours without a stand, five days of brown water nobody reported, an appointment review deferred, a cabinet he cannot open, six words that cannot be unwritten, and a maintenance claim on the clinic gate that is still unpaid.

### Character Change

- Jonas moves from being the issuer of every term to being the issuer of none of the ones that matter. He mends a term he is forbidden to issue, issues one he is not supposed to, and discovers that the second of those is the one that will take a street off a map. He confesses, unprompted, to being the man in the van in 2019, and he discovers that his own habit — reaching for competence instead of asking — is the same act whether the box is a departmental form or a kitchen.
- Ruth Nwosu is asked properly, for the first time, about something that is not the door, and refuses the number. Her hours are corrected from the fifteenth of March and the arrears are not settled by it. She keeps a red diary from a newsagent and tells him about it so that he knows she has not been silent.
- Margo Doré refuses a machine at three in the morning, refuses to be a category, and then finds the fault in his clause. Her rota is not a document and it is not a person and it turns out to be the only thing on that street that told anybody anything.
- Sanaa Iqbal pays the price of the clause she accepted: nine minutes unanswered in a resuscitation room, and a patient on a ramp in the rain, and a design that works exactly as written. She does not reconcile with Jonas and does not blame him, and she says the day she named the day it happened.
- Tomas Renn asks his fourth question in person, once, and then tells Jonas not to answer it.
- Bea Osunde tells him, twice, that he has stopped being the answer on that street and has not noticed.
- Selah Mora presses the retainer herself, by telephone, and Halvorsen commits to a board minute on the ninth and does not promise a cheque.
- The Weir residents do not resolve anything. Aurrie wants recognition, Margo wants the favour, Ottie wants to carry on unlisted, Hector wants the number and refuses the number, and Bo wants the bank and refuses the story. Three positions are on a wall with a name against each and one line with a ring round it and nothing inside.

### Power and Continuity State

Jonas ends Stage 2 at the same limit, and the limit has been shown to him from the other side. He can still restore one missing field at one local plate and amend one clause at a plate he has already worked. He cannot renew a term he did not issue, cannot be the issuer where a local holder has standing, and cannot unwrite an honest finding. The seam mark has extended past the elbow into the upper arm, his right hand is numb to the elbow, and the maintenance claim on the west-clinic care entrance is unpaid.

The gate term has been renewed three times, out loud, by the named bearers, and the words *not yet agreed* are still beside Selah Mora's hours. The published 11:00–13:30 gap has cost a real patient nine minutes on a Monday because the on-call clinician was in a resuscitation room, and Bram's refusal of Thursday has left a weekday morning with no holder on the board and no rule that says who covers it. The registrar's question is answered, publicly, and the answer costs him the standing he had at the Weir and puts an inspection on a street with no address. The series 6 response is due on the twenty-eighth and the Reclamation Season review is on the first of April.

CivicCore has made no offer. Rook has not appeared. The Assembly has not voted. The old-canal shelter and the old pump stay closed and the bridge stays isolated. The Lowglass clinic-and-housing annex has not been touched. The Quiet Cut, the civic erasure, the origin of the community-watch term, Iona's preserved identity, Evan's counter-amendment and second signature, the public administrator title and the blank-bearer request all remain unspent.

### Handoff

The next batch opens the contractor's bargain. The pressure is already on the board: a non-civic classification awaiting a review, a pre-review inspection that nobody on the Weir will help him write a consent for, a series 6 response due on the twenty-eighth, an appointment review deferred to a review of the review, a chamber that will not hold a surge, a tap that runs brown, and a term that somebody on a fence is now going to have to renew without him. Chapters 31–36 must bring CivicCore's direct offer, Rook's demonstration, the blanket continuity waiver, the Assembly vote and the separate Lowglass clinic-and-housing annex closure; Chapters 37–40 begin the nine-witness coalition. The Weir's three-way disagreement, the unsigned retainer, and Tomas's unanswered fourth question must all still be standing at the end of Chapter 40.

## Volume 01, Batch 0004 — Chapters 31–40

### Story Progress

The batch opens the contractor's bargain and closes it, and it does not go the way anybody in the room expected. A contractor makes an offer that is genuinely better at keeping hinges running than the department is — a permanent appointment, root-level notice access, the continuity of every term Jonas has ever touched, and the arrears and the return-of-care hours of two women he owes — in exchange for a signature that says no local term, holder, refusal or standing practice in this city is incompatible with continuity maintenance. Jonas wants parts of it. He prices it in two columns in his own pad, in the register he uses for jobs, and closes it. He then spends a chapter being unable to find a single sentence in the document he objects to, tells a room that every reason he has got is about himself, and refuses it anyway — for a new reason, and at a cost he cannot argue with afterwards. His appointment is closed, his maintenance claim is assigned to him in writing by default, two contractor crews come off the Saint Orra queue, and the participation fund that would have paid Ruth Nwosu's nineteen years and Selah Mora's eight hours a night withdraws forty minutes after it was offered.

The second half of the batch is a building. Oren Pell closes **Brine Wharf** in Cooper's Reach under a renewed flood-safety order, and he is right about the building: thirty-four millimetres of drop in January, fifty-four by the end of March, in the corner the escape route discharges into. **Jonas measures it himself and makes the closure more certain rather than less**, and the order is served on the older figure because a person may not be moved out on a measurement taken the day the order is made. Eleven flats are emptied at 18:00 into a bought-out hostel for fourteen nights, the last of them the night of 12 April, the district's only registered refuge store goes into a lock-up on Marsh Lane behind a key that belongs to a rota which starts at seven in the morning, and the appeal that follows costs a deposit, a two-thousand-pound bond payable because they will probably lose, twenty-three days' notice, and **one woman's name written in the fourth line of a form**.

The Weir is not behind the annex and does not want to be. Yasmin Rohan refuses alignment in a room and Pia Santos refuses it in a yard, separately, for the same reason arrived at independently, and Oren Pell forbids the Weir's evidence from the appeal and tells them why in a sentence: the record is unreliable and the department will say so and the department will be right.

### Midpoint

Chapter 34. The batch's turn is not the refusal, which is the expected shape, and it is not the closure, which is the volume's working method. It is the moment Jonas kneels on a slab with a tape and a lump hammer in a basement in Cooper's Reach and measures a number that is three millimetres worse than the one in the order, and says so, in a room, to the people the order is going to move out. **The technician's competence is used to make a displacement more certain.** He is good at this and it is exactly the thing Bea Osunde has been telling him he does.

### Climax and Cost

Chapter 36. The Reclamation Season maintenance review splits the classification in a way nobody asked for: **6-L/14 accepted as non-civic plant, no civic claim attaching to the chamber or the bank, and 6-L/19 not accepted, in scope pending an inspection that is not scheduled because there is no location of record.** The barrier they built with a church-hall tin is now officially nobody's, and the one instrument that could have put a name on the tap cannot be invoked by anybody. In the afternoon the Assembly delays the continuity contract to 2 June, receives the loft's paper as a city document over Jonas's objection — **no door number, no list of houses, and nobody named**, as Sore certifies twice-read, loses Iven Sore's motion on the standing orders twice over, ratifies the annex closure, and then **creates an interim post for the man who classified a street as plant. Jonas declines in the room and Royden Achebe takes it in his own name**, having withdrawn a draft in the registrar's answer two weeks earlier because a department cannot be the holder of a plate. Jonas raises it and is ruled out of time. He is the only person in the building who could have caught the contradiction and he is not allowed to finish the sentence.

### Character Change

- **Jonas** says the third telling **himself**, to Bea Osunde, and is told it is not sufficient — that the people who said it to him all had a stake, that the real job is walking into rooms and asking people for things, that there is no instrument for that, and that she is not his conscience. He also, in front of a statutory panel, **declines to produce true evidence** — a legible condition line on a plate he has worked — because a minute is a list. He is for the first time using honesty and concealment in the same mouth and both were deliberate. He decides not to ask the two witnesses he wants most, writes nothing on either line, and tells nobody.
- **Sanaa Iqbal** makes the best argument in the city for the document he refuses and says she does not want it, for her own reasons, in front of witnesses, and asks for that to be minuted so nobody can later say she said no out of loyalty. She is then called at six minutes past six in the morning of Thursday 3 April, answers on the second ring, and the number is thirteen minutes against nine.
- **Tomas Renn** gets his question answered four times in one week and in principle not at all, and stops asking for the answer and starts asking Jonas to be the man who says out loud that the city was saved by luck and a rota with a hole in it.
- **Bea Osunde** refuses to be his conscience and tells him the job has no instrument, and that he will have to build it out of strangers without her.
- **Oren Pell** becomes a partial ally and the standing reason the Weir's problem is unappealable, and says so himself.
- **Ruth Nwosu** refuses two shifts in writing, says the date out loud in a corridor so it would be on the record, and refuses the nineteenth in advance.
- **Pia Santos** produces the correct procedural argument about a count without being asked, and names the most frightening thing she has heard this month: that the loft's own spokesperson has decided not to stand up.
- **Marta Sowle, ninety-one,** is awake at ten past six in the morning, asks who is holding the door, and gets *holder not named, door opened on a declaration, nineteen minutes* onto the public board.

### Power and Continuity State

Jonas ends Stage 2 at the same limit and has now performed **two bounded civic edits at hinges he did not issue**, the second at the Bellow Market cold-store hatch: one field, precedence only, **at the instruction of the named holder of the collectors' self-collect term, who is named on it by rule eleven of the collectors' rules and consented to being findable in the room in front of eleven people**, issuer the market's working chef, claim unpaid. He declined three civic repairs in the batch for want of a witness, a holder, or a room, and each refusal was correct. He still cannot renew a term he did not issue, cannot read the root, and cannot make anybody consent. The seam mark has extended past the elbow into the upper arm, the right hand is numb to the elbow, the maintenance claim is unpaid and has been assigned to him in writing, and he is grade three, probationary, with no appointment and no cabinet.

**Six of nine witnesses are on the card and one refusal is written on it in full.** The two blank lines are a Lowglass resident and a hospital. The Weir stand term has been renewed by the rota on Saturday 5 April to 06:00 on Saturday 19 April, at the cost of a boy of seventeen becoming findable, with a flood forecast for Thursday the seventeenth and a packed trap nobody has money to clear.

### Handoff

Chapters 41–48 complete the nine-witness coalition and close Volume 01. **The volume's calendar is anchored at Chapter 21 = Thursday 13 March and every weekday is counted from that anchor, not from the real calendar; 1 April is a Tuesday, 8 April a Tuesday, 9 April a Wednesday, 14 April a Monday, 17 April a Thursday, 19 April a Saturday, 23 April a Wednesday.** The surge is unspent and the clock is on the wall: the weir stand term expires 06:00 on 19 April and a flood is forecast for the seventeenth. The ninth of April brings the loft's paper to a hospital board, read by a woman who will not stand up. The clinic term expires 06:00 on 8 April, is renewed at 22:00 on 7 April to 06:00 on 15 April, and Ruth Nwosu refuses the fifteenth. The appeal panel sits 23 April and the committee report on the contract is 2 June. The Weir's three-way disagreement, the unsigned retainer and Tomas Renn's two-refusal rule are all still standing, and the last civic term Jonas did not read into a public record is still unread in a chamber at the bottom of a lowland dip.

## Volume 01, Batch 0005 — Chapters 41–48 (VOLUME ENDING)

### Story Progress

The batch completes the nine-witness section and closes the volume, and it does both without pretending the coalition succeeded. Jonas rules a nine-line job card with six names and two deliberately blank lines, and in five weeks the card ends as **eight names, five refusals written in full, and one line that is a key with no holder.** Every refusal is real and every one of them costs somebody: Fola Adeyemi's four reasons against a gas account, a supplier, a lease, a licence and a rota; the Weir's four lines read back unaltered, refused once and not twice because a hearing would then say there were two people on the seventh line; Roz Idris, standing up in a yard in the rain to refuse in front of twenty-two people; Mina Okafor, striking out her own line in her own hand on the ninth of April because her condition had a date on it and there was no cheque; and Dr. Sanaa Iqbal, in a corridor in front of two witnesses, giving a reason he had not prepared her for and then telling him she has been trying to get a second named clinician on that roster since 24 March and had not told him because he would have made it a thing about a card.

The cost of a witness becoming a real one is the batch's engine. **Mina Okafor withdraws on the ninth and the retainer clears on the seventeenth — nine days late — and Selah Mora refuses to ask her to come back, because a witness who comes back out of politeness is a witness you can lose twice.** The trap on the Weir is not cleared, because two people at 05:05 on the Wednesday is two people, and Margo Doré refuses Jonas's offer with the reason that undoes him: *if you go down that bank you stop being a technician who comes to help a street and you become a claim.* The care term expires at 06:00 on 15 April because a renewal clause requires two people to be in a corridor at ten at night and cannot require anybody to be there, and a woman of eighty-four goes out at half past twelve instead of being told at half past twelve, and the figure that goes in the public box is forty minutes — the wait between the van closing and the family being told anything at all, not the time the door took — because the least-bad option was a night orderly with a laundry bag who is on nobody's rota.

The surge arrives as weather, height and time — 910 millimetres either side of 03:00, a flap that shuts itself on the flood exactly as designed, and a bank that goes at twenty to five because Jonas wrote on a loft wall in March that it would not hold a surge. **Three of the nine do the wrong thing and are corrected by other people**: a pump operator holds a storm overflow for seventy minutes to protect a market and a fish merchant standing in cold-store water tells him to turn it over, and the market loses a compressor and a floor of shelving; a school caretaker opens a school at twenty past two on his own key and a teacher who is not on the card shuts it; a platform is closed and a lowland loses its only wheeled exit. Perdita Sow is in the water for four minutes and is alive. A Lowglass block evacuates. A substation is dark for nine hours and the operator box on the fault report is blank and stays blank.

**CivicCore's attempt is a schedule clause, not an invasion.** Paragraph 11 of the Saint Orra service agreement of 2019 defines a node as in failure where its civic term has not been continuously in force for more than twenty-four consecutive hours, and the care term was not continuously in force for forty hours, and that is true, and Jonas says so himself on a wet ramp at twenty to two in the morning. A registrar with a car and a wet telephone suspends the determination on a different ground — *a contractor's contractual definition of failure is not a civic determination of a node's state* — writes both halves into one record in one hand, and tells Jonas to say no to a woman in a good coat **not in a strong voice**, and then says: *this is not a victory, and I gave them the witness by being in a car instead of in a room, and I have run out of ways of not doing it.* The attempt is dated and in the register and suspended and not defeated.

### Midpoint

Chapter 46. The batch's turn is the moment the registrar reads the condition line into a **record** and the thing Jonas refused on 1 April comes back anyway and lands on somebody else. **A minute is a list; a registrar's record describes a state of a node. The distinction is a technicality that costs a man a burden.** IF THE FLAP IS NOT SHUT, THE BANK IS NOT A WALL is entered on 18 April, and a civic work with no holder and no issuer is an unclaimed burden, and it attaches to the nearest responsible maintenance route, and the department had already given Jonas one, and a registrar may not attach two to one route without a determination — so it attaches to the interim lowland post, which is held in his own name by **Royden Achebe**, the man who classified the street as plant. He accepts in nine words out of a yard and says he would rather have had it before he had the post. **Jonas was right on 1 April and it cost the street a claim on a man seventeen days later, and the registrar put in the file, for the file and not for him, that the line was available and was not.**
