# Continuity State

## Bootstrap canon

- Nacre is a contemporary estuary city of roughly 1.3 million people, with eight official districts, nine Lattice service districts including Lowglass, and the Old Ward civic core.
- The Civic Lattice is a physical and social permission network woven into civic infrastructure. It is not a simulation, a portal network, or a mind-reading computer.
- A valid permission has a holder, scope, witness, term, claim, and precedence. A missing part creates a seam.
- The Lattice can authorize access, duties, service claims, and compatible rules. It cannot create matter, force a choice, read intent, rewrite memory, resurrect the dead, or grant skill unrelated to an existing service.
- The current story begins three months before a twelve-year Reclamation Season that renews root clauses.
- Lowglass is a real neighborhood erased from human maps and municipal indexes eighteen years ago during an off-cycle emergency renewal known as the Quiet Cut; the Lattice retains its underlying witness claims.
- Iona Vey is the first administrator and the original architect of the Continuity Project. Her name, notices, and design influence must be present in Volume 1; her full preservation and proxy role are the final antagonist reveal.
- Evan Mercer disappeared during the Quiet Cut. He did not survive as a secret helper; he died after cutting a lowland node, though the exact record remains hidden in early volumes.
- Jonas Mercer’s access comes from a fault-maintenance credential containing Evan’s counter-amendment and its unresolved second signature. It is not bloodline destiny or a prophecy.

## Protagonist continuity

- Jonas is 32, an NDC field technician, observant and practically competent but controlling through competence.
- He wants permanent employment, a repaired Lattice, and proof that his father did not abandon his family.
- He needs to share authority and accept that a technically correct repair can still be morally wrong.
- He must not become a passive hero, a face-slapping genius, or an all-purpose solver. Every new capability requires a witness, a limit, and a cost.
- Jonas can mender one local term in Volume 1. The claim and seam mark remain active consequences.

## Relationship continuity

- Jonas and Sanaa Iqbal have professional friction at the opening. One truthful disclosure earns limited professional trust, not colleague status or romance, in the first batch.
- Tomas Renn represents maintenance labor. His visible responsibility bond is unresolved at the end of the batch and gates Jonas’s first Stage 2 civic edit at a shared node; it is not quietly signed in Chapter 10.
- Pia Santos is an unlisted-route courier and independent civic actor, not a disciple or romantic interest. She speaks for herself in the opening batch but does not become Lowglass’s sole spokesperson.
- Nell Ardent is a legal ally who can challenge Jonas in public.
- Nadia Mercer is Jonas’s living mother and family anchor.
- One slow-burn relationship only: Jonas and Sanaa. No harem.

## Power limits

- Ordinary field repair uses tools, schematics, and work orders.
- Fault reading shows relationships between active faults and terms; it does not reveal intent or guarantee correctness.
- Rule mending requires a physical anchor, a bounded scope, a witness, and a claim.
- Transfer and precedence require willing stewards and public consequences.
- Root reading is unavailable in the opening batch and is not an instant reveal.
- System notices are short, incomplete, and fictional civic maintenance messages. Do not use real software jargon or frequent panels.

## Opening-batch progression guardrails

- Chapters 1–2 are Stage 0 work: ordinary tools, attended procedures, and routes that expire or close on-page. Jonas does not become a bearer, create a supernatural claim, or receive a seam mark.
- The first major repair in Chapter 3 activates only a limited Stage 1 Fault-reader view. Sensory bleed requires a physical anchor and a trusted witness; it does not reveal intent.
- Chapters 4–10 may diagnose local terms and record consequences, but Jonas cannot mend a civic term, transfer a holder, reconcile nodes, or issue a permanent appointment or citywide order.
- The first Stage 2 repair belongs to Chapters 11–18, after Tomas’s visible responsibility bond. Its claim, seam mark, and social cost must remain active until the responsible people discharge or renegotiate them.
- Temporary routes in Chapters 1–10 have a named custodian, a stated expiry or closure, and an on-page disposition. They are not carried forward as Jonas’s personal authority.

## Mystery and antagonist continuity

- Opening mystery: unauthorized access follows an erased neighborhood and a blank root field. The first batch may show an unlisted service route, an incomplete index, an I. Vey seal, and an empty root field, but not the full explanation.
- Oren Pell is a local pressure figure enforcing a valid safety closure, not the mastermind.
- CivicCore’s direct offer, the full Lowglass history, Evan’s counter-amendment and second signature, the maintenance-route explanation, the public Iona reveal, the “last working system administrator” title, and the “find the blank bearer” notice are reserved for later Volume 1 sections. The empty field is not a hidden person.
- CivicCore’s Alden Rook, Continuity Bloc leader Iven Sore, Quiet Archive head Cora Quill, and Iona Vey form the later antagonist ladder. None may be introduced as a new final enemy.
- The continuity default is a structural pressure: when a permission reaches the end of its term with no living steward, the Lattice narrows it to the last verified essential service. It is not a sentient villain.
- The final answer is that the original Lattice required distributed living witnesses; a proxy amendment during the Quiet Cut let one identity stand in for the city. Jonas’s final solution distributes root authority and revokes permanent succession.

## Ending constraints

- Jonas loses direct root access in the final volume.
- Iona survives under public oversight and loses command authority; she is neither resurrected nor absolved.
- Evan remains dead; Jonas resolves his relationship to the missing father without resurrection or complete forgiveness.
- Jonas and Sanaa remain together by choice and keep independent professional lives.
- Nacre’s final Civic Commons has nine rotating Lattice service-district steward seats, multiple witnesses, public renewal, explicit claims, a maximum seventy-two-hour emergency term, and no inheritable root office. A six-of-nine vote can activate only a non-coercive common link; dissenting districts keep local terms.
- The final transition has two clocks: a nine-month political window and a conditional seventy-two-hour physical cascade window that begins when the first linked hinge fails.
- The central mystery is answered. Questions about other cities and the first Lattice experiment remain open without introducing a new final threat.
