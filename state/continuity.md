# Continuity State

## Stable series canon

- Nacre is a contemporary estuary city of roughly 1.3 million people, with eight official districts, nine Lattice service districts including Lowglass, and the Old Ward civic core.
- The Civic Lattice is a physical and social permission network woven into civic infrastructure. It is not a simulation, a portal network, or a mind-reading computer.
- A valid permission has a holder, scope, witness, term, claim, and precedence. A missing part creates a seam.
- The Lattice can authorize access, duties, service claims, and compatible rules. It cannot create matter, force a choice, read intent, rewrite memory, resurrect the dead, or grant skill unrelated to an existing service.
- The story begins three months before a twelve-year Reclamation Season that renews root clauses.
- Lowglass is a real neighborhood erased from human maps and municipal indexes eighteen years ago during an off-cycle emergency renewal known as the Quiet Cut; the Lattice retains its underlying witness claims.
- Iona Vey is the first administrator and the original architect of the Continuity Project. Her name, notices, and design influence must be present in Volume 1; her full preservation and proxy role are the final antagonist reveal.
- Evan Mercer disappeared during the Quiet Cut. He did not survive as a secret helper; he died after cutting a lowland node, though the exact record remains hidden in early volumes.
- Jonas Mercer’s access comes from a fault-maintenance credential containing Evan’s counter-amendment and its unresolved second signature. It is not bloodline destiny or a prophecy.

- Signed in the River Stacks shop on the second day of the bridge sequence, read aloud, and stapled to the shop's yellow board. ### The barrier on the lowland dip

- The flap gate at the bottom of the dip: a disc of green-painted iron on a cast frame, four feet across. The **pintle is rusted solid**. The **sill beam is rotten through the depth across the middle third**, hollow to the hand.
- **There are two sets of bolt holes in the frame.** The current pintle was fitted into holes forty millimetres out from the original, larger, sound ones, so the flap has never sat square. This is why the fill behind the bank is full.
- The chamber floor is **1.60 m below datum**; the sill's top face is **1.10 m below datum**. **A neap low is 1.5 m; a spring low is 2.4 m.** Therefore *in a neap the chamber never clears the beam* and the work can only be done in a spring. Margo Doré had believed the opposite for twenty-nine years; Jonas corrected her from the almanac on 21 March and she accepted it in one paragraph.
- **Tides:** floods on the 8th, 15th, 22nd, 29th and 6th. Springs 12th–22nd. Neaps 22nd–26th. The usable working window is the **morning ebb: low water between 05:05 and 05:30, everything out of the chamber and gone by 07:30**. The evening ebb is dark and was refused. Four days of tide is **nine hours and ten minutes** of work; the cost is paying people for the waiting. **The 04:00 hour on the loft wall paper is the muster, not the working time** — Jonas corrects the paper on the 17th, and says so on the 21st at 03:30 when he is the one sitting in the kitchen.
- **The centuries-old operating error:** the flap was being shut **by hand on the ebb**, which holds water behind it, which is why the bank boiled. Under the frame, painted on the concrete beneath a lead seal: **DO NOT CLOSE ON THE EBB. CLOSE ON THE FLOOD. 6-L/14 — LOWLANDS — 8·III.**
- **The repair, completed Friday 21 March:** rotten middle third cut out; new seasoned ash beam; new pintle; stops set by Jonas three times; bank cut back two metres and revetted with four tonnes of stone; flap set to shut on the flood. It shut itself at **02:10 on 22 March, unattended**, and the barrier held for four hours and ten minutes.
- **The honest limit, written on the loft wall by Jonas:** it will hold an ordinary tide and a great deal of rain. **It will not hold a surge.**
- **There is no name on the bank for any flood.** A ring of pencil on the loft wall reads **THE 22ND, AT 03:00, ON A BANK. NO NAME.** On the 22nd, Perdita Sow was on it alone with her own lamp, having swept that road on Sundays for eleven unpaid years, and rang the loft at 02:10 to say two words.
