# Continuity State

## Stable series canon

- Nacre is a contemporary estuary city of roughly 1.3 million people, with eight official districts, nine Lattice service districts including Lowglass, and the Old Ward civic core.
- The Civic Lattice is a physical and social permission network woven into civic infrastructure. It is not a simulation, a portal network, or a mind-reading computer.
- A valid permission has a holder, scope, witness, term, claim, and precedence. A missing part creates a seam.
- The Lattice can authorize access, duties, service claims, and compatible rules. It cannot create matter, force a choice, read intent, rewrite memory, resurrect the dead, or grant skill unrelated to an existing service.
- The story begins three months before a twelve-year Reclamation Season that renews root clauses.
- Lowglass is a real neighborhood erased from human maps and municipal indexes eighteen years ago during an off-cycle emergency renewal known as the Quiet Cut; the Lattice retains its underlying witness claims.
- Iona Vey is the first administrator and the original architect of the Continuity Project. Her name, notices, and design influence must be present in Volume 1; her full preservation and proxy role are the final antagonist reveal.
- Evan Mercer disappeared during the Quiet Cut. He did not survive as a secret helper; he died after cutting a lowland node, though the exact record remains hidden in early volumes.
- Jonas Mercer’s access comes from a fault-maintenance credential containing Evan’s counter-amendment and its unresolved second signature. It is not bloodline destiny or a prophecy.

## Ending constraints

- Jonas loses direct root access in the final volume.
- Iona survives under public oversight and loses command authority; she is neither resurrected nor absolved.
- Evan remains dead; Jonas resolves his relationship to the missing father without resurrection or complete forgiveness.
- Jonas and Sanaa remain together by choice and keep independent professional lives.
- Nacre’s final Civic Commons has nine rotating Lattice service-district steward seats, multiple witnesses, public renewal, explicit claims, a maximum seventy-two-hour emergency term, and no inheritable root office. A six-of-nine vote can activate only a non-coercive common link; dissenting districts keep local terms.
- The final transition has two clocks: a nine-month political window and a conditional seventy-two-hour physical cascade window that begins when the first linked hinge fails.
- The central mystery is answered. Questions about other cities and the first Lattice experiment remain open without introducing a new final threat.
