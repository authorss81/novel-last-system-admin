## Required Chapter Movement

### Chapter 11 — The Bond

Open with the aftermath of the attended test and the pending appointment. Tomas makes Jonas read a visible responsibility bond in front of workers and an affected witness. Jonas initially tries to write a broad guarantee because broad language feels safer. Tomas makes him narrow the scope, identify who may refuse, name the maintenance claim, and state the expiry. Include a failed practice or rehearsal that shows why an undocumented promise is unsafe. End with the bond visibly accepted or conditionally ready, not with a civic edit already performed.

### Chapter 12 — The Door’s Terms

At the west clinic, inspect the gate and map the six parts of the local permission without turning the scene into a lecture. Ruth performs the existing night watch; Sanaa explains the patient need; Mina records the handoff. The missing or conflicting term is a bounded local access problem, not the root mystery. Jonas and the witnesses disagree about whether the gate should open only for urgent care, for all verified clinic transfers, or for a return-of-care window. End with a written desired service, a named bearer, a term/expiry, and a claim ready for the first repair—or with a clear practical failure that forces that choice.

### Chapter 13 — The First Mended Term

After the bond is visible and the witnesses understand the change, Jonas performs the first civic repair. Keep the action grounded in a physical anchor and a bounded local plate. The existing night-watch steward remains bearer; Sanaa is a separate informed witness; Jonas is issuer. A short Lattice notice may identify the missing term but must not choose the solution. The edit succeeds enough to open the gate for the current service window. Show the first seam mark and the physical cost of the claim. The fallback schedule quietly points to Jonas’s personal availability instead of a named successor. Do not explain every consequence in a speech.

### Chapter 14 — The Useful Mistake

The gate works, and the crews begin using it. Jonas’s competence earns practical trust, but the fallback behaves exactly where he did not look. He tries to solve the problem through more repairs, longer hours, and a larger personal schedule. Tomas and Ruth force him to distinguish “the gate is open” from “the service is safe.” Sanaa notices that he has again made himself the last available answer. End with a patient or transport team arriving during the gap and a transfer being denied or delayed because no willing successor has accepted the schedule.

### Chapter 15 — The Price of an Open Door

Complete the denied transfer on-page. The patient is not a symbol: give the person a name, a concrete need, and a clinical or practical consequence. Jonas must state what the gate did, what it failed to do, and who can stop the next use. Sanaa refuses to certify the repair merely because the hardware is working. The seam mark and maintenance claim remain visible. This is the local midpoint reversal: the first civic repair is technically successful and socially harmful. Do not resolve the relationship or the claim in one conversation.

### Chapter 16 — What the Claim Requires

Jonas tries several practical responses—more coverage, a temporary waiver, an undocumented personal schedule—and each fails because it makes him the invisible owner. Tomas requires a public record of the failure; Sanaa requires clinical disclosure; Ruth will not let the night-watch duty be erased to make the schedule look clean. Allow the conflict to remain uncomfortable. A legal ally may appear only if the scene earns the need; do not let that ally solve the claim. End with a decision to renegotiate the local schedule and accept the debt rather than hide it.

### Chapter 17 — The Existing Bearers

Ruth, Sanaa, Mina, Selah or the Bellow Market steward, Tomas, and Jonas map a successor schedule and a public appeal route for the gate. The transfer that was delayed stays in the record. The improved arrangement can reduce future harm, but the first seam mark and Jonas’s maintenance claim persist until a real duty discharges them. Keep the bearer distinct from issuer. The characters should disagree about what “available” means. End with the gate stable under a local, renewable term and Jonas facing a new request that the old root route may be needed for maintenance.
