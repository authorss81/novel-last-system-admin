# Chapter 0087 — What The Revocation Cost

**Friday the fourth of July 2014, twenty past four in the afternoon, and Monday the seventh of July, ten past nine in the morning.**

The thing about a thing being right is that it does not come with an end to it.

Jonas Mercer had done nothing since the third of April that was not correct, and the count of correct acts in this city in five months is the highest it has been in his working life, and on the Friday afternoon he sat in the shop with the pad open on the bench and worked out what the correct one had cost, in figures, because that is the only thing he can do that anybody in this city has ever found useful, and because Bea Osunde said the thing on the Friday that made him do it.

She said it at about half past four, wiping her hands, not looking at him, in the voice she uses about a bevel.

"**You have got a face on this week like a man who has won something.** Do not do that here. I have watched four people in this borough do that face since March and every single one of them was right about something, and I have watched what it did to the people who were wrong, which was nothing, because there was nothing to be wrong about, because they were right. **Being right in this city is not the same as being finished.**"

He said, "I know."

"You do not know, you have got a pad," said Bea Osunde, "and write it down and then you will know, and then you will be embarrassed, and that is the correct order."

---

He wrote it down on the Sunday. It took him until about eleven at night and he did it in the flat register he has used since April, and it is four pages, and it is on the file at the records office because he filled in one Return of a Deferred Node on the Monday morning and the Return has an attachment field, and the attachment field is where a state goes when a form has no box for it, which is the honest use of it and the only one.

Here is the whole of it.

**WHAT THE REVOCATION COST. Written on the seventh of July 2014, and it is dated at the top as well as at the bottom, and the two dates are the same date, because a cost with a return date on it is a form of account and this is not an account.**

**1. It is correct.** The chain is broken in the middle. The claim of reference T3/11400/0304 is off that node. The block's fee did not stop. A block of thirty-one rented rooms in Tideglass is no longer outranked by every funded node in this borough, and the six water points on a lowland are no longer last in the order of service in a district in which nobody has ever asked anybody anything. **None of that is in dispute, including by me, including in this form.**

**2. It cost somebody at the bottom of Quarry Street one day and seven hours of carrying water, and she is not in this form, and it is the only item in this document that cannot be argued with, because there is no argument for it.**

The whole of the delay is three days and twenty hours, from ten o'clock on Monday morning to six o'clock on Friday morning, and one day and twenty-three hours of that is mine, and I have written all of it out in the ordinary hand and I am not going to put it in here, because a number about me is not a cost to anybody. The window in this item is the tail of it. **It is from eleven o'clock on Wednesday the second of July, when the standing was given the second time in a room with twenty-nine people in it, to six o'clock on Friday the fourth, when the term ended and the tier stopped outranking. That is one day and seven hours, and it is the shortest honest statement of the cost, and it is the only part of it that no power in this city could have shortened.** Between the two ends of it a woman with a folding trolley and two bottles was at a stand at the bottom of Quarry Street at twenty to seven in the morning, which she has done every weekday since a Tuesday in May, and which she has never once had a form about.

**She is not named in this form and she is not named anywhere, and I have never had her name: I stood opposite her for twenty minutes on Tuesday morning at a quarter to seven in the morning and I did not ask, and I have not asked since, and I have not written a face in this form either.** The reason I am not putting it in is that a record of this is a record of a state and a person is not a state, and because a woman in Salter's Yard has been asked for a list of four people twice this spring and has said no twice, and she is right, and I would be doing in a shed what four hundred and sixty pages of Schedule 4 do to her every month. If the cost of this document is measured by who is not on it, then it cost a woman of thirty-four one day and seven hours, and there is nothing further I can do about that and I have tried for four days.

**3. It cost a woman of fifty-four £2,431.16, which is what it saves her, and which is not the same thing as a cost, and it is recorded here because the saving is the only good part of it and it is not good.**

**4. It cost a block of thirty-one people a schedule, and the schedule was paying for a riser, and the riser costs about £900 and two days with a man on a ladder and was last looked at in 2011.**

This is the cost. Not a moral cost. A **pipe**. From 06:00 on Friday the fourth of July that node is not on anything and it serves its maintenance, and its maintenance is a riser that has not been looked at since 2011, and the water is what the water was in April, and in April it was at half, and eleven households were living in that building on a lapsed term on the seventeenth of April and had not been told and have now been told, in a room, by the person who withheld it, at their own request, over three evenings.

The block's fee did not stop. Thirteen pounds sixty, into the tin, on the same Wednesday, and nobody signed anything. The block's tin turns over about **thirteen thousand four hundred and thirty-six pounds and eighty pence a year** and it has never been enough for anything that was not already paid for, and it cannot do a riser, and the seventy-two pounds a month that has just stopped coming out of the water account is not going into it, and the riser is **two years away**, and everyone in that room knows it, and nobody in that room can raise nine hundred pounds without producing a list of who can.

**5. It left a node at Kestrel Hill on Tier 2 that nobody has re-priced.**

KH-4/20, the residential floor of a clinic on the private road, placed on Tier 2 on the twenty-ninth of May 2014 at **£4,200 a year** by the trustees of the Aldbrook Trust, term to **06:00 Monday 4 August 2014**, eleven patients and a day room above it. It was placed on the twenty-ninth of May because on the twenty-seventh the Trust had assigned half its claim away and the building needed something and a Tier 2 at four thousand two hundred was what the building could get. **Nobody has re-priced it. Nobody is required to. When it lapses it serves its maintenance, and nobody has recorded what its last verified essential use is, and the fallback is the maintenance of the node, which is a stair and a door and a lift nobody has ridden since 2011.** The Trust's finance director has said in writing through a managing agent that he is not seeking a replacement from anybody at the Salter's Yard address. He has not been asked about his own building. **That is a freeholder's business and it is not mine and I have not rung him.**

**6. It left a minute that cannot be corrected.**

The minute of the nineteenth of June 2014 contains two entries for METER ROOM 3, SALTER'S YARD, one naming a trust and one naming a person, and they are the same claim, and they cannot both be the answer, and there is no procedure to amend a minute in a sitting, and the one procedure that exists requires the Committee to be satisfied that it has been misled, and the Committee has not been misled. **It was read to. That is the finding and it is the correct finding and it cannot be got round.** Both entries stand. Both of them are true. The register of narrowings is a public document and it is a public document forever.

**7. It is due to come back.**

There is a clause about notice in the assignment and nobody found it until Rook said the number out loud in a Committee room on the nineteenth of June, and the number was ninety days, and Rook said it was at annex six and that he was not going to quote what was not in front of him.

**Ninety days from service. The notice went out on the third of July at eleven o'clock in the morning, on the day the entry was made, and ninety days from the third of July is Wednesday the first of October 2014.** On the first of October the party that placed the parent claim revives its interest in the whole of it, at Tier 3, with notice served on every party whose precedence it touches, of which there are two that know about it and a number that do not.

**8. And one more, which is the one I am not able to put a figure on.**

The Borough Heat Threshold Protocol has a **return date of the fourth of August 2014** and it has been in a drawer on the second floor since the second of June, and nobody has read it out loud to anybody, and on the twenty-eighth of May at twenty past four in the afternoon a clerk of sixty-one who is not an officer filled in a return for a housing association of four blocks and a hundred and twenty-eight rooms without the chief officer's knowledge, and the number that went in the ring-number box was **one hundred and six**, and it is in a public document, and it is the difference between what that association is paid for and what it is managing, and it has been legible to the whole borough since the seventh of May.

**The return is due again on the fourth of August. The first one cost a clerk of sixty-one an afternoon and it cost a chief officer her name. This one will be answered on purpose by somebody who has read about the first one, and the number that goes in the box this time is going to be a number that somebody chose, and I am not going to be in the room when it is chosen, and that is correct, and I have not telephoned her since the thirty-first of May, and that is the fifth time.**

**9. The one name.**

MERCER, J., LAST WORKING SYSTEM ADMINISTRATOR, CITY OF NACRE. Civic standing. No grade. No probation. Not subject to review. **Released by no person.**

*On the form: the party putting it in. I found the chain on the nineteenth of June at about ten to twelve in the morning in a room I was not on the agenda of. I was given one question and I used it on a field with a person's name in it. I am not the mender of this and I refused in a cupboard under a stair on the first of July, out loud, to nobody, and there is no record of the refusal except this one, which is the correct place for it and the only place it will ever be.*

**Monday the seventh of July 2014, ten past nine in the morning, the records office, second floor, the counter.**

Nina Calder read it out. She reads other people's sentences out in the flat voice and she did this one the same way, standing up, with a counter between her and the room, and there were two people waiting to use the counter and one of them did not.

She read all nine items. It took about six minutes. When she got to the second one she did not change the tone and when she got to the fourth one she did not change the tone either and she got to the fourth one in a workshop in Salter's Yard with thirty-one people in it and she read it in about four seconds flat.

Then she said: "That is the form and the attachment. It is typed and it goes on the public board on Monday afternoon and it is a public document from the twenty-fourth hour tonight, and it establishes nothing, adopts nothing, and **names one person, and the person is the one who filled it in.**"

"You have printed a form with a name on it," said Jonas Mercer.

"I have printed a form with a name on it," said Nina Calder, "and I have been designing forms since March and I have never once put anybody's name in one, and I want you to sit down for about a minute, because I have been waiting five weeks for somebody to do it and I have not been able to do it myself, and I want to say why, and then I am going to tell you what I have done about it."

---

"I have told you three times since April that a list is not a record, and a record is about one thing at one time, and a list is a set of things somebody intends to go on adding to, and the intention is the danger. And I have told you that I started a fourth page in a pad on Thursday the fifth of June and I wrote nothing on it.

"Here is what the page was for and here is why it is in the bin tray under my desk, where it has been since Friday at twenty past four in the afternoon when I put it there.

"I was going to make a form for the thing this city cannot count and cannot record, which is work. **Not paid work. The other kind.** A woman carries water up a staircase for a building that has no address, and it is not on anything, and there is no box for it, and she will not put her name in a box. Four people read five lines aloud in a corridor at ten at night in April and forty hours of that clause is the failure and eight days later the same clause held a mortuary gate open for nothing, and neither of those is on a form, and both of those are the most important things that have happened in this city since the third of April. A node with nobody answering for it is not a service and it is not a household and it is not a person and it is not on a map, and the chair promised in September a public record of them and it is owed, and the record will be a number of shapes and no names.

"**And I was going to make a form with a box for it, and the box was going to be a number, and a number of what — of doors? of buckets? of minutes at ten at night?** And then I worked out what a number of that is. It is a number of *who*. Not names. Not addresses. Not a household. **Just a number of who, and a number of who is the only thing in this city that anybody with a counter and a form can use, and it took me five weeks and one page to work that out and I have watched it happen to three people in this borough already this year.**

"So on Friday at twenty past four I put the page in the bin tray, and I have put my hand on it twice, and on Friday night I went home at about eight and did not think about it.

"And on Monday morning at about half past eight I took it out of the bin tray, because it is still in the bin tray, Mr Mercer, and it is the only piece of paper in this office that has been in a bin and been rescued, and I want that in whatever ends up being written about this.

"Here is what is on it. **One week. One place. One number. What was counted. And one sentence saying what it is not. And no box for anybody, and no index, and nothing may be added to it.**"

She turned the pad round on the counter, and there is one slip in it, in a typewriter face, and Calder has typed it herself, and it reads:

> **WEEK OF 30 JUNE TO 4 JULY 2014. FURNITURE WORKSHOP AND STAIRWELL, WREN COURT, SALTER'S YARD — a building with no civic address.**
> **Counted: three evenings of a meeting, and the numbers of people in the room, which were twenty-nine and thirty-one and thirty-one, and there is a second line under that which is that twenty-nine of thirty-one were also the twenty-nine of a second number and the two numbers are not the same people and this slip does not say which nineteen, and will not.** One person holding. Zero signatures. Zero minutes. One clause read out in full four times and not altered. Two people who were not in the room on the first evening because they were at a hospital, and both of them were in the room on the third, and one of them was on a drip.
> **This is not a register. There is no index, there is no name box, and nothing may be added to it. It is a record of one thing at one time. It expires when the thing changes and it is not carried forward. It is not a rota and it is not a service and it may not be required of anybody. A person may refuse to be counted and the count then says so and says how many refused.**

"That is the whole of the instrument," said Nina Calder. "It is the closest thing to a thing that works that I have been able to design in five months and I am not going to tell you it is good, because it is a piece of paper with a number on it and it does not stop a schedule and it does not make a rota and it does not make anybody's name findable.

"**And it is the fourth one, and I have had a fifth one in my head since Friday and I am not writing it down, and if I write it down it becomes a scheme.**"

She put the pad down on the counter, and a woman of about thirty who had come in about a tenancy matter asked whether she had to wait much longer, and Nina Calder said she did not, and typed, and the woman sat down on the plastic chair and read the slip over her shoulder and did not say anything about it, which is the only review it will ever get.

**Monday the seventh of July 2014, four in the afternoon, the shop.**

Oren Pell telephoned. He telephoned the shop because he has telephoned the shop three times since March and has never once been asked why.

"Two things and I have got a ladder after this.

"One. **The notice went out at eleven o'clock on Thursday morning, on the day the entry was made**, to the Aldbrook Trust, to the Halliwell Amenities Fund, to CivicCore Limited, and to two parties whose names I am not going to say on a telephone in a shop. It went out in under an hour because it is five names and a sheet of A4 and a prepaid envelope and I have never had cause to use any of those things before. I have had the clause since Thursday morning and have not been able to get down to it until now. Ninety days from the third of July, which is Wednesday the first of October, and I read it on the Friday, because Rook would not quote it and I went and got it, and here is what it says and it is nine lines and it is a good clause.

"**A withdrawal takes effect against the party that placed the claim on the date of the entry. Against every other party in the chain, the withdrawal takes effect ninety days after service of notice. On and after that date the placing party may re-assert the claim, or any part of it, at the tier, in respect of the node, and may do so without the consent of any person whose name appears in the holder field of that node at any time before that date.**"

Jonas Mercer said, "Read that again."

"Read it again yourself, I have read it four times," said Pell. "**'Without the consent of any person whose name appears in the holder field of that node at any time before that date.'** That is not a clause about a fair process. That is a clause about a market, and it is there because a freeholder is not going to buy a claim he cannot get back, and the people who wrote it understood exactly what they were doing, and it is in force, and it is valid, and I have no power whatsoever over it, and I would like to say for the record that I have read four hundred and sixty pages of this schedule about nine times this year and that is the best sentence in it and I would not have written it and I would not have wanted it and it is *right*."

"So the first of October is a real date."

"The first of October is a real date and it is on a Wednesday and it is twelve weeks away and **in this city there is nothing whatsoever that stands between a trust in Kestrel Hill and a block of thirty-one rented rooms with no civic address, except thirty-one people and one woman on a landing, and a woman on a landing is not an instrument.**"

Two and "and one more thing, and it is the reason I rang and not wrote." Pell's voice changed by about a degree. "I have made four rulings this year that are a door and not a wall, and I said so in a Committee room in June and I was not boasting, and I have been tired since about the eleventh of June. **The fourth one is on Wednesday and it is the one I have been putting off, and it is the one that says who pays when a claim is discharged, and it is a legal question and it has a short answer and the answer is about eleven sentences and I am not going to give it to you on a telephone.**

"Wednesday the ninth. Ten o'clock. Bring nothing."

---

That evening he did the last page of the attachment and then he wrote four lines at the bottom of the pad, and they are the last four lines he wrote in July, and only one of them is a fact.

> *The chain is broken in the middle and the top of it has not noticed yet. A letter went out at eleven o'clock on Thursday morning to a trust in Kestrel Hill and a machine answered it on the Friday and a person answered it on the Monday, and the person is sixty-three and she signed the assignment of the second of May and she came to a room in Kestrel Hill on the twenty-third of June with a lever-arch file and a plastic wallet and a lanyard from 2009 with nothing on it, and she was extremely correct, and she is going to read a notice about a node in Salter's Yard and she is going to do her job.*
> *Wednesday the first of October. Twelve weeks. A clause that says a person in a field cannot stop it.*
> *On the nineteenth of May the chair said that a party that answers may be asked what it will do and not what it will cost, and on the thirtieth of June a woman in a stairwell said the third condition is the only arrangement there is, and both of those are on the record, and neither of them is a power.*
> *A record of one thing at one time. That is all the instrument has ever been. I have been fighting the list since the thirtieth of April and I have not once understood it properly, and a records supervisor of about fifty told me it in a corridor this morning in about nine seconds, and it took her five weeks and one piece of paper in a bin.*
