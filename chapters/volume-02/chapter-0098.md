# Chapter 0098 — The Letter

**Thursday the eighteenth of September 2014, half past nine in the morning, the shop.**

The first draft took him until about eleven and it was two hundred and ten words and it was addressed to the managing agent of the freeholder of four blocks on Carrow Lane, and it was the first letter he has ever written in his life for anybody, and he said so on the second page of the pad, to nobody.

Bea Osunde read it standing at the end of the bench, which is where she reads things, and she read it twice and then put a finger on the fourth line and stopped.

"You have written *the households of the block*."

"Yes."

"That is the only sentence in here that could hurt somebody, and you have written it three times, and in one of them you have written *the eight households*." She handed it back. "**A count. In a letter. To a managing agent. In a year when that block has thirty rooms and no mains water in any of them and none at all since the sixth of May, and a chief officer has put ninety-two in a public box on purpose.** You have written the number eight into a document about a building with no water in it since the sixth of May, and the number eight is a number of who, and a number of who is the only thing in this borough anybody with a form can use."

He looked at it for about four minutes.

"They have been asked for this twice," he said. "Once about 2008 and once about six years ago. Ivy Marsden is seventy-two and she has carried water for thirty rooms since 1998 and she asked for one letter saying the buckets are hers, and she was refused twice in sixteen years, and the second refusal went to a company and not to a person. **And on the eleventh of July a woman telephoned this shop and gave no name and said she was one of the eight and said there is no date on it and that is why she is telephoning a shop.**"

"Then write the year."

"The year is on the stopcock tag."

"Then the letter is nineteen ninety-eight and it is about a stopcock, and Mr Mercer, **the tag has been in a cupboard that has never had a lock since 1998 and the tag is the only document anybody in this borough has ever produced about that block.**"

---

He rewrote it twice more and at about twenty past two in the afternoon he walked forty-one minutes to the records office with both drafts in his coat and put them on the counter in front of Nina Calder and did not say anything at all, which is a thing she appreciates.

"Type it," he said.

"No."

"You have not read it."

"I have read the top of it through the counter, which is enough." She did not touch it. "**I am not typing that and here is the reason and it is the reason I have been designing forms since March and the reason I put a page in a bin in July.**

"A letter typed on this office's machine, with this office's crest at the top because every template in this building has the crest at the top, is a departmental document. And a departmental document is a record. And a record is a thing that gets kept and indexed and asked for, and the first person who asks for it is not the woman it is about, it is **the next managing agent**, in four years, when the freeholder has changed, and that agent will be looking for a list of who carries water in a block with no water in it, and there will be one number in the letter, and it will be the only number in it, and it will have come out of a building where eight households have been carrying the water for thirty rooms since 1998, and it will be a number of who.

"**A letter is not a form. A form is a thing that gets kept.** And I have watched one of yours go on a public board in July with a name in a box and I read it out in a flat voice for six minutes to a woman waiting about a tenancy, and it was correct, and it is a record of a man who filled it in. I am not doing that to a letter about eight people and a bucket."

"Then it does not exist in a form anybody can keep."

"It exists in a hand," said Nina Calder. "**Write it out. Do not type it, do not print it, do not put a crest on it, and do not put a date in a box — put the date in a sentence like a person.** And when you have written it, put it in an envelope, and take it to the post office, and do not come back into this office with it again, because the moment it comes through that door it is a record and it gets a number."

---

**Thursday the eighteenth of September 2014, about five in the afternoon, the shop, on the back of a delivery note.**

> *Why this letter has taken two days and I have not written it yet, and I am writing it here because I cannot put it in a form and there is nowhere else to put it:*
> *The letter has to say the buckets are theirs. And the buckets are one woman's, and Ivy Marsden is seventy-two and she has said so out loud, in a Methodist hall, in May, and I was in that hall and I did not ask her anything.*
> *The letter cannot say whose. Not because of her. Because **if it names one household then the freeholder's agent will ask for the other seven, and there is no version of me that gets to say no to a managing agent on behalf of a block with no water in it, because I am not a party to the building, I do not hold its maintenance, and on the fourteenth of April that agent's standing instruction was that four meters come out of a cupboard that has never had a lock, and the stopcock turns, and the tag says 1998, and the riser died in February 1998 and has not been replaced since.** The agent is not a villain. The agent is a person with a standing instruction and about nine hundred and fifty other blocks.*
> *So the letter says 1998, and it says the block, and it says the stopcock, and it says the tag, and it says the number of rooms, and it does not say a single name, and it does not say eight, and it asks for one thing, which is that four meters do not come out of that cupboard.*
> *And it says at the bottom, in my own hand, that I wrote it and that I am not authorised to write it. Which is the wrongness. The wrongness is not the four meters and it is not the 1998. **The wrongness is that there is my name at the bottom of it, and there is nobody else's, and the woman it is for has not asked me to write it, and nobody has asked her anything.** And I have spent five months refusing to put a name in a field, and I am going to put a name at the bottom of a letter, and the only reason is that a letter with no name on it is not a letter, it is a leaflet, and two people have been refused a leaflet twice in sixteen years.*
> *There is a woman in that block who telephoned this shop on the eleventh of July at twenty-five past four and gave no name and gave me a number and said she would not answer if I rang her back, and I have had the number in a pad for ten weeks and I have not rung it, and it is a Friday tomorrow and there is a Thursday next week and there is a Thursday every week for about three years.*

---

**Friday the nineteenth of September 2014, ten past four in the afternoon, the shop.**

The telephone rang four times.

He did not say his name. He did not say hers. He had eleven seconds in which to say the thing he had been carrying for ten weeks and he said a different thing.

"You telephoned me on the eleventh of July at twenty-five past four."

"Yes."

"You said you were one of the eight and that a letter had been asked for twice and that the second time went to a company and not to a person, and you said there is no date on it and that is why you telephoned a shop."

"That's what I said."

"I am not going to ask your name and I am not going to ask which eight." He put the pad flat on the bench. "**I am going to ask you one thing and then I am going to do what you tell me and that is the whole of the telephone call. What does it have to say.**"

There was a pause of about four seconds.

"**The year.**"

"The year."

"**Put nineteen ninety-eight on it and do not put who on it.**" Her voice did not change and it did not go up, and she said it the way a person says a thing they have said to themselves a lot. "That is the whole of what I have wanted for sixteen years and I have never said it to anybody and I have said it to a shop in about nine seconds. **The buckets are ours. Everybody in that block knows they are ours. There has never been one single thing in writing that says so, and that is not a hardship anybody in that block has ever suffered from, because you do not need a piece of paper to know you have been carrying water up four flights since you were thirty.**

"And in about four years one of us is going to be asked by somebody, and it is going to be a form, because it is always a form, and if there is a piece of paper with a year on it then the form is a form about a year and not a form about me. **And I am seventy-one and there are women in that block who are older than me and there is one who is seventy-two and she has asked for a letter saying the buckets are hers, and I am not going to put her name on a piece of paper that I have been telephoned by, because the day it is on there she is a person somebody can ask.**"

"I am not going to put anybody's name on it."

"I know you are not. **That is why I telephoned a shop and not a council.**" A pause. "Now. Are you going to send it."

"To the managing agent. By post. By hand to the post office, and it will not be typed, and it will not have a crest on it, and there will be a date in a sentence and not in a box."

"Good."

"And I am not going to send you a copy."

"Do not send me a copy." She said it fast. "**Do not send me a copy and do not tell me that you have sent it. I have thought about that for ten weeks and I have got it down to that and I am telling you why, and it is the only reason I am still on this telephone.** A person who knows that a letter has been sent is a person somebody can ask whether the letter has been answered. And if somebody asks me that in about two years — and somebody will, because the agent's office rings — then I will be a woman in a block who has had a letter sent, and I will be a record, and I will be a name, and **I have been a person with no date on me for eleven years and it is the only thing in this borough that is mine.**"

"You will be a person with a year on it," said Jonas Mercer. "That is what is on the paper. And that is a document, and a document is what six agreements in four blocks have not been sent back since January, and two of those six have told her to her face that they will not sign a thing that makes her name a door, and she has not chased them, and I heard all of that in a room with a transcript in it on Thursday, and I know what I am writing. And I am writing it anyway, and it is not your decision and I am not asking you to make it and I would have written it if you had said no."

"I would not have said no," said the woman. "**I have said no to two things in sixteen years and one of them was them and one of them was a company, and there is a third thing I say no to every week and it is my own name.**"

The line went.

He wrote the letter out at the bench from half past four until about ten past six, on one sheet, in ink, in a hand that is not his best, and it is four paragraphs long and it is dated in a sentence, and here it is entire.

> **To the managing agent, freehold, four blocks, Carrow Lane. Written on the nineteenth of September 2014.**
>
> **I am writing about the block of 1938 at the rear of those four blocks. It has thirty rooms. There is no mains water in it. The internal riser has not existed since February 1998 and has not been replaced, and no water has come into that building from the mains since then. A freeholder's agent isolated the incoming supply on the sixth of May 2014 and the block has had no water at all since that day. What the households of that block have carried, by hand, in containers, up four flights, is that water, and they have been carrying it, in whole or in part, since 1998.**
>
> **No household is named in this letter. No household is asked to confirm anything in this letter. No list of anybody accompanies this letter and none will be supplied, and a request for one will not produce one.**
>
> **The stopcock for that block is in the meter cupboard. The cupboard has never had a lock and there is no key. The stopcock is reachable under a cover plate with four slotted screws and a plastic lifting handle, and it turns. The tag in the cupboard reads STOPCOCK / 1998, and that tag has been in that cupboard since the riser failed, and it is the only document that has ever existed about this arrangement.**
>
> **I am asking for one thing, which is that the four meters proposed for that cupboard are not installed. The reason is the year on the tag. If a meter is installed, then the water in those thirty rooms has a landlord, and in about four years somebody will ask the landlord to say who draws from it, and the answer to that question is a list, and a list of who draws water in a block with no water in it is a list of the households that are still there.**
>
> **This letter is not sent by the housing association and the housing association is not a party to it and has not asked for it. It is written by me. I am a probationary grade three technician. The civic standing I hold in this city has no grade, no department and no probation, and it gives me no authority whatever over the supply, the freehold, the block or the households, and I have never in my working life had authority over any of them. I am not authorised to write this letter and I have not asked anybody's permission to write it. I am writing it because it has been asked for twice since about 2008 and because a person telephoned this shop on the eleventh of July and said that there is no date on it, and there is one now.**
>
> **MERCER, J.**

He read it four times, and the fourth line of the second paragraph was the one he had written nine ways, and the ninth one was the best one and it was the shortest.

He put it in an envelope, and put the envelope in his coat, and did not take it to the post office until Monday morning, because the post office on the corner does not take letters on a Friday after half past four and he did not want it in a box in a shop.

---

**Friday the nineteenth of September 2014, twenty minutes to nine in the evening, the shop.**

Bea Osunde shut the roller and did the till and came back through and looked at the envelope on the bench with the crest off it and the ink.

"You've written it."

"I have written it."

"You've put your name on it."

"Yes."

"You've not put hers on it."

"**No. And I have left a space where the number eight would go, and the space is on purpose, and it is the only thing in this letter I am proud of, and I have been in this trade eleven years and I have never once been proud of a space.**" He put his hand flat on the bench, the way he puts it flat on a sill. "And there is going to be an agent's clerk in that office in about eleven days reading a letter with one name on it and a year on it, and she is going to be told that she cannot have a list, and she is going to have to take that on trust from a man who has no authority over anything, and she is going to take it, because the reason she will take it is that there is a year on the page and a stopcock in a cupboard and a tag that has been there since 1998, and **a year is not a person, and that is the whole of what I have got and it is enough to stop four meters and it is not enough to help anybody, and those are two different sentences and I have spent five months learning not to put them in the same paragraph.**"

"And the woman on the telephone."

"Is seventy-one," said Jonas Mercer, "or thereabouts, and she did not give me her name and I did not ask and I have written the word *households* nine times and I have not written a single one of them with anything after it, and she is going to find out tomorrow morning from a woman on the second landing whose water it is not, that there is a letter, and she is not going to be told by me, and **that is the first correct thing I have done since the third of April and it took me ten weeks and one telephone call and I did not make it.**"

He did not write it down anywhere, which is the first time since March that he has done a thing and not written it down, and he noticed that on Saturday morning and did not do anything about it.

He had a pad in his coat with ten weeks of a telephone number in it and four months of a woman's back in a workshop in it and eleven weeks of a Tuesday in it and a year of a registration in it, and on the Saturday he did not open it, and the reason is that the letter is going into a post box on Monday morning with no typed copy and no crest and no number box in it, and the whole of what he has learned since the fourth of April is that the moment a thing goes into a machine it becomes a thing a counter can give to somebody else.

**The block's node is still at Tier 2 at four thousand two hundred pounds a year. There is no water in that block. The fee did not stop and the letter does not stop it, and the letter is not going to fix a riser, and he wrote that on the outside of the envelope in pencil on Thursday afternoon and then rubbed it out, because a letter that says what it is not is a second letter.**

On the Sunday he planed the surface of the bench and oiled it and took the ghost of an old date up out of the grain, and he wiped the bench with his thumb.
