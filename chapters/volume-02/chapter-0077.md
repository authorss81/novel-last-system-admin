# Chapter 0077 — The Report on 6-L/14

The report was filed at eleven o'clock on Thursday the twenty-ninth of May in a room in Gantry Ward with two doors, and there was nobody from this account in it, which was the correct arrangement and which had been arranged on Wednesday in about nine words on a telephone between two men who have never been in a room together.

Jonas Mercer was in a chamber on the lowland at eleven o'clock on the twenty-ninth of May, with a torch in his left hand and a tide coming up behind him, because Emeka Rusk had rung the shop at ten past eight and said the screens were worse and he wanted somebody in the chamber who was not him, and Jonas Mercer had got on a bus.

He was not a party. He is not a member, not an officer of the department, not a submitter and not a holder. On the eighteenth of April a deputy registrar told him, in a car park, in about ninety seconds, that he could be at the back of a room if he could stand being at the back of it, and that if anybody put a microphone on him the registrar would stand up and say that he was not a party, and that this is the entire extent of what a register entry buys a man in this city.

So he was at the bottom of a lowland chamber with a torch while Royden Achebe filed nine hundred words on a piece of ground that Jonas Mercer has not been able to reach since the fourteenth of March, and did not try to reach, and had not been asked to reach.

---

What the report says, and it is eleven pages and nine hundred words and it is correct.

**6-L/14. CHAMBER AND BANK. Report of 29 May 2014. Field services manager, NDC, in his own name, on the interim lowland maintenance post.**

The chamber is a concrete structure of 1938 with a silt trap on its upstream face and a flap gate in a frame on the downstream face and a sill beam of softwood, original, in the bottom of the frame, and the bank on the downstream side is revetted in loose stone to a depth of about four hundred millimetres at the toe and nothing at all above it.

**The chamber is not maintainable. The bank is not maintainable. The works owed are four tonnes of stone and one sill beam.** The stone is at about £210 the tonne delivered and the beam is at about £340, and the two of them together come to about £1,180, and the report says that in one line without any argument at all, and the line is the most useful line in the document.

**And the report says: the burden of 6-L/14 should be released.**

And then it does not release it, on the last page, in four lines, and Jonas Mercer did not see those four lines until Friday morning and they are the whole of the chapter.

> **A burden is not a claim. A burden cannot be refused by the person who holds it, because a burden is a work with no counterparty and there is nobody for it to be refused to. A burden may be released only as a claim, and a claim may be released only to a successor who consents to hold it.**
> **The successors to this burden are the persons who would use the structure. There is no party that answers for 6-L/14. There is no district, no number, no location of record, and no holder, and there has not been one since the sixth of April.**
> **I am therefore unable to release this burden and I am required to report that it should be released, and I am recording that these two things are true at the same time, and I do not have a mechanism that makes them not both true, and I have looked.**
> **I am not permitted to name a successor. I have had this post since the first of April and I have looked for a way to name a street and there is not one, and I have concluded that the reason there is not one is the correct reason.**

Jonas Mercer read those four lines standing in a chamber on the lowland at about twenty past eleven on the Friday morning, in the version Royden Achebe read to him in the open doorway of a works van on the tide, with a bag of gravel at his feet and his hands in his pockets, in about four minutes, and he read them twice.

“He read it out to me in a van,” he said afterwards, in the shop book, “which is not where it is supposed to be read and which he chose on purpose because of the tide and not because of anything else.”

---

What the report does not contain, and it took three people and eight weeks to get it that way, is the point of the whole thing.

**There is not one resident of the Weir in it. Not one name, not one household, not one count, not one address, not one reference to a stand, a stair, a bench, a tin, a tap, a bucket, a path, a letter, a bicycle, or a woman of sixty-three with an opinion. There are no people in it at all.** The eleven pages are about a chamber, a trap, a flap gate, a sill beam, four tonnes of stone, a level, a date, and a man.

And getting it to that state was not luck. It was three decisions by three people, made in three rooms, in eight weeks, and not one of them made for the purpose of writing a good report.

**The first was a registrar in an office on the eighteenth of April**, who read a condition line into a public record because the law required him to make a record of the state of a node, and put under it, for the file and not for any person, the sentence that the condition line on 6-L/14 was available to be recorded on the first of April and was not. Nobody has ever been asked to explain that sentence and it is the reason there is a document in this city that describes a piece of ground as it actually is, and it is four lines long and it is in a minute, and the fourth line is an accusation with no name in it.

**The second was a service manager on the first of April**, who took an interim post in nine words on a telephone from a yard in Gantry Ward because a department cannot hold a plate, and who said then, and says now, that he would rather have had it before the post, and who has spent eight weeks writing nine hundred words about a piece of ground he has not been able to reach, and who is going to keep the burden for the rest of his working life because a burden cannot be refused.

**The third was a probationary grade three technician on the fourteenth of March, in a records room, in a hurry, with a question mark after 6-L/14 on a piece of paper, who wrote six words into a field because the field would not take blank and there was nowhere else to put them:**

> **NON-CIVIC PLANT**

Six words. Written in eleven minutes. And at the Reclamation Season maintenance review they were accepted, and the acceptance was recorded as *6-L/14 accepted as non-civic plant, no civic claim attaching to the chamber or the bank*, and Jonas Mercer was in that room and said true evidence out loud and said nothing about this, deliberately, on the same afternoon, and has not been asked to unwrite them by anybody, and nobody in this city knows that they are his.

**No civic claim attaches to the chamber or the bank.** That sentence is the reason a service manager could report in the first person and in the plainest words that a chamber and a bank are not maintainable and that the burden should be released. If there had been a civic claim on it, he would have had to name the claim, and the claim is a street, and a street is a location of record, and a location of record is a survey, and a survey is a count, and a count is the price.

**And the six words are also the reason nobody can pay the four tonnes of stone and the sill beam.** No civic claim attaches, so there is nobody to be invoiced, so the £1,180 in that one useful line cannot be put on anybody's schedule, because a continuity fee buys a node and this is not a node; it is not maintainable; it has no term; and it is not on a map, and there is no map, and there is no form, and there is no address.

---

And the eleven pages name a duty that is held, right now, in a completely different instrument, by the same man, in a different building, on a different file.

**Page nine, paragraph three.** *The outfall inspection duty, which is described at 6-L/16 and which was transferred to the holder of this post on 17 May 2014, is not discharged by this report and is not affected by the recommendation at paragraph eleven.*

Jonas Mercer read that standing in a works van doorway at eleven forty-five on Friday the thirtieth of May and did not say anything at all, and then said:

“Which file is that on.”

“A different one,” said Royden Achebe. “The six dash L slash sixteen duty is in Nina Calder's typing on the series 6 lowland chain file, in your handwriting in six lines, with my name twice and yours on both. This report is on the field services file in a folder of its own, and it was opened on the first of April, and it is in a drawer, and the drawer is in a cabinet in a corridor in Gantry Ward.

“Those two documents are about the same bank. One of them says the bank should be released and cannot be. The other one gives somebody a duty on four hundred and fifty metres of spur four hundred metres away which comes round this corner in a boat. **And there is no rule anywhere that anybody has to read both, and neither of them knows the other exists, and I have known about both of them since the eighteenth of May and I have not been able to find anybody in this city who is required to put them in the same drawer.**”

“Then what happens to the duty if the report is accepted.”

“The duty stays with me,” said Royden Achebe. “The report is about the chamber and the bank. The duty is on the spur. If the report is accepted and the burden is released, then the chamber and the bank have got a man in a field services file who has said out loud in writing that they are not maintainable, and the spur has got a man in Nina Calder's typing who cannot give it back, and both of those men are me, and I have got one pair of hands and I am in this van on a Friday morning because the tide is in.”

“And the acceptance.”

“The department will accept it. It has to. It is a report and the finding is correct and the man is right and the four tonnes of stone are real. It will be accepted at about level three, which means it goes in a tray and it does not come back out for about eleven weeks, and in eleven weeks the tray will be opened by somebody who has never heard of any of this, and the acceptance will be a signature.”

“Which makes a vacancy.”

Royden Achebe stopped with his hand on the van door.

“No,” he said. “It does not make a vacancy. A vacancy is a thing that has to be *made* — you have to have a post and a person in it and a decision to let the person go. What it makes is a **line.** A line on a file with my name in it and the words *reported not maintainable, release recommended, not released* in the next column, and in eleven weeks a signature against it, and a line with a name in it that is a piece of ground nobody maintains. **A line like that does not sit on a file for a year. Lines like that get found.**”

He got in the van and put it into reverse and stopped it again.

“Eleven weeks and then somebody comes to work out what the line is for, and they will want to fill it, and there will be a man standing there with a form and a piece of paper, and he will not be me and he will not be you, and he will be somebody with a directory and a telephone number, and I have known that since the first of April and I have not been able to do one single thing about it, and I want that in your book, because it is the last thing in this account that is actually mine to say.”

---

Nina Calder said the other thing.

She said it on Friday the thirtieth of May at about ten past one in the afternoon, in the records office, to a man who was standing up, with the folder of returns on the shelf and the bureau open because it was the last hot day of that week and there was a fan in the window.

She said it before he asked, and she said she had been sitting on it since Wednesday, and she said it in one go and then she said she was not going to say it again.

“**The last time there was a gap in that file it was 2011.** It got filled inside about four months by a man who was sent out from estates, and the thing that got him the job was being told the line was plant.

“And I have never found out where he was before that. Not in nineteen years. He is not a bad man. He is a good man and he is careful and he is now somewhere else and I have worked with him for nine years on two other things and I have never once been able to ask him, because you cannot ask a man who does not know that you have noticed a gap about where he was before he was told the line was plant.

“He had, from what I have been able to put together out of about six things that were said near me in nine years without anybody meaning to say them, a fortnight. **A fortnight somewhere in this city with a bucket under a tap.** That is all of it. That is the whole of what I have got, and I have been sitting on it since Wednesday morning because a service manager told a technician in a corridor on Thursday that a line on a file had got a name in it that says *not maintainable* and that in eleven weeks somebody is going to come and fill it, and I went and looked in a drawer.

“**That is my sentence and I have said it out loud, and it is not a warning, and it is not about anybody, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I would like it noted that I said it on a Friday afternoon to a man who was not going to be in a room when it mattered and who has been the only person in this city who has ever taken a thing I said and used it exactly.”**

Jonas Mercer said, “It is in the book.”

“It had better be,” said Nina Calder, “and I am not going to ask to see it, and you are not going to show me, and that is the arrangement and I have had it with you since April.”

---

And in a drawer in a corridor in Gantry Ward, at about ten past two on the afternoon of Friday the thirtieth of May, in a folder of its own, eleven pages sat on top of a piece of ground that no civic claim attaches to, and in a different building, in Nina Calder's typing, on a file that already had his name twice and Jonas Mercer's twice, six items sat on four hundred and fifty metres of spur.

One said: *a node with no term in force is not disconnected, it is deferred, and it serves its last verified essential use, and if none is recorded, the use is the node's maintenance.*

The other said: *the structure is not maintainable and the burden should be released and it cannot be.*

Both of them were true. Both of them were about the same bank. One of them was from the eighteenth of April and one of them was from the twenty-ninth of May, and they were in two drawers, in two buildings, on two files, and **there is no rule anywhere in this city that anybody has to read both, and neither file knows the other exists, and no person is required to be in the way.**
