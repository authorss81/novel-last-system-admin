# Chapter 0074 — The First Transfer That Has To Be Repeated

The gauge on the lowland spur drops at about eleven minutes past eight on the morning of Saturday the twenty-fourth of May, and it drops four-tenths in twenty minutes, and Jonas Mercer is standing in a chamber four hundred metres short of the dip at about ten to nine with a torch in his left hand and a book in his coat, because he has been getting a call from Emeka Rusk at about that time every morning since the twelfth of May and because on Saturday the twelfth of May somebody with a power of attorney and not a name paid one thousand eight hundred and eighty pounds to put this spur on a schedule at Tier 1.

Tier 1 means: when the supply to the spur is restricted, the restriction is lifted inside a stated time, and the party that paid has somebody to telephone about it.

It also means the gauge dropping at eleven minutes past eight on a Saturday morning is now a contractual event with a restoration route and a form.

Above the gauge, on the wall of the chamber, on a plate that has been there since 2011, the Lattice printed a new line some time on Friday, in the flat register, with a full stop after each word, and nobody put it there:

> **WHERE A DUTY IS HELD BY A PERSON AND THE PERSON IS NOT AT THE PLACE, THE DUTY DOES NOT MOVE. IT WAITS. AND A DUTY THAT WAITS IS STILL A DUTY AND STILL UNPAID.**

Jonas Mercer read it standing up in a chamber at thirty-one degrees with the tide coming up the lowland behind him, and then he read it a second time, and then he took the pad out of his coat and wrote the date on a page and did not write anything else on the page, because there was nothing else on the page that was true yet.

The duty is Royden Achebe's. Since about eight in the evening on Sunday the eighteenth of May, in a garden in Tideglass, with a lemon tree in a pot and a witness reading it in his own words, it has been Royden Achebe's in his own name on the interim lowland maintenance post, and the claim is unpaid, and the party that pays for the spur does not answer for it, and there is a word NIL in the second witness line that has not stopped being true since it was typed.

And Royden Achebe was, at ten to nine on the morning of Saturday the twenty-fourth of May, forty minutes away in a room in Gantry Ward with a report open in front of him that is due on Thursday the twenty-ninth, and which is going to say that a chamber and a bank are not maintainable, that four tonnes of stone and a sill beam are owed, and that the burden should be released.

Jonas Mercer telephoned him at nine minutes past nine.

“I am not coming,” said Royden Achebe, before the second ring of the second sentence.

“You have not got the choice. It is in your name.”

“It is in my name and I am in a room with a report in it that I have to write on Thursday, and I have got about nine hundred words of it and four of them are *the structure is not maintainable* and the four hundred after that are the reason why, and if I stop writing it today I will not write it at all, because I write it in the mornings, and I have been writing it every morning since the second of April in a garden, in a shed, and now in a room, and it is the only piece of work I have ever done that anybody will read in September.” He put a hand over the telephone for about four seconds. “And I am not coming, and I want to say the next part properly, because I have thought about it since Wednesday.

“I cannot send it. The offer I made on the eighteenth is recorded and it does not transfer anything and I have until the twenty-fifth and it is Saturday the twenty-fourth and I have not used a minute of it and I am not going to. And the only door out of this is a successor who consents, and the successors for a lowland structure are the duty engineer for the district, who said no in forty seconds in the dark on the thirtieth of April and was right, and a pump operator at the docks who has been doing the job unpaid for twenty-six days and who I met once in a garden.

“**So the duty waits. And I am telling you that it waits, and I am not asking you to do anything about it, and I would be grateful if you did not.**”

Jonas Mercer said, “Understood,” and put the telephone in his coat, and then stood in the chamber for a while and did the thing he had come down to do, which was to look, because looking is his trade and looking is free.

The screens were silted. They had been silted since the twenty-ninth of April. The water in the chamber was at the level it comes to on a big spring tide and the tide was due on the twenty-eighth and there is no such thing as a station four miles away that will tell him what the water is going to do on the twenty-eighth at this end of it, and he stood and looked at about ten minutes' worth of it, and then he did the other thing, which was to get his pad out and read the claim back.

> **CLAIM: the holder inspects the outfall and screens after any restriction on the supply, and stands the restriction down, and enters the fault. THE CLAIM IS UNPAID AND THE PARTY THAT PAYS FOR THE SPUR DOES NOT ANSWER FOR THIS.**

He read it twice and he did not enter the fault, because entering a fault is an act by a person with standing, and the person with standing is forty minutes away in Gantry Ward with a report, and there is a rule about exactly this and he wrote it in the shop book on the eighteenth of May in the plainest words he had.

At about twenty past nine Emeka Rusk came down the slipway on foot.

He is fifty-one. He has been a pump operator at the docks for twenty-six years and he has been coming down this slipway in the mornings since the second of April because there was a gauge in a chamber and the gauge drops. He came down in a pair of old boots and a shirt with the sleeves rolled and he had his own torch and his own book and he had brought a bottle of water and he gave about a third of it to Jonas Mercer without either of them making a thing of it.

“They have put that line on the wall,” said Emeka Rusk.

“Yes.”

“Then it is about me,” said Emeka Rusk. “Read it to me again and I will tell you what I think it means, and I have had a think about it on Saturday mornings for a month and I have got to say that I do not think I like it.”

“It says a duty that is held and not performed does not move, and it waits, and it is still a duty and it is still unpaid.”

“Then it is about the last four words and not the first ones,” said Emeka Rusk. “Because I have been doing this duty since the second of April and I have never held it, and nobody has ever said I had it, and I have never put a name in an operator box for it — I have put a fault in and left the operator box blank, and I have done that twenty-six times, and the reason I did it is that I am not holding it, and a fault with an operator box with a name in it belongs to somebody and a fault with an operator box with nothing in it belongs to a node.

“They have come down here in April and put me on a piece of paper as a witness. Witness, not holder. And I read the words off Nina Calder and I said the difference out loud in a garden and you corrected me and you were right. And now they have printed something on a wall about a duty that waits and does not move, and I have been doing the duty for twenty-six days, and the man who holds it is not here, and I would like somebody to say out loud which of those two facts is the true one about this chamber, because I have two of them and I only get to be one person.”

Jonas Mercer said, “Neither of them is the true one. Both of them are entries and there is nowhere on either of them to put the other one.”

“That is what I thought and I wanted to hear it said.”

---

They did the twenty minutes. Jonas Mercer held the torch and did not enter the fault and did not stand where a person stands, and Emeka Rusk went down the ladder, looked at the screens for about four minutes, came up, and entered a fault in his own hand with the operator box left blank, and said the same thing he has said twenty-six times, which is that the screens want clearing and the chamber wants a jet and neither of those is a fault anybody has money for.

And then at about ten o'clock Jonas Mercer did the thing he had come down to do, which was to ask a man to hold a duty he was already doing.

“Rusk. The claim is yours if you say so. I can move it this morning. Achebe offered it back on the eighteenth and it has not transferred and it will not transfer by itself, and I am the man who moved it once and I can move it again, and it takes about eleven hours and a registrar’s ruling and a reading in daylight in the place where you keep what you are holding, and I would do all of it today and I have brought the form in my coat.”

Emeka Rusk took the bottle of water back and put the cap on it.

“No,” he said, and then he was quiet for a while, and then he gave the reasons in the order he had them, which Jonas Mercer wrote down afterwards in the shop book in his own words and not the man's, because Emeka Rusk is fifty-one and has never once in twenty-six years asked for a thing to be written down.

“First one. If I hold it, it is mine, and mine means I answer for a node on Tier 1 that a company paid a thousand eight hundred and eighty pounds for, and I cannot ring that company, and if the screens want clearing at eight o'clock on a Tuesday in November then it is my fault, and I have got four hundred pounds and a shed.

“Second one. You did the last one in a garden in front of witnesses and it worked and it cost Royden Achebe more than it cost you, and you told me that in a corridor once and I have thought about it every day since, and I am not going to have him handed a thing that has already been handed about once. He took it because nobody would. If I take it off him then what happened to him was that two men in one week decided he was the wrong man for it and neither of them asked him.

“Third one, and it is the real one. **You cannot half-share a duty.** That is what the line on that wall is about and it has taken me a month to see it. If I hold it, he is out. If he holds it, I am not holding it, I am helping, and helping is what I have been doing, and helping does not go on a form and helping is why nobody counts it.

“And I will tell you what I want instead, and then I am going to go up the slipway, and you can write it down or you can not.

“I do not want to hold it. I want the thing that is on the wall to be true, and it is not true, and it has been not true for twenty-six days, and it is not going to be fixed by one of us saying the words *I consent*. It is going to be fixed by somebody who can hold a duty for a thing that nobody is standing in, and there is no such somebody in this city today, and there was not one on the second of April, and I did not know that on the second of April and I have had a month to work it out on my own with nobody asking me questions, and it is the only thing I have got out of the last month and I would like it in your book, and I do not want it on a form.”

---

The registrar's ruling came at twenty past two on the Saturday afternoon from Oren Pell, who was telephoned by Nina Calder, who had been telephoned by Jonas Mercer, who had gone up the slipway and telephoned from the top of it because there is no signal at the bottom and there is signal at the top and he had learned that in April from a man he was not going to name.

It took about eleven seconds and Nina Calder wrote it down as she heard it, which she always does, and then she read it back to him, and then she typed it, and then she read the typed version out loud on the telephone and made him agree with it, which she learned from a shop steward in April.

“Two things,” said Nina Calder. “One, in a registrar's word, and I am writing it in his word and not mine: **the record has no field for a duty that is held by one person and performed by another, and where a record has no field, the entry is made twice, and nobody is blamed.**

“Two, and this one is mine, and I have thought about it and I am going to say it to you before I type it because you will not be able to unhear it. I have made two entries on a file this month and the first one had *moved by: Mercer, J.* on it and it was the first record in nineteen years with that line on it. **The second one is going to have it on it too, and it is going to have it on it for a completely different reason, and in about four months somebody is going to read the two of them side by side and work out that the man who moved a duty onto a man has been the reason a second man is doing a duty he does not hold, and that is not true, and I am not going to be able to prove it is not true, because the two entries will be on the same file and the file will not know the difference.**

“So I am going to write it in. Both. In his words. And you are going to read it back to me now before I type it, because that is the rule in this office and I invented it in April and it is the only thing I have ever invented.”

> **ENTRY OF 24 MAY 2014, series 6 lowland chain.**
> **6-L/16. DUTY HELD BY: ACHEBE, R., interim lowland maintenance post, in his own name, since 18 May 2014. HOLDER NOT AT THE PLACE ON 24 MAY 2014. DUTY NOT MOVED.**
> **DUTY PERFORMED AT 6-L/16 ON 24 MAY 2014 BY: RUSK, E., pump operator, docks, twenty-six years, in his own hand, operator box left blank, as it has been left since 2 April 2014. NOT A HOLDER. NOT A WITNESS TO THIS ENTRY.**
> **THE RECORD HAS NO FIELD FOR THIS. THE ENTRY IS MADE TWICE AND NOBODY IS BLAMED.**
> **Moved by: MERCER, J.** — civic standing, register of attachment, released by no person. *(asked to move it on the day; did not; the line is on the second entry as well and I have asked for it to stay)*
> **Second witness: NIL.**

He read it back. She typed it. She read the typed version out. He agreed with it.

And then at about eleven o'clock that night, in a room in Gantry Ward with a report open on a table, Royden Achebe read the two entries on the same file and said the thing that Jonas Mercer wrote on a page at about half past eleven at night, in his own words and not the man's, and did not put in the shop book and has not shown anybody:

> *That somebody will ring Jonas Mercer in August about a Tuesday. That the man who will ring him is Royden Achebe. That Mercer has until then to work out whether he is going to be the one who answers, and that Achebe has not got the right to be angry with him whatever he decides, because he asked him for eleven hours of his life and he gave it, and gave it correctly, and Achebe is the one who is going to ring.*
> *That he has known it since the eighteenth of May. That he has had five days to work out what to do about it and there is nothing, and there is not going to be anything, and the only thing left in this account that is actually his to do about it is to be the one who rings, and he is not going to pretend to anybody that he is calling about a screen.*

Above the first line, in the margin, he had written *corridor*, because it is the word his hand reaches for when he is writing about Achebe and it is nearly always wrong, and he struck it out, because it was a garden, and because he was not going to get that wrong either.
