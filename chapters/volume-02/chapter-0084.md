# Chapter 0084 — The Node With a Person In It

**Friday the twenty-seventh of June 2014, twenty-seven minutes past two in the afternoon, outside Wren Court, Salter's Yard.**

It is a building over a furniture workshop, four storeys, brick, built about 1908 and added to in the sixties, with a shopfront that has said SALTER'S on the glass since before anybody living in it was born and a stairwell on the north side that is open to the air because the door has been broken since March and nobody has had it fixed and the landlord is not the landlord.

There is no civic address. That is not a hardship anybody in Salter's Yard has ever suffered from and it is not a thing they would thank anybody for. It means that no instrument in this city can put a house number on it, and therefore no return, no register, no schedule, no complaint, no council directory entry, no postal redirection on a change of name and no letter from a hospital can be addressed to it, and it has meant that for nineteen years the block has been invisible to every process in Nacre that begins with a form.

The temperature was twenty-six degrees. He came on foot and he came at twenty-seven minutes past two because he had decided at about eleven that he was not going to arrive at a time that looked like an appointment, and then had decided that twenty-seven minutes past two was exactly an appointment, and had come anyway.

She came out into the yard at about ten past three, having been told by somebody that there was a man at the bottom of the steps, and she came out because she is the steward and that is what happens.

She is fifty-four. Nineteen years in the block, fourteen of them as named steward. Black, silver-haired at the front and not at the back, a cardigan over a work apron, reading glasses on a chain, and on the back of her left hand in biro, not faded, four days old, a claim reference, and she has washed it once and it has not come off.

Jonas Mercer said, "Mrs Fofana."

"Mr Mercer," said Yolanda Fofana. "You have a shop."

"I have a shop."

"Then that is what you are, and I would rather have it said first than last, and I have got about nine minutes before I have to go back in and wash my hands." She did not ask him in. "So. You have come up a step and it is the fourth time anybody has come up that step in a year who was not selling something or carrying something, and three of the four were wrong for us and one of them was right, and the right one was a woman from the council who came about the gas in January and wrote down what I told her and took it away and never came back with it, and she was right for us, and I have never worked out whether that is because she was right or because we were."

"Now then. **How long have you known.**"

"Since Thursday the nineteenth of June," said Jonas Mercer, "at about ten to twelve this morning, in a room with about fifty people in it. The clerk found it at ten past one by putting two pieces of paper next to each other."

"That is eight days," said Yolanda Fofana. "The form was printed on the fifth of May and it was a public document at a counter on the seventh, and it took me until Monday the twenty-third of June to pick one up and find my own building on it, which is seven weeks, and you have had eight days of knowing, and you have not telephoned me and you have not sent anybody, and you have come up a step on a Friday afternoon. Mr Mercer — I want you to hear this properly, because I am going to have to lean on it later and I would rather it were in your ears now than in a file.

"**That is the first correct thing anybody from that building has done in nineteen years.** Every one of you comes, and every one of you asks me a question, and not one of you has ever come and not asked me a question. So whatever it is you have come up here to say, say it, and if it is a question then I will answer it, and it has been four days since Monday and four days is not a fortnight, and I am not going to pretend to you that it is, and I would rather have the four days."

---

He had thought about it for eight days and it came out in nine words.

"Do you want to withdraw the claim at that node."

And Yolanda Fofana did not answer it.

She looked at him for about four seconds and then she said: "No," and Jonas Mercer, who had come up the step braced for anything, was not ready for that, and she saw it and she was not unkind about it.

"No," she said. "I am not going to answer that on a step at a quarter past three on a Friday with six people behind that window who can hear the whole of it, and I am not going to answer it to a man I met once, in a workshop, in April, for about eleven minutes.

"And I am not going to answer it because I have not read the three assignments, and I am not going to say yes or no about a thing I have not read, and I have been in this block nineteen years and I have signed things on behalf of thirty-one people for fourteen of them and I have never once signed a thing I had not read, and I am not starting in June.

"So here is what is going to happen. **I am going to read them.** They are in a public register and the counter will give them to anybody who asks and I asked on Tuesday and there is a woman at the second floor who copied them out for me onto four sheets of her own paper, and she said, and I am telling you because I want you to know what kind of counter it is, that she did not trust herself not to improve them, and I have never had a stranger be careful with a document on my behalf before and I did not know what to do with it so I have written her name down.

"And then I am going to do the arithmetic, which I have already started, and then I am going to call a meeting, and then I am going to say something. And that will not be on a Friday afternoon. **It is going to be in this building, in front of the people it happens to, on a night after the weekend, and I am going to choose the night on Monday and I am going to tell you the night on Monday, and you are going to come to it, and I am going to be able to check that you have come, and that is not distrust, that is nineteen years.**"

"I have to be able to ask you one question a day, in a room anybody can hear," said Jonas Mercer. "I have not got a pass for this and I am not asking for one. And I have just used it, and you have not answered it, and I am not going to ask a second one on this step, and if you want to tell me what you will do you will tell me, and if you do not want to tell me then I will come back on Monday at twenty-seven minutes past two and ask you that."

"Then say the second one on Monday," said Yolanda Fofana.

"That is the right question," said Yolanda Fofana, about four seconds later, to the yard and not to him. "And you know how long it has been since anybody asked me that one? Not *what do you think about it*. Not *are you all right*. Not *can we send you a leaflet*. **What will you do.** In nineteen years. Not from the council, not from a trust, not from a company. A man on a step, on a Friday, who does not push."

"Right," she said. "I have got a few minutes and then I have to go back in and wash my hands, and I am going to use them on this and not on being polite.

"**I am going to say it twice.** Once to you, now, in my own words, in daylight, and once again in front of that block, out loud, with thirty-one people in the room, because I am not taking a node out of a chain on one person's word on a step, and I have watched four months of this city do exactly that — one person, one corridor, one thing said at ten at night — and it held a mortuary gate open for nothing in April and it cost a woman of eighty-four forty hours in the same month, and both of those are true and I have read both of them because they are in a minute and a form like everything else.

"So: **I am going to instruct it. Not you. Me, in my own name, to a registrar, on a Wednesday night, in front of my own block. And that is the whole of what I have decided on this step and I am not deciding anything else on this step.**"

---

He walked back to the shop, which is forty-one minutes, and it was about five o'clock, and Bea Osunde was cutting a length of oak threshold on the bench and did not look up, and he stood in the doorway for a minute and a half and then went and held one end of it while she rolled it round, which is a thing you can do and which does not require a conversation.

On the way he worked out, in the way he has worked things out since March, which is by counting, that there were five things he had wanted from that step and had got four of them, and that the fifth one — *will you do it* — had not been given, and that he was not entitled to it, and that a man who wants to be told yes by a person on a step is a man who has come up four hundred steps too many to hear it.

He wrote three lines that night at the back of a delivery note, and the first two of them are the only part of this business in five months that he has not had to argue with anybody about.

> *She has not said yes. She has said she will say it twice, and the second time is in a room with thirty-one people in it, and the room is the thing being protected and the thing being put at risk, and she worked that out on a step in about four seconds without any help.*
> *Three days to Monday. She has set the day and she will check that I came, and she is right to check. I have not set a single date in this city since April and she has set one on a step in four seconds, and she has never once been asked for a date.*

And on the third line he wrote the question he was not going to be able to answer, and then he did not delete it, because he does not delete them.

> *Nobody in this city is allowed to ask her what it will cost, so nobody will ask her, so it falls to me, and I do not know the number, and I am not going to invent one.*

---

**Monday the thirtieth of June 2014, ten in the morning, the furniture workshop under Wren Court.**

Six people, because it is a Monday and eleven households work shifts. There was oak and sandpaper on the trestles where the chairs went, and a plastic wallet on top of it with four sheets of paper in it, each of them handwritten, and a rent book with a rubber band round it and about eleven months of arithmetic in it.

She did not make him sit at the head of anything. She put the wallet on the bench, squared it, and said:

"Right. You have come and you have not brought anything, which I noticed and which I have decided to believe is a coincidence, and I am going to read you four things and then I am going to do some arithmetic and you are going to find it in about nine minutes, and then I am going to ask you one question that I have not got an answer to, and you are not going to have an answer either, and I want that in advance so that neither of us is disappointed.

"**One.** The fourth of March, a company with a standing authorisation placed a claim at Tier 3 for a building in Kestrel Hill. Eleven thousand four hundred pounds a year. Signed with a power of attorney.

"**Two.** The twenty-first of March, the whole of it to a trust that owns the building. Money changed hands and the money was fair.

"**Three.** The second of May, one-half of it to a fund with a trustee who is seventy-one and who paid for it and thought it was good value.

"**Four.** The twenty-seventh of May, that half to a managing agent who runs four hundred and ten flats. Six hundred pounds. I have looked at that one twice because six hundred pounds for half of eleven thousand is a price, and the price is about a tenth of what the half is worth, and I have worked out why, and it is because a share of a claim is a share of a *place in a queue* and a place in a queue that you will never need is worth a tenth of nothing. A man in an office told me that in a room in Kestrel Hill and he said it without being asked, and he was right, and I have thought about it every day since."

"And then there is no five. **That is the last one and it is not an assignment and it is not signed by anybody, and it is the reason I have written *three assignments* at the top of this page and not four, because one of the four things I have just read to you is a placing, and a placing is not a hand.** On the third of June a woman in an office in Kestrel Hill went into a cupboard under my stairs with a torch and read a plate off the wall and entered my name in a box because the plate says the holder of this point pays for it and the point is not the payer's. **And she was right.** The point is not the payer's. The point is a pipe. And I am the holder of it, and I did not know until Monday the twenty-third of June, and she was not asked, and she has not slept about it either, and she is a managing agent of twenty-six years who is now frightened that she has done something wrong to a woman in Salter's Yard."

She turned the wallet round.

"There. Four links and a torch. I could write it out in a minute. What I cannot write out in a minute is what it has cost, and that is the next thing, and I have done it four times and it comes out the same and I am going to do it now, and you may look."

---

She opened the rent book on a page with a bit of tape down the side of it.

"Eleven pounds a week for nineteen years, and thirteen pounds sixty since the first of June, and thirty-one households, and nineteen of us pay it into the tin and the twelve do not and the twelve are not the subject of this and I am not going to let anybody in this workshop turn them into the subject of this.

"**Thirteen pounds sixty, times nineteen, is two hundred and fifty-eight pounds forty a week.** That is the tin. That has been the tin since March, and before March it was two hundred and nine a week, and it has paid the water, the bins, the stair light, and the electricity, and it has never in nineteen years paid for a riser, and it was never supposed to, because the riser is a freeholder's job and the freeholder has never been told about it.

"Now. The node. From the eleventh of March there was a **share** on it. A quarter of a Tier 3, and a quarter of eleven thousand four hundred is two thousand eight hundred and fifty, and there is a registration charge of thirty, so the node carried **two thousand eight hundred and eighty pounds a year** of somebody else's money, and it came in at **seventy-two pounds a month**, and it came in through the same instrument as my money and your money, and I could not tell you to this day which pound of it was which.

"And here is the part I have not been able to say out loud in three days, and I am going to say it now and I would like a glass of water before I do."

He got her one.

"**The seventy-two pounds a month was not being paid by anybody in this building. It was being charged to the water.** The water account for this building is the one that the nineteen of us have paid every week for nineteen years without a gap, and since the eleventh of March the block's share of that Tier 3 claim has been landing on it, and the water account has been about six hundred pounds a quarter short ever since, and I have found that out the way you find out about a leak, by adding up what goes out.

"**Two thousand four hundred and thirty-one pounds and sixteen pence.** Four quarters. And I have paid it. It is my money, it comes out of a workshop that has been losing contracts since March because thirty-one households who used to give me a stair rail and a shelf and a door frame have got a water bill and nothing left over, and **it is not the chain's money, it is the water bill, and the two of them have been in one envelope since the eleventh of March and I have been opening it every month and only counting one side of it.**

"And I have never said that to anybody, not to the block, not to the managing agent, not to the twenty-six, and I have not said it because there is no line in any of this for a woman in a workshop to put *the steward is short* on, and I am not going to invent one at seventy-one."

She closed the book.

"So that is the money, and there are two of them and I am not going to mix them again now that I have said them out loud.

"**The two thousand four hundred and thirty-one pounds and sixteen pence is mine and it is going to stop on Friday because the seventy-two pounds a month will stop coming, and that is a saving and it is not a win and it is a hole in a water bill that I found in May.**

"And the **two thousand eight hundred and eighty pounds a year does not come back to this building at all.** It is gone, on Friday morning, to a trust in Kestrel Hill that has not noticed, because it is not mine and it is not yours and it never was. And it was paid for a riser. A riser that has not been looked at since 2011, and that is about nine hundred pounds, and I have carried that number since 2011 the way you carry a number you cannot reach. **So on Friday morning eight hundred pounds of money that was paying for the water stops paying for the water, and the water is going back to what it did in April, and that is the whole of the arithmetic and I have been sitting on it since Friday and I have got it right and I do not like it.**"

---

Jonas Mercer had the answer to the fifth thing he had wanted, and it was not the answer he had wanted, and it was the correct one, and it took him about eleven seconds to stop trying to help.

"Do you want to withdraw the claim," said Yolanda Fofana.

"Yes," she said.

"Then it is not yours to withdraw," said Jonas Mercer. "It is yours to instruct, and it will be entered by a registrar, and I want to say the next four sentences carefully because I have been thinking about them since Monday and I have got them wrong twice.

"**A valid assignment transfers a claim. A claim is a duty. The duty at the bottom of that chain is yours, because your name is in the field and because you live at the point. And a duty that is yours is a duty you can instruct somebody else to do, and the somebody else is not me.**

"I am not going to do it. And I am not saying that to be careful. I am saying it because the man who found a chain is not the man who ends it, and if he ends it, then in a year somebody will say *he ended it because he found it*, and then the next man who finds a chain in this city will not dare, and that is the whole of the market we are trying to take the price out of. **I would rather be the man who found it and could not touch it than the man who found it and did.**"

Yolanda Fofana put the wallet down and said, "Right," and then said, "No, that is not right, hang on—"

"No," he said. "There are three things, and you have got to say all of them, and the second one is going to cost you more than the first and I would rather you knew that today than on the night."

"Go on."

"**One. That is what I have just said, and I would like the refusal in your file in your words, not mine.**

"**Two. You have said you will say it twice, and the second time is in front of the block, and that is not a courtesy and it is not a good idea. It is required. A registrar cannot act on a standing given on a step or in a workshop with six people in it. It has to be given aloud, in a room, after the chain is known, by the person the chain lands on, and it has to be given twice, and the second time is the one that counts, and the second time is the price. That is not me being careful. That is the shape of it and I have been in five weeks of it and I have watched four people try to do it once and it does not hold.**

"And three, and this one is for me and not for you, and it is the reason I came up a step on Friday and the reason I did not telephone you.

"**You are going to ask me what it costs.** And nobody in this city is allowed to ask you what it costs — I have spent nineteen years finding that out from the other side of it — and so it falls to me, because you are allowed to ask me and I am not allowed to refuse. And I have to give you a number, in writing, before the night. And I do not have the number. I have got a shape and the shape is not a number. **And I am not going to invent one for you, and I would rather tell you that on the thirtieth of June than tell you something on the Wednesday that turns out to be a person.**"

---

They were still standing there at half past four in the afternoon, in a yard, with the light going, when she said:

"Then come up."

And she took him up the open stair, because he had said the workshop was not enough and it was not the night, and they went up four flights in a building with a broken door on the north side, and there were eleven flats on that stair and he counted them because he counts.

In the stairwell, between the first and the second landing, with the window open on the yard, Yolanda Fofana stopped and turned round and said, in nine words:

"**Who pays, when a resident refuses the price.**"

And it was not a question she had prepared and it was not a question about a tier, and she said it the way you say a thing you have been carrying up a staircase for a fortnight.

And Jonas Mercer opened his mouth, and there was nothing in it, and he stood in a stairwell in Salter's Yard for about nine seconds and had nothing at all, because the answer is that somebody does, and the answer is that it cannot be a body, and the answer is that a clause nine is standing there with a photograph of a meter in it, and the answer is that nobody in this city can put a person in a field and be challenged for it, and every one of those four answers is true and none of them is one.

"I do not know," he said.

"No," said Yolanda Fofana. "You do not, and you did not try to, and I watched you not try to and I have not seen a man do that before and it took you about four seconds to not do it, which is longer than most people take to make something up."

She went up another three steps and turned round again.

"I have got an answer," she said. "I worked it out on Monday night and I want you to hear it and I am not asking you whether it is right.

"**Somebody who is not them.** That is the answer and it is the only one there is, and it has always been the only one, and it is in the plate in my cupboard, which I have known about for nineteen years and have never once had anybody to tell.

"And in this building the somebody who is not them is me. And I have been it since 1995, and I have been it on the eleventh of April and on the seventeenth of April and I will be it on the seventeenth of April next year. And it is not a system and it is not a scheme and it is not a fund. **It is a woman with a workshop and a tin and a rent book, and it holds up until she is sixty-one, and then it holds up until somebody is sixty-one.**"

"That is not an answer, Mrs Fofana."

"It is *an* answer," she said. "It is not a good one. I am aware that it is not a good one, and I have been aware of that since Monday, and I would like it in a document that it is not a good one, and I am the only one in this country who is going to write it down, so it will have to do.

"And here is the part you will not like, and I am going to say it now so that you cannot say, on a night in a room with thirty-one people in it, that nobody ever told you."

"**There is no version of this where thirty-one people hold a node.** I have looked. A body is nine seats and a district, and a building with no address is not a district, and I am not nine seats and I am not a district and I am not going to be. And a tier may not be challenged by a person. **So if this building ever holds anything again, it is held by one of us, and it is held by me, because I am the one they can find at an address, and the price of that is that the day the chain comes back, I am the one who cannot be asked anything by any of the thirty-one, and that is exactly where I am already, and I have been there for fourteen years.**"

She looked down the stairwell at him.

"You wanted to know what I will do. **That is what I will do. I will keep being the one who cannot be asked, and I will do it for nothing, and it will be the arrangement we have already got, and none of the thirty-one will have to be told that they have been put in it.**"

Then she said the last thing, from the landing, and it took about nine seconds and it is the reason she is in this volume and not a footnote in it.

"And Mr Mercer — April. On the eighth of April I told this room there was a mains matter, and a woman in flat twenty-two rang me that afternoon about a term that had gone off on the first, and I did not ring her back for three weeks, and when I did she had stopped asking. **That is not the same as being forgiven, and I have not been forgiven, and I have not rung her since, and she has not rung me, and I am telling you now because you are going to find out about it from somebody who is not me, and I would rather you heard it in a stairwell than in a room with thirty-one people in it.**"

She went up the rest of the flight and shut a door, and a card on the second-floor landing said NO POST beyond this point in bad weather and nobody had taken it down in eleven years.

---

He walked out at twenty to five and got about two hundred yards before he stopped, on the corner, in the rain, and took the pad out, and wrote five lines, and they are the five lines he has never shown anybody.

> *She has done the arithmetic and she can defend every line of it. She is going to be two thousand four hundred and thirty-one pounds and sixteen pence better off on Friday and eight hundred pounds a year worse off in water, and she has never told anybody either half, and I am not entitled to either and neither is anybody else.*
> *She has worked out that thirty-one people cannot hold a node, and that the arrangement is one woman who cannot be asked, and she has decided to keep it, and she has told me the reason in a stairwell and the reason is that it spares the other thirty.*
> *And she is the answer to a question this city has been asking for four months — who pays when a resident refuses the price — and the answer is a person, and it is unpaid, and it is findable, and it is unchallengeable, and it lasts about two years.*
> *Which is exactly the shape of the thing I have been in front of since March, and I have not written that down anywhere, and I am not going to, because if I write it down it becomes a finding and it is not a finding, it is a woman on a landing.*
> *Tuesday. Thirty-one people. Second time. I have to be in that room and I am not going to be asked to speak and that is the correct price of the thing I refused on Friday.*
