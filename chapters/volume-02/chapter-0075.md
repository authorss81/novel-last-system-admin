# Chapter 0075 — Three Consecutive Days

The protocol activated at about nine o'clock on Monday the twenty-sixth of May, which was the third consecutive day, and nobody in the borough found out about it from a telephone call, because the notice on the Old Ward board had been up since the nineteenth with an enquiries number on it and a crest on it, and the board is outdoors, and it had been thirty-one degrees on the bridge at four o'clock on the Friday.

By nine o'clock on the Monday there were four people in this city who had read that number and rung it. Two of them were in the Saint Orra and District Flood Forum and one of those was a woman from a nursing home in Kestrel Hill who had a legitimate reason and got a sympathetic person. The fourth was a records supervisor in the Old Ward who had rung it to find out whether the form and the protocol were the same instrument and had been told, courteously, that she should put her question in writing to the address on the notice.

By half past ten on the Monday morning the lowland had four people standing at the end of the lane at a standpipe with a forty-second cycle on a tap that had run freely for most of the previous month, and the reason was not the heat. The reason was that the borough had narrowed that supply for the season, and the narrowing had been performed, and it had been entered, and the entry had been made in the form and in the log, and the two had not been required to agree.

The log in the standpipe cabinet said the restriction was lifted on the second of June at six in the morning.

The line on the form said the restriction was lifted on the fourteenth of May at six in the morning.

Nobody had lied. The form is not required to agree with the log. That is the whole of it, and Ellis Fenn in the basement had printed the condition that says so, and neither of the two men who wrote the two entries in the two books had ever spoken to the other, and one of them works for a contractor and one of them works for a borough and the difference between their entries is a fortnight and it is the whole of what a person on that lane is drinking on a Monday morning at thirty degrees.

---

Saint Orra west's incoming water came up out of a chamber on the north side at about eleven minutes past three on Monday morning and did not come back.

Jonas Mercer was not there. He was told about it at about seven twenty by Ronan Vasquez from the estates office, who had eleven years in that building and who had said in a room in May that a riser is a pipe in a building and took it off a list in nine minutes, and who rang the shop because the shop's telephone was on the board with all the other trades and he did not have anybody else's.

The node is **6-51**, estate water, the tank feed, the chamber, the meter, the two tanks on the roof and everything they are connected to. It has been in the borough's asset register since 1974. Its holder field has held the estates office for eleven years and the estates office is three people and one of them is on leave until June.

It went into failure at about eleven minutes past three on Monday morning and it had no fee and no tier and no restoration route, and what that meant on Monday morning was: no stated time, no party to telephone, and a restoration that happens when the borough's emergency crew gets to the bottom of a list with about forty things on it.

They got there at about twenty past eleven. Eight hours. There is no fault to speak of. The valve at the chamber had a gland nut that had been done up in a hurry in 2011 and a strainer that had a rag in it, and the rag had been in it since somebody dropped it, and the crew took the strainer out, took the rag out, put the strainer back, and put a new gland nut on, and Jonas Mercer has written the word *rag* on four different kinds of plant in twenty-nine years and he does not know how it got there and neither did anybody else and the eight hours were the eight hours.

The second consequence of 6-51 going into failure at eleven minutes past three on the Monday was that between about half past four and about ten past seven on Monday morning the domestic water in a teaching hospital in this city ran at about a fifth of what it is designed for, and the reduction was performed correctly and entered correctly in two books, and the eight hours and the reduction and the restoration were all of them correct, and nobody in that building was harmed by any of it, and the decontamination room on level zero was on a tank and the tank was full and the twenty minutes a week of logged chamber temperature did not have to be anybody's problem that week.

And on the Tuesday morning at about ten o'clock, in a service manager's office at Saint Orra west, with a form on the table and a folder and the tide table for the end of the month still in the folder because he had been looking at it on the Friday for a reason of his own, Gunnar Halvorsen made a decision that was correct.

---

“I am not asking you,” he said, before Jonas Mercer had got the chair out from under the door. “I am telling you, because you are in this building at eight in the morning on a Tuesday reading a plate, and I would rather you heard it from a man with the money than from a corridor in a week. If you want to leave, leave now, in the next two minutes, and I will not think anything of it, and I will not have asked you anything.”

“All right,” said Jonas Mercer, and put the bag down.

“Right. Here it is, and it took me about eleven seconds on Monday night and I have had all night to be frightened of it, and I am going to tell you it straight and then I am going to go and do it.

“**I am spending one thousand eight hundred and eighty pounds of site activity budget to put six dash five one on a continuity schedule at Tier 1, with effect from tomorrow, and it is the only door in the room.**

“I have two thousand one hundred and forty pounds uncommitted in the activity budget for this financial year. I have got about eleven days of it in there. Tier 1 is one thousand eight hundred and eighty. The forms department can activate a schedule on a node in three working days and after that the node has a restoration route and a stated time and a party to telephone, and if that valve sticks again on a Sunday night I ring a number and a crew is inside a time that is written down, and the trust’s own procedure stops being a list with forty things on it.

“And it is the only door in the room, and I am not going to soften that, because there are three other things I could do and I have written all three down. I could get a tanker, and a tanker on a Monday night is about six hundred pounds a load and there is no line for it in this budget and I would be paying it out of a line for something else. I could requisition, and requisitioning a plumbing crew on a node that is not a hospital node is a form that takes four days and goes past a board that meets on Thursdays, and the board is next Thursday, and the next Thursday is the twenty-ninth, and I have got something else on the twenty-ninth that I would rather did not happen. And I could do nothing, and do nothing is what happened on Monday, and Monday was eight hours, and the eight hours were correct.”

“Why are you telling me.”

“Because of the twenty-first of April,” said Gunnar Halvorsen. “I asked you a money question in a doorway and you said no in ninety seconds and you were right, and I have thought about that every day since, including this morning, and I have not told anybody that, and I am telling you because you are going to hear about the money from somebody else inside a week and I would rather it came with the number on it.

“And because you are the only person in this borough who will tell me what a thing is. I asked you in April whether a fee could pay for a service contract and you told me it could not, and it could not, and that has been the reason I have not been able to spend this money for six weeks, and I have been holding it in an account for six weeks with nothing in it that anybody could complain about.”

He put the form down on the table, squared, with the tier filled in and the node filled in and the date filled in and the term filled in, and it had a crest on it and a form number and a signature line.

“Here is the part I have not said out loud to anybody, and you are going to hear it and then you are going to go and do whatever you do with plates. **A continuity fee buys a node. It does not buy an hour of anybody’s time, and it is not an hour of anybody’s time, and if anybody in this building ever tells a member of staff that this fee has paid for a gap in a rota then they are lying and I will deny having heard it.**

“The eleven to half past one gap is still a gap. It is still on the board in the corridor and it is still unfilled and it is still unfilled because it has to be filled by a person and a person has to be paid, and a person is not a node. Priya is still two days a week until Friday. On Friday she is nothing. The twenty minutes a week on the decontamination verification has still got nobody against it, and I wrote it on a board in a corridor and nobody has put their name near it, and that is not a rota either.

“I have just spent all but two hundred and sixty pounds of the activity budget on a piece of plant in a chamber, and I did it in the daylight, at a table, on a Tuesday morning, with a form, and I would do it again this afternoon, and I want you to sit there for about a minute and understand something, because you are the man who has been the only person in this city that anybody will say yes to for five weeks and I am about to be the second, and it has taken me four months and a schedule to get there, and I did not have to be brave, and I want somebody to know that I did not have to be brave.”

---

Jonas Mercer did not say anything for about a minute, and he used the minute, and what he did in it was put his hand flat on the form the way he has put his hand flat on about forty sills and covers and ledges since March, and read the node number, and read the tier, and read the term, and checked the date, and worked out that three working days from Tuesday the twenty-seventh was Monday the second of June, and worked out that the node would be on a schedule and paid for from the first of June, and worked out that on Friday the thirty-first a part-time on-call clinician line that had been paid for out of a dead 2011 coffee-and-cakes appeal came to an end, and put both of those numbers next to each other in his head and said nothing about either of them.

Then he said: “The rag in the strainer has been there since somebody dropped it. Whoever was on that crew on Monday should write *rag* in the fault box and not *debris*, because debris means a design and there was no design.”

“That is the entire contribution you are making.”

“Yes.”

“All right,” said Gunnar Halvorsen, and picked the form up, and then he stopped with it in his hand, and he said the thing that Jonas Mercer carried for the rest of the day and wrote down that night in three lines and never showed anybody:

“And do not do the thing you are about to do. Do not sit in that chair and decide that you have found the pattern in it, because you have not, and because in April I asked a man in a doorway a money question and got the best answer anybody has given me in nineteen years, and it cost me six weeks of holding money in an account for nothing, and if you tell me this afternoon that the fee is the wrong instrument I will believe you and I will do nothing, and Monday was eight hours, and I am not doing Monday again on a Sunday.

“**I am not asking you for anything. I have never once in six weeks asked you for anything and I am not starting at ten o'clock on a Tuesday. Sign the witness line if you are a witness. Otherwise go and read your plate.**”

Jonas Mercer did not sign the witness line, because he was not a witness and had not been asked to be one, and he went and read his plate.

---

At about half past three on the Tuesday he was in the yard at River Stacks, on a slab, with a coping stone that had shifted in February and that he had been putting off for the same reason he had been putting off the cabinet: it was not urgent.

Bea Osunde came out at about four with two teas and did not sit down, and stood about four feet away with her back against the bench leg, and said:

“Halvorsen has bought something with money he did not have.”

“He has bought a node on a schedule at Tier 1 for a thousand eight hundred and eighty pounds out of an activity budget of two thousand one hundred and forty, and the node is the tank feed, and the restoration route is stated and the crew came back inside the time on Monday anyway, which he knows, and it is in his gift to say the fee did it.”

“I have not asked you a question,” said Bea Osunde. “I have made a statement and I would like you to work out who said it and what it is worth, and then I am going to drink this tea and go and cut a length of pipe, and I am not going to say the other thing.”

“Which other thing.”

“The other thing is that I said to you in this yard on the seventh of May that if you kept your hand away from that box you would be the only person in the city who could write in it, and that that would be a vacuum with your face in it.” She drank her tea. “A vacuum with your face in it is a hole. A hole is what everybody is standing round looking at. A man with money in a room and a form on a table is not a hole, and the money is not yours, and the form is not yours, and the building got its water back inside eight hours on Monday and will get it back inside the stated time in future, and there is not one thing in that sentence you could have done better.

“And I have watched you for four months, and you have never once in four months been in a position where doing nothing was the correct thing, and I have not got a single useful thing to say about it, and I am going to cut a length of pipe.”

Jonas Mercer sat on a slab at thirty-one degrees in a yard in River Stacks for about a quarter of an hour and did not say anything.

Then he took the pad out of his coat and did not write the lesson on it, and wrote four other things instead, in the shop book, with the date, the way he does.

> *26 May. Six dash five one on a schedule at Tier 1 from 1 June, £1,880, out of an activity budget of £2,140. Signed by G.H. in daylight at a table with a form on it and a crest on it. Not asked of me. Restoration route stated.*
> *Fault box on Monday read DEBRIS. It was a rag. Whoever wrote debris thinks there is a design. There is no design. There is a man on a ladder in 2011 who put a gland nut on with his sleeve.*
> *The eight to eleven minutes past seven reduction on Monday was correct and entered in two books and the two books agreed, and I want to be careful, because that is the first time in six weeks that two books have agreed and it is the thing I have been writing forms about.*

And then, on the next line, because he had been thinking about the lowland since Monday morning and had not written it and was not going to not write it:

> *The six lowland points have been on a trickle since 30 April. The spur has a fee and a restoration route and the points have nothing, and the fee is the company's, and the answer to who answers for the six is the ninth line of a form and the word is written in my records supervisor's handwriting and dated the twentieth of May. In August somebody will be able to put £11,280 next to a 4,000 word return and a reduction entered in two books, and all three of them will be correct, and the thing that has no line anywhere in this city is a queue of four people at a standpipe on a Monday morning at thirty degrees waiting for a tap with a forty-second cycle on it.*

---

The consequence of a good decision is that the thing it was about stops being the problem, and nothing else changes.

The node was restored at about twenty past eleven on the Monday night and it had never stopped, in fairness, but on the Tuesday morning the tank feed had a stated time on it and a party to telephone and a term to the last day of the fee year, and the plate in the chamber said all of that in a form nobody had to read, and Jonas Mercer read it standing up in a chamber on the Tuesday afternoon with the tide on the lowland behind him.

**Twenty metres from the end of Carrow Lane, Block C had been without a drop of water since the sixth of May and was now on its seventeenth day, and the Tier 2 that its node has been carrying since the eleventh of March was still being paid, at four thousand two hundred pounds a year, out of a rent roll that is four thousand three hundred and sixty-one pounds and sixty pence short.**

**Four miles away and one street lower, six water points had been on a trickle since the thirtieth of April and were in their fifth week, and the one at the top of a slipway on a street with no address was serving its own maintenance, and its maintenance is a pump, and the pump is in a silt trap, and the trap cannot be cleared by anybody, ever, and in thirty-one degrees a trap is the only thing anybody on that street wants, and a man said exactly that to Jonas Mercer on the lowland on the Tuesday afternoon in about eleven words and Jonas Mercer did not offer anybody a pump and did not go up the path and did not telephone anybody.**

Every repair in that week was correct. The chamber was cleaned and a new gland nut was fitted in eight hours on a Monday and a stated time was written on a plate on the Tuesday. The block's water had been on since the sixth and nobody could turn it on. The lowland had a form and a log and a queue. The agreement on four blocks was carrying a fee that bought the continuity of a node with nothing behind it. A register of attachment in the Old Ward carried a man's name and released him by nobody. A committee had recorded that it could not define a word, and a chair had made two determinations, one of which required the whole city to answer a form by a sitting that nobody had set a date for.

And at about four o'clock on Wednesday the twenty-seventh of May, in a corridor, Gunnar Halvorsen told Jonas Mercer a date.

“Thursday,” he said. “The twenty-ninth. In three days I have to put my name against a figure and it is not the node. The node is done and the node is fine. It is the gap.

“I have two hundred and sixty pounds left in the activity budget. A clinician covering eleven to half past one, five days, for the rest of the year, is not two hundred and sixty pounds, and I am not going to pretend it is. On Thursday I have to choose out of three things: I go back to the trust for money I have already had refused twice in writing, or I leave the gap on the board published and unfilled for another year, or I put a number on it and I find out who that number is for.

“And that is a money question and I asked you one in April and you said no in ninety seconds and it was the best answer I have had in nineteen years, and I have been thinking about it every day since, and I am not going to ask you again, and I am telling you the date because on Thursday the twenty-ninth I am also going to have to file Royden Achebe's report on a piece of ground I cannot reach and I am not going to be able to be in two places and I would rather somebody knew where I am not.”

“Which place am I not.”

“I am not going to tell you that, and that is a decision I have made and not an accident, and you will find out in about nine days when the transcript is up, and I would rather you found it out from the transcript than from me in a corridor on a Tuesday in a heat,” said Gunnar Halvorsen, and went to answer a telephone.
