# Chapter 0086 — The Fee Does Not Stop

**Thursday the third of July 2014, nine o'clock in the morning, the registrar's office, second floor of the civic offices.**

Oren Pell read six lines on a sheet of A4, and behind them a page in a hand he has not seen before in his life, which was a note of what a woman had said out loud in a room in front of twenty-nine people at eleven o'clock on the previous night, and the note had the time on it and the number of people in the room on it and nothing else on it, and no names, and he read it twice.

"Right," he said. "That is the second one. **I am entering it now, and I entered nothing yesterday, and the reason I entered nothing yesterday is dated on the record I made yesterday afternoon and the date on it is the second of July, and anybody who wants to look at why a registrar refused a request for one day has a date.**"

The term at that node ran to **06:00 Friday the fourth of July 2014**. The entry says, in the form, in the words of section nine of the 2008 regulations, that a person may withdraw a claim held in their own name at a counter without notice to any other party and without any formality.

He signed it, and then he did a thing he has not done in nineteen years, which is that he copied a person's words into a registrar's record in her own words and did not summarise them, and Frances Tolley, who types for the Committee and who was not in the room and was told about it afterwards, said that what came out of that office on the Thursday morning was the only registrar's record anybody in this city has read with a wet thumb.

---

**Thursday the third of July 2014, half past seven in the evening, the furniture workshop under Wren Court, Salter's Yard.**

They had been in there since eleven o'clock on Wednesday night and they had had two hours' break in the middle of the day and the meeting went on across three evenings, and nobody organised that, and nobody had to: the woman in flat twenty-two brought a flask and Cynthia Akintola went and got bread, and at about eleven o'clock on the Wednesday the meeting had reached the point where the room understood that the thing that had happened was not a victory and had better not be treated as one, and at that point everybody went home and slept and came back.

There were thirty-one in the room on the Thursday and that was all thirty-one, and the two who had been at the hospital with a relative on the Wednesday were there, and one of them was on a drip and had been told the meeting was not a meeting about her water and had come anyway.

**Yolanda Fofana** stood at the end of the table with a sheet of A4 in her hand and four pages of her own handwriting on it that she had done on the Tuesday night, and she said, before she said anything else:

"Right. Last night I said the thing twice and you all heard it and it is entered and it is done, and the claim comes off this node at six o'clock this coming Friday morning, and nobody in this room is going to hear a bang, because there is no bang, and I want to say that now so that nobody sits here for two nights waiting for one.

"And now the other half, and the other half is why I have made this three nights long, and I am going to be honest with you about the order of it, because I did not know the order of it on Monday and I do know it now.

"**The money stops on Friday. The water does not. And those two sentences are going to fight each other in your heads for about a fortnight and I am not going to be able to be in the room for all of it, and I am telling you that now so that when I am not in the room you do not think I have gone off you.**"

---

She did the water first, because she had learned in nineteen years that the water is the thing that makes people listen.

"Right. From six o'clock on Friday, that node is not on a schedule. It is not Tier 3, it is not Tier 2, it is not Tier 1, it is not on anything. And I know what that sounds like — it sounds like it stops.

"**It does not stop. It goes back to March.** Which is to say it goes back to the thing it was doing before anybody paid anything for it, which is the thing it was doing on the fourteenth of April, and on the seventeenth of April, when the water was at half.

"And I have been in this building for nineteen years and I have got the riser looked at once, in 2011, and it is a pipe that comes up through the workshop and nobody has been up it since, and the last time I rang anybody about it I was told it would be about nine hundred pounds and two days with a man on a ladder, and I have carried that number since 2011 the way you carry a number you cannot reach.

"So I am not going to stand here and tell you that withdrawing that claim has cost you nothing, because it has cost you the schedule, and the schedule was paying for the riser, and nobody in this room is the riser.

"**And I want the ones who were here on the seventeenth of April to put their hands up.**"

Eleven hands went up in a workshop in Salter's Yard, and she looked at them and did not count them out loud and did not look at any of the faces, and one of the eleven was a man of forty-four called Malcolm Reid who had put his hand up with the rest and who was one of the twelve who had not signed in March, and whose hand came down about two seconds after everybody else's, and everybody in that room saw it, and nobody said anything about it, and that is worth more than anything anybody said that night.

"The eleven of you were on a term that had gone off. All eleven. Since the first of April. **I have known that since the first of April and I have not told you, and I told this room on the eighth of April that there was a mains matter, and that was a lie, and I said it out loud in front of twenty-six of you in April and nobody stopped me and nobody was going to.**"

Somebody said, "Mrs Fofana—"

"No, let me. I have had it since the Thursday of the third of April. I did not ring one of you back for three weeks. On the eighth I told this room there was a mains matter, and if I had rung a woman back on the ninth and said the true thing then the true thing would have been in this building by the tenth, and there are eleven of you who were in this building on the seventeenth of April, and not one of the eleven would have stayed. **That is not the same as being forgiven. I have not been forgiven and I am not asking for it tonight, and if any of you want to say something about it I would rather you said it now than carried it.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, and then a woman of thirty-one called Debbie Okpara said, "I knew mine had gone off. I knew in April. I have never once minded, because you have never once asked me, and I would have paid it if you had asked me, and that is the whole of my view and I have had it since April and I have not said it because I did not think it was mine to say."

That was the last thing anybody said about it, and it lasted about eleven weeks.

---

**The money, on the Friday morning, was the part that could be checked, and it took four seconds to check, and nobody in that building had to take anybody's word for it.**

At ten minutes past six on Friday the fourth of July a charge of seventy-two pounds came out of the account of a furniture workshop in Salter's Yard, and it was the fourth consecutive month it had come out of that account, and the person who found out was a man of sixty-one called **Gideon Harris** who does the block's books on a Thursday for a pint and a set of keys and who telephoned the workshop at about a quarter to seven to say one sentence, which was: *it has gone out again, and it is the same seventy-two, and it is quarter past six in the morning and I am not going to say anything about it twice.*

Nobody in the block said anything about that telephone call except one woman of forty-one, who said at about half past eight, in a yard, to about six people, "**That is the last one. That is the last seventy-two pounds. From March, every month, and that is the last one.**" And then she went to work.

At twenty to nine Beverley Annis telephoned the shop and Jonas Mercer took the call and she said nine sentences without stopping and one of them was a number.

"The same seventy-two pounds is going to be credited to that account within the hour, and it is a credit and not a refund, and it is the block's one-quarter share of a claim that has been withdrawn, and it will not come again in August or in October or ever, and I have told our client's finance director personally and he has asked me to pass on that he is not going to be seeking a replacement from anybody at that address, and I believe him, and I have written it down that he said it.

"And I have read your lines off the back of your hand from a records office in June, which you wrote on your hand in a records office, and I have to say that I found them on the internet. **Do not write things on your hand.**"

**And that is the fee, and the fee did not stop.**

---

The third condition took two and a half hours and it was the thing they had come for, and the thing they arrived at was not on anybody's list because nobody made a list.

Fofana put it in front of them at about ten past eight on the Thursday night in about ninety words:

"I said three conditions when I took this on fourteen years ago. Residents are told a card exists before anything else. The answer to any request for a list of people is the address of this building. **And the fee does not stop, because the day the fee stops is the day somebody does not get water.**

"That has held for fourteen years. It is the best sentence I have ever said and it has kept this building going and I would say it again. And it has been proved, in a room, over two nights, by me. **The fee did not stop. And on Friday the water goes back to what it was in April, and in April the water was at half, and the sentence did not save it, because the sentence is about money and this was about a pipe.**

"So the third condition has to be dealt with by the people it protects, and not by me, and I have been waiting nineteen years for a night when that sentence was true."

Here is what the thirty-one arrived at, in the order they arrived at it, and none of it was signed and none of it was minuted and there is no minute.

**The fee does not stop.** Thirteen pounds sixty, into the tin, the same tin, the same Wednesday, and nobody is asked to sign anything and nothing is signed, and when somebody said *can we at least have a receipt* she said yes and then said that a receipt is a document and a document is what twelve of them refused in March, and that came out of the room, and it took four minutes, and it is worth more than the money.

**Clause nine is not tidied.** It is read out in full again in the room on the Friday night, by Cynthia Akintola, and it is not repealed and it is not suspended and it is not rewritten, and the four who missed a month are told in front of everybody that it is there, and that it will be there in September, and that the room has not voted to remove it and **cannot** vote to remove it, because a room of thirty-one people in a building with no civic address is not a body and cannot vote on anything, and if anybody wants to know what clause nine is for it is for exactly that.

**The twelve are not asked to sign, and the four are not written down.** The two numbers — nineteen and twelve — are going to be in a public record before the end of the month, and Esio Kotto said, on the Thursday, "Then somebody is going to see nineteen and twelve, and somebody in that building is going to write a list of the twelve, because that is what you do with a number box, I do it at work." And Yolanda Fofana said, "Then they will have twelve, and they will not have the four, and they will not have the eleven, and they will not have us." And Malcolm Reid said, "They will have us," and she said, "They will have us," and that was the end of that, and it was the only time in three nights that anybody agreed with her quickly.

Then the riser, and the money, and the choosing, and that was the hard part and it took the best part of the Friday night and it is the reason anybody in that building calls this a meeting.

The tin turns over about **thirteen thousand four hundred and thirty-six pounds and eighty pence a year** — nineteen households at thirteen pounds sixty a week — and before the first of June it was ten thousand eight hundred and sixty-eight, and for nineteen years it has never been enough for anything that was not already paid for. It pays the water, the bins, the stair light, the electricity, the workshop's insurance and a share of the roof, and it has never once paid for the riser, and the riser is about **nine hundred pounds** and two days with a man on a ladder, and it was last looked at in 2011.

"So it is a straight choice," said Yolanda Fofana, "and I am not going to pretend it is not, and there is no clever answer and I have looked for one for three days. **Either the riser gets nine hundred pounds that we do not have, or it does not get it, and the water stays at what it does when the building is busy, which is half.** And I know what I sound like. I sound like a woman asking thirty people to find nine hundred pounds for a pipe in a wall, and I have been asking for things in this room for fourteen years and I have never once asked for that.

"And I have got to say the other half of the arithmetic out loud or it will look like a win, and it is not. **Two thousand eight hundred and eighty pounds a year has just left this water account, and it is going to a trust in Kestrel Hill that has not noticed, and it is not in the tin and it never was in the tin.** And two thousand four hundred and thirty-one pounds and sixteen pence of my own has stopped coming out of me, and I am going to be two and a half thousand pounds better off by Christmas, and the water in this building is exactly as it was in April, and that is the whole of what has happened here and I have known it since Monday and I have not been able to say it out loud until now."

Nobody said anything for a while.

Then a man of seventy-three called **Amos Boateng** said: "So it is two years."

"It is two years," said Yolanda Fofana. "Or it is nine hundred pounds in a drawer, and everybody in this room who can find nine hundred pounds finds it and everybody who cannot does not, and we do not know which of those it is going to be, and I am not going to stand here and pretend that a room like this one can tell."

"And if somebody could find it," said Malcolm Reid, "we'd have a list. In a fortnight. Of who could find it. And that is a list of who has got money and that is worse than a list of who hasn't."

"So it stays two years," said Yolanda Fofana, "and nobody in this room is going to be asked, and if anybody finds nine hundred pounds in a drawer in the next two years I am going to ask them what it was for and they are going to tell me it was for something, and I will take it and I will not ask, and that is the whole of the arrangement and it is a bad arrangement and I am not going to improve it at midnight because it is mine."

---

And then, at about eleven o'clock on the Friday night, it came out. Nobody had put it on the agenda. Nobody had intended it. It took four sentences and it was not Fofana who said the first one, and the reason it is the last thing in this chapter is that it is the last thing in the room.

Cynthia Akintola said: "Yolanda. Is anybody else in this building going to be able to take that off you."

And Yolanda Fofana said, "No."

"Then who," said Cynthia Akintola.

And Yolanda Fofana said, "Me. And it will not be because I have agreed to it. **It will be because there is nobody else, and there is a plate in the cupboard that says the holder of this point pays for it, and there is one person in this building that anybody can find, and she is fifty-four and she is standing in a workshop in an apron, and on Friday morning the claim comes off and on Friday afternoon somebody is going to have to hold the node, and the only person in this room who can is me, and this room cannot decide that, and I am not going to pretend it can.**"

Cynthia Akintola said, "Can we decide it."

"No," said Yolanda Fofana. "**That is the thing I have been trying to say to you for two nights and I have not said it. Thirty-one people in a building with no address are not a body. We are not a body. We cannot be seated, we cannot be given nine seats, we cannot vote, we cannot require anybody to do anything, and we cannot be challenged by anybody either, because a tier may not be challenged by a person.** So we cannot be a steward board, and we cannot be a committee, and we cannot be a board, and we cannot be a committee of one either, and there is nothing in this city that thirty-one people who are not a body can become in order to be asked a question by anybody.

"And I have thought about that for fourteen years and I have never said it out loud, because you do not say it out loud to a room you have to live in.

"So the arrangement stays. **One person. Findable. Holding a thing she cannot be asked about by any of the thirty-one people whose water it is.** And that is the third condition, and it is not mine any more, and I am not going to say it in a hall four miles away again. I am saying it here, to the thirty-one, once, and the fee does not stop, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I have not been thanked, and I would like to get on with the riser."

---

They got up at about twenty past eleven on Friday the fourth of July 2014.

Nobody had signed anything. Nothing was minuted. The clause nine sat in a folder in a drawer two feet from where she was standing and it was read out in full four times over three nights and it was not touched.

And at about five past twelve, in the doorway, with her coat on, Esio Kotto said:

"There is a date, though."

"What date."

"There is one date anybody in this room has been given since March, and it is not ours, and I have been sitting on it for two nights. **The fourth of August.** It is the return date on the heat form. The one with the crest on it that you can get at a counter on the second floor. The one that asks a residential place to confirm occupancy and a named responsible person for every address they have got. Seventy days after the twenty-sixth of May, which is when it went up on a board in the Old Ward, and nobody in the Old Ward has read it out loud to anybody either."

"And it is a Thursday," said Yolanda Fofana.

"It is a Thursday," said Esio Kotto. "**And nobody in this room knows whose Thursday it is.** It is not our date. We are not a residential care premise. There is not one of us in this room who is on that form. **And it is the only date this building has been given in four months, and it is on a piece of paper in a drawer four miles away that nobody has read out loud, and it is about a boiler.**"

Nobody said anything else. Yolanda Fofana wrote it on the back of her hand, under the biro that had almost come off, and the date was the fourth of August, and it belonged to a form about a gas inspection in 2011 and a housing association four miles away that had answered it in a ring-number box and had not known that it had.

---

**Friday the fourth of July 2014, one o'clock in the afternoon, the shop.**

He did not telephone the workshop. He had been given a number at twenty to nine that morning and a woman with a fold-out trolley had been in the shop on Wednesday with a hand-lettered sign that said SALTER'S because the glass had been broken since March, and she had bought two bags of offcuts and left, and neither of those two things was about a Tier 3 claim, and both of them had been in his head all afternoon, and the shape of a business that has to sell a bag of offcuts is a shape, and a shape is not a record, and he has known that since April.

Bea Osunde came through at about one o'clock with a piece of ply under her arm, looked at the bench, looked at him, and said, "You have not touched anything since eleven."

"No."

"Then go and eat something," said Bea Osunde, "and do not write it down, because whatever it is, it has got somebody else's name on it and not yours, and that is the good version."

He did not write it down. He ate a sandwich in the yard at half past one and got about four sentences further than the sandwich.
