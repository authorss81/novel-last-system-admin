# Chapter 0097 — The Tray

**Monday the fifteenth of September 2014, eleven in the morning, field services, Gantry Ward.**

The office the tray had gone to and come back from was on the ground floor of a municipal building with a car park at the back and a smell of wet coats, and the officer on the desk was the one Jonas Mercer had spoken to on the twelfth of August, and he recognised him and said so, and that made it slightly worse.

"You again."

"Twice in a month," said Jonas Mercer. "I want to ask you about a name."

"You are not entitled to a copy of the acceptance, I told you that in a corridor five weeks ago, and I am not going to say it a second time in a nicer way." He turned the screen about four inches. "**What I can tell you is that the signature is genuine and dated and the tray is closed and there is a file. And what I am not going to do is photocopy a file for a man who is not a party, and I have thought about that for five weeks and I would still not do it.**"

"I am not asking you to photocopy it."

"Then what are you asking me."

"**I am asking you whether you would read me the index.**"

The officer looked at him for about four seconds.

"That is a very stupid way to ask for a thing," he said, "and it is the only way anybody has ever asked me for anything in two years, and I am not going to do it, and I am going to do it, and if anybody asks me I will say you were difficult about it."

The index of the 6-L/14 file is on the inside of the front cover and it is nine lines in a printed box and it does not have a heading.

> **6-L/14 — LOWLAND CHAMBER AND BANK. Unclaimed burden attached 18/04/2014. Holder: interim lowland maintenance post (R. ACHEBE, in his own name).**
> **Field report 11pp, 900 words, filed 29/05/2014, 11:00. No civic claim. No invoicee.**
> **Reclamation Season review, 1/04/2014: 6-L/14 — NON-CIVIC PLANT. ACCEPTED. NODE LEAVES THE REVIEW. NO CIVIC CLAIM ATTACHES TO THE CONTENTS OF THE CHAMBER OR THE BANK.**
> **Report p.9 ¶3: the outfall inspection duty described at 6-L/16, transferred to the holder of this post on 17/05/2014, is not discharged by this report.**
> **Level-three acceptance, 13/08/2014: REPORTED NOT MAINTAINABLE. RELEASE RECOMMENDED. NOT RELEASED.**
> **Second tray, raised 12/08/2014, open. Query 2 outstanding: is there any party who may consent to the works.**

"That is nine lines and it took you four seconds and I have read it about nine hundred times," said the officer. "**And I will tell you the one thing in it that I have never told anybody and that I have thought about for five weeks, which is that nobody has ever read those nine lines in the order they are in. People read the last one and go away.**"

"The last one says not released."

"The last one says not released, and the second-to-last one says a man put his name on it on the thirteenth of August, and he is not the man who wrote the report, and he is not the man who does the work, and I do not know whether he knew what any of the words meant, and neither do you, and there is a form in a tray somewhere in this building with a signature on it and no obligation on any human being in the borough to have read one word of the document he signed."

He turned the screen back.

"There is a second tray," he said. "It went on the twelfth. Somebody has to answer a query and the query is a form with two questions on it, and the second question is *is there any party who may consent to the works*, and the answer is on page four of the report and page four says no, and that has been known since the twenty-ninth of May, and I have had the second tray on my shelf since Friday and it is going to sit there until somebody finds it or I retire."

"Do not retire," said Jonas Mercer.

"Do not retire," said the officer. "**That is the funniest thing anybody has said in this room in two years and I have got to go and open a tray.**"

---

**Monday the fifteenth of September 2014, ten past one in the afternoon, the counter, the records office.**

"Six lines," said Jonas Mercer. "Post and district. Level three, field services, Gantry Ward. And you read the file on Monday morning and you read the fourth page and you told me about the first three pages on Wednesday and you did not tell me about the fourth, and there is a second tray open on a shelf in Gantry Ward with a query on it and nobody is chasing it."

Nina Calder put her machine in neutral and looked at him for a while.

"His name is **Peter Naylor**," she said. "**Forty-four. He came into field services out of estates in 2011 and he has been at level three ever since.**"

She said it in the flat voice and then she did not say anything for about eleven seconds, and then she said the rest of it in a different way, and Jonas Mercer stood at a counter and listened to a records supervisor of about fifty say a sentence she has been carrying since May.

"That is him. **That is the man I told you about in a records room in May and I have never been able to ask him and I am not going to now, and neither are you, and that is not a request, that is me telling you what I am going to do with my Thursday.**

"He has a fortnight somewhere in this city with a bucket under a tap. It is not a joke and I have never said it to a second person and I have now said it to you, and I have said it because you have just handed me six lines that are a fact and I have got nothing else. **He is the reason four tonnes of stone are not going to be bought by anybody, ever, and he does not know that, and he signed a piece of paper on the thirteenth of August that says not released, and he is forty-four, and I have worked with him for nine years and I have never once been able to ask him whether the line he signed is the line he believed in, and the reason I have never been able to ask him is that six words went out of this office in March under a reporting officer number that is not mine, and the words made the thing he was signing about a pipe instead of a service, and I typed the form, and I did not know what they were, and I have never asked him and I am not going to now.**

"So I am not going to ring him. And if you go up there and ask him what he thought he was signing then you will be the fourth person in this city to make that man explain himself to a stranger and he has had three of those and one of them was a woman of sixty-one who rang a counter in April and I have got the date of that one too."

---

**Tuesday the sixteenth of September 2014, ten past seven in the morning, the lowland chamber, 6-L/16, Carrow Lane.**

It was not raining. It had been warm and dry for a week and the ground had a crust on it and the chamber hatch was open and there were two men in it with a socket set and a camera on a pole and a screen on the wall that had been rebuilt once in 2011 and had been promised a rebuild in April.

**Emeka Rusk was the holder of nothing and was doing what he has done twenty-six times since the second of April, which is the work, in his own hand, with the operator box left blank.** He is fifty-one and has been at the docks for twenty-six years and he did not look up when Jonas Mercer came down the ladder, and he said, "You are not holding anything."

"No."

"Good," said Emeka Rusk, and went on.

Royden Achebe was in Gantry Ward with a socket set of his own and a man from Manchester and did not ring, and did not come, and had said on the fourteenth of August that he would not, and had not, and had told Jonas Mercer so on a telephone in a shop in a sentence that was not eleven words and has not been counted since.

Jonas Mercer stood on the lip for three hours and eleven minutes and did not go down the ladder and did not touch the plate. He wrote down the time he arrived in his own hand, and the time he left, and the number of people on site, and what was done, and he wrote his own name at the bottom of it, in the line that says what the man who moved it is, and there was no line on the form that said what the man who watched it is, so he wrote his name in a margin and initialled the margin and gave a copy to Emeka Rusk and kept one.

**Nobody was blamed. The entry was made twice, because the record has no field for a duty held by one person and performed by another, and that is a registrar's ruling of the twenty-fourth of May and it is still the only ruling this year that anybody has ever used twice.**

And at about twenty past ten, a man in a CivicCore apron came over the lip with a clipboard and a folded sheet and stood next to Jonas Mercer for about four minutes without saying anything, and then he said:

"Wendell Pryce. Continuity field manager. **I have got four tonnes of stone in a compound in Gantry Ward that has been sitting since the twenty-ninth of May and there is a mason in it who is on a day rate, and I am not going to insult you by pretending I do not know what that is worth.**"

"Morning."

"Right." Pryce did not look at the chamber. "The screen is being rebuilt on a node we hold and I have a crew on site and a stone merchant's yard behind me and about eleven minutes before my crew goes to the next one. **That bank is about sixty feet from where those two men are standing. You have got four tonnes of stone and a sill beam on a file in this city that somebody has recommended and not released, and I am not asking you to release it, because you cannot, and I am not going to insult you by asking.**

"I am asking whether you have got a piece of paper that says anybody may put stone on that bank tonight."

"No."

"**You have not got a piece of paper, or you have got a piece of paper and it says not released.** Which is it."

"It says not released."

"Then that is the same answer and it took four minutes longer to get to it." Pryce wrote something on the clipboard and did not offer it. "**And I am going to say the thing that the mason is going to say to me on Friday and then I am going to go. If we put four tonnes of stone on that bank tonight, then the bank is better, and it is better tomorrow, and it will be better at the next surge, and there is nobody in this city who can say to me afterwards that I put it there. If we do not, then in about four years the sill goes, and the chamber fills, and there is a flap gate at the bottom of a dip that is not maintainable, and the households in four hundred yards carry water because of it, and the file says not released, and the file has said not released since the twenty-ninth of May and nobody has been hurt yet.**

"**Both of those are true. I am not asking you which one is correct. I am asking you to say it into your margin there, because you have got a margin and I have watched you use it.**"

Jonas Mercer wrote three lines into the margin, and read them before he initialled them, and he wrote this:

> *There is a man in this city, forty-four, who came out of estates in 2011, and he was told a line was plant and he got a job out of it, and he has a fortnight somewhere with a bucket under a tap, and there is nobody in this borough who has ever been able to ask him one question about any of it, including the records supervisor who typed the six words out under my number in eleven minutes in March without knowing what they were, and including me, because I wrote them.*
> *That is the answer to your question and it is not a fix and I have written it down because you asked me to write it down and because a mason on a day rate is going to ask it again on Friday.*
> *I cannot tell you which of your two things is correct and I have been asked that four times this year by people who were right, and every one of them was told no by a man in eleven seconds, and this is the first time in five months that the only honest answer has been a sentence about a man with a key and a bucket.*

Pryce read it, which he had not been asked to do, and put the clipboard under his arm.

"That is a worse answer than the one I came for," he said, "and I am going to be honest with you, Mr Mercer, because we have had one conversation in this city before and it was not one, and because we are two men doing a job at seven in the morning on a Tuesday and there is nobody here to be careful in front of. **I have been in this borough eleven years and the thing that has kept it running is that somebody eventually puts the stone on. It is never the same somebody and it is never on a form and it is never a schedule, and if I stop doing it then somebody is going to stop doing it, and about nine years later a woman of sixty is going to be standing at a gate at the end of an embankment with a pencil.**

"**That is my whole argument and it is not in your margin and it cannot be, because your margin is about a man in 2011 and mine is about nine years.** I am not going to put stone on that bank tonight, because you have a piece of paper that says not released and that is a real thing and I am not going to be the man who was told no in a margin and did it anyway. And I want you to understand that I have never not done it before and I have no idea what I am going to do instead. **So you have not solved anything. You have got a piece of paper and a margin and a man with a key and a bucket, and in about four years one of us is going to have to say the sentence out loud, and it will not be a registrar, and there is no schedule for it and there is no form and there is no party to serve it on.**"

He went up the ladder and across to his crew and did not say anything else, and Jonas Mercer stood on the lip until about ten past ten and then went and got a hatch cover and put it back, which is a thing anybody can do.

---

**Wednesday the seventeenth of September 2014, twenty past two in the afternoon, the corridor outside field services, Gantry Ward.**

He came back for the second tray, and it was not chased, and it was on the shelf where it had been since Friday, and the officer had put a rubber band round it and written the date on the band in biro, and Jonas Mercer saw the date and understood that it had not moved in two days because nobody had touched it and nobody was going to.

"Still open."

"Still open." The officer turned the screen. "**And I am going to say the thing I have been not-saying since Monday, and then I am going to tell you that you are not getting a copy of any of it.**

"The line on that file is the only line in this building that has been true since the twenty-ninth of May and nobody has been hurt by it. **That is not a good outcome. That is a file that has been sitting still.** And the reason it has been sitting still is a man of forty-four who opened a tray on the thirteenth of August and did in ninety seconds what I have been unable to do in five weeks, which was to put his name in a box so that the thing would be his and not mine, and the reason he did it is not bravery, it is that the box is there and he is the officer of the week.

"So the works are not going to be done and they are not going to be un-done and in four years somebody is going to find that line and read *not released* and think it means a decision was taken. **And the decision was taken by a man in about ninety seconds and the reason he had ninety seconds is that four hundred and sixty pages and eleven pages and a nine-line index and a second tray with two questions on it all sat in three buildings and two files and one drawer, and there is no rule anywhere that anybody has to read both.**"

"And the second question on the query."

"**Is there any party who may consent to the works.** And the answer is on page four of the report and it is no, and it has been no since the twenty-ninth of May, and it was no on the thirteenth of August when he signed, and it is going to be no in four years." He put his pen down. "Write *no* on it and put it back and it will be answered in about four months by somebody who is not going to know that anybody asked."

Jonas Mercer did not write it on.

"You are not entitled to answer that query," said the officer, "and you have been in this building three times in five weeks and you have not once asked me for anything and the second time you asked me to read you an index, which is a technique, and I have decided to permit it, and if you come back a fourth time I am going to tell you to go away."

He will come back a fourth time in December and he will be told to go away, and he has decided in advance that he will not argue about it.

---

He walked back with the two doors in his head and the margin in his coat, and the field services car park was empty by then, and the only thing left open anywhere in Gantry Ward at that hour was a tray on a shelf with a rubber band round it. He went out onto the forecourt and sat on the wall for a while, and then on the corner outside a hardware shop he wrote four lines on the back of a delivery note, and the last of them is the one that is going to sit in a drawer on a lower floor for another year.

> *The acceptance was signed on the thirteenth of August by a man of forty-four called Peter Naylor who came out of estates in 2011 and has a fortnight somewhere in this city with a bucket under a tap, and Nina Calder has carried that sentence since May and gave it to me on Monday afternoon and told me not to use it, and I have written it in a margin in front of a contractor and initialed the margin and given him a copy, and a man on a day rate is going to ask the same question on Friday and I have not got a better answer.*
> *A second tray went out on the twelfth of August and came back on the shelf on the seventeenth, with two questions on it, and the second question is whether there is any party who may consent to four tonnes of stone and a sill beam. The answer is no and it has been no since the twenty-ninth of May, and there is a rubber band round the tray with a date on it, and nobody has moved it, and nobody is going to be hurt by it, and in four years somebody is going to read not released and think that a decision was taken.*
> *Wendell Pryce did nine years in one sentence on a lowland lip at twenty past ten on Tuesday morning and I have never met him before and he is not a villain and he is not softened and he is right, and the sentence was about a woman of sixty at a gate with a pencil, and I have known that woman for four months and I have never seen her face, and he has not either, and both of those are in the same paragraph and neither of them is evidence of anything.*
> *On the front of that file there are nine lines and the third one says 6-L/14 — NON-CIVIC PLANT, ACCEPTED, NODE LEAVES THE REVIEW, dated the first of April, and there is no name on it anywhere in the borough, and the fourth line names a duty at 6-L/16 transferred to the holder of this post on the seventeenth of May, and the man who holds that post is the man who lodged the classification in March, under a reporting officer number that is mine. Three facts, one drawer, one building. **The duty is held by the man who put the words in and the words were mine, and the file says who holds the duty and it does not say whose words they were, and there is no rule anywhere that anybody has to read both.**
> *The claims box closed on the eighteenth of July and the card is sealed in an envelope in a drawer on a lower floor and I have not been to that floor since and I am not going to this year, and nothing was ever written into it, and a stair cannot be held in a field and that is the only reason in the world and it is not a good enough reason and it is the reason I have got.*
