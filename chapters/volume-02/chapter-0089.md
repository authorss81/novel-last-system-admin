# Chapter 0089 — Two Days In September

**Thursday the tenth of July 2014, ten in the morning, a first-floor room above a building society on Hollow Bank, the offices of the Saint Orra and District Flood Forum.**

There is no such thing as a flood forum in this city with nine seats and a quorum and a minute book. There is a room above a building society, a ring binder, eleven plastic sleeves, a laminated card with a plastic sleeve that has been replaced twice, and eleven people who turn up.

**Gareth Raynor-Hale**, about fifty, resilience officer, full-time, in post two years and four months, had a printout on the table with the top edge folded over, and he had been writing the September quarterly report since Monday, five weeks early, because the terms of reference say the report is written in the month before the quarter it covers and that month is August, and he does the terms of reference, and he is the only person in the room who does anything at all, and doing it five weeks early is the same kind of thing.

"Three weeks," he said. "That is what I have got, and I am telling you the figure because I am writing a report and the report goes to print in three weeks and there is a section in it that says *unresolved* and nothing under it.

"I have had that section since the twenty-eighth of April. It came out of an operations review. Somebody wrote *lowland evacuation capacity: unresolved* in a box on a form and that is where it has been for eleven weeks and it is the only honest sentence anybody in this city has produced about the lowland, and I have not been able to write what goes under it, and I have asked nine people what goes under it, and eight of them told me where the number came from and one of them told me the number was not a number of people, and it was not."

He turned the printout round.

**lowland evacuation capacity: unresolved**

"There. That's the heading. That's the whole of the section. Underneath it there is nothing, and it goes to print in three weeks, and a member of this forum has asked me in writing for a figure, and he is entitled to it, and I have not got one, and I have not lied about not having one in any document, and I have been sitting on this for eleven weeks.

"Now. **Nora Bell.**" He said the name the way he says every name, which is with the source attached, in the room, for the minutes, because that is a thing he does and it has made him unlikable in four meetings and indispensable in two. "Nineteen years of pencil marks in a book at the end of that embankment. Nine hundred and forty. **It is not nine hundred and forty people.** It is nine hundred and forty *times* that somebody stood at the barrier end and could not get across. A few hundred people, more than once each, and the number of people is not on the page and she has never tried to work it out, because — and I am quoting, and I asked her in a room and she said it and it is in the minutes of the sixth of May — *because if I work it out then it is a number of people.*"

He put his pen down.

"**That is the only number in this borough that anybody has ever kept about the lowland and it is a woman's own private record and it is not in any inspection and it exists because nobody ever asked her for it and she kept it anyway.** And I am going to write it in a report, and I am going to write where it came from in the same paragraph, and if the paragraph is two lines long then the report is a document that a person can be found in by it, and I have thought about that for eleven weeks and I am going to do it anyway, because the alternative is a section that says *unresolved* for the fourth consecutive quarter and this forum gets put in a review in November."

---

Jonas Mercer asked one question, and he had it ready when he came in, and he had brought nothing.

"Have you got the empty-crossing clause in that file."

Raynor-Hale did not pretend not to know what he meant, which Jonas Mercer has always appreciated about him more than is reasonable.

"Page ninety-one of the March pack, paragraph four, and yes, I have got it, and I am going to put it in the report in September with a sentence about it, and I would like to know what that sentence is going to be from you before I write it, because I asked you about it in a stairwell in April and you told me the truth and I have thought about what you said for eleven weeks and I have not been able to use any of it."

"Dev Singh is going to fight that clause for eighteen months on his own," said Jonas Mercer. "He has a daughter and he is not a councillor and he is not on your forum and nobody in this room is going to help him and nobody is going to be able to. **And I knew that in April and I told you in a stairwell and I did not telephone him, and I have not telephoned him in three months, and I would like that in your minutes and I would like it in your report in my words, because it is mine and I have been carrying it since the third of April and I have now put it in two rooms.**"

"I am not putting it in the report in your words," said Raynor-Hale. "I am putting it in the report in mine, which will say: *the empty-crossing clause at the lowland crossing is contested by the operator and no other party; the resilience officer has sought a second question and been refused by the operator on grounds he has given and would give again; no party other than the operator is affected by the contest and no party other than the operator is engaged in it.* Which is worse for him and better for the report, and I have been doing this job two years and four months and I have learned exactly one thing, which is that a contested clause with one party in it is a smell, and a smell in a report is worth a sentence and a sentence is worth a fight, and I am not going to waste it on *a man I spoke to in a stairwell.*"

Jonas Mercer did not argue that, and it was the correct decision and it took him about two seconds, and both of them knew it.

"He would deny it," he said.

"He would deny it," said Raynor-Hale, "**and he would not be lying,** and I have got that in the file too, and it is the only sentence in this pack that frightens me more than the clause does, because a man who will lie is a man you can move and a man who will not lie about a thing that is true is a man who has to be beaten, and this forum has no procedure for beating anybody, and it has never needed one, because for twenty years everybody who came to this room was frightened of a review."

---

Then Raynor-Hale put his hand flat on the folder, and said, "And now the thing I have actually rung you about, and I have rung the shop twice this month and I have got a name and address off a board and I would have rung the board if I could not, and I am going to say what I want and then I am going to stop talking.

"**The chair promised, in a Committee room in May, a public and minuted record of the nodes in this city with no party answering for them.** She said it, it is in the transcript, and it was the best thing anybody did in that room. It was referred to September. I have chased the chair's office three times since the second of June. I have had two replies and both of them said the record was in preparation.

"**And I want it, and I want it from you, and everybody in this city who has ever produced a number has told me it is impossible and I have started to believe them, and I am asking you anyway because you are the only man I have met who counts things.**"

Jonas Mercer said, "I cannot give you one."

"You have not tried."

"I have tried for about four minutes in my head while you were talking and I have worked out what I would have to do, and I am going to tell you what it is, because you write down where every answer comes from and I would rather you wrote down this one too.

"**To produce a count of the nodes in this city that have no party answering for them, I have to go and get it from a schedule.** Because that is the only instrument in this city that holds a node, a tier, a holder and a date together in one line, and if a node is not on a schedule then it is not in the schedule, and if it is not in the schedule then it has to come from somewhere else, and everywhere else it comes from is a *return*, and a return is a form that a party fills in, and the whole point of these nodes is that there is no party.

"So the count of the nodes with nobody answering for them has to be made by reading a register with chairs, and taking out the shapes that have got nothing in them and counting those. **And that is the ninth time I have been made to do a thing by a schedule in five months and it is the first time I have understood why the chair's sentence in June matters.**"

"What sentence."

"'A party that has been asked to confirm occupancy has been asked for a state and not for a list, and a party that answers with a list has answered a different question.' That is a plate, and it is a condition, and it is not advice, and you can do exactly what it says: ask somebody for a *state*. Ask a district what is happening on its lowland. Ask the department for the number of standpipes it maintains. Ask a housing association what it does about a block of 1938. **Every one of those is a state, and every one of them is answerable, and every one of them will be answered by a person who has been doing it for nineteen years and who does not need to be asked twice, and I have been asking the wrong question for eleven weeks.**"

Raynor-Hale wrote nothing down, and then wrote it down.

"Go on."

"And then I get seven state answers and I turn them into a number, **and the number is a count of what seven people called a standpipe in July 2014, and it is not a count of the thing, and there will be a second one in January with a different number, and in a year somebody will use the January one, and the January one will be a fact about seven people in July and it will be a fact about the lowland for a decade.**"

"And the number you could have made in four minutes," said Raynor-Hale.

"**One.**" Jonas Mercer put his hand flat on the table, the way he puts it flat on a sill. "There are six. There are six because a chief officer worked them out on the twelfth of March and wrote them on her own wall on the twenty-eighth of April, and because a man gave the figure to a chair in a corridor at ten to eight in the morning on the nineteenth of May, and it went into a minute, and the minute does not say which six.

"And there is a second number. **Six, and one of them I can name, because I broke a chain in July and the breaking of it made one of them findable.** It is a stand at the bottom of Quarry Street. And I can name it, and it is a fact about a node, and it is not a fact about the woman who is at it at twenty to seven every morning, and her name is not in this room and it is not in this report.

"And if I gave you six, you would write *six*, and the six would be in a public report in September, and in about four years somebody would go and find the six, and they would not find six water points. **They would find six addresses, and a report that can be matched to a list of addresses is two documents away from a document with people on it, and there is a records supervisor in this city who has spent five months designing a form so that she never has to hold the second one.**"

The room was quiet.

"That is the refusal," said Jonas Mercer, "and I have made it once before, to a man in a car park in April, and I want to say the part that is new, and it is the part that matters and it is the reason I am not going to give you six.

"**I could have made it in about four minutes and nobody would ever have found out that I had made it, and that is the reason I am not going to.** Four minutes off a public record is free, and free is how this city got here, and in five months I have been the man who writes things down and I have watched a woman put a number in a box that she did not choose and lose her afternoon to it, and I am not going to be the man who hands a resilience officer a figure he has manufactured and watches it become a fact in September because he was in a room and it was Thursday."

---

Then Raynor-Hale gave him a piece of paper, which was the membership page of the forum, folded, with eleven names on it and a twelfth line.

"Read that out and then tell me what you would do about the last line."

Jonas Mercer read it out. Ten names, and then a line. The tenth name was **NORA BELL**, and beside it, in a column headed *present*, a tick in a different ink.

"There is no eleventh name," he said. "And the twelfth line says *seat*."

"It says *seat*. It said *seat vacant* in April, and I took the vacant out in June on the grounds that a vacant place invites somebody to be put in it. And then I was asked, by two people in this room, to put it back, and one of them was the chair of this forum, and she was right and I was wrong, and **so the line says *seat*, and it will keep saying *seat*, and that is the whole of it and I have stopped trying to improve a line on a piece of paper.**"

He took it back off him and put it in the folder.

"So: the eleventh place is empty and it is not written *vacant* and Nora Bell's name is in the attendance column in a different ink, and Nora Bell has come to four meetings of eleven and has spoken twice, and both times she has said a number that nobody asked her for, and the second time she has said it she has said where it came from, in the room, for the minutes, and that is the only reason I have a section heading in a report and it is because a woman of about sixty decided that the record was not complete without her.

"**And I am not going to write *vacant*, and I want you to say in front of Nina Calder in about four months why I did not, because I have been told not to by a registrar and I would like it minuted by somebody who is not me.**"

---

**Friday the eleventh of July 2014, a quarter past four in the afternoon, the shop.**

He was on the bench with a piece of oak and a marking gauge and the door open on the street because it was thirty-one degrees, and the telephone went, and it was a woman, and she did not give a name.

"I am telephoning the shop because I am not telephoning a council," she said. "I have telephoned a council once in my life and I was put on hold and the person came back and said there was nobody in that department and hung up."

"What is it about."

"**I am one of the eight.**"

He did not say anything.

"You will know which eight. There has not been a letter written and it has been asked for twice and the second time was about six years ago and it went to a company and not to a person, and it is not about the buckets and it is not about the money, and everybody that has ever come to this street has written something down and taken it away and the thing that was asked for has not been written.

"**So I am telephoning a shop because you are the only person in this city I have heard of who has written down a thing that was against himself.** That is the whole of why I am telephoning. It is not a complaint. There is nothing to complain about. There is a piece of paper that two people asked for and nobody has written and I am not going to say which eight and I am not going to say which street and if you ring me back on this number I will not answer and I am giving it to you so that it is not lost.

"It is not urgent," she said. "**There is no date on it and that is why I am telephoning a shop, because there is a date on everything else in this borough and the one thing we have been asking for since 2008 is the one thing nobody will put a date on.** Thank you for not asking."

The line went.

He wrote it down. All of it, and the number, and the time, and the words *thank you for not asking*, and then he sat on the crate in the yard for about forty minutes with a pad in his pocket that he did not take out, because the sentence he had to write was not a sentence, it was a **letter**, and he has never in his life written a letter for anybody, and on the thirty-first of May a woman of sixty-three in a kitchen told him in four lines that the day this street has a pump somebody will ask what it is attached to and the answer will be a paper and the paper will have one of her neighbours' names in a field on it.

He did not write the letter. He wrote four lines instead, and the first of them is the only one that is a fact.

> *A telephone call, Friday the eleventh of July, twenty-five past four, from a woman who gave a name that was not a name. One of eight. A letter asked for twice and owed since about 2008. I have the number, and it is on this page, and I am not going to ring it back, and I am not going to put her in anything.*
> *The letter is still owed. Nothing I have done in five months has made anybody write it and I have now been asked for it by somebody who did not ask me for anything else, and that is the correct order and it is a sentence in a document and a document is what everybody on this street is frightened of.*
> *Somebody will ring this shop about a Tuesday in about two weeks' time. The Tuesday will be in August. I have known since the eighteenth of May that it will be Royden Achebe and I have known what he will say, and I have written it out in April and I have not done anything about it, and I have still not worked out whether I am going to be the one who answers, and there is a shop steward and a records supervisor and a woman on a landing who are all better placed to answer it than I am, and two of them have told me so and one of them has not been asked.*
> *Three weeks to a report with a hole in it. I have refused a number I could have made in four minutes and I would do it again on a worse day. That is the first thing in five months that I have done in this city for no reason at all, and there is no instrument for it and I am not looking for one.*
