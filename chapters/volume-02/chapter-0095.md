# Chapter 0095 — Nine Seats

**Monday the first of September 2014, ten past nine in the morning, the counter, the records office.**

The request came in on Friday afternoon on a sheet of paper with a Committee crest on it, from somebody's office, in a hand that had been taught to write carefully, and it asked Nina Calder to produce **a standing procedure for the cooperative steward board**, one page, for distribution to the persons about to fill the seats.

She read it standing up. She did not sit down. She gave it back.

"No."

"I have not read it out."

"You have read it out in your head and you read it out in your head in about four seconds, and it is one page, and it is the fourth time in four months that somebody in this city has asked me for one page." She put the sheet face down on the counter, which is a thing she does. "**And I am going to say it properly, in the right order, and then I am not going to say it again in this office.**

"I have designed forms since March. Two of them you can get at a counter. I refused three times to make a fourth and one of the three was in a bin and one of the three is on a wall in the vestibule with a person's name in a row on it, and a man with a register put his own name in it on purpose in July because a form that has never had a person's name on it cannot tell the difference between a state and a person. **And I have had a fifth one in my head since the seventh of July and I have not written it down, and I am telling you now, in a room, that I am not going to write it down, and nobody is going to argue with me about it, and if somebody does argue with me about it I will put the sheet in the drawer with the laminated heat card in it and it can stay there until the borough is dissolved.**

"**Because that page is the form, and the form is a scheme, and a scheme is a list with a future in it.** You write one page for a body of nine seats and in four years that page is in a drawer and there are nine names on it and somebody wants to add a tenth, and then it is a register, and the register is on a wall, and the wall is in a room that people come into. **A form is the last thing a person writes and the first thing an institution keeps.** I have watched three of them happen in this borough this year and one of them is a heat protocol with a number box in it and a chief officer's name under it that she did not put there, and the number in that box is in a photograph."

Somebody behind her said, "Miss Calder—"

"**And I am not doing it for a reason anybody else can use, and I am not going to explain it twice.** I am doing it because you asked me for one page on Friday afternoon and it is the fourth time, and this time the reason is not money and it is not a chair and it is not a registrar. It is that a body which has been described to me for three months as nine seats does not need a procedure from me. **It needs somebody to be in a room and be wrong out loud.** And I have read a regulation that says a body of that kind may require a party to answer and may not require a person to do anything, and the whole of that regulation in four pages is the procedure, and it was written in 2008, and it is better than one page would be, and it is a horrible thing to be true and it is the only true thing anybody has got."

She squared the sheet.

"I am not drafting it. **Tell them Calder refused. Do not put a reason on it, and do not put my job title on it, and do not let a registrar's title go next to it either, because the last time somebody put a job title next to a refusal in this city it became a finding about the standing orders, and a refusal with a job title on it is a policy, and I do not want a policy.**"

---

**Monday the first of September 2014, twenty past four in the afternoon, the vestibule of the Old Ward.**

There was a sheet on the board. It had been up since half past two and it was not on Committee paper and it had no crest on it, and it had been typed in an office on the second floor and nobody had signed it, and Jonas Mercer read it standing up in eleven feet by nine and it is the whole of what happened that day.

> **COOPERATIVE STEWARD BOARD. Section 11, continuity regulations 2008. Eight seats filled 1 September 2014. Ninth seat to be filled in a room, in front of people.**
>
> **A seat is not an appointment. A seat is a person who is already doing a thing.**
>
> **Eight seats, one for each service district the layer draws. A seat is filled by the person named in answer to a question about a state and not about a list, where that person lives under the node they answer for. Nobody is appointed to anything. Nobody is asked in advance whether they will sit. A person who is named without being asked may decline afterwards, in their own words, and nothing follows from it.**
>
> **The ninth seat is filled separately, and it is filled in a hall, and it is filled in front of people.**
>
> **There was one refusal.** It is on a piece of paper in a file, and the man who refused it read four pages of a regulation before he did it, and the reason he gave is on the second page, and it is not about money and it is not about standing, and it has been carried round this building for eleven weeks and I have watched four people walk past it, and it is the most important sentence anybody has said in this borough since March.

> **Wren Court, Salter's Yard — thirty-one rooms, no civic address — was not seated. The room tried. Thirty-one people in a building with no civic address are not a body and cannot be seated, cannot vote, cannot require anybody to do anything and cannot be challenged, because a tier may not be challenged by a person. Being right about that has not helped them since the fourth of July and it did not help them on Monday afternoon either and nobody is going to pretend that it did.**

> **The eight who are seated, and how:**
> **Y. Amoah, 41, ambulance duty officer, eleven years. The state: what happens at this door at ten at night.**
> **I. Sesay, 51, caretaker, Tarn Street school, thirty-one years. The state: a gate with a key in it and a line in a form that is his fault.**
> **D. Fahey, facilities supervisor, Saint Orra west, six years. The state: this is a stair.**
> **I. Tarrant, car park supervisor, eleven years. The state: nobody has ever asked me what it is for and I have assumed it is a barrier.**
> **S. Umeh, 58, market porter and lock-up keeper, thirty years, Bellow Market. The state: the shutter and the padlock and what the padlock is for.**
> **K. Ferris, 33, night bus controller, nine years. The state: the last bus and who is standing at the stop when it does not come.**
> **W. Crabbe, 62, linen supervisor, nineteen years, a care home in Gantry Ward. The state: the laundry runs on one machine, and when that machine is off the linen is in a corridor at the end of somebody else's shift, and nobody has ever been told whose shift it is.**
> **G. Nye, 44, self-employed heating engineer, twenty-one years, three shifts a week. The state: the plant room in the basement and the man who is nearest when it goes.**
>
> **None of the eight is a party. None may require a person to do anything. None may be given money by anybody, because the board has no money and the eight have not asked for any and there is no fund and there is no fund coming. None is a member of a committee. None is on a list.**
>
> **And the honest line, which is this: any one of the eight can leave.** They were named without being asked, and a person who is named without being asked may decline afterwards in his own words, and nothing follows from it, and that is the whole of the power this borough has been able to give anybody in a room since the fourth of March. It is not the same as being put in a room and told to stop, and it is not the same, and a records supervisor in this city wrote on the second of June that for nine seats to be anything other than nine chairs one of the nine has to be able to be put in a room and told to stop, and removed in front of the other eight, by people who did not put him there. **That has not been done. It cannot be done. The eight were seated anyway, and the reason they were seated is that a refusal about a door is a refusal about a door, and a door with nobody behind it is a stair, and everybody in the hall on Thursday knows exactly what a stair is.**

He read it four times. It was typed in a hand he knew, and the last paragraph was in the flat register and not in the typewriter face, and what it said about power was that eight people can leave and that nobody in this city can put anybody in a room and tell them to stop, and there were nine seats.

**And the ninth, on that Monday afternoon, could not be put out.** The other eight can be unseated in a fortnight, by declining, one after another, in their own words. She cannot be unseated by anybody but herself, and she can do exactly as she likes, and there are nine people in a Methodist hall on Carrow Lane on Thursday who are about to be told that, and none of them knows it, and nobody is going to tell her.

The fourth page of a regulation is in a lever-arch file in a clerk's own hand. A registrar said nine words about it in a corridor on the Thursday of the twenty-first of August and would not read it out. A records supervisor read the whole of it on the morning of Tuesday the nineteenth and gave a reason in a records office and was not argued out of it, and the reason she gave was that a person may not be required even to be told, and that is a sentence a chair said three times in a room in June and it is the load-bearing wall of everything anybody has done since the third of April.

**So on the fourth of September, in a hall on Carrow Lane, a woman of fifty-four is going to stand up in an aisle and say her own price, and part of the price is a thing that three people in this city have read and nobody has told her, and nobody in that hall is going to stop her working it out, and nobody in that hall is going to help her, either.**

**Wednesday the third of September 2014, twenty past eleven in the morning, the counter, the records office, second floor.**

"What is the ninth seat for," said Jonas Mercer.

"You are asking a records supervisor about a regulation she has read once," said Nina Calder, "and I would like it noticed that you are asking me and not the clerk, and that the clerk is the one who copied it out by hand, and that you have not asked her, and that I am going to answer you and it is going to be worse than the book."

She put the pad down.

"**A person who answers for a service and is not a body.** Nine words. It is on the second page, and it is the whole of the ninth seat, and there is nothing else about it in four pages. It has been in a book since 2008. It has been read in this city four times this year and three of those times were by men looking for a way not to constitute anything.

"And the ninth words on that page are these: **the ninth seat is not a veto, is not a chair, and cannot be removed by the other eight.** And I have read that sentence four times since Monday and I am not going to say anything else about it, and I am not going to say it at this counter, and if you ask me again I will put the laminated heat card back in the drawer and take the whole of this office with me."

"I was not going to ask you again."

"You were going to ask me again on Thursday," said Nina Calder, "and I want to say one more thing at this counter on a Wednesday, and then I am going back to the machine.

"**You have spent five months refusing to be the person in the field, and you have been very good at it, and I want to tell you what the ninth seat is, because you have not worked it out and you have read section eleven and you have not worked it out.** It is not a person who can be asked a question. It is not a person who answers. It is a **person who is findable, and is not asked, and cannot be removed, and the whole of the design is that she is the one person in the building that the other eight could find if they were allowed to look.**

"**That is the seat. And you are not going to be able to unseat it, and neither is the board, and I have not told the person who is going to take it and I am not going to be asked to, and there are two reasons and the second one is not mine.**"
---

**Thursday the fourth of September 2014, seven in the evening, the Methodist hall on Carrow Lane.**

There were about thirty people and about nine of them were on the front row and about six more were outside in the road because it was still warm at seven in the evening in the first week of September, and the hall has a stage and a folding table and a urn, and it is the same hall a chief officer stood in at half past ten on a Saturday night in May with about forty people in it, and about a hundred yards from that, across a car park and a turning, there is a block of 1938 with no water in it since the sixth of May.

**The ninth seat was filled at ten past seven, and it took eleven minutes, and the room did not know what it was watching for about the first four.**

She came in late and she was wearing the apron, because she had come from the workshop and it was a Thursday and the workshop had been open, and there were about nine people near the front who knew what was happening and about twenty-one who had come because there is a hall on this road and something is on.

She did not go to the table. She stood in the aisle between the second and third rows for about a minute and a half, and then she said:

"**I am not going to do this on a stage and I am not going to do it into a microphone, and I am going to do it here in the aisle, and I am going to say the thing that has to be said first and then somebody can ask me things for an hour and I will stand here for the hour.**

"Eight seats got filled on Monday. I know how. Somebody went round asking what a state is and the answer had a person in it. **Nobody was asked whether they would sit.** I have been in nineteen years' worth of rooms where somebody was asked and I know exactly what an ask does, and Monday was not an ask, and the eight of them are still frightened about it and two of them have already said they will stop if it turns into a job, and they are right, and if it turns into a job there is no rule in the world that makes them do it, because a person may not be required to do anything and I have not been in a room where that got said three times in a year until a chair said it in June.

"**So here is the ninth one and here is the price, and I am going to say the price out loud and I am not going to do it the way a person does a price when they want something.**

"**I am findable.** There is one of me. I have got a lease with my name on it and a workshop with my name over the door and I have been findable since 1995 and I have never in nineteen years been found by anybody holding a document. **That is the seat. The ninth seat is a person who answers for a service and is not a body, and the words under it are that she is not a veto, she is not a chair, and she cannot be removed by the other eight. Read that again. I cannot be removed by the eight people who are being seated this week, and I am not a veto, and I am not a chair, and there is no instrument in this city that can put me in a room and tell me to stop, and I have been told that by two people this week and I have not been able to get any further than it.**

"And I am not being paid, because the board has no money and I have not asked for any and nobody is going to find any. **I have been doing this for nothing since the fourth of July, and I have been doing it for nothing before that, and I am fifty-four, and I have a workshop, and I am going to be doing it for nothing until I am sixty-one, and after sixty-one I am going to be doing it for nothing differently.** That is the whole of what I have got to give and I have said it out loud in a room and about thirty people have heard it, and thanking me would make it a thing that somebody did for me, and it is not a thing anybody did for me. **It is a seat in a room where any eight of them can walk out of it and I cannot be put out of it, and I am going to take it, and that is the whole of the transaction and I have been in this building nineteen years and I have never once been thanked for anything that was a fact.**

"And the last of it, and then you can ask me anything.

"**The thirty-one of us are not seated. Monday tried. There is no civic address for the building and a body cannot be seated without one and nobody can be challenged who is a person, and it is right, all of it, every word of it, and it has not helped us since the fourth of July and it did not help us on Monday and it is not going to help us tonight.** So thirty-one people who have not been seated are standing in a hall on this road, on the same road, four hundred yards from a block with no water in it, and I am going to sit down at the front, and I am going to be a person in a room, and I am not going to pretend that is the same thing as them.

"That is the price. **I am findable and I am unpaid and I am a person and I am the one who cannot be removed, and I am the one woman out of forty people in this hall who anybody can write to.**"

She sat down on the front row, and about thirty people in a hall on a Thursday evening found it easier not to make a speech than it should have been, and one of them put the urn on instead.

---

**Thursday the fourth of September 2014, ten past nine in the evening, the Methodist hall.**

A woman of seventy-two had been at the back for the whole thing with a walking frame and had walked on Carrow Lane four hundred yards that morning, alone, and had not told anybody, and Jonas Mercer had seen her come in at about ten to seven and had seen her go out at about nine and had not asked her one single question, and he wrote that down in the four lines at the bottom of the page afterwards with nothing in it about her.

The eight who were seated were there and the ninth was there, and the first thing the board did — its entire first act, at ten past nine, before anything else had been said — was that **Oren Pell's written statement was read out**, and it had come by hand from the registrar's office that afternoon on one sheet with a paragraph number on it, and Frances Tolley had typed it out at the records counter at half past four for nothing.

> **A COOPERATIVE STEWARD BOARD constituted under section 11 of the continuity regulations 2008.**
> **Its powers are: to require a party holding continuity to answer; to receive the answer; to be asked to consider a matter. Those are its powers. They are the whole of its powers.**
> **It may not require a person to do anything, to hold a duty, to answer a question, or to be told anything. It may not be given money. It may not remove a person from a seat. It may not remove a person from a seat because a person is not a party and cannot be required to do anything, including the removal.**
> **No seat in it is permanent. No seat in it is a job. It has no quorum that can require a person. It has no appeal from it to anybody, and the reason it has no appeal is Schedule 4, page nine, and that is not an oversight of this determination and it will not be corrected by any instrument in this city within the next four years.**
> **Nine seats: eight for the service districts the layer draws, and a ninth for a person who answers for a service and is not a body.**
> **I am asked to state, for the record, whether that is a room. It is a room. It is a room with one power and no way to lose it, and a thing with one power and no way to lose it is nine chairs, and the ninth of them is a person.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, and then **Kathleen Ferris, thirty-three, night bus controller, nine years, who had not said a word all evening**, put her hand up and asked the only question anybody asked.

"**Then what are we for.**"

And there was no answer in the room, and they spent an hour and ten minutes on it, and they are not allowed to answer it, and they were told that in a paragraph on a sheet read out at ten past nine at night in a Methodist hall, and about thirty people went home at about a quarter to eleven knowing precisely what they were and precisely what they were not.
