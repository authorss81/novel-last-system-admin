# Chapter 0059 — The Return of a Deferred Node

The pre-sitting of the Standing Committee on Civic Continuity on Wednesday the thirtieth of April was a closed meeting, in committee room two, from ten in the morning until about eight in the evening, with no gallery and no public box and no minute that anybody outside that room might read, and Jonas Mercer was not in it and knew that he was not in it because Oren Pell had told him so on the telephone on the Sunday, in ninety seconds, and had said the sentence in a way that Jonas Mercer recognised, because it was the way Pell said a thing when he had found a piece of law that was on his side and could not wait to tell a man who would not like it.

So he was in the porch of the Old Ward public board at about twenty to eleven on Wednesday morning, which is the only dry place in that half of the city, with a bag over his shoulder and a pad in his left hand and a claim in the claims box that he had not put there and had not asked about.

The claims box had been empty for eleven days.

He had watched a man of about sixty put a folded sheet into it on Sunday the twentieth, and stand reading it for four minutes, and fold it again, and put it back in his coat and walk off, and he had not asked him about it then. There was one card in the box on Wednesday the thirtieth and it had not been there on Tuesday, and it was about a stair, and Jonas Mercer read the first line of it standing up and stopped reading it there on the grounds that it is ninety days before anybody has to do anything about it and a man who reads a stranger's card in a public box at twenty to eleven in the morning is a man who has started something he cannot finish.

He put it back where he had found it and went and sat on the step.

---

Nina Calder found him there at ten past eleven with two coffees and a look on her face that Jonas Mercer had seen four times in five weeks and had never once got used to.

“Three things,” she said, “and I have got a meeting at half past and I am giving you the facts and not the opinion because you have not asked me for an opinion since March and I would like that to remain true.

“One. Schedule 4 went into three systems yesterday afternoon. Two are the committee’s and one is the borough’s, and the borough one is a courtesy copy, and it went in as a tabled paper with a covering submission and the covering submission cites a shop steward’s finding, which I am not going to discuss in a porch.

“Two. Alden Rook has been in this building since quarter to ten and he is in the pre-sitting and so is the continuity counsel and so is a man from the water people and so is a man from the housing association, and the chair has allowed four people into that room who are not members of the committee, and she did that in four minutes this morning, and the reason she did it is that every one of the four asked to be there and every one of the four will lose money if the schedule does not stand.

“Three.” She handed him a sheet of A4, warm from a printer, with a departmental crest on it and a form number in the corner, and it had been printed at half past ten that morning by a records clerk called Doina Serkis who had never printed one of these before.

He looked at it.

> **NACRE DEPARTMENT OF CIVIC CONTINUITY — RETURN OF A DEFERRED NODE. To be completed by the party holding maintenance of the node and returned to the Old Ward public board by 8 p.m. on the seventh day. A node not on a continuity schedule is not disconnected. It is deferred. It serves its last verified essential use. If none is recorded, the use is the node’s maintenance.**
>
> **NODE — · LOCATION — · DISTRICT — · HOLDER — · TIER — · DATE OF DEFERRAL — · WHAT IT SERVES NOW — · WHAT IT SERVED BEFORE — · WHAT WOULD BE NEEDED TO RESTORE — · WHO ANSWERS FOR IT —**

“Who told you to make this,” said Jonas Mercer.

“Nobody,” said Nina Calder.

She sat down on the step beside him, which she does not do.

“I have been a records supervisor for nineteen years and every form I have ever issued has been asked for by somebody. I did not ask the Lattice whether it can publish a narrowing. I asked the man at the basement printer, who is thirty-one and has a print cardigan on, and he said: it will draw you a shape and a term and a date and it will tell you the claim under it, and it has never once in the history of that machine drawn what a thing stopped doing, and he said it the way people say things they have thought about on their own and have not been asked. So the Lattice cannot do this. The city can, because the city has paper and a printer and a records supervisor who is extremely annoyed.

“It is not adopted. It is not minuted. It is not in a schedule and it is not enforceable and it establishes nothing and it names nobody. It is a form and it is being issued tonight to nine parties and it is going on that board with the crest on it because a crest is the only thing anybody in this city will believe.” She took the coffee back. “There is a blank line at the bottom. Do not ask me about the blank line yet. Come back at six o’clock and ask me about the blank line, and I will answer you, and I have been waiting since half past ten this morning for somebody to ask me about the blank line and you are the only person in this city who is going to notice it.”

---

The first return came in at six o'clock and Doina Serkis brought it out of the records office on a bicycle with a plastic bag over it against the rain, and there were nine lines on it and Nina Calder did not take it into the basement and did not put it in a file, and she read it standing in the porch under the glass canopy with the board light on above her head, in front of a caretaker and a man who had come to read the box, and out loud.

“Seven forms back, out of nine asked. Six of them came in with nine nodes on them between them. One came back with the whole of the form blank and a word on it that said *unknown* and signed, and I am going to publish that one too, because it is the most useful thing on the sheet. Two did not return at all, and I am publishing those as two, because silence is also a return and it is the only honest way to count it.

“One. Marsh Lane substation. Tier two, holder on the borough's own schedule, serving a verified street claim, restored needs nothing, answered for by the borough.

“Two. Bellow Market loading hatch. Holder is a trader, and I am not going to print the trader, and the trader asked me not to, and I am printing the line and not the name, and Doina has been told twice.

“Three. Saint Orra west care entrance, six forty-one, holder on duty, Tier — none, serving, a verified clinic care claim, a verified transfer with a bed and a named receiving shift, and a return-of-care handoff named by a local steward. Restored needs: a rota and a payroll line. Answered by: the site administration, and the terms are read aloud at the twenty-two hundred handover, and the person answering is on the rota, and their name is on a board in a corridor two miles from here and not on this form, and I am not going to put a name in a field on a form that goes on a public board.

“Four. Carrow Lane Housing Association meter room, Marsh Lane. Tier two. Holder: the Carrow Lane Housing Association. What it serves now: four blocks, one hundred and twenty-eight rooms, including Block D lift and Block C shared stopcock, neither of which has a holder of its own.

“Five. Saint Orra west mortuary van gate, six forty-four, which has a last verified essential use in biro on the back of a laundry delivery note and no date and no initials, and I am reporting that on a form and I am reporting that the writer is a night orderly who is not on the rota, and Doina has put his name in and I have stopped her, because that is not the form's business and because if that form names him he is findable at a gate in this city for a year, and he is twenty-nine, and he did it in nine seconds because somebody was upset.

“Six. Saint Orra west car park barrier three. Same site, same tier, same answer, and I am putting it on the sheet as its own line because it is its own gate, and everybody who reads this is going to read those two as one thing and they are not one thing.

“Seven. The Saint Orra bridge west-lane hinge. Historic term, not resourced, two handovers in the crossing log from the eighteenth of April with both written under the fifty-eight, and the operator's name is in a dispatch book and is not on this form.

“Eight. A school gate at Tarn Street, holder recorded as a caretaker, no tier and nothing at all recorded for what it serves, and Doina, put a hand up, that one is a *key*, that is not a service, that one has never been a service, hold on.”

The caretaker said, “There is a line on it that says if none is recorded the use is the node’s maintenance, and a school gate’s maintenance is a caretaker with a key, and that is a person, so is that one fine?”

“It is a man of fifty-one who has asked for this three times and been told three times that a key is not a permission,” said Nina Calder. “It is the best answer on the sheet and it is not fine and it is going in. I am not going to fix it on a form in a porch in the rain at six o’clock and go home.”

She stopped there for a second.

“**Nine.** A lowland water point. That is all. It says, on a form printed at half past ten this morning by a clerk who had never printed one before, on the ninth line, in a set of nine: *a lowland water point.* No number. No location of record. No district, because there is no district. Holder: nothing. Tier: nothing. Date of deferral: nothing, because nobody has ever said when. What it serves now: a pump. What it served before: a pipe. What would be needed to restore: a person, a price, or a schedule.

“Registrar’s ruling, an hour ago, in forty seconds, on filing grounds only, in the porch: the node may not be described further, because describing it would require a location of record, and a location of record for that node would be a survey, and a survey is a count, and a count is the price.” She looked up. “That is Oren Pell. That is not my sentence and I have written it out longhand exactly as he said it, and he said *that is not my sentence* and then he said it, and he has been doing this for nineteen years and I have never once seen him refuse a line of his own.”

Nina Calder turned the sheet round so he could see the bottom of it.

There was a rule across the page, and under the rule there was nothing.

“That is the blank line,” she said. “That is not a design feature. I have got ten fields and nine lines and the tenth one is empty and it is not empty because I ran out of paper. I put it there because in about four months somebody is going to print a form like this on a district that has a name, and I want the shape of the hole to already be in the document when they do. A form with a hole in it is the only honest thing available to me this week and I have been a records supervisor for nineteen years and I have never issued one.”

---

Alden Rook came out of committee room two at twenty past eight on the Wednesday evening of the thirtieth of April, and Jonas Mercer was on the half-landing outside it because that is where a man who has been told he may not be in a room waits, and Rook stopped, and looked at him, and did not say no, and said:

“You have been on that landing for about three and a half hours.”

“Since six.”

“Then you have heard nothing at all, and that is the correct outcome, and I am going to be in this stairwell for twenty minutes anyway, because I have got a train and because you have got a face that has been standing on a step for three and a half hours and I have seen that face before, on a Saturday morning in a yard, when a man read four lines out loud and understood all four of them at once.

“I am going to argue my case at you, because you are the only person in this city who is allowed to hear it and cannot do anything about it, and because a committee room is the wrong place to make the argument properly and the right place to win it. So. Twenty minutes. Go on then.”

“I have got a question and it is the only one I have got.”

“Ask it.”

“Why is it not a favour,” said Jonas Mercer. “A man in a good coat with a folder sat in a car on an embankment for forty-five minutes on Wednesday morning at one o'clock and told me he could not turn twenty-five thousand two hundred pounds into a favour, and he gave me three reasons and the third one was that in two years there will be a rota of nights. So which of those three was true.”

Rook leaned against the rail and considered it properly, which Jonas Mercer had not expected.

“The first,” he said. “The first is true and it is the boring one. It is not a favour because the money is not mine and the schedule is not a contract I can break for a night.

“The second is true and it is the one he did not tell you. If I do it for nothing there is a record of a district improving on a night when a technician from the register was standing on an emankment, and the next six times that happen there will be a question about the technician, and I have run this business for eleven years and I have watched four good districts go bad that way. It is not malice. It is that a favour in a system is a hole, and the hole gets used.

“The third is the one he was told to say and he is right about it, and I would like to add one thing to it, which he does not know because he is thirty-eight and I am forty-six. There is no rota of nights. There is an acquisition. About two years after the rota of nights there is a tender, and the tender is for something called *emergency discretionary continuity, section four*, and it is priced, and it is awarded on a track record of discretionary actions, and the man who awards it is going to be a procurement officer in a council who has never met any of us and is going to be shown a spreadsheet.” He shrugged. “Everything I do gets read back to me in about three years as a policy. That is not a threat and it is not a complaint. It is just that I have been doing it long enough to know the shape of the thing I am making.”

“All right,” said Jonas Mercer. “Then here is my question, and it is the only one, and you may say no.”

“Go on.”

“Marguerite Oyelaran of the Carrow Lane Housing Association worked out on the twelfth of March that the six lowland water points could go on a schedule at one thousand eight hundred and eighty pounds each, eleven thousand two hundred and eighty pounds a year, and nobody in this city has asked her to, and she has written the number on her own board in her own hand because she is a better organiser than the four of us in that room. There is nobody to ask, Alden. There is no district. There is no address of record. There is no service district that contains it, because a service district is a shape the drawing layer makes out of the terms in force inside it, and there are no terms in force, so it is not drawn, so there is no district to challenge with, and there is no person who may be permitted to challenge, because — ”

“Because a tier may be challenged by a district or by a node in the same tier and not by a person,” said Alden Rook. “Yes. I know. I wrote the argument for it and I have read that clause more times than I have read the rest of it, and I would like to tell you something about it and then I am going to get a train.

“It is the best clause in the document. I have been doing this eleven years and the whole of my trade is getting a claim in front of a place that can weigh it against another claim, and that clause does exactly that and it does it in one sentence and it is defensible to a committee and it is defensible in a court and it is defensible to a woman with a folder. It is also the reason a man on an emankment in April can be beaten by an invoice. I know what I built. I have known since March. I did it because the alternative was the nineteenth-day queue and a queue is not neutral, it is just private, and a private queue is a man you know, and a man you know is worse than an invoice and much cheaper and much easier to ignore.” He looked at his watch. “You have about six minutes.”

“Will you publish your own narrowings.”

There was a pause of no length at all.

“Yes,” said Alden Rook. “And I want to be clear that I have thought about whether I am being sentimental, and I have, and I am going to do it, and here is the actual reason. On the twenty-ninth of April at eleven minutes past midnight a borough pump operator telephoned our duty line because our gauge had dropped, and we are on that gauge because we hold the maintenance, and the first thing that happened to that lowland at one o'clock in the morning was that a contractor's own pressure system noticed. Not a person. A system. And in about four months somebody in a council is going to notice that sentence in a minute and it is going to be the most damaging line available to me, and I would rather it be in my own document on page eleven with a heading than in somebody else's minute.

“Nobody is coming to ask her, by the way,” said Alden Rook. “I know that because she has told four people and none of them has done anything, and I know it because I did it in March on a bigger scale with a bigger number and nobody came to ask me either, and the reason nobody came to ask me is that the man who asks is always the man who has to be able to pay, and the people who cannot pay are not in the room where the asking happens.

“In about four months somebody will come to ask her. It will not be you. You are findable and you are in a register and that is a problem for you and not an advantage, and in about two years somebody is going to use that register to say this city has an administrator, and I am going to have been right about it in a stairwell and there is going to be nobody on my side. And when the person who comes to ask her comes, it will be somebody with a budget, and it will be a good thing, and it will not be a rescue.”

He got his coat off the rail.

“The nineteenth of May,” he said. “Two things and then I am going. One: somebody in that room is going to try to define *holder*, and they will get to it eleven minutes in, and the chair will not stop them, because stopping them is not the chair’s job. And the definition will be drafted by whoever has put a schedule on the table, and there is only one of us who has put a schedule on a table in the last nine days, and I have never even been in that room for an argument. I am not going to do it. I want you to know I am not going to do it, so that when somebody tells you the contractor is going to define holder, you can say that you asked him in a stairwell.”

“And the second thing.”

“The second thing is that I have read the thing your factory clerk printed this morning, because I was in the room when the printer was asked about it,” said Alden Rook, “and it is a very good form, and it has ten fields and nine lines, and the hole in it is the only part of it I would not have thought of. And I am going to make sure that CivicCore’s own returns come in on your form, and not on mine, in a fortnight, and I am going to do that without being asked, and if anybody asks me why I will say it is because your man with the bag read the clause about a lawnmower in April and told a woman of sixty-three that she was the answer to a street, and I have a district in Gantry Ward with two hundred and forty customers and forty-one hinges and nine of them that are not my staff, and I have known since the twenty-ninth of March that the nine are the ones I am standing in front of every day of my life, and it has taken a technician with a sling in a market and a corridor in a hospital to write it on a piece of paper where I can read it.”

He went down the stairs.

---

The claims box at the Old Ward board closes on **Friday the eighteenth of July**, ninety days from the Saturday the nineteenth of April, and it has one card in it about a stair, and the card above it says, in Nina Calder's typing, that a claim so stated does not establish a service, does not require a name, and may not be used as evidence of occupancy, population, households or persons.

The Standing Committee on Civic Continuity sits on **Monday the nineteenth of May** and it has not defined *holder* and it is going to try.

Three weeks later, on Tuesday the twentieth of May, the fourth return came in, and it was a form printed at half past ten in the morning by a clerk with a plastic bag over it against the rain, and it had a line on it for a clinic in Cooper's Reach with a Thursday session and forty-one patients on the list, and nine of them over seventy-five and three housebound, and the answer to *what would be needed to restore* said **a person, a price, or a schedule**, and the answer to *who answers for it* said **nobody**, and it was the first time since the first of April that anybody in the city of Nacre had written the word *nobody* in a public document and meant it.
