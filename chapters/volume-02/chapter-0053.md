# Chapter 0053 — Nine Seconds on a Delivery Note

The mortuary van came in to Saint Orra west at about twenty past eleven on Wednesday night the twenty-third of April and Ivo Bamba opened the gate, and it was the fourth time in nine days, and he got the gate and stood back and watched it come and then went out and shut it and latched it, because the latch is a latch and the gate has to be shut.

Then he stood in the yard.

He is twenty-nine. He is the night orderly at Saint Orra west, he came on at ten, he is not on the rota, he is not a holder of anything, and he has been in that building for two years and seven months. What he is is the man who is there at midnight, which is a fact about him and not about his job, and on the afternoon of the fifteenth of April he had been the least-bad option of a whole city that had run out of holders, and he had held a door open with his shoulder that he was not entitled to hold while a woman of ninety-one came out of bay nine on a chair at four minutes past four in the morning, and he had gone home at four.

He stood in that yard for about a minute and a half. The plate on the bollard is at shoulder height on the driver's side, and the notice on it had been up since about twenty past seven that morning and he had walked past it perhaps forty times.

> **A DEFERRED NODE SERVES ITS LAST VERIFIED ESSENTIAL USE. IF NONE IS RECORDED, THE USE IS THE NODE'S MAINTENANCE.**

And under it a rule and a cursor, and nothing in it, and it had been nothing in it for eleven years, and the plate had never once printed anything about it because the plate had never once needed to.

He thought: *if I write something in that, it can't close.*

He had a laundry delivery note in his left hand. It was the second of three sheets of the week's, and it had a list of eleven rooms on it and a signature at the bottom that was not his.

He wrote with a biro on the back of it, standing up, in about nine seconds, and he did not write it well, and he did not put a date on it, and he did not initial it, and it said:

> **ANYBODY WITH A DEAD BODY OR A PERSON WHO CANNOT GET IN ON THEIR OWN**

and then he went back inside at about ten to twelve, because he had eleven rooms to do and he had done two.

---

Halvorsen telephoned the shop at twenty to seven on Thursday morning and Jonas Mercer answered it on the fourth ring from the bench, having not slept, having been up since four with a pad and a bad idea.

“It's filled,” said Gunnar Halvorsen. “The field. It's filled. I did not do it and I did not ask anybody to do it and I have been in this building since half past five and nobody has claimed it, and the gate opened at twenty past eleven last night and closed again, and it is not a gate this morning, it is whatever is in that field.”

“Who was in the yard.”

“That is the whole of the reason I am telephoning you at twenty to seven on a Thursday morning,” said Halvorsen. “The answer to that question is going to be the most important fact in this city for about eleven months and I do not have it and I am not going to find it out by asking twenty-nine-year-olds in a corridor whether they have written on a plate.”

Jonas Mercer was in the mortuary yard in about eleven minutes, and Ivo Bamba was at the back of it at ten to seven with a hose and a drain cover, and had been on since ten the previous night and had eleven hours in and was due off in ten minutes.

Jonas Mercer did not ask him whether he had written in the field. He said, “Good morning,” and then said, “Can you open the box on the bollard, please,” and Ivo Bamba said, “That is not a fault, mate, I already looked,” and Jonas Mercer said, “I know it is not a fault. I would like to see the field.”

---

The box came off in about four minutes. Five of the six fields were printed and dated and had handwriting in them. The sixth had a rule and a cursor, and in the space above the rule, in biro, in a hand that had gone over the same letters twice, it said:

> **ANYBODY WITH A DEAD BODY OR A PERSON WHO CANNOT GET IN ON THEIR OWN**

It is not a good sentence. It has no date on it and no initials and it is a laundry delivery note's back and it will not last on the face of a cast plate for eleven months. It is also, exactly, right, and Jonas Mercer stood in a mortuary yard at about ten to seven on Thursday morning with a roofing slate under a cover and his forearm across it and understood that Saint Orra west's mortuary gate was going to keep working for a year because a man of twenty-nine had written nine words in nine seconds on the back of a piece of paper about eleven rooms.

He put his hand flat on the cover and said the thing out loud, because he had decided in the car that he was going to say it out loud, and because Bea Osunde had said in a shop on Tuesday that a man does not get to be modest on behalf of a mortuary gate, and because a man of twenty-nine deserves to be told.

“I have got to tell you what you have done and then I have got to tell you that I cannot undo any of it, and then I am going to ask you to keep working, and if you would rather I went away then say so now and I will go.”

“Go on,” said Ivo Bamba.

“That field is a **last verified essential use**,” said Jonas Mercer. “You are not on a rota. You are not a holder. Nothing about you has ever been on a plate. And a plate does not know that, and a plate does not know that you did not know it, and a plate has taken what you wrote and it is going to treat it as the answer to the question of what that gate is for. For a year. Possibly longer, because there is nothing on that cover to date it and nothing on that cover to say who signed it, and if somebody comes along in eighteen months with a better sentence and a date, yours will still be in the record underneath theirs, because a record is not a page.

“You have given a hospital’s mortuary gate a standing instruction. It cannot be withdrawn by you, because a record can only be replaced by another record and only by a person, and the registrar is a registrar and not a clerk. It cannot be sold. It cannot be challenged — a tier can be challenged by a district or by a node in the same tier, and you are not a district and you are not a node, and neither am I. And the man who wrote *this is a fault* on it does not exist, because it is not a fault, and there is nobody in the city whose job it is to object to a sentence.”

Ivo Bamba had the hose in his hand and had not moved.

“Nine seconds,” he said. “I had the delivery note in my hand. I did not even know I was writing a thing that counts. I thought I was writing a note.”

“I know. That is exactly how it works and I am not telling you that to make you feel small, I am telling you because you are going to be asked about it in a year by somebody with a lanyard and I want you to be able to say the true version, which is that you were tired and it was eleven at night and somebody you work with was on a board in a corridor about forty minutes.”

“That is the true version,” said Ivo Bamba. “I was also cross.”

“That goes in the true version as well,” said Jonas Mercer.

He waited.

“I am not going to ask you to take it back,” he said. “I want to be very clear, because I know what a kind man does at this point and I do not want you to do it. If you ask me to take that sentence out of that field I cannot do it. I can put another one over the top of it. The old one stays in the record. And if we take it out, the field is empty, and an empty field means the gate is the gate's maintenance, and I do not know what the maintenance on a mortuary gate is, and neither do you, and neither does the man you telephoned at seven o'clock this morning, and I have read that plate and there is no field on it for that either.”

“Then it stays,” said Ivo Bamba.

“It stays.”

“All right,” said Ivo Bamba, and put the hose down, and then said, after about four seconds: “Are you the one off the register?”

“Yes.”

“Right,” said Ivo Bamba. “Then I will say one thing and then I am going home to bed for eleven hours and I am not going to be back till nine tonight.

“I did not do it for the hospital. If I had done it for the hospital I would have written *a mortuary van* and left it there and gone to bed, and that is the whole difference and you can put it in whatever you are writing. I wrote the second half because on the fifteenth of April I was the one holding a laundry bag in a corridor with a door I had no business holding, and there is a number on a board in that building that says forty minutes, and forty minutes is not about a door. Forty minutes is about a family. And I have been the one on this side of that gate at midnight every night for two years and seven months, and I have watched four vans come through it in nine days, and if I had not written it then on Saturday morning that gate would have been a thing a man with a key could open, and a man with a key cannot get a stretcher through it on his own.

“So that is the sentence that is in that field, and I do not know what it has become, and I would like to go to bed.”

Jonas Mercer said, “You can go to bed,” and Ivo Bamba went, and Jonas Mercer was left in a mortuary yard at about five past seven on Thursday the twenty-fourth of April with the box off the bollard and a roofing slate and a pad in his left hand.

---

He put the cover back on. He wrote in the pad, in his left hand, four lines, the way he had written four lines on the Wednesday.

> *6-44. LAST VERIFIED ESSENTIAL USE recorded, Wednesday 23 April, about 23:40, by a night orderly on nobody's rota, in nine seconds, on the back of a laundry delivery note, no date, no initials.*
> *The sentence is correct. It is the right answer. It was written by a witness who did not know what the field was.*
> *That is the definition of a hollow term and this is the first one I have met in this city and it is in a hospital and it is ours.*
> *I did not go to the yard on Tuesday. I had it on a pad on Tuesday. I knew on Tuesday what was going to happen on Wednesday night and I did not go, and the reason is in a book in a shop written in a woman's hand on Wednesday afternoon, and the reason is: a person who stands there. There was a person who stands there. I did not ask her.*

He read the fourth line three times and did not soften it, which is the only thing about it that was not cowardice.

---

He telephoned the registrar's office at about half past eleven and Oren Pell took the call himself, and did not take it in a car, and did not do it at half past two in the morning, and Jonas Mercer understood both of those things and neither of them helped.

“Can it be struck,” said Jonas Mercer.

“No,” said Oren Pell.

“That was fast.”

“It is not a difficult question and it has a short answer and I have been waiting since Wednesday evening for somebody to ask it so that I could give you the short one.” There was a pause. “A hollow term is a permission that functions. That is the definition and it is not a criticism, it is the definition. Your orderly has written a permission that functions. It is a good permission. It is better written than anything on that plate. It works, it will keep working, and it does what you want it to do, and it was made by a person who did not know it was a permission.

“If I strike it, the field is empty. You have read the field's own sentence. An empty field means the node's maintenance. And what is the maintenance on a mortuary gate in a hospital that has a van coming into it four times in nine days.” He did not raise his voice at all. “It is a man with a key. I am sixty-one. I have been in this city for forty years and I have signed things off and I have refused to sign things off and I am not going to be the man who puts a plate back the way it was on Friday morning of a flood year and walks out of a hospital yard at nine o'clock and has somebody else's job in his hands by lunchtime.

“So no. It stands. And I am going to say the other thing now, because you have earned it by asking the short question instead of the long one, and because I would like it minuted if there is anything to minute, and there is not, so I am going to say it to you on a telephone on a Thursday morning in April.

“In nineteen years of this office I have struck four hollow terms. Four. And I know that there are about nine hundred of them in this city, because we do not count, because counting them is not anybody’s duty, and every one of the nine hundred is a sentence somebody wrote in nine seconds for the best reason available to them at the time. I have never seen one before that was in a hospital. I have seen them in laundries and in a stairwell in Juniper Flats and in a lock-up on Marsh Lane and once in a school, and I have always thought: that is a defect of maintenance. It is not a defect of maintenance. It is a defect of the design, and the design is that a record of what a thing is for can be made by anybody who is standing near the thing, and that has been true since 1996 and nobody in this city has ever been angry about it, and I have now been angry about it for about thirty seconds, on your behalf, and I am going to stop now and go to a hearing about a building in Cooper’s Reach.”

“Which will fail,” said Jonas Mercer.

“It will fail,” said Oren Pell, “and the reason it will fail is on the third page of the reasons, and the reason is that the water in that building is a supply and not a service, and I have read that reason three times since Wednesday and I have not been able to argue with it, and I have been in this office for nineteen years and I have never before been unable to argue with a line a panel wrote about water.”
