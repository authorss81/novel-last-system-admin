# Chapter 0082 — A Trust With A Folder

On Friday the twentieth of June Jonas Mercer read two hundred and forty pages of Schedule 4 in the borough's public inspection copy, in a room on the first floor of the civic offices, on his own, between a filing cabinet and a radiator, on a Friday, with a pen that was chained to the desk and a sheet of A4 he had asked for and had been given and had to sign for.

It is four hundred and sixty pages. He had read page nine in April and the sub-paragraph three on the glossary page in May and he had never read a page of it start to finish because there is no reason to and because everybody in this city who says they have read all four hundred and sixty pages is a doctor or a contractor.

He read the assignment clause on Friday the twentieth, at about eleven-forty in the morning, and it is on page 219, and it is eleven lines long, and there is nothing wrong with it, and it is the only instrument in this city that has caused the thing that happens next.

It says, in effect and in the flat register the whole of the document is written in:

> A claim placed under this Schedule may be assigned, in whole or in shares, to a party having authority in relation to the node. An assignment takes effect on execution. An assignment does not create a new claim and does not create a new reference. The assigning party remains the party that placed the claim. **The assignee takes the claim, and takes what is owed on the claim, and takes the duty of the claim.** A share of a claim carries the tier of the claim in proportion to the share, and a share of a claim is enforceable by the assignee against the assigning party in respect of the tier only.

Two lines of that are the volume.

*The assignee takes the claim, and takes what is owed on the claim, and takes the duty of the claim.* Not a share of a right to buy a service. The thing itself, and the thing that is owed on it, and the duty of doing it.

And the last line, which is the sentence a deed clerk writes in ten seconds and a city does not read for four years: **a share of a claim is enforceable against the assigning party in respect of the tier only.** Everything else about the claim travels down and none of it comes back up. There is no clause in those eleven lines about what happens at the bottom of a chain, because a claim at the bottom of a chain is at the bottom of a chain by being at the bottom, and the party who put it there is a party who had authority, and the Schedule does not require anybody to look down a chain, and it certainly does not require anybody to look at the bottom of one.

He wrote six lines on the sheet of A4 and gave the sheet back, and it went into the file he had signed for, and it is in a public inspection file in a radiator room, and nobody will read it, and that is correct and it is the correct place for it.

---

He went out to Kestrel Hill on Friday afternoon on foot, forty minutes' walk, at about half past three, because there is no bus that goes where he was going and he wanted to arrive having walked.

Kestrel Hill is where the offices are and the universities and the money, and the road he wanted is a private one with a gatehouse that has a planning application on the gate and a bell that is not wired, and a house at the top of it that used to be a maternity home and is now a clinic with eleven patients and a day room, and a **cast-iron node plate on the gatepost** with the shape every plate in this city has.

The plate had picked up a line some time on Friday afternoon, in the flat register, with a full stop after each word:

> **WHERE A CLAIM IS DISCHARGED, THE CLAIM IS DISCHARGED FOR THE PERSON WHO HELD IT AND NOT FOR THE PERSON WHO CAUSED IT, AND THE PERSON WHO CAUSED IT MAY BE FINDABLE AND IS NOT THEREBY ANSWERABLE.**

He read it standing up in nineteen degrees of rain with a car going past too fast on a private road, and it told him nothing he did not already know and it cost somebody the price of a thing, and that is what a plate is for.

The plate carried a tier line as well. **Tier 3.** He put his hand flat on the gatepost the way he puts it flat on a sill, and read the four fields off it, and the third field was a claim reference, and it was **T3/11400/0304**, and the reference in two plastic wallets in committee room two on Thursday was the same string, and the same string was in a box on a form in a records office on the second floor of a building in this city, and the same string was on a standing-charge line on a water bill four miles the other side of a lowland, in a building where thirty-one people lived and where a fee had gone from eleven pounds a week to thirteen pounds sixty on the first of June.

He did not ring the bell. He wrote down the address and the post code and the name of the clinic and the date and he walked back.

---

**Monday the twenty-third of June 2014, ten in the morning, first floor, a suite of three rooms over a letting agency in Kestrel Hill.**

He got in because Beverley Annis let him in, and she let him in because of the fourth sentence he said on the step, and he had thought about the order of the sentences for two days and he put that one fourth on purpose.

What he said was: *I am the man who was in the gallery. I am not on anything, I am not a party, I am not a member, I am not an officer of anything, I am not here to ask you for anything, and I am going to be here for about fifty minutes and then I am going to go, and I would rather you heard the first four sentences from me than from a newspaper. The fifth sentence is that a committee clerk read a claim reference out in a room on Thursday and it is the same reference that is on your file. The sixth sentence is that I have not telephoned any of you and I am not going to. The seventh is that whatever happened on the third of June is going to be in a public record by the end of the month, and you will read about it in a newspaper, and I would rather this room had heard it from me first.*

"The clerk," said Beverley Annis. "You were in the gallery."

"I was in the gallery," said Jonas Mercer, "and a woman of twenty-two years read it out at ten past one and I was the only person in that room who had read all four of your documents, and that is why I am the one standing in your office, and it is not a qualification and I am not offering it as one."

She said, "Right. Come up. I have a meeting at half eleven and I am going to have it anyway, and you may sit in it."

---

There were five people in the room and four of them had been named on a form.

**Beverley Annis**, fifty-four, principal of the managing agents who run four hundred and ten flats in Tideglass and two other districts, twenty-six years in post, and she had made a cup of tea for everybody including him without asking which kind.

**Dorothy Cresswell**, sixty-three, clerk to the trustees of the Aldbrook Trust, nineteen years in post, and she had come with a lever-arch file and a plastic wallet and a lanyard from 2009 with nothing on it.

**Dorothy Alderman**, seventy-one, sole trustee of the Halliwell Amenities Fund, eleven years, retired school caretaker, and she had come in a coat because it was the only one she had, and she had brought a notebook with a rubber band round it and she wrote in it four times in fifty minutes.

**Ceri Vaughan**, forty-five, continuity counsel, who had come on her own time and had said so in about eight seconds and had asked for her job title to be written next to it, for the same reason Oren Pell had asked for his in a records office in June: *a registrar who says a thing in a room should have his title next to it, and a lawyer who says a thing in a room should have hers.*

And **Alden Rook**, who came at a quarter past ten and sat down without a folder, which Jonas Mercer noticed, and did not think about again for eleven weeks.

What they had in front of them was four documents, which is one placing and three assignments, and somebody — Beverley Annis, before Jonas arrived, from her own files — had laid them out in order with about a centimetre of space between each one, on the table, face up, in the order of the dates, with nothing on top of each other.

Nobody had been asked to do that. They had done it because it is what you do when a man in a corridor tells you that four documents in two buildings turn out to be one thing.

---

Jonas Mercer asked one question in that room and he had thought about it for three days and it was the fifth one he wanted and not the first.

He said it at about twenty past ten, after twenty minutes of four people being extremely correct at each other, and he said it to the room and not to anybody in it.

"Who entered the name in the field at that node."

And Beverley Annis said: "I did."

And there were about four seconds in which nobody said anything, and then she said:

"Third of June. Twenty past eleven in the morning. I did it in the office here with the file open on this desk and I did not do it on a Tuesday or in a hurry, and I have thought about it since Thursday morning and I am going to tell you why, and I would rather tell you than have it found out, and I am not frightened of you and I have not slept about it either.

"The trust assigned a share of that claim down to my firm at the end of May. Not to me. To the firm. It is the only continuity arrangement we have ever had and it arrived in a letter with a reference on it and no invoice, and I sent it to our solicitor and he wrote back four sentences which I can give you if you want them.

"And then on the third of June I went over a meter room in Salter's Yard with a torch, because we are going in on Tuesday to change the riser and I wanted to know what I was standing next to. **And there is a plate in that cupboard, at the bottom, under the stopcock, and it says the holder of this point pays for it and the point is not the payer's.** And it has been on that wall since before the war and nobody has ever mentioned it to me, and there is a lock on the cupboard and I have the key, and the key was where the tag said it would be.

"And I read that plate and I thought: that is right. That is exactly right. **Because the trust is not the point. The trust is not even in the borough. The point is a pipe in a basement in Salter's Yard and the point belongs to the people who live above it and the people who live above it are thirty-one households and they have a named steward and the steward is the person who holds the point.** So I entered the named steward of the building into the holder field on the assignment schedule, in her own name, because the assignment says the assignee takes the claim, and the claim at that node is the point, and the point is not the payer's.

"And I would like somebody to explain to me, in about nine seconds, in what part of that I have done a wrong thing, because I have read it four times and I cannot find it, and I have been a managing agent for twenty-six years and I have signed a hundred and forty leases a month and I am not careless, and this is the first time in twenty-six years that I have put a person's name into a field on a document because a plate on a wall told me to."

Nobody said anything for about eleven seconds, which in that room was a long time.

"That is the whole of it," said Beverley Annis. "I did not know. I did not ask. Nobody asked me to and if anybody had asked me I would have told them I was not sure, and I was not sure, and I did not say so."

---

Ceri Vaughan said, "Then I am going to say the next part, and I am going to say it because Mr Mercer is in the room and he has found it and it is going to be read out in a Committee before the end of August whether I say it in June or not, and I made one of those three assignments and I would like it known that I made it and I would like it known that I am not going to be asked about it, and if I am asked about it I will answer it correctly, and I have answered it correctly now so that nobody has to ask."

She put her hands flat on the table. She had a legal pad and no pen and she did not need one.

"Mr Annis did the last thing. What I am going to describe is the four things before it, and I want to describe them plainly, because I have read four hundred and sixty pages of this document in my working life — not that I have ever been asked to and not that I have ever wanted to, but I wrote the precedent notes on two of its schedules, which is a thing I did in 2011 for a fee and have not thought about since.

"**One.** On the fourth of March a party with a standing continuity authorisation placed a claim at Tier 3 on a node in this city, for a building in Kestrel Hill, at eleven thousand four hundred pounds a year, and signed it with a power of attorney and not with a name. That is the ordinary way. That is how it is done. That is the mechanism doing exactly what it was built to do and nobody has anything to hide.

"**Two.** On the twenty-first of March the whole of it was assigned to the freeholder. Freeholders buy these. There are about two hundred and ten of them in this country and about nine in this borough and the reasons are all ordinary: a building with a lift in it, a building with eleven residents in it, a building whose residents are all over eighty. **A freeholder with a private water supply has a legal duty to maintain continuity, and Schedule 4 gives him a way to do it for a fixed sum, and eleven thousand four hundred pounds a year for a building is not a rip-off, it is a budget line, and I have never in my life thought that a budget line was a crime.**

"**Three.** The Aldbrook Trust assigned one-half of its claim on the second of May to a client of mine. That is me. That is the one I made. It was perfectly proper, it was properly executed, the trust had authority, the consideration was fair, and I did not invent the share — the share was already there, and my client's trustee had read on page two hundred and nineteen, which is the page Mr Mercer read on Friday, that a share of a claim carries the tier in proportion, and had worked out, correctly, that a share of a Tier 3 claim is an asset that can be held by a party that could not otherwise afford a place in a precedence order. **And my client paid for it, and got it, and thought it was extremely good value, and it was.**"

Dorothy Alderman, who had written in her notebook four times, said: "I did think it was good value."

"You did," said Ceri Vaughan, kindly. "So the fourth thing is the fault, if anybody wants a fault, and there is not one and I am not going to manufacture one, because Mr Mercer did not come here to find a villain and if I give him one he will go away with it and it will be worth nothing in September."

"And the fourth thing," she said, "is Beverley Annis, and it is a plate."

---

Then she said the thing that is the chapter, and she said it slowly, and she had clearly been carrying it since Thursday.

"**What an assignment transfers is a claim. A claim is a duty. So an assignment transfers a duty — and here is what nobody in this city has noticed and what I have only just noticed myself, sitting in a car park at half past eight this morning.**

"A share of a claim carries the tier in proportion and nothing else. It does not carry a share of the *work*. Not one word of page two hundred and nineteen says that a share of a claim is a share of the maintenance. A share of a claim is a share of a *precedence*. It is a way of being at the front of a queue.

"So I have just watched four sensible people move one duty four times, and every one of them passed it on lighter than they got it, because what they passed on was a share of a *place*, and what they did not pass on was a single job of work — and the job of work at the bottom of that chain is keeping a water point in a basement in Salter's Yard alive, and **not one of the five people in this room, including me, has ever been given that job, and none of us is going to be, because none of us maintains it, and because the party that maintains it is a firm that invoices for a stopcock.**

"And I have been doing this for twenty-two years and I have never seen it before, and I want to be very careful with what I say next, because I am not a regulator and I do not have standing here and I am not going to claim that anybody has broken a rule.

"**Nobody in this chain has ever asked what happens when the person at the end says no.**"

She put her hands off the table.

"And the reason nobody asked is not an oversight. It is a design, and the design is page nine, and it is the best sentence in the document. A tier may be challenged by a service district or by a node in the same tier, and it may not be challenged by a person. So the four parties in this chain are all *parties*, and all four of them can be challenged, and all four of them have a solicitor and a register entry and a place in an order. And the person at the end of it is a person, and a person cannot be challenged, and a person cannot be required to do anything, and a person cannot be required even to be told. **And so nobody in the chain ever had to think about her, because thinking about her would have meant putting a question to somebody who has no way of refusing it in front of anybody.**

"And that is not a flaw. That is the sentence doing its work. It has been doing its work since the first of March, and it is the reason that a man with a torch could put a woman's name into a field from a sentence on a plate and be entirely correct, and it is the reason that the Committee on Thursday could read two answers about one meter room into a record in eleven seconds and be entirely correct, and it is the reason that the woman has been in that block for nineteen years and has never once been asked a question by a body.

"And I would like to say — because I have been a lawyer for twenty-two years and I have never once said anything like this in a room, and I have said it to a man from a register and not to a Committee — I would like to say that if you had put a *person* in the middle of this chain instead of at the end of it, the sentence at page nine would have stopped the whole of it dead in about four seconds, and that is not a reason to keep her at the end of it."

Nobody wrote anything down for about half a minute.

---

Alden Rook said, at about twenty to eleven, from a chair with no folder in it:

"I am going to say the commercial part and I am going to say it in one paragraph and I am not going to be asked a question about it, and then I am going to be quiet for the rest of the morning.

"A share of a fee is cheaper than a queue. That is the whole of it and it is not a defence of anything, it is a description of the market, and it has been the market since the first of March and it is the market because it works.

"A queue costs money. A queue is money that is not recoverable if the thing does not happen, and money that is not recoverable is a budget line that a treasurer will cut in a year, and a treasurer who cuts it in a year is a service that narrows, and a service that narrows is a complaint, and a complaint is a person in a room being angry, and the person in the room being angry is a cost that does not sit in any budget at all and never has.

"A share of a fee is eleven hundred pounds a year, in my client's case, in a district where she has eleven thousand pounds and no lift and a hall roof, and it puts her at the front of a precedence order she will probably never need. **She is right that it is good value. I would have told her it was good value, and I did, and I was right, and I would tell her again tomorrow.**

"And here is the part I want Mr Mercer to write down, because I think he will otherwise get this wrong and I would rather he got it right from me than wrong from a Committee paper.

"**The market is not the villain. The market did exactly what a market does. What the market cannot do — what no fee, at any tier, in any amount, under any schedule — is tell a person at the bottom of a chain that she is at the bottom of a chain. That is not a price. There is no price for it. And so the price gets bought and sold four times and the notice never goes out, and the woman finds out from a form.**

"And I want to say the other half, because I am not a monster and I have never pretended to be one, and because it is worse: **if you break this chain, you do not fix that. You remove the only party in the borough that can be asked a question about it, and you leave her standing at the bottom of nothing, holding a plate, and the next man who comes up that path will be worse than me, and he will not have a folder, and he will be very sure of himself.**"

Then he said: "That is the paragraph," and he did not say anything else for thirty-eight minutes.

---

At about half past eleven Beverley Annis said the thing that Jonas Mercer wrote down, and he wrote it down on the back of his hand because he had not brought a pad and had not intended to be the sort of man who came here with a pad.

"Then what do I do," she said. "Because I have four hundred and ten flats and thirty-one of them are in that building and on Friday I have to give somebody an instruction about a riser. And I entered a name in a field on the third of June and that name is a woman and I have never spoken to her, and she has not asked me anything, and if somebody rings me between now and Friday and says *is there a claim on that node*, I have to say yes, and I have to say whose, and I do not have a sentence for that and I have had two days to write one."

Nobody answered her, and that was the correct answer, and she sat with it for about four seconds and then said, "Right," and got up, and the meeting was over, and Dorothy Alderman said, on her way to the door, in the voice of a woman of seventy-one who has been a caretaker for forty years, "You will say *it was put in by the trust*, love, and then somebody will ask which trust, and then you will be in it for a fortnight." And Beverley Annis said, "Yes," and Dorothy Alderman said, "I have been in it for a fortnight twice," and went out.

---

**Monday the twenty-third of June, ten past four in the afternoon.**

He was behind the counter in the shop and the telephone went and it was Nina Calder, from the second floor of the civic offices, and she was not ringing to talk about a form.

She said nine sentences and they took about forty seconds and she had clearly written them out on a pad in front of her first, which she does.

"Two things and then I have to go to a meeting.

"One. **A woman telephoned the records counter at about a quarter past two this afternoon and asked whether her name is on a form.** She gave her name, which I am not going to say to you on a telephone, and she gave the address of a building, which is not an address that is on any of our records because that building has no civic address, and the clerk on the counter looked, because that is what the counter is for.

"Two. **The records office is required to tell her, and the counter told her, and my clerk did it in about four minutes and it was correct and it was thorough and I have read the note of the call and I have not added anything to it.** The note says — and this is the clerk's wording and I am not improving it — *advised the party that the field is populated with her name, that the claim is at Tier 3, that the claim was placed on 4 March and assigned four times, that the counterparty at the top of the chain is a trust in Kestrel Hill, that no party in the chain has confirmed that the party at the end was aware, and that she may withdraw the claim in her own name at the counter without notice to any other party and without any formality.*

"That last clause is in the 2008 regulations and it is section nine and I have known it was in there since 1992 and I have never once had a reason to use it and it has taken a woman of fifty-four with no civic address, nineteen years in a building, and a plate under a stopcock to use it for the first time in this city."

Jonas Mercer said, "Is she all right."

Nina Calder said, "She asked whether she was in trouble, and my clerk said no, and she said **good**, and then she asked for the claim reference so that she could write it down, and my clerk read it out, and she wrote it on the back of her hand, and then she asked whether anybody was going to come and tell her what it was, and my clerk said that the counter could not say and that the records office does not send anybody anywhere, and she said: *right.*"

"And then," said Nina Calder, "she said one more thing, and I am telling you because you are going to be in this and I would rather you heard it from me, and it is not mine. She said: *four people have told me about this building in a year and none of them were from the building.*"

The line went.

He stood behind the counter for about a minute. The shop was empty and it was raining and there was a roll of duct tape on the bench that Bea Osunde had cut and would not be told was wrong, and outside the window four miles away and on the wrong side of a lowland, in a building with no civic address, a woman of fifty-four was standing in a kitchen with a reference written on the back of her hand, and the chain was four links long and it ended in her, and every single link of it was valid.

And the only party who could break it was standing in a kitchen with a biro mark on her hand, and she had not been asked anything by anybody, and she was not going to be asked by a Committee, and a Committee had said so in public on the Thursday before last, in a minute, at about twelve o'clock, in words that were correct.

He did not telephone her. He had decided that on the bus on Friday and it had not changed, and it was not cowardice and it was not method, and it was the only thing he was sure of: **that if he rang her before Friday, then the four days before Friday would be four days in which she had been asked a question by a man from a register, and she would never afterwards be able to say, in a room, that nobody had ever asked her anything.**

So he did the shop, and he did the shop's other two jobs, and he wrote a shop delivery note, and at about half past five he wrote four lines on the back of a delivery note and put them in his coat pocket, and they were the only four lines he wrote that day.

> *Four assignments. Every one valid. Nobody lied. Nothing forged. A duty got lighter four times and nobody was ever given the work.*
> *The person at the end of it has found out from a form with a crest on it, in a records office, on a Monday afternoon, and she has written the reference on her hand.*
> *I am going to ask her one question on Friday, in daylight, and I am not going to ask her what it will cost, and I am not going to be the reason anybody in this city finds out that anybody was ever going to.*
> *And if she says yes — and she will not say yes to a man she has not spoken to, and I would not accept it if she did — then I am going to have to be the one who does not do it. That is the shape of it. I can see it from here and I have not walked up the path yet.*
