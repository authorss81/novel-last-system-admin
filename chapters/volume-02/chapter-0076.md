# Chapter 0076 — A Form With a Crest On It

On Thursday the twenty-eighth of May at about ten past nine in the morning Jonas Mercer was standing in the porch of the Old Ward with his bag at his feet, reading a line that had come up on the enamel strip at the frame of the notice board overnight, in the flat register, with a full stop after each word, and it was the fourth line in a fortnight and the only one of the four that he had not seen somewhere in a book first.

> **A PARTY THAT HAS BEEN ASKED TO CONFIRM OCCUPANCY HAS BEEN ASKED FOR A STATE AND NOT FOR A LIST, AND A PARTY THAT ANSWERS WITH A LIST HAS ANSWERED A DIFFERENT QUESTION.**

He stood and read it twice, and then he went down the ramp and across to the records office on the ground floor, because Nina Calder was in on a Thursday and because the two forms were in the same building and he wanted to see them on the same table.

They were on the same table by half past ten, and Nina Calder had laid them out with a rule between them, and she had done that at about eight o'clock before he arrived, because she is a records supervisor of nineteen years and there is no version of this in which you bring two documents to a records office and do not first put a straight edge down the middle of the table.

“Left,” she said. “That one is the borough’s. Crest, form number, and the return address is a customer services team on the ground floor of the building you have just walked out of, and it came off the heat protocol on Monday morning and it is on the counter in a plastic wallet with twenty-five other things in it. Right. That one is mine. It has my form number and my form number is two digits lower than theirs because I printed it in a hurry in a basement and Ellis Fenn gave it to me with the lid still off.

“Now tell me why I have brought them into one room, because I did not bring them into one room for a nice reason.”

“Because they are the same instrument arriving twice,” said Jonas Mercer.

“They are **not**,” said Nina Calder, “and if you go into any room in this city in September and say that, you will be wrong in a room in front of about nine people, and I am not going to have said it.

“Their instrument asks a question. Mine asks a question. Neither instrument has a power to make anybody answer, and that is the only thing they have in common, and if you build a case on that you have built it on the absence of a power, which is the single most reliable thing in this city and I have been relying on it since 1974.

“Here is what they are. This one — ” she put a finger on the left-hand sheet, “— is a request, under a heat threshold, to a premises, and it wants two things, and the two things are in two boxes, and the boxes are labelled *number of occupied addresses in your custody* and *named responsible person for each address*.

“And this one — ” the finger moved, “— is a return, on a form that nobody is required to answer, about a node, and it wants a state, and a date, and a party that answers, and a blank rule at the bottom.

“And the thing I have had since Wednesday morning, and the reason I put the rule down, is that **their first box is a number box.** It is not a word box. It is not a state box. It says *number*, and it has a line under it the width of a ruler, and whoever laid that form out for the printer did not put an upper limit on it, and a box with a line in it and no ceiling in it is the most dangerous object in the civic office, and I have had a records office for nineteen years and I have watched two hundred men fill one in and eleven of them put *all* in it.

“**They have asked a landlord for a state, and given her a box that will only take a list, and if she answers with a number she has answered a different question, and her answer will be read as though it were the answer to theirs.**”

---

The thing that made it a fact rather than an argument was on the shelf.

Nina Calder pulled the folder of answered returns off the shelf above the printer and put the twentieth of May on the table, and it was the fourth return, and on the ninth line, in her own handwriting, in biro, at the bottom of the ninth line and not at the bottom of the page, there was a word.

Jonas Mercer read it and did not say anything about where the word came from or who had said it first, because it was on a form with a date on it and it was a week old and it was hers, and there was nothing on that page that was his.

“Do you want to say the date on it out loud,” said Nina Calder, “because that is a habit you have and I would rather you did it in here where I can hear it.”

“Tuesday the twentieth of May. The fourth return. Cooper's Reach clinic line. And *nobody*, in your hand, on the ninth line, where the form has a gap.”

“Good. Now here is the thing I have been chewing on for four days and I am going to say it to a technician because there is nobody else in this building who can be trusted with it.

“On the nineteenth of May a chair made a determination and one line of it was that where the answer to *who answers for it* is no party, that is to be recorded as no party, in those words, and read into the record as no party. And I have had the word on a form in my own hand for eight days and it is in a gallery transcript and it is not in a minute yet, because there is no next sitting, and I want you to understand what that means practically and not philosophically, because you are not going to enjoy the philosophical version.

“**It means that for eight days this city has had a public obligation with no date on it.** The chair said *by the next sitting*. There is no next sitting. Nobody has set one. So a determination that requires every party holding continuity in this city to answer a form, in public, on a form, by a date that does not exist, is not a thing with a deadline. It is a thing that is now in the room, in everybody’s post, and nobody has to receive it, and nobody can fail to receive it, and the obligation is discharged — when it is discharged — by the act of answering and not by anybody receiving the answers.

“Nobody has to be in a room. A clerk does not have to sit in a room with a table and a stack. The answers arrive at a counter. And every one of them is a document that has been made public by being answered, and there are nine on the board and I put the ninth one there myself, on my own, on the Saturday night, with a drawing pin, and I have never told anybody in this building that I did it, and it is a piece of paper in the rain under a canopy.”

She squared the two forms against the rule.

“That is the same sentence in the two forms, and neither of them has a date, and I have been trying since Monday to work out whether that is a fault in the borough’s drafting or a fault in mine, and I have got to the bottom of it this morning and the answer is that it is not a fault in either, it is the **absence** of a fault, and I want it in writing somewhere and I have decided the writing is going to be in my pad and not on a plate, because I am not putting a sentence of mine on a plate in this city and I would like somebody to notice that I have just declined to do a thing I have done four times in a fortnight.”

---

Marguerite Oyelaran had the protocol form face up on the desk in the two rooms above the betting shop on Thursday the twenty-eighth of May at about half past two in the afternoon, and it had been face up since Monday, and the window was open four inches and there was a piece of card in it.

“I am not filling it in,” she said.

“You have had four days.”

“I have had four days and I have not filled it in and I am not going to, and I want to tell you why in about ninety seconds so that you can go and do something else with your face on.

“Two boxes. **Number of occupied addresses.** And **named responsible person for each address.** And the first one wants a number, and the second one wants a hundred and six names — not a hundred and six, I will give them a number, it wants a named person for every address, and there are a hundred and twenty-eight addresses and I am not going to put a hundred and twenty-eight people’s names on a document with a crest on it that I did not ask for, in the ninth week of a year in which I have already put a hundred and twenty-two consent marks through a hundred and twenty-two letterboxes.

“And if I put one name in that box then I have put a name in a holder field, and you and I stood in a corridor on a yard in May and I said no to six of them in nine seconds, and I have not stopped saying no, and I have also not stopped putting names on things, and I would like somebody to explain to me in one sentence how a woman is allowed to do the second thing and not the first thing, because if the answer is *it depends what the form is for*, then the form decides, and the form is not mine.”

“The answer to your question is that both of those are correct and the difference between them is who you are frightened of,” said Jonas Mercer. “I have not got a sentence for that. I have got a Tuesday.”

“Then go and have your Tuesday,” said Marguerite Oyelaran, and picked up the telephone, because it was twenty to three and the telephone runs until three.

---

The person who did it was **Denise Hoyle**, and she is sixty-one, and she has done the letters and the arrears on a Tuesday and a Friday for six years in two rooms above a betting shop, and she is not an officer, and she has never been in a room, and her name is not on the letterhead, and on the Friday morning she did not know for about a day and a half that anything had happened.

What happened is this. On Thursday the twenty-eighth of May at about four o'clock in the afternoon, the form came back through the internal post with the other post, because the internal post goes down at four and does the round of the offices and the offices are all on this road. It was in a plastic wallet with twenty-five other things in it, on the twenty-sixth, and it was still in the wallet on Thursday at four o'clock, and Denise Hoyle opened the wallet because she opens the wallets.

There is a form number on it and a crest and a return address and a date, and the return address is a counter on the ground floor of a building in the Old Ward, and there is a printed line at the bottom of the left-hand column that says **RETURN BY [DATE] — THIS FORM IS A PUBLIC DOCUMENT ONCE COMPLETED**, and Denise Hoyle has opened about four hundred plastic wallets in six years and she is extremely good at what is in them and she is not a fool.

She did not fill in the borough's box. The borough's box is not hers; there is no number in the association's records that is a number of *occupied* addresses, because the association keeps a rent roll, and a rent roll counts rooms that are let and rooms that are not let, and it does not count where anybody sleeps, and she is sixty-one and has worked in two rooms above a shop since 1988 and she knows the difference better than anybody who has not.

She filled in two things and she filled them in the correct order and she filled them in with a biro.

In the first box: **106**.

In the second box, which wanted a named responsible person for each address, she did not write a hundred and twenty-eight names. She wrote, in the space provided, on the line under the label, the words:

**M. OYELARAN — CHIEF OFFICER — CARROW LANE HA — BY POST AND TELEPHONE**

and she put the ring number in the box marked *telephone*, because the ring number is on the letterhead and she has rung it four hundred times.

And then she put the wallet back on the shelf, at about twenty past four, and she went home at half past four, and on the Friday morning at twenty past nine she had a stack of arrears letters to take to the post office and she was not thinking about a plastic wallet at all.

---

The telephone rang at twenty past nine on Friday the twenty-ninth of May and it was the customer services team on the ground floor of a building in the Old Ward, and the woman on the end of it was being extremely helpful and had read out a figure four times.

“Your occupancy figure has been recorded as one hundred and six,” she said, “which we have received, thank you, and it is a public document now. There is one query I have to raise before it goes on the file, and it is not a complaint. Our housing data for the same four blocks is one hundred and twenty-two on the rent roll. So we have a difference of sixteen and I have to ask which sixteen, because we have a form that says one hundred and six and a rent roll that says one hundred and twenty-two and the two of them have to be the same set of addresses.”

Marguerite Oyelaran listened to the whole of it standing up, with the telephone in her hand, with the queue in the hall downstairs already building, and she answered the first question, and then she said: “I am not answering the second one this morning, and I want you to write down that I have said so, and I want you to write down the reason as well.

“The sixteen is sixteen rooms on my rent roll with nobody living in them, and the reason they are on the roll and not off it is that a tenancy ends on a date and a date is not something anybody has money to bring forward, and I could have ended ten of them in April if I had had four hundred pounds and I did not have four hundred pounds, and that is the whole of it and it is not anybody’s conspiracy, it is my rent book.

“I am not giving you the other fourteen and I am not giving you a list, and you may not have a list, and if your form needs one then your form needs something this city has not got.”

“We are not asking for a list. We are asking for the figure.”

“You are asking for a figure and I have given you a figure, and the figure is one hundred and six, and the number you are holding now is one hundred and twenty-two, and I would like you to understand in your own office what those two numbers are, because I have understood it in mine and I have had four days.

“A hundred and twenty-eight rooms. A hundred and twenty-two of them on a rent roll, because six households in four blocks have never signed anything and have never been collected from and have never been chased, and two of those six have told me to my face that they will not sign a thing that makes my name a door. A hundred and six of the hundred and twenty-eight have somebody living in them, because twenty-two households have moved out since the twenty-sixth of April.

“So the only difference your two numbers show anybody is **sixteen**. Sixteen rooms on a rent roll with nobody in them. And I will tell you exactly what sixteen rooms in a public document is, because I have had four days and I have thought about nothing else: it is a void. It is a landlord's exposure figure. It is what a funder, an inspector, a buyer or a landlord's solicitor takes out of your system in about four seconds and puts at the top of a page with a red line under it, and it reads as bad management, **and it is bad management, and it is the least of what is in those three numbers.**

“The number the association is actually paid for is ninety-two, not one hundred and twenty-two, because Block C has been on a void allowance since the sixth of May and has collected nothing, and the shortfall is four thousand three hundred and sixty-one pounds and sixty, and that figure is not on your form and is not going on your form and is not anybody else's business.

“Now here is the half I am actually going to say to you, and I would like it written down, because I have worked it out since Friday morning and it is the worst thing I have learned in eleven years and I have learned it about my own four blocks.

“One hundred and six, minus ninety-two, is fourteen. **Eight of those fourteen are the eight households still living in a block that has had no water since the sixth of May, who have been carrying it in buckets for thirty rooms since 1998, and six of those fourteen are the six who never signed.** Those two numbers are the only two numbers in this whole business that anybody in this borough would be able to recognise as hardship, and they are the only two numbers that are not on anything, and they cannot be, because every document that could hold them is a list of who does not pay.

“**So the legible difference is sixteen, and sixteen is void, and void is my fault. And the illegible difference is fourteen, and fourteen is eight people with a bucket and six people with a door, and that is nobody's fault at all, and there is no instrument in this city that can say so.**”

There was a pause of about four seconds on the line.

“I am obliged to say that the form has already been recorded,” the woman said. “It came in yesterday afternoon. It is a public document on completion. I cannot take it back. I can query it, which I have done, and I can note your answer, which I have done, and the query and the answer both go on the file.”

“Thank you,” said Marguerite Oyelaran.

She put the telephone down and sat in the chair she had been sitting in for eleven years with a queue of eleven people in a corridor two rooms below her and a Saturday morning coming and a form in a plastic wallet on a shelf.

**And the afternoon she had left was the afternoon.** Because the thing she had been going to do on Friday the twenty-ninth of May was a block meeting in a hall on Carrow Lane, and about forty people were going to be in it, and it was the first meeting since the eighth of May, and the eleven households in the hostel had asked for it, and she had told them on the Wednesday morning that it would be Saturday.

And she spent Friday morning doing the thing that had arrived, which was telephoning four households at about five past ten, and two of them were not answering, and one of them asked her — pleasantly, almost kindly, in the way people are kind when they are putting you somewhere — *is there a problem with my account, Marguerite*, and she said no, and she went back to the queue, and she did not tell the six, because telling the six is making a list, and she has refused to make a list for eleven years and she was not going to start because a woman in the Old Ward was being helpful.

---

And on the Friday morning at about eleven o'clock, in a room in Gantry Ward, a man with nine hundred words of a report in it was going to have to file the report on a piece of ground he could not reach, and Jonas Mercer was going to be in the lowland chamber at eleven o'clock because Emeka Rusk had rung him at ten past eight and said the screens were worse and he wanted somebody in there who was not him.

Which he was. And he went, and did not ask, and did not say that he was going, and did not say that he would not be able to come out of the chamber before half past twelve. Which was the arrangement, and it had been made on the Wednesday afternoon in about nine words on a telephone, and neither of them had said out loud at the time that he was arranging it, and neither of them says so now.
