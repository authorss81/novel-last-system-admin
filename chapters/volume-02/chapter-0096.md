# Chapter 0096 — The September Report

**Friday the fifth of September 2014, half past nine in the morning, a first-floor room above a building society on Hollow Bank.**

The proof had come back on Wednesday. It had come back on Wednesday because Gareth Raynor-Hale, about fifty, resilience officer, two years and four months in post, lanyard with a plastic sleeve replaced twice, had gone to print on the thirty-first of July, five weeks before the month the terms of reference say the report is written in, and had then put it in a drawer, and had taken it out on Wednesday and put it on a table in front of a man with a pad.

"There is one thing wrong with it and it is the thing I told you about in July," he said. "And I have been wrong about it for eighteen weeks and I have had a printer's proof in a drawer since the thirty-first of July, which is the worst sentence I have ever said in a room."

He turned it round.

> **Saint Orra and District Flood Forum — quarterly report, September 2014, section 4.**
>
> **lowland evacuation capacity: unresolved**
>
> **State: unresolved. As at 4 September 2014 there is no party in this borough who answers for the lowland crossing and there has not been one since 6 April 2014. No party has been asked for a figure and no figure has been supplied by anybody, including this officer.**
>
> **Source of the only figure that exists about the lowland, stated here in full because it is a public document and a public document cannot hold a thing without saying where it came from: a private record kept for nineteen years by a person who lives at the barrier end, in pencil, in a book. Nine hundred and forty marks. It is a count of occasions and it is not a count of persons. The number of persons is not on the page and has never been worked out and will not be.**
>
> **Next state: not before 31 December 2014.**

"There," said Raynor-Hale. "**That is a state and a date and a source in one paragraph, and it is the first time in four quarterly reports that that section has had anything under it at all, and the reason it has anything under it is that you said the first two lines of it out loud in this room about ten minutes ago and I typed them out and I did not improve them.**"

"I did not write the first two lines."

"You dictated the first two lines and I wrote them and you told me to say *as at the fourth of September* because you are a man who dates things and I have never known you to be wrong about a date, and I have never in two years and four months had a source for a figure in a report before and it is the best paragraph I have ever put in one of these and it has been in a drawer since the thirty-first of July."

Then he said the thing he had been sitting on, which was the price, and he said it before Jonas Mercer could say anything at all.

"**I am going to say the price and I have known it since Wednesday and I have had three days to decide whether to run it and I am going to run it.**

"Nora Bell is a name on my membership page. She is in the tenth place and she has been to four meetings of eleven and she has spoken twice and both times she has said a number nobody asked her for and said where it came from, in the room, for the minutes. **That paragraph is going to put her in a document that goes to the Assembly and is public in September, and it is going to say that the source of the only number we have about the lowland is a woman who lives at the barrier end, and in about two years somebody is going to go and look at the book.** Not her. The book. I have thought about it for three days and I am going to do it anyway, because the alternative is a fourth consecutive quarter with *unresolved* and nothing under it and this forum gets put in a review in November, and I have watched four things get reviewed in this borough this year and three of them got worse for the people in them.

"And I want it minuted that I did it anyway, and I am not going to pretend it was forced on me by a man with a register, and I am not going to be in a position in about two years where a member says *you were told by somebody in July*, because **you did not tell me to do it. I had the number in May and I had it in July and I had it on the thirty-first of July when I sent it to print, and the only reason it is not in that drawer is a state, and the state is yours, and I have decided to keep you out of it in the minutes and to put my own name on it instead, and you are not going to argue with me about that.**

"You are going to argue with me about it," he said, "and I am going to say the same thing again."

"Then say it again."

"**A public report that says *unresolved* for four quarters running is a document about nothing. It is the only sentence anybody in this city has produced about the lowland since the twenty-eighth of April and it is on a form in a drawer, and it is the reason this forum is not in front of anybody.**"

---

**Friday the fifth of September 2014, about eleven in the morning, the vestibule of the Old Ward, ground floor.**

Under the register of narrowings, on the enamel strip where the old lines went, there was a new one, printed in the flat register, with no crest and no form number and nothing under it.

> **WHERE A CONSENT MAY BE GIVEN, AND MAY BE GIVEN AGAIN, THE RECORD OF THE SECOND CONSENT IS NOT EVIDENCE THAT THE FIRST WAS WITHDRAWN, AND THE PARTY THAT TOOK THE FIRST CONSENT MAY RELY ON IT UNLESS THE RECORD SAYS OTHERWISE.**

Jonas Mercer read it four times standing up in front of it. **It is not signed. It is not dated. It is not on the Lattice and it is not on the city's paper and nobody in this building has admitted to putting it there**, and he has spent five months learning that a plate nobody can claim is either a fault or the most expensive thing in the city.

There were twenty-six days to Wednesday the first of October. He did not say it out loud in that room and he did not take his pad out, and he went up the stairs and out and down to the shop.

---

**Saturday the sixth of September 2014, twenty past two in the afternoon, the barrier end, the lowland embankment.**

He went on foot and it is about fifty minutes from the shop and he went for no reason he could have put in a document, which is the only reason anybody goes.

The barrier end is where the embankment footpath comes up to a gate, and the gate is where a person stands who wants to get to the other side and cannot, and there is a rail on one side and a wall on the other and about eleven feet between them, and a woman of about sixty has been coming to the end of that wall for nineteen years and has kept a book in pencil.

There is a ledge, and a lip of concrete at the end of it, about a foot above the path, worn pale, and there are pencil marks on the lip going back and forth and they have been washed out and made again so often that the concrete under them is going.

**The book is not here. The book is at an address about a mile from here and it is in a hand that is not anybody else's, and it has been kept since about 1995, and the marks on the lip are what happens when you have carried a thing in your coat and put it down on the only flat thing there is and cannot find your pen, and then found your pen, and then did it again.**

**Nine hundred and forty. Times, not people, and the number of people is not on the page and has never been worked out, because if it were worked out then it would be a number of people.**

He stood there for about twenty minutes and nobody came and nothing crossed and a bus went past on the far side.

Then he walked back, and on the way he worked out the thing he had actually come out to work out, which was that the paragraph in Raynor-Hale's report said *a person who lives at the barrier end*, and that a person who lives at the barrier end is a person whose address is a ledge, and that this borough has never had a civic address for a ledge, and that a resilience officer in a first-floor room above a building society has just put nine hundred and forty pencil marks on a concrete lip into a public document in the exact words that let anybody who reads it find the ledge.

He had known that on Wednesday. He had known it on Wednesday at about half past three with the proof in front of him and had said nothing, and had typed nothing, and had dictated two lines about a state and said nothing else, and had let the other four lines of a paragraph stand because Raynor-Hale had written them himself.

That was the whole of his part in it. It had taken eleven seconds.

---

**Monday the eighth of September 2014, twenty to seven in the morning, the bottom of Quarry Street.**

Nine households on about a hundred yards with containers, and one of them was a woman of thirty-four with a folding trolley and two bottles and a crate, on her way to a shift at the sorting office that starts at seven, and Jonas Mercer stood at the wall opposite because that is what he has done three times.

She had seen him twice before. On the first of July it had rained. On the second time he had stood there for twenty minutes and said nothing, and she had not said anything either, and they had both found out the other one was not going to.

She stopped with the trolley.

"You are the one who cost me the day and the seven hours."

"Yes."

"**I know it was you, because a man came and stood over that plate in the rain and did not say a word to anybody, and there was one of those, and afterwards the water was there again and the day before it was not.**" She moved the crate onto the trolley. "And I have got a shift, and you have not got a reason to be here at twenty to seven, so I am going to say one thing and then I am going to go to work.

"There is no date on me. There is no date on this stand. There is a plate on that post that says what a place is for and it does not have a name on it and there has never been a name on it in eleven years, and I know what happens when a name goes on a plate. I have watched a woman in a cardigan put a name in a field in a cupboard in June because she read a plate with a torch, and she had to ring a counter to find out what she had done.

"**So I am not going to tell you my name and you are not going to ask me, and if anybody ever writes a piece of paper about this stand there is going to be a date on it and no person on it, and that is the only deal there is and I have been keeping to it for eleven years with nobody helping me.**"

"I have not got a piece of paper about this stand," said Jonas Mercer.

"No," said the woman. "You have got a face. I have seen it three times." She pushed the trolley off. "Go and do your Monday."

He wrote nothing down that day or the next. He had a piece of paper in his coat and a pencil in his pocket and the day was Monday and there was a quarterly report going on a table at ten o'clock, and a woman of thirty-four went to a shift at seven with a trolley, and there is no date and no person on a stand at the bottom of Quarry Street, and a man who has been there three times has never once had a name and did not ask.

---

**Monday the eighth of September 2014, ten in the morning, a first-floor room above a building society on Hollow Bank.**

The Saint Orra and District Flood Forum tabled the September quarterly at ten o'clock, and Councillor Martyn Craddock chaired it, forty-four, longest-serving member since March, and the roll went round and the eleventh place was still empty and the line still said *seat* and nobody wrote *vacant* and nobody had stopped saying it either, because Gareth Raynor-Hale had been told to put it back and had and had stopped trying to improve a line on a piece of paper.

The report went round. Eleven plastic sleeves and a ring binder and a laminated card with a plastic sleeve that has been replaced twice, and eleven people who turn up, and about fourteen who turned up for a quarterly.

**In section 4, under `lowland evacuation capacity: unresolved`, there were four sentences and a source.**

Somebody read out the second of them, which was the sentence about no party having answered for the lowland since the sixth of April, and then somebody read out the third, which was the one about a person who lives at the barrier end, and the room did the thing rooms do.

**Nora Bell said the sentence.** She is about sixty and has a cardigan on and a pencil behind her ear and she has been to four meetings of eleven, and she did not wait to be asked, because a member of that forum has now asked her twice, which she has told him about both times.

"**You are all going to ask me for the number of people.** And I am going to say the same thing I said in May, and I would like it in the minutes in my words and not in yours, and it is the only sentence she has ever said in this room.

"**Because if I work it out then it is a number of people.**"

The chair said it would go in the minutes in her words and it did.

And then a member asked her whether the figure could be used to work out a cost per head for a barrier scheme, which is the single most useful thing anybody has ever wanted to do with nine hundred and forty pencil marks, and Nora Bell said no, and gave the reason, and she said the same sentence twice, because the first time nobody had written it down.

The chair wrote the answer into the minute in her words and then wrote the member's question underneath it, and the two sentences are about four lines apart in a document that goes to the Assembly, and nobody in the room moved the second one up to meet the first one, and that is the whole of the governance of this borough in September.

And there was one more thing in that room and it was in the file and Jonas Mercer had put it there in a stairwell in April and had said it out loud in a corridor in July and had not telephoned the man it belonged to.

> **4.3** The empty-crossing clause at the lowland crossing is contested by the operator and no other party. The resilience officer has sought a second question and been refused by the operator on grounds he has given and would give again. No party other than the operator is affected by the contest and no party other than the operator is engaged in it.

**Dev Singh was in that room.** He is a dispatcher and he has been for eleven years and he has a daughter and he is not a councillor and he is not on the forum, and he came to a quarterly because there is a laminated card that gets him into about nine buildings and he came to this one and he sat at the back and he did not speak.

Jonas Mercer was at the back on the other side of the room with nothing in his hands and did not go to him, and there is a rule between two men from May and neither of them is going to be the first to break it, and it is not a rule about the eleventh of October any more, it is a rule about a stairwell in April.

And Raynor-Hale had written it in his own words, which are worse for Dev Singh and better for the report, and Jonas Mercer had asked for that in July and had been refused, and it had gone to print on the thirty-first of July.

---

**Monday the eighth of September 2014, twenty past twelve in the afternoon, the corridor outside committee room two.**

"Three days ago a man with a laminated card put a paragraph in a public report about a woman who lives at the end of a wall," said Jonas Mercer, "and the woman did not mind and the officer took the blame for it, and neither of those is the thing I want to ask about."

"Then ask."

"**What happens on the first of October.**"

Raynor-Hale stopped walking.

"No."

"You asked me in July what I would do about it and I told you I did not know, and I still do not know, and I have not known since the eighth of July and there is a man in a shop who asked me the same question in the same week and neither of us will discuss it in a shop." He put his hand flat on the wall, which is a thing he does not do and which Jonas Mercer has never once seen him do. "**And you have a report going out and a review in November, and a date on a wall in Kestrel Hill, and I am going to say four facts and I am not going to give you an opinion and you are not going to give me one.**

"**One: a notice was served at eleven o'clock on the third of July on five parties and a determination made eleven days ago did not touch it and does not need to and cannot. Two: the placing party has not noticed. Three: the only person in that chain who has spoken to anybody about any of it is a clerk of sixty-three with a lever-arch file, and she has answered a machine once and a person once, and she will do her job with a notice in October, and her doing her job is the whole of the danger. Four: the clause says the placing party may re-assert without the consent of any person whose name appears in the holder field of that node at any time before that date, and the holder field at that node has had a person in it since the third of June and that person was told in the same week by a party, and she consented to a withdrawal on the third of July, and she is findable, and nobody can be asked to be told anything.**

"**And the fifth thing, which is not a fact and which I am giving you because you put a ledge in a public document: a consent can be given twice, and the record of the second consent is not evidence that the first was withdrawn, and a party that took the first consent may rely on it.** That is on a strip in a room in the Old Ward and it is unsigned on its face and undated, and somebody has written two initials and one word on the back of it in pencil, and *not mine* is the word, and I am not going to tell you whose it is, and if you work it out you will not write it down."

He looked at the wall.

"**Twenty-three days, Mr Mercer. I have a review in November and a report going out and a ledge in a document and I have just had four facts and a fifth thing put in front of me with no question on the end of them and I have got a laminated card that gets me into about nine buildings and one of them I should not have been in.**"

---

**Monday the eighth of September 2014, ten past four in the afternoon, the shop.**

The proof was on the table in the room above the building society and the report was going to a print run on the ninth and there was a paragraph in section 4 with a woman's surname in it, and he had put two lines of it there on Friday morning and had known what the other four said since Wednesday afternoon, and he had said nothing, and it had taken him eleven seconds to say nothing.

Bea Osunde came through with a length of ash on her shoulder and put it on the bench and looked at him.

"You've done the thing where you go quiet for a week and then write a lot."

"I have."

"Any good?"

"**A state and a date and a source, in one paragraph, in a document that goes to the Assembly, and it is the only honest paragraph in it, and it has put a woman of about sixty and a book she has kept for nineteen years into a public document, and she knew before it went and she is not in the room and she has not asked to be.**" He put his hand flat on the bench, which is what he does, and which is the only thing about him that Bea Osunde has never once asked him to stop doing. "And a chief officer put ninety-two in a ring-number box on the Monday morning on purpose, and the difference between her two public numbers is a number and not a household, and there is no instrument in this city that can turn a difference into a household, and that is the only reason nobody is going to be hurt by it this week, and it is also the reason somebody is going to be hurt by it in about four months and I could not tell you who and neither could she."

"That's twice this week," said Bea Osunde, "that you've told me a thing about a person in this borough and then stopped before the name. In April it was three times in a day."

"I know."

"Then the names are the work, Mr Mercer, and you have not started, and I have watched you since the fourth of April, and I would rather you did it badly in a hurry than well in a year." She picked the ash up off the bench. "**And I am not going to be standing in this shop in a year saying that you were careful.**"

She went out to the machine. He stood in the doorway for a minute with twenty-three days in front of him and did not take the pad out, and on the wall behind the bench, where a previous tenant of this shop had pencilled a date and never rubbed it out, there was a second date about two inches away from it, and neither of them had anything to do with a price.
