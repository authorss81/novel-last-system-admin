# Chapter 0063 — The Saturday Decision

He was in the yard at River Stacks from about half past eight on the morning of Saturday the tenth of May, and he did four things that took him until one o'clock, and all four of them were ordinary, and he chose every one of them.

He rebolted the third bracket on the cabinet in the corner where the shop's own riser is, which has been loose since February. He put a new edge on the workbench lip with a file and a piece of emery, standing up, because it catches people's coats. He took eleven readings off the Saint Orra bridge west-lane relay through the access hatch, logged all eleven in the shop book in his left hand, and closed the hatch and wrote the date on it, which is not required and which he does anyway. He made two cups of tea at about eleven and took one of them to Bea Osunde at the bench and did not say anything for the best part of three quarters of an hour.

Bea Osunde looked up at about twenty to twelve.

“You have been in this yard for three and a half hours doing work.”

“Yes.”

“You do not do work in this yard on a Saturday. You go to places. You have been in a car for nineteen days going to places where a person is standing.” She put the plate down flat. “It is a quarter past twelve. Whatever it is that you are not doing, you are doing it with a spanner, and I want to say that I have watched you do that before and it has never once worked, and I am not going to be the one who says the sentence this time because I have said it.”

“There is a meeting at ten in his office,” said Jonas Mercer. “Four nodes, four thousand two hundred pounds, four documents, and he asked me on the twenty-first of April which node was worth it and I said no in about ninety seconds, and he has told me twice since then that I am not to be in the room, and this morning I am in this yard with a spanner and I am going to stay in this yard with the spanner until it is over, and if he rings me I am not going to answer it between ten and eleven.”

“That is the correct decision,” said Bea Osunde, “and I want you to note that I have not been told about it and I have worked it out from a spanner, and that is the fourth time this year.”

He did not telephone the shop. He did not go up the Old Ward. He did not go to the lowland, because the lowland was where he went when he could not be somewhere else, and he was somewhere else. He sat on the crate at about ten past one and listened to the rain not come and thought, at intervals, about a gate.

He did not get up.

---

He rang the shop at about ten past two and Nina Calder answered it and said, in a voice with no preamble in it whatsoever, “Do not ask me anything. I have been on the telephone since half past ten and I have four things and I will tell you all four and then I will hang up.”

“All right.”

“One. **Three of the four are not services.** A staff stair is not a service. A car park barrier is not a service. The riser is a wet riser that serves one room on the ground floor of the west site and there is a man in the estates office who has written on a piece of paper in a hand you could park a lorry in — *this is a pipe in a building* — and who has removed it from the list himself, this morning, under his own holding, and it is gone, and nobody had to agree to it because the schedule says the party holding the maintenance may take a node off a schedule as well as put one on, and the estates man holds the maintenance of a wet riser because a wet riser is a pipe, and the moment somebody asked the question it took nine minutes.

“Two. The gate goes on it. Tier two, four thousand two hundred pounds, six forty-four, the mortuary van gate, effective from the eleventh of May, and the term runs to the twelfth of October at six in the morning, and the twelfth of October is a Sunday, and I did not know that was a Sunday until this morning and neither did he.

“Three. And this is the one you are going to want and I am going to say it once. The care entrance plate printed at about twenty past twelve today and it has a line on it in the flat register and the line is that six forty-one **is on a continuity schedule at Tier 2** — and there is no field on that plate for a schedule, and there is no field on that plate for a tier, and there has never been a field on that plate for either of those things since 2011, because the care entrance was never a node in failure and was never meant to be scheduled, and the plate has got five fields and a cursor and one of the five is *holder* and none of them is money.

“So the door of a hospital is inside a priced thing and the door does not have anywhere to keep the price, and Halvorsen has not been billed for it and does not know who will be, and the plate cannot say, and I have been in that corridor since half past one and I have asked Mina Okafor twice and she has asked twice and neither of us has got an answer and the clerk from the trust’s finance side has said *it will fall out of the year end* and that is the entire extent of the knowledge in the building.

“Four.” A pause. “That is three and a half hours of my Thursday. You did not ask me a single question and I have told you four things and I have not told anybody any of them, and one of them is the reason that man has not spoken to me since March, and I am going to hang up now, and I would like it noted that you were in a yard with a spanner when I told you.”

---

The plate on the care entrance in the middle of the Saint Orra west corridor printed at about twenty past twelve on Saturday the tenth of May and Jonas Mercer read it standing in front of it at about half past three, with Mina Okafor on one side of him and about forty people in a corridor that is a service and not a room, going past in both directions at the rate of a hospital goes past.

The five lines were still there. They are always there.

> **NO UNATTENDED ACCESS — WEST CLINIC CARE ENTRANCE**
> **HOLDER — the named watch or care worker on duty. WITNESS — two named people at every handover, read aloud.**
> **IF NO HOLDER IS ON DUTY: THE ROUTE IS CLOSED. An emergency opening must be declared in writing by the named on-call clinician, entered in the log, and written on the public board.**
> **TERM — from the first service window at 07:00 to 06:00 six days later. No self-carry-forward.**
> **CLAIM — the issuer maintains this until a named successor steward accepts that maintenance, or the term expires. UNAIDED. UNPAID.**

And under it, in a sixth position, in the same flat register, with a full stop after each word, one line, and it was not a fault notice and it was not a fault, and it read:

> **THIS NODE IS ON A CONTINUITY SCHEDULE. TIER 2. THE SCHEDULE EXPIRES 06:00, SUNDAY 12 OCTOBER 2014.**

Mina Okafor read it over his shoulder and did not say anything for about four seconds, and then she said:

“That is a door.”

“That is a door.”

“That is a door with a tier on it and an expiry on it and there is no field on this plate for a tier, and I have been looking at this plate every night since the second of April, which is thirty-nine nights of them, and I have read every one of the notices that have come out of it, and in five weeks nothing has ever appeared in a field that was not already on the plate with a cursor in it.” She put her hand on the wall. “Mr Mercer, I have worked in this building for nine years and I have never in nine years seen a thing get put on a plate that the plate had not got a place for.”

“It is not a field.”

“Then where did it come from,” said Mina Okafor, “and who put it there, and when was it put there, and is it in a file, and if it is in a file then which file, and if it is not in a file then in what is it, and I have asked twice and I have been told *it will fall out of the year end* and I would like to write *it will fall out of the year end* in the box, and I would like to write it now, and I would like somebody to tell me it is wrong.”

He looked at the box on the wall behind her, which is Marta Sowle's box, which has nineteen minutes and forty minutes and the retainer crossing-out in it, and which is nine feet from a plate that has just acquired a price.

He did not tell her it was wrong. He had been asked that question four times in five weeks and he had never once been the person who could.

“No,” he said. “I cannot tell you it is wrong, and I want to be exact about why, because you have asked me twice and you are owed an exact answer. I cannot tell you it is wrong because I do not know who put it there. I have been in this building three times since the twenty-first of April and I have not asked one question about the schedule, because the schedule is a contractor’s document and I have spent six weeks not asking anybody about a contractor’s document, and that has turned out to be a thing I cannot defend in a corridor.”

“All right,” said Mina Okafor, and she wrote it in the box in about nine seconds, which is a thing she is fast at, and then she said, “And now you are going to answer a question and I have asked it twice and got a shrug both times, and I am asking it a third time and I would like the answer in a sentence, because I have got a clerk coming at four and I am going to have to hand somebody something.”

“Ask.”

“What does the care entrance cost now.”

“Four thousand two hundred pounds a year,” said Jonas Mercer, “for the node on the other side of this building, and the care entrance is on the same schedule at the same tier, and the plate in front of you has got nowhere to write a number, and I have not got a number, and if you ask me what the care entrance costs I have to tell you that I do not know, and I have not known since half past twelve this afternoon, and the only person in this city who could tell you in about four minutes is sitting in an office four floors up and he has not been able to tell me either, and that is not because he is hiding it. It is because on the plate there is no place to put it.”

Mina Okafor wrote that in the box too, in her own hand, and it took about eleven words and a half, and then she read it back to herself once, and then she said:

“Then I am writing *the answer to *what does the care entrance cost* is that there is no field on the plate* on a public board, with the date, and I am going to put it under the forty minutes and not under the recent notices, because it is not a notice and it is an event, and I decided on the twenty-third of April that an event goes in the box and I have not been told I was wrong.”

“Do it.”

“Mr Mercer.” She had the pen down and she was not writing. “On the twenty-third of April I put a line in this box that said *a node not on a schedule is not disconnected, it is deferred,* and I asked somebody to tell me it was wrong, and nobody did, and it has been on that wall for seventeen days and it has been correct every day, and now there is a tier on the door.” She put the pen back in her pocket. “That is not a complaint. I am putting it in the box because I would like there to be a date on it. If a price ever comes out of that plate I would like there to be a piece of paper in this building that says *a woman asked on the tenth of May and nobody knew*.”

“Yes,” said Jonas Mercer. “Put the date on it.”

---

Gunnar Halvorsen came down the corridor at about twenty past four with a jacket over his arm and four sheets of paper in his hand, and he stopped, and he did not ask permission, and he gave it to Jonas Mercer standing up in a corridor with about thirty people going past, in about four minutes, because he is a service manager who has been in post nineteen years and has run out of people to tell.

“I am going to give you the morning and then I am going to go and write a minute, and I want to be clear that I have made the decision and I have not slept on it and I did not ring you, and you did not ring me, and neither of us has to say anything about that.”

“All right.”

“Ten o’clock. Four of them at the table and me, and the four thousand two hundred next to them in a folder because I have needed a physical object in the room all month. And the first thing that happened is that Dermot Fahey, who has been the facilities supervisor here for six years and who I told in March that he was not the answer, put a sheet of paper down and said four words.

“‘**This is a stair.**’”

He held the first sheet up so that Jonas Mercer could see it from a foot away, upside down, in a corridor.

“Second was Ines Tarrant from the car park, who has been the car park supervisor for eleven years, and she wrote: *car park barrier three is a car park barrier, it is not a service, there is nobody in this car park who is a customer, they are all staff, and I have been doing this job for eleven years and nobody has ever asked me what it is for and I have assumed it is a barrier.*” He laughed once, without any humour in it at all. “I have assumed it is a barrier. Eleven years. Nobody has ever asked her what it is for and she has assumed it is a barrier. In a city with a document in it that says *if none is recorded the use is the node’s maintenance* and forty thousand people.”

“And the riser.”

“The riser is where the morning went.” He turned the sheet over. “Service riser one. It is a wet riser, it is cast iron, it is in a duct on the west side of the service corridor, and it serves exactly one thing on this site, which is the decontamination room on the ground floor, and it has a valve on it, and there is a stopcock at the bottom, and it is a **pipe in a building.** That is not me. That is Ronan Vasquez in the estates office, who has been here eleven years, and he did not come in with a sheet of paper, he came in with a piece of A4 with a diagram on it, and he drew the riser in about ninety seconds and put a cross on the two taps it serves and wrote underneath it, in that enormous hand:

> **THIS IS A PIPE IN A BUILDING. IT IS NOT A SERVICE. I HOLD ITS MAINTENANCE. I AM TAKING IT OFF THE LIST.**

“And he took it off the list, in the room, at about half past ten on Saturday morning, under his own holding, and he did not ask me, because the schedule says the party holding the maintenance may remove a node from a schedule as well as place one on it, and I have had that document since the twenty-second of April and I have read page two twice and I did not see it, and neither did you, and neither did anybody in this city, because **nobody has ever asked whether a thing on a list of things to pay for is a thing.**”

Nobody in the corridor heard any of it, which is the entire function of a corridor, and two people in it stopped and one of them was a porter and one of them was a man carrying a laundry bag.

“And then the fourth document was the fourth one,” said Halvorsen. “Which was the gate, and which was the porter, and which I was not going to put on the table at all, and which Ronan Vasquez told me to put on the table, and which is in fact not a document from a person. It is a delivery note, the back of one, in biro, no date, no initials, and it says four words that I am not going to read to you in a corridor because I have read them four times this morning and they are his and he does not know that I have them.”

“He knows they are on the plate,” said Jonas Mercer.

“He knows they are on the plate and he has never asked what a plate is. Four of us read that thing at that table on Saturday morning and we all read it and then nobody said anything for about a minute and a half, and then the mortuary porter — who is the only one of the four of us who was there, and he came in at half past ten, and he had written the thing, and he does not know he had written it — said: *that is mine, is it.*”

“He asked whether it was his.”

“He asked whether it was his, out loud, in front of a service manager and a facilities supervisor and the estates office, and I said yes, and he said *right*,” said Gunnar Halvorsen, “and then he said, and I have it in my own handwriting in my own diary and it is the only thing I have got out of today: *then I would like it left where it is and I would like it not to be on a form.* And I have put that in the minute. And he has not signed the minute because a mortuary porter does not sign minutes, and I have written *attended, declined to sign* which is what the rules say you write.”

“So it is the gate,” said Jonas Mercer.

“It is the gate. Four thousand two hundred pounds, Tier two, six forty-four, effective from tomorrow, the eleventh of May, to six o'clock in the morning on Sunday the twelfth of October, and it is the most expensive gate in Nacre and it is the only one anybody in this country is arguing about, and it is expensive because a man of twenty-nine wrote four words in about nine seconds on the back of a piece of paper because somebody was upset, and those four words are the reason it is worth four thousand two hundred pounds a year, and if you take them off the plate then it is not four thousand two hundred pounds a year, it is a door for a van and somebody who is upset, and it shuts for everybody else, in four years, on a Friday afternoon, in a room, off a piece of paper in a binder. And that is what a man of sixty-one has been telling me for three weeks in the only words he has got, and he is right, and I have now written *the gate is the gate* on four sheets of A4 with my name on it, and I would like you to understand that I did not decide which node was worth the money. I decided that three of them were not real, and the one that is real is the one where somebody has already paid.”

He put the four sheets together and squared them.

“Nothing was voted. There was no vote available; I have four people, not a committee, and what I have done is sign a schedule under a clause that says *the party holding the standing continuity authorisation may place nodes on a schedule at any time.* I did it at twenty to one this afternoon. Nobody voted. **The most consequential thing that has happened to this site since the seventeenth of April was decided by one man with a folder in a room with three other men in it and a porter who did not know he had written anything**, and I have been in post nineteen years and I have never done that and I could not tell you this morning at half past eight which of the four I was going to do, and I want you to have that in the same breath as the four thousand two hundred pounds.”

“Then the care entrance,” said Jonas Mercer.

“Yes. The care entrance.”

“There is a line on the plate in this corridor that says six forty-one is on a continuity schedule at Tier two, and there is no field on that plate for a schedule and no field on that plate for a tier, and it printed at twenty past twelve today, and nobody in this building put it there.”

“Then somebody has put it there,” said Gunnar Halvorsen, “and I have got a theory and I am not going to tell you the theory, and I am going to ask you a question instead, and I have never asked you one, and I want the answer before Monday. **Who at CivicCore is authorised to enter a schedule against a plate in this borough?**”

“Your standing continuity authorisation is on the site administration,” said Jonas Mercer. “I have read it. It is you.”

“It is me,” said Gunnar Halvorsen. “It is me and it is a clause in a 2019 service agreement, and it says the party holding the standing continuity authorisation may place nodes on a schedule at any time, and it does not say *at the node*, and I have read that clause about nine times this morning and I did not read the word *node* into it either, because I have been reading it since the twenty-second of April as a man who has four nodes and four thousand two hundred pounds, and I have not once read it as a man who can put a **plate** on a **schedule**.”

He looked at the corridor for a second.

“And the plate on this door is not a node,” he said. “It is a **chain**, and there are two other things on it that are not nodes, and one of them is a door that a woman of ninety-one has asked me about by name, and the other one is a question about a lowland, and I have not read them, because I have never in nineteen years read a plate from the top.”

He started to walk, and then stopped, and turned round, because he had one more thing and he had been carrying it since about one o'clock.

“The date at the end. The twelfth of October, six in the morning, that is the end of the fee year for this site and I did not know it was a Sunday until Ronan Vasquez said it in the room at about ten to one, and he said it the way you said the thing about the stair.”

“What did he say.”

“He said: *it expires on a Sunday. Every site in this borough expires on a Sunday. I have been putting these on for eleven years and I have never once had to think about what a Sunday is, because the last three times it has expired nobody in the building was standing there on the Sunday.*”

“And on the eleventh of October there will be a second named clinician on that roster and four people in a handover at ten o'clock at night,” said Jonas Mercer, “and that is the last handover this site will have inside a schedule, and none of the four of them knows that, and I have worked out in a corridor on a Tuesday that the term read on the fourth of October is the last one that counts.”

“I have not asked any of them,” said Gunnar Halvorsen, “and I am not going to, and I would like to tell you exactly why in one sentence, and then I am going to write a minute. **Every time I ask one of those four people about a date, one of them ends up on a rota because of the asking, and this building has been taking people onto rotas since the second of April because of being asked, and I have watched four of them go short-term and I have watched one of them refuse a Thursday and ask for the refusal to be left where it was.** So I am not asking. You may tell them. I would rather they heard it from somebody who is not the man with the money.”
