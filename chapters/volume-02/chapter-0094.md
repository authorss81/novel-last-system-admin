# Chapter 0094 — A Public Case

**Monday the twenty-fifth of August 2014, nine in the morning, the shop.**

The notice was on the board by the till, on A4, with the crest of the civic offices on it and a date and a room, and it had been up since Friday and four people had read it and one of them was the man who had pinned it up.

> **IN THE MATTER OF A DETERMINATION AS TO SCHEDULE 4 (CONTINUITY PRICING) TO THE SAINT ORRA SERVICE AGREEMENT OF 2019**
> **APPLICANT: CARROW LANE HOUSING ASSOCIATION (chief officer: M. Oyelaran)**
> **RESPONDENT: CIVICCORE LIMITED (operations director: A. Rook)**
> **Before: Oren Pell, deputy registrar, with a public gallery and a transcript.**
> **Thursday 28 August 2014, 10:00, committee room two.**

Jonas Mercer read it standing up, twice, with a chisel in his hand.

**He had not telephoned Marguerite Oyelaran. That was the fifth time since the thirty-first of May and it was the fifth time the thing had happened without him, and this time she had not merely gone on without him, she had gone at him, in public, on a piece of paper with a crest on it, pinned to a board in a shop, and she had not told him, and there was no instrument in the borough that required her to.**

"Bea."

"Mm."

"She wants me."

"She has not asked you for anything because she does not know you exist," said Bea Osunde, "and I am not going to help you with that, and if you ring her I will put the chisel through the telephone."

---

**Monday the twenty-fifth of August 2014, half past two in the afternoon, a corridor, second floor, the civic offices.**

The aide had come and found him, which is the thing that happens four times a year, and she was nineteen and she had a lanyard and a printed sheet and she was extremely embarrassed about both.

"They want to know whether you'll give evidence."

"No."

"I had to ask."

"You had to ask," the aide said. "Can I say *he declined*."

"**You can say he has no standing, which is the same sentence and is the truth, and if you want to say it in a nicer way: there is nothing in this case he knows that a party does not, and there is one thing he knows that a party does not, and it is that a woman with a folding trolley has been at a stand at the bottom of Quarry Street at twenty to seven every weekday since a Tuesday in May, and she is not in this case and she is not going to be asked about it, and if I go into that room the first thing that happens is that a reporter asks me who she is.**"

That was the eleventh of August's answer delivered on the twenty-fifth of August and it was the same shape as the others, and the aide wrote it down in the wrong place on the printed sheet, and Jonas Mercer took it out of her hand and corrected it himself with a biro, and gave it back.

"Who then," she said. "The chair's office said there were two people in the city with standing for this and one of them is her."

"The other one is Nina Calder and Nina Calder will not be a party, and I know that without asking her, because she has refused to be a party to anything since the fourth of March and she has refused it four times in front of a committee clerk and a records supervisor has a folder with the four refusals in it and it is not a folder she made for anybody else."

---

**Thursday the twenty-eighth of August 2014, ten in the morning, committee room two.**

Nineteen in the gallery before eleven and about thirty standing by the end, and the front two rows to an unchecked press list, and a woman from a trade paper took a photograph of the empty chair at the applicant's table on her phone at four minutes to ten and nobody stopped her.

**Oren Pell sat at the top of a table with a file and a fan and a transcript, and he said, before he took a single note, that he had made four rulings this year that were doors and not a walls, that he was not going to boast about them, and that he would be ruling again in about an hour and would be as brief as he could be, and that the applicant was entitled to her full time and the respondent was entitled to his.**

Marguerite Oyelaran was at the applicant's table with a ring-number file and no assistant, and she is forty-seven and has been the chief officer of that association for eleven years and has never once been in a room that had a transcript in it, and she had a sheet of her own arithmetic on top of the file and she did not use it and put it back under the file twice.

Alden Rook was at the respondent's table with Ceri Vaughan, and there was a folder, and it was not a big folder.

"The applicant will be heard," said Pell. "The respondent has said he will not contest the applicant's statement of the facts. **Mr Rook, is that right, and are you going to say it into the transcript.**"

"It is right and I am going to say it into the transcript, and I am going to say it before she starts, and I want it minuted that I offered to say it before she started." Rook put his hands flat on the table, flat the way Jonas Mercer puts his hands flat on things. "I do not contest a single fact the applicant has put in front of you. I do not contest the fee, the date, the number of rooms, the block with no water, the sixteen, the ninety-two, or the four thousand two hundred pounds. **I do not contest that she obtained a tier by paying for it, because she did, in March, with her own money, out of a fund that was short by three hundred and five pounds and sixty pence, and she has defended every line of it in public twice and she was right both times and I have said so.**"

He turned and looked at her, and it was not theatre.

"And I want to say the other half of it into the transcript before she begins, because she is going to say something about my staff and she is going to be right, and I would rather the two of us were not arguing about my staff in a room with nineteen people in it.

"**We found out.** That is the whole of my case and it is the whole of my weakness. We audit the nodes we hold. Nobody else in this borough has audited anything since about 1994, including the people who have been in the building, including the party at the other table. **A paid claim is a more trustworthy claim only because the payer found out, and that is a true sentence, and it is a terrible reason to price a door.** I have said it better in a corridor and I am saying it worse in here because here it goes in a record."

"Somebody sat down," said Petrie Okonjo from the members' bench, quietly, and the transcript took it.

---

**Oyelaran's statement took nineteen minutes and the transcript runs to eleven pages, and this is what she said in it.**

"**I am asking this Committee to rule that the order of precedence between two nodes cannot be set by the amount of a fee.** That is the whole of my case and I have made it one sentence because I have watched four hundred and sixty pages of Schedule 4 turn a queue into a market and I am not going to help it.

"I am not asking you to strike out Schedule 4. **I have read enough of Schedule 4 this summer to know that I could not write it and that every word in it is true, and I am not the woman who is going to stand up in a room and say a document is a scam.** I am asking you to take one sentence out of the way of the order, and I am asking you to do it on my application and not on anybody's, because I am the one who can stand up and be asked whether I understood what I was buying, and nobody in this room can be asked that about a lowland.

"Now. **What I bought.** In March this association placed nodes for four blocks at Tier 2. Four thousand two hundred pounds a year. It is not disputed. A hundred and twenty-eight agreements went through a hundred and twenty-eight letterboxes in January and February, and a hundred and twenty-two came back signed, and six never came back at all, and two of those six have told me to my face that they will not sign a thing that makes my name a door, and I have not chased them, and I have had their reasons and they were right.

"**What it bought me.** I do not know. That is the honest answer and it is why I am standing here.

"**What it did not buy me.** A riser. The riser in this association's stock died in 1998 in one block and it has not been replaced. And on the sixth of May this year a freeholder's agent isolated the supply to that block and it has had no water since, and it is still on Tier 2, and **the node for a building with no water in it is at Tier 2 at four thousand two hundred pounds a year this morning.** I found that out in a minute in June. I have been paying for the continuity of an empty pipe for four months.

"And I want the Committee to hear the rest of that, because it is the reason I am here and it is the only reason. **Four thousand two hundred pounds would not have fixed it.** There was a day in April when I sat in that block and worked out what a riser costs and it is about nine hundred pounds, and if I had had nine hundred pounds on the second of May I would have had water in that block by the ninth, and I did not have it, and nobody from this Committee was going to give me it, and the four thousand two hundred pounds had gone on a schedule in March.

"So I am not asking you to give me money and I am not asking you to give me a tier. **I am asking you to say that the order is not a price list, and I am going to say this next part because six words were written in a records room in this city in March, in eleven minutes, and a panel accepted them on the first of April, and the six words were *non-civic plant*, and they are on a series six sheet, and I have known about them since the second of April, and I have never been able to find out who wrote them, and I am not going to find out, because the day I find out is the day it becomes a document about a person instead of a finding about a pipe.**

"That is what those six words did. **They took four tonnes of stone and a £1,180 bill off anybody's schedule and put them on nobody's invoice, and the other half of that same meeting voted on the other node and said no, and nobody has ever written a sentence anywhere that says how the two halves of that morning are related, and the reason the stone on the bank at the bottom of that dip is going to be paid for by nobody, ever, is those eleven minutes in a records room.** And I am not here to defend it and I am not here to attack it. I am here because I have been paying a price and I could not find out what it was for, and when I did find out I found a man in a corridor in June who had found a chain and a woman on a landing who had been holding the bottom of it for seven weeks, and neither of those two things happened because of me."

Oren Pell said: "Mr Mercer is in the gallery and is not a party and is not a member and I am not going to let anybody look at him, and the applicant has chosen her words well and I am going to allow the record to stand as it is."

---

**Rook's reply was four minutes and it is on page nine of the transcript.**

"I am not going to argue with any of it. **I am going to say one thing and it is the reason I did not contest her statement, and then I am going to sit down.**

"Mr Oyelaran has told this room that she obtained a tier by paying for it and that she does not know what it bought. **I would like to point out that for nineteen years, in a building of thirty-one rented rooms with no civic address, nobody bought anything, nobody audited anything, and no party was in breach of anything, because there was no instrument that required one.** The reason I know about that building is that a woman in it read a plate in a meter cupboard with a torch and entered her own name in a holder field and then, when she found out what she had done, told us. She did that. Not me.

"So the applicant's case is this: **a price bought nothing.** My case is: **a price bought an audit, and the audit was the only thing that happened, and it happened because the money had to come from somewhere, and I did not do it for the money.** I would do it again at the same margin and I would not do it for a pound more, and I know how that sounds in a room with nineteen people in it, and I would like it in the transcript that I have said it in a Committee room in June in almost the same words and I was not asked to leave."

Nobody asked him to leave.

"**And I will say the thing that is going to matter to her in November and not today. If the price stops ordering the queue, then somebody has to be able to say why a thing is at the front, and that is not a number, it is an argument, and arguments have to be had in public, repeatedly, by people who are not going to be paid to have them.** I can do that. I have four hundred people and a board with forty-one hinges on it. **I am not frightened of losing the price. I am frightened of the day somebody has to stand up in this room and say *we cannot afford to keep that door open* and there is no price on it, and nobody in this city has ever had to say that sentence out loud with their name attached.**"

---

**Jonas Mercer's one question, at four minutes to twelve.**

"Does this determination do anything to the notice that was served on the third of July."

"It does not," said Oren Pell, "and I am going to say the next part before the respondent does, because he was going to say it anyway and I would rather it was in the transcript in the right order.

"**The notice served at eleven o'clock on the third of July 2014 is untouched by anything I say today.** It is a clause about service, and it has ninety days in it, and ninety days from the third of July is Wednesday the first of October, and that is thirty-four days away, and there is nothing in Schedule 4, in this determination, or in this city that will move it, shorten it, or require anybody who has been served to be aware of having been served."

Rook said: "Correct," and put his pen down.

---

**The ruling was made at ten past twelve and it took one page and it is on page fourteen of the transcript and it is the whole of what anybody in this room got.**

> **DETERMINATION. In the matter of a determination as to Schedule 4 (continuity pricing) to the Saint Orra service agreement of 2019. Applicant: Carrow Lane Housing Association. Respondent: CivicCore Limited. 28 August 2014.**
>
> **1. Where the order of precedence between two nodes is set by the amount of a continuity fee paid, and by no other criterion, the tier so acquired is not a determination of priority.**
>
> **2. This determination is made on a sub-substitution of the Schedule and is limited to precedence. It does not affect the validity of the Schedule, the validity of any placement, the maintenance of any node, the physical works of any party, the amount of any fee paid before this date, or the notice served on the third of July 2014.**
>
> **3. Nothing in this determination requires any person to do anything. No party is required to refund any sum. There is no mechanism in this city by which a fee already paid may be recovered, and none is created by this determination, and I have said so in public twice this year and I am saying it a third time because a member wrote it into a note and I would like the note to be right.**
>
> **4. The class of node to which paragraph 1 applies is: a node whose precedence was acquired by the payment of a fee and which is not otherwise distinguished from any other node by anything that is not itself a fee.**

He read paragraph four out twice and then stopped, and put his pen down, and there was a silence in that room that four of the people in it had not been in a room for before.

"**I am going to read you an example, because paragraph four is a definition and definitions in Schedules have destroyed more services than weather ever has.**

"A node in a building of thirty-one rented rooms in Tideglass. Tier 3, reference T3/11400/0304, withdrawn at six o'clock on the fourth of July 2014 at the consent of the person in the holder field, who is a person and not a party, and who could not be required to be told and was not, and who was told by a party for no reason anybody has yet been able to establish.

"Is that node distinguished from any other node by anything that is not a fee? **The holder field is empty. The node's last verified essential use was never recorded. There is no party, no address, no district and no invoicee. And an empty field is not a distinction. An empty field is the absence of every possible distinction.**

"So it is in the class. **It is in the class and there is no consequence provided for, and that is not a gap in this determination, it is the shape of the thing.** A determination can remove a criterion and it cannot invent a replacement in ninety seconds, and anybody who tells you otherwise is selling you something at a tier."

"So what happens to it," said Douglas Imre.

"Nothing," said Oren Pell. "**Nothing happens to it until somebody does something to it, and I am not going to pretend that I have ruled on what that is, because I have not, and because a registrar who rules on the consequences of his own ruling in the same breath is a man who has stopped being a registrar and become a policy.**"

And then a woman at the back of the gallery, who had come in late and had been standing, said the question that had been in the room the whole time.

"Saturday morning at ten o'clock," she said. "The managing agent's meter-round at that building is on a Saturday morning at ten o'clock. She has got a list. **What is she going to find on Saturday morning at ten o'clock.**"

Nobody answered her, and the chair asked her to state whether she wished to be recorded, and she said she did not, and it was in the transcript that she did not, and the transcript has her as *a member of the public*.

Jonas Mercer wrote two lines on the back of his hand in the gallery, standing, and put the hand in his pocket before anybody could see it, and it said:

> *Saturday the thirtieth of August, ten in the morning, and it is a Saturday morning and not a Saturday night, and that is the only thing I have got to hold on to. Beverley Annis has a meter round at Wren Court on Saturdays at ten and she has never once been told anything about any of this and she is going to walk into a cupboard under a stair and read a plate.*
> *And the plate is going to say that the node serves the maintenance of the node. And there is no rule anywhere that says she has to be told that the thing she is looking at is now the only case in Nacre.*

---

They were on the pavement outside the civic offices at ten past one and Alden Rook was not prevented from saying four sentences to him, and said them at the door and went in.

"You did not get what you wanted," he said.

"No."

"Nor did I." Rook had the folder under his arm. "**I lost the thing I was selling and I have kept the thing I built, and I have known since about eleven o'clock this morning which of those two was going to happen, and I have a service floor with forty-one hinges on it tomorrow night and nobody has been able to put a number on what I do.**

"And that is the whole of the accounting and I have never in twenty-two years had a better one, so I am not going to be sour about it in a doorway. **But I am going to say the sentence I have been sitting on since about eleven o'clock and then I am going to go and do a boiler.**"

"Say it."

"**A thing that was going to happen anyway now has to be argued for.** That is the price of what you have done and it is not a price on the Schedule, it is a price on every argument anybody is ever going to have to have from now on about why this door stays open. **And in about two years the first person who cannot be bothered to make that argument will find a piece of paper that says a thing used to be Tier 2, and the piece of paper will be true, and it will have my company's name at the top of it and the name of the officer who signed it, and there will be nobody in the city who can be asked to say no, because the officer is not a party and the officer can be asked what he will do and not what it will cost.**"

He went in.

Jonas Mercer stood on the pavement of the Old Ward for about four minutes after that, in the sun, with a date in his pocket and a new class of node in his head and thirty-four days in front of him, and then he walked forty-one minutes to the shop, and did not ring anybody, and did not go up a landing in Salter's Yard, and did not go out to Kestrel Hill, and wrote four lines on the back of a delivery note, and only one of them was a fact.

> *Thursday 28 August 2014. Priority access is invalid in this city where it was bought with a fee and nothing else. One sentence, one page, eleven pages of transcript, a sub-substitution, and CivicCore Limited is not dissolved, is not re-run, and is not an offer. It keeps the network. It loses the queue. The cost of the narrowness is not mine to pay and Alden Rook named it in a doorway in under a minute and he was right: a thing that was going to happen anyway now has to be argued for, and nobody in this city can be required to make an argument.*
> *The applicant did not ask for her money back. She was asked about money by a member and she said no into the transcript and she gave the reason, which is that four thousand two hundred pounds would not have fixed a riser and she worked that out in a block in April, and she said in that room that there are six words in a records room in this city that put four tonnes of stone on nobody's invoice and took a street's obligations off at the same time, and she said she has known since April and has never been able to find out who wrote them and does not want to.*
> *She is not angry with me. I have never once spoken to her since the thirty-first of May and she has stood up in a room with nineteen people in it and described exactly what I did in June and did not describe it as mine. That is the whole of the sixth time and I am not going to telephone her, and she is not going to ring me, and I am not going to be in a room with her in November unless somebody makes me go and puts me in a gallery again.*
> *Two days to Saturday morning at ten o'clock. A meter round. A plate in a cupboard under a stair that says a node serves the maintenance of the node, and a woman with twenty-six years in post and four hundred and ten flats who will read it and who cannot be asked a question by anybody in this city, and an empty holder field is not a distinction, and I wrote that down in a gallery and then put my hand in my pocket.*
