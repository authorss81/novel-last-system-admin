# Chapter 0099 — What A Person In A Field Is Worth

**Monday the twenty-second of September 2014, twenty past two in the afternoon, the shop.**

Oren Pell had never been in the shop. He had telephoned it three times since March and had never once been asked why, and he came in on a Monday afternoon with a sandwich in a bag and a fan's worth of him about it, and stood at the end of the bench where Tomas Renn stands.

"Right," he said. "Three things and then I am going to do a stair in a school in Gantry Ward and I am going to be late for it because of this."

"Sit down, Mr Pell."

"I will not, because if I sit down this becomes a conversation and it is not a conversation, it is a thing I am putting on a record." He put the sandwich down on the bench, which is a thing he does. "**One. I brought this.**" He put a sheet of paper on the bench. It was the registrar's written statement to the board, one page, with a paragraph number in the left margin and a date, and it was the same sheet that had been read out in a Methodist hall at ten past nine on the fourth of September, and Jonas Mercer had a copy because Frances Tolley had typed it out at a counter for nothing on Thursday afternoon.

"You have had that since Friday."

"You have had that since Friday and you did not telephone me, and I am not hurt about it, and I am going to say why you were right not to telephone me and then I am going to say the thing I came to say, and then you are going to be a man who was told no by a registrar for the fifth time this year and we will both get it over with."

"Go on."

"**I have read page four of section eleven again and I am not going to tell you what is on it, because Frances Tolley has it in her own hand and Nina Calder has read it and the two of them have between them decided that a woman in Salter's Yard is not going to be told, and they are both right, and if I put it in your ear you will take it to a room within a fortnight and somebody in that room will try to be clever with it.**

"So: page four is not mine to give you and that is the end of it."

"And the thing you came to say."

"The thing I came to say is that I am not going to say it again."

"What."

"**There is no mechanism in this city to make the top of a chain pay, and there is no mechanism in this city to make a person take a duty, and they are the same sentence, and who pays for the refusal is nobody.**"

He said it flatly, at a volume that would not carry past the counter, in a shop, at twenty past two on a Monday afternoon, and it took him about eleven seconds.

"I said that to you on the ninth of July in an office with a fan on a windowsill and I have said it in a corridor and I have said it in a Committee room and I have said it in a public determination a fortnight ago in front of nineteen people and a transcript, and paragraph three of that determination says it a third time, and it is now in a document that goes to the Assembly, and I have no intention of saying it a fifth time to anybody with a pad.

"**So I am not going to say it. And I am not going to refuse it either, because you did not ask. You have been not-asking it since about May and it is the single most competent thing you have ever done and I have watched you do it in four rooms and you have not done it once in this shop, and I have come here on a Monday afternoon to be in a shop with you for about four minutes and to hand you a piece of paper and to tell you that I am finished.**"

"Then what do you want."

"I want you to know that I have been tired since the eleventh of June and I am now tired in a different place, and the different place is that I have started being able to answer the question and I do not like the answer, and a man who can answer the question and does not like the answer is a man who has started making policy, and I have a registrar's job and not a policy job, and I would rather be extremely tired in the old place for another two years." He picked the sandwich up. "**And I want to say one more thing and then I am going, and it is not a rule and it is not advice.**

"You have spent five months not asking. It has been the correct thing every time and it has cost somebody a day and seven hours and it has cost you a paragraph number on a public form and it will cost you something else before the first of October and there is nothing I can do about any of it.

"**The day you ask somebody, it will not be me, and it will not be a committee, and it will be a person in a room who can say no. And when they say no, that ends the ask.** And I am telling you that as a man handing over a job, not as a man giving a rule, and I would like it written in your pad in those words so that in about two years, when there is a minute somewhere that says you were told, it is a man with a sandwich on a bench in September and not a registrar in a corridor in July."

He got to the door.

"Mr Pell."

"**Do not telephone me about the first of October.** I have four minutes and two of them are already gone. Forty-one minutes, Mr Mercer. I have walked it. I did it in about forty minutes in May and I have thought about it every day since and it has not got shorter."

---

**Tuesday the twenty-third of September 2014, half past three in the afternoon, the bottom of Salter's Yard.**

He walked it. Forty-one minutes, and he had walked it in July and in June, and the shopfront has said SALTER'S on the glass since before anybody living in that building was born, and the glass has been broken since March, and the stairwell on the north side is open to the air because the door has been broken since March and nobody has had it fixed and the landlord is not the landlord.

There were two vans in the road and a man in an apron at the bottom of the workshop ramp with a clipboard, and he was not Beverley Annis, and Jonas Mercer stood at the wall opposite for about eleven minutes and then walked back forty-one minutes to the shop and did not go up.

**He did not go up because the ninth seat is the only person in this city he is allowed to ask.** That is not a sentiment. It is the whole of the design of a four-page regulation from 2008: a tier may not be challenged by a person, a person is not a party, a person may not be required to do anything and may not be required even to be told, and therefore there is no instrument in the borough by which a man with a register can make a woman in a landing answer a question about a Saturday in October, and there is also no instrument by which he can make her answer it now.

And if he goes up on a Tuesday afternoon, then he is a man who has come up a step, and on the twenty-seventh of June he came up a step and asked her one question, and she said no, and he asked one question a day thereafter, and that was a rule between two people and it worked.

**And a rule between two people works for about eleven weeks.**

---

**Wednesday the twenty-fourth of September 2014, ten past four in the afternoon, the kitchen off the third-floor landing, Wren Court, Salter's Yard.**

There is a kitchen on that landing and it is not anybody's, and it has a table with three chairs at it and a fourth chair somebody carried up from the workshop in March, and a cooker with four rings of which two work, a fridge with a padlock on it that is a hasp and a padlock and the padlock is a good one and it is on the fridge because eleven people in this building have been thieving from each other in a warm week since about 1996.

Yolanda Fofana was doing something with a sharpening steel and the back of a saw and there was a cardigan over the apron and the reading glasses were on the chain and not on her face.

"Sit down," she said, without looking up. "One of those chairs is stable."

He sat down. He had been in the building three times in five months and the last time there had been twenty-nine people in a workshop at eleven o'clock at night and he had stood at the back and said nothing, and he was not going to do that again.

"You've come up the step again."

"I have walked past the bottom of it forty-one minutes twice this week and I did not come up on Tuesday and I have come up now, and I am going to tell you why I did not come up on Tuesday, and then I am going to ask you one question, and then I am going to go, and it is going to take about four minutes, and I have never once in five months been able to get to the point in under four minutes so you may want to sit back."

She put the saw down.

"Go on, then."

"You are the ninth seat." He said it plainly, because she had said plainly and because anything else would be an insult. "**A person who answers for a service and is not a body. Which is nine words on the second page of a regulation from 2008, and it is the only thing in this city that I am allowed to ask a question through, and the reason is the reason you have said out loud in a hall on the fourth of September: you are not a party, and a person may not be required to do anything, and so nobody can make you answer, and so you are the only person in this borough I can ask and be refused by.**

"That is not a compliment. I want that understood. **It is the only reason I am in this kitchen and it is a mechanism and it is four pages old and it is not fair to you and I have known that since the fourth of September and I have not come up since, and the reason I have not come up is that I worked out on Monday that asking you a question is now a thing I can do whenever I want, and a thing I can do whenever I want is a rota, and I have spent five months refusing to be a rota for four other people and I am not going to be one for you in my last three weeks of a year.**

"So I am going to ask it once and it is the question I have been carrying since the eighth of March and it is not mine, it is the question in your building, and it was asked in your stairwell on the twenty-seventh of June in nine words and I could not answer it then and I have had eighty-nine days.

"**What happens to a service when the person who knows the work cannot be paid for it.**"

The sharpening steel stopped.

"No," said Yolanda Fofana.

"You have not—"

"**I know what you have been carrying. I have known since about the eleventh of July and a man with a sandwich on a bench said a version of it out loud on the ninth of July and it has been repeated in three offices since, and the version they repeated is the wrong one and I heard the right one.**" She turned the saw over. "You have not asked me a question, Mr Mercer. You have handed me a question you have been carrying for five months and asked me to hold it, and that is what people do with me, and it is what everybody has done since the third of June except that most of them put it in a field first.

"So no. **I am not going to answer that on a Wednesday afternoon in a kitchen, and I am not going to answer it in September or in October or in a room, and it is not because I do not know the answer.** I have known the answer since the fourth of July and I have said it out loud in a workshop to twenty-nine people and in a hall to thirty, and the answer is me, on a landing, in an apron, for nothing, findable, and good until I am sixty-one. **I have answered it. I answered it before you arrived and I answered it to the people it is about and I am not going to answer it again to a man with a register, because the second time you tell a person the answer to their own life they start listening for the shape of your sentence instead of the shape of the answer.**

"And there is a second thing and I am going to say it because you walked forty-one minutes twice and did not come up and you have earned one sentence that is about the twentieth of July and not about me.

"**The day after the first of October. Somebody asked that in a shop in July and it has gone round this borough and I have been asked it twice since, by people who are not you, and I am not going to answer it either, and I want that said in your pad in the words *she declined to answer it*, because the reason I am not going to answer it is not manners.

"**Because the first person in this city who says that answer out loud is going to be quoted in October by a man with a lever-arch file who is going to do her job, and the answer is going to be *ask her again tomorrow, she is in the field anyway*, and that is true, and it is the clause working, and it is not defeating anything, it is complying with it, twice, and the only thing that stops it is a person who has decided in advance, in her own words, on a date, what she will say when she is asked the second time.** And I am not going to say it out loud to you, because you are a man who writes things down, and a person who has decided in advance what she will say is not a person anybody can find, and if you write it down then in four years there is a piece of paper in a file that says what Yolanda Fofana will say on the second of October, and I am not going to be that in a drawer. **Findability is not answerability. Your registrar said that on the ninth of July and he was right and it has been in my head for ten weeks and that is the whole of what I have got to offer you this afternoon.**"

She picked the saw up again.

"Then you answer it," she said. "**You have been carrying it since March and you have asked me for it four times in a corridor and a stairwell and a kitchen, and I have watched you not answer it, and the reason you have not answered it is not manners either. It is that you think you owe me an answer. You do not owe me an answer. You owe one to the lowland and to a block of 1938 and to about nine people in four hundred yards who carry water, and none of them are in this kitchen.**"

"I have got one," said Jonas Mercer. "**It took me two hundred days and it is not very good and I am going to say it now because you have given me the only thing you have got, which is permission to say it here.**

"**Nobody pays. Nobody is required. Nobody is released, by anybody, from anything, ever.** I have written those three sentences in this account about nine times since the eighth of March and every one of them is a registrar's ruling or a record in a file and not one of them is a service, and there is no instrument in this borough that can make the top of a chain pay and there is none that can make a person take a duty, and your friend with the sandwich on the bench said both of those in a shop on Monday and then said he was not going to say them again.

"And here is the fourth thing, and I have never said this out loud to anybody, and I have had it since the seventh of July and I have been carrying it round like a stone in a shoe.

"**The only instrument anybody in this city has built in five months that touches any of it is a slip on a public board in the vestibule of the Old Ward that says a person may refuse to be counted, and the count then says so and says how many refused.** One week. One place. One number. No index, no name box, nothing may be added to it. And it is the closest thing to a thing that works that anybody has designed since March, and it came out of a bin.

"**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped.** That is the end of my answer. Not because it is cynical. Because it is what I have found, and I have found it four times, and every time I have found it somebody has come back at me in about four weeks and said *then we will put somebody's name in it*."

He put his hand on the table, flat, the way he puts it on a sill.

"That is what I have got," he said, "and I have said it out loud in a kitchen, and you are the fourth person this month I have said it to and the other three are a registrar with a sandwich, a records supervisor who has read a fourth page of a regulation, and a clerk of sixty-three in Kestrel Hill who has a lever-arch file and has not spoken to me once, and not one of the three has anything to add, and **the woman has just told me that the only thing my answer lacks is everybody it is about, and that none of them are in this kitchen, and she is right, and I am not going to be able to fix that in this borough.**"

She looked at him for a while with the steel against her knee.

"Right," she said. "**Then that is the shape of the last month, and you can take it downstairs with you, and it is the only useful sentence anybody has said in this kitchen in nineteen years and it is not about me.**

"Now go downstairs before the six o'clock crowd, and do not come up on a Tuesday again this year, and **do not write that down** — and when you do not write it down, which you will, because you are a man who writes things down, write instead that a woman on a landing in Salter's Yard said the second half of it and would not say the first half, and let the reader work out which is which."

**Thursday the twenty-fifth of September 2014, half past four in the afternoon, the shop.**

He wrote it down. All of it except the thing she had told him not to write down, and he wrote that down as a line that said she had told him not to write it down, which is the only honest way to keep it.

He wrote the whole of it with his left hand by the end of Thursday, because the right one had gone at the fourth line and stayed gone, and the seam mark past the elbow had gone a colour he had not seen before, and there is a thing he has been meaning to do for a month, which is to mention it to somebody, and on Thursday the twenty-fifth of September he did not mention it to anybody.

> *Monday the twenty-second, a registrar, in a shop, on his feet, with a sandwich on the bench. He did not say it. He came to tell me he was not going to say it again, and to tell me that the day I ask somebody it will be a person in a room who can say no, and that when they say no it ends the ask. I have had five of those this year from five men who were all right and I have never once been given the same sentence twice, and this is the first time anybody has handed me a job instead of a ruling, and he asked for it to go in the pad in those words so that in about two years a minute cannot say I was told by a registrar in a corridor in July.*
> *Wednesday the twenty-fourth, a kitchen on a landing, four minutes, standing up. I asked the question and she knew what I was carrying before I got it out, and she said no, and the no was to the carrying and not to the question. She has answered the question twice already, to twenty-nine people in a workshop and to thirty in a hall, and she is not going to answer it a third time to a man with a register. Then she told me to answer it myself, and I did, and she sent me downstairs.*
> *And the day after the first of October: she declined to answer it, and she gave the reason, and the reason is that the first person in this city who says that answer out loud is going to be quoted in October by a woman with a lever-arch file who is going to do her job, and the answer is **ask her again tomorrow, she is in the field anyway**, and it is true, and it is the clause working, and it is not defeating anything. It is complying with it twice.*
> *Six days to Wednesday the first of October. On and after that date the placing party may re-assert at the tier, at that node, without the consent of any person whose name has been in the holder field of it at any time before that date, and a trust in Kestrel Hill has answered a machine once and a person once and has not noticed that it has been notified. On the second of October a woman is going to be in that field again because she has to be, because nobody else can be, because a meter cupboard under a stair has a plate on it that has said *the point is not the payer's* since before the war, and she is the only person anybody can find. Nobody can stop that. There is no instrument in this city by which a person in a holder field can be taken out of it, and she has told me in a kitchen that the only thing that can stop it is a person who has decided in advance, in her own words, on a date, what she will say the second time — and that she will not tell me, and that I am not to write it down, and I have not written it down, and I have written down that I was told not to write it down, and that is the first page anybody in this city has produced since March that cannot be given to anybody.*
> *Two people it cost something to. A woman with a folding trolley at the bottom of Quarry Street, who is not named anywhere and whom I have stood opposite three times without asking her one question. And a chief officer of forty-seven who stood up in a room with nineteen people in it on the twenty-eighth of August and described exactly what I did in June, in public, and did not describe it as mine. I have still not telephoned her. That is the fifth time, and I am not going to telephone her, and she is not going to ring me, and both of those are now a fixed position in the human geography of this city.*
> *Saturday the eleventh of October is sixteen days away. Two tired porters are going to read a term in a corridor at ten o'clock at night and neither of them knows what is in it. I am not going to be in that corridor. Tomas Renn and I will not discuss it, and that rule between the two of us is the only instrument either of us has produced this year that is any use at all.*
