# Chapter 0070 — The Nineteenth of May

Committee room two on the first floor of the Old Ward has a horseshoe of nine chairs, a table at the head of it, a clerk's desk below the window, a public box on the left-hand wall, and a gallery behind a rail at the back with two rows of fixed seats and about forty folding ones, and a caretaker who came in at half past seven on a Monday morning with a stack and put them in himself and was told to be there by ten.

Jonas Mercer was in the fourth seat back on the left-hand side at ten to ten on the morning of Monday the nineteenth of May, and he was in it because a clerk of twenty-two years' standing had told him at half past seven to take the left-hand side, and because the front two rows on the right went to a press list that nobody checks at the door.

**Fenella Marsh** in the chair. **Councillor Iven Sore.** **Councillor Douglas Imre**, who was forty-one and had been on the committee four years and had chaired nothing. **Councillor Hargreaves.** **Councillor Petrie Okonjo.** **Councillor Denman.** Six members, five of them in the chair's left-hand field of view, and every one of them had read the papers, and the papers were on the table in front of them in four colours.

**Oren Pell** in attendance, as registrar, with a folder and a coat over the back of the seventh chair, which is the thing he has done for nineteen years and which is not a place.

**Alden Rook** and **Ceri Vaughan** for CivicCore, on the fourth and fifth chairs along the right-hand wall, with a folder each, and a third person from CivicCore at the end of the second row who had a lanyard and who was not introduced.

**The gallery was full at ten to eleven.** Forty-one folding chairs and about thirty more people standing at the rail. A woman with a clipboard on the third row who was not a journalist. A man in a high-visibility vest from a contractor who was not on the papers and who was asked twice by a member's aide to move down the aisle and moved down the aisle.

And at about half past ten, on the back row, on the left-hand side, a woman of forty-seven in a good coat with a folder on her knees, who was the chief officer of the Carrow Lane Housing Association, who was not a party, and who had spent the morning saying nothing to about eleven people who had wanted to talk to her about a circular.

Jonas Mercer looked for Tomas Renn for about four minutes in the first recess and did not find him, and knew he was not there, and did not telephone him, and had promised on the twenty-seventh of April in a shop yard not to be.

He was not on the agenda. He was not a party. He was not a member and he was not an officer of the department. He was a man in a gallery who had been in a room like this one in April as a witness to somebody else's determination, and had been asked nothing, and had asked one question. **And he had promised, in a yard, on a Sunday morning, not to speak in this room, and he had not spoken in it, and the only person in this building who knew what that had cost him was a shop steward in River Stacks who was not going to find out.**

**Item one, four minutes.** The determination of the seventeenth of April, noted and suspended. The registrar's record of the eighteenth of April.

Oren Pell read his own record into the minute in the flat voice he uses for reading other people's sentences, and it was four lines and a finding, and the fourth line was the one Sore had stopped him from finishing in April in a different room:

> **REGISTRAR'S RECORD, 18 APRIL 2014, 19:10. A CONTRACTOR'S CONTRACTUAL DEFINITION OF FAILURE IS NOT A CIVIC DETERMINATION OF A NODE'S STATE. A NODE WITH NO TERM IN FORCE IS NOT DISCONNECTED; IT IS DEFERRED, AND IT SERVES ITS LAST VERIFIED ESSENTIAL USE, AND IF NONE IS RECORDED, THE USE IS THE NODE'S MAINTENANCE.**
> **FOR THE FILE AND NOT FOR ANY PERSON: the condition line on 6-L/14 was available to be recorded on 1 April 2014 and was not.**

Nobody said anything about it and it went into the minute, and it was the first of the three things the city put into the record on that day and it was a month old.

**Item two, one minute forty.** The cover note by the clerk, read in full by Frances Tolley, slowly, in four sentences, and on the fourth sentence a man from CivicCore leaned forward about two inches, because the fourth sentence said the Return of a Deferred Node was not before the Committee, was not adopted, was not minuted, was not enforceable, and established no service, no holder, no district, and no number of nodes in this city, and was printed on the papers solely because the covering submission at item three referred to the number of deferred nodes and no other instrument in this city was capable of supplying that number.

**Item three, from about eleven.** Schedule 4 to the Saint Orra service agreement of 2019, continuity pricing, four hundred and sixty pages, and a covering submission of four pages dated the twenty-fourth of April, and the tabling list at item five said *referred on*.

Councillor Imre identified the difference on the record in about ninety seconds, and he did it flatly and without heat, and he was right.

“The tabling list says this schedule is before the Committee to be referred on. The covering submission, at paragraph two, invites the Committee to approve Schedule 4 and to adopt it as a schedule of the Committee for the remainder of the financial year. Those are two different things in front of us. I have read both. I would like the chair to rule which of them we are doing.”

“I am not going to rule,” said Fenella Marsh. “That is not a chair’s job and I am not going to pretend it is. What I will do is put it to the Committee, and the Committee will tell me, and it will take four minutes and it is the first thing we do.”

It took eleven minutes and the answer was: the schedule is before the Committee on the covering submission and the Committee is invited to approve and adopt it, and no member is able to move an amendment, and the chair has noted the discrepancy and will record it, and the Committee will proceed on the document in front of it.

It proceeded on the document in front of it.

Councillor Petrie raised one thing before anybody sat down, and she raised it in thirty seconds and got it in.

“The covering submission quotes a finding. It is the finding of the Civic Continuity Workers' Union, filed on the eighteenth of April, and it is quoted on page one as support for a procurement remedy. There is nobody from the union in this room. I have looked at the tabling list and at the gallery and I cannot see the shop steward or anybody from the shop, and I am not asking that anybody be found, and I am asking that the minute record that the author of a document relied upon by the contractor is not present.

“They are not,” said Fenella Marsh. “The finding is in the bundle, behind the covering submission, where the clerk put it because there was nowhere else to put it, and it is a finding and it is in the author's name alone, and a finding is a finding whether its author is in the room or not, and the Committee is not going to treat the absence of a person as a criticism of anybody. It will be minuted that the author is not present, because the clerk minuted it when I asked, and the clerk minuted it before I asked, which tells you what this clerk is like.”

**Nobody succeeded at beating a price with a sentence. It was attempted four times in three hours and it failed four times, and every failure was correct.**

The first was Alden Rook, and he did it without being asked, and he was the best in the room.

He did not define *holder*. He had said in a stairwell on the thirtieth of April that he was not going to, and he did not, and he never will. He stood up at ten past twelve and made the argument of the volume in four minutes, and it was this: the schedule does not price access to urgent care. The schedule prices the continuity of a node. A care claim is not a node. The west clinic care entrance is not in failure and was not in failure in April and is not in failure now, and it is not on a schedule, and Schedule 4 did not touch it, and the door at that clinic has been open every night since the second of April on a term read aloud at ten o'clock by two people who can say when it ends, and that is not the committee's doing and it is not the contractor's doing and it is the only thing anybody in this city has done right since the third of April.

Every sentence of that was true. It was in the room for about forty seconds before somebody said the next thing.

The second was a member — Hargreaves — who said: *then a hospital has been working on a volunteer arrangement for six weeks while a contractor prices the alternative, and that is the finding of this hearing.*

And Alden Rook said: “No, it is not, and I am obliged to say so. A volunteer arrangement is a term with an expiry and nobody knows when it expires, and when it expires the door is a deferred node, and Schedule 4 is the document that tells you what a deferred node does before it happens rather than afterwards, and I have never in my life argued that a schedule is a kindness. I have argued that it is cheaper than a queue and I have argued that in a stairwell to one man, and I would like it minuted that I have never said it was good.”

The third was a member of this Committee asking a question from the floor of the gallery, which is irregular, and which the chair allowed, and which was the best four minutes anybody had all day. The question was about a lift in a block of flats, and who it belonged to, and the member from Salt Row put it to a woman of forty-seven who is not a member and not a party and who is in the back row on the left-hand side with a folder on her knees, and she answered it, and she gave four sentences.

She said that a lift is not a service and that she has said so in a room with nine people in it and been told no in ninety seconds, and that on the eleventh of March this year she bought four blocks of continuity for sixteen thousand eight hundred pounds and read page two of a contractor's schedule in eleven minutes without knowing that she was doing anything anybody could object to, and that the schedule does not care why a thing is a service, it cares that a payment is coming in, and that she passed the whole of it on at two pounds sixty a room a week because there is no version of this in which an association with a hundred and twenty-eight people in four blocks carries sixteen thousand eight hundred out of a void allowance, and that she is going to lose Block C in June and has said so in writing.

And a member said: *that is a matter for the association and not for this Committee.*

And Marguerite Oyelaran said: **yes it is**, and that was the end of it, and the chair said thank you, and nobody moved, and there is no record anywhere in the minutes of what a lift is.

The fourth was a member asking Rook, in about twenty seconds, whether the contractor's own glossary supplied a definition of *holder*, and Ceri Vaughan, forty-five, continuity counsel, who has made exactly one offer in this city and had it refused in a room, said:

“It does. Page four hundred and sixty, sub-paragraph three. It is the schedule's own definition. It has been there since the first of March and it is not a CivicCore definition, it is the schedule's, and it would save this Committee a morning, and I am offering it in the spirit of not wasting anybody's time.”

The room went extremely quiet.

Because it was a good offer. It was, as Ceri Vaughan said, in the spirit of not wasting anybody's time. A four-hundred-and-sixty-page document had been sitting in a folder since the first of March, and it contained the thing the Committee had adjourned a fortnight for, and the thing had been in it the whole time, and the contractor had said nothing about it for three weeks.

Oren Pell leaned forward about an inch and said, to the chair, not to the room, in about eleven seconds:

“Chair. A definition given in the course of considering a document is a determination of that document. The Committee has not determined to determine Schedule 4, and if a definition of a term in a schedule is read into a minute today, that definition is the Committee’s from the moment it is minuted, and the Committee will have adopted the instrument it was asked to consider, in a room, in nine seconds, by a definition, and nobody will have voted on it.”

Marsh looked at him for two seconds.

“That is correct,” she said, to the room. “And I am going to say what I am going to do about it, and it is not the chair’s job, and I am doing it anyway, because I have been chairing this Committee for six years and I have watched two things happen in it and both of them happened because nobody in the chair stopped them in time.

“Councillor Imre, you had a motion. I am not stopping you. I am telling you what happens if you move it, and then I am going to let you decide, and I am not going to rule on it, and I would like the Committee to understand that when the motion is disposed of, it will be disposed of in this room and not in a corridor next week.”

Councillor Imre looked at his own handwriting for about four seconds, and then put the pen down, and said:

“Then I withdraw it, Chair, and I would like the withdrawal in the minute with the reason, because the reason is the registrar’s advice and the reason is good and I do not want it to be a rumour in six weeks that the Committee declined to define *holder* out of cowardice. It did not decline out of cowardice. It declined because a definition read into a minute on this afternoon would have adopted a four-hundred-and-sixty-page schedule without a division, and I am not prepared to do that and neither is anybody else in this room.”

The clerk typed it.

And at twenty past one, on a motion moved by the chair and agreed without a division, the Standing Committee on Civic Continuity **recorded that it was unable to define the term *holder* as used in Schedule 4**, and put in the minute, in the words of Councillor Imre, that this was not a failure of courage, and that the Committee would report on the definition at its next sitting if and when a definition was before it.

**The first recess was from twenty past one to half past one.**

Jonas Mercer went down to the porch, because he had been in a room for three and a half hours and because there was a new line on the public board and he wanted to see it with his own eyes at a distance of forty feet.

The returns sheet was still up, in its acrylic, with CivicCore's own return pinned over the third line, which had arrived on the fourteenth of May as Alden Rook had said in a stairwell it would, unasked, on the city's form, and which answered for the same meter room that the borough's return answered for, and there was no precedence between the two, and both entries stood, and there was nothing on the board and nothing in either return about which of them outranked the other.

And under the returns sheet, on the enamel, under the line about the claims box, a new line had printed, in the flat register, with a full stop after each word, and nobody had put it there, and it read:

> **WHERE TWO TERMS APPLY TO ONE USE AND NEITHER HAS PRECEDENCE, THE USE IS PERFORMED AND ENTERED, AND NOBODY IS BLAMED, AND THE ENTRY IS MADE TWICE.**

He read it standing up in a porch in the sun at about half past one in the afternoon and understood, in four words, that it was not about CivicCore and the borough, and that it was about a housing association's meter room and a price list, and that the sentence it was describing had been the shape of a form on a wall since the thirtieth of April, and that the honest answer to the Carrow Lane return was a number that had been in the record for two hours, and that the Lattice had arrived at the same answer from the other end.

And at ten to two he went back upstairs, and the sitting resumed at two, and the ninth line of the first return was read, and the chair read the ten field names out in order, and there was a gap where the ninth line's holder field should have been, and the room waited four seconds for somebody to fill it.

Nobody filled it.

“Then,” said Fenella Marsh, “the ninth line is a lowland water point with no number, no location of record, no district, no holder, no tier and no recorded date of deferral, and the answer to *what would be needed to restore* is a person, a price, or a schedule, and the answer to *who answers for it* is not on the form.

“And I am going to say the word, because somebody in this room is going to say it in the next ten minutes and I would rather a chair said it than a gallery. **The answer to *who answers for it* is nobody.**

“And I am going to say it in this room and not in the minute, because a chair should not put a word into a minute that a records supervisor has not yet put into a form, and I would like Ms Calder to know that I have said it in words and she may write it tomorrow, and that if anybody in this room writes it in anything tonight I would like to be told and I would like to know who.”

“Chair,” said Nina Calder, from the second row of the gallery, where she had been sitting for three hours with a folder on her knees, “the answer to *who answers for it* is a word and the word is *nobody*, and I have had that form since the thirtieth of April and I have been waiting nineteen days for a chair to ask me the question, and I would like to write it tomorrow, and I would like it written in my own words and I would like the form to be a public document, and I would like you to notice that the only reason I can do that is that you read four sentences out loud this morning.”

**Councillor Sore moved at two twenty-five, and it was not the thing anybody expected, and she explained it in four sentences, and she did not explain it.**

“I am moving something and I am going to say what it is in four sentences and then I am going to sit down, and I am not going to say why I have moved it, and I would like that in the minute.

“One: the Committee is asked to consider a document whose entire operative content is a price list.

“Two: a Committee that is unable to define the word *holder* is, I submit, entirely capable of counting what has been paid for a schedule.

“Three: I am not asking this Committee to like the number. I am asking it to be in the minute.

“Four: a Committee that will not do the arithmetic of a document it has been asked to uphold is not a Committee.”

Councillor Imre said, “I have no idea what you are doing.”

“I know you have not,” said Councillor Sore. “That is the correct condition in which to do it.”

There was no figure. The chair asked the parties present. CivicCore stated four, of which one was a tier one fee of one thousand eight hundred and eighty pounds for a lowland spur placed on a schedule on the twelfth of May, which was in the room, in a corridor, in front of a chamber, for a week. The borough stated that it held no schedule in this city and could not say what it did not hold. A housing association, from the public seats, stated sixteen thousand eight hundred pounds for the year for four nodes, and the chair repeated it back to be sure, and Marguerite Oyelaran said that it was correct, and the chair asked her to state it a second time, and she did, and said that she would like it minuted exactly as she had said it both times, and the clerk typed it.

And the motion went into the minute as an outstanding request for a return of schedule commitments, and it was the only motion anybody made all afternoon that anybody in the gallery applauded, and Fenella Marsh said, “The gallery will refrain, please, this is a Committee and not a public meeting,” and it stopped, and it had been going on for about two seconds.

At twenty to three the chair took one matter from the floor of the gallery and allowed it without a motion and without notice, on the ground that it was a point of order about the papers, and a woman of thirty-nine stood up in the second row and said one sentence and sat down, and it is in the minute.

“The Committee is asked to note that the only instrument in this city capable of producing the number it has been asked about was printed on the thirtieth of April and has no adoption behind it,” said Nell Ardent.

“That is noted,” said Fenella Marsh, “and I am going to say why it was allowed in without notice, and then I am not going to say anything else about it: it is allowed because it is a sentence about this Committee's own papers and a member may object to a paper at any time, and because the woman who said it has spent the morning in this room saying nothing at all to about eleven people who wanted to talk to her, and I noticed, and I have been sitting in a chair for six hours thinking about who is going to ask the first question in this city about a price list, and it was never going to be me.”

**At ten past three the chair made a determination, and it is the only reason anybody will read this transcript in September.**

“The Committee is not able to define the term *holder*. It is not able to determine whether it is invited to approve Schedule 4 or to receive it, and it has disposed of the discrepancy by noting it and declining to rule on it, which is the same thing and is not the same thing. It has not defeated Schedule 4 and it has not adopted it, and Schedule 4 stands, and it is referred on to the next sitting.

“Two things arise from that and I am going to make them, because they are mine to make and I have made nothing all day.

“First: the Return of a Deferred Node is received into the records of this Committee. Not adopted. Received. I am aware that the cover note says it is not before this Committee, and the chair's function is to receive documents, and I am receiving it, and the receipt does not adopt it, does not adopt anything it says, and does not make any person in it a party to anything.

“Second, and I am going to read this out twice because it is the operative sentence: **every party holding continuity in this city is required to answer a Return of a Deferred Node, in public, on the form circulated with these papers, by the next sitting, and the answers are to be read into the record of this Committee as they are received.**

“And where the answer to *who answers for it* is no party, that is to be recorded as no party, in those words, and it is to be read into the record of this Committee as no party.

“I am aware that the second half of that sentence will produce a public record of nodes in this city that have nobody answering for them. I have thought about that for four minutes and I have decided to do it, and my reasons are in this order: a node with no answering party is a fact about a node and not a fact about a person, and this city has no instrument that can record a fact about a node, and it has four hundred and sixty pages of price list, and at some point somebody is going to have to be the person who starts the record. It is me. I am the chair. I have got a gallery of forty-one people and a minute and a form, and nobody else in this building can do it, and in about four years the chair of this Committee will be somebody else and will not know why any of this happened, and that is the correct arrangement and I have thought about it and I am doing it.”

She looked up from the paper.

“And the reason, which is the only reason any of this is worth doing, and I am going to be exact about where it comes from because I have been asked twice this afternoon.

“**A party that answers may be asked what it will do, and not what it will cost.** That is not in the document. I did not write it. I have had it in my head since the second week of April and I cannot tell you where I got it from and I have been asked twice this afternoon by members of this Committee who assume it is on page nine, and it is not, and I would like whoever can tell me where it comes from to tell me, because I think it is the most useful sentence anybody has said in this borough since the third of April and I think it has been said to me four times by four people in four rooms without any of them knowing they were saying it to the chair of the Committee that will be asked about it in September.

“The sentence that *is* in the document is underneath it, at page nine, and it is the best sentence in the document, and it is the opposite of mine: **a tier may not be challenged by a person.** And I have read that at four people this month in four rooms and every one of them was being polite to me and every one of them was telling me the true thing.

“And here is what I have worked out about the two of them together, at about half past three this afternoon, and it is the only thing I have added to this determination that was not on the sheet in front of me. A tier may not be challenged by a person — so a person is never in the way, and a claim nobody can answer is a claim nobody is ever in trouble about. A party that answers may be asked what it will do — so a party that answers is in the way, permanently, and the moment that sentence exists in the record it exists in the mouth of whoever reads it out, which in this borough is apparently a woman with a form and a telephone number, and I cannot stop that and I have stopped trying.

“So the first sentence is the price of the second one, and the second one is the only reason the first one is worth anything, and I have not got a source for the first one and I have got a page number for the second one, and I am going to put both of them in the minute and let a court or a committee in six months decide whether I am a careful chair or a woman with a good instinct and a bad afternoon.”

She sat down.

Pell wrote nothing. Pell had not written anything for about eleven minutes and was not going to start.

At ten to four the chair said, “There is one matter from the gallery and I will take it and I will take it last, and I will tell the person now, so that nobody has to wonder: it is the man who brought me a document at ten to eight this morning, which is on the table in front of me and which I read in full at eleven minutes past eleven, and which is dated the twenty-eighth of April, and which names its source.”

She read the four lines again, and the four statements, and then she stopped and looked up.

“Eleven thousand two hundred and eighty pounds a year,” she said, “for six lowland water points, and nobody has asked. Thank you. That is the figure, and it is in the minute, and it was in the minute at eleven minutes past eleven, before you said it, and that is the only reason I can permit it.”

Jonas Mercer stood up in the fourth seat back on the left-hand side of the gallery, once, and did not use a microphone, and said what he had read.

“Six lowland water points. Tier one. One thousand eight hundred and eighty pounds per node per year. Eleven thousand two hundred and eighty pounds a year. And nobody has asked.”

He sat down.

“You have not asked me a question,” said Fenella Marsh, to the room, mildly. “You have read a document. That is the correct procedure and it is the only procedure available to you, and the Chair is not going to improve on it by inviting it.”

“I will take a question,” she said, to the gallery. “One.”

“Chair,” said Jonas Mercer. “Is the Committee going to require somebody to answer for the six?”

“No,” said Fenella Marsh. “The Committee cannot require a person. That is the sentence on page nine and it is the best sentence in the document and it is the reason I have been on my feet since a quarter to two doing nothing that I am allowed to do. **A tier may not be challenged by a person.** So the answer to your question is no, and the reason the answer is no is the same reason nobody has asked her.

“The answer is: a party that answers may be asked what it will do, and not what it will cost. The six nodes have no party that answers for them. That is not a mystery and it is not a scandal and it is not anybody's failure. It is the position of a thing that nobody maintains and nobody has priced and nobody can be found at, in a city where being found at is the price.

“And this Committee is not going to make a person at six water points on a lowland. I am not going to do that and I would like it minuted that I was asked to and declined in the only words available, and the words were page nine.

“What this Committee is going to do is the other thing, and it is smaller, and it is the only thing available to a chair, and it is that in September there is going to be a public, minuted record of a number of nodes in this city that have no party answering for them, and the number will be more than one, and the record will be a record of a state and not of a person, and there will not be a single name on it, and there will be no address, and no survey, and no count of anything that lives anywhere.

“Which means that in September, for the first time in the history of this city, there will be a document that says, in public, in a minute, that six lowland water points have nobody answering for them. And the next document anybody makes about those points will have to argue with that sentence, and the sentence will be there, and it will have a date on it, and it will have been read out in a room with forty-one people in it and put in a minute by a clerk who has been doing it for twenty-two years and who did not have to be asked.”

“Thank you,” said Jonas Mercer, and sat down, and did not say anything else, and that was his one question in a public room.

Marguerite Oyelaran came out of the same door at about half past four and stood on the step in the good coat with the folder and waited, and did not come over.

“Mr Mercer,” she said, when he got to the bottom of the steps. “I have been in a room for six hours and at about twenty past twelve a member of that Committee put his hand up and asked me a question about a lift, and I gave him four sentences, and a different member wrote them down in his own hand, and the chair said thank you, and that was the end of it, and there is no record anywhere in the minutes of what a lift is.”

“No.”

“And then you did your thing,” she said, “and I have not stopped being annoyed about it. You got a number into a minute at eleven minutes past eleven and the Chair read it out and said *that is the only reason I can permit it*, and she read your four statements as well, and I read the fourth one about four times this morning before I knew you had written it, and I have sat in a gallery for six hours thinking about it.

“*I do not ask any party to place the six nodes on a continuity schedule. I ask that the figure be in the record of this committee.*”

“Yes.”

“That is the only sentence anybody has said to me in six weeks that I did not have to work out the second half of on my own,” said Marguerite Oyelaran. “And on Thursday morning I was in a corridor with a circular with a paragraph number in it and a queue of eleven people on my stairs, and on Friday morning I was on a telephone at half past eleven telling a man in a trade association that my morning was gone, and this afternoon a councillor from Salt Row asked me in front of forty-one people whether a lift is a service, and I told him the whole of it, and he wrote it down, and it is in a minute in a building in the Old Ward, and it will be read by nobody, ever, and my afternoon is still gone.”

She put the folder under her arm.

“I am not withdrawing my objection to the number. I want that said. I said *nobody has asked me* on the twenty-eighth of April on a wall in my own hand because I wanted to see what you would do with it, and what you did with it was put it in a minute without a speech and without a list and without a name on a street, and I have not had that from anybody in eleven years and I am not going to be the person who says it was the wrong thing to do, because on Thursday morning I signed a man's name onto a page because his block is on a photocopy and I would have done the wrong thing too.

“And on Wednesday, in the council chamber, a councillor asked whether this association should be in the room at all. I would like you to think about that sentence once before you go, because it is going to be in every trade circular in this borough by Friday, and it was not about me. It was about the fact that a housing association is the only kind of body in this city that has one person’s money inside another person’s rent and no instrument anywhere that anybody can see.”

She went down the steps and got into a car and drove off with her own money, which she has done since 2011.

Under the glass canopy, in the only dry place in that half of the city, with the returns sheet in its acrylic and the claims box beneath it with one card in it about a stair, and the enamel strip at the frame with a new line on it about two terms and one use and an entry made twice.

And pinned to the frame, on a piece of A4 with a council crest on it, in a typeface of about 2011, a notice that had gone up that morning while he was in a room:

> **BOROUGH HEAT THRESHOLD PROTOCOL — ACTIVATION ON THREE CONSECUTIVE DAYS AT 27°C OR ABOVE. FROM THE DATE OF ACTIVATION, RESIDENTIAL CARE PREMISES ARE REQUIRED TO CONFIRM OCCUPANCY AND A NAMED RESPONSIBLE PERSON FOR EVERY ADDRESS IN THEIR CUSTODY. ENQUIRIES: [a number].**

Three consecutive days at twenty-seven degrees. It had been twenty-nine on the Old Ward bridge at four o'clock that afternoon. The next four days were forecast at thirty, thirty-one, twenty-nine and twenty-eight.

The spring tides were on the twenty-first and the twenty-second and the twenty-eighth and the twenty-ninth and the first of June, and the tide table was in a drawer in the shop and he had checked it on Saturday for a completely different reason.

He stood and looked at it for a while, and then he took out the pad with his left hand and wrote, at the bottom of a page that already had the twelfth of October on it, four lines, and they are the last four lines he wrote in the month of May, and no one in this city read them until the summer.

> *It is not defeated. It is referred on. It is going to cost somebody a lift in a block and a gate at a hospital and a handover that two tired porters will read on a Saturday night in October without knowing what they are doing, and it is going to cost all three of those to people who have not got a vote.*
> *A city can keep a service floor. That is the half I still believe and I cannot prove and neither can they. The other half is the price of the floor when somebody cannot pay it, and nobody in this city has yet had to answer that question about a person, because a person cannot be challenged and a tier can, and every single thing that happened in that room today was arranged around that one sentence, and the sentence is on page nine, and it was written by the contractor as a protection, and it has worked.*
> *So the question, in the only words I have got, and I am putting it on this page because nobody is going to ask me and I have been told twice not to ask anybody: can a city keep a shared service floor when urgent access is legally priced — and when a resident refuses the price, who pays for the refusal?*
> *Not the association. Not the contractor. Not the city. Somebody in that block, on the first of June, in a heat, in a room with a fixed stopcock in a cupboard nobody has a key to.*
