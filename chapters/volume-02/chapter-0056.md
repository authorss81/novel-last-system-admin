# Chapter 0056 — Do Not Come

Tomas Renn had been in the shop yard on the Sunday morning of the twenty-seventh of April with a printout in his hand, and Jonas Mercer read it standing up against the wall with his tea going cold on the crate, and it was the fourth page of a covering document, and it was Tomas's own finding being used.

> **Continuity architecture is not a source of service failure in the reviewed incidents. The 3 April and 15 April 2014 disruptions are properly characterised as rostering and continuity-guarantee failures, not design failures. Accordingly the remedy available to this committee is a procurement remedy: continuity should be held by a party able to guarantee it, and the guarantee should be priced and should be enforceable.**
>
> *(CivicCore Consortium, covering submission to the Standing Committee on Civic Continuity, Schedule 4 (continuity pricing), 24 April 2014. Citing: Renn, T. — Minority submission to the Standing Committee, 18 April 2014, finding 1.)*

He read it three times.

“They have quoted you,” said Jonas Mercer.

“They have quoted me,” said Tomas Renn.

He said it without any heat at all. He put the printout on the crate, squared it to the edge with the side of his hand, and sat down on an upturned bucket, which he does not do, because he does not sit in this yard.

“Finding one,” he said. “*The civic term architecture was not the failure on the third of April and was not the failure on the fifteenth.* Nine words, my name, my hand, four days early, and a minority report attached because Ada would not sign and Cuthbert thought the last sentence was a provocation and he was right and it was not taken out.” He looked at the printout. “And in four days it is the load-bearing sentence in a contractor's covering submission, and it is load-bearing in a way I could not have predicted in eleven years of asking this city a question.”

“How is it load-bearing.”

“Because it is true, and because it is the only sentence anybody has produced this month that says a design did not fail.” He turned the page over. “Read the paragraph after it.”

Jonas read the paragraph after it. It said that continuity should be held by a party able to guarantee it, that the guarantee should be priced, and that a priced guarantee is preferable to an unaccountable queue.

“Which is a good argument,” said Tomas Renn.

“Which is a good argument.”

“It is a *good* argument, and that is the cost, and I told Bea Osunde the cost on the Friday in the shop and I told her in about the words I am about to use with you, and I have been sitting here since ten o'clock on a Sunday morning waiting to see whether I can still say it out loud to somebody.” He rubbed his face. “I have said for four months that a city cannot keep naming people one at a time until the water comes, and the water came, and eleven people were in a Methodist hall, and this is the first document anybody has produced that says *do not name people, hold the guarantee* — and it is the first document anybody has produced that says that, and it is a contractor, and it is using my sentence to say it.”

“It does not have your whole submission.”

“No. It has finding one and it does not have the finding that came under it.” He took the printout back. “Because I did not put one under it. I put the architecture finding in and then I stopped, because the fourth question was still open and I was not going to write an answer I did not have. So there is a document in the world with my name on the top of it that says a design was not to blame, and there is no document in the world with my name on the top of it that says what it should be instead, and in June somebody will stand up in that room and read finding one and ask what the union proposes, and the honest answer is: nothing, because a demand became a finding, and a finding can only be cited.”

He said the last four words with a kind of flatness that Jonas Mercer had not heard from him before.

“Cuthbert Nkemelu telephoned me on the Saturday morning,” said Tomas Renn. “He is one of the two who did not sign. He has been quoted. Not the submission — the minority report. The whole of it, in a document going into a committee folder, as *the shop stewards Ada Kwarteng and Cuthbert Nkemelu consider that the last sentence of this submission is a provocation*, and it is a footnote, and it is a footnote about my two colleagues, in a contractor's document, and it is going to be read out in a chamber in June by somebody who did not write it and who does not know what a provocation is.”

“And Ada.”

“Ada has not telephoned me,” said Tomas Renn, “and Ada voted against the submission and Ada will vote against it again, and Ada is going to be asked by two of the people in that room why the shop has a finding with a minority report and no demand, and Ada is going to tell them, and she will tell them well, and she will not mention that she and I did not speak about it first.” He looked at Jonas. “That is the cost. I have paid it. I knew I was paying it on the Friday morning and I paid it anyway and I would pay it again and I am not going to be a man who tells you the price was a surprise.”

“Can it be fixed before the nineteenth.”

“No,” said Tomas Renn.

He said it the way he said *a refusal ends the shift and not the job*, which is a thing he does about four times a year.

“Nothing in that folder is going to be unquoted between now and the nineteenth of May, and I am not going to try. I have spent a month being a man who put a thing in a document so that it could not be softened, and this is the other side of that, and the other side is that a document is for reading and I have made one worth reading.” He folded the printout in half. “And there is one thing I am going to ask you for and it is not help, and if it turns into help I will take it back.”

“All right.”

“Do not come to the nineteenth of May.”

Jonas Mercer stood in a yard in River Stacks on a Sunday morning and found that the sentence took him the length of one breath to get past, and that the reason it took that long was that he had been thinking about going for nine days.

“Why.”

“Because of what is written at the top of the register,” said Tomas Renn, “and because of what is not written at it, and because in that room on the nineteenth of May somebody from my party is going to stand up and say that a shop steward's finding in April is now being used by a contractor, and they are going to be right, and they are going to say it, and the only thing that will make them stop is the person whose name is in the register being in the gallery, and I will not have that sentence started by a continuity contractor in front of eleven people.”

“So it becomes yours.”

“It is already mine,” said Tomas Renn. “I wrote it down. In a document. On the eighteenth of April, with my name on it, so that it would survive me being wrong. That is what a document is *for*, Mr Mercer, and the whole of the last month is the first time in my working life that I have understood that sentence, and I am not going to have it turned into a man in a gallery being visible.” He looked up. “I am not asking you to be loyal. I have no claim on you and the shop is not a party and I said so in a doorway in April and I am saying it again. I am asking you to do the thing you have never once done in five weeks, which is to stay out of a room where you would be useful.”

“All right,” said Jonas Mercer.

“Say it properly.”

“I will not come to the nineteenth of May,” said Jonas Mercer, “and I will not be in the room, and I will not send anybody, and I will not write anything for anybody to read out.”

“Good,” said Tomas Renn. “Now go and do your round, and do not tell Bea I sat on a bucket, because she will make a thing of it.”

Oren Pell telephoned at about eleven and it took ninety seconds.

“You are not coming to the pre-sitting on the thirtieth,” he said.

“I had not intended to come to the pre-sitting.”

“You are not coming because you are not on anything, and I want to be more exact than that, and I want to be exact because in ten years somebody is going to write a history of this and I am not going to be in it.” The registrar's voice was flat and slightly bored, which is how it reads when he is being exact on purpose. “A pre-sitting of the Standing Committee on Civic Continuity is a closed meeting of the committee. It is not a public hearing. There is no gallery, there is no public box, there is no minute that anybody outside that room may read, and there is no business on the agenda at which a person who is not a member, not a party, and not an officer of the department has any standing whatever. You are none of those three. You are a man in a register that I invented in a hurry on Saturday morning to keep a burden off a street that has no address, and I have been waiting nine days to say this to you, and you have not telephoned me once, which I took to mean that you had understood it.”

“I had not understood it. I understood it about a minute ago.”

“Good,” said Oren Pell. “Then stay out of that room, and be easy about it, because you are going to be in four hundred of them over the next five years and the whole of the craft is knowing which ones matter. On the nineteenth of May there is a public hearing with a gallery and a minute and a public box, and you may be in the back of that one if you can stand being in the back of it. In the pre-sitting you would be a rumour with a lanyard.”

“And CivicCore?”

“Alden Rook will be in the pre-sitting in a room with no gallery, with no minute, and with no public box, and he will do it because the whole of the work of that document is done in rooms like that and he knows it, and I have never been able to make that fact sound as serious as it is.” A pause. “And he will be very good, and he will be right about a third of it, and the third he is right about is the third that wins, and I have no standing in that room either, because I am an officer of the department and the department has a position and the position is that the schedule is a schedule.”

“Nina Calder says he will be there.”

“Nina Calder has been putting Schedule 4 into three committee systems since Wednesday evening and she will have put it into this one by lunchtime tomorrow and Rook will have read the covering note before he gets in the car,” said Oren Pell. “That woman is not on my staff and I have never once been able to say thank you to her, and if you see her on the way to wherever you are going, say thank you to her from me, and do not put it in a file.”

The Saint Orra bridge at about half past three on the Sunday afternoon had a west lane, a westbound trolley signal, a tram deck and a barrier end, and the south span was still isolated and the empty-crossing clause was still in the file, and there were two handovers in the crossing log from the eighteenth of April with *both* written under the fifty-eight, and Dev Singh was on the west approach with his coat off.

“The two handovers are still in the book,” he said. “I have told three people this week. I am going to keep telling people.”

“Dev —”

“I have had it on a file for nine days,” said Dev Singh, “and in nine days I have not been given a single hour of paid time on it, and the file is where it was on the eighteenth, which is in a drawer, and I have asked for it twice, and both times I have been told that it is a historic term and historic terms are not resourced. And on Tuesday in June, with no water on that deck, that clause is still the reason an ambulance loses eleven minutes at this bridge, and I am not going to be a man who says a flood took it out of the file.”

“I have not been able to do anything about it.”

“I know that,” said Dev Singh, “and I am not asking you to, because you are the only person I know who has been asked to do something about it and I have been trying to work out for nine days why I never actually asked you, and I have worked it out, and here it is, and I am going to say it once and then I am going to go and open the barrier for a tram.

“You have got a bag and a lamp and there is a man on the card and a register with your name in it and every one of those is a reason I do not have to ask you, and every one of those is why I would not ask you if I had wanted to. If I ask you, then in four months I have got a technician who owns my clause. And you would own it, and you would keep it, and you would be the man who could not put it down, and in about two years somebody would use you to say the city has an administrator who happens to care about a bridge. So I am going to fight it for eighteen months, on my own, badly, on a file with no hours on it, and I would like that written down by you somewhere that is not this bridge.”

“I will write it down.”

“Do not write it down in the shop,” said Dev Singh. “Bea reads everything. Write it on a card and put it in the coat, and if you ever tell anybody I said it I will deny it, and I will not be lying.”

And then he said the other thing, on the way to the barrier, without stopping, the way he says the things he actually means.

“And while you are here. I have run the numbers on your nineteen.”

“What nineteen.”

“Median first attendance on a failed civic hinge. Nineteen days. That is the department's, it is on a laminated card in dispatch, and it has been the same for four years, and it has got worse since the thirty-first of March because two contractor crews came off this queue and it went back to where it was in 2011.” He pulled the barrier across. “Now here is the number that is going to be in my head for the rest of my working life and it is not nineteen. It is forty minutes. On the fifteenth of April a term was not in force for forty hours, and a woman of eighty-four was in bay six, and the department would have got to that hinge in nineteen days. So write nineteen down next to forty somewhere, and do not let anybody tell you a number is a detail, and do not tell me I have made that up, because I have been doing this for twenty-six years and I have never once had a number mean nothing.”
