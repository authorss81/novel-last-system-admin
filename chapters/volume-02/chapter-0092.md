# Chapter 0092 — Somebody Who Is Not A Doctor

**Thursday the fourteenth of August 2014, twenty-five minutes past nine in the morning, the shop.**

The telephone went at about a quarter past nine and Jonas Mercer had the bench cleared and one hand on a chisel and the other on a piece of ash he was not going to be able to close his right hand round for another month anyway.

"Mercer. Achebe. Two minutes and I am on a van."

"Go."

"**Tuesday the sixteenth of September.** There is a restriction on the lowland spur — 6-L/16 — notice served Friday, nine o'clock, to hold it off for four hours while they rebuild the screen. It is a booked restriction, it is on a node that has a fee on it because CivicCore paid the thousand eight hundred and eighty pounds with a power of attorney in May, and so it has a stated time and a party to telephone, which since May has meant me.

"And I am going to be in Gantry Ward. I have known it since the second of July. There is a man coming out from Manchester on the Tuesday with a socket set and a camera and I have to be in a stairwell with him for a level-three query on a different file, and I cannot be on a lowland at seven in the morning and in a stairwell at half past ten, and I have tried to make it work twice on paper and it does not work.

"So that is the telephone call," said Royden Achebe. "**I am not asking you to do it.** I want that on your side of the line before I say anything else, because I have had this number since the eighteenth of May and I have known since the eighteenth of May that I would be the one who rang it, and I have had eleven weeks to work out what I was going to say, and about nine minutes of the eleven weeks were about the screen and the rest of it was about not pretending."

"You are not asking me to do it."

"I am not asking you to do it, I am not asking Emeka Rusk to do it, and I am not asking anybody to do it. **The duty is mine. I have held it since the seventeenth of May. I cannot give it back, the offer was made inside the hour and the offer does not transfer a claim, and the only door out of it is a successor who consents, and there is nobody in this city I can ask to consent to a Tuesday morning on a lowland.**"

"Then what is the call for."

Royden Achebe took about four seconds.

"**It is so that there are two of us who know the time.**" He said it flatly. "That is the whole of it and I have thought about it for eleven weeks and it is a small thing. On the last one — the twenty-fourth of May — the duty waited and nobody was at the place and Emeka entered the fault in his own hand and you did not enter it, because entering a fault is an act by a person with standing and the person with standing was in Gantry Ward with a report. And then he was done and I was out of it, and nobody was in the wrong, and a woman with a trolley at the bottom of Quarry Street was short of water for a day and seven hours and it is on a form with your name at the bottom of it.

"I am not asking you to take the duty. **I am asking you to turn up, and to keep your hands off the plate, and to write down the time you arrived and the time you left, and to have it entered twice, because that is what happened in May and the two entries are the only honest thing anybody produced out of it.** And if the man who comes from CivicCore wants a piece of paper out of me he can want one, and the answer will be the same as it was in May, which is that there is none.

"That is the call. Do not ring me back, I have a van at half past."

**Thursday the fourteenth of August 2014, about two in the afternoon, the counter, the records office.**

"It came back signed," said Nina Calder.

"When."

"Wednesday the thirteenth. It went Monday morning on a departmental van to Gantry Ward and it came back Wednesday afternoon signed, and the name on it is the name of the officer who signed it, which is a man I have never met and I am not going to describe to you."

"His post."

She looked at him for about two seconds over the top of the machine.

"You had a name and you did not ask for it, and you had a post and you did, and out of the two you have chosen the smaller one, and I am going to give you the smaller one because I am not going to give you the other." She turned round a sheet, which is a thing she does four times a year. "**Level-three acceptance officer. Field services, Gantry Ward.** That is the post and that is the district and that is all of it, and I am going to say why that is all of it, and then I am going to go back to this woman's folder.

"Royden Achebe filed eleven pages and nine hundred words on the twenty-ninth of May in a room with two doors and nobody in it who works in this building. He did it correctly. **What came back on Wednesday is a signature by a man whose job is to open trays.** He has not read it, he could not have read it, he was not asked to and there was no reason in the world why he should have, and he signed a form that says in a column *reported not maintainable, release recommended, not released*, and he did it in about ninety seconds, and he will go on opening trays for another six years.

"And in about four months that signature is going to be the only thing in this city that stands between four tonnes of stone and a sill beam and a chamber that a man cannot reach, and it is going to stand there because nobody was ever going to think that the man who signs things deserves to be told what he is signing. **You cannot move a signature. You said that to a man in a corridor last week and you were right and it is the sentence I would put on the door of this office.**"

"You are going to ask me if I am going to send him a copy, and I am not, and I have not done anything about four things this year and one of them was a page in a bin." She turned back to the machine. "You are not entitled to a copy of it and neither am I and I am not going to photocopy it for you in a drawer, and if you want to see it you will have to ask the department that holds it, and they will say no, and that is the correct answer and you should take it."

He was on level two at Saint Orra west at half past three that afternoon for a bracket on the shop's riser cabinet that has needed doing since the eighth of June, and he went up the stairs past the card table the way he goes up every staircase in that building, which is slowly, and he stopped at the card table because Mina Okafor had two fingers up and was looking at him.

"You've not been in since July," she said.

"I've been in."

"You've been on level zero and out the front." She put her pen down. "**A man asked me yesterday at about half past three whether a supplier needs a letter or a name, and I said a name, and he has not been back, and the whole corridor has been speculating about it for twenty-four hours and none of it is right.**"

"Who asked you."

"Ronan." She said it the way she said things that were about to be difficult. "Ronan Vasquez. Estates. He has been on that floor since before the department changed its name twice, and he came up on level one and asked me whether a supplier needed a letter or a name, and I said a name, and he said *a name is a person*, and I said yes, and he stood there for about a minute and a half. And then he asked me what the twenty minutes a week actually is. And I said it is reading the drum and writing your name under the week. And he said *right*, and went down, and he has not come back up and I have not told anybody and I am telling you because you are the only person in this borough who is not going to go and ask him whether he meant it."

"What did he say to you."

"He said one thing before he went." She turned a card over and showed it to him and did not let go of it. "**It is not a service, it is a person reading a strip, and I have held the maintenance of that room's pipework since 2011, and if anybody makes this a rota I will stop doing it and they can have the paragraph number instead.** Which is — " she looked up, " — Mr Mercer, that is the fourth thing somebody has said in this building this year that I have not got a wallet for, and there are four hundred and twenty women in this borough whose instruments come out of that room and **not one of them knows it has been said, and the man who said it has not signed anything, and the acceptance goes back to the supplier on Monday if it is not on it by Friday.**"

**Friday the fifteenth of August 2014, ten past four in the afternoon, level zero, Saint Orra west, outside the decontamination room.**

The decontamination log is on the tray on the wall by the door on level zero and it is a printed grid of fifty-two Sundays with a printed box for a temperature and a printed box for a signature, and on the Friday afternoon of the fifteenth of August a man of forty-eight in a work top with a council fleece over it and nothing on the front of it signed the supplier's acceptance, and the acceptance is a form, and the form is not the log.

The log is a Sunday. The acceptance is not a Sunday. That is the whole difference and it took eleven weeks and a non-conformance and four hundred and twenty women to arrive at it.

He came up with a flat sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet and he put it on the bench and he did not put it on a hook and he did not put it on a board, and Jonas Mercer noticed that and had to stand still for a second.

"Ronan."

"Mr Mercer." He had a pen in his hand and the cap off and he had not signed anything yet. "I want to say what this is and what it isn't and then I'll sign it, and I'd rather say it out loud than write it, because if I write it then it's a condition and a condition ends up on a plate in about four years and I have spent eleven years taking things off lists."

"Go on."

"**The twenty minutes is not a service.** It is a person reading a strip. I have held the maintenance of the pipework in that room since 2011 because the riser came up through a duct that nobody else would take and I took it because it was a pipe. I am not on a rota. I am not clinical. I do not have a badge for that floor and I have not had one since March and I have been on it anyway four times on a Sunday because there was nothing to stop me and because it is a Sunday.

"And here is the thing nobody has thought through, and I have thought about it since yesterday afternoon and it has taken me the whole of that, so I am going to say it once and then I am going to sign.

"**Signing a log is not a proposal to make it a service.** Nobody in this building has worked that out in eleven weeks and I want it on the form, in my words, because the second it stops being a person reading a strip and starts being verification *by the estates office*, it is a duty with a department against it, and then it is a rota, and then I am a name in a field, and then in about two years somebody can put me in a room and ask me what I will do, and I have watched four people get put in rooms this year and not one of them wanted to be in it.

"So it goes on the form. And I'm not asking anybody to agree with it. I'm writing down what I am, so that in about four years when somebody wants a rota for that room, there's a piece of paper in this city that says in the hand of a man who actually does it, that it isn't one." He looked up. "**Do not put it on the corridor board. That board is a rota. My name goes on a log and it does not go on that board, and both of those are true and neither of them cancels the other.**"

"Understood."

"I'm not finished. Two things." He put the pen down flat. "**One: I am not on that board and I never will be, and I know there are people on that floor who think a rota would fix it and I have read what Dr Iqbal said in June because Fiona Grieve read it out in a corridor, and I am telling you I know what it says and I am telling you I agree with her, and I am doing this anyway, and those two things going together in one man is the whole of the design of this building.**

"**Two: it is not going to work.** Sunday, first one, a quarter past eight in the morning, and I will be here, and I will be here on the seventeenth, and the twenty-fourth, and the thirty-first, and I will do it until the end of October because that is when the year ends. **After October it will lapse, because I have got a job and a job in November has a department in it, and a department is a rota, and if I do it in November then the twenty minutes has got a department on it and I said the thing out loud on Friday afternoon and I am not going to un-say it in November.**

"And I want to be fair about why I am here on a Friday afternoon and it is not the supplier's clock, because the supplier granted nothing until October anyway and the eleven weeks in that engineer's sentence in July runs from the day he raised it. **It is not the clock. It is that a form has been sitting on a wall for eleven weeks with fifty-two boxes in it and one of them has my name going into it today, and after today the thing on that wall is a record instead of an absence, and there is a difference, and the difference is the whole of what I have got to give.**

"And I am not going to be asked to be careful about that, and if I am, I will say this again, and the third time somebody asks me that in this corridor I am going to stop answering and that is not a threat, it is what I do."

Jonas Mercer said, "You have not been asked."

"No," said Ronan Vasquez. "**Nobody has asked me anything. That is why I am here and that is the whole of the reason and I have got to get this signed before the office shuts because the acceptance has to be in a tray and not on a bench.**"

He signed at twenty past four. His name is **R. VASQUEZ, ESTATES**, and it is on a supplier's acceptance and on a piece of paper in a plastic wallet, and it is not on the board in the corridor on level two, and it never will be.

Ronan came back up with the form at about ten to five and Calder was not there, and Calder had sent the sheet.

It was a single sheet, headed with the supplier's own reference, stapled to the second page of the acceptance, and it was a copy, and at the top of it in a typewriter face there was no crest and no form number and no index of any kind, and the four lines on it were in a hand he knew from a counter and a bin tray.

> **THIS RECORD IS ONE THING AT ONE TIME AND IT IS NOT A SERVICE AND IT IS UNPAID.**
> **A PERSON MAY REFUSE TO BE COUNTED AND THE COUNT THEN SAYS SO AND SAYS HOW MANY REFUSED.**
> **ANY PROPOSAL TO MAKE IT A SERVICE MUST COME BACK TO THAT TABLE.**
> **THE FOURTH LINE IS COPIED HERE WORD FOR WORD, WITHOUT AMENDMENT AND WITHOUT A SUMMARY, BECAUSE THIS IS THE FIRST DOCUMENT IN THIS CITY THAT DESCRIBES THE PRACTICE AND IT IS A COMPLIANCE DOCUMENT.**

Fiona Grieve had said that third sentence on the ninth of June and had said it in a room of nine people and had said she had no standing and had spoken three sentences in the whole morning, and this was the third one, and it had been copied into a supplier's acceptance form by a records supervisor of nineteen years who had been told in June to do it and had waited until somebody else had signed first.

"That's Calder's," said Jonas Mercer.

"That is Calder's," said Ronan Vasquez. "**And I want you to understand what she has just done, because I have read it twice and I am an estates supervisor and I know what a compliance file is.** She has put a governance sentence into a compliance document. The fourth line says any proposal to make this a service has to go back to a table of nine people in a room on level two. It is now in a supplier's acceptance, on a supplier's reference, with a paragraph number against it. **So the first person in this city who tries to turn twenty minutes a week into a rota will not be arguing with a doctor and a rota and a porter. They will be arguing with a piece of paper that is already in the file, and it will not be me holding it and it will not be them, and neither of us will have to be the person who says no.**

"That's not a fix," said Jonas Mercer. "That's a door somebody else left open in June."

"It is the same thing," said Ronan Vasquez, "and I have been doing this for eleven years and it is nearly always the same thing, and you have got about a fortnight of the summer left and you look like a man who has been counting."

**Friday the fifteenth of August 2014, ten past ten at night, the shop.**

He did not go up to level two. He went and looked at the board in the corridor on the way out, because he has looked at that board every time he has been in the building since the fourteenth of March, and it is still the same board.

A weekly grid made out of a magnetic strip and about forty magnets.

**The Thursday row has a gap in it from 11:00 to 13:30 with no name against it, and it has had no name against it since the second of April.** Above the gap, a printed card in Bram Osei's own words, refused since the first week of March, asked for by him twice since then that it stay exactly where it is: *cannot do Thursdays, no reason I can give, sorry.* Under his card, at a different angle, a card in Priya Ranganathan's handwriting, added on the second of June at her own insistence: *Priya Ranganathan held this line 2 May – 1 June, paid out of the 2011 coffee-and-cakes appeal. I asked for my name to stay here. It goes in the minutes unedited.*

The terms on the plates by the door were six days. The gate still ran on its local term.

**And there was no new name on the board, and the gap was where the gap had been on the fourth of March, and a man who signs twenty minutes a week on a Sunday had said out loud on level one that his name would never go on it, and he was right, and nobody was going to make him.**

Fifty-seven days to Saturday the eleventh of October. Forty-seven to Wednesday the first, and the second of those is the one with a clause in it, and neither of them is his, and he has not been asked about either of them since the eighth of July and he is not going to be.

**Monday the eighteenth of August 2014, ten past nine in the morning, Saint Orra west.**

The supplier telephoned at about ten past nine that morning and it was not the engineer who came in July on the telephone, and it was not anybody this borough has a name for, and he said four sentences.

"**Part of it we can grant and part of it we cannot.** The going-forward verification, yes — signed, twenty minutes a week, a named person, that closes the forward half. **The historical half we cannot grant.** Every Sunday on that grid that has a tick in the temperature box has an empty signature box, and the boxes cannot be signed now, and a compliance record that cannot be reconstructed is not reconstructed. So the non-conformance is not withdrawn. **It carries forward and it is on the annual report in the autumn with its paragraph number, and it will read that the verification was not evidenced for a period of three months, and that it is now evidenced weekly by a named person, and that the named person is not on a rota.**"

"And the paragraph number."

"The paragraph number is ours and it does not go away because a man in a work top signed a piece of paper. **That is what a paragraph number is. It is a thing that is true, and a signature is a thing that is done, and in about nine years somebody is going to read the report in a room and put the two side by side and the report is going to be the one that is still there.**"

Nobody said anything for a while. A trolley went past with a linen bag on it and the wheels went over the threshold the way they always do.

Yvonne Amoah came past at about ten past ten with a card under her arm and stopped and read the sheet over Mina Okafor's shoulder, and said one thing, which was to nobody in particular and to everybody.

"**Then the report is the one that is still there, and it will say a name.** And I want it minuted — I am not on a rota either, I have said that in this corridor in April, and I have watched two doctors and one porter and one man in estates give this away in five months and every one of them did it for a good reason and every one of them is now in a paragraph. **It was not a rota on the Wednesday. It is a paragraph in the autumn. And that is worse, and it is worse because you can appeal a rota and you cannot appeal a paragraph.**"

And then Dr Sanaa Iqbal came out of the clinic at about ten past ten, and she had not asked anybody for anything, and Jonas Mercer did not ask her anything either, and she spoke to him for about ninety seconds on the way past, and she gave him something.

"Nine," she said. "Bay nine, level one, the bed by the window. The node at the end of that bed has been going since the fourth of July and the plate says the thing it says when there is no last verified essential use, and it is not the plate that is wrong. **I am giving you that as a fact. I am not asking you to mend it and I am not asking you to look at it, and I am not going to be argued out of the rest of it.**

"And I am giving it to you for a reason and I want the reason said out loud, because you are the only person in this city who cannot be asked to do anything and that is the only reason you are safe to hand a fact to. **Somebody in this building will be asked about that bed this year and if I hand it to the estates office it becomes their rota and if I hand it to a manager it becomes a line on a board, and if I keep it, then in about four months I am a doctor who knew and did not say.** And you are a man with a register and no grade who is not on anything, and there is no instrument anywhere in this borough that can put a duty on you, and I have known that since the nineteenth of June when a chair said it out loud three times in a room and meant every word.

"So that is what you are. **You are the person who can be given a fact and cannot be asked for anything, and it is the only safe place in this city and there is nobody in it but you, and I have thought about that for eleven weeks and I have decided it is better than the other four things and I am not going to explain the other four things.**"

She went. He did not write it down for about an hour and a half, and then he wrote it down, and the entry has the date and the time and the bed and no name, because the last part is hers and she did not give it.
