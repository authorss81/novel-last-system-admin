# Chapter 0079 — A Board With Nine Seats On It

He went up the path at about eleven on the morning of Sunday the first of June, on foot, in the sun, with a bottle of water in his bag that he did not offer anybody and did not drink from, and he had not been asked to go, and he was not a party, and there was nothing he was going there to do.

That was the whole of the reason, and it took about a hundred and nine steps and about four minutes, and at the top of the slip he stood still for a moment the way he had learned to stand still at the top of a slip in April, because the light is different up there and there is more of it than there is anywhere else in this city.

The plate on the frame at the top of the slip is the 6-L/14 plate and it is one of the oldest castings on the chain, and it had picked up a line some time on Friday night, in the flat register, with a full stop after each word:

> **WHERE TWO INSTRUMENTS DESCRIBE ONE PIECE OF GROUND AND NEITHER KNOWS THE OTHER EXISTS, BOTH OF THEM ARE TRUE, AND THERE IS NO RULE ANYWHERE THAT ANYBODY HAS TO READ BOTH.**

He read it standing up in thirty degrees with the lowland behind him going white in the sun.

Then he went in, and told Margo Doré what he had put in a minute in the Old Ward on the nineteenth of May, in about four minutes, in the order it happened, with the date on the sheet and the hand it was written in, and he did not put the sheet on the table and he did not read it out, because she has said in her own kitchen that a document is a thing you hand to a person and not a thing you perform at somebody.

She listened to the whole of it standing up with her back against the dresser and her arms folded, and did not interrupt, and when he had finished she said:

“Say the number again.”

“Eleven thousand two hundred and eighty pounds a year. Six points. A thousand eight hundred and eighty each. Tier one.”

“And you did not ask me.”

“No.”

“You did not ask me, and you did not ask Ottie, and you did not ask Bo, and you went into a room with forty-one chairs in it and you read a number out that prices six water points, and one of the six is the stand at the top of this slip, and the chair who read it out said in the same breath that the Committee is not going to make a person at six water points on a lowland and would like it minuted that she was asked to and declined in the only words available.”

“Yes.”

“Right,” said Margo Doré. “Now I am going to say two things and I am going to say them in this order and then I am not going to come back to either of them for as long as it takes you to be able to live in this city.

“**The first one is that you have not done it again and you have done it worse.** You have promised me a sentence and a fortnight, and you have kept the sentence, and you have used it, and you have used it on my water, in a room, without me, and I have been sitting here for thirteen days waiting to find out whether anybody in this city would ask me a question before they asked a question about me, and I have decided that I do not want to know the answer to that any more, because the answer is going to be no, and I have had it four times, and this morning it came with a price on it in your mouth.

“And the second one is that **I am not angry with you**, and I want that said out loud in a kitchen, because you are going to go away from here and do something with it and I would rather you did not.

“You did the thing you said you would do. I asked you for a sentence and you went and found where a sentence had been printed on a plate in four rooms in this borough and you took it to a chair. I would have said no. I have said no to you three times and I would have said no again, and my no would have been ten words and it would not have been in a minute, and in September when somebody comes with a piece of paper and a date, the sentence on the paper will be mine and it will have been in a room.

“So there it is. That is the whole of what I have got and I have had it ready since the twentieth of May and I have not been able to use it because you have not been in this kitchen since the thirtieth of April, and I have been aware for thirteen days that the not coming was the politest thing you have ever done and it has cost me thirteen days of not knowing.”

She got the jug and put two cups down, which she has not done since the thirtieth of April, and did not offer him any information about the six, and he did not ask her for any, and neither of them said the word *schedule* in that kitchen for the whole of the twenty minutes he was in it.

What she did do, at about ten past twelve, standing at the sink with her back to him, was the thing that has to be said on this street before anybody in this borough finds out about it in August and comes up here with a form and a photograph.

“I am going to say a thing now so that it is said, and then I am going to wash these two cups, and you are not going to argue with me about it and I am not going to hear it.

“A man will come up this path in about two months with a pump. Not a man like you. A man with a van and a clipboard and a scheme and a photograph of a stand, and he will have money and he will have a way of making it look like a gift, and he will be correct — that is the thing about them, they are always correct, and the water will come and the stand will work and there will be six families on this street who have not carried a bucket since a Tuesday in May.

“And I am going to say no to him. In my own words, to his face, in this kitchen, and I am not going to be talked out of it by anybody, and I would like it in the record that I said it on the first of June, in advance, to a man who came here for nothing, and not to a pump.

“Because a pump is a thing, and a thing can be bought, and the day this street has a pump somebody is going to ask what it is attached to. **And the answer to that will be a paper, and the paper will have one of my neighbours’ names in a field on it, because that is how any of you have ever kept a door open since the third of April, and I have watched all of you do it and I have not done it once, and I am sixty-three and I would like to be able to say that in about ten years’ time without it being a lie.**”

The three positions were on the wall where they have been since March, with a name against each, and a fourth line under them with a pencil ring round a space with nothing in it, and the ring had not been gone over since the fourteenth of March and Jonas Mercer did not look at it for longer than a second and a half.

Nobody was added to the rota. There was no rota. Bo Trimble came in at about half past twelve with a bag of shopping and stood in the doorway and said, “There is no water,” and Jonas Mercer said, “I know,” and Bo Trimble said, “You are not going to fix it,” and Jonas Mercer said, “No,” and Bo Trimble said, “Right,” and went home, and nothing was added to him, and he was findable at four hundred yards in a lane, and had been since the nineteenth of April, and would be at six hundred tomorrow.

---

The room on the second floor of the civic offices on Monday the second of June had a table, six chairs, a fan in a window that did not work, fourteen returned forms in two plastic wallets, a records supervisor on her own time, a committee clerk who had booked the room and had no business in it, a deputy registrar who had come of his own accord and had said so, a resilience officer with a plastic sleeve, a chief officer of a housing association who had come on her own account and had told nobody she was coming, and an estates officer from a hospital who had answered a form in nine minutes and had brought the form with him because he wanted it recorded that he had answered it in nine minutes.

**Nina Calder** put the fan against the wall and left it there and opened both wallets on the table, and there were fourteen answers, and eight of them were on her form and four were on the borough's and two were on both, and the two on both answered for the same meter room and neither of them knew the other existed, and that had been the position since the eighteenth of April and it had not resolved itself and it was not going to.

“There is no sitting,” she said. “That is the first thing and I am going to say it and then I am going to stop saying it, because I have said it in three rooms in nine days and every time I have said it somebody has told me a date and I have gone and looked for the date.

“The chair required every party holding continuity in this city to answer a return, in public, on the form, by the next sitting. There is no next sitting. It has been referred on to a sitting that has never been dated, and nobody in this building can date it, and I have asked three people this week and two of them said *after the recess* and one of them said nothing at all, which is the same answer with more conviction.

“So: a determination with a deadline and no date on it. I have had that phrase in my head for nine days and it is the correct phrase and I am not going to argue with it, I am just going to tell you what it means on a Monday morning with fourteen pieces of paper on a table. **It means the obligation is discharged by the act of answering. Not by anybody receiving it. There is no room in this city that has to open, on any date, in front of anybody, and the fourteen of you have all of you complied, and every one of you has made a public document by doing it, and none of you is in breach of anything and none of you will ever be told that you are not.**

“That is the truth about it and I have said it twice this month and I am not saying it again. It is on the table. Look at it.”

Ronan Vasquez, forty-eight, estates office, Saint Orra west, eleven years in post, took his own form out of his pocket and put it face down on the table and said: “I answered it in nine minutes on the eighth of May and I have brought it in because I want it recorded that I answered it in nine minutes, and I would like the Committee to know that a man who has been in post eleven years can answer a form about his own plant in nine minutes without a photograph, and that a form which cannot be answered in nine minutes is asking about something else.”

“Then let us have the thing that is actually in the room,” said Oren Pell.

---

He had come at a quarter past ten on his own time, and he had a folder, and he had not been asked, and he said so in about ten seconds, and then he did not say anything else for about six minutes while Frances Tolley explained the mechanism.

Frances Tolley is the committee clerk of the Standing Committee on Civic Continuity and has been for twenty-two years, and she had found it in about an hour and a half on the Friday, in the 2008 regulations, and she had it on a piece of paper because she had copied it out by hand, which she said was because she did not trust herself not to improve it.

“Section eleven,” she said. “Continuity bodies. **A body of that kind is constituted with nine seats: one for each service district drawn by the layer, and a ninth, for a person who is not a district.** The ninth seat is not a veto and it is not a chair and it cannot be removed by the other eight. It is written as *a person who answers for a service and is not a body*.

“And then there are about eleven lines about how it is named and who chairs it and how often it meets, and I have not got that far, because I got to the ninth seat at about ten past two on Friday afternoon and I read the rest of it standing up in the corridor and then I went and stood in a corridor for another twenty minutes, and I have never done that before, and I have been a committee clerk for twenty-two years and I have read a great many worse pieces of drafting than this one.

“I have not put it to the chair. I want that on the record this morning, because if anybody in this room thinks I have gone behind the Committee’s back, then they have not read section eleven, because **section eleven is permissive. It says *may be constituted*. It is not a duty on anybody. It is a power, and I found a power, and I found it because fourteen pieces of paper are on a table with nowhere to go, and I would like somebody to tell me today whether I should put it in front of her.**”

“Who does the other eight seats go to,” said Jonas Mercer, from the wall, where he had been standing for half an hour.

“Service districts,” said Frances Tolley. “The nine ordinary ones the layer draws. Which is eight and a ninth, and the ninth is the person, and that is the whole of the design and it took me forty minutes to understand why it is like that and about six seconds to understand that it is the only reason anybody wanted it.”

“Why did you find it,” said Oren Pell.

“Because there is nowhere to put the answers,” said Frances Tolley. “That is the whole reason. I am a clerk. I have fourteen documents that a Committee required, in public, on a form, by a sitting, and I have no sitting, and I have looked in the standing orders and there is no provision in the standing orders of the Civic Assembly for a required answer to arrive with nowhere to go. **A return with nowhere to go is a return that has not been made. I have known that for twenty-two years and I have never had to write it down before.**

“So either somebody receives the answers, or the answers are post. And if the answers are post, then in about three years this city will have answered a form in public fourteen times and received it none, and that is a completely different city, and I do not think anybody in this room would recognise the difference if it were put to them as a design.”

She put the piece of paper on the table.

“That is the naming. That is what it is called. **A cooperative steward board.**”

---

“No,” said Oren Pell.

He said it before anybody had finished, and he said it at a volume that made a woman at the counter two floors down put her head round a door, and Frances Tolley wrote it down, because she writes down everything.

“Say why,” said Marguerite Oyelaran, from the table, and Oren Pell looked at her and said, “I am going to say why, and I have thought about it since Friday, and I have been a registrar for nineteen years and I have never once in nineteen years said a thing like this in a room with a records supervisor and a committee clerk in it, and I would like Frances to put my job title next to it, and then I am going to say it, and then I am going to sit down for the rest of the morning, and I would like somebody to stop me if I go on, because I have a habit and it is a bad one.

“The ninth seat is a person who answers for a service and is not a body. That is a beautiful sentence and it has been in the 2008 regulations for six years and nobody has ever constituted one, and I want to tell you precisely why, and it is not money and it is not the ninth district and it is not that you cannot seat a street, and I have heard that sentence said in a Committee room in May by a member of this Committee and it is a good sentence and it is wrong.

“**It is page nine of Schedule 4. A tier may not be challenged by a person.**

“A body of nine seats, eight of them districts, and the ninth a person, has exactly one power under these regulations. It may require a party holding continuity to answer, and it may receive the answer, and it may be asked to consider a matter. It cannot require a person to do anything. It cannot require a person to hold a duty. It cannot require a person to answer a question, and it cannot be asked by a person to answer a question, and there is no appeal from it to anybody, and the reason there is no appeal from it to anybody is page nine, and page nine says a person is not in the way.

“So the ninth seat is a chair. Not a chair with a person in it, which is what a person always is. **A chair with a person in it that nobody can ask a question.** A steward board whose ninth seat cannot be challenged is not a steward board. It is a register with chairs.

“And I want to be very careful here, because I am not the man who killed it. The reason I am the man saying it is that every one of the four hundred and sixty pages of that schedule is a register with chairs, and it has been in force since the first of March, and it has cost this city a lift in a block and a gate at a hospital, and it has had a chair in front of it for four months and nobody has ever said this sentence in a room, and a chair on the nineteenth of May said the opposite of it in public — she said a party that answers may be asked what it will do and not what it will cost — and she was right, and she also said she could not say where she got it from.

“**And I have been a registrar for nineteen years and I have spent four months waiting for somebody in this city to say the other half of it, and it has just been said, in a room, by a clerk, by accident, while looking for a place to put fourteen pieces of paper.**”

He sat down.

Nobody said anything for about eleven seconds.

“He is right,” said Frances Tolley, and she wrote it down, and then she said, without looking up: “Chair, if I am going to put this to her, I need to know what I put. Because if the reason is *we cannot afford it*, that is a minute, and I can minute a reason and I have minuted worse. And if the reason is what the registrar has just said, then that is a finding about the standing orders and about section eleven, and it goes to the committee as a committee matter and not as a scheme, and it will be reported on at a sitting that has not been dated, and the fourteen of us will still be post.”

“The chair will hear of it,” said the member's aide on the telephone, from a desk in the Old Ward, in a voice that had been asked a question and was answering it in about nine seconds. “The chair's position, which I have been given and which I am reading: **the chair will not constitute a body at all on the strength of a permissive power found in a corridor on a Friday afternoon, and will say so in writing if anybody puts it in writing.**”

“Then write it down,” said Oren Pell. “That is the second useful thing anybody has done in this room in nine days and it has been done by a woman reading down a telephone.”

---

**And at twenty past eleven on Monday the second of June, a member's aide rang Frances Tolley back and gave her a date.**

It is the kind of thing that takes a chair nine days and a clerk one telephone call, and it is the whole of what this chapter is for, and Nina Calder wrote it on the back of a form in her own hand and read it out and nobody made a speech about it.

**The next sitting of the Standing Committee on Civic Continuity is on Thursday the nineteenth of June 2014, at ten in the morning, in committee room two. The returns are to be read into the record as they are received. The question of the term *holder* is on the papers if and when a definition is before the Committee, which the chair has said will not be arranged by anybody.**

“Nine days,” said Frances Tolley, to nobody. “Nine days ago that determination had no date on it and it was not a deadline and the fourteen of you had complied and nobody was in breach and nobody would ever be told. And this morning it has a date, and the only thing that has happened is that I telephoned an office at eleven o'clock and asked a question I have been allowed to ask since 1992.

“That is not a finding. I am not going to call it a finding. But I am going to write the two of them on the same piece of paper, one above the other, with the date on both, and I am going to put it in the front of the folder, and in about four years somebody is going to read that piece of paper and work out that the state of the world in this city is determined by whether anybody rings anybody.”

She wrote it. It took eleven seconds.

---

They got up at about half past eleven, and it took twenty minutes, and Marguerite Oyelaran went out first because she had a thing at two, and the estates officer went second because he had a van, and Raynor-Hale went and left a laminated card on the table, which he said was for the room and not for anybody in it, and which said **HEAT PROTOCOL — ACTIVATED 26/05 — RETURN BY 04/08** in seven words and a room number in the civic offices, and nobody picked it up for about ninety seconds, and then Nina Calder picked it up and put it in a drawer, and said, “That is the first thing anybody has put in a drawer in this room in three weeks and it is going in a drawer because it is the only correct place for it.”

Nina Calder said the last thing.

She said it to the table, at about ten past twelve, with both wallets shut and the fan against the wall and nobody left in the room but two people, and she said it in the flat voice she uses for reading other people’s sentences, and then she said she was going to say it once and was not going to be asked about it.

“Three lists,” she said. “I have got a register of attachment in the registrar's office with one name on it and a form I printed myself in a hurry. I have got a register of narrowings on a public board in a porch in the rain with fourteen of them on it, which I also printed myself, in a hurry, in a basement, because a printer in a cardigan told me the truth in a corridor. And there is now a third thing that a clerk found in a regulation in about an hour and a half and which is called a cooperative steward board and has nine seats and a person in the ninth one.

“Three lists in one city, and I have started a fourth page in a pad on Thursday, and I have stopped, and I stopped on Friday morning before I wrote anything on it, and here is why and it is the only thing I have to say about it.

“**A list is not a record. A record is about one thing at one time. A list is a set of things that somebody intends to go on adding to, and the intention is the danger, and I have watched a housing association put a hundred and twenty-two letters through a hundred and twenty-two letterboxes and a plate get falsified in four days, and I have watched a form get a photograph of a room out of a cupboard, and I am not going to be the fourth.**

“For nine seats to be anything other than nine chairs, one of the nine has to be able to be put in a room and told to stop, and removed in front of the other eight, by people who did not put him there.

“That is the whole of it. There is no second half. I am not asking anybody a question and I am not expecting an answer, and I am going to be here on Thursday the nineteenth of June at nine o’clock with a folder, and I would like anybody in this room who thinks I have got that wrong to say so in writing, and I will put it in the folder with their name on it, and I will not answer it.”

She picked the fan up and carried it out.
