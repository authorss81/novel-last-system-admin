# Chapter 0081 — The Nineteenth of June

He read the four lines on the front steps in thirty degrees, and they were the four he had written at the bottom of a page in the first week of June, and nobody in this city read them until the summer.

And then he did not telephone anybody, and the reason was that there was nothing on the four lines to telephone anybody about. They were a question. A question with a district in it and no date, and no date is a thing you can put in front of anybody, and a thing you can put in front of anybody is a thing you do not put in front of a person.

---

The ten days between were not his.

On Tuesday the tenth he was in the shop from seven until half past five and he did four things and three of them were other people's. Bea Osunde cut a length of four-inch duct and would not be told that the bevel was wrong, and he did not tell her, and it was wrong. Tomas Renn came in at about eleven on his own time and put a folded piece of paper on the crate and said it was a menu for a Sunday in October with four people on it and no rule attached, and asked him not to do anything with it, and then said *I know, I know*, and took it back and put it in his coat. A man came for a key and was told the price and paid it and went away and the price was right.

On Wednesday the eleventh he was in the records office on the second floor for about an hour and ten minutes, and Nina Calder had a pad on the table with nothing on it, and he looked at it, and she said, "Do not," and he said, "I was not going to," and she said, "You were looking at it the way you look at a sill."

She had started that page on Thursday the fifth of June and had written nothing on it since. What she had told him on the second was the reason, and the reason had not changed: *a list is not a record, a record is about one thing at one time, a list is a set of things somebody intends to go on adding to, and the intention is the danger.* She had a form, and a register with one name on it, and a board in a porch in the rain, and she was not going to have a fourth.

He asked her one thing while he was there, which was whether the fourteen answers had gone anywhere. She said they were in two drawers and one of the drawers was in that room and one of them was in a records store on a lower floor in a fire-resistant box, and that both drawers were the chair's determination and not hers, and that on Thursday they would come out of the drawers and go into a room.

"And if nobody is in the room."

"Then they are in a room," said Nina Calder, "with the door shut."

On Friday the thirteenth he was in a corridor at Saint Orra west at about eleven in the morning and read a board and did not ask anybody anything. The gap was still there. The refused Thursday was still there, in Bram Osei's own words, and beneath it in a different hand somebody had written *no reason given* and Bram Osei had not rubbed it out and had written under it *no reason I can give in a corridor.* The terms on the plate were six days. The decontamination verification log on level zero, when he went and looked at it through the door of the room, had a printed grid of fifty-two Sundays in it and a name against none of them since the ninth of May.

He looked at it for about a minute and a half and thought about the fourth line on a governance file in a folder belonging to a trust, and about four hundred and twenty women in this borough, and about a paragraph number, and about a supplier's quarterly visit that was in July and a day in July that nobody had fixed.

He did not go up to level zero. There was nothing on level zero for him. He went down and out and he did not write it down, and the not writing it down is going to matter in about three weeks, and it will matter to somebody else and not to him.

He did not telephone Marguerite Oyelaran, whose return was due on the fourth of August, and who had been off the telephone to him since the thirty-first of May, and who had a hundred and six occupied addresses in a public document and a shortfall of four thousand three hundred and sixty-one pounds and sixty pence and a block of 1938 that has had no water since the sixth of May. He did not go up the path at the top of the slip, and there was no reason to, and the not going was correct and cost him nothing, which is the only kind of not-going that costs nothing.

---

**Thursday the nineteenth of June 2014, ten in the morning, committee room two, the Old Ward.**

It is a determinations meeting on fourteen returns and not a hearing about a price, and everybody in the room knew which one it was before anybody spoke, and the difference is the whole of the chapter.

Councillor Fenella Marsh in the chair. Councillor Sore. Douglas Imre. Hargreaves. Petrie Okonjo. Denman. Oren Pell in attendance, as registrar, with a folder he did not open. Frances Tolley at the clerk's table with two plastic wallets, a bundle of fourteen returned forms in a cardboard box, and a wall chart with a column for each return and nothing yet written in the column.

The gallery filled at about eleven, as Frances Tolley had told him it would, and the front two rows went to an unchecked press list, and there were forty-one folding chairs and about fifty people standing at the back by eleven-fifteen, and Jonas Mercer was one of the standing ones, at the back, on the left, with nothing in his hands, and he was not on the agenda and not a party and not a member and not a departmental officer, and a member's aide said so into a microphone at the chair's request at nine forty-five, and the minute records it.

Alden Rook was in the room. He had been sent the papers as a party holding continuity in this city, which he is, and he had a folder, and he had come on his own account, and he sat at the party's table with Ceri Vaughan and did not interrupt for an hour and ten minutes.

The determination of the nineteenth of May required every party holding continuity in this city to answer a Return of a Deferred Node, in public, on the form, by this sitting, and the answers were to be read into the record as they are received, and where the answer to *who answers for it* is no party, that is to be recorded as no party, in those words.

Marsh read them in. It took from ten past ten until about eleven twenty-five, and it was the most boring seventy-five minutes of anybody's year, and it was correct throughout, and every single thing said in it was true.

A party holding continuity in Gantry Ward answered for four nodes and named a firm and gave dates. A party in the borough answered for one node on a hospital corridor and named a person in a job title and said the term had not been continuously in force for a period exceeding twenty-four consecutive hours on two occasions in April, which was the contractual failure definition in paragraph 11 read back to the Committee in a form nobody had asked for. A transport operator answered for eleven nodes, nine of which were not services, which was the correct answer, and which nobody had designed a box for.

And then, at about eleven twenty-eight, Frances Tolley read out the two returns that are the subject of the rest of this volume.

> **METER ROOM 3, SALTER'S YARD, TIDEGLASS. Party holding continuity: THE ALDBROOK TRUST, Kestrel Hill, as freeholder of the fee payer, one-half. Tier 3. Claim reference T3/11400/0304. Answer received 6 June 2014.**

> **METER ROOM 3, SALTER'S YARD, TIDEGLASS. Party holding continuity: THE NAMED STEWARD OF THE BUILDING, named in the form as received. Tier 3. Claim reference T3/11400/0304. Answer received 16 June 2014.**

The chair read both into the record as they were received, because that is what the determination of the nineteenth of May said she would do, and she did it, and it took about eleven seconds, and neither party in the room knew that the other had answered.

Then she stopped, and looked at the wall chart, and said, "Clerk, those are two answers about one meter room and they cannot both be the answer, and I have no power to stop them and I have no power to take either of them out, and I am going to ask for it to be recorded that I read them as they were received on the instruction of a determination this Committee made on the nineteenth of May, and that the instruction is the reason there are two of them."

"Recorded," said Frances Tolley, and it was, in those words.

Imre asked whether the Committee wished to adjourn the item. The chair said no, and said why, and the reason was three sentences long: *"If I adjourn it, it does not go away, and if I take a second morning, then in six weeks somebody will say the Committee received it and dealt with it, and what I have done this morning is receive it, and receiving it is not the same as agreeing with it, and I am not going to be a Committee that has dealt with a thing because it was tired on a Thursday."*

And then the two answers sat in the record, and the room did the thing that a room does, and in the front two rows two people began talking about the press list, and Jonas Mercer stood at the back of the gallery and worked out what had just happened, which was that a determination is a machine for putting answers into a record, and that the machine does not know what an answer is, and that on the nineteenth of May a chair told a city that answers were to be read in as they were received, and the city has spent five weeks since then finding out what that costs.

---

He had one question. Frances Tolley had told him the rule in the corridor on the second of June and Pell had said he would stand up and say so if anybody put a microphone on him, and nobody did, and he had been in the room for an hour and forty minutes with it in his hand.

He put it at about ten to twelve, when the chair was between items, and he said it to the chair and not to the room, and he said it in nine words.

"Chair, is anybody going to telephone the person in that field?"

And the chair answered no.

She answered it at length, and she was right, and this is what she said, and it is in the minute:

*"No. And I will not require a person at that meter room, any more than I would require one at six water points on a lowland, and I have asked for that to be minuted twice in this city already and I am asking for it three times. **A tier may not be challenged by a person. A person is not a party. A person may not be required to do anything, and — this is the part that took me four months — a person may not be required even to be told.** There is no instrument in this Committee, in the department, or in Schedule 4 that will put a telephone call in my hand, and I have looked, and Oren Pell has looked, and the honest position is that the holder of that field is a woman who has not been told that she is holding it, and that this Committee cannot tell her, and that is not a failure of this Committee, it is the design of a document that has been in force since the first of March and that nobody in this room is able to define the operative word of."*

Then she said, from the chair, in a slightly different voice, the sentence that the record has and that four people in the room have since quoted:

*"If that person has not been told, then somebody in this city is going to tell them, and it is not going to be this Committee, and I would like the minute to say that I have said so."*

And Pell said, without opening his folder: "The records office is required to."

And the chair said: "Then the records office will, and it will be on the day it happens and not on the day I said it would."

---

**It was Frances Tolley, and it was at ten past one, and she did it with two pieces of paper and a pencil and about ninety seconds, and nobody in the room helped her.**

She had the two returns in front of her and she was reading them against each other, which nobody had asked her to do, because the determination said *read as received* and reading two things against each other is not reading as received.

"The reference is the same string," she said. "It is in two different places on two different forms and it is the same string. It is on the borough's in the claims box and it is on mine in the box I added in May that everybody in this room told me was a mistake, and it is T3 slash eleven four hundred slash zero three zero four, and it means the tier and the annual fee and the day of the month it was placed."

"Meaning what," said Sore.

"Meaning it is not two claims," said Frances Tolley. "It is one claim with two answers, and the answers are about the same claim, and I have never seen that on a form before because a form asks you who answers and it does not occur to anybody that the answer can have a history."

And then Oren Pell did the thing he does, which is to say what a thing is in about four sentences, at speed, as though he has been waiting nineteen years to be asked.

"A claim placed under Schedule 4 carries the tier, the annual fee, and the date of placement, in one reference, and the reference never changes. A claim may be assigned, in whole or in shares, to a party with authority, and an assignment does not make a new claim and does not make a new reference, and the assigning party does not stop being the party that placed it. So the reference tells you a tier, a fee, and a day in March, and nothing else, and **a reference is not a chain.** A chain is a set of assignments, and it lives in a deed box or a minute, and it is not on any form in this city, and the reason no form in this city has a chain on it is that a form asks a question about a state and a chain is a history, and Nina Calder has been saying since April that those are two different instruments and she is right."

"Whose claim," said Councillor Sore.

"The parent of that reference is a node in Kestrel Hill," said Oren Pell, "and the parent of the parent is the party that placed it, and the placing party on the fourth of March was a company acting under a standing continuity authorisation, signed by a power of attorney and not by a name, which is the ordinary way and is correct, and the party to whom the whole of it was assigned on the twenty-first of March is the freeholder of the building, which is a trust, and I can read you the box number of the assignment because it is in a public deed register and I have it in front of me, and I am not going to, because it is four documents in two buildings and a clerk would have to go and get them, and nobody in this room is going to send anybody."

He closed the folder.

"And I am going to say the method out loud once, because it has not been said in this room in four months and it is the only useful thing a registrar has got on a Thursday afternoon, and then I am going to sit down.

"**A price is a statement about what a thing costs. A chain is a statement about who is standing behind the price. The Committee cannot unmake a price and it has no power to and it should not try. What the Committee can do, and this is the only door, is name the chain in public, in a room, and put the last person in it into a position where somebody has to ask her a question.** Every one of the last four months has been spent on the price. Nobody has read the chain. That is not a scandal. It is an ordering, and it was the correct ordering until today, because there was nothing in the chain to read."

Nobody spoke for about four seconds.

"That is the method," said Alden Rook, from the party's table, and he had not been called on, and everybody let him, and that was also correct. "I want to say three things about it and I want to say them to the Committee and not to the registrar.

"**First: everything Registrar Pell has just described is valid.** I know it is valid, because my company placed the parent of it and my company has the file. Four assignments, every one of them made by a person with authority on a day when they had authority, and not one of them is a trick and not one of them is a lie, and if the Committee wants to call it a chain then it is a chain, and I would rather it were a chain in a minute than a rumour in a corridor, and I have said for four months that a rumour in a corridor is worse than a price on a plate.

"**Second: he is right that the method is naming the chain, and he is right that it ends with somebody asking her a question, and I want the Committee to notice what that means.** It means the output of this method is a *person being asked*. And four months ago this Committee decided, correctly, in public, that a person may not be required to do anything and may not be required even to be told. So the Committee has built a method whose final step is the one step the Committee is not permitted to take. That is not a flaw in the method. **That is the method working.** It is supposed to end in a person, and then it is supposed to stop, and then somebody outside the room has to do the last thing, and today the Committee has said out loud that it will not be that somebody, and the records office will.

"**And third, and I am going to say this because nobody else in this room is going to say it and I am not going to leave it in a minute where it gets found in a year.** If that chain is named and that woman is asked and she says no, then in ninety days the party that placed the parent of it revives its interest in the whole of it, at Tier 3, with three months' notice served on everybody whose precedence it touches, and it will re-assert precedence over every unfunded node in that district, and there are six water points on a lowland in this borough that nobody has asked anybody to put on a schedule, and the standing at the top of a Kestrel Hill trust is not a person and it does not get tired and it does not get frightened and **it will be back on the first of October with a letter and a solicitor, and by then the woman will have had a fee stop and found out that stopping it was cheaper than it looked, and the Committee that named the chain will have gone home in June.**"

"Two minutes to one o'clock," said Frances Tolley.

"The notice period is ninety days from the date of withdrawal and it is at annex six and I have not got it in front of me and I am not going to quote it," said Rook, "and I am not going to be asked about it by a member today, because if I am asked about it by a member today I will answer it, and it is not mine to answer."

---

They got up at twenty past one.

Nina Calder had a folder and a page in it and she said, at the door, to nobody, "I have got two lines in a minute that say the same thing about one meter room and there is no procedure in the standing orders of this Council for correcting a minute in a sitting, and I have checked, and there is one in about four years, and it requires the Committee to be satisfied that it has been misled, and the Committee has not been misled, it has been *read to*, and so the two lines are going to be in the record of this city for good."

"How long," said Sore.

"However long the record goes," said Nina Calder. "The other half of it is that neither line can be taken out and the second one is not wrong, and neither of them can be amended, and so a meter room in Tideglass is going to have two answers in a public minute for as long as the paper lasts, and there is a Trust on one of them and a person on the other, and they are the same claim, and the claim is at a tier that outranks a standpipe on a lowland, and it came out of Kestrel Hill through four assignments, and **nobody in this room has read the four assignments and I am not going to, because I am a records supervisor and I have a form and a pad with nothing on it and I have told a man in a records office that I am not having a fourth.**"

---

Oren Pell found him in the corridor at ten past two, and Pell had the folder, and Pell did not open it, and what he said was this and it took about ninety seconds.

"Mr Mercer. You are not on anything, you were not a party, you asked one question and it was the right one, and I want to give you two things and then I am going to go and write a minute.

"**One.** The holder field at that node is populated. It has been populated since the third of June, which is the day the last assignment was entered, and it is populated with the name of a person, and it is a person's name and not a company and not a trust and not a firm of solicitors, and I have read it and I am not going to say it to you in a corridor because if I say it to you in a corridor then it is in a corridor.

"**Two, and this is the one you will want to be careful with.** She is findable. She has been findable since the seventh of May, when a form with a crest on it became a public document obtainable at a counter, and there is a name on a public document, and anybody in this city who can read can find her, and you cannot protect her by not saying her name, and you know that, because you have spent five weeks watching people try.

"**And a person who has not been asked may not be named.** That is not a rule. There is no rule. It is a thing that Nina Calder has said out loud in a records office and a thing that I have written on the record about four hundred times, and it is the only thing standing between a public document and a person's front door in this city, and it is not enough, and it is what there is.

"So: the chain is revocable, and it is not revocable by me, and it is not revocable by this Committee, and it is not revocable by the party that made it, and it is not revocable by you — and I want to be careful here, because you will hear yourself say *I* in about four days and I want the sentence in your head now, in a corridor, from a man who is not your friend: **the only party that can withdraw a claim is the party that holds it, and the party that holds that claim is a person, and the person has not been asked, and until she has been asked she is not a party to anything and she is a signature on somebody else's instrument and nothing more.**

"That is the whole of it. It took me four months to be able to say it in one sentence and I have wanted to say it since the nineteenth of May and on the nineteenth of May the chair got there first and I was pleased about it for about a day and then I found out what the rest of it was."

Jonas Mercer said, "She has not been told."

"She has not been told," said Oren Pell. "She has a building, and a fee, and a plate in a cupboard with a sentence on it about the holder of the point, and she has had that plate since before any of us arrived, and she has not had a telephone call, and she is going to have one, and it is going to come out of a records office on a day of the week chosen by a clerk who is required to make it, and it is going to be the first anybody in this city has said to her, and it is not going to be you."

He went. He had a minute to write and it had two lines in it about one meter room that could not be corrected.

Jonas Mercer went down the stairs in the Old Ward and out into the afternoon, and it was nineteen degrees and raining very slightly, and he did not go and find anybody, and he did not telephone anybody, and the thing he was carrying was not a pad and it was not a document.

It was a chain with four links in it and a person at the end of it who was fifty-four years old, had been in the block nineteen years, had never once been asked a question by a body, and had, on Saturday the thirty-first of May, in a Methodist hall four miles away, been told by eleven of her own residents that **the fee does not stop.**

And she was going to have to be told that the fee was not a fee.
