# Chapter 0788 — The Middle Flag Of The Path

Monday the twenty-third of January 2023, in this borough. She spends the middle of the day out on her own front path at number nine in the cold, and one bay of that path is what the whole of it comes to.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES HAS THE MIDDLE FLAG OF HER FRONT PATH BEDDED HIGH AT THE DOOR END AND DROPPING TOWARDS THE RAIL AND MEANS TO LIFT IT AND PUT A BIT OF SAND UNDER IT, THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING TAKES THE BAY UP AND RELAYS IT ON A NEW BED TO THE LEVEL OF THE FLAGS EITHER SIDE, AND THE FLAG IS TRUE AND STAYS TRUE, AND A TUB OF IRON GOES BACK DOWN ON IT THE SAME AFTERNOON.**

She has been in her late fifties since before she came to number nine, and it is a Monday, and the woman who does her shopping is on the front step before the middle of the day with her cardigan folded over one arm and nothing else in her hands at all.

---

The fire is unlit. Four pads are stacked on top of the fridge door with the first at the bottom, and the room at the front of that house has not been opened since the summer. In the pocket of the apron on the back of the back door there is the back piece off an envelope, and there is nothing in the hinge of that door to put a piece of card into. That door comes down over the middle piece of her own old front step and the bottom of it runs out of line with itself by a hand's width where it crosses the stone, and it has done that since the autumn. Across the flags at the foot of the path the gate stands open on two wedges, and the spanner is lying on the flags behind them where it was dropped. The coal hole opposite the kitchen has a good lid on it, screwed down at the back corners, and that hole is shut and has been since the autumn, and none of that is the reason she is out here.

The front path is the line of flags that runs from her front door to the gate at the bottom of it.

There are about nine of them and they are old and hard and grey, and they were laid before she came. They run down past the doorstep to the front gate, and the middle of the path is the middle three of them, and everything that comes to that door comes over them.

The middle flag is the one nearest the door end and it is bedded high.

It came up in the autumn and she knows why, because the ground under it has gone down. The flag itself is sound. It is the bed that has gone, because a bed of sand with nothing in it under a flag in a path takes the water straight through and the water has been carrying the sand out from under the middle of it since before the summer, and the flag has come to sit down on nothing at all along a band across its middle.

So it is high at the door end and drops away towards the rail, and if you put a hand on the side of it at the doorstep it is about the thickness of two fingers proud of the flag next to it, and by the time your hand has travelled the length of the flag it has gone down level, and by the far end of it the flag is on nothing and there is a hole you could put your heel into.

The last flag at the gate end has been tilting towards the rail since she came and it goes the way it goes.

Her plan is a bit of sand.

There is a barrow of sand in the side passage, part of a bag that went damp and has gone to heap, and she means to get a bar in under the flag, lift it, rake out whatever is left of the old bed, and put about a hand's depth of new sand under it and set it back down, and that will do it.

---

The woman who does her shopping comes up the path about half way through the morning with the cardigan folded over one arm and nothing in her hands.

"You are not going to put sand under that flag."

"I am going to put sand under that flag."

"You are going to put sand under a flag that is high at the door end and low at the rail." She stops at the second flag down and looks at the middle one. "How deep is the sand where it is standing now."

"About as deep as my hand."

"And how deep is it in the middle of it, under the middle."

She does not answer that.

She crouches and gets the back of her thumbnail under the near edge of the flag and lifts, and the flag comes up about two fingers at the door end and about a thumbnail at the far end, and you can see the hole it has made in the bed of sand and you can see down into it.

"That is where your sand has gone."

---

She takes the bay up.

All three of them, not one, and that is argued for ten minutes at the door end with the two of them standing on it and nothing being lifted.

"A bay is the ground under one patch of a path," the woman says. "And you are not lifting a flag. You are lifting three, and if you lift one you will be back with this in three winters, because it is the middle of the path where the water goes and the middle of a path is a bay like any other piece of ground."

"I have lifted one of them before and it was all right."

"You lifted the one at the door end, which is not in the path of anything, and you put a hand's depth of sand under it, and that one is true, and it is true because there is nothing running under it."

---

Then she takes it up.

They get the three flags up on a length of angle iron and a lump of rubber, working from the rail end back towards the door so that they are not standing in the hole they are making. Underneath the bed of sand is a bed about as deep as a hand at the two ends of it and nothing at all in the middle, and the sand that is left in it is grey and cold and runs like sugar when you push your thumb into it, and there is a trough the width of a flag along the middle of the three where it has gone down to the hard stuff.

She does not put the same sand back, and the reason she does not is the trough.

There is rubble off the coal shed gutter in a heap in the yard and grit that came out of the side passage in the autumn, and sand that came out of a bag that went damp. She mixes the rubble and the sand on a board on the flags and then spreads it along the hole with her hands, a layer at a time, and she works each layer in with the end of the bar rather than dropping it and stamping on it, because the trough in the middle of that bay is where all the water goes and loose stuff put in it drops straight through again.

And the bed goes up a finger above the top of the old one and comes up level, and that is because she is not adding to a bed. She is filling a hole that the water took out of the middle of the old one, and the two inches she is putting in are going into ground that used to be at the top of it, and the flag goes down on the top of the new bed at the height it was at before and not a finger above it.

That is the whole of it. She beds each flag in the order it came up in and she goes over the bed the whole length of the bay with the back of the shovel, and then with the edge of a piece of batten, and she does that twice more, and every joint closes tight.

---

It is true.

She stands in the doorway and looks down the length of the path. The three of them come up level with the flag above them at the door end and level with the flag below them at the rail end, there is no dip under any one of them that she can find with the heel of her hand, and the joints are close all the way along the nine.

The woman stands at the rail with her arms folded and walks the length of it coming back up.

The woman looks along the path once more from the rail. "That is a good bay, and it is level with the flags either side, which is the whole thing and the only thing. You will not have to look at that path again."

---

Then the tub goes back down on it that same afternoon.

It goes back on before the light has gone off it, and it goes back on the middle of the bay, and it goes back on without a word from either of them.

It is a galvanised tub about as high as her knee and it stands about the width of two flags across. She has had it since before she came and it lives on that path. It is where the bulbs go in the autumn and where the bulbs come out of in the spring, and where the strings and the labels and the odd spade go when she cannot find them, and where the rubble and the roots and the grit that come out of that path go, because there is nowhere else in that yard for it to go and it cannot go down the passage.

It is full. She has spent the morning taking the roots and the old sand out from under three flags and there is more of it than would go in the barrow, so it is all in the tub and she has carried it out and put it down on the middle of the new bed.

It stands about the width of a thumb proud of the joint on either side of it, because it is a soft-bottomed tub and it has settled about a thumb into a bed that was packed with a shovel handle an hour ago, and if you put your heel on the path that afternoon you go through the joint and down into the rubble at the rail end.

The woman who does her shopping is gone by then. She went out of the gate while there was still light, with her cardigan on her arm, and she did not follow her out of it, and did not offer to, and stood in the doorway and watched her go and then went back out to the flag.

She stands in the late light looking down one plane of path from her front door to her gate with a tub of iron standing on the middle of it, and that bay is the best thing that has been done to that path since she came, and it is that bay that a tub of iron and a shovelful of nothing to do with it will go into, and she does not see it, because from where she is standing it looks like a tidied yard.
