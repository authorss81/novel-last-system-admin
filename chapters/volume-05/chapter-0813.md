# Chapter 0813 — The Bottom Course Open

Tuesday the sixteenth of May 2023, in this borough. From about half way through the morning until the last of the light he is at the back of his own unit with a wall of blocks, and that wall is the whole of that day.

**A MAN IN HIS EARLY FORTIES HAS ABOUT FOUR FEET OF A WALL OF BLOCKS AT THE BACK OF THAT BUILDING WITH ITS BOTTOM COURSE OPEN ALONG ONE SIDE SINCE HE CAME, AND MEANS TO TAKE THE LOOSE ONES OFF AND REBUILD THAT SIDE DRY WITH BROKEN WALL AND CHIPPINGS; THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN DOES THE SAME JOB ON THE OTHER END OF THE SAME WALL ON THE SAME AFTERNOON, AND WHERE THE TWO REBUILT ENDS MEET IN THE MIDDLE THERE IS A JOINT BETWEEN TWO PEOPLE'S WORK BETTER MADE THAN EITHER END OF THAT WALL HAS EVER BEEN, AND NEITHER OF THEM EVER FINDS OUT.**

The unit, on that Tuesday. He has run the place for years. The wall at the back of that building runs the width of the yard along the boundary and it is a boundary wall of concrete blocks, four courses high, and about four feet of the bottom course along the near end of it has been open since he took the place on.

Four feet of it with nothing at the bottom and four courses sitting on the top of a hole.

---

The wall itself is a good wall and it is not going anywhere.

It is four courses of dense concrete block on a strip foundation, laid dry with no mortar in a single joint, and it has stood along that boundary since before the war because that is what a dry wall does on a good foundation: it weathers and it moves and it does not crack, and the money in it is in the bottom two courses and everything above that is on top of the money.

What has gone in that four feet is the money.

---

The foundation under it is a strip of concrete about four inches thick and about eight inches wide, cast in a trench, and it is not cracked and it has not moved. What has happened is that the ground under the edge of it has gone, which takes a foundation off its own ground at the edge and leaves the concrete hanging there with nothing under it, like a shelf with the bracket off one end.

The whole weight of that wall is going through four feet of concrete that is supported at the near end and at the far end and in the middle it is in the air.

---

A block wall with a bottom course gone is not a wall that has dropped. It is a wall that is standing on its own bond at the end and leaning over the hole in the middle, and it will go on leaning until the end of it slides off the bed it is standing on.

The blocks in the bottom course along that four feet are not loose. They are sound. They are sound because they have been sitting in the air since he came and they have not been wet except when it rains straight into the hole, and what is under them is nothing at all.

---

He means to take them all up, dig out the hole, put broken wall and chippings in, and put them back dry.

Dry is the word. That wall has no mortar in it and never has, and it is not going to have any, and a block wall with no mortar in it is a wall that can move and take up what the ground does underneath it, which is the only reason a wall like this is still standing where a mortared one would have cracked in half.

What a wall like that needs is a footing that is level and gets rid of its water, and neither of those things is a matter of mortar.

---

He starts about half way through the morning with a lump hammer and a bolster and a cold chisel.

A block does not want to be taken up. He knocks the face off the first one at the top and takes it out whole in three pieces, and then the two above it come out whole, and by the middle of the morning the bottom course along two feet of that wall is in a heap on the grass and there is a hole in the wall about as deep as a spade blade.

The hole is not straight down. It is a place where the ground has gone out from under the foundation and the foundation is a strip of concrete about four inches thick with the bottom of it hanging in the air.

---

He digs that out.

It takes the afternoon because it has to go to firm ground and the firm ground in that corner of that yard is about four feet down. He has the bar and a spade and a short length of pipe he uses as a rammer, and he fills the bottom of that hole with the broken brick that has come out of the wall itself first, in layers, and tamps each layer with the end of the pipe until the pipe will not go in, and then puts a spadeful of the chippings on top of that and tamps that too.

The broken brick is out of the wall. It is a wall that has been standing in the weather for thirty years and the mortarless joints have filled with rubble over that time and there is more usable brick in the bottom course of it than there is anything else.

---

Then he levels it.

A block wall laid dry on a bed wants a bed that is level along its whole length, and the way you get that is to lay a straightedge on it and work the top of the footing up and down with a bar until there is nothing under the straightedge anywhere along its length, and then to check it across as well.

That is the whole of the footing and it is about three hours of work and the straightedge is the only tool in it.

---

The woman from two doors down comes round the side of that building about the middle of the afternoon and there is no door at the back of that unit and nothing to knock on.

He is inside by then, getting the second barrow of chippings out of the yard where it was delivered, and she walks straight past the open end of that wall and down to the far end of it and puts a bolster on the first block at that end the same way anybody would.

She does not look along the four feet of wall. There is nothing to see along it and she has come about one end of it.

---

He comes back out with the barrow and goes to his end.

He can see her from where he is standing, at the far end of that wall, down on her knees with her back to him, and he cannot see her hands and he cannot see what she has done or is doing, and the four feet of wall with the open end of it in between means he cannot see that end of the footing at all.

They are in the same yard for about two hours and neither of them walks the length of that wall.

He lays his blocks. The bed is level and the face of the wall above tells him where every block wants to be, because the face is the datum and it has been the datum for thirty years and it is not going anywhere, and he lays each block on it, and beds it down with the butt end of the bolster, and packs a slate offcut under the low corner, and checks it with a short spirit level across the top, and moves on to the next one.

The slate offcut is a piece of roof slate broken off the edge of a stack, and a slate is the only thing you put under a dry block for the one reason that slate does not crush. Anything soft under a block goes flat in a wet winter and the block goes over. A slate takes the weight and does not.

The bed does not move under a block once the block is on it. That is what four feet of broken brick and chippings packed with the end of a pipe does, and it is the whole reason for it.

---

He can hear her, and he does not go over.

There is a sound of a bolster on a block about three times the length of that wall from where he is, and it stops and starts the way that sound stops and starts, and after about a minute there is another sound, which is the sound of a bar going into something that is not going to want it.

He does not go over and he does not call across. There is no reason to. She has come about a footing and she has a bolster and she is doing what he would have done, and the part of that wall she is at does not need him and he does not need it back.

It is the first time in a good many years he has had somebody working in his yard on something that mattered to both of them and neither of them, and he would not say that out loud to anybody, and he certainly would not say it to the person at the other end of that wall.

---

She goes before the light has gone.

He watches her go round the corner of that building with her bolster and her spade and he does not follow her round and he does not go down to that end of the wall to look at it, because there is no reason on earth to look at four feet of somebody else's footing.

She has laid her blocks and packed them and done the same job on the same day with the same materials and he is not going to stand there and take the top off it.

---

He finishes his own end with about an hour of the light left.

Then he goes for the line and the blocks and puts a line along the top course the whole four feet, the course he has laid and the course she has laid, and takes his coat off the nail and hangs it over the line and stands back and looks at where the bottom of the coat hangs.

He moves it a foot further along and hangs it there, and moves it another foot and hangs it again, and he walks that line the whole four feet four times over it, and there is no place in the middle of that wall where the bottom of the coat hangs one thing rather than another.

That middle is a line about the thickness of a finger between his blocks and hers, and both of them built up to the face of the wall above it from opposite ends on the same afternoon, and the four courses that were always in that wall do not know that anything under them was ever open, and the top course of it is the best-made line of work on the whole of that boundary, and neither of them will ever know that it was made by two.
