# Chapter 0539 — The Sack Upstairs

The Wednesday of the first of January 2020, from about half past seven in the morning until about five in the afternoon, in one building in this borough.

**NINE YEARS AND SHE HAS GONE UP THOSE TWO FLIGHTS WITH A SACK ON HER SHOULDER AT HALF PAST SEVEN EVERY MORNING OF THE WEEK, AND SINCE NOVEMBER A MAN IN HIS MID SEVENTIES ON THE BOTTOM FLOOR HAS STOOD WAITING AT THE BOTTOM OF THE WAY UP, AND SHE WENT PAST HIM AND UP EVERY MORNING ANYWAY, AND IN THE AFTERNOON OF NEW YEAR'S DAY HE CAME DOWN THE FLIGHT WITH IT.**

She is a woman in her early sixties and she lives a street away with her younger sister and a grandson, and she has never once slept in this building, and she has been coming here at half past seven in the morning and going out at about four in the afternoon for nine years.

There is a top floor. There are four flats and the top floor is one flat and it is let by the week to people who are in the borough for a working week, and she has had it in one week at a time since the February of 2010, and there has been no argument about it and no arrangement and nothing in writing of any kind.

What she does in there is nothing that anybody would describe. There is a thing in it she does not sleep in and a surface she works at and a seat by the window she does not use. She takes a flask and something to eat and a paperback and a mending job and a portable radio she does not listen to much, and she does the mending and reads about four pages of a chapter of something and drinks two-thirds of the flask and goes out at four.

What she carries is a sack.

The sack has a strap on it. It holds the flask and the food and the mending and the book and a sweater she is not working on, and it is about as much as a woman of that age is going to be able to take up two flights on any morning, and it has been that since 2010.

She goes up at half past seven. It takes her about nine minutes to be at the top with it and about six to come down without it, because coming down without it is the easy part.

---

The man is on the ground floor and has been since 2008 and is a man in his mid seventies.

He is not a relation and he is not a friend of the family and he has never been in the top flat in his life. He knows she is on the top floor the way everybody in that building knows it, which is to say that he knows it the way he knows which of the four other doors is his.

What happened in November is this.

He came in one morning from the shops at about ten past seven carrying what he carries, and she came in at half past seven from the direction of the street, and the two of them went past each other in the space by the door, and he went out again and stopped on the second step and came back in and said, "I'll take that up."

And she said, "There is no need."

And he said, "I know there is no need."

And she said, "Then leave it."

And he said, "All right," and went out, and stood in the street for a while, and went up the street and did not come back that day.

The whole of it took the best part of a minute and there were people in the space by the front door at the time, because there is always somebody in that space by half past seven, and it was not said quietly.

After that he came in on the mornings. He came in and he stood on the bottom step at about ten past seven and he did not say anything at all, and she came in at half past seven and went past him and up, and he stayed where he was, and about six minutes later she came back down and he was still there, and then he went out and she went back up the street.

That is what happened for a month, and then it stopped.

He stopped it himself. Nobody asked him to and she did not ask him to and there was no conversation about it at all. He came in on the eighteenth of December and she came in at half past seven and went past him and up, and he stood on the bottom step, and she got to the top and came back down and he was still there, and she stopped on the bottom step and said, "You have been standing there half an hour."

And he said, "I have."

And she said, "You have a hip that goes."

And he said, "It does not," which was not true and which he knew was not true and which he said because she had been at the shops that morning and had seen him go past the chemist at a speed.

And she said, "Right."

And she went up the whole way with it.

She has done that every morning since. That is what she has done about it, and there is not one person in that building or on that street who could tell you that anything had been decided.

---

What nobody in that building has done is mention it. There are four flats and there are people in them who have lived in that building for thirty years and not one of them has ever asked the woman on the top floor what she is doing there for six hours a day, and not one of them has ever asked the man on the ground floor why he was standing on the step at half past seven, and it has not been raised by either of the two people it concerns.

There is a version of this that becomes something else. She knows what it would become and she has thought about it on the walk down to the shops and back, and the version is that he carries it up and she stops carrying it, and then one morning he does not come, and then she has to go back to carrying it up, and by then there is a thing that has been arranged.

So she carries it up. That is all she has done about it and that is the whole of her answer, and it does not need saying out loud to anybody, and she has not said it out loud to anybody.

**SIX WEEKS AND FOUR PEOPLE IN THAT BUILDING HAVE WATCHED THE SAME THING AND NOT ONE OF THEM HAS SAID ANYTHING TO ANYBODY ELSE ABOUT IT, AND THE ONLY THING THAT HAS ACTUALLY CHANGED IN THAT BUILDING SINCE NOVEMBER IS THAT HE NO LONGER STANDS ON THE STEP.**

On the first of January the water in that building was off from ten in the morning until about two in the afternoon.

Nobody in that building knew why. There is no notice and there was nobody there to ask, because the man who does the water in that building comes on a Tuesday and it was not Tuesday, and there is a part of the ground floor he shuts and does not open again in the same day.

She was up at half past seven with it and she had the top floor from about ten past eight until four, and there was no water in that flat for four hours, and she does not use much water. She made the mending do. She read more than four pages of a chapter of something, which she does not usually do.

At about twenty to four she went down with nothing and out and along the front of the building and away round the far side of it and back, which is what she does, and she came in at about five past four.

He was at the bottom of the way up with her sack standing beside him.

Not holding it. Standing beside it. It was on the step where he stands, with the strap laid over it, and he was at the door of his own flat with his back half turned and he had evidently been in the way up and then gone back down.

She stopped.

Nobody in that building went past while that was happening, because at five past four on the first of January of a year nobody had anything else to do, and the two of them stood there for about ten seconds.

Then she said, "Put it down."

And he said, "I have put it down."

And she said, "You have carried it down from the top."

And he said, "It was coming down anyway."

And she said, "It was not coming down at four o'clock. I come down at four o'clock with it. I go out at four o'clock without it."

And he said, "I know."

And she picked the sack up off the step and put the strap on her shoulder and took it in the door, and he went into his flat and shut his door, and that was the whole of it.

She has been up at half past seven every morning since the November and she has carried it up. He has been at the bottom of the way up on some of the mornings and not on others, and she has gone past him and up, and he has stopped standing on the step altogether now and is simply at the door of his own flat, and on the morning of the first of January it had ended with him having carried it down and her having taken it back off the step at the bottom.

**SHE WENT DOWN AT TWENTY TO FOUR WITHOUT IT AND CAME BACK AT FIVE PAST FOUR AND FOUND IT ON THE BOTTOM STEP, AND HE HAD CARRIED IT DOWN FROM THE TOP, AND SHE SAID PUT IT DOWN AND HE SAID I HAVE PUT IT DOWN, AND SHE TOOK IT OFF THE STEP AND PUT THE STRAP ON HER SHOULDER AND WENT UP IT AGAIN.**
