# Chapter 0246 — The Column

Friday the seventeenth of June 2016, from about eleven in the morning, the shared yard at Penhale, and the office hut on the concrete.

**A PACT DOES NOT HOLD BECAUSE ANYBODY IS KEEPING IT AND IT DOES NOT HOLD BECAUSE NOBODY IS KEEPING IT, AND THE ONLY WAY TO FIND OUT WHICH IS TO GO AND STAND IN THE ONE PLACE WHERE THE WHOLE OF IT IS VISIBLE FROM, WHICH IS A HUT WITH A LEDGER IN IT, AND WHAT YOU FIND OUT IS A COLUMN THAT NOBODY HAS LOOKED AT SINCE THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF APRIL.**

The woman who has been in the Methodist hall on Carrow Lane about two years got a bus to the end of the road at about ten to eleven and walked the last half mile, because there is no bus from the end of the road, and she had not been to Penhale before and had not wanted to go.

The bend in the road is not a village. There are eight or nine yards down it, some of them open, some of them not, and the shared yard is one of the open ones, and it is a slab of concrete with a shed at the back that belongs to a man who is not here, and racking that belongs to somebody else, and a pipe that belongs to the water, and two trucks that belong to a haulier, and a bench, and a fence.

Nothing in it belongs to anybody who uses it. That is what it is for, and it is the only thing about it that anybody will explain to you, and the woman with the key of the hall in this borough had said so on a Sunday in April without being asked and had then said there was a second half to it that she did not have.

The wall sheet is framed and hung by the office door at a height that means you have to stand up to read it, which is not a design decision anybody made. It is A4 in a wooden frame that has been painted twice and it is about six inches of type across the top and one line of it.

The woman from the hall stood in front of it for a while.

"That's the one," she said. "That's word for word the one that was on the bench in the wind, and I've read it about four times since and I keep expecting it to have more on it by now." She put her thumb under the frame. "It's got a second line."

"It has," said the man in his forties, who keeps the book under Haldane Perry and who has been in that hut a long time. "Nobody prints the second one."

"Who does?"

"Nobody prints it. It's on the wall. It's been on that wall longer than I've been in this hut and it came off the machine that set the first one, and the first one came off the same machine as a sheet about the size of a postcard that's on a wall about eight miles up the estuary with a man's name under one line of it." He shut the hut door behind him with his heel. "You can read the second line if you want to. I'll tell you now that I have not got anything to tell you about it, because I have got a theory about what it is for and I have never once been asked, and I am not going to stand in a hut and guess at a woman, and nobody in this yard would and the man on the bench would tell you the same and mean it more than I do."

She read the second line. She did not say what it said, and neither did he, and that was the end of it.

"Can I see the book?"

"You can see the book," said Haldane Perry, from the bench, where he had been sitting since about a quarter to eleven with the ledger shut on his knee, and he was eighty-nine and had not got up, and nobody asked him to.

She asked him whether she could take the sheet down and take it away, for an afternoon, to have it looked at properly.

"No," he said.

"Why not? It's a piece of paper in a frame."

"Because you'll bring it back," he said, "and you'll have it in a bag on a bus and you'll have had it in the rain, and a thing in a frame is a thing that's meant to be in a frame, and that frame's been on that wall since before your mother drove, and there's nothing in it anybody wants to look at more closely than that." He put his thumb on the shut ledger. "And I'll tell you the other reason, which is that you'd take it away and come back and ask me what it means, and I've got four men and a woman in this county who could put a bit of paper in front of me and make me say something about it, and you're not one of them and I'd rather you stayed not one of them."

She said that was fair. She said it because it was.

---

The carbon book is on the desk under the window in the hut and it is a book that has been kept the same way since before the lanyard, and the man's lanyard says PENHALE SHARED YARD and, in a different hand, since 1987.

The columns are ruled in ink and the ruling is straight and it has not been done by hand for a very long time, which meant somebody printed a template at some point and every book since has been the same template, and that is the single most reassuring thing in the whole arrangement and nobody in this borough has ever noticed it.

There are a great many columns ruled in. Nearly all of them are jobs in, jobs out, dates, dockets, hours, and the arithmetic, and they get written in every day of a working week, and the pages go yellow faster than anybody in this hut would like.

One of them is headed with nothing at all, and under the heading there is a gap in the writing about two inches deep, and the gap runs the whole length of the page and it has been in every book since the template was printed.

"What's this one."

"That one came from a lady with a bag in April," said the man in his forties. "A line in it that isn't a claim. Took about four seconds. It's the easiest thing anybody's asked me in my time in this hut and I told her at the time it was worth exactly nothing, and I still think it's worth nothing, and a column's a column, and if you wrote a hundred of them down they'd still be words in a book that four of us turn the pages of."

"You've not written anything else in it."

"No."

"Has anybody asked you to?"

"No."

"Could anybody make you?"

"There's nobody in this county that could," he said, "and that's not a boast, it's the reason I said yes in four seconds and I'll say it again tomorrow. There is no one to make me and that is the entire arrangement, and every yard on this bend is like it, and there are nine of us, and about four of us have never sent a crew out and never took one in and we put money in exactly the same as the five, and you'll not find a bad word said about any of the four."

"Then how do you know it's working?"

That was the question she had come the half mile for.

He looked at the book for a bit, and then he turned it round on the desk so that she could see it, and put his finger on the column headed with nothing at all.

"You don't," he said. "That's the answer and it took you twenty minutes to get to it and I'm glad you got to it, because it's the only true thing anybody has said at that wall in about nine years. I could tell you how much money went through that shed last year. I could tell you to the penny and I'd be right, and I have never once been asked and I am not going to be asked by a woman who's come on a bus with a shopping bag, because the day somebody writes a number like that down it stops being a fact about a yard and becomes a figure about a yard, and a figure about a yard is how you get a yard that somebody has to have a good year." He shut it. "Nobody counts it. That's the whole of it. Nobody's counting it, so it can't fall. And the line your friend put in the gap in April is the only thing in that book in nine years that anybody wrote because they wanted to and not because somebody wanted it out of them, and it isn't worth anything, and it is in there."

Haldane Perry had not opened the ledger at any point in this.

"Aye," he said, to nobody, on the bench, in the sun.

Nobody in that yard asked the woman from the hall what she was going to do with any of it, and she did not tell them, and she got the bus back at about ten past one and the same half mile, and she did not write anything down on the bus and did not write anything down at home, and she has not mentioned it to anybody in the hall on Carrow Lane since, except once, to say that she had now seen the original of the thing off the bench, and that the two of them were printed on different days by the same machine.

**AND THE COLUMN HEADED WITH NOTHING AT ALL HAS ONE LINE IN IT THAT IS NOT A CLAIM, AND A WOMAN WALKED HALF A MILE BACK TO A BUS WITH A SHOPPING BAG, AND THE FRAMED SHEET IS STILL ON THAT WALL.**
