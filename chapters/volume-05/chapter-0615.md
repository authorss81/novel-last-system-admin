# Chapter 0615 — The Man at the Top of Our Road

It is the twelfth of January 2021 and it is a Tuesday, and it starts before half past seven and goes on past six, in this borough.

**A WOMAN SIX HOUSES BELOW HIM TOLD HIM AT THE BUS STOP THAT THE MAN AT THE TOP OF THEIR ROAD HAS HAD TO GO INTO A PLACE, AND HE HAS SPENT THE WHOLE DAY TRYING TO FIND OUT HOW SHE CAME BY IT, AND HE HAS NOT FOUND OUT, AND HE HAS NOT BEEN UP THE ROAD.**

He is a man in his early sixties. He lives alone in a road of terraces that runs up a hill and he has done since before the summer. He is on the bus at about ten past eight most mornings and gets off at the same place and has done for a good many years.

The man at the top of the road has been at the top of the road for a good while and is in his early seventies and lives on his own, and he is the only person in that road whom this man would call by name.

What she said was this.

He was at the stop at about ten past eight and she was there before him, which was unusual, and the bus was not due for another bit.

And she said, "You'll have heard about the man at the top."

And he said, "Which of them."

And she said, "There's only the one at the top."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "He's had to go into a place."

And he said, "Has he."

And she said, "His daughter sorted it. He didn't want to go."

And he said, "Which daughter."

And she said, "The one in Leeds."

And he said, "Right."

And the bus came round the corner and got in and she got on it and went to the back like she does, and he stood on the pavement and missed it on purpose, which he has never done before in his life.

Here is the thing that has been wrong since about half past eight.

She did not say where she had heard it. That is ordinary. People say things they have heard.

She did not say it as though she were passing it on either, and that is not ordinary. It came out the way a person says something they have already decided the answer to.

And she called him the man at the top. Nobody on that bus calls him that. He has a name and it is on his post and there are hundreds of people in this borough who could tell you it, and she is a woman in her late sixties who has lived six houses below him since before the war and has spoken to him twice, both times about the recycling, and she has never once used his name.

---

Here is what he has spent the day on, and it was not the man at the top of the road.

The man at the top of the road is not why he could not eat his lunch. The man at the top of the road is a thing he would be able to walk up there and check on this evening and that is not the difficulty.

The difficulty is where it came from.

A woman who has spoken to him twice does not know that a man at the top of their road has gone anywhere. And a woman who has spoken to him twice does not know what he has for dinner, let alone what has happened to him.

So she heard it somewhere, and he had that worked out before the middle of the morning, and then he spent the rest of the day on the second half of it, which is that he cannot get to the somewhere.

He went through it in order and here is the whole of what he got.

One: the shop on the parade, where she goes most mornings, and where there are three people on the till and one of them is a man who talks.

Two: her own house, six houses below his, and whoever is in it.

Three: the church, which she has not been inside since about the summer before last, so he put that one to the side.

And then he stopped and added the fourth, which he did not want to add.

Four: the man at the top of the road himself.

He sat with that one in a car park on the estate for a good while in the middle of the morning and he could not make it go anywhere either.

He went into the shop on the parade at about ten past twelve because there was nothing in the house.

He has been going into that shop for most of a decade and the woman on the till who is not the manager knows his order, and the manager does not know him at all.

And he said, "Has your boss been in this week."

And she said, "Which boss."

And he said, "You know."

And she said, "He's in on the Thursday. You know he does that."

And that was the end of it, and he bought a paper he did not want and a bag of apples he did not need and he came out.

He stood outside for a bit. That is worth recording because it is what he did with a good part of the middle of that day.

There is a bus stop a short walk from that shop door and he went and stood at it, on the wrong side, and watched who came in.

He did that three times over the day and he worked out afterwards that he could not have told anybody what he was expecting to see, and that if the woman had come into that shop at any point he would not have gone in.

That is the honest shape of it. He did not want to catch her. He wanted to be somewhere else when she was not.

Here is the rest, and it is short.

He tried the church at about two, from the road, because there is a board outside it and the board has been the same since before the summer and he stood and read all of it and there was nothing on it about anybody.

He called his daughter at about half past two because his daughter is in Leeds.

He said, "Do you know anything about the man at the top of our road."

And she said, "No. Why?"

And he said, "No reason."

And she said, "Have you heard something?"

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Dad."

And he said, "I'll ring you back."

And that call lasted about a minute and a half and he put the phone down and sat in the kitchen with it on the table in front of him.

That is the first place where he could have said it. That is where he could have said, somebody at the bus stop told me your man's gone into a place.

He did not say it and he has not worked out why and he has been over that call about six times since.

At about half past five he got as far as the bottom of the road and turned round and went home.

He was not tired. That is not what stopped him. He had eaten and he had not drunk anything since the middle of the day and he was in good order and he got to the bottom of that road in no time at all and stood there.

And what stopped him, and he has been honest about this to himself and to nobody else, is that if he goes up there he has to knock, and if he knocks, then whatever she told him at a bus stop this morning becomes something he did.

Not something he found. Something he went and checked, and then went and told somebody about, and then everybody on that road has it.

And if he does not go up there, then it is a thing a woman said to him at a stop on a Tuesday morning, and it stops at him.

He has not decided that that is the better of the two.

His neighbour came up the path at about ten past six with a casserole dish she does this most Fridays and does not do on the other Fridays, and he let her in and she put it in the oven and stood at the kitchen door.

And she said, "You look like you've had a day."

And he said, "I've had an ordinary one."

And she said, "You've not touched the road."

And he said, "I know."

And she said, "You'd know if you went up there."

And he said, "Would I."

And she said, "He'd have the light on."

And he said, "Would he."

And she said, "He keeps it on till about nine."

And he said, "Right," and she went down the path, and he shut the door and stood in the hall and worked out that he now had a second thing he had not known before the casserole dish, which was that a man at the top of that road keeps a light on until nine o'clock.

He does not go up there. He has not gone up there.

He has also not told one person in this borough that he has been told anything at all.

**SHE TOLD HIM AT THE STOP THAT THE MAN AT THE TOP HAS HAD TO GO INTO A PLACE AND SHE CALLED HIM THE MAN AT THE TOP AND SHE DID NOT SAY WHERE SHE GOT IT, AND HE HAS BEEN ABOUT THIS BOROUGH ALL DAY AND HAS NOT BEEN UP THE ROAD, AND HE KNOWS NOW THAT THE MAN AT THE TOP KEEPS A LIGHT ON UNTIL NINE.**
