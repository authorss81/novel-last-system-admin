# Chapter 0868 — The Tenth Flag

Sunday the tenth of December 2023, in the front path at number nine from the time the frost has come out of the joints until the street has gone quiet. The tenth flag, and what a thumb finds on its edge.

**THERE ARE NINE PATH JOINTS FULL AT NUMBER NINE AND THE TENTH OPEN UNDER A FLAGGED FLAG WITH A LIP ON IT, AND SHE MEANS TO TAKE THAT FLAG UP AND CARRY IT ON ITS EDGE AND DRAW THE TWO HANDS APART ONCE TO SEE WHETHER ANYTHING GIVES, AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING GOES IN THROUGH THE SIDE GATE WITH A BAG AND SAYS NOTHING.**

The path is eleven flags long from the gate to the front step and it is nine years old, which is young for flags.

Nine of the joints are full. They are full the way a path gets full, which is with grit off the front bed and a season of leaves worked down into it, and that is what a joint is for. A full joint is a joint that is doing its job.

The tenth is open. It is the one in front of the step and it is open for about a foot along its length and closed for the rest of it, which is what a joint under a lipped flag looks like from above.

A lip on a flag is a projection on the underside of it, about half an inch deep and half an inch wide, running along one long edge, and it is there so that the flag below beds on the flag above instead of rocking on it. You do not see one. You do not need to. You find out that a flag has one by putting your heel on it, and a flag with a good lip under it takes your weight and stays down, and a flag without one gives you a knuckle's worth of movement and puts your weight on one corner.

That tenth flag has got a lip on it. It has had one for nine years and it has got one still, at the top end, where the flag is thick.

At the bottom end it has not got one any more, because a lip is made out of the thickness of a flag, and the thickness of that flag is not the same at both ends.

She has it up in the middle of the morning and she has it on the flags beside it with the face that was up turned over, because the underside is the only place the lip shows.

Then she takes the two edges of it, thumb and first finger on one side and the same on the other, and draws the hands apart once.

Not hard. Once.

That is the test for a cracked flag and it is the only test there is. A stone that is going to break is broken through about two thirds of the depth before anything shows on the face, and the first thing your hands tell you about it is that it gives. It gives about the width of a thumbnail and then it stops and the sound of it stopping is the sound you were listening for. If the hands come together and meet and stop with a knock, the stone is whole.

They meet with a knock.

The flag is whole. It is not cracked, it has not cracked, and it is not going to crack, because a flag that has survived nine winters and the foot of every person who has crossed this path since it went down is a sound thing.

Which means the tenth joint is open for a reason that has nothing to do with the stone and everything to do with the edge of it.

So she does the other thing, which is the one that tells her everything.

She carries the flag on its edge with a thumb and a first finger at each end of the thickness, and she reads it the way you read a coin by running a thumb along the rim.

At the top end, which is the end against the house, it is an inch and a half.

At the bottom end, which is the end in the joint that is open, it is three-quarters of an inch.

In the middle it is about an inch.

There is no wear anywhere on it and no chip on it and nothing on either face. It was dressed at one end and not at the other, which happens when a flag is worked after it is laid rather than before, and what a man does to the end of a flag to make it bed is take thickness off it, and he takes it off with a bolster and he takes it off from the end that will be lowest.

Which is why it is the top end that is thick, and there is a small flat dither of pale stone about two inches across on that end where the bolster stood when it was dressed, and the face round it is still the colour it was when the flag was taken out of the ground, because a dressed patch takes the weather in a year and the rest of it takes it in about forty.

There is a lesson in that and she knows what it is and does not act on it. A flag that has been dressed at one end is a flag that has been worked once already, and a flag worked twice is a flag with no thickness left at the end that wants it. Whatever is done to the tenth joint in the spring has to be done with what is in the stone and not with what can be taken out of it.

The consequence of that is not subtle and it is not going anywhere.

The lip on that flag is half an inch deep and it is made out of an inch and a half of stone at the top end and three-quarters of an inch of stone at the bottom end, and half of three-quarters of an inch is three-eighths, which is the lip at that end, and a lip of three-eighths in a joint that wants half an inch of bedding is a lip that does not reach.

So the flag beds at the top and stands a quarter of an inch high at the bottom, and the joint under the bottom of it is open for a foot along its length, and the grit that would go into it goes in under the top end where it cannot be seen and never gets there, because the top end is the end that is bedded.

She puts the grit from that foot of the joint in by hand while she has the flag up, and about half of it stays on the flags beside her because there is nowhere in that joint for it to be until the flag comes down again.

That is the whole of what can be done to an open joint with two hands and no bar, and it is a different thing from what was wanted, and she does it at all, because a joint that is emptied of its grit in December is a joint that has less in it to come up out in the spring.

That is the whole of the tenth joint and it has been the whole of the tenth joint for nine years.

It cannot be put right in this weather and it is not going to be put right by anybody on her own, because the flag has to come up and be laid again the other way round with its thick end at the low end, and a flag that has a lip on it is the most awkward thing in a path to get back down, because it has to go in on the lip and settle onto it at the same time, and that is done by two people and a bar and not on a Sunday in December.

---

The woman who does her shopping comes through the side gate about eleven with a bag on her arm and goes up the side path and in at the front door and does not come out again.

They do not speak and there is nothing to speak about. The flag is not on the side path and it is not in the house and neither of them looks at the other.

---

By the time that path is done in the spring, the tenth joint will have been the only one in it still open, and the flag over it will still be a sound flag lying the wrong way up.
