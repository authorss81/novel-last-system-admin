# Chapter 0268 — Bored

Friday the seventh of October 2016, from about ten in the morning, a parade of shops in Tideglass, and a hall off the other end of it.

**A GANG WENT OUT ON THE MONDAY AND CAME BACK ON THE THURSDAY, A VAN CAME BACK MIDWEEK, A PRICE CAME UP, AND SOMEBODY AT FOUR O'CLOCK SAID IT WAS THE FIRST WEEK SHE COULD REMEMBER BEING BORED IN, AND NOBODY DISAGREED.**

The week had been made of the following and none of it was anybody's business.

A gang of four off a firm at the far end of the parade went out on Monday morning to a house in Corrance Lane that had a chimney to come down, a flue to be rebuilt and a stretch of wall to be made good where the flue used to be. They were out five days of it and back in on the Thursday, and the hours went on a sheet that went into a folder in the back of a van and out of that folder on the other side of that water, and nobody in this borough has ever seen that sheet and nobody in this borough is ever going to.

The foreman of that gang is a man who is not on anything, and he was not on anything on the Monday and he was not on anything on the Thursday. When they had the last of the dust down on the Thursday afternoon he stood in the front room of that house in his own working clothes and waited for a man to come and sign a sheet. The man came. The foreman signed it. The man put the sheet into a plastic wallet, said nothing about the week, and asked him nothing about the week, and the foreman said nothing about the week, and then drove a van back to this borough with a broken nearside mirror on it and a roll of tape on the dash that had been there since the Friday.

The woman who does the books at that firm is not on anything either and was the only person in that firm who had any idea what the four men had done all week, and she got the sheet off the van's dash on the Friday morning and keyed it into a spreadsheet and shut the laptop, and there is no list anywhere in this borough with a name on it and there is not going to be.

---

The van came back on the Wednesday off the bridge at about ten past ten with no near mirror on it and a roll of tape on the dash, and the driver said the near one went on the Friday before last in a yard off the Marshmont road and that he had meant to get it done on the Sunday and had not, and that it would be done on the next one.

The price came up on the Thursday. A firm of heating engineers who have a shop two doors off the parade have had a laminated card in their window since before the summer, and on Thursday the figure for coming out was twenty pounds higher than it had been, and on the card, under the figure, there is a line about a level of service and one about a charge for the second hour after that, and there is no line at all with anybody's name on it and there never was.

"Twenty pound," said the man behind the counter on the parade, reading it through his own window. "For what. For coming."

"For coming and saying the thing's the thing's not the thing," said Tomas Renn from four doors down, who had read it through his own window, which is four windows away and further along.

"Who's paying twenty pound."

"Nobody in this borough's paying twenty pound. That's why it's gone up."

---

The hall off that parade is free on a Saturday because nobody rents it, and since the middle of September the woman who has had the key to it for about nine years has been opening it in the afternoon of a Friday as well, because it is on a meter and it is warm, and there is nowhere else in this part of the town to sit that is warm and free, and she does not ask anybody anything when they come in and she does not ask them anything when they go.

About five of them were in it at about half past three on the Friday.

There was a man who comes for the last hour and does not stay, and a woman with a shopping trolley who had been round every charity shop in the borough by then and had won nothing, and a man off a shift at the yard at Sarn with a paper of chips he had not opened, and the young woman of about twenty, and the man from the parade.

They did not do the six. They did the price of a boiler, which was the twenty pounds and the two prices underneath it, and a man in the third house in Corrance Lane whose roof had now been off since April, and which the man from the parade called not a roof any more, it's a hobby, for the fourth time that month, and nobody improved on it and nobody was rude about it either. Then they did about ten minutes on a woman who wanted to know whether anybody had ever had a firm quote for a job and got one, and nobody in that room had, and a man in his forties said he had once, in about 2009, and that it had come in at about half of what the job actually cost, and that he had not had the job done.

At about ten past four the woman of about forty-two with the key to the hall shut the gas off at the meter by the door, and had the padlock and her key in her hand and her coat over the back of a chair, and said it to the room on her way through, the way you say a thing you have been carrying about for two days and have not said.

"This is the first week I can remember being bored in."

Nobody disagreed.

"Since before Christmas," she said, to the radiator, which has been out since about 1998. "Since I had the key, and I have had the key nine years, and I have not been bored once since then, and I know that is not much of a thing to have been, and it is the first one."

"That's not a lot of a thing," said the man from the parade, comfortably, without looking up.

"No," she said. "No, it isn't."

She put the padlock on and went out into the wet, and the man from the parade put his chair on top of another chair, and the woman with the trolley went off up the parade to look for a bin, and the man off the shift at the yard at Sarn took the unopened paper of chips away in his pocket because he had decided to, and the young woman of about twenty put the other chairs away because there had only been five of them and the man from the parade had not done his.

**AND THE GAS WENT OFF AT THE METER BY THE DOOR AND THE HALL KEPT WHAT HEAT IT HAD FOR ANOTHER HOUR AND A QUARTER, AND THE BOY CAME IN AT TEN TO SIX TO FIND SIX PLATES AND A CLOCK NINE MINUTES FAST.**
