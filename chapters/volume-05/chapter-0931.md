# Chapter 0931 — Into The Crossed Gap

Thursday the eighth of August 2024, at the store door of the unit while the yard stands dry from noon. Inside a closed fit.

**HE HAS A STORE DOOR AT THE UNIT STANDING A FINGER OFF THE INSIDE FACE OF ITS FRAME AND HE MEANS TO CROSS HIS FIRST AND SECOND FINGERS AND PUSH THE PAIR INTO THAT GAP WITH THE HAND TURNED TO FACE INWARDS, TO FIND OUT HOW FAR THE GAP RUNS AND WHAT STANDS BEHIND THE LEAF.**

That door has been planed once along its bottom rail since the week it was hung, and the frame it hangs on has kept its line through every year since; it is pine boards about seven feet by three on three butt hinges, closing into a frame of the same timber, and the leaf now stands a finger wide of the frame all the way up the closing side. Timber that size takes its own weight to every plank of it, and this leaf is still taking it.

He wants the gap the leaf is standing off from, and he wants to know what it has become rather than what has been done to it.

A door that shuts into a frame has one place where the two parts meet and everywhere else they merely pass, so the first and second fingers go in crossed to get at the part that merely passes. The timber is cool and slick along the arris and gritted down the face of it, and the two crossed fingers reach about an inch further than either of them would alone.

The gap runs the whole height of the leaf without narrowing anywhere. Behind it, two inches back, stands the back of the frame, and that back face is dry and pale and has never been painted in the forty years the door has been shutting against it.

---

The leaf was hung before the building was dry. Timber hung green goes off its size as it dries, and it goes off most across the grain, so a door hung in the first week of a building loses a quarter of an inch across its width by the following spring. Nothing was done about that then, and the door still shuts, because a door shuts by its corner and not by its face.

The hinge side has gone tight and the closing side has gone open, and the open side is where the air comes in. A door that shuts by its corner can shut a finger open along a whole face and still latch, and the whole reason a door of this age is worth having at all. A door a finger open on the closing side and tight on the hinge side is hanging on its hinges with a weight on it, and the weight on it is the closer, which is on the head of the frame, and it is the same closer that closes the leaf of every other store door in that yard and it was set the same week as the rest of them.

The closers on the store doors at that unit were all hung with their pin a quarter of an inch off the head, and that drop is what the leaf swings down into. A leaf that drops as it swings closes slower and with more noise than one that does not, and a leaf that closes slower stays open longer and lets the yard into the gap for longer as well.

It is the swing that dropped that door and not the season. The door went down a quarter of an inch in its first winter and most of that in the first week of it, and it has gone on closing an eighth of an inch since then and no more.

---

The frame was built in four pieces, and the joints between them are the places where the timber has moved most. The joints are dowelled and the dowels are oak, and every one of them has stood proud on the face the leaf closes against. A proud dowel in the closing joint is not a fault.

It is what lets a door close on the same place in a building that has moved, and there are four of them in that frame and all four stand proud by the same amount. The arris of the frame along its closing edge has been rubbed back by that same amount, and the rubbing has taken the paint off in a band about an inch wide that runs the whole height of the door.

Where a frame is rubbed on its closing edge the paint is gone and the wood beneath it is a shade paler. A pale band down the whole height of a door is the plainest record there is of a leaf that has been shutting hard against something, and it can be read from the yard at thirty feet by a person who has seen one before and by nothing else on the page of a day.

The leaf is pine and it is faced both sides, so that gap is a gap between two pieces of timber and not a hollow. The core of the leaf is still a lining about a quarter of an inch thick, set in grooves in both faces. Lining a leaf leaves it stiff enough to keep a plane true across it and soft enough at its edges to be planed flat, and the arris of the closing side is the one edge of a lined leaf that is not square to anything.

That arris is a sixteenth out of square and it has been a sixteenth out of square for forty years. Every pass of a plane over that edge has made it worse, because a plane takes the high side off and leaves the low one where it is. Where a leaf stands out of square by a sixteenth at its arris it presents less of itself to the frame, and the space between the two widens at that one line and stands at a finger all the way down, which is why the gap does not taper at the head or at the foot of it.

The three butt hinges are steel and their leaves are bedded in the frame and in the door alike, and the screws are round-shanked and older than any screw now sold, and they go two inches into soft pine. A screw that far into soft pine goes on turning a little every year. The frame face along the closing joint shows a fine pale fan round each screw head, where the timber has crushed under the head and moved back a hair since, and the fans have not joined together because the joint between the frame pieces is the only place the timber moved enough to open.

He draws the crossed fingers out of the gap, closes the hand on nothing and puts it in his pocket, and stands a foot to one side of the door with his shoulder to the wall and the yard behind him. At the side door of the unit store, out of the yard, stands the woman from two doors down with a canvas bag and no sign of setting it down.

"Your ridge has gone black over the flats."

"It has done since the morning."

"Then I will get the awning out before it comes."

"It will not come down on it before four."

She shifted the bag from one hand to the other and kept it off the frame where the damp could not get at it, and stood in the doorway with the bag in her hands until the draught had gone past her, and then she went on across the yard with the bag and left the doorway empty behind her, and the door stood where it stood and the gap in it went on being a finger wide. She took the long way round the store to get out of the yard and she did not use the side door.

What went into words was a ridge, an awning and a time, and nothing about the gap in that door entered any of it, and the leaf was a foot away from her while she asked and stayed a foot away while she answered, and the crossed fingers were in his pocket by then. He had not taken the hand out again while she was at the door.

The leaf stays a finger off the frame it shuts against, the paint inside the gap matt and the paint outside it chalky, and the arris between them a sixteenth out of square and staying there. By the time that first winter is over there will be another eighth of an inch gone off the closing side, because the leaf has dropped as far as the nearer hinge allows and the nearer hinge is at the top.

The fit and the gap are the door's own and will go on being the door's own, and by the time the frame comes to move again it will be moving the two of them together, the way it has moved them every year since it went up.
