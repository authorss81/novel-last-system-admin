# Chapter 0393 — On the Table

Friday the twenty-fourth of February 2018, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past ten at night, a shop off a parade, and a room at the back of it, in Tideglass.

**A MAN IN HIS FORTIES HAD A BOX IN THE BACK OF HIS VAN FOR A FORTNIGHT AND HE CARRIED IT ABOUT WITH HIM AND USED IT FOR NOTHING, AND ON THE AFTERNOON OF THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF FEBRUARY HE PUT IT DOWN ON THE TABLE IN A ROOM HE HAD BEEN INTO TWICE BEFORE AND HAD NOT BEEN INTO FOR MOST OF A YEAR, AND HE SAID NOTHING ABOUT IT TO ANYBODY IN THAT ROOM, AND HE WENT AT ABOUT FIVE O'CLOCK, AND HE HAS NOT BEEN INTO THAT ROOM SINCE, AND NONE OF THE PEOPLE IN THAT ROOM HAS PUT IT TO ANYBODY.**

The shop is on the parade and the counter is along the left-hand side and through the wall at the back of that counter there is an opening into a small room that used to be a store, with a kettle on a table in it, four chairs round that table, and a sink in the corner that works. The woman who works in a shop takes that room from about four in the afternoon on a weekday.

It is about nine foot by ten with no window in it and one lamp in the middle of the ceiling, and there is a second lamp in a flex that comes out of a hole in the wall and hangs over the table because the one in the ceiling is not on a pull.

---

The box is a cardboard box about eighteen inch by twelve and twelve deep, brown, with the top folded over and about a foot of the seam run with brown tape that has gone hard and yellow, and it has a hole in one corner about the size of a coin where a mouse or something has been at it.

There is about thirty pound in it.

Inside the box there is a small electric fire. It is the kind with a grille across the front and a fan behind the grille and a coil behind that, on two feet, about fourteen inch wide and about twelve deep and about fourteen tall, and it is cream and brown and it has a flex about six foot long coming out of the back of it with a moulded plug on the end.

It worked when it came out of that house. He knows it worked when it came out of that house because the woman whose house it was said it worked, and he tried it in a workshop about a week after he got it and it ran and it was warm.

It has not worked since. He has had it on a bench in his own front room for about two hours in the last fortnight and it did nothing at all, and the coil behind the grille is a flat spiral of ribbed steel and you can see from the front of it that nothing has broken.

He does not know what is wrong with it. He is not going to find out.

He got it out of a house on the Cleeve road in the middle of January.

He was in there for a day and a half at the start of January doing a kitchen, and there was a small electric fire in a box at the bottom of a wardrobe in the front room, and the box had been packed by somebody and the tape had been on it a long time, and he said he would take it away because it was in the way of the wardrobe.

The woman of that house had said she would put it out for him, and he said he would take it when he came, and he came on the fifteenth of January and it was at the front gate in its box with the tape on it.

He put it in the back of the van with a sack round it.

---

It has been in that van for a fortnight and it is not in his house and it is not in his yard and it is not at his mother's and it is not at the shop of a man he knows who has a yard at the top of the town.

The reason is that he has been in and out of that van every single day since the fifteenth of January. He has gone to that van, he has come away from that van, and he has never once in fourteen days been standing at the back of it with his hands free and somewhere to put a box.

He has put it on the seat beside him twice. He has had it on the passenger seat with the seat belt over it, which he does not do. He has had it on his own bed at home for one night, on the floor, and he carried it back down to the van in the morning before the girl was up.

On the Thursday before the twenty-fourth he had it out on the hardstanding at the bottom of a job and it rained and he took it back in and the bottom of the box was damp and the fire was damp and that is the second time he has thought about what is wrong with it.

It is a box of about thirty pound that is in a van.

He came into that shop at about half past four on the Friday with the box under his arm.

He put it down on the table at the empty end, the long side to the table and the taped seam up, and he pulled the chair out and sat down in it.

Nobody said anything about it.

The woman who works in a shop was at the hatch with her sleeves up and the kettle was already on because it was the end of the week and about four people come through that room at the end of a week. The man who works nights was at the window end with his bag of ice on the floor between his boots, which is where he is most days, and he had been in that room since about twenty past four.

The man in his sixties was at the other end with a cup. There were three people in that room and then there were four, and that is all the room takes.

The man in his thirties came in about ten minutes later for a box of something and went out through the hatch and did not come in.

She asked him once what she had been asking everybody in that room since the middle of last year, which was whether the boxes should come in on the Friday or the Saturday, and he said the Friday, and that was the only thing said to him about anything, and it was not about the box.

He was in that room about half an hour.

Nobody in that room mentioned the box to him and he did not mention it to them, and at no point in those half an hour did the box stop being a thing on a table.

The man who works nights moved his carrier bag about six inches along the floor under the table so that the box was not against the wall on his side. He did that about four minutes after it went down, and he did it without looking up, and the woman who works in a shop saw him do it.

The man in his sixties did not look at it once. He was reading a paper and he had a cup and he was on about the fourth or fifth page of something, and the box was at the other end of a table nine foot long and he was not going to look at it.

The woman who works in a shop put her elbow on the table nearer it than she would otherwise have done, and when she cleared the cups at the end she put hers on top of the box, because that was the flat place, and the box took it.

The box was on that table for four and a half hours. It was there at about five o'clock when he stood up.

He went at about five o'clock.

He put his cup on the table, he said nothing to anybody, and he went out through the hatch and along that counter and out of the front of that shop and got in his van and drove off, and he had nothing in his hands, and there was a fire in a box on the table behind him and he was not going to be told about it and he was not going to have been asked.

He has been in that shop three times since the twenty-fourth of February. He has gone in the front, along that counter, paid for what he came for, and out of the front.

He has not been through that hatch. He used to go through that hatch, not often, but he used to, and he has not been in that room since the Friday afternoon of the twenty-fourth and there is nothing at all in the way and the hatch is where the hatch has always been.

Nothing has been said to anybody about it.

The woman who works in a shop has not asked the man in his forties about it and is not going to. The man who works nights has not asked and is not going to. The man in his sixties has not asked and does not know there is a question to ask.

The reason is not care. It is that in about four seconds, when that box went down on that table and nobody said anything about it, all three of them worked out the same thing, which is that a man who has carried a thing about with him for a fortnight and then put it down in a room without a word is not a man you ask about a thing.

And there is no form of words for it that would be about the box. Anything they said would be about him, not about the box, and all three of them would be doing it in a room with a person in it, and there is no version of that in which it comes out right.

So the box is on that table and the woman who works in a shop has put things on it, and the man who works nights has moved his bag so that it is not jammed, and the man in his sixties has never looked at it, and the three of them have not said one word about it to each other or to anybody.

**THE MAN WHO WORKS NIGHTS CAME INTO THAT ROOM ON THE MONDAY AFTER AND SAT DOWN AT THE WINDOW END AND THE BOX WAS ON THE TABLE AND HE SAID NOTHING, AND HE HAS BEEN IN THAT ROOM A GOOD MANY TIMES SINCE AND THE BOX IS STILL ON THE TABLE.**
