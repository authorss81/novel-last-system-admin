# Chapter 0618 — The Chair She Was In

It is Wednesday the twenty-seventh of January 2021 and it opens at nine and shuts at seven, and every hour of it happens in this borough.

**A WOMAN IN HER MID SIXTIES WHO HAS BEEN COMING TO HIM SINCE BEFORE THE SUMMER TOLD HIM IN THE CHAIR THAT HE SHOULD GET HIS GIRL TO RING HIM, AND HE HAS NOT GOT ONE, AND HE HAS SPENT THE DAY ON WHETHER SHE WAS TALKING ABOUT HIM, AND HE HAS NOT ASKED HER AND SHE HAS GONE.**

He is a man in his early thirties. He has taken over a barber's shop on a parade of traders off the high street and he is good at it, which is not a boast, it is why the shop is busy on a Wednesday when most of them are not.

He lives in a shared house about fifteen minutes away with two other men and there is a woman who does his washing who is not one of them.

---

Here is what happened, and it took a few minutes of the middle of the morning and it started for no reason at all.

She was in the chair for a cut she has had at about that rate since before the summer, and she is a woman in her mid sixties who has a card in her handbag and a bus to catch, and she talks, and everybody who cuts her hair knows that.

And she was talking about her granddaughter's swimming.

And she said, "She's in with them now. Not the little lot, the big lot, and they're in the lane with the postbox."

And he said, "Good."

And she said, "She'd have said no. That's what I said. You never say no at that age."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Then she went and did it in October."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "You should get yours to ring you."

And he said, "Get who to ring me."

And she said, "Your girl. If she's at that school, that's about the age she'd be."

And he cut, and looked in the mirror at the back of her head, and said nothing at all for what felt like a while.

And she said, "You all right."

And he said, "I'm all right."

And she said, "You've gone quiet."

And he said, "I've been thinking about a bit at the back."

And she said, "You've been thinking about a bit at the back for a good while."

Nothing else was said about it. That is worth being exact about.

He finished the cut. He did the sides and the back and the front and he did it as well as he does it, which is to say he did not drop anything and he did not rush the neck, and she looked at herself in the mirror the way she always does and said the thing she always says about the back, and paid, and went.

And he has not been able to get any further than that and he has been over the whole of it about four times since.

She did not ask him if he had a girl. She did not say she was sorry. She did not say the girl's name, or the year, or where she lives, or anything at all that would let him check it.

She asked him to get her to ring him, as advice, the way you advise a man about his car.

Here is what he has got, and he has been over it, and this is all of it.

The shop is on a parade off the high street and some thirty people come into it in a week and he has been in it every day since before the summer and there are two barbers on a Saturday and it is him alone the rest of the week.

She has been coming since before the summer and a good while before that. He knows how long because the card is in her handbag and he has seen it every time and she reads a line off it for the length of a haircut and then puts it away.

So he knows her face and her card and her bus and the fact that she has a granddaughter in the lane with the postbox, and she knows a girl in her late teens.

There is no girl. He has never had one. He has told two people that he is not going to have one and one of them was sitting at his own mother's table.

And she did not look at him as though she were asking. That is the whole of it. She had it as a fact. She was not fishing and she was not being kind and she was not being odd. She was talking about his daughter the way she talks about her own granddaughter, which is to say without any weight on it at all.

---

He went through the rest of the day with the rest of it.

He had six men in after her and he did all six and he cannot tell you one thing about any of their hair, which is not usual and which he noticed happening.

At about half past twelve he got his phone out and looked at his mother's number, and he put it back in the drawer, and he has thought about that since and cannot say what it was.

At about two he worked out a way to test it, which is the thing he is most pleased with and most unhappy about.

There are three photographs in the shop. One is on the wall behind the mirror and it is the man he took it from, who had that chair for as long as anybody in the parade can remember. One is on the shelf by the door of the shopfront as it was before the fascia changed. One is a print of the whole parade bought off a market stall, and there are nine people on it and he cannot tell you one of them.

He stood at the shelf and looked at all three of them for long enough for the shop to be dead quiet, and he worked out that if she had put a girl in his head then she had done it off his own face and not off anything in the room, and that he has therefore got nothing, and that the only test that exists is the one he cannot run.

At about half past five a man came in who does the tax on the shop and who has been coming for a while and who is not a talker, and got a cut, and paid, and went, and the shop emptied.

And he stood behind the chair she had been in with a brush in his hand and about an inch of hair on it, and he put the brush in the box, and he looked at the mirror, and he could see the chair and the floor under it and the window and the front of the shop.

He has worked out where she was sitting when she said it. She had her own face in the mirror. And she had his, behind and to the side, which is the only way a person cutting hair can be looked at by a person in a chair.

And he has worked out that she would have been looking at him when she said it.

His flatmate was in the kitchen at about half past six when he came in, and asked him what he wanted, and he said he did not know, and the other one said he would do beans on toast, and he said all right.

And he sat at the table and said, "Who came in today."

And the other one said, "I didn't see. You were on your own all day, weren't you."

And he said, "I was."

And the other one said, "You all right?"

And he said, "Yeah."

And the other one said, "You've said that twice."

And he said, "It's true twice."

And the other one said, "That's not the same as being fine."

And he said, "It is where I'm at."

And that was the whole of it, and the beans were good, and he has not asked her, and she has a bus to catch and she will be back in that chair again before the month is out, and he will be standing at the other side of the room with her own face in the mirror and hers behind him, and there is no version of the next time she comes in which he asks.

**SHE ASKED HIM TO GET HIS GIRL TO RING HIM AS IF SHE HAD ONE IN HER LATE TEENS AND HAD PUT IT TO HIM AS ADVICE AND NOT AS A QUESTION, AND HE HAS BEEN IN THAT SHOP EVERY DAY SINCE AND HAS NOT WORKED OUT WHETHER SHE MEANT HIM, AND HE WILL NOT ASK HER, AND SHE WILL BE BACK IN THAT CHAIR AGAIN BEFORE THE MONTH IS OUT.**
