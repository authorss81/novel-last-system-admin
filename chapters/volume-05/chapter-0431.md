# Chapter 0431 — One Sink

Thursday the second of August 2018, from about half past four in the afternoon until about ten that night, a shop off that parade, and a verge off a side street, in Tideglass.

**BOTH OF THEM WANTED IT AND ONE OF THEM GOT IT, AND THE OTHER ONE SAID NOTHING AND WENT AWAY, AND HE DID NOT MIND, AND NOBODY WHO SAW IT COULD HAVE TOLD YOU WHICH OF THOSE THINGS HAD HAPPENED.**

There is a fireclay sink.

It is a Belfast sink, thirty inch by twenty inch by about fourteen deep, cast in one piece with a raised rim and a soap dish moulded into the back of it, and it weighs a bit under a hundred and fifty pound. It came out of the ground floor of a house at the top of that street in the middle of April when the house was taken down, and it has been on that verge ever since under a sheet of polythene with the front door of the house leaning against it and a brick on the polythene.

It is not worth a great deal. It is worth about the same as a good weekend.

It is also exactly the thing that a man in this borough who has spent his life in other people's kitchens has wanted maybe twice in twenty years, and there are two of them, and both of them know it is there.

---

The man in his forties was told about it at about half past four that afternoon by a merchant in the town eight miles off who had been asked whether there was a big fireclay going cheap and who said there was, and who did not want it.

That merchant does not know him. He is a man who sells building materials and does not get out of his yard, and he had a man in front of him at about half past four who said the words big fireclay and cheap, and he said yes, there is one, and where, and that was the whole of the conversation, and the man went straight out to his van and was on that verge inside two hours with four other men in a shop in between buying a coil of galvanised wire he had no need for.

He got to that verge at about half past six.

There were three other men there. Two of them were looking at it and one of them had a tape out and was doing the obvious thing of measuring the hole where it was going to go. The man in his forties stood at the foot of the verge for about a minute and a half and did not say anything to any of them, and at about ten to seven he pulled the polythene back, and the four of them carried it into the back of his own van in about a quarter of an hour.

The man in his thirties came up that street at about ten to seven.

He had heard about it at about half past four that afternoon, across a counter off that parade, from a man who deals in pipe and who had it from a man who had it from a man who had it from the man who took that house down. That is how everything in this borough travels and it travels the same week.

He came up the street at a walking pace with a bag over his shoulder because he had been up that street for a job in a house near the top of it and had gone back the way he had come. He turned in at the end and the van was still there with the back open and the polythene was on the ground and there were three men around the back of it.

He stopped on the other side of the road.

He wanted it.

None of which is complicated, and none of which is noble. He had been in something over fifty kitchens in this borough in nine years and every one of them had the same pressed steel thing in it, and he had wanted a real one for about two years and had not once said so to anybody, and had not gone looking for one, and had not known until about a minute before that he still wanted it.

He stood on the opposite side of that road for about a minute.

The man in his forties looked up and saw him and lifted his chin about an inch, the way a man does to a man he knows and has no business doing anything for.

The man in his thirties said, "Just had a look."

"Right," said the man in his forties.

And that was all. There was no claim made and none withdrawn, and no offer of a share and no question about who had been there first, and the man in his thirties turned round and went back down the street at the same walking pace with his bag on his shoulder and did not look at it again.

---

He did not mind.

He wants that to be said in the right way, and it is his own wanting so he is the only one who can get it right, and what he can say is this. By the time he had stood on that opposite kerb for about a minute, most of the wanting had gone out of it. Not all of it. He will not be able to look at a Belfast sink in a shop window in this borough for the rest of his life without about a second of that, and there is no way of arranging that out.

But it did not sit on him. He has been passed over for a job of his own about six times in nine years and every one of those sat on him for a week. This did not sit on him at all. He went down that street, and he turned up the side street at the end, and he put his bag in the back of his own van in a yard and shut the doors, and about four minutes later he found that he was whistling, which he did not notice doing and would not have been able to report.

There is a version of that evening in which he has said something. He has had it in his mouth twice. Once on that kerb, when the man in the van lifted his chin at him, and once in that shop at about ten past five that evening when he went back in for a pack of screws he did not need and stood at that counter for about a minute and a half while the woman bagged them.

Neither time came out. There was no moment in either of those two places at which a sentence of any kind would have been the right sentence, and a man does not make a sentence at a woman over a pack of screws.

There is nothing in that. Not minding is not a virtue and it is not a defeat and nobody awarded him anything, and the only person who has any idea that he wanted that sink is him, and he has not said the word sink to one single person since about a quarter past seven on that Thursday evening, including to himself in his own head, where he has been using the word other than that.

He went home at about nine. He ate. He did about half an hour of something at a table and went to bed at about half past ten.

He has not gone back up that street, and he did not go back the following week to see whether there was anything else on that verge, and if there had been something else on that verge he would not have wanted that either, and he is not going to find out whether that is true.

Nobody in this borough has any idea that there were two men after that thing at all. Nobody has ever told the man who got it that anybody else had a look at that thing. The other two men on that verge that evening were gone before the one who was watching arrived and neither of them was asked what he was there for and neither of them would have told him. One of them heard about it on Thursday at a counter off that parade and did not go and look until he happened to be walking back that way, and the other was told by a merchant in the town eight miles off who could not use it. Those two are the only two people alive who were in it, and the man who walked away from that verge has never said one word about it and the man who got it has never known there was anything to say.

**HE WENT DOWN THAT STREET AND SHUT THE BACK OF HIS VAN AND FOUND HE WAS WHISTLING, AND HE HAS NEVER SAID ONE WORD ABOUT IT TO ANYBODY, AND NEITHER MAN KNEW.**
