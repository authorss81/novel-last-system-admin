# Chapter 0874 — A Post Wider Than Its Stile

Friday the fifth of January 2024, at the gate of number nine from the time the dew has come off the stone until the street has gone quiet. How wide that post is, and what the gate it carries is doing.

**THERE IS A GATE POST AT NUMBER NINE CARRYING A GATE THAT HANGS OUT OVER THE ROAD AND SHE MEANS TO PUT BOTH ARMS ROUND THAT POST AS FAR AS THEY GO AND THEN TAKE THEM IN A HAND'S WIDTH ON EACH SIDE, AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING COMES TO THE FRONT DOOR AND STANDS ON THE STEP WITHOUT COMING IN.**

The gate has stood open on its stay since the autumn and it does not lie square, and nobody in that house has called anybody about it.

It is not a latch that has gone. The stay is in its place and the foot of the stay is in the top hole in the post and there is a wedge in beside it, and the gate hangs from its hinges and rests on the stay and stands where it stands. If you push it, it goes towards the post and stops about four inches short of it and will not go any further.

That is the fault, and it has been the fault for years, and the question is which of three things is causing it.

Three things can put a gate four inches off its post and the three of them are not the same fault and only one of them is under the gate.

The hinges can be wrong. The post can be out of true. The leaf can be the wrong leaf.

The hinges are the easy one and they are not it. The straps are where they have always been and the top strap has drawn up into the wood about a sixteenth, which is what a strap does, and the pin in it is loose, and a loose pin at the top lets the top corner of a gate swing out about half an inch and no more than half an inch, and four inches is not a hinge.

The leaf could be the wrong leaf. It could have come from a gate twice the width and been cut down. But the wood on it is the same age as the frame and the frame is the same as the wood on the post, and a gate leaf with a new rail on it would have a rail on it.

So it is the post.

There is a fourth thing people do not think of, which is that the gate is not hung on the post at all but on a wall beside it. Some gates hang off the face of a pier and the post is only there to carry the stay, and a gate hung off a pier face at the wrong height stands out over the road for ever with nothing at all wrong with any part of it.

This one is hung on the post. She has put a hand behind each of the two straps and found the strap going over the top of the post and down the far side with the pin in a hole in the metal, which is how it has hung since it went up, and there is no wall and no pier within four feet of that hinge.

She gets her arms round it at the height of the top hinge first, because that is the height the gate actually hangs at.

Both arms go round and overlap by about a hand and that is the width of the post at the hinge: a foot.

Then she goes down to the foot of it and puts her arms round there and they do not overlap at all, and she takes them in a hand's width on each side to get them to meet, which is the whole technique and there is nothing clever in it.

A foot four at the ground. A foot under the hinge.

Four inches, and all of it at the bottom.

That is not a post that has rotted and been cut back, because a rotted post loses material and you can see where the loss is and it goes soft and dark. This is the opposite. The wood is sound the whole way up and there is no decay in it anywhere she can reach, and the foot of it stands further out into the road than the part above it by four inches.

Which means something has been packed round the foot of that post, and it has been packed for long enough to become the post.

There is stone in it. She can get a thumbnail into the joint on the road side of the post at about eight inches and there is a wedge of stone in there driven hard and dovetailed, and mortar-coloured stuff gone hard as a stone around it. Not mortar she has ever seen laid, and not laid this year. It is what has been pushed in and pointed over with a lump of the old stuff by whoever dug that post out in the winter before last, and who dug a post out in the frost and packed it back the same afternoon with stone off the front path and old stuff out of a bucket is a person who was in a hurry and did not want to be out in the dark twice.

And that is the whole of the fault.

A gate post is meant to stand straight. If a post leans, the gate on it leans, and the latch end of the gate swings out away from the post it should close against, and the gate will not shut. If the foot of the post is packed out four inches further into the road than the part under the hinge, then the post is leaning that four inches over the height of a man, and the gate is hanging off the top of it, and the latch end of the gate stands four inches out from the post in all weathers for ever.

It also explains the three holes in it, which have been there longer than the packing.

The top one takes the foot of the stay. The middle one is empty and rusted round the inside, and it is empty because the stay has been moved up a hole since the gate went up, which is what a stay does when a gate is not closing square on the post and somebody gets tired of levering it. The lowest one is opened out into a slot with rounded ends and a lip standing proud on one side of it, and no wedge goes into that hole any more and it is not going to be filled.

All three of those are a record of a gate that has never once been shut square, and they are all older than the stone round the foot of the post, and that is the order of the two faults in this yard: for years the gate was hung badly, and then in one afternoon in the frost somebody dug the post out to do something else and packed it four inches out of true to get it back in, and made the first fault into a fault that cannot be got round.

---

There is no wear on the post anywhere above the packed stone.

That is the last thing she looks at and it is the thing that settles it. Above about ten inches the post is the colour it was painted and the paint is unbroken round the whole of it, and the face of it on the road side has nothing on it at all, because the gate has never run against it, because the gate stands four inches clear of it and always has.

There is no wear on the road side of the leaf either, except a band across the bottom rail about a foot wide at the outer corner, which is the stay taking the weight of the gate in that one spot for two winters and doing exactly what it is there to do.

The only wear in that gate is on the underside of the stay foot and on the wedges, and both of those are doing a job and neither of them is a fault.

The woman who does her shopping comes to the front door about four and knocks and stands on the step and does not come in.

"That'll be the last of the light."

"It'll be the last of it."

"Ours went in October."

"Ours went in the September."

She stands there a little longer with her bag at her feet and then she goes down the path and the door shuts.

She stands on the step with her bag at her feet and the gate is twenty feet away at the front of the yard behind her and she does not turn round to look at it, and the road is dark and getting darker.

---

And nothing is going to be done about that gate in this or any weather.

A post a hand wider at its foot than it is under the hinge, and that is the whole of why that leaf has stood out over the road since the winter before last.
