# Chapter 0420 — The Same Hour

Saturday the sixteenth of June 2018, beginning at ten in the morning and ending twenty-five minutes later, a shop off that parade, and a counter at the parade end of it, in Tideglass.

**EACH OF TWO MEN LEARNED ONE TRUE THING ABOUT THE OTHER IN THE SAME HOUR, FROM TWO PEOPLE WHO WERE NEITHER OF THE OTHER MAN, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS PUT EITHER THING TO ANYBODY AND NEITHER OF THEM IS GOING TO.**

It is her own shop, back from that parade and round a corner, and she locks it herself. The counter is hers from ten. A gate at the back opens on a yard.

The man behind a counter at the parade end of that parade sells pipe and fixings and has been in that building since 1988 and owns nothing in this borough.

---

The man in his forties went into that shop off the parade at about ten past nine on the Saturday with a list written on the back of his hand.

He has bought things from that counter for six years. He buys the same few things, and the man behind it knows what he is coming for and does not ask, and there is about four minutes of conversation in the average visit and most of that is the weather at one end and something else at the other. He had gone down to the yard at the back of her shop for a length of conduit for a job on the Monday, and he had put his own offcut in that yard in the March, and it was not where he put it, and that is the whole of that.

He was in that shop for about eight minutes. He said, "Have you got my offcut."

And the man behind the counter said, "Which offcut."

"The three-quarter one. Green."

"You didn't have a green one. You had a white one and you had that on your own van."

"I had a green one off the roll in the yard."

"You did not," said the man behind the counter, and went to do something else.

That is the whole of that exchange, and it was the ordinary way about eight minutes in, and the man behind that counter said one other thing. He said it to the back of the man in his forties while he was at the till, in the ordinary way a man says a thing that has been sitting on him for a fortnight.

"He came in for a set."

"Who did."

"The younger one. Off the parade. He has been in three times and he bought a set."

"He always hires them."

"He has never hired a thing off me," said the man behind the counter. "Not one, not in six years. He has been in here four times a month since January and he has never hired anything. He came in on the Tuesday and he bought a set."

He said no more than that, he said it to a man's back, and he has not mentioned it since. The man in his forties stood at that till, did not turn round, and said nothing.

A man who hires every tool he puts his hand on is a man doing one thing for a while. A man who buys a set in the June has stopped saying no to himself about something. That is arithmetic and it is not a fact about a man, and the man in his forties did it while he waited for his change, and thought nothing more about it until the middle of July, when it came back to him for about ten seconds and then went away again.

He knows nothing else about the man in his thirties. He does not know where the money is coming from. He did not ask and he did not go and see and he is not going to.

---

The woman who works in a shop opened that shop at ten on the Saturday.

She has the counter to herself from ten, and the door is locked behind her for the first hour, because a shop that shuts itself has one hour in a week when nobody comes in, and that is the hour she puts her own stock back and does her own book.

A little after half past ten the man in his thirties came in for a length of quadrant and a coil of wire, which he buys from her and not from the pipe merchant, because she is three minutes' walk from his own front door and he is not.

He was at the middle of that counter and she was at the near end and there was nothing on that counter that either of them could not have got for themselves.

He said, "Is your man in."

"My man."

"The forties one. He is about nine foot in front of that counter most weeks and he has picked a length up about four times since February and he has never once asked whether we are all right."

"He is not mine," she said. "He is a customer and he is not going to hurt anybody."

"He is not that," the man in his thirties said. "I only asked."

"He is not short of hands," she said. "He has got a yard past the junction with three men in it and there is more coming, and not one of those three is on anything anybody here would recognise, and it is not a firm's name over a door and it never has been. They are three men who work in a yard and he is the one who pays them, and every job that goes out of it goes out with his own name on it and nobody else's name anywhere."

"That is a good way," he said.

"It is the only way he knows," she said.

And he said, "Right," and paid for the quadrant and the coil, and she put them in a bag, and he carried them out to his own van, which is standing in that yard at the back with that gate open.

That was the whole of what he heard about the other man that morning, and every word of it was true. It went in about eight seconds over a counter, and she has not mentioned it since, and she did not know she was telling him anything at all.

She has told him what that yard is before, in the March, in the ordinary way, because it is the sort of thing she knows and he is the sort of person who asks. She had no idea he was in that shop at ten past ten, and she had no idea the man in his forties had been in a shop about two hundred yard away at ten past nine, and neither of those men knew anything whatever about the other one at that point in the morning, and it did not occur to either of them to look.

At about five to that hour she wanted a length of board down.

There is a rack along the back of that shop above about seven foot with about thirty lengths on it. The ones at the back are the ones she cannot get at without the stool she is too short of, and there are three lengths of six by three at the back of that rack with nothing on the end of them and nothing holding them, and they are the three that everybody asks for.

She got the stool out and was on the second step of it with her weight on the front of the counter and her hands round the near end of the top one, and it came out about eight inches and stopped, because a board of that length coming out of a rack like that is about a hundred and twenty pound swinging on one end, and everybody in this trade knows that and no trade does it in front of a customer.

She got a foot off the stool and said, "Somebody hold that end."

The man in his thirties was nearest the door and he took the end in both hands.

At the other end of that counter the man in his forties had his list in his hand and was about to leave, and he neither stood still nor said anything, and he came about four foot along that counter and took the other end, one hand under it and one on the front edge, and they had it off the rack and up and over and onto the rack against the far wall in about four seconds.

The bottom end went about two foot past her on the way round and she had to step back for it. Neither of them touched anything else. Neither of them said one word.

He put it down square with the other twenty-nine, and the man in his thirties let go and went out and said, "Right," to the back of the shop, and she said nothing at all and went back to her book.

Nothing else passed between those two men that day, and nothing will pass between them, and neither has put a word of the other's business to a living soul, and there is nobody in this borough they could both be asked about.

The man in his forties went out at about five to that hour with a length of conduit under his arm and got into his own van in that yard. The man in his thirties came out of her door at about ten past it with a bag and went across that parade on foot to a job at the top of a street. One of them turned up the parade and the other one went across it, and they were about a hundred yard apart on that pavement for about a quarter of a minute, and neither of them looked over.

**HE PUT A LENGTH OF BOARD DOWN SQUARE WITH TWENTY-NINE OTHERS AND SAID RIGHT TO THE BACK OF A SHOP, AND THERE ARE TWO MEN IN THIS BOROUGH WHO KNOW ONE TRUE THING ABOUT EACH OTHER AND NEITHER OF THEM KNOWS WHICH MAN TOLD THE OTHER.**
