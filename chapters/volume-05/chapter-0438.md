# Chapter 0438 — The Half-Landing

Tuesday the fourth of September 2018, from about eight in the morning until about half past six that evening, a common stair in a building off Marsh Lane, in Tideglass.

**A WOMAN WHO CLEANS IN THAT BUILDING PUT BULBS IN ON FOUR MORNINGS IN SEPTEMBER AND A MAN WHO LIVES ON THE TOP FLOOR OF IT CAME IN EARLY ON THE FIRST ONE AND STOOD ON A LANDING THAT HAD BEEN DARK SINCE JUNE, AND NEITHER OF THEM EVER PUT IT TO THE OTHER AND HE NEVER FOUND OUT.**

She has been in that building at about eight in the morning four mornings a week for two and a half years. She does four buildings and that one is the second of the four, and she has been in and out of its door several hundred times, and there is no morning she cannot tell what is on that stair.

The half-landing between the second and the third floor has had no light in it since the first week of June. The fitting is up in the ceiling and it is a pendant with a shade on it, and the bulb went at some point in May, and nobody reported it, because there is nowhere in that building to report it to. The landlord is a company in the next town out and there is a number on a letter in a box on the ground floor and nobody has ever rung it.

She saw the woman in the ground-floor flat about three weeks before that, in the yard, coming off the back of a van with a hand trolley, and she said that the light on the half-landing was out. The woman said she had meant to say something about it and had not got round to it.

The first time she said it, the woman in the ground floor did nothing about it.

The second time, three weeks later, the woman said that she had been given the same answer twice already, which was that the man on the top floor had a company for it and had been away, and that she was not going to keep going up there.

So she went to the shop on the corner on the Friday and bought four bulbs and paid for them herself and put them in her bag in her own cubby, which is under the stairs in the yard and is hers, and which nobody who owns that building has ever once opened.

She kept the second bulb in her apron for a week and a half before she used it, and there is no reason for that, and when she thinks about it she thinks she was waiting to see whether somebody else would do the one on the half-landing.

---

The man in his forties is on the top floor of that block and has been there since the June before last and works days, and he is in about half past six most evenings.

He and she had spoken once, in June, in a doorway about fifty yard from there, for about two minutes. She had been coming out of a shop with a bag and he had been standing with a carton and a roll of edge trim balanced on his forearm. She had asked whether the shop took returns on a Saturday. He said it did. She said the one she had wanted was the wrong width. He said that was most of them. She laughed and he did not, and that was the conversation.

He does not know where she works and he does not know her name. He has thought about that doorway twice since and both times about the carton.

On the fourth of September he came in at about half past six and went up that stair in the dark as he had done every evening since June, and got to the half-landing and put his hand out for the wall, and there was light.

It is a warm bulb of the sort you buy four to a bag, and the shade is a plastic bowl about a foot across, and it is not bright, but it is enough to see a hand in.

He stood on that half-landing for about a minute. He went up the rest of it and unlocked his door and put his bag down and stood in the passage of his own flat and listened to the stair.

Then he came back down to the half-landing and stood there again and thought about nothing in particular, and went up.

---

She did three more things in that building in the fortnight after, and none of them was the light.

The week after, she took a bag of rubbish down off the second-floor landing where somebody had put it out to be collected and it had been rained on, and put it in the bin store, and the man on the top floor heard her and knew who it was and did not come down.

The week after that, she got the back door of the bin store up off its bottom stop with a folded piece of newspaper, because it had been catching for months and it is the sort of thing that takes a minute, and in the same week she changed the bulb in the passage that goes down to the cellar, which had been out longer than the half-landing had.

He saw her twice in the building.

Once she came out of that cellar door at about ten past eight with a bulb in her hand and he was on the half-landing coming down with his own bin bag, and neither of them said anything, and she went out through the front and he went down the last three steps and along the passage to the yard.

The second time was the last of those mornings and she was on the second landing with a cloth over her shoulder and he was going up. She said, "Sorry," to get past him. He said, "Go on."

That is the whole of what has ever been said between them.

It is still done. All four of those bulbs are still in. She does not clean that building on the two days after that, so the half-landing is dark on those mornings, and the man on the top floor is in on a Thursday about twice a month and has never mentioned it to anybody in that building or anywhere else.

The woman in the ground-floor flat heard about it in the yard in the first week of October, from the woman who cleans, and said that was nice of her. The woman who cleans said it was nothing, and went on.

He has not found out. He knows a woman with a cloth over her shoulder put two bulbs in and a bin store door up off its stop in a building he lives in, and he has not asked her and he is not going to. There is no version of the next six months in which he starts that, because the two of them are never on that landing on purpose and he is not going to arrange to be.

**A HALF-LANDING OFF MARSH LANE IS LIT FOUR MORNINGS A WEEK AND DARK ON THE OTHER TWO, AND THE PERSON WHO PUT THE BULBS IN IS THE ONLY PERSON WHO KNOWS.**
