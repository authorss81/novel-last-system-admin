# Chapter 0326 — A Firm in Another Town

Saturday the third of June 2017, from about half past ten in the morning until about half past one in the afternoon, a hall on Carrow Lane and the back of a shop off a parade, in Tideglass.

**A MAN IN HIS SIXTIES SAID IN A ROOM ON A METER THAT A FIRM IN A TOWN NINE MILES OFF HAS GONE OVER, AND A WOMAN WHO WORKS IN A SHOP TOOK IT OUT OF THAT ROOM IN HER OWN WORDS, AND A MAN WHO WORKS NIGHTS ASKED HER WHICH FIRM IT WAS IN A DIFFERENT ROOM, AND SHE WAS NOT ANSWERED.**

The hall on Carrow Lane runs off a front room and you pay a meter for the light in it, and there is an urn against the far wall that a man who works there looks after, and a case at the end that is shut with a padlock when the room goes.

At about half past ten on the Saturday morning the urn was on and four of them were in that room. The man in his sixties had taken the corner chair, the one with the split in the seat that he has had for years. The man in his forties was sat nearest the door with a cup in front of him and his forearms laid out either side of it. The man in his thirties had come in late and had put his bag down on the floor instead of hanging it up. The man who does that hall had been in since ten and was still on his feet with a cloth over his shoulder.

"I had a man at the works on Wednesday," said the man in his sixties.

He put his cup down about two inches from where it had been, and everybody in that room let him get to it, which is a thing that room does.

"He says there's a firm gone over. Down the estuary. Does the outsides of houses, does roofs, does that sort of thing, and he says you cannot get anybody now."

"You cannot get anybody," said the man in his forties.

"You cannot get anybody."

"Right," said the man in his forties. "Well. That's a thing a man at the works says on a Wednesday, and I'll not say he made it up, and I'll not say he didn't. But it came into this room at second hand off a man who was told it, and it is not one of the four on that table and nobody is putting it beside those four, and nobody's going to count it, and I'm not going to hear anybody at this table say the number has gone up."

"It's not a fifth," said the man who does that hall, from the far end.

"It isn't anything," said the man in his forties. "It's a sentence about a firm in a place none of us has been in. I've been in that room twice in twenty years and it's got a car park."

"It's got an industrial estate," said the man in his sixties.

"Then it's got an industrial estate," said the man in his forties. "And when a man has heard a thing twice he has not got a thing that is better. He's got the same thing with the other thing's coat on. A thing you've heard before is not a thing that's been checked, and it isn't a smaller version of a thing that's been checked, and it isn't a second of the same. It's the same size as it was and it's no heavier."

He said all of that in a voice not much above the kettle, and the man in his thirties said that was fair, and the man in his sixties said he was not arguing with him, he had only said what a man at the works told him, and the man in his forties said he knew that and that nobody was accusing anybody of anything.

And the man in his sixties said, "I haven't got the name of it. He hadn't either. He said the name of it was on a van and the van had gone."

---

The room at the back of that shop has a kettle, a fold-down table, a rack of delivery notes and a cold room with a door on it, and it is reached through the shop, and the shop on a Saturday has a man on the front till until one and a woman on the back counter and the shutter up until half past five.

The woman who works in a shop was on the back counter that morning because the woman who holds that counter on a week of days was in at a hospital appointment, and the Saturday one was somewhere else, and the man who works nights was in the back with his coat off and his cup on the fold-down table, and he is in that room most mornings before noon because he goes in at two.

The sentence got to that room at about twenty to twelve, and it got there in the woman's own words and not in anybody else's, because she was in the hall on Carrow Lane at half past ten and heard the whole of it.

She said it to the man who works nights while she was putting a cup down.

"There's a firm gone over. Down the estuary. The outside of houses, that sort of thing. A man at the works told the man in his sixties and he told me."

"Who told you the man at the works told him."

"It came through him," said the woman who works in a shop. "That's how it came."

The man who works nights drank his tea.

"Which firm," he said.

---

There was a knock at the back of that shop at about five to twelve and the man who works nights got up and let a man in with a fridge on a hand truck.

It was an under-counter fridge with a compressor on the back of it and it was in its box, and the man with the hand truck had come up the alley with it on the tray and got it as far as the back door, and it would not go through.

The back door is eight hundred wide and the fridge is a shade over that, and the compressor is the part that will not go first, and that man had it square in the doorway with the tray tipped and it was not going any further, and he said so.

"There is a bar on the outside of that door," said the man who works nights.

There was. It is a steel bar on a plate that hooks over a staple on the frame, and it goes across the outside of that door so the door cannot be opened from a yard or an alley without somebody in the shop knowing, and it is put on at half past five and taken off at about half past seven in the morning.

"Take it off," said the man who works nights.

"He'll want to know why," said the woman who works in a shop.

"He'll want to know why when it's off, not when it's on. Is it off?"

It is not off at midday, and it is not on either, because it was on the Friday night and nobody came in on the Saturday to take it off.

The man with the hand truck looked at the bar for a while.

"I can't be doing that," he said. "I'll take it in, I'm not stood in a doorway holding a thing that size on my own, and I'll not be the one that has a fridge on a threshold with a back door open on an alley. Take the bar off and I'll take it in."

"I'll not take it off," said the woman who works in a shop. "There was a woman in that terrace behind this yard in March that had her back door off its hinges twice in a fortnight, and that bar is on because somebody decided it would be on."

"And if the bar's on, it stays on," said the man with the hand truck, "because the bar's the only part of this I can be stood behind, and I'm not having that conversation with a man with a fridge and a bar in his doorway at midday on a Saturday."

Nobody said anything for about a minute.

Then the man who works nights lifted that bar off its staple, and the woman who works in a shop watched him do it, and the man with the hand truck took the fridge through in one go on the tray with the compressor last, and they had it against the far wall of the cold room and the door shut.

And then the three of them stood in that corridor and worked out that the fridge is on the swing of that door and the door is a hundred and ninety on the hinge and the fridge is two foot further in than the swing allows, and there is no way to have both.

They tried it twice. On the second try the door came back on the front of the box with about four inch to spare and stopped.

"That bar is off now," said the woman who works in a shop.

"That bar is off now," said the man who works nights.

"It's off till somebody puts it on, and nobody is going to put it on because the fridge is in the way of it, and the key for that bar is in an office in a town nine miles off that shut in the autumn." She looked at the man with the hand truck. "You will not be able to tell anybody why, and I am not going to be able to tell anybody why, because if I say it is a fridge I will be asked what is behind the fridge."

"How long does it take you to move a fridge," said the man with the hand truck.

"To move that fridge, about an hour, and I can't do it because I can't get my hands round it in there and there is nobody else in that shop who can."

The man with the hand truck said he was sorry about that and went, and nobody tried to make him stay, and he did not say he would come back and he did not leave a card.

The man who works nights was on the fold-down table again at about twenty past twelve and he came back to it on his own.

"Which firm," he said. "You said a firm."

"I know what I said."

"You said a firm and it's gone over. I asked you which one about an hour ago and you haven't answered me, and I'm not saying that to catch you out, because you were in the middle of a door at the time and I've been in the middle of a door at the time myself this morning."

"I have not got it," said the woman who works in a shop. "There was no name. That's the thing about it. There was a man at a works, and a man in a hall, and a firm in a town we haven't been in, and nothing else. There's no name on it anywhere, and if I go back to that room on Monday and ask that man what the firm is called, I am a woman in that room asking a man of that age where he heard it from, and either he tells me a firm in a car park or he tells me that I should not have asked, and either way that sentence stops here and does not go anywhere else."

"Then I can't do anything with it," said the man who works nights.

"You can't do anything with it," said the woman who works in a shop. "Neither can I. And nobody's told anybody the number's gone up, and nobody's got a piece of paper, and the bar across that back door is off and stays off, and there are three things in this borough this morning that I cannot put next to each other, and I am not going to try."

The man who works nights said that was about right, and drank his tea, and said one more thing, which was that if anybody came in that shop and said a firm had gone over, he would want to know the name before he gave it a quarter of an hour of his morning.

And at about half past one the woman who works in a shop shut the front and the man who works nights went out into the street, and the bar across the back of that shop stayed off the frame, and there is a fridge in a cold room in a position it cannot be got out of without a second pair of hands, and nobody in that shop has said a word about it to anybody in that parade.

**THE MAN WHO WORKS NIGHTS ASKED A WOMAN AT HALF PAST ELEVEN WHICH FIRM HAD GONE OVER AND GOT NOTHING, AND A BAR THAT WAS ON A BACK DOOR OFF A PARADE IS STILL OFF THE FRAME, AND NINE MILES OFF, IN A TOWN WITH A CAR PARK IN IT, A FIRM THAT DOES THE OUTSIDE OF HOUSES IS OR WAS SOMETHING THAT NOBODY IN THIS BOROUGH CAN NAME.**
