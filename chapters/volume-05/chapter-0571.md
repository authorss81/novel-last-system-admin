# Chapter 0571 — Every Monday Since 2012

The Saturday of the thirteenth of June 2020, and the Mondays before it, in one building in this borough and in two flats.

**HE HAS CARRIED SOMETHING UP TO HER DOOR EVERY MONDAY MORNING SINCE 2012 AND ON THE THIRTEENTH OF APRIL HE CARRIED IT UP FOR THE LAST TIME AND LEFT IT ON THE FLOOR OUTSIDE HER DOOR AND WENT BACK DOWN, AND SHE LET HIM GET TO THE BOTTOM OF THE RUN BEFORE SHE OPENED HERS AND TOOK IT IN AND HAS NOT PUT A PIECE OF IT ON THAT THING SINCE.**

He is on the ground floor and she is on the second, in a building of three storeys with a way through the bottom of it and about nine flats in the whole thing. He is a man in his early forties. She is a woman in her early sixties and has been in that flat since before he came to live in the building.

On Mondays he takes a bundle of small logs up to her floor in two carries, because the run at the end of that way is narrow and he will not manage it in one.

He has done that since 2012. He does not knock until he is up there. He knocks and she opens the door and he puts the bundle inside against the side by the television where she has always kept it, and she stands there while he does it, and he says the day or nothing, and he goes back down.

There is no list. Neither of them has ever written the day down and neither of them has ever needed to, because it has been the same Monday for eight years and he has never once been late and she has never once been out.

What is at the end of it is that there is more wood in that flat than the thing it goes in can burn in a year, and that has been true for about two years, and neither of them has ever said so.

---

On the Monday of the thirteenth of April he took it up as usual.

He got as far as her door and he did not knock. He put the bundle down on the floor against the door, and he went back down, and he went to work.

He has not gone up that building again. She is quite certain about that and has never gone to find out, because finding out would mean asking, and she is not going to ask.

She was in the flat. She heard him on the run and she heard him stop, because he always stops, and she waited for the knock for what she thought was about four seconds and then heard him go back down.

She waited until she heard the front door of the building. Then she opened hers and got the bundle in and shut it again.

It is still there.

It is against the side by the television in the same place, and it has not been moved, and it has not been broken down into anything smaller, and she has not put a single piece of it on that thing since the thirteenth of April.

She has plenty of wood. There was some in the press at the back before he ever began, and she has burnt all of that, and she has not needed what he brought, and she has not said so.

On the Monday after that she was at home and the run made the noise it makes and nothing came up it.

On the Mondays after that, same.

She sees him at the front door on the ground floor about twice a week and he sees her and they say the day and they say whether it has been raining and they have never once stood there and said anything else, and on none of those days has either of them looked at the other one for longer than the sentence takes.

---

What she is carrying is not the wood and it is not the eight years. It is about four seconds on the Monday of the thirteenth of April, when he did not knock, and the length of the wait before she understood that there was not going to be a knock.

She has not asked him. He has not said. Between the two of them there has not been one word about it, and there is nobody else in that building either of them would say it to.
