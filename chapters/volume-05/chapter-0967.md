# Chapter 0967 — Behind The Bench Leg

Undated in this draft.

**THE BENCH AGAINST THE STORE WALL HAS A SHELF FRAME WITH A NARROW VOID BEHIND THE REAR LEG.**

The bench at the unit stands against the store wall under the covered way, built of softwood legs and a top of two planks with a shelf frame halfway down.

The bench is about five feet long and two feet deep, and it stands on four square legs with stretchers between them on three sides. The rear legs stand clear of the wall by about three inches, because the wall behind bellies out at the height of the shelf and the bench was set to clear it.

Between the rear stretcher and the wall there is a narrow void, shut above by the bench top and below by the shelf frame, and shut at either end by the rear legs. It travels the bench extent, sunless at noon, dust banking where shelf kisses wall.

The wall behind is corrugated sheet on battens, and one batten runs along just behind the bench at the height of the shelf. The batten is rough-sawn and full of knots, and one knot has fallen out to leave a round hole through which the dark of the cavity behind can be seen.

The bench top is worn smooth along its front edge where hands have taken it through years, and the shelf below carries a folded sack, a bundle of twine, and three short offcuts of batten. None of these touches the wall, and each stands clear of the void by a finger width.

This bench timber numbs the back of the hand slowly, softwood-cold and unhurried. The strap tying leg to stretcher shocks cold at once, thin iron answering skin. The corrugated sheet behind draws most heat of all where a fingertip meets it through the knot hole.

The legs were cut from one length and still show the same grain running through them, and each carries two pegs where the stretcher meets it. The pegs stand proud by a hair and have never been cut flush, which is the mark of a bench put together in haste for a job and then kept.

The stretchers run between the legs with a housed joint at either end, and the housing on the rear stretcher is shallow on the left and deep on the right, so the stretcher sits a little out of true. The shelf boards rest on the stretchers without fixing and can be lifted singly, though none has been lifted for a long time, and dust lies thick along their ends.

Softwood seasons where it stands, and a bench under cover seasons slowly because the air moves little. The top planks have shrunk across their width by about an eighth along their length, opening a narrow slit between them that runs from one end to the other. Through that slit the dark of the void below can be seen in a thin line, and crumbs of dust fall through it when the bench is knocked.

Shelf sack lies flat-folded, cord cinched till fibres dent the crease where hauled. The offcuts beside it are of different lengths and show different saws, one rough and one fine, and each carries a pencil mark near one end. The marks are short lines and nothing more, made to show where a cut was meant to fall.

The wall sheet behind the bench carries bolts in every third trough, and one bolt near the middle stands proud where the batten behind has shrunk. The head of that bolt is bright on one side where it has been rubbed, and dull elsewhere with rust. Rain that gets under the eaves runs down the sheet behind the bench and dries there without reaching the shelf.

Dust gathers behind a bench in the way it gathers behind anything seldom moved. It lies deepest where the shelf meets the wall, in a soft bank that rises toward the corner, and thinner toward the front where the air moves. A dead beetle lies in that bank near the left leg, legs folded, and a wood chip beside it shows where the shelf was trimmed to fit.
Fingers pass back over the rear stretcher into the void behind, with the palm down on the shelf boards and the tips reaching toward the wall.

Planar entry suits shoal darkness; dorsal faces deciphered tactually, not bathymetrically. Progress drifts leg to leg, pausing at ledge timber and slipping the knot aperture.

The opening answer is the distance backward. At either leg the fingers reach the sheet after about three inches, and in the middle they reach the batten sooner, after about two, because the wall bellies there. The batten itself is rough and ridged, and the knot hole in it is smooth round its rim where the knot shrank before it fell.

The following answer is what leans on the wall within. Along the greater run dust alone lies, soft and deep enough to bury fingertip ends. Near the middle there is a folded scrap of paper wedged between the batten and the sheet, thin with age and soft at its folds. Downward touch leaves it dead; lateral nudge walks it sideways till it halts at the next knot.

The paper rests parched, featherweight, lending heat to nobody. The batten around it is dry and draws little. The sheet beyond draws most where the finger finds it through the hole. Under the left foot the flagstone aches cold upward, deep quarry manner.

The fingers come out from over the stretcher with dust on their tips and nothing else on them. The void persists exactly, the paper scrap resting where touch abandoned it, one finger-breadth sideways from its start.

Shelf planks overhead ignore the palm, spanning stretcher to stretcher under trifling freight. The bench carcass never budges wallward, feet sunk into flag cups that grip them.

Covered-way rainfall descends past the bench, never upon; hence pallid woodwork and merely edge-rusted iron. The flags beneath the feet were laid on sand that has washed out at one corner, and the foot there sits deeper by half an inch. The sand that remains is pale and loose, and the soil below shows through where it has gone. A broom passes the bench each week and leaves the corners alone, so the bank of dust behind grows deeper through the year while the front stays clean. Under cover dew never ventures; void powder keeps perennial aridity.


The crossbeam above the covered way spans wall to post in a single oak length, joined at either end with a tusked joint pegged twice. The pegs stand proud and burnished where sleeves brushed them passing. The beam itself sags by half an inch at its middle under the roof load, a curve visible only when sighted along its lower arris. Pigeons favour that beam for its warmth, and their claws have scored the upper face in thin lines.

The flags beneath the bench carry a greenish film near the wall where drips fall, dark and slippery after rain, paling to grey in drought. The film maps the drip line exactly, widening where two drips merge. A beetle patrols that film at night, leaving a threadlike trail that glistens till dawn. That trail evaporates by first light, re-laid nightly on the same circuit.
---

The neighbour two doors along waits at the covered-way mouth yardward, halting shy of the storefront under the roof, palms vacant.

Fingertips have withdrawn from the dark behind the stretcher and lie idle on the shelf edge while the caller pauses at the mouth of the way.

"There was a dog loose down the lane this morning."

"It was back with its owner by midday."

"They are raising money for the hall roof again."

"They will be raising it till spring."

The rest concerns the lane and the hall fund and nothing that stands on the timber nearby. The caller keeps the way-mouth, advancing not a pace wallward. The bench abides, gloom crouching aft of its leg. The covered way above is dry and the roof sheet above sheds rainfall toward the channel without spilling. A pigeon has roosted on the crossbeam for two winters, and the droppings beneath mark where it sits.

The bench frame carries its shelf and its dust and asks nothing of the wall behind it, since the wall stood there first and will stand there after.
