# Chapter 0722 — The Only Person In The Building

Tuesday the third of May 2022, in this borough, and not in this borough, because the house is off the ring road. He is on the first-floor landing of it from first light until dark with the doors off their frames, and the whole of it is four doors and a telephone on a landing wall and one call he could not leave alone.

**A MAN IN HIS LATE TWENTIES TAKES FOUR DOORS OFF THEIR FRAMES ON THE LANDING OF A HOUSE OFF THE RING ROAD AND IS THE ONLY PERSON IN THE BUILDING, AND PICKS UP A TELEPHONE ON THE LANDING WALL WHEN A BELL GOES IN A ROOM AT THE BACK, AND TELLS A STRANGER THERE IS NOBODY HERE BUT HIM.**

He is a man in his late twenties. He is in the house all day. A woman who booked him by telephone is coming back to pay him. He speaks to her at the door, and to a woman he has never met on a telephone in the morning.

---

He starts with the first door because the first door is the one with the lever on that has come off.

The house is a semi off the ring road with the man at work all day and the woman booked him on a Sunday from a telephone and gave him a key off the side and said the whole job was four doors off their frames and the linoleum up, and she said she would be back at the end of the day.

He is on the landing with three doors off and the fourth off by the middle of the morning and the frames bare to the stud and the lino up in the two rooms at the back, and the whole of it is stacked against the landing wall out of the way with the faces to the wall.

He does not do this in the time he told her. He did say he would and he knows he did say it, and he has been on this landing since first light.

---

At about the middle of the morning a bell goes in the room at the back.

Not on the landing. Behind him, in the second back room, out of sight round the corner, and he cannot see the telephone from where he is.

The bell goes about four times.

He stands on the landing with a screwdriver in his hand and looks at the landing wall, and there is a telephone on it at the end by the airing cupboard, cream, on a little table, and the receiver is on the cradle and the bell has stopped.

He puts the screwdriver down on the top of the door stack.

Then he picks it up again and puts it in his pocket, because he is not going to leave a screwdriver on a landing of a house with four doors off, and because he wants his hands free, which he will think about later.

---

He picks the receiver up and says "Hello," which is not a thing he planned to say.

"Hello — is that the house with the green gate?"

"Which top road?"

The woman stops. He can hear her stop.

"Oh," she says. "Sorry. I thought — that's not it, is it."

"No, this is off the ring road."

"Right. Sorry, love."

And then she does not ring off, which is the part he does not expect, and she comes back with a different question, and it is worse.

"Right," she says. "Have you got him, by the way? He's the one who did the kitchen in — he said he'd come and he's not turned up, and I've been ringing that number all morning."

---

"There is nobody here but me," he says.

There is a pause.

"You're not him."

"No."

"Right. Well, tell him when he comes, will you, that I've been ringing since — never mind. Is there anybody else I could ask."

"No."

"You've not heard of him? The man who did the kitchen?"

"No."

And she says "Right" and rings off, and the line does that click, and he is standing on a landing with a receiver in his hand in a house where somebody else's kitchen is being talked about.

---

He puts the receiver back and it does not sit square and he sets it square with his thumb.

Then he stands there and works out what he has just done, and it takes about as long as it takes him to pick up the screwdriver again.

He told the truth. All of it. There is nobody here but me. He is not the man. He has not heard of him.

And in the length of that call a woman has now got in her head the address of a house off the ring road where there is a man who is alone and who has not come, and she will telephone back, and when she telephones back there will be a man in this house and he will be the only man in it, and that is very nearly how it will be every time.

He has not done anything wrong. He has said no three times to a stranger and offered her the truth each time.

And he has arranged, without meaning to, for a man he has never met to have a standing in a house he has never been to, in a street he has never walked down, on the word of a woman ringing a number she had off somebody.

---

His own mobile goes in his pocket a little after the middle of the day and he does not get it out, because his hands are full of a door and because he has decided, somewhere between the middle of the morning and now, that this is a day when the telephone in his pocket is not going to be answered.

That is not a decision he made in words. What happened is that he picked up one in the morning that had nothing to do with this house and everything to do with a bell at the back of a building where there was nobody to hear it, and it turned out fine, and the price of it was the length of one sentence, and the price of not taking it is nothing.

So the mobile goes, and stops, and he goes back to the frames.

---

The bell in the second back room goes again in the afternoon, twice, and he does not go.

He stands on the landing with a chisel in his hand and he listens to it go and he waits for it to stop and he does not go and down there in that room there is a telephone on a table against the far wall with the bell in it and no receiver on the cradle.

That is a telephone that has come off its cradle by itself, or a bell wire that has touched something, or a dial that has stuck, and every one of those is a fault and every one of them is a thing he would look at for nothing.

So the bell goes four times on a landing he is not on and he does not go, and after that it does not go again, and there is no more from it before dark.

---

She comes at about ten to six with the money in an envelope.

"Four doors," he says. "And the lino's up in the two at the back. It's taken me most of the day and not because of the doors."

"The keys are on the side." She hands him the envelope. "There's more than we said."

"It's the time."

"You've been here all day."

"I've been here all day."

She counts it out on the landing and gives him the notes and he puts them in his pocket, and then she says:

"Somebody rang this number."

"A woman did."

"About what?"

"About a man who did her kitchen and hadn't been," he says.

"Oh, I don't know," she says. "That's not mine. I've only had this house since February."

And there is the thing standing right there in the landing with her, and it is one question, and it is about a kitchen, and she has just told him he might as well be able to say whose.

He does not ask it. He has the notes going into his pocket and he is half a turn from the stairs, and asking her would mean he had a reason to want to know, and a man with a reason to want to know is a man she will remember.

So there is a pause on that landing about the length of a step.

Then she says, "You're not going to want to bother," and goes down the stairs, and the door at the bottom goes, and the street is out there doing what a street does at that hour.

---

He is in the van at the end of the day with the doors shut and the four doors on a rack in the back with the faces in, and the radio off, and the long straight ahead of him empty.

There is a woman in a street off the ring road somewhere who now has the address of a house off the ring road, and a man in it, and the two are not the same man, and she does not know that, and he cannot tell her, because telling her would be a call, and a call is the thing that started it.

He has a job on Monday at a house on a road off the ring road, about a stair, and it is a short one.

Somebody could telephone this house at any point between now and then and get him, and he would say the same three sentences he said this morning, and each time it would be less true and more costly.

He takes the mobile out of his pocket and puts it face down on the dash above the wheel and does not put it back.

That is not for the shop. It is so that if it lights up he will see it do it. There is one number on it with no name on the other side and nothing else about any of this written anywhere on it, and it went off this afternoon while he was standing on a landing with his hands full of a door, and he did not get it out, and he is not going to, and he does not want to find out on Monday that he should have.
