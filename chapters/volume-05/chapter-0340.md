# Chapter 0340 — A Bed Base and a Table

Sunday the thirtieth of July 2017, and the Thursday of the sixth of July, a building by the water, and a shop off a parade, in Tideglass.

**A METAL BED BASE WITH A MATTRESS ROLLED AND STRAPPED TO IT WAS PUT DOWN ON THE STEP OUTSIDE A DOOR AT THE TOP OF AN OUTSIDE STAIR ON THE SIXTH OF JULY AND TURNED DOWN, AND A FOLDING TABLE WITH A TILED TOP WAS PUT DOWN INSIDE THAT SAME DOOR ON THE THIRTIETH OF JULY AND USED ONCE, AND THE MAN WHO BROUGHT BOTH HAS NOT SAID ONE WORD ABOUT EITHER OF THEM TO ANYBODY.**

That stair is an outside one on the back of a block of flats, nine treads, straight, with a rail screwed to the wall side of it, and it comes up to a landing that is not in the weather, and there are four doors off that landing and the one at the end of it belongs to the woman of about fifty-five.

He is not the man in his thirties. He has a van with his own name over the cab and no lettering on the back of it, and he emptied a house on the Cleeve road in May, and he came up that stair twice in June, and on the thirtieth of June he was turned down at that door in about a second and has not said a word about it to anybody since.

Then he came back twice in July, and the two of those are the whole of this.

---

On the Thursday of the sixth of July he came up that stair at about half past ten in the morning with a bed base and a mattress.

The frame is a metal thing about six foot long and two foot across, folded in half down the middle and strapped with two lengths of webbing, and there is a foam mattress rolled against it and strapped to the frame with a third length, and the whole thing weighs about what a man can get up nine treads with and it took him two goes with it and he had it on that landing in about four minutes.

He got it up to the top and set it against the wall of that landing outside her door and knocked twice with the back of his hand.

It filled the step from the door frame to the rail on the other side of it. The frame had come apart at one corner where it has been in a van for three days, and the frame is painted on one side and bare on the other, and the mattress was strapped to it with a length of orange webbing that has a ratchet on it and he had put the ratchet on tight enough that the webbing has marked the foam.

She opened the door with her coat on and looked at the thing on the step and did not look at him.

"No," she said.

He did not ask her why. He did not say anything at all, not one word, and there was nothing in the four or five seconds between her no and him bending down to the straps either, and he took it back down that stair in three goes and pulled the street door to behind him at the bottom and put it in the back of his van over the boards and shut the door and drove off, and he has not been up that street since the Thursday of the sixth of July except on the thirtieth, which is now.

The mattress was on that van for the whole of July and it went out with the rest of that load in the third week, and there is no way of following it and nobody in this borough has asked.

There was a thing he could have said on that step. He has carried two hundred things up steps in this borough and he knows what a person says on a step when they have brought something they did not have to bring, and he said none of it, on the sixth of July, to a woman about fifty-five in her own doorway, and that is not a thing he is proud of and it is not a thing anybody has ever heard about either.

On the Sunday morning of the thirtieth of July he came up that stair at about half past ten with a folding table.

It is about three foot by two foot with a top of off-white tile in a wooden frame and one folding leg that is stiff at the hinge, and it came out of a house on a road four streets off on the Monday of that week when a woman changed her mind about a table, and there is a mark on the underside of the frame where somebody has put a cup down on it.

He got it up the stair in one go, under his arm, and knocked twice.

She opened the door.

He told her what the table was and how big it was and where it had come from, in about four sentences, and then he said that if she did not want it he would take it back down and that would be the end of it, and he was on the point of turning round with it when she said one word.

"Inside," said the woman of about fifty-five.

He put it on the floor of that room about a foot inside the door and went out and shut the door and went down the stair and pulled the street door to behind him at the bottom.

She used it once.

She had that table standing at the foot of the bed from about half past ten until about one in the afternoon, and what she did with it was take the blanket off the foot of the bed — which is the one that has lain folded in three at the foot of that bed since the winter and is there for a draught off that landing door — and hang it over the edge of the table and beat it with a clothes brush she keeps on the sill, on the side, the way anybody does it, and there was about nine days of grey in the middle of that blanket, and the window was open and the air got under it and took the dust off in a slow line, and she stopped about four times to put a hand behind her ears.

She had not done that to that blanket since it came in that room and she had been carrying it about folded in three for seven months, and it is a bed blanket, and there is nothing about it that was ever going to be fixed, and she beat it anyway.

Then she folded the blanket in three and put it back across the foot of the bed, and she put the table on its edge against the wall under the window, about a foot along from the carpet that is standing on its side against that wall.

It is a table and not a place for anything. Nothing is on it. It is a foot across the edge and it is a foot short of the ceiling and the tiles on it have a cup mark on the frame and that is the whole of it, and nobody in that building has asked her about a table, and she has not mentioned a table to a living soul, and she is not going to arrange her life around a thing a man carried up nine treads.

---

On the thirtieth of July the man with a van stood at the counter of that shop for about four minutes in the afternoon and bought nothing at all.

"Have you got a short length of four by two," he said.

"Not today," said the woman who works in a shop. "It's Thursday they come and it's not Thursday."

"Right," said the man with a van, and he put his hand on the door and then did not open it.

"You've been up the Cleeve road."

"I've been up the Cleeve road," said the man with a van.

"Twice since the end of June, or I'll say nothing about it and you'll go."

"Twice since the end of June," said the man with a van.

"What were you doing."

"Taking two things up that stair," said the man with a van, "and I am not going to say what they were, and if I did say what they were you would go up that stair and stand outside that door, and I would have put a thing in front of you that you would have to be polite about, and neither of us wants that, and that is why I am not going to say what they were. That is the whole of it. You have got about twenty minutes before that shop shuts and you have got a shop to open."

"I do not want to go up there," said the woman who works in a shop.

"I know you don't," said the man with a van, and he opened the door and went out into the parade and down it, and he did not say one more word about the sixth of July or the thirtieth of July, and neither did she, and she stood behind that counter for about ten minutes with a till that had been full and was not.

**A TABLE STANDS ON ITS EDGE AGAINST A WALL UNDER A WINDOW IN A ROOM AT THE END OF A FIRST-FLOOR CORRIDOR WITH A CUP MARK ON THE FRAME, AND A MAN WITH A VAN WENT DOWN A PARADE TOWARD A SHOP THAT SHUTS AT HALF PAST THREE WITHOUT SAYING A SINGLE THING ABOUT IT.**
