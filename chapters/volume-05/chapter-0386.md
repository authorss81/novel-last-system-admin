# Chapter 0386 — One Question

Wednesday the twenty-fourth of January 2018, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past nine at night, a shop off a parade, in Tideglass.

**THE WOMAN WHO RUNS THE SHOP OFF THAT PARADE THAT SHUTS EARLY ASKED A MAN IN HIS FORTIES ONE QUESTION ABOUT HER OWN SHOP ON THE MORNING OF THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF JANUARY, AND HE ANSWERED IT IN ONE SENTENCE, AND WHAT HE SAID WAS TRUE, AND SHE HAS WRITTEN IT NOWHERE AND TOLD NOBODY, AND A MAN IN HIS THIRTIES WAS AT THE END OF THAT COUNTER AND HEARD IT AND HAS NOT PUT IT TO ANYBODY, AND THE MAN SHE ASKED DOES NOT KNOW THAT ANYBODY WAS CLOSE ENOUGH TO HEAR HIM.**

Her shop is on that parade about fifty yard back from the front of it, between a place that does keys and a place that is shut. It is about sixteen foot wide and it has a counter along the left-hand side when you come in and a space behind it about five foot deep, and the shutter goes up at about half past eight and comes down at about six on a weekday and at about half past three on a Saturday, which she has done for years.

There is a bell on that shop. It is a small brass hand bell about two inch across on a short coil, screwed to the head of the frame inside, and a push on the outside of the frame at about the height of a man's coat pocket, and a wire from the push through the frame and along the head of it to the bell.

It has not rung since the middle of December.

She found out on the twelfth of December. A man had come in with a bag of nails and had pushed the outside of that door with his shoulder because his hands were full, and nothing had gone off, and he had said "that's not right" and gone on paying, and she had stood there for about four seconds and then gone up on a chair and put her hand on the bell itself and hit it with the heel of her hand and nothing had happened.

The coil it hangs on has come off. The wire has come away from one terminal and is hanging about two inch below it, and it is not a long job, and she is not going to do it, and she has known for five weeks that she is not going to do it.

---

There is a number on a piece of card in the front of a box on the shelf in her own kitchen, for a man who does electrical work and does the wiring side of anything. It is on that card with three other numbers and she has had the card since 2015.

She has not rung it. She has taken the card out twice and put it back.

The reason she has not rung it is not that she does not want a man in the shop with the shutter half up. The reason is that she has had that bell off her mind for five weeks and it has come off her mind again every time, and a woman who has a job going all the time that she has not rung anybody about is a woman who would be embarrassed to start, and she would rather have a bell that does not ring than a bell that rings and a man in her shop remembering that she took five weeks to make the call.

The man in his forties came into that shop at about ten past ten on the Wednesday morning of the twenty-fourth of January with a length of conduit and about four foot of flexible in a coil, which he wanted to be cut, and she cut it while he waited.

He is in that shop most weeks. He has been going in there a long while and he buys what he needs and he does not talk, and she does not talk to him, and they have got to the point where she knows whether he wants it cut and he knows she is going to cut it.

He had put the conduit and the coil on the counter and was standing at the far side of her with his hands in his pockets until she had finished, and she put the offcuts in the tin and the tin under the counter and said the price of it, and he got his money out.

And then the woman who runs the shop that shuts early said, "Will I do anything about that bell, do you think."

He had not been looking at the bell. He turned and looked at it, which is a thing a man does when a woman in a shop asks him about a fitting over her head.

He thought about it for about three seconds.

"I have never had one of those done," he said.

That is all that either of them ever put to the other about it. She said "Right," and he put his money on the counter and she took it and gave him the change and he went out and pulled the shutter's edge to behind him, which he does not do and which she noticed.

It is true. He has never had a shop bell done. He has never had a shop. He has had a bell out of a house twice and both times it was a bell that was already off the frame and had been in a box, and the first of those was his own and the second belonged to a woman who was going into a care home and did not want it, and he took it home and it is in a garage and he does not know what happened to it.

So he answered her the only way he had, and it is the only answer there is to give if you are asked something you have no experience of, and it was true, and it gave her nothing at all.

The man in his thirties was at the other end of that counter with a box of clips under his arm, about eight foot from where she was standing.

He had come in about three minutes before the man in his forties and he had gone to the far end because there was nothing at the near end he wanted, and he was reading the labels on the boxes on the low shelf with his back half turned to the counter.

He heard both halves of it. He heard her ask and he heard the answer, and he is one of about four people in this town who know that that bell in that shop has not rung since the middle of December, and he is one of about four people in this town who has heard a man say, in answer to a woman asking him for help about her own shop, that he has never had one done.

He has not put it to anybody. He has not put it to her, which he could, because she is at that counter six days a week and he is in that shop most weeks, and he has not put it to the man in his forties, which he could, because in all the time he has been going in there they have had about four conversations and the man in his forties will answer a question put as a statement.

There is nothing to put it about. It is a man in a shop saying what he does not know, and a woman in a shop who asked him because he was the one standing there.

---

She did not ask him again.

She nearly did on the Saturday, which is the day that shop shuts at half past three and which is the day she is in it on her own for the longest. He comes in on a Saturday sometimes. He was in that shop on the Saturday after with a box of plugs and she had the card in her apron pocket and her hand went to it twice, and he was at the near end with the plugs and she was at the far end putting that shop's hours up in the window for the following week, and she did not ask him.

And she did not ask the man in his thirties either, and there are two reasons for that and one of them is that she does not know him and the other one is that a woman who asks two men in a shop whether anybody does a thing has told both of them that nobody did it, and she is not going to be a person in that shop who says that out loud twice.

On the Monday she put the piece of card in the bin. Not the whole card. She tore the number off it, which she did with her thumb down the edge of a knife, and the three other numbers went in the bin with the rest of it, and the number off it is in her apron pocket and is not in the bin and is in a pocket that has been washed once since.

The man in his forties was in that shop on the Friday of that week and on the Monday after and again a fortnight later, and she rang nothing through for him but what he came for, and he said what he came to say and nothing else, and not one of those three times did either of them come back to a bell, and neither of them knows there was a question.

He does not know that the man in his thirties heard it. He was facing a counter with a woman in it and a bell over his head and he did not turn round.

He has not raised it with anybody and does not mean to, and a version of him exists in which he brings it up over a pint with a man he does not know, and he is not that man, because a man who is asked about a thing in a shop and cannot answer has learned something about the shape of his own knowledge, and that is not a thing anybody takes to a pub.

**HE WENT OUT AND PULLED THE EDGE OF THE SHUTTER TO BEHIND HIM, WHICH HE HAS NEVER DONE IN THAT SHOP IN ALL THE TIME HE HAS BEEN GOING IN AND WHICH SHE NOTICED, AND THE PUSH OUTSIDE THAT DOOR STILL DOES NOT GO, AND SHE IS STILL BEHIND THAT COUNTER SIX DAYS A WEEK.**
