# Chapter 0762 — The Bottom Of The Rail

Monday the third of October 2022, in this borough. She is at her own lock-up and yard from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a door rail cleared off the top and a corner of the floor with all of the grit in it.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FORTIES WANTS THE GRIT OUT OF THE BOTTOM OF HER ROLLER DOOR'S RAIL, THE WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK BRUSHES IT AWAY FROM THE DOOR INTO THE CORNER, AND THE GRIT IS LEFT UNDER THE CORNER OF THE RACK.**

She is at her own lock-up on Monday with the grit packed in the bottom of the rail and the roller door coming down hard on it. The woman who books work comes through the doors with a hand brush, takes the second half of the rail, and puts the grit where it cannot be got at.

---

The van went out at seven and has not come back and does not need to. She is in the lock-up with the doors up and the morning coming in off the yard across the concrete, and the roller door has been coming down hard at the bottom since the summer and has been going up with a hitch in it for a fortnight, and the hitch is at the rail.

The rail runs the width of the opening at floor level, a bar of steel let into the concrete with a slot in the top of it the width of a thumb. It is grey with grit along its whole length, packed in the slot, standing proud of the concrete in a low ridge where the grit has been coming in with the wheels and drying there.

She has a screwdriver and a hand brush and she means to get the slot cleared from one end to the other with the screwdriver, run the brush down the whole length after it, and put a drop of oil in so that the door comes down and does not knock.

The woman who comes to book work comes through the open half of the doors at the middle of the morning.

---

"You have got a brush for that."

"I have got a brush for that and I have got a screwdriver for the slot."

"The screwdriver is for afterwards." The woman comes in with her coat already off and over the end of the rack. "You will take the slot out first and then shut the door on it and grind it into the concrete again. You brush it first and you do it twice."

"How many times."

"As many as it takes."

She stands looking at the ridge of grit along the rail with the screwdriver in her hand.

"The slot is the whole of it. That is where it sticks. If I brush the top and leave the slot the door will come down onto a floor of grit and grind it in again by Friday."

"It will come down onto a floor of grit and you will open it again on Friday and half of it will be in the concrete and half of it will be under the rack where you cannot get at it." The woman takes the brush off her. "Brush it away from the door. Into the corner. Away from the door, not into the slot."

She lets her do it, because the woman is right about the second Friday, and she stands at the bench with her arms while the door goes down and the brush goes down the rail with its bristles on the flat and the grit moving ahead of it in a grey wave, and the woman turns round at the end and comes back with the grit pushed up the concrete into the corner where the rack stands.

It goes on all the way in one movement and takes about a minute.

"That is it."

"That is the top of it." The woman stands with the brush against the concrete. "Run it back the other way now and get the last of it, because that is what is loose and that is the part that would get in under the door and into the shoes of anybody in this yard."

She comes down off the bench and takes the screwdriver and puts it in the slot at the near end and scrapes along it. The slot is clear for the first half inch and then it stops dead and the blade goes in no further, because the grit in there has been packed down into the slot and set and the point of the screwdriver meets it and rides up on top.

"Try the other end."

The woman comes to the other end and looks down the length of the rail and then at the corner where the grit is standing in a heap against the concrete up to the leg of the rack.

"It is all out of there and in here."

"It is all out of the slot and in the corner." She scrapes again and the blade rides up on the set grit and comes out with a grey smear on it. "And that is packed in, and I am not getting it out with a screwdriver, and it is going to be the same on Friday."

---

"It will not be the same on Friday."

"It is the same now." She puts the screwdriver down. "You have taken everything off the top of the rail and pushed it eight foot away into a corner where the foot of that rack stands in it, and in a week the wheels will have brought half of it back and it will be in the slot again, and this time it will be fine grit on top of grit that has set, and I will be the one with the screwdriver in my hand in the winter saying the same thing."

"That is what a corner is for." The woman puts the brush down against the rack leg. "You do not put grit back into a slot. You put it where it can go. And I will take the heap out of the corner in a bucket when the bins go, and that is the end of the grit and there is nothing else to be done with it."

"There is something else to be done with it."

"There is one thing." The woman looks at the rail and then at her. "That is not done with grit. That is done with the door. A door that comes down hard at the bottom is not a door that is wrong at the bottom. That door is coming down too fast because the motor is set too high for the weight of it, and the grit is only in it because it was in the rail before it ever started knocking. You could clear that slot every day of the week and in six weeks it will be back."

She does not argue with that, because she has been listening to that door come down for two years and knows it is faster than it was.

Then she goes and gets a dropper out of the van's side pocket and puts two drops in along the rail, first end and second end and in the middle, and the woman takes the brush and runs it once more down the top of the rail and back so that the oil goes the length of it, and the grit stays where it is, in the corner, packed against the leg of the rack in a grey heap about the size of a bucket.

She puts the roller door down on it. It comes down heavy and knocks and then the bottom edge settles onto the set grit in the slot at the near end and stops, and the door goes up on the second try with a hum and no hitch at all.

"That will last until the grit comes back."

"That will last until the grit comes back." She stands and looks at the door. "Which will be the first time the wheels come through it wet."

---

The woman goes out through the half of the doors that is open and gets into her car and goes. She stands a while in the doorway looking at the corner, and then she goes down on one knee in front of the rail at the near end and runs a finger along the slot the whole length of it.

It is smooth and open for the first half and then her finger knows the difference, the way a finger knows a nail head in a dark room, and there is grit set down into it as far as she can go.

She puts her hand flat on the rail and leaves it there, and then she stands up, and takes the dropper and the brush and the screwdriver back into the van, and shuts the side door, and stands in the lock-up with the grit heap in the corner and the box on its corner behind the bench where it has been all summer.
