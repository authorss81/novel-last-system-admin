# Chapter 0346 — The Last Man to Have It

Sunday the twentieth of August 2017, from about half past nine in the morning until about five in the afternoon, a yard at the top of a street, a shop off a parade, and a van in a car park, in Tideglass.

**A FOUR-FOOT STEP LADDER WENT INTO A YARD AT THE TOP OF A STREET ON THE FIFTH OF AUGUST AND CAME OUT OF A SHOP ON THE SIXTH AND WENT BACK INTO THAT YARD ON THE SIXTEENTH AND WENT OUT OF THE SHOP AGAIN THIS MORNING, AND TWO MEN ARE BOTH CERTAIN THEY WERE THE LAST TO HAVE IT, AND NEITHER OF THEM IS SURE, AND NEITHER OF THEM IS GOING TO ASK THE OTHER.**

There is a yard off the Cleeve road up a street that goes nowhere, and it is behind a row of workshops, and you get into it through a gate on a latch that has not been shut properly since before the summer, and there is about thirty foot by twenty of hardstanding in it and a bench against the wall and a length of wall about nine foot high with nothing fixed to it.

There is no sign on the gate and there is no lock on it and nobody here has ever been granted the use of that yard by anybody.

---

The ladder went in there on the Saturday before the sixth, at about half past four in the afternoon, and it was the man in his forties who put it there, and he put it against the wall on the north side about two foot off the ground on its edge with its feet on the bench, because a ladder stood on its own in a wet yard for a fortnight comes up with green on the feet of it.

He had it out of the back of his own van at about ten past four because his own is in the workshop and the workshop has a man in it who would have asked. He said nothing to anybody about it then and he has not said anything about it since, and there is no way of following a ladder anywhere in this borough.

On the Sunday morning, the sixth, the man in his thirties went up that street at about ten with his bag.

The gate was open. The ladder was against the wall on the north side with its feet on the bench and a spanner sitting on the top tread, and the spanner is a spanner, and it is not his and he did not know whose it was then and he does not know now.

He used it for about half an hour. He had a job that morning with a wardrobe on the second floor of a house two streets down, and the wardrobe wanted moving about six foot and everything else in that room wanted shifting first, and a wardrobe is a thing a man will not shift alone on a four-foot ladder, whatever anybody says.

He put the ladder back against the wall on the north side with the spanner on the top tread, and thought about it for the length of time it takes to shut a gate, and did not shut the gate.

Then at about half past four he was in the shop off that parade where the woman works, buying about nine inches of a four by two, and she had the back room door open and the light on in there, and he asked her a question about it in the middle of that shop.

He did not say where it was from. He said it was on a wall behind a place up the Cleeve road where anybody can walk in and that he would not leave it there over the weather, and she said all right, and he took it out to his van and put it on the boards under the blanket he keeps on the boards for that, and drove off with a four by two on the passenger seat and the ladder in the back.

She watched him do all of that from behind her counter and she said one word, which was all right, and she did not ask him a single other thing, and he did not say a single other thing, and neither of them has mentioned it since.

On the Wednesday of the sixteenth the man in his forties went into that shop at about a quarter past twelve for a bag of plugs, and the woman who works in a shop was on the telephone with her back to the room, and the back room door was open and he went through it to see what she had got in a rack on the far wall that he has been after since July.

The ladder was standing against the far wall of that back room with the spanner on the top tread, and it was the same ladder, and there was about a fortnight's worth of dust on the ground under it in a rectangle where its feet stood, which told him it had not moved and had been put there on the sixth of August and had not been on since.

He stood in that back room and had it all in about four seconds, and what he had was that the ladder had come from that yard and had been put in her back room by a man who wanted a bit of board out of a shop and had wanted somewhere to leave a ladder, and that she had said all right to it.

He did not ask her. She was on the telephone. He picked the spanner up off the top tread and put it in his pocket, which was a thing he did without deciding to, and carried the ladder out through the shop past the counter where she was standing with the receiver against her shoulder and her hand over the mouthpiece, past her, and out of the door, and she said nothing, and he said good afternoon, and he took it up to that yard on his way back and stood it against the north wall with the feet on the bench again.

He has not told anybody he took a thing out of a shop's back room. He has not told the woman, and he has not told the man in his thirties, and nobody in that parade is a person he could tell.

---

This morning the man in his thirties went into that shop at about ten to ten with his bag for a coil of wire, and the woman who works in a shop said good morning and he asked her for the wire, and while she was at the rack at the back he went through into the back room and the ladder was standing where it was, with about a fortnight of dust round its feet and no spanner on the top tread, and he had it in the back of his van inside a minute.

She saw him go through. She came out of the rack with the coil and he was not in the room and she went and looked and the ladder was not there and the marks in the dust were not there either, and she went back to the counter and put the coil on it and he was at the door with the bag.

"That is dear for a coil," she said.

He put what it was on the counter and he said, "It is," and she said yes it is, and he said, "Aye," and went out into the parade with the bag and the wire in it and the ladder under the blanket in the van.

She has not asked him where the ladder came from. She watched him take it out of a back room at about ten to ten on the twentieth of August and she has not asked him and she is not going to, and there is a thing about that shop and that back room and that ladder that she is not going to say out loud to anybody including herself, and it is that she does not know where it came from either.

Here is the whole of it and there are two versions of it and neither of them is a lie.

The man in his forties' version is that the ladder came out of that yard on its own, because everything in this borough comes out of yards on its own, and then a man put it in a shop because a man needs a place to put a ladder, and he was in the back room and the spanner was on it and the dust said it had been there a fortnight, and he took it back to the yard because a ladder left in a shop's back room is a thing a shopkeeper has to be able to say no to, and he took it and put it where it was and has not thought about it since the Wednesday.

The man in his thirties' version is that the ladder was in that yard on the sixth of August with a spanner on it, and it was there on the sixth and the yard is open to anybody, and it stayed in that yard for three weeks after that, and it was in that yard on Saturday night and it was in that yard on Sunday morning, and the man in his forties has not set foot in that street since the summer because there is nothing on it he wants and he has a workshop at the bottom of the parade with his own ladder stood in it.

Both of them are wrong about one thing and neither of them will ever find out which. Both of them are certain that the other has never had a hand on it. The man in his forties is certain because the ladder was in his own yard and in a shop he has been in four times since March. The man in his thirties is certain because the ladder was in that yard and in a shop he has been in about thirty times since March, and a thing in a shop is a thing a shop has.

Neither of them is going to ask the other. Asking is one question and the answer to it is the other question, and neither of them has an answer that would hold up after it had been given.

The spanner is in the man's pocket in the man's van, and nobody has ever put a question to either of them about the spanner, and a spanner is not a name and a spanner is not a document and a spanner is not a thing anybody in this borough has been keeping a list of.

**A LADDER WENT OUT OF A SHOP'S BACK ROOM AT TEN TO TEN ON A SUNDAY MORNING IN AUGUST UNDER A BLANKET IN A VAN, AND IT HAD BEEN IN A YARD FOR THREE WEEKS BEFORE THAT, AND TWO MEN WHO HAVE SPOKEN ABOUT NOTHING ELSE TO EACH OTHER THIS YEAR ARE BOTH CONVINCED THE OTHER HAS NEVER TOUCHED IT.**
