# Chapter 0210 — Two Names

Wednesday the ninth of December 2015, from about two in the afternoon, an office on the second floor in Ravensmere.

**A MAN FROM THIS CITY WHO IS ON NOTHING IS BEING PAID BY A CITY ACROSS THE WATER FOR WORK HE IS DOING IN HIS OWN CITY, AND BOTH CITIES CALL IT SOMETHING DIFFERENT, AND HE IS THE ONLY PERSON WHO CAN EXPLAIN THE DIFFERENCE, AND NOBODY IS GOING TO ASK HIM.**

There is a room on the second floor with a table at the front of it and a window that has been painted over at some point in the last thirty years, and on the second Wednesday of every month about nine people come into it and are paid.

They are not paid by machine. There is a tin on the table with a lock on it that somebody carries from a different building, and the money is counted out into envelopes in front of the person, and the person initials the corner of a sheet, and the sheet goes into a tray and the tray goes down a corridor to a filing room that Jonas Mercer was not taken into and did not ask to be taken into.

It is the right way round, as far as anybody in that office is concerned. The money is in the envelope before the work starts. That is not a favour and it is not a favour's opposite either; it is the condition of the whole arrangement and everybody in the room knows it and nobody in the room thinks it is remarkable.

**AND THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THE WALL BEHIND THE TABLE THAT AFTERNOON, printed at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A PERSON MAY BE PAID BY A BODY THEY HAVE NEVER ENTERED.**
> **THE PLACE A PERSON WORKS IN IS NOT THE PLACE THAT PAYS THEM.**
> **BOTH OF THOSE CAN BE TRUE AT ONCE AND USUALLY ARE.**

Jonas Mercer was not in the room. He was in the corridor outside it with his hands behind his back, because he had not asked to be in there and had not been asked and had said out loud at the door that he would be in the corridor, and a man with a clipboard had said that was fine and had not made a face about it.

The last name on the schedule is the man of about thirty-nine.

Jonas Mercer has had that sheet in his head since the thirtieth of September, when a duty clerk read nine names out in a room over a shop and the last one was a name with a place under it, and the place was this city, and he had not looked at anybody in the room while she was saying it, and he was looking at a man of about thirty-nine in a corridor in another city at about twenty past two that Wednesday, and he knew the name and he did not use it, and there is nobody in that corridor who could have heard him use it.

The man of about thirty-nine is a strong man with a working face. He had come in off the morning boat and he had a sandwich in a paper bag and he had a job at a pumping station in his own city, in Tideglass, four days a week, on the distribution side of the estuary, doing the work of two men because the third one left in the spring.

He was paid, in the room, by Ravensmere, in an envelope, at Ravensmere's rate for the work, which was not the rate the same work is paid at on his side of the water, and the difference was not small.

The Tideglass water undertaker's own paperwork, which Jonas Mercer has never seen and could not produce if he were asked for it, calls it a relief contract. Ravensmere's sheet, which Jonas Mercer was shown at the end of the corridor by a man with a clipboard and which he read in about a minute, calls it an attachment, and says in one line what an attached man is, and the line says a man attached to the works.

The man of about thirty-nine came out of the room with the envelope in his hand and did not open it in the corridor, which Jonas Mercer noticed, and put it inside his jacket, and then, because he had been standing next to a man who was clearly waiting and had not said anything at all, said it anyway, the way people do.

"You're not from here."

"No."

"You've come a long way for an envelope."

"You've not got any idea." He said it without heat. "It's the pay day, isn't it. You can work that out, can't you. Come over on the pay day, every month, over the water, sit in a room, initial a corner, put it in your pocket, and go back. That's a morning and a day and a morning. I've done that four times since September and I am going to do it in January."

"You could be paid here."

"I could be paid here. I could be paid in a shop in that street for what I'm doing and I'd be home by six." He shifted the envelope. "And then this side stops. There's no argument about it. There is no version of me saying no to that, and if I did say no then in about a fortnight the man at the pumping station in my own city would find out there was a spare man on the distribution side, because that's how it works here, and then he'd want his own overtime, and then I'd be on a different thing, and then the money from over the water would stop, because it isn't a wage and everybody in that room knows it isn't a wage, and so I get the water."

The corridor went on being a corridor.

"Down here they call it a relief contract," he said. "Which is a bit like it. I turn up, I do the work, I go home, nobody's got me. Over there they call it an attachment, and an attachment is a thing that belongs to something, and I have got a house and a road and a bed about two miles from where I stood at seven o'clock this morning, and I am attached to a works on this side of an estuary that I have never once been asked inside."

"You could say the word and somebody would have to change it."

"I could say the word to who?" He almost laughed. "I have not got anybody. I am not in your borough. I am not in a union here, I am not on a list here, I am not on a list there, I have got an envelope and a name on a sheet and a name on a sheet in my own city that says relief, and the two of them have been true at the same time since September and there is one person in the world who knows that, and he is standing in a corridor, and he is not going to say anything about it, and I have to go and catch the four o'clock."

**AND JONAS MERCER DID NOT ASK HIM ONE QUESTION. HE COULD HAVE. HE IS THE ONLY PERSON IN EITHER CITY WHO COULD HAVE, BECAUSE HE IS THE ONLY PERSON WHO HAS READ BOTH OF THE TWO WORDS, AND HE STOOD THERE AND LET THE MAN GO DOWN THE CORRIDOR TOWARDS THE LIFT WITH THE ENVELOPE STILL SEALED, AND HE DID NOT, AND HE IS NOT GOING TO, AND THE ARRANGEMENT IS IN WRITING AND THE WRITING IS CORRECT.**

At about half past three the afternoon item came up and Jonas Mercer was not in the room for it and the man of about thirty-nine was, because he had come to be paid and not to do anything else and both of those had been arranged in a way that suited everybody.

The item was the arrangement of the thirtieth of September. There were two documents on the table, both of them correct, and both of them taken from a permission that is in a plastic wallet in a filing room in a city on the other side of an estuary and is not in any building in this borough at all and has not been since the day a woman in a waterproof took it back on the six o'clock train.

The first document was the number of people the arrangement had moved, and it was six, and the six were named in the room, and the dates against the six were the dates on a sheet that went back across the water in a plastic wallet in a duty clerk's bag and has not been in this borough since the day she took it. Jonas Mercer was in the corridor and heard none of it and has not seen any of it since, and the six are six people in a city on the other side of the water who have not been asked anything by anybody in either of them.

The second document was a sentence about where a claim arising out of the work is made, and it was correct, and Iselin Krogh read it out in the voice she uses for things that are already settled, and nobody argued with it, and the minute says the item was noted.

Jonas Mercer was in the corridor for the whole of that with his hands behind his back, because a man with a clipboard had said that was fine on Wednesday and had said it again on Friday and had not said it a third time, and because there is nothing in Ravensmere for him to be in a room about.

At about four the lift came and went and the man of about thirty-nine was in it going down, with the envelope still not opened, and the two of them had not exchanged another word, and the door of the second floor shut and the building went on holding water for three towns until six o'clock.
