# Chapter 0877 — Half An Inch Under A Trestle

Monday the fifteenth of January 2024, under the bench in the yard at the unit from the time the dew has come off the flags until the last of the light has gone off the store roof. How much room there is under it.

**THERE IS A BENCH ALONG THE WALL OF THAT YARD STANDING ON TWO TRESTLES AND HE MEANS TO LAY A HAND FLAT ON THE GROUND AND PUSH IT FORWARD UNDER THE BENCH AND THEN TURN IT OVER SO THAT THE BACKS OF THE FINGERS ARE AGAINST THE UNDERSIDE OF THE BOARD, AND THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN IS AT THE STORE DOOR WITH IT OPEN BEHIND HER.**

The bench has been against that wall under the gutter since the spring and it has not been moved since it was put there, and it is the sort of thing that goes in a corner of a yard and is not thought about again.

A scaffold board on two trestles, a foot and a bit above the flags, hard against the wall.

He knows what the top of it is like. That is the whole of what he has ever wanted to know about it and it took him the better part of a day with a bundle of cards.

What is under it is the other half of the question, and the flags under that bench are in one piece where the ones round it are scuffed pale, with the dust lying flat across the joints and joined at the edges of it, which is what a floor looks like that has had nothing stood on it and nothing walked over since it was laid.

Which is exactly why whatever is under it has been able to do what it has done.

A bench on two trestles in a yard in this country stands on two trestles and two trestles stand on ground. The ground goes down and the bench does not, and then the bench is a thing on stilts, and then water goes under the board instead of off it, and the board goes off at the ends of it, and the whole of that goes on the one surface in a yard that no arrangement of daylight ever reaches.

So he gets down and puts his hand flat on the flags at the front of the bench, palm down, and pushes it forward under the board.

It goes in about four inches and the wrist comes up against the front edge of the board, and he turns the hand over on itself so that the backs of his fingers are against the underside of the board.

Four inches of air at the gate end, under the front edge, clear the whole way along the board, and the board is a good deal higher in the middle.

He can feel the underside with his fingers from the front edge back to about the width of a hand, and that is all, and that is all he needs.

Then he does it at four places along the bench, and the four answers are not the same, which is the finding.

At the gate end: four inches.

A foot along: four inches.

Half way: three and a half.

At the wall end: not four. Under two.

He goes back and measures that end twice, because it is the end against the wall and the wall is the last place in a yard that goes for anything.

Under two inches. And when he puts his fingers up and runs them along the underside at that end he can get the pad of his thumb on the underside of the board and his fingers on the ground at the same time, with the hand closed as far as it will close.

That is the whole of it. The bench is not levelling itself to the wall. The ground is rising to meet it.

The yard falls to the gate.

That is the shape of the whole of that yard and it is not a fault anybody put there. The yard was made to fall away from the house so that the water has somewhere to go, and the wall the bench is against is the highest part of the fall, and the ground at the foot of that wall is higher than the ground at the gate by about two inches across the width of a bench.

Which puts the whole of the finding in one place.

The bench is not sagging and it is not tipped and it is not on the wrong trestle. It is on a camber. At the gate end it stands on four inches of nothing and at the wall end it stands on under two, and the difference between those two numbers is the ground, and the ground has not moved and is not going to move.

He puts his hand flat on the flags under the wall end and pushes down and the ground is solid. It is the same made ground as everywhere else in that yard. It is simply higher.

And the trestle at that end is bottomed out.

He puts his fingers in and finds the foot of it, and between the foot of the trestle and the ground there is about half an inch.

Half an inch, on a trestle, is nothing. Half an inch is what a trestle has under it when it is set down properly and the ground under it gives what the ground gives.

But it is half an inch on a camber, which means that if anybody ever wanted that bench another two inches lower at the wall end, there is nothing for its foot to stand on. There is half an inch of air where there ought to be ground, and it is not going to be packed and it is not going to be dug, because the whole of the rest of that yard is on the same made ground and there is no stone under any of it to pack it with.

The underside of the board tells the rest of it.

With the hand turned over under there he can find a band along the underside of the board that is darker than the rest of it, and that band is at the gate end and it runs about four feet, and it is where the damp comes up off the flags and into the underside of the board and stays.

At the wall end the underside is dry. Dry all the way back along the board past the middle, on a board that has had four months of weather on the top of it in a yard.

He takes his hand out and sits back on his heels for a while.

So the bench is drying at one end and not drying at the other, and that is not because of anything about the bench. It is because one end of it is over ground that is two inches higher than the other end and is therefore never the end that a wet yard puts its water into.

---

The woman from two doors down comes through the yard at about eleven and goes along to the store door and stands with the door open behind her.

He was up on the flags with his hand under the bench when she came round the corner and she stopped about where the bench begins and stayed there.

"Your gutter's overflowing."

"It's not mine."

"Oh, it is. Look at the joint."

He looks at the joint. The joint where the downpipe from that gutter goes into the drain at the bottom of it is full of three seasons of leaf and there is a bead of water standing at the top of it, and he says it wants a pole and a pair of gloves and that is not a Thursday job. She says her side has been doing the same since Christmas and hers is worse than his. He says hers is always worse. She says it is only ever worse on the day he is standing in his own yard looking at his own downpipe.

Then she goes back out the way she came and the store door is open behind her the whole time she is standing there.

---

So it stays where it is.

Four inches under the near trestle, half an inch under the far one, and a yard that goes up the whole length of itself in between them.
