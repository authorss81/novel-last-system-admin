# Chapter 0665 — The Brush On The Floor

Saturday the fourth of September 2021, in this borough. She is in her own house from first light until midday and she goes out of the front door once and round the side once, and the whole of the morning is a brush she does not move and a gate she does not shut.

**A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-EIGHT STEPS OVER A BRUSH ON HER OWN PASSAGE FLOOR ON A SATURDAY MORNING TO REACH HER OWN KITCHEN, AND LEAVES A GATE STANDING OPEN AGAINST A COAL SHED WALL, AND SEES DAMP HAS GOT TO THE EDGE ABOVE THE FRONT ROOM, AND COMES DOWN WITHOUT TOUCHING ANYTHING.**

She is a woman in her late seventies. She is on her own all morning. Nobody knocks.

---

She wakes with the passage light on, as it has been since June, and she leaves it on while she gets her dressing gown from the back of the front door, and the light falls down the runner to the middle where the brush lies with the handle across the walking line.

She looks at it from the doorway of the back room and does not bend for it, because bending for it would make it a thing she had picked up, and she has decided it is a thing that gets stepped over.

She lifts her right foot over the handle and then her left, and the sole of her slipper brushes the runner and not the wood, and she is past it and on the tiles of the kitchen before she has had a thought she can put words to.

---

The kitchen is at the back of that house and the front room is between the front door and the kitchen, which is why she goes through the room she has decided not to sit in every time she wants a cup, and this morning she keeps her eyes on the doorway and not on the sideboard, which has nothing on it.

The table is at the kitchen end under the window, square with the edge of it a gas envelope face up, pencil side up, with six things on the back of it in pencil and nothing crossed off.

---

She puts the kettle on and stands at the sink while it heats, and the sill over the sink is empty, and she is glad of it in a way she does not examine.

---

She eats toast at the table on the green chair with the cushion on it, which has not been in the front room since the thirty-first of May, and the table does not rock because the leg is back in dry with a card on the joint and an ash shim planed and sawn flush under the foot on the window side.

---

She wipes crumbs off the cloth and pushes them onto her plate, and while the tea cools she reads the six things without picking the paper up, because picking it up would make it a thing she was doing something with.

The gate. The front room. The window in the back room. The hotplate on the left. The bath tap. The passage light.

Four of six are done and she knows which four without counting, and there is no pencil in that house and there has not been since before Christmas.

---

The front door is not on the latch and never has been since before Christmas, and she hears it move a little in the wind while she drinks, and she does not go to it, because a door that is not on the latch is not a thing she can shut by going to it.

She finishes and washes the plate and the cup and puts them on the drainer, and she wipes the cloth flat on the table and pushes the green chair in, and she stands at the kitchen doorway with her hands on the frame.

---

The passage runs from the front door to the kitchen, about four feet of runner that came with the house, and the brush is in the middle of it about two feet inside the door, exactly where she put it on the first, handle across and not along.

---

She goes down the passage in her slippers to look at it properly, and she stops a pace off and looks down, and there is a curl of old egg-coloured gloss in the crimp and grey dust in the ferrule and it has not been washed and it is not going to be.

A clean thing on a floor looks done on purpose. She worked that out on the Wednesday and she still thinks it is true.

She steps over it again, out towards the front door and back, to make sure the step can be done without turning her foot too far, and it can, and she goes back to the kitchen and gets her coat from the back of the front door.

---

The reason for the coat is the gate, and the reason for the gate is she wants to see it in daylight with nobody about, and it is Saturday morning and the road has nobody on it and has had nobody on it at this hour since before Christmas.

She goes out of the front door and shuts it behind her, and she goes round the side on the grass past the coal shed on the walked-flat line, and the dew is still on the grass where nobody has walked but her.

The gate is standing back against the wall of the coal shed out of the way, as she left it, and the path behind it is clear and the road in front of it is empty.

---

Both drop bolts are in the pocket of the apron she has on under the coat, and she puts her hand in and feels the cold of them through the cloth, and she does not take them out, because taking them out would be a step towards putting them back in their sockets.

She stands on the inside of her own gate with the path behind her and the road in front of her, and she looks up and down the road without moving her head much, and there is no van and no figure and no bus, and it is not a road anybody has a reason to be on.

She did not open that gate so that somebody could come up the path. She opened it because it had not been left open, and she wants it to stay open.

---

She goes back in through the front door and shuts it behind her, because it is shut when she shuts it, and it does not latch and it never has, and she steps over the brush on her way to her own kitchen without breaking her stride.

In the kitchen she takes the coat off and hangs it on the back of the front door, and she takes the apron off and hangs it beside it, and the bolts chink a little in the pocket and then are quiet.

---

She stands at the kitchen window and looks at the gate from there, and the gate swings a little in the wind against the coal shed wall and does not come onto the path.

---

At midday she goes to the bathroom under the landing to empty the bowl, because the bath tap has been dripping since before Christmas and there is a folded cloth in the bowl under it and she empties it morning and night.

She lifts the cloth out and wrings it into the bowl and carries the bowl to the bath and tips it away, and she puts the folded cloth back in the empty bowl under the drip.

While the next drip gathers she looks up without meaning to, and the brown mark on the bathroom ceiling is still there, about a foot across with a hard edge, and the edge looks darker than it was in August.

---

She puts her thumb to the edge of the mark and takes it away, and there is grit on her skin, and she wipes it on the cloth in the bowl and the grit stays in the weave.

The landing is over the front room. She worked that out on the Friday night in August with a torch, standing on a stool from the front room to hear a cistern weeping at the far side of a joist, and she has not been up the stairs since.

She stands on the half landing now with her hand on the rail, because she has come up without deciding to, in her slippers, with the bowl still in the bathroom behind her.

---

From the half landing she can see the ceiling of the landing where it meets the hatch with the fixed ladder, and she can see a faint darkening at the edge nearest the front of the house, where the water would have to travel to get from the bathroom side to above the front room.

She does not go up the ladder. She has never been up it and she is not going to start, because she does not know where the pipe comes up and never has, and she cannot turn the supply off from downstairs.

She listens for the weeping she heard in August, and the house is quiet except for the tap dripping below, and she cannot hear it from here and she does not expect to.

---

She comes down and goes back to the bathroom and picks up the bowl with the cloth in it and sets it back under the drip, and she washes her hands at the basin and dries them on the towel on the back of the door.

In the kitchen she sits at the table under the window on the green chair with the cushion on it, and the gas envelope is still face up square with the edge, and she does not touch it.

The second list is on the front of that same piece of paper in her own handwriting, eight boxes put back and four to the loft and two and the curtains and one and the green tiles and four and the stair, and the top corner of it is gone, taken by tape, and neither woman can write on that paper again, and she does not turn it over.

---

She sits until the light goes off the kitchen window, and she does not go into the front room, and she has not sat in there since the sixteenth of July because there is now a question in that room and she has never been able to be a person being asked about a thing.

The rush-seated chair is against the wall by the window in the front room and nobody sits on it. The sideboard has nothing on it. The ceiling in that room is white and it will not be white by the winter.

She gets up and puts the kettle on again and stands at the sink, and the passage light is still on, and the brush is still in the middle of the runner where everybody walks.

---

**AND A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-EIGHT HAS GOT THROUGH A SATURDAY MORNING WITHOUT MOVING A BRUSH SHE PUT ON A PASSAGE FLOOR SO THAT IT WOULD BE STEPPED OVER, AND WITHOUT SHUTTING A GATE SHE STOOD OPEN SO THAT IT WOULD STAY OPEN, AND THE DAMP FROM A ROOF SPACE HAS GOT TO THE EDGE ABOVE THE FRONT ROOM SHE WILL NOT SIT IN, AND FOUR OF SIX IS DONE AND NOTHING HAS BEEN CROSSED OFF.**

(End of chapter 0665)
