# Chapter 0406 — Nobody's Lid

Tuesday the twenty-fourth of April 2018, beginning at about ten past nine in the morning and ending at about half past four in the afternoon, a yard off a street off that parade, and a shop on that parade, in Tideglass.

**A MAN BEHIND A COUNTER ON THAT PARADE TOLD A MAN IN HIS FORTIES IN ABOUT FIFTEEN SECONDS THAT NOBODY HAD HAD A CERTAIN LID OFF SINCE BEFORE CHRISTMAS, AND HE COULD NOT HAVE KNOWN IT AND DID NOT ASK, AND A DAY LATER THE MAN IN HIS FORTIES TOOK THAT LID OFF AND IT WAS TRUE, AND HE HAS NEVER ASKED ANYBODY WHERE HE HEARD IT.**

The yard is at the back of a terrace of eight houses off a street that comes off that parade. It is about thirty foot deep and about eighteen wide, and it has a brick store standing in the corner of it that is the full height of the store, with a door of two boards and a frame and a bar across the outside of it, and a padlock and a hasp that were put on by the man who owns the store in about 1994 and have not been touched since.

That store is twelve foot by nine and it is dry and it is floored in concrete and it is the coldest place in that yard in any weather, which is why the water in it is about as good as the water anywhere in that terrace.

In it there is a water tank. It is a sectional tank of four panels a side, made up in about 1961 and moved into that store in about 1974 when the terrace was first metered, and it stands on a steel stand about two foot off the floor, and it is about eight foot long and four foot wide and three foot deep, and it is full to within five inches of the top with a board lid of three planks screwed down over the whole of it.

There is a float valve in the corner nearest the door, about the size of a fist, brass, on a tail of three-quarter pipe, and it goes through the side of the tank and out into the yard to a stop tap. There is an overflow the same size as the feed, which is how you can tell a tank is running, and it discharges through the wall of that store about eighteen inch off the floor.

---

The man in his forties has been in that yard three times in April.

The first was the third of April, for about nine foot of copper pipe and a set of clips to run a downpipe on the side of the third house along, which is a job he got off a man in a shop and which took him about three quarters of an hour.

The second was the seventeenth, to look at the standing of a soil pipe on the back of the last house along, which he did not end up doing anything about and has not thought about since.

The third was the morning this happened.

He was in that yard at about ten past ten on the Tuesday because he was waiting on a man in his forties's own van to come round a corner and he had not got a bag of tools with him and wanted the standpipe on the side of the last house, which comes off that store and is the only place in that yard you can get a run of water without turning the stop tap off at the top.

He was in the shop on that parade at about ten past ten on the Tuesday for a roll of tape, which is where this chapter starts.

He was at the near end of that counter waiting for a man with a knife to cut a length of conduit about four foot long, and the man behind the counter was doing somebody else's first, and the man in his forties had his back half turned and was looking at the window.

He said, "Has anybody had that tank lid off?"

The man behind that counter said, "Which tank."

"The one at the bottom of the yard behind the eight houses off that street that comes off here. Off the parade. I was in there this morning."

"Nobody's had it off since before Christmas, I'd say."

That is what he said. It took about fifteen seconds from the start of it to the end of it, and then the man behind that counter cut the length of conduit and put it in a bag, and the man in his forties paid for the tape and the conduit and went, and neither of them said one word about a tank after that and the man behind that counter has not mentioned a tank to anybody since, and put to him he would answer at once that he has not the faintest idea where he got it, and that is not an evasion, because he has not, and it reached him out of the air the way most things reach a man in a shop, which is to say that it arrives.

Here is where it came from and how far it had been carried, and none of it is a chain anybody can follow.

The man behind that counter was told it in the last week of March by a man in a fleece who was in that shop on the Friday afternoon, and the man in a fleece had it from a man who used to keep the keys to those yards and does not keep them now, and the man who used to keep the keys was told it by a man who had a look at that tank at Christmas and mentioned it to a man he worked with.

Nobody in that line owns a yard, owns a house, or has a key. Nobody in that line has been in that store. The man in a fleece goes to the works. The man who used to keep the keys has not been down that yard since about 2014.

The man in his forties stood in that shop for a second after he had paid for the tape, and then went out and got into his own van and drove round to that yard.

He had a reason. He had had water off that standpipe twice in April and both times it had been a warm brown, and both times it had cleared after about half a minute, and a thing does that and then stops doing it and you do not chase it, and he was not chasing it, he was using it for a bucket.

He got the bar off that store at about half past ten. The padlock and the hasp were not locked. They have not been locked since about 1994 and everybody who has ever been in that store, which is not many, has had the same thought about that hasp and none of them has ever mentioned it to anybody.

He got a screwdriver out of the van and undid four screws and put the three planks of that lid down on the concrete.

There is a smell off that tank. It is not a bad smell. It is the smell of a tank that has not been looked at since before Christmas, which is a smell like a cellar with a wet floor in it, and it went up the inside of his nose and he put his head back and looked in.

The water was about five inches below the top and it was completely flat and it was the colour of tea.

And at the bottom of that tank there was about four inches of it, settled out of the water and lying across the whole floor of the tank in a skin, and it had gone into the joints of the four sides and into the corners where those sides are bolted to their frames, and where a skin like that has been lying undisturbed for months the side it has been lying against has a clean line across it about the height the water has been standing at, and this one had two of those lines on it, which means the level has come down twice.

The float valve was stuck wide open. He put his hand in and turned the arm over and it would not move, and the arm was stiff with scale where it had been sitting in one position since Christmas, and the overflow was running. He stood there and listened to it and he could hear it, a thin noise of water going out through a pipe in a wall, and that is what a tank sounds like for a hundred and twenty days.

Nobody in that terrace knows that. The water went in at the top of the yard and went out the same way it did before 1974 and the bills have been about the same for as long as any of them has been there, and the man at the end of that terrace pays the same as the man at the parade end and has never once asked why.

He did not do anything about it that day. He put the lid back on and put the four screws back in and put the bar back across that door, and he drove back to his own place and put the tape down.

He went back on the Wednesday of the second of May and did that tank.

He took the three planks off and carried them out and laid them on the yard wall, and he bailed it with a bucket into a tub and carried the tub out to the road twice, and it took him about an hour and a half to get it down to the bottom and about another hour to get at the four corners with a small hand scythe and a bucket.

He came back on the third of May with a new float valve and took the old one off and put the new one on, and he was in that store from about half past seven until it was about two in the afternoon, and he scrubbed the four sides out with a wire wool and a weak mix, and he put about four teaspoonfuls of a milky thing in and left it, and he filled it from the standpipe, and he let it stand a day, and he flushed it through the overflow, and he filled it again.

He charged the man at the end of that terrace one hundred and ten pounds and took a cheque for it, and the man at the end of that terrace said, and it took him about four seconds to say it, that nothing in that yard had surprised him since 1974 and that this had, and that is the whole of the conversation about it.

The man in his thirties was in that yard on the second of May about half past ten, on a job at the second house along, and the tank was open and the man in his forties was up on a plank in it, and he stood at the gate for about a minute and then went and did his own work. He has not mentioned that tank to anybody since and is not going to.

---

**HE BAILED IT OUT IN A TUB AND CARRIED IT OUT TO THE ROAD TWICE AND PUT THREE PLANKS ON A WALL AND CAME BACK WITH A NEW BRASS VALVE IN HIS POCKET, AND A MAN BEHIND A COUNTER ON THAT PARADE TOLD HIM IN FIFTEEN SECONDS THAT NOBODY HAD HAD THAT LID OFF, AND HE IS NOT GOING TO FIND OUT HOW A MAN WHO SELLS PIPE KNEW.**
