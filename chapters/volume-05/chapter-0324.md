# Chapter 0324 — A Saturday in May

Saturday the twenty-seventh of May 2017, from about ten to eight in the morning, and later that same evening, out on that parade in Tideglass, the low end of Peverell Street, a building by the water, and a hall on Carrow Lane.

**IT WAS THE FIRST DRY MORNING SINCE BEFORE CHRISTMAS, AND A GUTTER HAD BIRDS IN IT, AND FOUR SENTENCES HAD BEEN GOING ROUND A TABLE IN A HALL SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY, AND A MAN PUT A QUESTION AT HALF PAST EIGHT THAT NOBODY ANSWERED, AND A BOOK ON A SHELF IN A BUILDING BY THE WATER IS OPEN AT NINETEEN ENTRIES WITH A BLANK LINE UNDER THE NINETEENTH.**

The gutter on the parade had about nine of them in it at about ten to eight, sitting along the lip of it in a row facing the same way, and they went over the roof tops of that street at about nine in about four minutes and were not seen in that street again that day.

The greengrocer's came up at about ten to eight with a man inside it on a step, and the woman who runs it carried two crates in herself and stood the rest against the front of the cold room where they go. The shop that shuts early was shut and its roller was down and had been down since two o'clock on the Thursday. The radio shop next door to that had a shutter of its own standing at about the height of a man's chest on a good day, with a man in behind the counter and nothing coming out of the set, and two more sets out on the pavement on a piece of board with the prices on small cards in marker, and the cards had gone soft in the sun.

At the top of Peverell Street the bin for gritting stood where it stands with about a hand of gravel in the bottom of it and a dry twig in that, and the hole punched through one side of it was still there and still about the size of a closed hand.

At the top of Marsh Lane a van stood with its back to a wall and nothing written on the side of it, its tarpaulin bundled down over the top rail and its load space empty, and on any count anybody keeps of that van it is standing there on all but about two days in ten.

At the low end of Peverell Street the gable over that bay was in the sun at about half past nine and two slates on it had the heads of their nails standing about half an inch proud of them, and a woman was sitting in that bay behind a table with a plant on it, and there was nothing under that gable and nobody came along that pavement and looked up at it.

---

The bay in that building by the water is a bay off a first-floor corridor and it has a door on it that does not shut over the catch, and the book is on the shelf in that bay and it is the second shelf up.

It is a bound book with the edges rubbed, open at nineteen entries, and the last of them is a yard lamp. Somebody went round and reported that bulb out about November two years ago and put it against a heading that is kept over for that, and the column under that heading has not been touched since. Underneath the line of it the page carries on as page for the width of two fingers and then stops about two inches short of the foot of the book, and those two inches are nobody's space. They are the space the book has.

Count the days from that lamp going into the book to that Saturday morning and the count is nine hundred and thirteen.

A woman came into that room at about half past ten with a woman she had come in with, and they wanted a chair out of it, because that corridor was being painted in the morning and the paint had gone onto a chair on that corridor and the woman who had the flat had said it was not hers.

They took the chair that was at the end of that corridor against the wall, and they went out again with it between them, and neither of them was within six foot of that bay, and neither of them knew what was on the shelf in it, and the door on that bay stood open behind them when they left it.

Up the stairs from that bay, on that first-floor landing, a vacuum cleaner is still lying over in the corner where that landing widens by the fire door, with about half a bag of grit in its filter and its lead dropped across the top of it, and the wall behind it is not straight and it does not stand against it.

---

At half past eight on the Saturday evening there were nine of them in the hall on Carrow Lane and the meter was on and the window was still open at the top because the room had been warm since about six.

The man in his sixties had the chair in the corner with the leg coming through it. The man in his forties was at the near end with both hands down on the table either side of a cup, as he has them. The man in his thirties had his bag on the floor by his foot and had come in at about eight and had not taken his coat off. The man who works nights was by the door in his coat, as he is. The man from the parade had got there early, as he does, and was sitting with a cup in both hands. The woman who works in a shop had turned her chair about and was sitting with her back to that window. The woman of about fifty-five came in early and hung her coat over the back of the chair. And the man who does the hall had the urn going at the far end of that table with a cloth folded over the top of it, and was at the door taking a chair off its hook with his coat still on.

There are four of them and there have been four since the twelfth of January, and the four on that table this evening are those same four, and nobody in this borough has managed to lay one of them beside another.

"Four," said the woman who works in a shop, to nobody in particular.

"It's four," said the man in his forties.

"Nobody's put one on and nobody's taken one off and nobody's said where any of the four came from, and I'm not going to sit here and add up four things I've never seen the edges of."

The man in his forties said that if four were going to be anything else tonight, it was not going to be by one of them counting, and that a man who changes four into five to see what five looks like has found nothing and has only made work for everybody else sitting here.

The man in his sixties said, "What are they between them, then. The four."

Nobody at that table said anything to that, and the man who does the hall put his chair back against the wall and said that the tea was going and he was not going to make it up again that night.

Then the man from the parade put his cup down about an inch from the edge of that table.

"There's a thing I've been turning over since the winter," he said, "and it's not one of those four and I'm not putting it beside those four. I'm asking whether there's any difference in this town between a thing that was done and a thing that somebody has written down about. Because I've been in a room where a thing was done, and I've been in a room where a thing was written down, and the one that was written down was a better thing than the one that was done, and nobody in either room has ever gone back and put the two side by side. And I'd like somebody in this room to tell me whether that can run the other way. Whether a thing that's been written down can turn out to be worse than the thing that happened."

"That is a question," said the man in his forties.

"That's a question," said the man from the parade.

"Then it stays one," said the man in his forties, and nobody at that table took it any further, and the man from the parade picked his cup up and drank out of it.

The man in his thirties said that there were two slates on that gable with the nails standing proud of them, and the man in his forties said he had heard about the slates from a man in a shop, and that he was not going to take it further than that either, and the man in his thirties said that was fair.

The gas went out at nine o'clock, the case was shut and locked after that, and the chairs went up on the wall, and the porch light came on as the door opened, and it was still burning about a quarter of an hour later when the corner lamp took over outside and did whatever it does.

**AND NINE PEOPLE WENT OUT OF THAT HALL INTO A STREET THAT HAD BEEN DRY SINCE BEFORE CHRISTMAS, AND A PADLOCK WENT ON THE CASE BEHIND THEM, AND TWO SLATES ARE STILL ON A GABLE AT THE LOW END OF PEVERELL STREET WITH THE HEADS OF THEIR NAILS STANDING OUT OF THEM, AND A WOMAN IS STILL SITTING BEHIND THAT BAY WITH A PLANT ON HER TABLE.**
