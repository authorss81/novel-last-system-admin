# Chapter 0383 — A Wall Like That

Tuesday the sixteenth of January 2018, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past nine at night, a yard off a turning by the railway arch, in Tideglass.

**A MAN WHO HAS TRADED OUT OF THAT YARD FOR AS LONG AS ANYBODY THERE HAS KNOWN SAYS THAT A WALL LIKE THE ONE ALONG THE BACK OF IT WILL NOT TAKE A SCREW, AND HE HAS SAID IT FOR AS LONG AS THE OTHER MAN HAS BEEN IN THAT YARD AND HE PUTS THOSE IN FOR EVERY FIXING HE MAKES INTO IT, AND ON THE AFTERNOON OF THE SIXTEENTH OF JANUARY THE MAN WHO PUTS THEM IN WENT DOWN ON HIS KNEES AT THAT WALL TO TAKE ONE OUT AND COULD NOT, AND WORKED OUT WHY, AND SAID NOTHING TO ANYBODY.**

The yard is behind a row of shops that backs onto the railway arch, and it is a small square of concrete with a brick wall along two sides of it and a palisade fence along the third and the railway arch along the fourth, and the arch is what you hear all day and is the reason the yard is quiet.

There are two men in it. The older of them has had that yard since before the arch was closed in, and he sells fixings and small tools out of the front of it and takes in work he does not want, and he has a great many customers and does not know most of them by anything but a trade.

The other man works in that yard and has done for a couple of years and is a good deal younger and does most of the going out on the jobs.

The man who works nights came into that yard at about twenty to five on the afternoon of the sixteenth of January to pick up a coil he had left on the bench, having been up since about two and out and back, and the two men in that yard did not know that was what he was there for and did not ask.

---

A wall like that will not take a screw.

That is what the older man says, and he has been saying it for longer than the younger man has been alive, and he says it about any wall that has ever been built out of a soft block or a cavity or anything that was made to be filled.

The way he says it is not a rule and it is not a boast. It is how a man talks about a thing nobody has ever shown him to be wrong about. He has put a fixing into a soft wall in his own hand and watched it turn in it. He has had a plate come away off a wall in a house he was not even working in. He was told it once, at the start, by a man who is not working now, and it was said as a fact about the world and not as an opinion, and it went in the way those things go in.

The resin comes in a cartridge and a gun goes on the end of it and it costs about what a box of screws costs, and it takes about a minute longer per fixing than a plug does, and it works.

The younger man has been carrying that gun up ladders for about a year. He has done about four fixings into that kind of wall since the summer and there are dozens of them on jobs all over this borough, and every one of them is holding perfectly well, and he has never once pulled on one to see.

On the sixteenth of January he was taking a length of batten off the back wall of that yard.

He had put it up himself in the autumn to hang things on, and he had put every screw into it with the resin, four of them, because that wall is a wall like that, and he was taking the batten down because the older man had decided that things were to hang on the palisade side from then on.

The first one came out. It was a screw into a resin plug and the resin had gone all the way round the shaft of the screw and had set hard, and the screw came out whole with a lump of resin on the end of it like a collar, and he was pleased with that.

The second one did not.

He got a claw hammer on the head of it and pulled and it did not move, and he put the claw under the head and levered and the batten came off the wall about the thickness of a coin on that fixing and stopped dead, and the plaster on either side of it opened up and about a foot of it came away in a piece.

That is not what a screw in a soft wall does. A screw in a soft wall turns, and the head comes out, and there is nothing to hold on. What he had was something else.

---

He went down on his knees at that wall at about half past four and put two fingers round the second screw and worked at it with the tip of a screwdriver for a while and then stopped and looked at it properly.

There was a ring round the head of that screw where the resin had come out of the hole and run down into the face of the block, and the resin in that ring had set hard and had gone round the outside of the screw as well as round the shaft.

He scraped a bit of it off with the blade of a screwdriver and looked at what was under it.

The block is the colour of a man's shirt. He put his knuckle against it and knocked it, twice, and it is not a soft block and it is not a cavity. It is the hardest thing on that yard, and you can lean your weight on that wall and it does not move, and there is a furring batten run down it about two feet off the ground and that is what he was fixing into and not the wall at all.

He got up off his knees and stood there with the screwdriver in his hand.


The older man was out of that yard at the time, at a house at the end of the row.

The younger man had about three hours to say it and did not use any of them.

The reason is not that he was being kind to the older man and it is not that he was afraid of him. It is that there is nothing to say, because the older man is not wrong about a great many walls and is right about a great many of them, and there is no arguing the point with him without a job in front of both of them, and there is no job in front of both of them at present.

And the man who put the resin in is the man who put it in on the strength of what he was told by a man who is not working now and who could not be asked, so that there is nobody standing in that yard to say that the wall on the back of it is not a wall like that.

Everything the older man has done on the strength of it is fine. Every plate, every bracket, every shelf, every cupboard and every sign he has fixed into a wall of that kind in this borough since the summer is exactly where it should be and is still exactly where it should be, and there is not one job in this town going to come back in a year because of a resin plug.

He will go on saying it. He will go on sending the other man up ladders with the gun, and the gun will go on working, and the cartridges will go on costing what they cost, and neither of them will ever open this out.


The man who works nights came into that yard at about twenty to five that afternoon and was in it about six minutes.

He came in at the gate and the older man was out and the younger man was down on his knees at the back wall with a length of batten on the floor beside him and a claw hammer and a screwdriver and about a foot of plaster lying face up in the dust, and he stood there and looked at that for a moment and picked his coil up off the bench and went out through the gate.

He has not told either of them what was happening at the back of that yard, then or since, and he is not a man who would go back and ask what had happened to a wall, and he will be in that yard again.

**THE YOUNGER MAN KNOWS NOW AND WILL CARRY THE GUN UP THAT LADDER NEXT MONTH, AND THE OLDER MAN WILL SAY THE SAME THING HE HAS ALWAYS SAID, AND NOTHING ABOUT THE WALL ON THE BACK OF THAT YARD IS GOING TO BE PUT IN FRONT OF EITHER OF THEM, AND A THING THAT WAS FIXED INTO IT WITH RESIN IN THE AUTUMN IS STILL HOLDING UP A BATTEN WITH A THING ON IT.**
