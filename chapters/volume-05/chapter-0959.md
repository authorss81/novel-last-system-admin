# Chapter 0959 — Behind The Cast Gutter

Sunday the first of December 2024, at the cast gutter on the back wall at number twelve with the wall dry since Friday. Behind a gutter.

**THE GUTTER HAS COME AWAY FROM THE WALL AT ONE OF ITS JOINTS AND STANDS AN INCH OFF IT AT THAT END, AND THE HAND GOES UP BEHIND IT AND IS PUSHED ALONG THE RUN OF IT UNTIL THE HOLLOW BEHIND THE CASTING STOPS, TO FIND OUT HOW FAR THE HOLLOW GOES AND WHAT IS AGAINST THE WALL AT THE END OF IT.**

The gutter is cast lead and not a length of anything else, and it has been on the back wall of that house for the better part of a hundred years, in lengths of about three feet, and every one of those lengths is screwed to the wall through the back of it.

The screws are put in through the back of the lead and into a batten behind, because lead cannot be screwed into at its front face without splitting it, and the back of the casting is the only place a length of cast gutter can be fixed to anything at all. The last length along that run has pulled off the wall at its joint with the one before it, which is the joint at the far end where the wall turns the corner, and it has come away because the batten behind that end was never there to be screwed to and the fixing at the corner took the bracket instead.

So the last length stands an inch off the wall at the corner end and lies flat against it at the other end, and behind it there is a hollow a foot high and three inches wide that goes the whole length of that section of wall and is shut at both ends.

The wall behind is the solid old brick of the back of the house with a lime render on it gone hard and pale, and that hollow is the only air in the whole of the back of that building that a hand could be put into.

---

The hand goes up behind the gutter with the fingers together and palm up, and then it is pushed up along the run of the casting, along the back of the lead, with the whole of the front of the hand following the gutter and not the two points of the fingers.

Pushing up the run of a hollow is a different thing from pushing into one, and the difference shows in where the hand stops. Pushing into a space stops at whatever is at the back of it. Pushing up along the length of one stops where the length stops, and what stops it here is the end of the hollow and not a wall.

That hollow measures three inches across and a foot from the front of the gutter to the wall, and it runs the length of the last casting and then shuts, because past the fixing at the corner there is a downpipe and a hopper and no casting behind it at all. So the hand goes up behind the casting and comes to the place where the gutter ends and the downpipe begins, and the casting is under the fingers for as far as that is and then it is not there any more.

The back of the lead is soft. It is lead, it has been on that wall for a hundred years, and it has a wave in it that was put there by the man casting it and it carries that wave the same way it carries the one that came out of it lying in the sand. A wave in the back of a cast gutter is what makes the whole thing work. Water comes down a length of gutter along the channel of it and cannot get out sideways because the back of the gutter is corrugated in the opposite direction to the front, so the two faces never line up, and a cast gutter is stiff in that it does not move when a ladder is put against it and it is soft in that it can be dressed round a corner by hand.

The bracket at that corner is a cast bracket let into the wall, and it is on the front of the wall and not the back, which is why the last fixing screw has gone into mortar and not into a batten, and mortar is not a thing a screw stays in. A hollow behind a gutter that has come off a wall is a place water can get into from behind, and this one cannot, which is a thing about lead rather than about this gutter.

Lead is not a hard metal and nobody who has worked with it calls it stiff. It is heavy enough to stay where it has been put, soft enough to be dressed over a wooden mallet, and stiff enough along the length of a casting to carry itself when a man has one end of it and lets the other one go. It also goes on doing what it was told to do for a very long time after the telling, which is the other half of why an old gutter is still on a wall. That last casting is stiff its own length and soft across its width, which is what a sand cast thing is, because the sand broke down around it after four or five castings and the second casting in the same box came out slightly rough on the back.

The bracket at the corner is cast into the wall and has been painted over twice, and the paint on it has crazed where the weather gets at the top of it, so it is the brightest thing on the back of that house and it is the one thing about that gutter visible from the yard. Water has been going over the front of that length of gutter for a hundred years and it goes over it into the downpipe and down the downpipe onto the paving, and none of that water has ever been behind the gutter because the back of a lead gutter is not a place water gets into.

Nobody has been up a ladder at that corner since the wall was done and there is nothing up there that has been looked at, and the gutter has come away on its own over about four years by being full of leaves in the autumn and heavy with rain in the winter. The screws along the rest of the run are still in the lead and still in the battens, and they are stiff with rust at the shank, and the slots in their heads are all that still catch light.

Below the gutter the render on the wall is gone hard and gone out of colour, and along the course above the downpipe it has drawn back off the brick and left a gap behind it no thicker than the edge of a coin, and there is nothing behind that either.

---

Her sister is at the far corner of the yard where the wall returns, a few feet off the gutter, having come across the flags from the house, and she is not coming any nearer to it than that.

She looks at the roof edge for a while with her hands down, and once she turns her head about ninety degrees and keeps the rest of herself exactly where it was, and the yard goes on round both of them. Nobody has ever asked her about that gutter and nobody ever would, and the hand was down before she was at the corner of the wall. That space behind the casting is shut at the downpipe end and at the other end it runs under the wall into the void behind the lean-to, which is a second space and not this one.

Behind that gutter a hollow runs the whole length of the casting, a foot high and three inches across, with a wave in the back of the metal that was made in a mould, and it is read by a hand going up along it and coming out at the far end of it, which is the way in which a hollow behind a gutter is read.
