# Chapter 0941 — Down The Sunk Joint

Sunday the twenty-second of September 2024, at the line where the path has sunk at the kerb of number nine with the sun on the flags. Down an opened joint.

**SHE HAS A CONCRETE PATH AT NUMBER NINE THAT HAS SUNK AGAINST THE KERB AND OPENED A LINE ALONG IT, AND SHE MEANS TO PUSH THE HAND DOWN INTO THAT LINE WITH THE THUMB LAID ALONG THE NEAR EDGE, TO FIND OUT HOW DEEP IT GOES AND WHAT IS IN THE BOTTOM OF IT.**

The path was laid in three weeks at the end of April and it has been walked on since and swept since, and the kerb beside it was set at the same time. Three summers is enough to open a line like that one, and it has opened along the kerb and nowhere else.

It is a path of concrete flags about two feet by one foot, laid on edge, with a kerb of granite down both sides and the kerb at the garden edge keeping the path up about four inches above the soil. The opened line runs where the kerb and the outer row of flags ought to be touching and no longer are.

---

A joint opens and a joint closes and both of them are the same joint, so what the hand wants is which way this one went. So it goes down with the thumb along the near edge of the flags and the fingers following it. The top arris of the flags at that edge is blunt where the flags have broken away, and blunt in the way concrete goes blunt when the cement has gone out of the face of it and left the sand standing.

The line goes about a foot along the kerb and then closes to nothing. Where it is open it is a knuckle deep at the kerb and shallower towards the path, and at the bottom of it the flags' own bed shows: a sand and shingle bed about three inches deep with the sand gone pale and dry and the shingle still round.

A path of flags on edge is laid on a bed and not on the ground, and the bed is the reason a path like that one keeps its shape for a while and then loses it. A flag on edge stands on its short edge with its long faces vertical, and it bears down on the bed and not out to the sides, which is why a path of them does not spread sideways the way a path of slabs laid flat does.

The kerb does the sideways work. A granite kerb is a beam laid along the edge and it is what stops the flags walking out into the gutter, and this one is doing its job on the garden side and has nothing at all to do on the other side, because on the other side the flags are free to go where they like and go where they like they have.

Where the ground under the bed is soft the flags sink into it and the joint between them opens from the top down. Hard ground under a bed will not let a flag sink at all, and then the joint opens nothing at all. Three summers of rain have done the first thing under about a foot of that path. The rest of it is sitting on ground that has not shifted at all.

The sand in the bed is a river sand, and river sand is the wrong sand for a bed under flags because it has no edges to lock with. A bed laid with a coarse shingle locks and takes a load; a bed laid with sand goes down into itself the first winter and keeps going. Which is the whole reason the line has opened where it has and not somewhere else, and the kerb has not moved a thousandth and the flags have not broken.

There is one flag in that run where the line has not opened at all, and it is the one nearest the gate. It is a different make from the others. It is a hard grey and the thickness of a third of an inch more than the rest, and it was probably off a job somewhere else and put in to fill a gap, and its bed is the same sand as everybody else's.

That one flag is the reason the line has a beginning and an end. Before that flag the bed is one thing and after it the bed is another, and the line between the two closes as completely as the flag is wide.

The woman who does her shopping is standing in the porch of number nine with her back to the front door and her face to the lane, and she is not going in and she is not going out.

"There was a queue at the shop at ten."

"There is a queue at the shop at ten."

"Then they have put the bread on early."

"They put the bread on a Friday in July and nobody has told them otherwise."

She stands there a while with the sun coming through the porch and the lane on the other side of her, and she does not turn round towards the door, and the front door behind her stays shut the whole time.

A shop and a day of the week, that was all of it, and the hand was up out of that line and down at her side before she came round the end of the porch, and neither the kerb nor the bed nor the flag that was not like the others entered into any of it.

A kerb of that period is granite because granite came out of the ground in a lump and could be dressed to a line, and a line of granite keeps its top edge for a hundred years. A concrete kerb would have cracked in that time and the flags would have gone with it, and there are plenty of paths in this town with cracked concrete kerbs and a row of flags along them that have lifted and tilted into a run of little steps.

The kerb on this path was set on a bed of its own, and the bed under the kerb is the same sand as the bed under the flags because the man who laid it had one barrow of sand and no second load. That is a small thing and it is the whole of why the kerb is standing true and the flags beside it are not.

Where the flags have sunk, the sand has gone down with them and the kerb has not, and the two have come apart at the joint by about as much as the flags themselves have sunk. The joint has opened from the top down because the sand went down under the flags and the sand along the kerb is still up against its side, which is why the line is open at the kerb and closed two flags in.

Nobody swept the sand out of it and nobody pointed it. A pointed joint along a path edge is a joint that has been made to look better than it is, and the ones that are pointed along that path are the ones that have moved most since, because a hard mortar in a joint that has to move is worse than a loose one.

There is a difference between a joint that has opened and a joint that has been left open, and the difference is about four inches. This line has opened by the width of two fingers at the kerb, which is about half an inch of flags going down, and it is a joint doing what a joint beside a fixed kerb does when the flags go down and the kerb does not.

Along the rest of that path the kerb and the flags have gone down together, because the whole of it was laid on ground that was firm and has stayed firm. Nobody dug a trench along that line in the spring to put anything in or take anything out. The ground under that foot of path was simply softer than the ground under the rest of it, and softer ground under a bed of flags does one thing and one thing only.

The line keeps its foot and its three inches of pale bed and its hard flag at the end of it, and it is arranged in such a way that a path will go on opening along a joint it has no way of closing unless somebody lifts the kerb and takes up the whole run from end to end.
