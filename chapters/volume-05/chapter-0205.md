# Chapter 0205 — The Line

Wednesday the eleventh of November 2015, from about nine in the morning, a first-floor room over a shop in Tideglass, and then a building by the water.

**AT ABOUT NINE IN THE MORNING ON WEDNESDAY THE ELEVENTH OF NOVEMBER ALL SEVEN CLAUSES OF A DOCUMENT MADE IN THIS CITY ON THE THIRTEENTH OF AUGUST STOPPED BEING IN FORCE AT THE SAME MOMENT, AND NOBODY DID ANYTHING, AND NOBODY WAS ASKED, AND NOTHING WAS CANCELLED, AND THE LINE UNDER THE DATE ON PAGE TWO IS STILL EMPTY AND HAS BEEN EMPTY SINCE THE THIRTEENTH OF AUGUST.**

Jonas Mercer had a gas ring and one chair and a window that looks at the side of a warehouse, and he had made tea too strong, and at about nine o'clock he put the cup down on the boards.

There was no sound. That is the whole of what he could have put afterwards, and it is not much of a thing to put. What he could say was that something which had been on since the middle of August was not on, and that it had gone out of him all at once and without any particular moment inside it, the way a sound in another room goes out when the person in it stops talking.

He sat where he was for a while. Then he put his coat on and went down.

There is a building by the water in this city with a records room on the ground floor, and Nina Calder has been the records supervisor in it for nineteen years. She has refused seven things. She is on no committee and she has not been asked about any of the seven and she is not going to be. Jonas Mercer had not asked her for anything in a long time. He asked her, at about half past nine on the eleventh of November, for a book.

**AND THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE IN THE LOBBY OF THAT BUILDING THAT MORNING, printed and fixed to a post at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A LINE UNDER A DATE IS NOT A SIGNATURE.**
> **ARRIVING ON THE DAY IS A DIFFERENT ACT FROM PUTTING A NAME IN IT.**
> **NEITHER OF THOSE IS FALSE AND NEITHER OF THEM IS A DUTY.**

She had it on a hook behind the door and she took it down for him because it was in the way of the door, and she said she had never known anybody to read one, and she said that as a fact and not as a complaint.

"You've come on a Wednesday," she said. "They don't come on a Wednesday."

"I wasn't with anybody."

"You can look at it at that table," she said. "You can't have it out of the building, and I couldn't tell you that officially, because there is no official about it."

The working book is on a table by the wall. It is about two inches deep and it has a cloth back and the corner of the spine has gone. The register of narrowings is a different book, on a shelf, in a hand that has not changed since somebody in this city decided that a register was a good idea. Jonas Mercer did not open the register. He never opens it.

The working book has a column ruled in pencil with a heading over the top of it in the same pencil, and in the column there is a name, the hour the person was called, the hour they went home, and the thing the person was called for. Nothing else goes in it. That has been true since the thirteenth of August and it is the only part of that document anybody in this city has ever been able to get hold of.

He turned the pages back. There was a run of them from the middle of August to the last week of September, all in one hand writing quickly, and it went on and on, and it had not been emptied, and nobody had wiped it, and there was no line through anything and no note in the margin and no correction of any kind.

"It is not a field any more," he said.

"No," said Nina Calder. "It is a column with things in it."

**AND THE MECHANISM CAME DOWN IN ABOUT A QUARTER OF AN HOUR AND HE DID NOT GET IT OUT OF HER AND HE DID NOT PUT IT ANYWHERE ELSE, AND IT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAPPENED IN THIS CITY ON THE ELEVENTH OF NOVEMBER.**

"A clause that says an order is renewed, or is not renewed, needs a person," he said. "There is no body in this city that can require a person, and a thing that needs a person is not a thing a city can do by itself. It came off what it was standing on at nine o'clock this morning and it did not come to the end of itself, because there is nothing in it that ends. It has not expired. It has not been cancelled. Somebody would have had to cancel it."

"And nobody has," said Nina Calder.

"Nobody has. Nobody could have, without becoming a person who was supposed to have done something about a date. There are two people in this city with a department over any of this. One of them made it. The other one did not. Neither of them is going to be asked to sign anything and I am not going to ask them, and if I asked, then I would have had to explain why I had picked one of them, and there is no answer to that which is not me deciding that one of the two of them is the person this city ought to be frightened of."

She turned her chair round and got up and went through to the middle room, and came back with a cup, and he understood that the conversation was not going to continue and did not try to continue it.

"It is on a card," he said, to her back. "In a Methodist hall about two miles from here. Both halves of it on one card. A body that cannot require anybody cannot be told what it will do. That has been on that card since the spring and I have read it about nine times, and every one of those times it was a fact about a meeting I was not at. It is the first thing it has ever been the answer to."

She came back with the cup and put it down on the desk and did not sit down again.

**AND HE SAID THE OTHER THING, WHICH IS THE ONLY THING HE KNOWS IN THIS CITY THAT GOES ON BY ITSELF, AND HE SAID IT ONCE, AND SHE DID NOT WRITE IT DOWN.**

"There is a gate that goes up at seven on a Sunday morning and stops at six on the Sunday morning after that, six days long and not a week, with no date on it anywhere in this city. That has not been renewed once. It cannot be, because renewing it is not a thing anybody does, and there is no line under a date on it, and no page two, and no hand on it. It goes up because it went up last Sunday and it will go up next Sunday and it does not need anybody for any part of that."

He put his hand flat on the open page, on somebody else's handwriting, and did not say anything for a moment.

"That is what a thing looks like when it does not need a person," he said. "And it is not a moral advantage. It is only a thing that does not need a person."

Then he got up and went to the window and looked at the yard, which has three cars in it and a skip and a gate that was painted shut some years before either of them came to that building.

"And I have not had anything to do about clause six since the eighteenth of September," he said, when he had come back. "That is the only reason I had anything to do about it. Not because anybody asked me. Because it was in force, and the only thing anybody could do to clause six while it was in force was stop it, and you can only stop something that exists. I have been the only person in this city who could tell you which of two clauses outranks the other since the fifteenth of August. That is not a gift anybody gave me. That is a hole in a piece of paper and I have been standing in it."

"And now," said Nina Calder, "you're standing in a hole with nothing in it."

"Yes," he said.

He said the rest of it to the book and not to her, which is a thing he does.

"There is a man at the top of Peverell Street with a back that has been bad since the spring, and he came out on the eighteenth of September because a hand went on him, and nobody in this city has asked him one thing about that back since, and that hand is not on him now. It is not off him either. It is nothing at all. It is not in a field because there is no field, and he is not a man who has been let go, and nobody is going to be told that this is what has happened to him, and if I said it in a room it would sound like a man explaining why the thing he had been standing in had stopped, which it would have been."

A drawing on a wall, a two-page permission, a rain, a flood, a man going to another city and a man being found in one are all not reasons, and he had said as much to somebody in a room in this city in September and had not been argued out of it by anybody since. None of them is a reason for a register not moving, and none of them is a reason for a line to be empty, and nobody in this city has ever been obliged to do anything about any of it.

He shut the book. He did not write in it and he did not take it anywhere. Nina Calder did not get up to see him out and he did not wait to be seen out.

**AND THE LINE UNDER THE NINETEENTH IS STILL BLANK AND THE REGISTER IS STILL AT NINETEEN AND IT IS STILL THE ONLY THING IN THAT BUILDING THAT HAS NOT MOVED, AND THAT IS THE FIRST FINDING OF THE ELEVENTH OF NOVEMBER AND IT IS NOT A RELIEF.**

On the top of Peverell Street a man of about twenty-three was carrying a bag down towards the parade with his weight on the side that is not the bad one, the way he has carried it since the spring. Jonas Mercer came up the street at about eleven and went past him at about the width of a pavement and did not stop, and the man did not look round.
