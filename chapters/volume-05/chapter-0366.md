# Chapter 0366 — A Hearth Set

Saturday the fourth of November 2017, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past four in the afternoon, a first-floor room in a building by the water, and the landing outside it, in Tideglass.

**THERE IS A FIREPLACE TOOL SET STANDING IN THE MIDDLE OF THE FLOOR OF A ROOM THAT HAS NEVER HAD A FIRE IN IT, AND THREE PEOPLE ON THAT LANDING SAW IT BEFORE THE END OF SATURDAY MORNING, AND NOT ONE OF THE THREE OF THEM HAS SAID A WORD ABOUT IT, AND THE MAN WHO BROUGHT IT UP DID NOT KNOW THAT EITHER OF THE OTHER TWO HAD LOOKED AT IT.**

The building is a house that was cut into flats about two years back and it is four storey with a slate roof, and a stair runs up the back of it outside to a first-floor landing with a stair on down from there to the ground and out into a yard.

The woman of about fifty-five has the middle room on that landing and has had it since before the summer. It is a small room, about twelve foot by nine, and there is a bed against one wall and a heater against another and a window that looks up the side of the next building and gets about two hours of sun in the middle of the day in a month when there is any.

There is no fireplace in that room. There never was one. There is a chimney breast on the left-hand wall that goes up past the ceiling and the plaster on it is a different colour from the plaster on the rest of the wall, and the blocked opening is in the wall of the next room along, and you can see where the opening was from where the bed stands.

The room next door along belongs to a woman whose door does not shut over further than the width of a hand, and she keeps it like that all the year round and at all times in between, and she is in that room most of the day and the gap of that door is about three inch of nothing.

At the far end of that landing there is a man who has been in that flat about two years and who is not up before about seven.

---

She was in that room on the Friday night until about half past ten and the floor of it was clear from the door to the bed and along the bottom of the walls, because she keeps it that way, and there was nothing on it at ten o'clock on the Friday night except her boots and a heap of bedding.

There is a hearth tool set in this borough of the kind that stands up on its own. A shovel and a brush on a rail and a pair of tongs, all three on a stand about eighteen inch high with a foot at each end and a bar across the top, and the whole thing weighs about as much as a full kettle and can be carried in one hand by a man who is not thinking about it.

It was standing in the middle of that floor at about half past seven on the Saturday morning, about five foot in from the window and about two foot out from the wall with the chimney breast on it, standing square, with its shovel down on the left.

It was not dusty. It was not dusty at all. The rail was bright where it had been rubbed and the stand had two little rust spots on the front foot and one on the other, and the brush head was newish and pale at the ends of the bristles, and the handle of the shovel had a dark patch on it about the length of a hand.

There is one mark on the concrete under it, a clean half-moon, and the rest of the floor around it has got the grey film on it that a floor gets.

She came in at about half past seven, saw it from the door, and stopped in the doorway with her hand on the frame for about two seconds, and then she came all the way in and shut the door behind her and stood and looked at it.

She has never in her life had a fire. She has no grate and no chimney in that room and nothing to burn and nowhere to burn it, and the coal fire of her childhood was in a house that is not this one and is a long way from here.

There is nothing in that room that set wants.

She did not ask.

That is the whole of it and it took about four minutes. She walked round it once, the way a person walks round a thing in the middle of a floor of a room they know, and she looked at the clean half-moon on the concrete under the foot, and she looked at the window, and she looked at the wall with the chimney breast on it, and she did not touch it, and she did not go out of that room and she did not knock on anybody's door.

She was carrying two jugs, hooked through each other, at about half past eight, and was going down to the bottom of that outside stair and she stopped about two foot inside the door and stood there with the two jugs hooked through each other, and then she came back in and put them down and moved the jugs one at a time to the other side of the set, going round the outside of it, and then she put her boots on over the top of the other jug, and went down.

The woman in the room next door along had her own door standing open that morning to about three inch, as it is, and she was in that doorway at about ten past nine with a cup in her hand, and she could see across that landing and through the open door of the middle room, and the set was standing in there where anybody coming up that stair would walk round.

The two of them said nothing about it then, and nothing has been said about it at any time since, and the two of them have been on that landing together nearly every day since the summer and have said a great deal to each other about the price of things.

The man who has that flat came out of his door at about half past ten on the Saturday with his coat over his arm, and he had to cross the width of that landing to get to the stair, and he went across it the way a man goes across a landing in his own house, which is not looking at anything.

He saw it. He was carrying that set up that outside stair himself at about ten o'clock on the Friday night with one hand on the rail and one under the foot of it, and he had stood in that middle room for about a minute and a half and put it down in the middle of the floor and looked at where it was and moved it half a foot to the left and looked at that, and then he went back down and shut the door of that room and went out into the yard and up the side of the next building to a house in Cleeve Street and came back a quarter of an hour later and let himself in and has not been out of that building since.

He does not know that either of them has seen it. He has not been near that room. He has not asked her whether it is in the way, and he has not gone back in there, and there is nothing about that set that would make a man ask a woman in a room he is not standing in, because a man who asks that question is a man admitting he put a thing down in it.

---

At about half past three on the Saturday she wanted the wall behind the bed, because there is a mark on the wall where a picture frame was in June and she has been meaning to get the picture back up since the summer, and the bed is against that wall.

The set is standing about five foot in from the window and the bed is against the wall and the only way to get between the bed and that wall is the way it is in every other direction, which is round the set.

She could have shifted it. It weighs about as much as a full kettle and she has shifted a full kettle about four times a day for two years.

She stood in the room at about half past three looking at the two of them, the bed and the set, and she did not move it, and she has not moved it since, and she does not intend to.

It is standing in that room at half past four on that Saturday afternoon with a jug hooked over the top rail of it because there is nowhere else in that room to put a jug, and neither of them has mentioned the jug either.

**IT WAS STILL STANDING IN THAT ROOM ON THE MONDAY MORNING AND ON THE WEDNESDAY AFTER THAT, AND THE WOMAN NEXT DOOR ALONG CAME OUT ONTO THAT LANDING ON THE SUNDAY FOR A BAG AND STOOD IN THE MIDDLE OF IT FOR A WHILE WITH HER HANDS ON HER HIPS LOOKING AT A DOOR, AND THE MAN AT THE END OF THAT LANDING WENT OUT TO HIS BIN AND CAME BACK IN PAST IT AND SHUT HIS OWN DOOR, AND NOT ONE THING WAS SAID ABOUT ANY OF IT BY ANYBODY.**
