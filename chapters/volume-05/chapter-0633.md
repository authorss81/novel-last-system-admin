# Chapter 0633 — Every Door On The Row

Thursday the eighth of April 2021. She is on one road of houses in this borough from about ten in the morning until about six in the afternoon and she knocks at eight doors and she goes home and puts her shoes outside and sits on the stairs.

**THE WOMAN WHO SAID THE FIVE WORDS IN HER OWN KITCHEN ON THE FIRST OF MARCH HAS DECIDED TO GO ROUND EVERY DOOR ON THE ROW AND SAY THEY CAME OFF HER, AND A MAN IN HIS LATE THIRTIES OPENS HIS DOOR AND ASKS HER WHY SHE IS SAYING IT, AND SHE GIVES HIM THE TRUTH AND LEAVES OUT THE HALF THAT MATTERS MOST.**

She is a woman in her late thirties and there is a man in the house with her. She lives at the end of a terrace of twelve off a road that runs up off the top of a parade.

There is a man at the end of the row and she used to put his bin out on a Wednesday morning and then did not, and she did not stop it kindly.

---

Here is what she did, and in what sequence, and she has written it out in a notebook which is not a diary and is not for anybody.

She got up at about half past eight on the Wednesday and she wrote three lines and then she wrote them out again because the first three were about the man at the end of the row and she is not doing this about him.

What she wrote was this.

One: on the first of March she said five words in her own kitchen to the man in the house with her, about a bin, and she did not invent them.

Two: she has since found out that they are round. She has found that out on her own. She has not been told by anybody and no woman has come to the door and said it to her. She has watched it. She has been watching it since the second week of March and she can put a date to the first time she heard them outside her own kitchen and the date is not the first of March.

Three: they have been put on two people. One of them is a woman up the row who has done nothing to anybody and who has been telling her the woman two doors down started it, and the woman two doors down has not, and the other one is a girl on a different estate who has never been in this borough in her life and who cannot be got at.

And then she wrote the thing she actually decided, which is this.

I am not going to tell anybody where it started. I am going round the row saying I said it first.

And then she stopped, and she looked at that, and she added underneath: *and that is not the same and I know it is not.*

Here is the round, and it took most of the day, and here is what she got at eight doors.

One. Nothing at two of them.

Two. At four of them somebody let her in and stood in their own hall and let her finish.

Three. At two of them she was not let in and she said it through the door and it is worse through a door and she will not pretend otherwise, and she still said it at both.

And here is the sentence she used, and she had it written out and she did it off the page and then she did the last four off the page as well.

And she said, "It's me. The five words came off me. I said them in my kitchen on the first of March about a bin and I never meant them and they've been going round ever since."

And it is worth writing down what happened at each one, because she expected it to be the same every time and it was not.

At the first door it was nothing. The woman said "Right" and shut it and she thought nothing of it and she has thought about it a great deal since.

At the second it was nothing again.

At the third the woman said, "Have you been round the others?"

And she said, "I've been up to four."

And the woman said, "Right," and shut the door, and that one sat with her for the rest of the day because she had not asked the question she had expected to be asked.

One of them was a man in his late thirties, and that is the one this chapter is about, and she had to get through the rest of them before she got to him.

At the fourth the woman said, "I heard them off the woman at number eight."

And she said, "Number eight didn't say them first. Number eight said them in about the middle of March and got them off me, or off somebody I got them off. It's the same five words in the same order."

And the woman said, "Everybody's saying that now."

And she said, "I know. That's why I'm doing the row and not the whole street."

And the woman said, "You could do the whole street."

And she said, "I could. I'm not going to."

At the seventh a woman said, "Well, it's about time somebody owned it."

And that was the worst one of the eight and she has not been able to put down why.

At the last one a man said, "Is that right, though? Does he not ask?"

And she said, "No."

And he said, "No?"

And she said, "He doesn't ask anybody for anything. That's what people mean when they say it. And I have been in his kitchen and he has said please to me about the bin and about the lawnmower and about a lift in the rain, and I've never once told anybody that, and I'm not going to start now. I'm saying where the words came from. That's all. They came from a woman who was annoyed about a bin and not from anybody who has done anything."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "It's not a badgering."

And he said, "Nobody said it was."

And she got to number five at about half past four in the afternoon and she had been round the row twice and she had knocked at eight doors and had not been let in at two of them, and her feet were wet through at the top and her shoes were wet through at the bottom and she had nothing left to say and she had about a quarter of an hour.

He came to the door with a plate in his hand.

And he said, "No, it's not that, whatever it is."

And she said, "Can I say two sentences."

And he said, "Say one."

And she said, "The five words about a man never asking for anything came off me, and I said them in my kitchen on the first of March about a bin."

And he said, "Right."

And he said, "You've not come round here before."

And she said, "I lived here when you came."

And he said, "I know you did."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "That's it."

And he said, "That's not it."

And she said, "That's it."

And he said, "You've come round every door on this row on a Thursday afternoon and you're stood on my step and that's it."

And she said, "Yes."

And he said, "Why are you doing it."

And she said, "Because I want it stopped."

And he said, "Stopped where."

And she said, "At the person it's on."

And he said, "Which person."

And she said, "A woman up this row has been telling people for weeks that the woman two doors down started it, and the woman two doors down has not. And there's a girl on Kenner Street on the estate who's never been in this borough and three people have said it about her and none of them has ever spoken to her."

And he said, "You don't know that about the girl."

And she said, "I know it's going about. I don't know about her."

And he said, "Right."

And he said, "Come in if you're coming in."

And she said, "I'm not coming in."

And he said, "Why not."

And she said, "Because I'd want a cup of tea and then I'd be talking for an hour and I'm not doing this as a favour to you, I'm doing it as a thing I'm doing."

And he said, "Right."

And he said, "The woman up the row."

And she said, "The woman at number eight."

And he said, "Have you been to her."

And she said, "Not yet."

And he said, "Why not."

And she said, "Because I'd be doing it again. Because she'd have to hear why, and she does not need why off me, she needs to be told it's stopped."

And he said, "And you."

And she said, "And me."

And he said, "Is it about you."

And she said, "It's not about me."

And he said, "You said it in your kitchen about a bin. That's about you and about the man at the end of the row."

And she said, "It's about him not asking me."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "It is. I want that said. It is about a man who did not ask me for something, and that is the whole of the five words and everything else they've been hung on since the second week of March is not mine."

And here is the half she did not say, and she has not said it to anybody, and it is the half that is actually true.

It is not that she wants it stopped for the woman at number eight.

It is that she would rather a sentence went round this borough and belonged to her.

She has been carrying the true version since the second week of March, and the true version is that a woman in her late thirties said a thing about a bin in her own kitchen on a Monday morning because she was annoyed, and it went round four streets and out to an estate and got attached to two women who have never done anything to anybody, and that the sentence is now about people who have never said it and were never asked for anything.

And if she stands at eight doors and says it came off her, then it is about her.

And she would rather it was about her. She has thought about that on and off since the Wednesday night and she has not found a version of it she would be able to say to the man on the step at number five, and there is not one coming, and she did not try.

And the thing she was not going to tell anybody and did not tell him is that the woman at number six said a sentence out loud to the man in the house with her, and that a woman at number eight has been handing the same five words round this row since the middle of March, and neither of them knew it was a shape, and both of them have gone on carrying it afterwards.

And she is not going to be the one who says that either, because the minute she says it, it stops being a sentence about a man and becomes a story about two women on a row who passed something on, and then it is on them, and she has seen what that is like from inside.

So she said: because I want it stopped. At the person it's on.

And that is true. And it is the half of the thing that costs her the most and it is the half that does not explain anything.

---

He shut the door at about five and she went down to the end of the row and sat on the low wall by the parade for a bit.

Here is what she has.

She has knocked at eight doors on a Thursday afternoon and she has said the words at all eight of them and she is not going round tomorrow.

She has said where it started, and what she has not said is why she wanted to be the one with it, and that is the half that would have made the other seven doors shut.

And she has not been to number eight, and she is going to, and she does not know what she will say when she gets there, and she has known for about an hour now that she will say the same thing she said to the man with the plate, because it is the only sentence she has got that is both true and a reason.

And there are two women in this borough who have spent the best part of a month being said about in five words they did not say, and one of them is on this road and can be reached by walking up it, and one of them is on an estate about a bus ride away and cannot be reached by anybody at all, including her.

And she has done what she decided to do on Wednesday night at half past eight and it has not gone anywhere and it has not been allowed to go anywhere else.

She went home and put her shoes outside the door and sat on the stairs with her coat still on for about half an hour, and the man in the house with her came in at about half past six and stood at the bottom of the stairs and did not say anything for a while.

**SHE WENT ROUND EIGHT DOORS ON ONE ROW ON A THURSDAY AFTERNOON AND SAID AT ALL EIGHT OF THEM THAT FIVE WORDS CAME OFF HER, AND AT NUMBER FIVE A MAN ASKED HER WHY SHE WAS SAYING IT AND SHE SAID BECAUSE SHE WANTS IT STOPPED AT THE PERSON IT IS ON, AND SHE DID NOT TELL HIM THAT SHE WOULD RATHER IT BELONGED TO HER, WHICH IS THE HALF THAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE.**
