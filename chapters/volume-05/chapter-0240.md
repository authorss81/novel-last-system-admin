# Chapter 0240 — Four Minutes

Sunday the fifteenth of May 2016, from about half past seven in the evening, a hall off a parade of shops in Tideglass, and the corridor outside it.

**WITHIN FOUR MINUTES OF SOMEBODY SAYING THE WORD, THE ROOM SAID OUT LOUD THAT A COMPACT IS NOT A THING A ROOM CAN AGREE TO, AND WHAT THE ROOM COULD DO INSTEAD WAS WORK OUT WHO WOULD ACTUALLY BE BOUND BY IT, AND THE ANSWER TO THAT WAS A LIST OF PEOPLE NOBODY IN THAT ROOM HAD EVER ASKED, AND THE LIST WAS LONGER THAN THE ROOM, AND THE LIST WAS NOT SOMETHING THE ROOM WAS ASKED TO PRODUCE.**

The hall is free on a Sunday because nobody rents it and the woman with the key is in it and does not charge for the gas, and the meter takes pound coins, and she had put one in before anybody came because she had been doing that since the February and it was not going to stop now there were nine of them.

About nine people came. The women outnumbered the men, which they usually do, though not by much on a Sunday in June.

Mrs Obi, who is about thirty-six, sat at the front with her pad on her knee. She wrote the date at the top of a page and shut it again, which is the whole of what she has ever done to it in two years.

The man of about eighteen who washes up in a kitchen at the back of the parade was in and had been twice before. The young woman of about twenty who is at a college four days a week was in. A man from the works on the far side of the river came, because he comes when he can. The woman of about forty-two with the key was in, because it is her hall. Two others. A man from the parade. And the man in his sixties who is a building estimator by trade, who came to the first of these about six years ago and has been in both halls ever since, and who has never once been elected to anything, and who does the thing nobody else will do, which is to take whatever the room has been carrying for a month and put it into a shape.

He had it in a shape in under four minutes and the whole of it took him one sentence.

"A compact is not a thing a room can agree to."

He said it standing up, which he does.

"There's no such thing," he said. "I've been in rooms for six years that tried it and I have watched it fail dozens of times and it never fails for the reason people think. It's not because there's no constitution. It's because a room agreeing to something is the easiest thing in the world, and a room that agrees to something has agreed to a thing that is now the room's. The moment the room owns it, the room has to keep it, and a room that has to keep a thing needs somebody to be in charge of it, and then you have a committee, and everybody in this borough knows what a committee is."

"So what can a room do," said the man from the works.

"The other thing. Not better. Different." He turned round and wrote on the back of a handbill with the back of a pencil. "A room can't agree to it. A room can work out who would actually be bound by it. Those are the only two doors in there and only one of them's open, and it's the one nobody wants, because the answer's a list of people who've never been asked, and there's more of them than there are of us, and if the room spends tonight making that list then the room has spent itself on a list, and I'll not do it and I don't think anybody else in here should either."

"Will you make it then?" said Mrs Obi.

"No. And I want to be clear that that's not me dodging. That list is the only useful thing in this borough tonight and there is not one person in this hall who should be the one making it, including me, and if the list ever gets made it should get made by the people on it, one at a time, out of a conversation, over about six months, and by then it'll be a different list."

They talked about the documents for a while after that, and nine people managed in an hour what nine people always manage, which is to hold three things at once and lose two of them.

He put the version on the table anyway, because he is that sort of man and because the room had not heard it.

"Three of the six are hanging on a document," he said. "I'll say it because if I don't, somebody will, and it'll be worse. You take the sheet in a corridor in a city on the far side of the water and you take the four-line sheet on a wall in a town up the estuary, and you put them next to each other, and question four falls, and question five falls, and half of two falls. It is a beautiful argument. I made it on the eleventh of April in a different room and I have not been able to unmake it since." He put the pencil down. "I can't produce either of them. One of them has a man walking past it a hundred times a week and the other one has about two hundred people a day walking past it, and one of them is about four hundred yards from a gate that a man of eighty-six opens by hand at seven in the morning, and there is nobody in this borough who has ever asked him what time he opens it, and the other one is an estuary and a Tuesday away. That's all that's in the way and it has been in the way since I first heard it. I have said so in a room before and I'll say it again and I'll still not build a Sunday night on it."

"Then what have we done for two hours," said the man of about eighteen.

"Wrong question," said the young woman of about twenty. "We knew that at half past seven. We came to see whether anybody thought of a better way to be wrong."

---

The corridor outside that hall has a floor of a tile that was chosen in the sixties and has been chosen over about three times since, and Jonas Mercer stood in it with his coat over his arm from a quarter to eight until about ten, and he did not go in and was not going to, and the door was open about four inches, which it always is, because the fire notice is on the wall opposite and somebody has to be able to read it.

He did a sum against the wall with a biro on the back of his hand while he waited, and it came to five hundred and thirty-six, which is a line under the nineteenth entry of a register in a building by the water that has not moved since the September before last.

**AND THE GAS WENT OUT AT TEN AND SOMEBODY READ THE FIRE NOTICE ON THE WALL OPPOSITE GOING OUT, AND JONAS MERCER WENT DOWN THE STAIRS AND OUT ONTO THE PARADE, AND THE REGISTER IS AT NINETEEN.**
