# Chapter 0367 — Nine Words

Tuesday the seventh of November 2017, from about half past three in the afternoon until about eight in the evening, a room at the back of a shop off a parade, in Tideglass.

**A WOMAN SAID NINE WORDS AT A TABLE IN THAT ROOM AND TWO PEOPLE AT THAT TABLE HEARD TWO DIFFERENT SENTENCES IN THEM, AND THE ROOM DID NOT NOTICE, AND NEITHER OF THOSE TWO WILL EVER TELL HER WHICH ONE SHE MEANT.**

The shop shuts at about half past three and has shut at half past three on the same day every week since before the summer, and the room behind it has a table in it with four chairs round it and a hatch through the wall to the shop floor, and there is an electric kettle on a stand on the sill and a jug on the sill and a tin of biscuits that nobody in that shop touches until the room has filled up.

The woman who works in a shop had the shop shut by about ten past four and came through the hatch and washed the jug and filled it and put the kettle on, and she was in that room with her sleeves up over the elastics when the man who does that hall came in the back way at about half past four.

He comes in that way on that day every week because that room takes about six minutes off his walk to the bus and he has been doing it for about two years, and he said good afternoon and she said good afternoon and he sat down.

The man in his sixties came in the same way about ten minutes later, on his way to a stop, and he had been going into the back of that shop for about six years and sits where he sits.

The man in his thirties came in at about five for a bag of sand he had left on the shelf at the back of that shop on the Friday, and got the wrong end of the room, and was given the bag, and sat down at the table because there were three men and one woman in it and there were three chairs on the far side and one on the near.

---

The talk was about a man who used to come in on that day and has not come in on it since the summer.

Nobody in that room can say what he did. The woman who works in a shop knows he came in for years and that he stopped coming in and that he had a way of standing at that counter, and the man who does that hall knows he was on a football side and that he once carried a ladder up a street for the man who does that hall, and the man in his sixties knows nothing about him at all except that he used to be in the room.

He is not in the room. He is not in this borough. Nobody knows that for certain either, and the woman who works in a shop has said so twice and has not been asked to say it again.

The man who does that hall was of the opinion that he should be let to get on with it. The woman who works in a shop said that he had been getting on with it for years. The man in his thirties, who does not know the man and is on the wrong end of the table, said that a shop was a shop, and meant it, and went back to the bag of sand between his feet.

The man in his sixties said nothing at all about that man at any point, and had not before, and did not after.

And then the woman who works in a shop put her hand round the jug and said, "We will not be saying another word about it."

Nine words. She said it to the table and not to anybody in it.

The man who does that hall heard it about the man who used to come in on that day, and he heard it as a thing that had been settled, and it is what she meant, and he had about half a second in which to put back the thing he was going to add, and he did not put it back, and he has not raised it with anybody since.

The man in his sixties heard the same nine words about himself.

He is on nothing. He has been in the back of that shop on that day every week for about six years and has been on nothing the whole of that time and has never been asked to be on anything and has never been told anything about himself by anybody in that room, and there is no book anywhere with his name near it and there is no paper of any kind with his name on it, and what he has got instead is about six years of one afternoon a week, and being on nothing is not the same as being a man nobody has anything to say about.

He heard that he was finished with. He is not finished with anything. He heard that whatever had been going on round him, and it had not been going on, was shut.

He has not asked her a question about it and he is not going to, and there is no version of the asking in which it is a question, because the only way to ask is to say that he thinks she was talking about him, and a man who says that has admitted that he has been listening for it.

There is a thing he came into that shop on the Tuesday with, and it had been on him since about half past four, and he did not ask it.

He wanted to ask how many people come into that hall on a Wednesday. He has wanted to ask it since about the middle of the summer. He goes in on a Wednesday and he sits at the back and he has done that for about six years, and he has never once had any idea of the number, and he would not be surprised to be told that the number is five or that it is thirty, and both of those would tell him the same thing, which is where he sits.

He had got as far as the door of that back room twice. Once in September and once at the end of October, and both times the room was full and both times he turned round and went out, and neither time was because of anybody in the room.

He sat there that Tuesday with that on him and listened to about nine minutes of talk about a man who used to come in on that day, and at about ten past five he decided not to ask it, and he did not ask it, and he has not asked it since, and he does not know that anything was said at that table in the quarter of an hour before he came in through the front.

He is not a man who has been put on anything by not asking. He comes in on his own account on that day every week and he has done it for six years, and he is nobody's guest in that room and he has not asked anybody for anything in that time, and being on nothing is not the same as being a man who is owed an answer.

---

The man in his thirties heard nothing. He was on the wrong end of that table with a bag of sand between his feet and he was looking at the hatch and the shutter behind it and he heard a woman say something and did not take it in, and he put his hand on the table at about half past five and said he was going and picked the bag up and went out through the back way and shut it behind him.

The room did not notice. Two people at a table went quiet for the length of a jug being filled and neither of them said anything about the other one, and the third said nothing because there was nothing for him to say.

The man who does that hall stayed until about half past six and then said he was going to catch the six and went out through the back way, and the woman who works in a shop washed the jug and the kettle and the tin of biscuits and stood at the hatch for a minute with the light off over the shop floor.

She was back at that counter the next morning at about ten to nine and has been at that counter every morning since, and she has not asked either of them what they took it to mean, and she would not put it to them if she did, because a woman who asks two men what they heard is a woman who has admitted that there was more than one of it.

**SHE WENT HOME ALONG THE BACK STREETS AT ABOUT HALF PAST SIX ON THAT TUESDAY WITH THE NINE WORDS STILL IN HER MOUTH AND NOTHING BEHIND THEM, AND SHE HAS SAID THEM OUT LOUD ONCE SINCE, TO A MAN BUYING A WRAP, AND HE DID NOT LOOK UP.**
