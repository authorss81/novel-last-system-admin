# Chapter 1001 — Six Lines On A Delivery Note

Undated in this draft.

**THE OFFICE AT THE UNIT HAS ITS SASH DOWN AND A WINDOW LEDGE ALONG THE BOTTOM OF IT.**

The office is a room nine feet by seven off the yard with a desk in it, a plan chest, a stool, and one window that looks into the yard at the back of the next building. The sash has been down about four inches since the summer so that the air gets in, and there is a ledge along the bottom of the window about the width of a hand and a half, and the ledge is painted the same colour as the frame and is the only part of that room that a person could put anything on. It is the fourth dry day running and the light comes along the ledge in one strip and about an inch higher on the wall above it, and he has been coming in at eight and going out at the front and the ledge is where everything that has been in his hands for a fortnight has ended up.

A delivery note is on it. They come in pads of fifty from the office in the second bay and the sheets are good stiff paper with a line across the head of them for the yard and a column down the right for the money, and the pad at the unit has been used down to about the third of it since the spring because he keeps it for jobs that are too small to raise and the price of raising them is more than the jobs are worth. This one was the first sheet off the pad, and it is clean except for a pencil line he has drawn across it about a third of the way down, and on the back of it, upside down to the printed head so that it would come out the right way up if you turned the sheet, there are six lines in pencil in his own hand with a figure against five of them.

The six are the season. The step outside number two came up whole and went down proud at both ends, and he did it in a drizzle in about two hours, for nothing. The wall behind the range at number twelve, where the bottom corner is a half inch out with everything behind it and which he did not do because the sheet he set down about it became a sheet about the woman. The corner of the breast at nine, which he read with a lath and a mirror and did not touch.

The partition at ten, which came down in a day and moved nothing. The bottom rail of the back door at ten, which he measured with the fold of the rule and did not put a strip in because she would not have it. The window at number ten, which is still in its frame.

He sits down at the desk with the note turned round to him and looks at the figures. Two pounds ten for the step. Forty pounds for the wall behind the range, which he never gave her either. Fifteen to mend the corner of the breast, which is a figure for a thing he did not do. Sixty-five for the partition, which is the only figure on the note that anybody has ever paid. And nothing at all on the line for the window, because the window is not done and he has not begun it.

---

There is also the seventh thing and he does not put it down, which is that a sheet of paper with six lines and five figures on it is the most dangerous object in this office, because the woman who books work at the lock-up would read it in about four seconds and would read the same thing into it that he has been reading into it since Tuesday, which is that a man with a page full of jobs wants to be entered against them, and it would be true, and she would be right to refuse it, and the refusal would be right, and he would spend the rest of the season being a man who tried to get himself into a book on the strength of work nobody asked him to do. He puts the flat of his hand on the ledge and presses along it the whole way, frame corner to wall corner, the way you press along a lath to find where it is not solid, and at about two thirds of the length his hand finds the soft place, which is a thin patch where the paint has lifted over wood that has gone slightly back and never been made good because there is nothing in the office that anybody ever puts on that ledge. He has put the note on that ledge on four different mornings this week and taken it off again on four different mornings, and every morning he has read it and every morning the six lines have been the same six lines, and this morning he got to the fifth thing about the note, which is that five of the six figures are figures he asked somebody for, and not one of the five was asked for by anybody.

That is the arrangement, all of it. The step was his. The range wall was his. The breast was his. The partition was his. The door rail is still his, and he turned down the only offer of work anybody has made him in this street, because a strip of wood would have gone on the list and the list was never what that work was for.

He got to five numbers in six months by being the first one through a door, and the sixth line is a window in a room that has been shut all summer, with nothing against it, and the figure for the sixth is in a folded piece of paper in a woman at number ten's front room and came out of a tin that came back into the dresser in the autumn with exactly what went into it, and he cannot put that figure on this sheet because the moment a figure is on a sheet of paper it is a job and the next question about a job is who asked for it, and the answer for the sixth is a woman who had to work at being asked, and if he puts that on the note then the whole sheet is an application and not a list.

He folds the note in half on the line he drew, and then in half again, and puts it in the inside pocket of his coat where he has not put anything since the spring, and the coat is on the back of the door and he takes it off the door to do it, which is the first time this season he has put his hand in a pocket for a document rather than for a pencil.

The thing he cannot do is find a reason for the sixth line that is not her fault, and having failed at that he reads the five figures again and finds that he cannot find a reason for any of the six that would read properly in a room with the woman who books work in it, which is not the same as not having a reason. A man's season is not a bad season. It is a season made entirely of the right things done entirely the wrong way round. The ledge is bare in front of him.

---

He goes out through the side door into the yard at ten past ten on the fourth dry morning running, and he goes up the frontage to the lock-up with nothing in his hands at all, which is a thing he has not done since he was twenty-one years old, and the bag is on the bench under the roof light with the drawing-board on it and the corner of the board still off square where he has not had a minute to put it right. The window ledge along the bottom of the office window at the unit runs even, the whole length of it, except at the one soft place about two thirds of the way along, and there is nothing on it, and the light is coming along it now in a strip that reaches the far corner and stops.
