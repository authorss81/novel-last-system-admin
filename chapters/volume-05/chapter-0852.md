# Chapter 0852 — The Front Of The Drawer

Sunday the fifteenth of October 2023, in the yard at the unit from the first of the light until the store door has to be shut against the wind, and the drawer eased and waxed is the whole of that Sunday.

**THE FRONT OF THE BENCH DRAWER AT THE UNIT STICKS ON ITS SWOLLEN RUNNER AND HAS RUBBED A BRIGHT PATCH ON ITS LOWER CORNER AND HE MEANS TO EASE IT WITH WAX WORKED INTO THE BEARING EDGE WITHOUT OPENING IT, AND THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN GOES INTO THE CABIN AND COMES OUT WITH A ROLLED MAT UNDER HER ARM.**

That drawer is in the oak bench, under the top, on two solid beech runners let into the sides of the case. It was re-fitted in the spring when the oak was planed off the top and the whole bench came up a sixteenth.

It sticks.

It sticks at the end of the run and not at the start, and the front of it rubs the case as it comes in, and there is a bright patch about the size of a thumbnail on the lower corner of the drawer front where the paint has gone off down to the bare deal, and there is one the same size on the case below it where the drawer has been hitting.

The runner on the far side took up in September. Beech takes up with the weather and takes it up on the side that has been damp longest, which is the side away from the wall, and a runner that takes up a sixteenth in three months takes a drawer up with it until the front is out of true and it rubs.

He does not open the drawer. There is a bagged brush in it, put in there in the spring when it was cleaned and put back, and there are the spare shims from the batten work in the pocket of the front, and taking any of that out to get at the front means standing it on the flags of a yard in October while the front is off its runners.

He starts by working it.

---

Drawer in and out, in and out, the whole length of the run each way, and by about the fourth one the sticking is at the very end of the in-stroke and not through it. That is the runner, and it is where the swelling has got to.

Then the wax. There is a stub of it on the shelf above the bench, the end off a candle that went into a door in the spring, and he takes it down and scrapes a flat off one side of it with the blade of a screwdriver.

He puts the wax on the top edge of the drawer side, on the strip that rubs the case, and not on the runner at all.

That is the whole of it and it is worth saying out loud because everybody puts it on the runner. Wax on a runner is wax on a wooden slide in a wet yard, and in a wet month it goes soft and soft things gather grit, and by December the runner has a skin of the yard's dust glued to it and the drawer that used to stick at the end of the run now sticks halfway along it.

Wax on the bearing edge is a different thing. It rubs, and the rubbing is what wears the wax to the shape of the rub, and what is left after an afternoon of work is a line about the width of a knitting needle that is exactly the width of the rub.

He works it in and out and the wax comes off the screwdriver onto the edge in a curl and then, over about the next dozen strokes, off it and into the case and onto his fingers and off again. He works the top edge first and then the front corner, which is the worst of it, and then the whole length of the top edge again because the corner takes the wax off it.

The bright patch tells him where the drawer has been rubbing and the dark line tells him where it is rubbing now, and for about four strokes after he does a corner there is a mark on the case that has not been there before and then it stops.

The woman from two doors down goes across the yard and into the cabin while he is at that and comes out with a rolled mat under her arm and goes out of the gates, and the gate goes to behind her while he is working the far end of the drawer.

He works it out a hand's width and can see round the front into the case, and there is the bagged brush lying on the bottom of the drawer where it was put in the spring, with its bag knotted and the knot as tight as it was.

He does not put his hand in for it and he does not move it, and he puts the drawer back in.

---

He puts the stub of wax back on the shelf above the bench, and the screwdriver goes back in the tin, and the drawer goes home.

He stands at the bench when the wind gets up on the yard and looks down at what is there. A drawer front closing on its own without a hand on it, a dark line of wax along the bearing edge the width of a knitting needle, and the bright patch on the corner still there, because wax does not put paint back on oak.
