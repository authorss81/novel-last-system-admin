# Chapter 0463 — The Way He Crosses the Water

Saturday the twenty-ninth of December 2018, from about ten past nine in the morning until about a quarter past ten. The first ninety yard of a street off a parade, in Tideglass.

**SHE HAS WATCHED HIM DO IT TWICE, SHE HAS DONE IT ONCE AND SHE GOT IT WRONG, HE HAS NEVER ONCE LOOKED UP, AND SHE IS GOING TO KEEP DOING IT.**

There is a place at the end of that street where a low step crosses a run of water that comes off the roofs of a building on the far side, and it is about a foot wide and about four inch deep in the middle of that street and it is there from October until about March.

The water comes off a gutter that is on a building about two hundred year old and comes across the street at an angle and goes into a grating by a wall, and it does not run off the footway, because the footway there is about thirty year old and has been laid the wrong way up.

The step is a slab about two foot long set into that footway at the point where the water crosses, and it stands about two inch above the footway on the downstream side so that you go up onto it and down off it.

There are about thirty people a day who cross it. Nobody thinks about it.

---

The man in his thirties crosses that street most days of the week at about ten past nine in the morning on his way to a place off it, and he crosses that water the way a man crosses water who has been doing it since he was a child and has never once thought about it.

He goes up the step with the foot nearest the wall, so that the up-step is the one that gets the wet. He puts his weight on it as it comes up rather than after it is up, so the slab takes it and not his ankle. He comes down off the far side with the same foot and puts it down beyond where the water is running, and he crosses that water in about two seconds and does not look at it.

There is nothing in that. There is no trick in it. It is a thing a person does about four thousand times before they are twenty.

She has watched him do it twice and she did not know the first time was a thing at all.

She has the front room on the far side of that street from him and her window is directly across from the point where the water crosses, and on the second of December and again on the sixteenth she was at that window at about ten past nine with a cup and she saw a man in a dark coat go across that water in a way that was different from the way the rest of that street goes across it.

The difference is that everybody else hesitates. Everybody on that street puts a foot out and stops and looks and then either steps round the water or through it, and about two people a day get their shoe wet, and she has watched people stand there for a second and a half for years.

He did not stop. He did not slow. He went up and over it in one movement and kept walking, and she noticed it the way you notice a thing that is out of the ordinary, and then she went and finished her cup.

And then she thought about it about four days later, standing at her own sink, and she has been thinking about it every day since and has not told anybody she is thinking about it.

She did it on the Saturday morning of the twenty-ninth.

She went out at about ten past nine with about four minutes before she was going to be late, and she crossed that water the way she had watched him cross it, and she got it wrong.

The mistake is that she put her weight on the step as it came up, which is the half of what he does that is right, and she also came down off the far side with the same foot, which is the half that only works if the foot nearest the wall is the one going up, and because she is left-footed and he is right-footed the foot that came up for her was the foot that went down, and she came down into about four inch of moving water at the side of that grating with a shoe on.

She got the rest of the way across in about a second and she stood on the far pavement and looked back at the crossing and there was a man behind her at about ten past nine in a dark coat who was about nine foot from it and walking away up the street, and he had not slowed and he had not looked at the water and he had not looked at her either, and he had not looked up at any point in the whole of it.

He has never once looked up.

That is the part she has been carrying about for a fortnight and it is not the part that is hers.

She is not disappointed. She had not expected him to notice and she has thought about why not, and it is because a man crossing a street at ten past nine in the morning on his way to work is not looking at the water, and is not looking at a woman on the far side of it, and is not looking at anything except about four foot of footway and the place he is going.

And if he had looked up, then what would he have seen? A woman crossing a street she crosses four times a week, having watched him cross it twice.

She would not have minded that. That is the strange part and it is the part that is keeping her doing it. She would not have minded at all, and she would have said good morning, and it would have been nothing.

What she cannot get past is what he would think if he had watched her, and she has worked that out and it is not good, and she is going to keep doing it anyway.

He goes across that water without stopping. He has been doing it for nine years and he does it the way everybody in that street does a thing they have done since they were children. If a woman on that street watched him twice and then came out and did it wrong, and he happened to see it, then she would be a woman who watched him.

And a person who has watched a man like that is not a person you want to be, at half past nine on a Saturday morning, in a street where a man like that is a man about half the people in that street have watched at some point for nine years without ever having thought about it.

---

So she has decided to go on getting it wrong, and she has decided to get it wrong better.

She went back out at about twenty past nine the same morning with a plastic bag over her right shoe, and she crossed the same four inches, and she came down into the water again on the same side, and she has been practising since and has got the right foot across it four times out of about nine and is not going to get to nine out of nine and does not think she is going to be doing it for very long either, because she is not a person who crosses that street four times a day.

She is doing it because for a fortnight it has been the only part of her day that belongs to somebody else, and she is not going to be the one who stops.

**SHE WENT BACK INSIDE AT A QUARTER PAST TEN WITH A PLASTIC BAG OVER HER SHOE AND PUT IT IN THE SACK BEHIND THE SINK, AND UP THAT STREET A MAN WENT PAST A SHOP AT TEN PAST NINE AND DID NOT LOOK UP.**
