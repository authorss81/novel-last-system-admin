# Chapter 0424 — Ordinary

Friday the sixth of July 2018, from about twenty past six in the morning until about half past eight that night, a back room off a shop off that parade, and that parade, in Tideglass.

**THAT PARADE WENT THROUGH THE WHOLE DAY AND CAME DOWN THE OTHER SIDE OF IT, AND ONE THING ON THAT FOOTWAY WENT WRONG EARLY AND WAS PUT RIGHT IN ABOUT HALF A MINUTE BY A MAN WHO WAS CARRYING HIS OWN STEPS, AND NOBODY SPOKE TO ANYBODY ABOUT IT, AND THERE ARE STILL FOUR ANSWERS GOING ROUND THIS BOROUGH AND A FIFTH HAS NOT JOINED THEM.**

Off a passage at the back of that shop there is a room about ten foot by eight, and there is a bench along one wall with nothing on it and nothing stored under it, and a shelf screwed to the plaster above that bench, and a heater on the floor under the window that has been off since the middle of May.

The window is a sash and the bottom half of it has not shut properly since before Christmas, and there is a wedge of stiff paper jammed in the gap at the corner to hold it, and the room is about four degrees colder than the shop and always has been, and the two of them have been like that since the shop was fitted out in 2016.

There is no table in that room and there never was one. It is where she eats, standing up, most days, at about half past one, off a plate, with her back to that wall.

---

The man who puts shutters up came down that parade at about twenty past six with his steps over his shoulder. He was going back up it at about half past seven and the bicycle was across the footway in front of the shop next door, the front wheel well out and the whole of it standing about two foot off the wall in the way of anybody going along with a sack.

He put his steps down on the pavement, went over, took the bicycle by its carrier with one hand, walked it about four foot, stood it up against that wall with the front wheel in the way a bicycle is stood, picked his steps up again, and went on up the parade.

Nobody was on that parade at half past seven in the morning who saw him do it, or who has mentioned it since. The bicycle belongs to a man who works in the town eight miles off and who left it there on his way in on his bicycle. He came off it at about six that evening, stood in the road about a second looking for the footway, found it four foot further in against that wall, got back on it and went home.

She was in that room at half past six that morning with the light off and her coat on, and she did not see any of it, and it is the only thing that happened on that parade that day which is not in this chapter.


She opened at ten.

When she put her own shutter up at ten she could hear the man who does the shutters up and down the top of that parade with the last of his lot, and he did not come her way and she did not go out.

About nine people were out on that parade over the whole of that day. Most of them were on it for two or three minutes each and had come in for a thing they knew the price of, and not one of them was holding anything for any of the other eight.

A van stood outside the shop that shuts early for a few minutes in the middle of the morning with its hazards on then drove on out of this borough. A man who works nights came up that parade in the early afternoon, went into a shop at the bank end, came out of it again, and carried on up the road without stopping at any of them.

The hall at the bottom end of that parade runs off a meter, and it stood shut the whole of that day, and the key to it was in the key of the man who shuts it, and nobody went near it.

There is a clock in that hall that does the lighting for the whole building by itself, and it puts the lights out at four in the afternoon, and there was nobody in that building at four in the afternoon on the sixth of July, and nobody has ever been in that building at four in the afternoon on a day when it was shut, and nobody in this borough has ever thought about that.


There are four answers to one question, and they have been going round this borough since about the autumn before last.

There are still four of them. One of the four has been in more mouths than the other three and has been in circulation for about a year, and the other three have each been said a handful of times and not since February.

There is no fifth, and none of the four has been taken back by anybody, and not one of them can be laid alongside any of the other three, because they are not answers to the same sort of thing and anybody who tries it finds nothing to set them against.

Nobody in this borough has ever said, in any room or on any pavement or across any counter, what the four of them are answers to. Nobody has written it down. The question they are supposed to be answers to is still a question and has been one since before Christmas.

It is not a question anybody in this borough is putting to anybody else.


Back in a block by that water, behind a door which has never caught on its own latch, there is a shelf, and a book on the shelf.

The book has nineteen lines written in it and there is a space under the nineteenth. That space had been open for one thousand three hundred and eighteen days on the sixth of July, and it is open, and there is nothing under it, and nothing has been put under it this year or the year before, and nobody was in that room on that day, nor the one before it, nor the one after.

There is one woman in this borough who has ever held that book, and there is nobody else. She was not asked about it this week, she has not gone anywhere near it, and she is not going to. Nobody has asked her a word about it this week and she has not gone anywhere near it and she is not going to.


And there is one other thing in this borough that nobody here could put into words if they were asked to describe it, and it has not changed since the middle of June, nor since the turn of the year.

At the bottom of an unmade lane off a side street, against a wall that no made road goes past, a sash window is standing on its edge on a grass head, with the glass turned in, and a piece of old putty sitting on the bottom of the frame.

Nobody has claimed it. Nobody has asked whose it is. It came out of a house at the top of that lane in the June, and there was a skip outside that house for three weeks, and the skip is gone and the window is not, and there is nothing written on it and nothing tied to it and nothing under it holding it up but the ground.

It is still there.


**SHE STOOD AT THE END OF THAT BENCH WITH HER PLATE AND HER BACK TO THE WALL AND ATE OFF IT IN ABOUT TEN MINUTES, AND A LIGHT IN THAT ROOM WENT OFF AT HALF PAST EIGHT AND THE PARADE WENT ON.**
