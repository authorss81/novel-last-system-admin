# Chapter 0512 — Two in the Morning

The Sunday of the seventeenth of August 2019, from about half past four in the afternoon until about half past nine in the evening. Two buildings about four streets apart and the whole of a shift, in Tideglass.

**A NOISE WOKE ONE MAN IN ONE BUILDING AND ANOTHER MAN IN A DIFFERENT ONE ON THE SAME MAY NIGHT, AND BOTH OF THEM HAVE A TRUE ACCOUNT OF SOMETHING, AND THE FIRST HAS BEEN HANDING HIS ABOUT IN PIECES EVER SINCE AND ANOTHER HAS GIVEN HIS ONCE, IN JUNE, TO ONE MAN, AND THAT MAN HAS PASSED IT ON TWICE SINCE, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO HAS EVER ADDRESSED THE OTHER AND NEITHER OF THEM CAN FIND THE OTHER, AND ON A SUNDAY IN THE SECOND HALF OF AUGUST 2019 A THIRD MAN SAID THE OTHER HALF OF IT OUT LOUD AT A STEP WHILE LEAVING.**

They have never spoken. They have worked in the same trade in the same borough since about 2004 and they are not friends and they are not unfriendly.

The first one has grey at the sides and works days. The second is the sort of man who is a bit under fifty and works the other shift.

Between them they have been in both buildings and neither of them knows the other one has been in the other one.


The first building is on a street of shops and there are eight flats over them and a yard at the back.

The second is four streets off and it is a building of offices and a yard and a place where four people work nights.

That is the whole of the geometry and it is the whole of the problem, and it took twenty-five years to be wrong about and about five minutes in May to become wrong about.


Here is what the first man says, and he has been saying it since the middle of May in pieces, to anybody who will stand still.

There was a noise at about two in the morning in the last week of May.

He was up on the wall that divides the back of that building from the next one, with a cigarette he was not smoking, on his own, at about two in the morning, because he could not sleep in June and he has not been able to sleep in June for years.

And there was a noise like a bucket going down a stairwell — a bang, and then something else after it that was smaller and longer — and he went to the side of the building and stood under the front overhang with his back against the brick for about ten minutes and nothing came down.

And then at about ten past two a man he has worked with came out of the side door of that building with a torch and a bucket and went round the outside of it with the torch on the walls all the way round, and said, "You all right?"

And he said, "There was a bang."

And the man said, "There's a bit come off the top of the steps."

And he said, "In this building?"

And the man said, "Aye."

And that is the whole of what he says, and he has said it in that shape to about nine people since May, and it has never once been contradicted by anybody.


Here is what the second man says, and he has said it once.

There was a noise at about two in the morning in the last week of May, and it was not in that first building.

He was on a call at the second building, four streets off, on his own, at about two in the morning, and there was a bang and then something smaller and longer after it, and he was certain of the wall it came through.

What came down was about four foot of a ceiling in a corridor on the ground floor of the second building, and it was lath and not plaster, because he put his hand in it.

There is no lath in that building at all. He has been inside it about four times in fifteen years and every set of steps in it is brick.

He said all of that in June, in a place four miles off, to a man he works with, over about four minutes, and the man he said it to is a man who talks.

That man has told it twice since. Once at the end of a shift in about the middle of July and once at the beginning of one in the last week of July, in front of five or six people.

Nobody has connected either of those to anything else, and the man who has repeated it does not know that there is a first man with a version, and the first man has never heard the second version at all.


And here is what happened on the seventeenth of August, and it is why this chapter is the length it is.

The man who repeated it came up that street at about half past five in the afternoon to ask the first man about a thing that had nothing to do with it, a scaffold, a delivery, four minutes of standing about something else on a corner.

And he said it as he was leaving, on the step, and he said it in the way a man says a thing he has said before without knowing he has said it before.

He said, "We had a bit of ceiling down, in the end of May, in the middle of the night."

And the first man stood on that step.

He did not say anything for about three seconds.

And then he said, "Was it."

And the man said, "Four foot of it. Lath. On the ground floor."

And the first man said, "That's not our building."

And the man said, "No, it's ours. It's ours at the back."

And he said, "Ours is over the parade."

And the man said, "It's the one over the parade, isn't it? It's the one with the flats on top."

And the first man said, "Aye."

And the man said, "Then that's me."

And the first man said, "There's no lath in that building."

And the man said, "There is in the ground floor. There's lath everywhere in that ground floor."

And the first man said, "Then that's the other end of it."

And the man said, "Which?"

And the first man said, "I've been in it. Four times in fifteen years. There's no lath."

And the man said, "You've not been in the ground floor then."

And the first man said, "I've not been in it at all."


Here is what the man who repeated it did with it, and he is a decent man and he did it well.

He apologised there and then for about ten seconds. He said he had got it wrong and that he was sorry, that he had told it a couple of times and had not had it right the first time either, and he meant that, and the first man believed him.

And then he said: "It'll be at your end and not ours."

And the first man said, "Aye."

And the man said, "Our one was upstairs."

And the first man said, "Then you'll have said it wrong twice and not once."

And the man said, "I could easily have."

And he got into his car and went.


And here is what the first man has done since about five o'clock on the evening of the seventeenth of August.

He has not told anybody. Not his wife, not the man he works with, and not the man who was there in May with a torch and a bucket.

He has been through it about four times.

What he has is a thing that happened to him on a wall at two in the morning with his back against brick, and a noise, and ten minutes of standing under an overhang waiting for a building to drop something on him, and a man with a torch who said *there's a bit come off the top of the steps*.

And what he now has, at about nine o'clock on that Sunday evening, is a second noise. A different noise, in a different building, on a different floor, with different material in it, at about the same hour on about the same night.

And he is quite certain both of them happened, and he is now in the position of a man in this borough who has two things he has told people about and cannot tell anybody how they go with each other.

The second one came out of a man who has said it twice and who believes it and who will go on saying it.


And the man who said it twice has not thought about it once since the seventeenth.

What he took away from that step was that he had got a building wrong and had been asked to take it back twice, and that the first man was not annoyed with him, which was the part he did not expect.

He has told nobody about being taken back. He has a rule about that and it is a good one and it is thirty years old.

**A MAN CAME UP TO THE STEP OF THAT SHOP ON A SUNDAY IN AUGUST 2019 AND WAS TOLD THAT THE FIRST BUILDING HAS NO LATH IN IT ANYWHERE, AND ANSWERED THAT HE HAD NOT SET FOOT INSIDE IT AT ALL.**
