# Chapter 0835 — The Flag That Rocked

Thursday the tenth of August 2023, by the front gate at number twelve from first light until the sun has left the flags, and a loose flag laid in May is the whole of that Thursday.

**THERE IS A LOOSE FLAG BY THE GATE AT NUMBER TWELVE AND SHE MEANS TO PACK UNDER IT WITHOUT LIFTING IT, AND HER SISTER IS AT THE KITCHEN DOOR WITH THE DOOR OPEN AND DOES NOT COME DOWN THE PASSAGE.**

That flag is the first one inside the gate, square, laid in May with the rest of the run, and called sound at the time because it sat without moving under a boot.

It moves now. Put a foot on the outer corner and it dips, put it on the inner and it rises, with a soft knock under it that was not there in spring.

The reason is under it and not in it. Ground laid in May was damp from the digging of the drain run, and sand brushed into the joints went down soft and stayed soft while the wet was in it. August has taken the wet out of that strip by the gate where the sun gets in all day, and the sand has shrunk back from the rim of the flag by the width of a fingernail. A flag laid sound on a full bed plays on a bed with a gap in it, and this one plays.

She does not lift it. Lifting is a job for two with bars, and a flag up out of a run wants the run around it taken up too, and the run was laid in May and is not to be disturbed for one corner.

Packing is the trade for a thing that stays down. Dry sand worked in under a lip from the side, a little at a time, until the movement goes out of it.

She starts with a bucket of sharp sand, a trowel, a brush, and a length of lath to feel with.

The joint on the outer side is the one to work through. It is open enough to take sand, dark at the top where dust has gone in since May, pale further down where the original bedding still sits.

She brushes the joint out first. Dust comes up off it in a soft puff, grey, with bits of grit in it from boots, and under the dust the sand of May shows, dry and loose at the top.

The brush is for the top only. Down in the joint the sand has set where the wet reached it and stayed loose where air got in, and the loose part is what she wants, because packing wants a clean way in and not a plug of dust at the mouth.

She works the lath into the joint along the outer edge, flat, and feels under the flag. The lath goes in about two fingers before it meets the firm part of the bed, and along half the length it meets nothing at all, which is the gap the summer has made.

That gap is the whole job. A flag with air under half of one side rocks, and a flag with sand under it does not.

She spoons sand onto the trowel and tips it into the joint, a spoonful at a time, easing it beneath with the lath kept flat. The first lot disappears without raising the flag at all, which tells her how empty that corner was.

The trick is to pack without raising. Sand tipped in will sit where it falls and make a small hill under the lip, and the flag will sit on the hill and rock worse than before. Worked in with the lath flat, pushed back and forth, it spreads and fills from the firm part outward, and the flag takes the weight a grain at a time.

She does it in three rounds. Sand in, lath under, boot on the corner to test, sand in again where the movement still shows.

Her sister is at the kitchen door with the door open through the morning, with washing in a basket, and calls once about the gate.

“Leave the gate as it is till you are through,” her sister tells her.

“I have it tied back,” she tells her, and goes on with the lath.

The gate stands tied back to the wall with string, out of the way of the bucket, and the tie is the same loop of blue string off the heap of edge stones, borrowed for the morning.

She thinks about May while she works, not as regret but as weather. The run went down true on damp sand, every flag tapped level with the rubber mallet, every joint brushed full. A job done right on ground that has since moved is not a job done wrong. Ground moves and takes good work with it, and the mending of it is not blame but upkeep.

The bucket of sand sits heavy by her knee, and the trowel handle has worn smooth where her palm has been all summer on other jobs. Her back aches from bending over the joint, a dull pull low down that eases when she straightens to test with the boot. Age has put that ache there and work keeps it, and neither is going anywhere.

The basket stays on the step by the kitchen door, and the washing stays in it, and nobody brings anything down the passage while the bucket and the lath are in it.

After the second round the knock goes out of the corner. The flag still gives a hair under the outer edge, enough to feel through a boot and not enough to hear.

After the third it sits. Boot on the outer corner, boot on the inner, boot across the middle, and nothing moves under any of them.

She brushes the surplus off the joints and into the bucket, and brushes the face of the flag clean. Dust lies along its lifted edge where the joint stood open all summer, pale against the darker stone, and she leaves it, because a brushed joint wants a day to settle before it is wetted and there is nothing to wet it with.

---

The bucket goes back with half its sand still in it, and the lath goes against the wall, and the string goes back on the heap of stones by the gate in a loop with the ends tucked under.

She walks the run from the gate to the back door and back, putting a foot on every flag in turn. None of the others move. The May run is sound except for this one corner, and this one corner sat sound in May on a bed that has dried since.

She stands at the gate when the sun has left the flags and looks down at what is there. A loose flag by the gate sits without rocking, dust along its lifted edge, packing under one corner, and on the gate post beside it the lichen, pale and dry, that was there in May and is there now.
