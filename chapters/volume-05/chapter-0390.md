# Chapter 0390 — The Chair

Saturday the tenth of February 2018, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past nine at night, a front room off a side street, and a shop off a parade, in Tideglass.

**A MAN IN HIS FORTIES CARRIED A CHAIR INTO THE MIDDLE OF A FRONT ROOM OFF A SIDE STREET ON THE MORNING OF THE TENTH OF FEBRUARY SO THAT HE COULD REACH THE FLOOR BEHIND IT, AND HE WALKED IT BACK AGAIN INSIDE THE HOUR, AND HE FOUND THAT ONE OF ITS LEGS WORKED IN ITS JOINT AND THAT IT WAS SHORT, AND HE FOLDED A PIECE OF THE BACKING OFF THE RUG AND PUT IT UNDER THAT LEG AND KNOCKED THE JOINT DOWN WITH THE HEEL OF HIS HAND TWICE, AND HE TOLD NOBODY HE HAD TOUCHED IT, AND THE MAN IT BELONGS TO SAT IN IT AT TEN PAST ONE THAT AFTERNOON AND NOTICED NOTHING AT ALL.**

The house is the second of six in a terrace on a side street that comes off the parade a hundred yard back, and the man in his sixties has had the ground floor of it since about 2013 and there are two rooms on the ground floor and both of them are his.

The front room is about twelve foot by ten with one window in it and the back of a small fireplace in it that has not been lit since Christmas and a rug about nine foot by six that came with the house and is the only thing in that room anybody has ever been careful about.

The chair is an old kitchen chair with a wooden seat and a low back with three uprights in it, and it has a wobble in it, and it has had a wobble in it for as long as the man in his sixties has owned it, and he has never once mentioned the wobble to anybody.

---

The man in his forties went in there at about half past ten on the Saturday morning because the man in his sixties had asked him to look at the floor at the edge of that rug.

What had happened is that the corner of the floor by the fireplace had gone up about a quarter of an inch in three or four places, and the rug had a ripple in it across the corner, and the man in his sixties had been putting his tea down on that corner and it had been rocking.

He had asked the man in his forties about it at the end of November, at the shop at the bottom of the street, in about thirty seconds, and the man in his forties had said he would come and look and had not said when.

He came on the tenth of February, which is ten weeks after he said he would come, and the man in his sixties had gone out at about ten past twelve and was back at about ten past one.

He moved the chair first.

The chair was standing against the brick to the left of the fireplace with its back to the wall and it is the only thing in that room that is ever against that wall, and he moved it out into the middle of the floor with one hand, without dragging it, by lifting it at the back and the front and walking it across.

The boards under it had gone up because a joist had shrunk and the board nearest that brick had lifted at the end. He got the long thin bar he had in the van and he levered the board up about half an inch, and there was about a quarter of an inch of daylight under it along its whole length, and he cut a strip of the rug's stiff backing about an inch and a half wide with a knife, folded it in three, and pushed it under the board along its length with the blade of the knife, and put the board down on it and it went down flat and stayed down.

That was about eight minutes. He got his eye down on the corner of the floor and it was true all the way, and the ripple in the rug went out of it while he was looking, because he pulled the corner of the rug straight and the boards were back where they should be.

Nothing was wrong. There is no leak under that floor and there is no joist that has gone and nothing in that room wants doing.

---

He was putting the bar away with his back to the room when he saw the chair.

The chair was standing in the middle of the floor on that rug and it had a wobble in it, and he had been in that room about half an hour, and he had been in and out of that house maybe thirty times since 2016, and he had never once looked at that chair.

He went over to it and put his hand on the seat and rocked it. It rocked.

He found the front left leg worked in the joint with the back one, which is a thing a kitchen chair does when the glue has gone at that joint, and he took the leg between his thumb and two fingers and pulled and turned it and it moved about a quarter of an inch at the seat and stopped dead at the bottom, which is what a loose leg does.

And the leg was short. He put his hand flat on the seat and ran it down the outside of the chair and the front left corner came up about a quarter of an inch above the other three, and there was a piece of the rug's stiff backing about two inch square under two of the other legs, where somebody had been dealing with the same thing years before him and had put down a piece of the rug instead of a piece of wood.

He did not have a piece of wood about him. He had the bar, a knife, a screwdriver and a tape.

So he took the strip he had cut for the floorboard and cut another piece about two inch square out of the end of it, folded it once, put it under that leg, and then he put his heel on the floor and pushed down on the seat with the heel of his hand twice, hard, and the joint closed over the leg and took, and he rocked the chair and the wobble was gone.

He put the chair back.

He put it back against the brick to the left of the fireplace with its back to the wall, and he put it back the way it had been, and he was particular about that in a way he was not about anything else he did in that room.

The back of that chair had a mark in the dust on the brick about ten inch from the corner and the outside of the back leg had been sitting in a certain place on the floorboards for long enough to take a patina, and he put the leg back into the patina, and it was about half an inch out, and he moved it.

Then he looked at it from the doorway the way you look at a thing you have moved, and it was the chair, and the room was the room, and there was nothing on the floor and no mark on the boards and the bar and the knife and the screwdriver were back in the bag.

He was in that house from about half past ten until about ten past one and the whole of what he did in it was look at a floor that was all right and put a chair back where it had been.

The man in his sixties came in at about ten past one and put his bag down and took his coat off and sat down in that chair.

He did not notice anything. He has not noticed anything since and is not going to. He sat in it and read a paper until about two and got up and made himself something to eat at about half past two, and he sat in it again after that for about ten minutes.

The chair does not wobble. If he had been asked about it he would have said the chair has always been all right, and he would have said it in a voice with no doubt in it at all, because it has always been all right as far as he has ever been concerned, and a man who has been sitting in a chair that wobbled for five years and then finds that it does not is not a man who notices that it does not.

The man in his forties was in that shop twice that day. Once before he went up that street, at about ten past ten in the morning, and once at about a quarter past one when he came back down it.

The woman who works in a shop was on that counter both times. He bought a box of plugs the first time and a roll of something the second time, and both times she said what a shop says and nothing else, and both times he put his money down and waited for his change and went.

She has not asked him what he was doing in that house. She watched him go up that street at about a quarter to twelve with a bag, and she watched him come back down it at about a quarter past one with the same bag, and there was a man in his sixties in that terrace who had been out, and she put the two of those together in her own head inside about four seconds and then went on doing what she was doing.

Not one word of it has gone to the man in his forties and none is going to. She has not said any of it to the man in his sixties, who came into that shop at about ten past one on that Saturday for a tin of something and said nothing about anything else, and he would have said something to her about it if she had told him that a man had been in his front room that morning, and she did not tell him, and she will not, because the day she tells him that is the day it is a thing that happened in that house.

**SHE WAS AT THAT COUNTER AT TEN PAST TEN THAT MORNING AND AGAIN AT A QUARTER PAST ONE, AND HE CAME IN BOTH TIMES AND SAID NOTHING TO HER ABOUT ANYTHING, AND THE MAN WHO SITS IN THAT CHAIR CAME IN AT TEN PAST ONE FOR A TIN OF SOMETHING AND SHE SAID NOTHING TO HIM EITHER.**
