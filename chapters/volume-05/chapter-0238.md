# Chapter 0238 — Being There

Friday the sixth of May 2016, from about eleven in the morning, the room at the end of the first-floor corridor in a building by the water in Tideglass, and a doorway at the bottom of Peverell Street.

**WHAT A RIGHT OF RETURN IS CANNOT BE FOUND OUT IN A CORRIDOR, AND EVERY VERSION OF IT THAT GOT WRITTEN ON THE BACK OF A HAND IN THAT ROOM ON THE SIXTH OF MAY WAS WRONG IN A DIFFERENT WAY FROM THE OTHER TWO.**

The woman who has the room at the end of that corridor is about fifty-five and is in on a Friday and is not in on a Sunday for anybody, and on the sixth of May she had a stack of somebody else's post on the corner of the desk and had been in about a quarter of an hour when he came to the door.

"Ask," she said.

"I want to ask you something that isn't about the drawer."

"Then you'll want to sit down, because everything anybody asks me that isn't about the drawer takes longer." She moved a folder. "Go on."

"What's the difference between a permission and a right of return?"

"It's a permission," said the woman of about fifty-five. "That's the word for it if you want one in a hurry, and it is the wrong one."

"That isn't an answer."

"No, it isn't. Here is why it isn't. You have asked me how many ways a person is allowed to do a thing, and that is the same question about both of them, and they will give you the same answer, and you would take that as proof they're the same and you'd be right to, and you'd be no use whatsoever." She turned the folder over. "So ask me the other half. Not what a person may do. What happens to the person who does it. We'll get somewhere in about four seconds, and I can't tell you which of us will like where it is."

"There's a sheet on a wall in a town up the estuary with four lines on it and a man's name under the second one, and one of those four lines says that what he holds is a thing he does and not a place he stands in. Twenty-two years on that wall. Not reprinted, because there's been nothing to change. And I have had that sentence in my head since before Christmas and it is true and it does not help me by one single thing, because a man who is told to be somewhere has been given a place, and he is in it now, and he can't get out of it by coming back."

"You've thought about that a lot."

"I've got a piece of paper in a drawer I don't understand, and a second piece of paper with a list on it, and both of them came from somewhere and neither of them came from anybody in this borough, and that will do it."

"What would you have to write, then, if you were going to write anything?"

He had three in his head and he wrote them out on the back of his hand with her biro, which she let him do, and then she read them upside down, which is faster and is what she does.

"If anybody in this borough does any work again, it may be done by anybody, and the person who does it may come back." She put the biro down. "That's a permission with a bad end on it. A permission is what you write for a thing that has already been decided."

"If a crew goes out of this borough and comes back into it, it will be sent to what this borough needs first." She read that one twice. "That's a rota. That's a rota with no name on it yet, which is worse, because it's a rota waiting for somebody to fill it in, and the somebody who fills it in is the boss."

"And the third one." She put her thumb on it. "Anybody in this borough can be asked, in advance, to be at a place they are not at now." She did not read that one out loud. "That's a duty. Don't."

"Then all three of them are nothing."

"No," said the woman of about fifty-five. "All three of them are something, and all three of them are worse than nothing, and that is the answer to what you asked me, and it took four minutes and a biro."

And here is the other half of it, and she said that to the folder.

"There's nothing to write, because if you write it, it's a form, and a form has got a box on it, and the box has got a person in it, and then you're back to the corridor and the name under the line. And if you don't write it, then anybody can pick it up and do whatever they like with it, and in this borough that's the same as it being true, because the only thing that has ever held anything up here for any length of time is a thing nobody could pin to anybody."

Jonas Mercer sat on the edge of the desk with his hands on his knees and did not say anything for a while.

---

The bottom of Peverell Street is about a ten-minute walk from the water and it is where the road gives up and becomes three houses and a wall and then a bank, and number forty-one is a house with a doorway about four feet wide and a step worn into a curve in the middle of it.

There is a pump in that doorway. It has been there since the wet week in the November before last and it is not plumbed to anything and it runs off a standpipe in the yard at the back that a man put in himself, and there is a length of hose coiled on the wall beside it that has been there since the same November and has not been touched since the water went down.

Jonas Mercer stood in the doorway at about half past one in the afternoon. He did not knock. The door was open about a hand's width because it is always open that far, and the man at number forty-one was inside doing something with a radio.

He did not ask him anything. That was the whole of what he did on that afternoon, and he did it for about two minutes, and then he went back up the street to the shop on the parade and stood in the road with Hanna Wray, who had come out to smoke, and who asked him what he'd found.

"That," he said.

"That's a pump."

"That man is in that doorway when the water's up and nobody has ever asked him to be, and nobody has ever promised him anything, and nobody is paying him, and he took his own name off a board in a lock-up three years ago because he had been on enough lists. That is the whole of a right of return and it isn't a document and it isn't reciprocal and it isn't a promise."

"Then you can't write it down."

"No," said Jonas Mercer. "You can only ask somebody to be there, and you have to ask them on the day, and if you ask them in advance you've made a rota row, and the first person you put in that row is Tomas Renn, and the last time somebody made a list of this borough's crews it went to a county in a post."

"He said no to you on Tuesday."

"He said it to Nell Ardent on Tuesday at the counter, and he said it again to me on Sunday through a shutter, and the woman with the key had said yes to his face while he was doing it. He has not given me a reason once and I'm not going to make him give me one."

**AND THE PUMP IS IN A DOORWAY AT NUMBER FORTY-ONE AND IT RUNS OFF SOMEBODY ELSE'S STANDPIPE, AND THE MAN WHO PUT IT THERE TOOK HIS NAME OFF A BOARD AND WENT BACK INSIDE, AND NOBODY WAS ASKED AND NOBODY WAS PROMISED ANYTHING, AND A THING THAT MAKES A RETURN A RIGHT RATHER THAN A FAVOUR IS SOMEBODY BEING THERE WHEN IT IS NEEDED, AND THAT IS A THING TO BE ASKED FOR ON THE DAY.**
