# Chapter 0448 — Two Plates

From about ten past three in the afternoon on Tuesday the sixteenth of October 2018 until the light had gone, one building and the two streets that come off it, in Tideglass.

**SHE KEPT MAKING IT, HE TOLD HER ONCE THAT IT WAS NOT WORTH THE TROUBLE, SHE TOLD HIM TO MIND HIS OWN, AND SHE MADE IT AGAIN, AND ON THE SIXTEENTH OF NOVEMBER SHE CARRIED IT UP THOSE STAIRS.**

The man at the table has not got out of that room since about March. That is how she puts it and that is the whole of what she has said about it to anybody.

He is not ill in any way that has a name anybody in that building could use. He is up, and he is heavy, and he gets himself to the window on a stick and back, and he sits down again, and he has been like that since about March. He eats. He does not eat what she puts in front of him. That is the whole of it and it has been the whole of it for about seven months.

She cooks for him on a Wednesday and a Sunday. Not those days especially. She cooks for him twice a week because twice a week is what she cooks, and she cooks for him because there is nobody else in that house to cook for, and she has never once in seven months put a plate in front of him on a morning when she had not meant to.

The plate goes up at about half past six. It comes down at about seven. Sometimes he has had four or five forkfuls and sometimes he has had two, and once, in July, he pushed the plate away from him with the back of his hand and got up and went out of the room, and the plate stayed on that table until the morning, and she took it down and washed it and did not say anything about it to anybody then or since.

It is the same thing every Wednesday and every Sunday. She has made it about fifty times and she has not once changed it, and when she caught herself thinking about changing it in the March she went out and did not think about it again. If she changes it, then it is a new thing she is trying, and a new thing she is trying is a thing that has failed, and if it has failed then there is one less reason to make it.

That is the arithmetic and she has never said any part of it out loud to anybody, not even in her own head in words, and if you asked her on a Wednesday why she makes it she would say she is hungry and she is cooking for two.

What he does with it is the thing she has never been able to say to anybody either. He looks at it. He puts the fork down about halfway through and pushes it to the far side of the plate, so that it is clear he has stopped, and then he leaves it there for a while, and after a while he eats a bit more. On a good night she brings that plate down two-thirds gone. On a bad one she brings down what she would have thrown out.

She has never asked him if he would rather she did not. It has not occurred to her to ask, and if it had, she does not know what she would have done with a no.


The man in his forties came to that flat on the Monday of the fifteenth of October about a stain on the ceiling in the hall, which is a thing you can look at in four minutes and cannot do anything about in any of them.

She let him in. He was on a ladder for about three minutes and came down and said it was the tank above the floor of the flat underneath, which is the sort of thing a man says to be pleasant, and she said she knew, and he was going.

She had two plates in her hands at that point. She had the Sunday one under her arm and she had the Monday one in her hand with a cloth over it, going up to the table, and he said, "Are you doing him again?"

She said, "I am doing him now."

He said, "There's no point in that."

She said, "You mind your own."

He looked at her for a second and then he apologised, which he did not need to do, and he said sorry about the ceiling, and he went out and shut the door behind him properly, which he does not usually do.

---

She stood on that landing afterwards with a plate in her hand.

He was right. That is the thing she is carrying and it is not the plate. He was entirely right, and he knew he was right, and there was nothing in that sentence about her husband that was not true, and she would have said so herself if anybody had asked her, and what made it go into his mouth and out of it in that order was that she has been carrying it alone for about seven months and a person will take a thing off you if you let them.

And she let him, and she told him to mind his own, and she has been angry with herself about it roughly once a day for a month, which is the whole of what that Tuesday cost her and nobody will ever know any of it.

She went up. She put the plate down on the table by him. He looked at it. He did not push it away that night, and he had four or five forkfuls, and she has not been able to work out what to do with that either, and it has not changed anything about the Wednesdays or the Sundays.

---

He has been in that flat twice since, in the middle of the day, about something else. He has not mentioned the ceiling, the tank, the plates, the stair or the landing, and neither has she. Neither of them has said one word about that Monday to the other and they are not going to, and if the subject ever does come up he will find he has nothing to say on it at all.

Here is the part that undoes her, and it is not about him and it is not about the plate.

In the August before last she did not go up that landing with anything at all. She went up at half past six as she does and she said to him, out loud, in that room, that she was not doing it tonight because she had done something wrong with a joint, and she went back down and ate her own dinner standing at the side of that sink.

He said, "Right."

She has thought about that evening probably a hundred times. He did not argue with it and he did not say that was daft and he did not say anything at all, and if she had put a plate in front of him that night he would have eaten a bit of it, because he is a man who does not make a fuss about a thing anybody has bothered to do.

She put a plate in front of him the next Wednesday. And the one after that. And that is what he was right about and that is what she cannot forgive herself for, and it is also the only reason the plate is still going up.

And on the Sunday after the fifteenth of October she did something she has not told anybody and never will. She took his plate off him about ten minutes before he had finished, and she took it out of the room, and she did not empty it, and it went in the bin in that kitchen with about four forkfuls on it, and then she washed it and carried it back out, and he said nothing and she said nothing.

She is still doing it, and the plate still goes up at about half past six on a Wednesday and a Sunday, and the man at the table still eats what he eats, and the rest of it goes down the side of that sink in a bowl and gets washed like everything else.

**ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF NOVEMBER A WOMAN WENT DOWN A STREET WITH A CLOTH OVER A PLATE IN HER HAND AND DID NOT LOOK BACK AT IT, AND PUT IT DOWN BY A WINDOW.**
