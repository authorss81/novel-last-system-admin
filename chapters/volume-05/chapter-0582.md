# Chapter 0582 — Did It Get Any Easier

The Monday of the twenty-seventh of July 2020, from about ten in the morning until about half past four in the afternoon, on a towpath by the river, in a street off it, and at one door.

**HE PUT ONE QUESTION TO TWO PEOPLE ON ONE DAY AND GOT TWO ANSWERS THAT CANNOT BOTH BE TRUE OF THE SAME PERSON, AND HE HAS TOLD NEITHER OF THEM WHAT THE OTHER ONE SAID, AND HE HAS HANDED ONE ANSWER TO ONE OF THEM AS A THING ABOUT A STREET AND SAID NOTHING TO THE OTHER, AND THE OLDER OF THE TWO WENT BACK INSIDE WITH A THING THAT HAD BEEN TOLD TO HER AND HAD NOT.**

The towpath runs beside the water for the length of about six streets and there are three places along it where anybody who wanted to sit could. At ten on a Monday morning in July there was one person on one of them and a good deal of water.

He is a man in his late forties. He has lived in that town for about nine years and before that in two other places, and there was a stretch of about two years in the middle of it that everybody in the town knows about and that almost nobody in the town has ever put into words.

The two people are a woman in her mid forties who has been here all her life, and a woman of seventy-two who came here in about 1974 and has been in that house since.

He asked the first one at ten, sitting down with nothing else in earshot.

"Did it get any easier?"

She thought about it properly. She gave it the couple of minutes it deserved and then she said, "From about the May. It did."

He said nothing to that. He has been thinking about the May for about a year.

He has been asking people this question in one form or another since the February and he had four answers before these two, and he asked these two because everybody in that town had told him they were the two worth asking.

---

He knocked at number nine at about half past four and the woman of seventy-two opened the door before he had finished and said, "I saw you go past."

He asked her the question exactly as he had asked it on the towpath.

She did not take a moment.

"No," she said. "Not one bit of it. You get to know what to do and then it's not any lighter, it's just that you know what to do."

He said, "Right."

Then, because he wanted her to have all of it, he said, "Somebody on your street said it turned for them after the May."

That was not a lie. It was not even much of a shape. The woman in her mid forties had said it turned for her after the May, on the towpath, at ten that morning, and he had handed it to the woman of seventy-two as though it were a thing about the street.

The woman of seventy-two took that in and stood in her own open door with it for about as long as it takes to count to four.

What she took from it was that she was the one who had not managed it, and she has not managed it, and a man in his late forties had stood on her step and handed her that at half past four on a Monday afternoon.

"There's a lot of us found it hard," he said, which was meant to take the edge off it, and which put a second thing on top of the first.

"Mm," she said.

Then she said, "You'll have caught the noon one at the bottom of ours," which was about something else entirely, and he said he would, and she shut the door.

---

He did not go back to the woman on the towpath. That was deliberate and he had decided it on the walk back at about half past one, and the reason he had decided it was that if he went back she would ask him what she had said, and he did not have an account of that which was both complete and something either of them could carry.

So there are two answers in a town of this size that were given to one man on one Monday, and both of them are accurate, and he has not put them together and is not going to.

The thing about the May is that the woman on the towpath was on her own with it and the woman at number nine was in a house with a husband in it and three daughters and a job, and it is entirely possible that it got easier for one of them and not for the other, and he has made a shape out of the middle of that because the middle of it is easier to hold.

**ONE QUESTION WENT TO TWO PEOPLE AND HE TOLD ONE OF THEM HALF OF WHAT THE OTHER ONE SAID AND TOLD THE OTHER ONE NOTHING AT ALL, AND THE ONE WHO WAS HANDED SOMEBODY ELSE'S ANSWER IS THE ONE LEFT HOLDING A THING THAT NOBODY SAID.**
