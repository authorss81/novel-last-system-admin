# Chapter 0582 — Did It Get Any Easier

The Monday of the twenty-seventh of July 2020, from about ten in the morning until about half past four in the afternoon, at a river wall, in a street, and at one door.

**HE PUT ONE QUESTION TO TWO PEOPLE ON ONE DAY AND GOT TWO ANSWERS THAT CANNOT BOTH BE ABOUT THE SAME THING, AND HE HAS TOLD NEITHER OF THEM WHAT THE OTHER ONE SAID, AND HE HAS PARAPHRASED IT DIFFERENTLY TO EACH OF THEM, AND THE OLDER OF THE TWO WENT BACK INSIDE WITH A THING THAT HAD BEEN TOLD TO HER AND HAD NOT.**

The wall runs along the top of the towpath for the length of about six streets and there are three benches on it. At ten on a Monday morning in July there is one person on it and a good deal of water.

He is a man in his late forties. He has lived in that town for about nine years and before that in two other places, and there was a stretch of about two years in the middle of it that everybody in the town knows about and that almost nobody in the town has ever put into words.

The two people are a woman in her mid forties who has been here all her life, and a woman of seventy-two who came here in about 1974 and has been in that house since.

He asked the first one at ten.

"Did it get any easier?"

She thought about it properly. She gave it the couple of minutes it deserved and then she said, "From about the May. It did."

He said nothing to that. He has been thinking about the May for about a year.

He has been asking people this question in one form or another since the February and he got four answers before the two in this chapter, and he asked these two because everybody in that town had told him they were the two worth asking.

---

He knocked at number nine at about half past four and the woman of seventy-two opened it before he had finished and said, "I saw you go past."

He asked her the question exactly as he had asked it at the wall.

She did not take a moment.

"No," she said. "Not one bit of it. You get to know what to do and then it's not any lighter, it's just that you know what to do."

He said, "Right."

Then, because he wanted her to have all of it, he said, "Somebody on your street said it turned for them after the May."

That was not a lie. It was not even much of a shape. The woman in her mid forties had said it turned for her after the May, on a wall, at ten that morning, and he had given it to the woman of seventy-two as though it were a thing about the street.

The woman of seventy-two took that in and stood in her own doorway with it for about as long as it takes to count to four.

What she took from it was that she was the one who had not managed it. That the whole of that street had found a way through it and she had gone into a cupboard and stayed there. She is seventy-two and she has buried a husband and she has not managed the thing, and a man in his late forties had stood on her step and handed her that at half past four on a Monday afternoon.

"There's a lot of us found it hard," he said, which was meant to take the edge off it, and which put a second thing on top of the first.

"Mm," she said.

Then she said, "You'll have caught the noon one at the bottom of ours," which was about something else entirely, and he said he would, and she shut the door.

---

He did not go back to the woman at the wall. That was deliberate and he had decided it on the bus at about half past one, and the reason he had decided it was that if he went back she would ask him what she had said, and he did not have an account of that which was both complete and something either of them could carry.

So there are two answers in a town of this size that were given to one man on one Monday, and both of them are accurate, and he has not put them together and is not going to.

The thing about the May is that the woman at the wall was on her own with it and the woman at number nine was in a house with a husband in it and three daughters and a job, and it is entirely possible that it got easier for one of them and not for the other, and he has made a shape out of the middle of that because the middle of it is easier to hold.

**ONE QUESTION WENT TO TWO PEOPLE AND HE HAS TOLD EACH OF THEM HALF OF THE OTHER ONE'S ANSWER, AND THE ONE WHO HAS BEEN LEFT WITH SOMETHING NOBODY SAID IS THE ONE WHO WAS TOLD SHE WAS TOLD.**
