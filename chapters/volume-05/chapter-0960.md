# Chapter 0960 — Under The Wooden Step

Wednesday the fourth of December 2024, at the wooden step beside the front door at number ten with the flags wet under it. Under a wooden step.

**THE SPACE UNDER THE STEP STANDS THREE INCHES HIGH AT THE MIDDLE OF IT WHERE THE GROUND HAS NOT COME UP, AND IT IS SHUT ABOVE BY THE TREAD AND BELOW BY THE FLAGS AND AT THE SIDES BY THE TWO BLOCKS IT STANDS ON, AND THE HAND GOES UP INTO IT PALM UPWARDS AND IS CLOSED THERE, TO FIND OUT HOW HIGH THE SPACE IS AND WHAT LIES UNDER THE TREAD.**

The step is one board on edge and two blocks, fixed to the wall beside the front door of that house about eighteen months ago by a man who wanted somewhere to put a bucket, and it is on the outside of the wall where the render is old.

The blocks are engineering brick set in old lime and gone hard, and they were set at the two ends of the tread and nothing was set in the middle, which is the ordinary way a step like that is put up by one person working alone. The tread is a length of softwood about eleven inches wide and an inch and a half thick and it keeps its own middle up across a gap and its two ends only just, because a board of that width laid on edge is a beam and a beam is strongest along the way it lies.

A board on edge across a gap is the stiffest thing a person can put under a door, so nobody who has ever tried it has got one to stay there. The block nearest the wall was set about a foot from it, and the gap behind the tread between the two blocks and the render is about a foot wide and about eleven inches deep, and it is dark in there in the middle of the day.

There is a spider's web across the back of that gap in the corner, drawn from the render to the near block, and it is the old one and not a new one. The grit along the back of the space under the tread came off the render above it, one grain at a time, and there is more of it along the same joint higher up than there is in the space, which says that the space under the board has been collecting less than the wall above it. Under it, between the two blocks and the wall behind, is a space about three inches high at the middle and shut at the top by the underside of the tread and shut at the bottom by the flags.

The ground under it has not come up because the middle of the tread is only just clear of it, and the ground at the two ends of the step has come up and is in contact with the underside of the board at the ends and not in the middle.

---

The hand goes up under that board palm upwards, fingertips first, and they go further than expected, and then the hand is closed there.

An open hand is about eight inches across the knuckles and a shut hand is about three. A space three inches high will not take an open hand and will take a shut one, so the whole of what that space is comes off the closing, and not off how far the fingers could reach before it began to close them. The fingers shut and the ends of them find the underside of the tread, because a space three inches high is about the height of the two knuckles of a shut hand and not much above them, and the tread is there for the whole of the width of the step.

Under the tread, at the back, against the wall, there is nothing at all, and there has been nothing at it since the step went up. There is a dry leaf in the left-hand corner, flat down, with the veins still in it, and a pale drift of grit along the back where the render of the wall has shed the odd grain, and neither of them is under the middle of the tread.

The space is three inches high and it is the height of a shut hand and not of an open one, and the two blocks at the ends of the board are the height of the same thing, so that a hand put up anywhere under that board finds the same three inches. Nothing under it has been moved and nothing under it has been put there. Two blocks and a board is a stiff enough step for a person to stand on and a weak enough one for water to get under, and what is under it is what any floor of flags put down on sand in a garden keeps in two years.

The lime under the two blocks has gone hard and pale and is stiff enough not to have moved anywhere along them, and the flags the blocks stand on have not been lifted, and the bed of sand the flags were laid on has not been dug out at any point under that step. The render behind it is old and hard and has drawn back off the brick, leaving a hair of a gap behind it, and it has dropped three grains of itself into that space and no more than three. A board laid on edge and carried on two blocks is the step that gets put up when a person wants their feet off the wet and does not want anything made, and it is the first thing anybody adds to a house and the last thing anybody takes away. It has no rail and it has no return and it has never had a second one, and it is fixed to a wall that was rendered before it went up and has been painted once since. It is the one outside of the four that has nothing in it and nothing fastened to it.

The blocks were set before the board was cut, which is why the board is the length it is. The blocks go where the ends of the step are wanted, the gap between them is measured with a tape, nothing is added to the figure, and the board is cut. What is under the two blocks is old lime and is hard as a stone, and what is under the flags is the sand they were laid on, and neither has been looked at since the step went up and neither has any reason to be.

No packing of any kind went under that step while it was being put up. No wedge and no packing and no shim of any kind sits between the blocks and the tread, because the board was cut a quarter of an inch long and the two blocks were set an inch apart less than the board is wide, so the board is kept down on both blocks by its own length.

The door beside it is a boarded door hung on two butts and it shuts hard at the bottom corner, and the reason it shuts hard is that the step is in the way of nothing and the door is clear of it by an inch and a half.

---

Her sister is at the corner of the shed from the side path, with the shed on one side of her and the path on the other, going neither way, and her hands are in her pockets.

"There is a board up by the road about the buses."

"There has been a board up there since the summer."

"Then the buses are still going the long way."

"They go whichever way the board says they go."

They get as far as a postcard that has come for the woman at number ten, and where it came from, and whether the one before it came from the same place, and neither of them makes anything of it. The shed corner is still round the side of the house where they cannot see it, and both of them are looking at the far end of the path.

That gap under the tread is three inches high and dry and there is nothing in it but a leaf and a drift of grit, while the ground under that step will come up to the board another spring and take the three inches away again.
