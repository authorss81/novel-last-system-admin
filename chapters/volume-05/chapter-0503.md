# Chapter 0503 — She Would Have Kept On

Friday, the fifth of July 2019, from about half past eight in the morning until about quarter past one in the afternoon. A row of houses off a parade, and the length of a back alley behind them, in Tideglass.

**A WOMAN HAS BEEN WASHING ONE MAN'S CLOTHES IN A BACK ALLEY IN THAT ROW ON A MONDAY AND A THURSDAY SINCE THE SUMMER OF 2016, AND HE HAD NEVER ASKED HER FOR IT AND DID NOT KNOW IT WAS BEING DONE UNTIL, IN MARCH, HE PUT IT TO HER WHETHER ANYBODY WAS GIVING HER ANYTHING FOR IT, AND SHE ANSWERED HIM WITH A DIFFERENT QUESTION, AND HE HAS NOT PUT IT RIGHT, AND ON THE FRIDAY HE FOUND HIS OWN WASHING STANDING IN A BAG IN THE PASSAGE AND WASHED IT HIMSELF FOR THE FIRST TIME, AND SHE CAME AT HER USUAL HOUR AND TOOK IT AWAY AND BROUGHT IT BACK FOLDED.**

The row is nine houses and it is the back of a parade, and there is an alley behind it about four foot wide and about a hundred yard long, and there is a standpipe at the top end of that alley and a slab at the bottom end of it under a window, and there has been a crack in that slab since before either of them lived in that row.

She is fifty-one or thereabouts. She has been in one of those nine houses since 1991 and she is not the owner and has never wanted to be. She is at work four days a week and is in that alley on the other two and on the Sundays.

He is seventy-three or thereabouts. He has been in the end house since about 2004. He cannot manage stairs and has not been asked to and would not be able to if he were.

They are not related and are not, as far as anybody in that row knows, friends. They have been in the same alley at the same hour for three years without a week missed.

Here is the arrangement, and it is not an arrangement, and that is the part that has taken three years to establish.

It started because of a Sunday in the middle of 2016. She had been in that alley on a Sunday morning with her own washing out and it was coming down, and she brought hers in. And he was at his own kitchen window with a basket he had carried out at seven and left standing, because he could not get a line up to the right height on his own and has never managed it.

She did not ask him. She went and got his basket and put it through with hers and washed his with hers and hung his out and took it in and put it in his passage, and she knocked, and she said, "That's in your passage," and she went away, and he opened the door about ten minutes later.

And then it was a Monday and a Thursday, and it was hers, and he never mentioned it, and in all the time since he has never once asked her whether she was doing it.

He did not find out until the March of this year.

Here is what happened in March, in a kitchen, at about twenty to twelve on a Thursday morning, and it is about ninety seconds of it.

The kitchen is at the back of his house off that alley and there is a table in it with a cloth on it and two places at it and a third place against the wall, and he was sitting at the far side of that table with his cup when she came in with the basket on her hip, which she had been doing every Monday and Thursday since that summer of 2016.

And he said: "Is that all of it?"

And she said: "That's all of it."

And then he put his cup down, and this is the part that has not been put right.

He said: "Are you getting anything for this?"

And she did not answer for what turned out to be about four seconds, and in those four seconds she worked out that he had known, or had not known, or had known and had let it go on, and she had no version of her own ready for any of the three.

And what she said was: "Would I carry on with it if you never said another word to me about it."

And he said: "You could have asked me."

And she said nothing.

And that is the whole of it. She took the basket out to the alley and back again and hung the washing and came in and said nothing else then and has said nothing since.

Here is what each of them has understood since March, and they are not the same.

He heard: *I want you to say something about it.*

And what he took from that is that he is a man who has been getting a washing done for him since 2016 out of a woman who is fifty-one and who does not have anybody in that alley, and that the correct thing to do is to stop it, and that he stopped it, and that he has been carrying on being unable to say the word out loud ever since because the moment he says it he is a man who has taken something out of a woman's hands on a Thursday morning and given her nothing for it.

He has not looked for a way to pay her. He has not mentioned it to anybody.

She worked it out about a week later, on a Tuesday, doing her own.

What she took from what she said was not that. What she took from it was that he had heard *I want you to say something about it*, and that a woman who says a thing like that is asking to be asked to stop, and that she is not asking to be asked to stop, and that she said the sentence anyway because it was the only true sentence available to her in a kitchen on that same Thursday.

So what she is carrying is not that he has not said it out loud. It is that she said a thing which was true, and it was heard as a request, and she has let it stand, and she has let it stand since the middle of March, and she has not corrected it, and she has not decided whether she is going to.

And on the Friday of this week he did something that was not about March at all.

---

Here is what she found at about twenty to nine.

She came in at the back of the row at about half past eight the way she has come in three thousand and some times, and his back door was on the latch the way it is on the Mondays and Thursdays, and she went through into the passage and there was a bag standing on the floor against the wall.

It was the kind of bag a man buys by the dozen. It was full. It was pulled tight at the neck in a way that is not a bow, and it had been like that for some time, because the plastic had gone white round the neck of it.

It was about two weeks' worth.

She put her own basket down and stood in that passage and looked at it and then she went and looked at his kitchen from the doorway, and the kitchen was clean, which it almost never is, and there was a roll of washing line on the table and a stopcock with a cloth over it and the three lines were down and the line outside had been taken in and put away.

She went out and got the bag and took it up the alley to the slab at the bottom end, under the window, and she did the whole of it.

And here is the thing about that bag that she has not told anybody and is not going to.

There was a tablecloth in it, and a tablecloth is not a fortnight of one man's washing.

She washed it, because it was in the bag, and she did not look at it, and when she had it out on the line on the Friday afternoon in that July sun she could not help the fact that she knew what it was, and she stood and looked at it, and then she took it in and folded it and put it at the bottom of the stack, and she did not put it on the table.

The rest of it was his own and was in no state.

---

What happened at about half past twelve is this.

She took the whole of it up the alley and put it in his passage on the top of the low cupboard, folded, in a stack, the way she has put it there three thousand and some times.

He was at the kitchen table.

She said: "There's a cloth in there."

And he said: "Is there."

And she said: "I put it at the bottom."

And he said: "Right."

And then she stood in the passage and waited for him to say the other thing, and he did not say the other thing, and after about a minute she said it herself.

She said: "I wasn't going to stop."

And he said: "You never said you were going to."

And she said: "No."

And then she said: "It's all yours now, anyway."

And he said: "There's no need to say that."

And she said: "There's no need to do it," and she picked her basket up off the floor, "and I wasn't going to," and she went out through the back door and up the alley and was gone.

He sat in that kitchen for about half an hour. Then he got up and put the cloth on the table where it goes and looked at it and then took it off again and put it at the bottom of the stack where she had put it.

**A WOMAN PUT A STACK OF WASHING ON THE TOP OF A CUPBOARD IN A PASSAGE AT HALF PAST TWELVE ON THE FRIDAY OF THE FIFTH OF JULY 2019 AND SAID THAT SHE WAS NEVER GOING TO STOP, AND A MAN SAID THAT SHE HAD NEVER SAID SHE WAS GOING TO, AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID THE OTHER THING, AND THE CLOTH WENT ON THE TABLE AND THEN CAME OFF IT AGAIN.**
