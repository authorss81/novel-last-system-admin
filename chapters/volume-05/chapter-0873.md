# Chapter 0873 — The Slack Off The Heap

Saturday the thirtieth of December 2023, at the heap by the wall at number twelve from the time the shadow has come off it until the light has gone. The string over the top of it taken up in both hands along the ground.

**THERE IS A LOOP OF BLUE STRING OVER THE TOP OF THE EDGING HEAP AGAINST THE WALL AT NUMBER TWELVE AND SHE MEANS TO TAKE IT UP ALONG THE GROUND IN BOTH HANDS WITH THE SLACK GATHERED AND WALK THE HANDS TOWARDS EACH OTHER ALONG IT, AND HER SISTER IS AT THE KITCHEN WINDOW FROM OUTSIDE WITH IT UP.**

The string has been over that heap since the morning it was tipped.

Six feet of edging went against that wall in the spring under a string, and the string went over the top and down both sides and its two ends were tucked under the bottom of it, one at each end. That is how a heap of stone is stopped from spreading against a wall without a single thing being fastened to anything. The weight of six feet of stone is a great deal more than the weight of a length of string, and the string does not have to be tied to anything, because the whole heap is sitting on it.

That is the arrangement, and it has stood since the spring, and it is the reason that heap is a heap and not a spread.

She wants to know how much string there is in it, and there is no way to find that out except along the ground.

The string cannot be taken off, and the point of the string is that it is on, so the only thing available is to gather up the slack where it lies and run the hands along it, which is what she does on the gate side first.

She puts one hand down flat on the flags at the near end of the string, slides it along towards the heap until her fingers go over the string, gathers what comes up into her palm without lifting the string off the ground, and does the same with the other hand from the other side of it. Then she walks the two hands towards each other along the line of it.

Four feet of that is the length of the heap.

Then the hands keep going.

What is on the ground either side of that heap is about a foot of string each side, and it is not lying there because anybody put it there.

A loop over a heap is a loop, and a loop that has more string in it than heap has room for does not bulge. It goes slack in the one direction where there is nothing to push it, and on a heap against a wall the two ends at the bottom are the two ends where nothing is pressing the string down and nothing is pressing it up, and slack in a string goes flat. It always goes flat. A string with slack in it does not rise into a loop and stand up off the ground, it lies down, because a string that stands up is a string under tension and a string under tension is a string being pulled.

So there is a foot of blue string lying on the flags at each end of that heap, and it has been lying there for some months, and everybody who comes through that gate has gone round it or over it, and nobody has picked it up and nobody has thought about it.

Which brings her to the question she was actually asking, which is not how much string there is.

It is how much the heap has settled.

Six feet of stone went in against that wall in the spring. It is five foot six now. That is not a guess and it is not a number she made up: the string is the record of what the heap was and the ends of it lying out on the flags are the difference between what it is and what it was, and a hand's width of slack at each end is a hand's width of settling through the whole length, which is a foot.

And the string has not been re-dressed, because there is nothing to re-dress it against. A heap of edging is not laid in courses against a line. It is tipped. A tipped heap settles and bulges and the string that went over the top of it stays where it went, and the only thing that ever puts a string back onto a settling heap is taking the heap down and putting it up again, which is a job with a barrow and a bar.

Which means the slack is going to keep coming.

Six feet that has gone to five foot six in one summer is a heap that is still finding itself, because a heap of broken stone does not settle all at once. It settles where the pieces are smallest first. The ones in the middle of a heap go down before the ones at the ends, because there is nothing under the middle but other stones and there is a wall under the ends and the ends are also carried on one side by everything else in the heap. Another six inches over the winter and the two ends of that string will be a foot and a half out on the flags, and in a year they will be off the flags and into the path, and nobody will do anything about it then either, because by then it will be a loop of string in a path and a loop of string in a path is not a fault.

It is only a fault on the day somebody wants to take that heap down a course at a time, and on that day the string has to come off first, and on that day it will not matter in the slightest how much slack is lying about the yard.

There is a wear on that string and it is worth looking at before she puts it down.

Along the top of the heap, where the string is in the open, the colour has gone off it. Nine months of light and rain take the blue out of a tying cord and leave a grey that is not far off the colour of the stones it is over, so that the top of that heap looks untied until you are standing over it.

Where the string crosses from one stone to the next and there is a gap between two of them, it goes down into the gap for two or three inches, and down there it has kept its blue, because nothing gets to it in there.

So the string is a pale line over the top of the heap with about nine blue bits in it where the joints are, and it has been like that all the summer, and nobody in that yard has ever seen it because nobody stands over a heap of six feet of stone with their face six inches from the string.

There is a wear on it along the top as well that is not weather. A tying cord over the top of a heap of stone gets walked on once or twice and nothing else, because there is nowhere to walk on a heap, and the two small places where the cord has been trodden are at the two ends where somebody has climbed up the heap to look at the top of it, which is a thing that everybody does once and that nobody has done since the June.

Her sister comes to the kitchen window from outside about two and puts her face in at the opening and stands there.

They do not say anything. The window is in the side wall and the heap is at the other end of the house against the front wall and there is the whole length of the kitchen between the two of them, and neither of them has come round the corner.

The slack is a foot at each end and it is lying where it lies, because that is the shape a slack string takes and the shape is not a fault.

And there is a loop of blue string lying out on the flags at number twelve, and it will be walked on before the day is out and stepped over by whoever comes through that gate in the morning, and neither of them will know that it is there.
