# Chapter 0781 — The Kerbstone Across The Ramp

Tuesday the twenty-seventh of December 2022, in this borough. She is at her own lock-up and yard from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a kerbstone laid across the top of her ramp, and a step where there was a fall.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FORTIES HAS THE TOP EDGE OF HER RAMP CRUMBLED WHERE THE FRONT END OF THE TROLLEY HAS BEEN CHOPPING AT IT AND MEANS TO KNOCK THE CRUMBLED BIT OFF AND SCREED IT, THE WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK LAYS THE OLD KERBSTONE THAT HAS BEEN STANDING IN THAT YARD ACROSS THE TOP OF THE RAMP, AND THE RAMP HAS A STEP ON IT, AND EVERYTHING THAT COMES UP IT COMES UP IN TWO GOES.**

She is a woman in her late forties at her own lock-up on Tuesday. The woman who books work comes through the lane gate at the end of the morning and does the second half her own way.

---

Inside, the roller door is up and the bottom of its rail has not been brushed since the autumn. The drawer in the bench is shut with the leads and the screws in it, and the box behind the bench is where it has stood since the summer. Trodden flat into a crescent, there is still a heap of grit at the leg of the rack in the corner of the floor. Outside along the wall the four pallets are still up on edge where she put them in October and she has not looked at the top of one since. The trolley is in the bay along the far wall with nothing on the deck.

The ramp is the concrete that runs up out of the yard to the sill of the roller door.

It has been there since the place was built and it is a fall and nothing else, running the width of the bay door and getting shallower from the yard up to the sill so that the sill is level with the concrete inside. It is about the length of two men.

The top of it is gone.

Not the whole width. There is a band about the length of a hand across the ramp where the front end of the trolley has been coming up for two years, and the concrete there has gone to rubble along the edge of it. Not deep. It is about as deep as your thumb, and the edge of it is all loose and runs off into grit. Where the trolley's frame has been over it, the loose has been taken out of the middle as well, so there is a shallow channel along the top of the ramp where the wheels have been running in the same line for two years.

Her plan is the edge.

There is a lump hammer and a bolster by the roller door, a shovel behind them and a bag of sand down by the ramp, and she means to knock the whole crumbled band out, clean it back to sound concrete and screed it, so that the top of the ramp is one surface again from side to side with nothing loose on it for a wheel to catch.

The woman who books work comes through the lane gate near the end of the morning with her hands in her pockets.

---

"You are not going to screed that ramp."

"I am going to screed that ramp."

"You are not." She comes up the ramp alongside her and puts her heel on the sound concrete at the side of it. "A screed goes onto a floor that is keyed, and a key is what the trowel cuts into the slab so the slab grips the screed. There is nothing on a ramp to key into. It is the ramp and the ramp goes."

"Then what is under a screed."

"Nothing. That is the answer, and it is the answer I have just given you." She stands at the top of the ramp and looks down the whole of it. "You are screeding the one thing on this building that has no key in it anywhere, and when the wet gets under the edges of what you put down, the edges are the first thing to go, because that is where a screed is thin."

"Then what am I meant to leave there."

"Something with a nose on it."

---

The kerbstone has been standing against the yard wall since the beginning of November, on end, where the skip was when it was taken away.

It is a length of dressed kerb about as long as her ramp is wide and about as deep as her two hands laid together. It is grey and hard and it has got a slightly rounded top from being a kerb for a hundred years, and there are two shallow grooves along the top of it where cart wheels have run in it.

She does not go at it with a sledgehammer. She puts two lengths of batten under it, one at each end, and gets the end of a lifting bar under the middle of it, and they get it up off the ground and turn it and set it down on its side at the top of the ramp.

They get it straight.

She does not do this by eye. She puts a straightedge across the ramp from the sound concrete on one side to the sound concrete on the other and gets the top of the kerb down onto it, and she takes the crumbled band out from under it as she goes, with a bolster and the edge of a bar, until there is nothing loose under the stone anywhere.

"How deep is it in the middle."

"About as deep as a thumb."

"Then the whole middle of that ramp is a thumb lower than it was the day the place was built, and it has been getting that way for two years because your trolley has been coming up in one line and taking the middle out of it." She stands looking down the ramp. "So the top of your ramp is a trough. And you cannot screed a trough."

---

Then she lays it.

The kerb goes across the top of the ramp with its length across the ramp and its depth into it, so that the top of the kerb stands proud of the sound concrete at the side by about the thickness of two fingers, and its face is square to the side of the ramp and its top is true from one edge to the other.

They bed it on sand. She spreads a bed of it the width of the kerb along the trough, packs it down with the back of the shovel, sets the stone into it and brings the top of it true with a piece of batten. Then she knocks the ends of the kerb into notches cut in the sound concrete either side with a bolster and a lump of brick, packs the notches tight with rubble out of the trough, and lays two lengths of flat bar across the top of the stone and bolts them down into the ramp.

"You have broken the edge off the concrete on both sides."

"I have cut a notch for it to sit in. The stone does not sit on the top of your ramp, it sits down in it, and those two bars go across the top of it and into the ramp, and there is nothing loose anywhere across the top of that ramp now for a wheel to get into."

---

It is a good piece of work and it is the right piece of work and it does stop the crumbling, because the crumbling was being caused by a wheel running in the same line every day and there is no loose concrete now for a wheel to get into.

They get the trolley out of the bay.

The ramp is empty and the trolley is inside and it is the only thing in the building that goes up it, and it is the thing that made the mess.

"You are going to try it."

"I am going to try it."

She gets a sack on the deck and puts the sack against the back rail and pulls.

---

The trolley comes up the ramp well. It comes up with the sack sitting where she put it and the whole thing wants to go straight on into the bay, and about a yard from the top the back of it comes off the concrete and hangs, because it has hung there since the new caster went on in November and it hangs there now.

Then it comes to the kerbstone.

The front casters hit the face of the kerb and stop dead.

Not slowly. It stops dead a yard down from where it used to stop, the sack goes forward against the back rail and stays there, and she is pulled forward with it because she is on the wrong side of the handle. The front of the frame is up against the face of the kerb, the back of the frame is standing on nothing at all, and the whole trolley is pitched forward about a foot in three with the sack against the front of it.

"It has stopped."

"It has stopped. It used to stop up at the top and this is a yard further down."

---

They push it up. Both of them, a hand under the frame at the front and the handle at the back, and the front casters ride up over the two inches of kerb with a lurch and come off the top of it and into the bay, and the back of the frame drops onto its two casters with a bang you can hear across the yard.

And then they do it again with a second sack, because there are two to come in.

It goes up in two goes, both times, and it is two goes every time from now on, and there is no way out of that without taking the kerbstone out or taking a piece out of it, and the whole point of the kerbstone is that it cannot be taken out.

She stands at the top of the ramp with the trolley in the bay and a sack on the floor and looks down it at the step of grey stone right across the fall, and the ramp has a nose on it, and the nose is the best nose that ramp has ever had.
