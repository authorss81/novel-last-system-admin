# Chapter 0320 — Twice, and About a Month Apart

Wednesday the tenth of May 2017, from about half past eight in the morning, a lane behind a row of shops off a parade in Tideglass.

**A FOLDED METAL GREENHOUSE FRAME WENT UP ON ITS LONG EDGE AGAINST BRICK IN A YARD AT THE TOP OF THAT LANE IN THE FIRST WEEK OF APRIL AND IS STILL UP ON IT AT THE END OF MAY, AND THE SAME SORT OF FRAME WENT AGAINST A WALL IN A DIFFERENT YARD FURTHER DOWN THAT LANE IN THE SECOND WEEK OF MAY, AND THE MAN WHO PUT THAT ONE DOWN HAD NEVER ASKED ANYBODY ANYTHING ABOUT THE FIRST ONE AND HAD FORGOTTEN THAT HE HAD PUT ONE ANYWHERE.**

That lane is one shop wide and about a hundred and twenty yards long and it runs off the back of the parade between a row of seven on one side and a wall on the other, and there are yards off both sides of it behind those seven, and every one of those yards has a wooden gate on a latch and a roller door and about eight feet of brick between one gate and the next.

The frame is painted steel in three sections, about six foot by two foot each when they are out, and they fold into a bundle about six foot long, a foot wide and four inches thick, and there is a bent corner on the second section where somebody has backed a car into it, and the whole thing weighs about the weight of a man.

---

In the first week of April a woman put one against the gable wall of the yard at the top of that lane, at about half past ten in the morning, standing it up on its long edge against the brick with the bent corner out of the weather.

She had it out of a lean-to at the back of a house halfway down the lane. The lean-to was glass on a brick wall and it was going over, and she had taken the three sections off the frame and brought them out on a sack barrow in three goes because she could not carry two at once.

She stood the bundle up against that wall and looked at it and it did not look like it was going anywhere.

Her neighbour's gate was shut. The next gate along was shut. There was nobody in that yard but her and the man who does that lane with a barrow, and he was at the bottom of it with the wheelie bins and had been for about an hour.

Nobody asked her a single question about it. Nobody came out of a gate and said what are you doing with that, or where's that going, or can you shift it, and nobody rang anybody, and the two people who live either side of her were at work, and the one person who came down that lane that day saw a woman standing a bundle of pipe up against a wall and carrying a barrow back up the yard and did not stop.

She did not put a word in with anybody about it. She put it against that wall and she went to work and she went to work again at the same hour the next morning, and she has not been up that lane since, and five weeks later it is against that wall, and the man who does that lane with the barrow has gone past it about five times and has never once asked anybody anything about it, because it is not his and it is not in the road.

The three gates either side of it have all been open and shut on their latches all that time and the wall has had about three weeks of weather on it and there is green coming up along the bottom of it where the bottom edge is standing in a puddle that does not drain off that side.

---

On the Wednesday morning, the man in his thirties came along that lane at about half past eight with a bundle like it under one arm, and it went about six foot along the boards of his van and then he carried the last of it and put it against the brick of the yard two doors above the shop, on the side with the sun on it, and stood it on its long edge and leaned it about two inches off the wall.

He had it out of a back bedroom on that row on the Monday, where a woman was having a small bathroom put in and had said the frame could go when he had the old bath out, and it came out in three pieces and it came out flat against the wall of a back room for about eight months and it had the same weight as the other one everywhere in this town.

"Can't you put it on the roof?" said a man who was on the top deck getting off at the bottom of that lane, and then got on again and went, so it was not really said to anybody.

"No," said the man in his thirties. "There's about six foot of it and I've got trestles on the boards and there's a wind coming up that lane from the back of the parade that would take it off the roof rack and put it through the front of that shop."

He put his bag down and got a bit of a rag out of it and wiped the bottom of the frame, and the bent corner on that one was not bent, and he noticed that about a second after he had stood it up.

The woman who works in a shop had the back door open on the alley side of that shop for about four minutes on the hour, and she came out with a bag of peelings in it and the back door stood open behind her, and the yard she was looking into was the one the bundle was in, and she stood in the alley about ten foot off it with the bag in her hand.

The gate on that yard was on its latch and a folded green thing about six foot long was standing against the brick inside it.

"That yours?" she said.

"It's been put down," said the man in his thirties. "It isn't the shop's and it isn't got anybody's name on it and it's not going in that shop. I'm working in the row for the week. It'll be there a few days and then it'll go."

"Where do these things go from here," said the woman who works in a shop.

"Nowhere in particular," said the man in his thirties. "That's the honest answer. I know about four of them in this town now, and two of them are up a garden on somebody who has taken them, and one of them is in a garage being a bench, and the other one is against a wall about a hundred yards up that lane."

The woman who works in a shop did not go and look at the wall at the top of that lane, and she had not been up there since the week before last when she took a bag of something up to a house, and if she had she would have seen a bundle of pipe against a gable wall about five foot up and a puddle round the bottom of it, and she would not have known it was anything to do with the man in the yard below her shop, and he would not have known she had been.

"I've got a back gate and no room," she said. "I'm not having it on that alley."

"You haven't got it on that alley," said the man in his thirties. "It's in a yard off a gate. If it ends up on that alley it'll be because the wind gets at it in a gale, and if that happens I'll take it back and put it somewhere else and I'll not be asking you to hold it."

"Fine," said the woman who works in a shop, and went back in, and shut the back door, and did not look at that yard again from that window for the rest of the day.

He had forgotten about the first one. He put it down at about ten past nine and he was back in that yard two days later for about ten minutes, and again the week after, and the thing was there both times, and the only person in this borough who has ever been told anything about any of them is a woman behind a counter who was told a thing about one of them by the man who put it there, and she cannot use it for anything and did not ask him one question about the other.

**AND A FOLDED FRAME IS AGAINST A BRICK WALL AT THE TOP OF THAT LANE WITH GREEN COMING UP ROUND THE BOTTOM OF IT, AND A FOLDED FRAME IS AGAINST A BRICK WALL TWO DOORS ABOVE A SHOP, AND THE WOMAN WHO PUT THE FIRST ONE DOWN HAS NOT BEEN UP THAT LANE AGAIN, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED HER ANYTHING ABOUT IT.**
