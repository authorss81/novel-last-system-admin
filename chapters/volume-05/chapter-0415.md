# Chapter 0415 — The Lime

Tuesday the 29th of May 2018, from about a quarter to eight in the morning until about half past five in the afternoon, a street off that parade, and a shop at the parade end of it, in Tideglass.

**A LIME STANDS ON THE LINE BETWEEN TWO BACK GARDENS WITH ABOUT TWO FOOT OF IT IN EACH MAN'S SIDE, AND ON THE NIGHT OF THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF MAY A LIMB CAME DOWN ACROSS THE FENCE, AND IN THE SAME WEEK BOTH MEN DECIDED NOT TO HAVE IT CUT AWAY, AND NEITHER OF THEM KNEW THAT THE OTHER ONE HAD ANYTHING TO DO WITH IT.**

That street runs about ninety yard, with an opening onto the parade at the top of it and a brick wall across the far end where it dead-ends, and there are six houses in it and a lock-up at the back. The gardens are about thirty foot deep and the fences are close-board on concrete footings put up about 1968, and the concrete has gone in a lot of them, and two or three of those fences move when you lean on them.

The lime stands in the boundary between the fourth house along and the fifth, and it is about two foot inside each garden, and it came up out of a crack in the concrete footing between them about fifty years ago out of nothing anybody planted. It is a good thirty foot high and the crown of it is wider than the two gardens, and it shades both of them and the top nine foot of the fence between them for most of the day, and there is a great deal of it in the roofs of both houses.

The man in his thirties has lived in the fourth house since about 2014. The man in his forties has a job at the front of the second house along and has been in and out of that street every day since the Monday before.

---

The man in his thirties came out into his own garden at about a quarter to eight on the Tuesday.

He had heard it in the night. It is not a noise anybody could describe afterwards. It is a crack, and then a long tearing, and then a thump when the end of it came down on the hard standing, and then nothing, and he lay there for a bit after it.

He came out with his dressing gown on and had a torch he did not need because it was getting light, and he stood at the back of his own hut and looked at it.

The limb is about seven foot long and about as thick as a forearm at the break. It came off the middle of that crown and went across the fence, and it is lying with about five foot of itself on his own hard standing and the other end up in the air on his side of the crown, held at about the height of the fence, with the wood broken out into about nine splinters the length of a hand.

It has taken a length of close-board with it off the fence. That length is off its nails at the top and hanging out over his side at an angle of about thirty degrees, and one of the nails is bent double and standing out an inch, and the rest of the fence is holding the limb about two foot off the ground.

Nothing else is down. There is nothing on the roof of that hut. The washing line he put up in a yard in a different street in April is not in this street and this is not that.

He stood and looked at it for about two minutes and then he went in and got dressed.

Here is the whole of what he thought about it, and he thought most of it standing in his kitchen with his tea going cold, and he has told nobody any part of it.

He has a right to have it cut away. It is lying on his own ground and it is holding his own fence and it is in the way of anybody who goes down the side of that hut with a barrow, and there is nothing in the world stopping him from getting a saw out and having it down in an hour.

And he is not going to have it down.

Not because of the other man. He has seen him in that street twice and has never spoken to him and does not know what his name is, and if everything else in this were different the other man would make no difference to it at all.

It is three things and not one of them is the other man.

The first is that a lime cut hard in the last week of May does not put out a wound and close it. It throws out water sprouts the length of a forearm in the June and dies back along the cut in the second year, and every lime anybody in this country has cut in June has a dead limb in it inside a decade. He has watched that happen to a tree at a house he used to be at and he has not seen anybody do it any other way and come out of it.

The second is that he does not know where the line is. He has been in that garden since 2014. The concrete footing that fence stands on runs in a line through that tree, and the tree came up in a crack in it, and it is as likely as not to be wholly on the other man's side, and there is no deed he has ever seen and nobody he would ask and nothing in that house to be sure of.

The third one he did not put in words. It is that he is not going to be in that street in a few years, and a thing you leave in a garden you are going to leave is a thing you cut properly or not at all.

The woman who lives in that house came through her back door with a cigarette at about ten past ten and stood on her step and looked at it.

She said, "That's come down in the night."

"It has," he said.

"That's the tree that was in the old man's when we came."

"It is."

"Are you going to have it cut."

"No," he said.

She looked at it a bit longer and then said, "It's a shame, it's a good tree," and went in. That was the end of it for that day. She has not raised it since, she said nothing about who else might want it, and she asked him for nothing else.

---

On the Tuesday morning she was outside a shop at that street's parade end with a pallet on a sack truck, delivering to a business that is not hers.

She was on the pavement on the far side from those gardens, high enough to see the crown of it, the limb under it and the length of close-board hanging. She pushed the truck along the fronts and stopped at the door she wanted and went in.

At about twenty to twelve she came back out to the pavement, and thirty foot further up that street a man was in his own garden doing nothing much with a bit of wood, and she stopped.

She said, "That limb's across your fence."

"It is."

"You're not going to cut it back?"

"No," he said.

She waited a moment and said, "Right," and took the truck on up the street and left.

Nothing else was said to him about that limb that day. She did not put a second question about it, and put to her she would say he did not want it cutting and go on. She went up that street once after it, in the August, and did not ask him anything.

The man in his forties came into that street at about half past four on the Thursday.

He had come to look at the front of the second house along, where he starts on the Friday, and to measure the front door opening, and he came in at the blind end, which is not the end that house is at, and went down the side of the fourth house and through the gap between the fourth and the fifth where a gate has not shut since about 2015, and came out into the back of the fifth and stopped.

He was standing in a garden he had no business in and he stopped anyway, because there was a limb across a fence with a length of close-board hanging off it, and a man does not walk past that without looking.

He looked at it for about a minute.

He could see under it from where he stood. The end of it was clear of the fence by about two feet and was not going to move on its own, and the break was clean on his side and ragged on the other, which is what a lime does.

He has a right to have it cut away. He owns the fifth house and that garden, and about two foot of that tree is in it, and the other end of that limb is over his side of the fence and about four foot off the ground, and he could have it down in twenty minutes with what is in his bag.

He put his tape back in his bag.

The reasons are his own and he has them in order, and not one of them is the other man, and none of them is about the fence or about who took the close-board off it.

The first is a thing he has about everything: a thing in this world is either his or it is not, and that limb is not his, and he does not put a saw into a tree in a garden he has got into through a gap in a gate that does not shut.

The second is that he has done six years of other people's houses and he has watched every tree that ever came out of one, and there is not one of them still standing that the tree has been in, and he has never once known why and it has never once mattered to him until a man he does not know was standing in a garden about four foot off him, and it mattered for about two seconds and then it did not.

The third is that he has no saw with him and there is a saw in a van eight miles off, and he is not going back for it, and that is not so much a reason as a fact, and the fact and the other two point the same way, which is what happens most of the time.

He stood there another twenty seconds. He looked up into the crown of it, and there are swifts in that tree, and there are four of them going in and out of a hole about chest high on the trunk side of the fork, and he watched them for about twenty seconds and then he went round the side of that house to the front door of the second house along and measured his opening and went home.

On the Friday morning she was buying a length of something in a shop off that parade at about a quarter to nine and did not see him.

She was in that street twice after that, at the end of that week and about a fortnight later, and she saw a limb over a fence and a length of close-board hanging, and she never learned which side of that fence he had been standing on when he looked at it, and no one has told her and no one is going to.

**HE PUT HIS TAPE BACK IN HIS BAG AND WENT ROUND TO THE FRONT DOOR, AND THERE IS A LIMB ACROSS THAT FENCE AND A LENGTH OF CLOSE-BOARD OFF ITS NAILS, AND TWO MEN HAVE DECIDED THE SAME THING ABOUT IT FOR REASONS THAT HAVE NOTHING TO DO WITH EACH OTHER, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS ASKED THE OTHER ONE ANYTHING.**
