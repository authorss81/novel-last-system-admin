# Chapter 0330 — Three White Things

Wednesday the twenty-first of June 2017, from about ten in the morning until about half past four in the afternoon, a lane behind a row of shops off a parade in Tideglass.

**A HALF-HEIGHT GATE IN ITS CLEAR WRAPPER WAS LEFT AGAINST THE BRICK OF A YARD IN THAT LANE THREE TIMES IN TWO MONTHS BY THREE PEOPLE WHO HAVE NEVER MET, AND A MAN WALKED THAT WHOLE LANE ON A WEDNESDAY MORNING AND FOUND THAT TWO OF THE THREE WERE GONE, AND HE WAS NOT THERE FOR EITHER OF THEM GOING.**

The object is a gate. Not a frame and not a length of anything. It is a close-board gate, about four foot by three, painted white, with a frame and two ledges and a diagonal, and it comes from a merchant wrapped in clear polythene with a strip of brown tape round the middle of it, and in the wrapper you cannot tell how long it has been on a shelf.

There are four houses on the top half of that lane and yards off both sides of it behind them, and there is a shop at the bottom of it, and there is a car park at the bottom of that, and the lane comes out into the car park.

The man in his thirties counted three of them.

---

The first was on the Monday in the third week of May, and he saw it because he was up that lane at about ten in the morning for a job at the top house and had to walk past that yard to get to the side gate, and it was standing against the brick of that yard on its long edge with the brown tape on the face of it, and he stopped and looked at it.

It is not in a road. It is in a yard, behind a gate on a latch, and it is not on the house, and it is not on the shop and it is not his, and there is nothing there to ask anybody about. He went up the lane again on the Thursday of the week after for a different job and it was there. He went up again on the Friday of the next week and it was there.

The second was in the first week of June at a yard further up from the shop, in the one that gets the afternoon, and he saw it from the middle of that yard because he was in there about ten minutes looking at a fitting that did not need replacing.

He told the woman who works in a shop about that one, from the alley, on his way past, in about four sentences, and he said the whole of it: that it belongs to nobody, that it is not the shop's and not his, that it will be a few days and then go, and that it is a gate in a wrapper and there is no way at all of telling whose it is while it is wrapped. She did not go and look. The last time she was anywhere near that lane she was carrying peelings up to a house, and had she climbed it again she would have registered a white shape against a wall behind a gate and nothing else about it, and the man who had left it would never have found out that she had been anywhere near.

The third was on the Monday, the nineteenth of June, at the yard on the left of the lane where the car park is, and he saw it at about four in the afternoon because he had been to the car park in his van and came out of it up the lane on foot.

He came down that lane on the Wednesday morning about ten past ten and he stopped.

The third one was gone.

The bottom yard, the one on the left where the car park comes out, is about nine foot across and the wall on the right of it is the side of a garage that is locked, and there was a wet rectangle on that brick about four foot long and a hand wide, and there was a scuff of white on the brick about a foot above the wet, and there was a scrap of clear polythene about the size of a handkerchief on the top of the kerbstone inside the gate.

The second one was still there. That yard is two doors above the shop and it is on the sunny side and the white of it had gone a dirty grey on the side that faces the wall, and the tape round the middle of it had gone brown and the polythene on the top edge had gone brittle and come away from the frame in a strip about a foot long and was sitting on top of the gate like a rolled thing.

He went up to the top.

The first one was gone as well. That yard is the top one on the left, and the brick it was against is a gable wall about four foot of a return, and there is a puddle at the foot of that wall that has not moved since before Christmas, and there is a green mark on the wall about the width of his two hands and the height of the bottom ledge, and there is nothing else at all.

He stood in that yard for a bit, and then he stood in the middle of the lane, and then he said a number out loud, to himself, on a Wednesday morning in a lane nobody is walking down.

"Three," he said.

---

He had never said a number about anything of that kind out loud to anybody in his life, and he did not know that he had three until that morning, and the three had been in his head in about that shape since the Monday, without being in words.

The first is the one that was there for four weeks. The second is the one that is there now. The third is the one that was there on Monday afternoon and is not there on Wednesday morning.

And there is no fourth, and there has never been a fourth that he has seen, and nobody in this borough has ever mentioned a white wrapped thing to him except the woman who works in a shop, and what she said was that he had told her about it.

He thought about writing it down. He has a pad in the van with about nine lines used in it in six months and a great deal of it is measurements off other people's houses with the address of the other people's house nowhere on the same line. He got into the van and put the pad on the boards and shut it again, because there is nowhere in this town to put a pad with a count on it in it, and a piece of paper in a van with three things on it is a piece of paper that gets put somewhere, and where it gets put is not a thing he can stand behind on a Wednesday.

The woman who works in a shop was in that parade at about half past four and he came by the front of it and she was on a step with a box of something at her feet.

"I counted them," he said.

"Counted what."

"The white things," said the man in his thirties. "I've seen three. Three since the middle of May, three yards on this one lane, and I have not seen any of them being put down by anybody. The first was at the top for about four weeks and the third was at the bottom on Monday afternoon, and today the first and the third are gone and the second's still in that yard and it's gone a colour."

She put the box down.

"You counted them."

"I counted them on that lane this morning with my mouth," said the man in his thirties. "That is the whole of the counting. There is nothing on anything anywhere that says how many of them there are in this town, and I know that because I have looked, which is not a thing a person says."

"What happened to the two."

"I have no idea," said the man in his thirties. "They were not in the road. They were not in a gateway anybody locks. Nobody in this lane has a gate that isn't a latch, and nobody in that lane asks anybody anything, and I have not asked anybody anything and I am not going to."

"And you could ask."

"I could ask one person about one of them and I'd get one answer off one person about one day, and then I'd have a thing I can't stand up next to the other two," said the man in his thirties. "I've been in this borough long enough to know what happens when one of us starts saying out loud what another one of us did. The first time somebody says a thing out loud, it's the second time somebody's asked whether it was really like that, and then it's a row about somebody's bin."

She picked the box up again.

"There was a man with a barrow on that lane at about six in the morning on the Tuesday," said the woman who works in a shop. "I was at my window. He went up the lane with a white thing on the barrow and I could see the middle of it and I could see the brown tape, and he did not stop anywhere and he went to the top of the lane and I could not see him come back because of the wall of the garage, and he came back with an empty barrow about ten minutes later and he went out past the car park."

"You were at your window."

"I was at my window at six in the morning because I have been up since half past four, and it was a nice morning and the blind was up because the blind stays up, and I saw him, and I did not go out, and I did not say anything to anybody about it, and I would not have said anything to him either if he had come through that shop."

The man in his thirties stood on that step.

"You saw it go."

"I saw a white thing go up that lane on a barrow."

"That's three then," he said, "and that's two, and neither of those is the same number as the other one, and there is nobody in this town who could stand in that lane and tell you which of those two numbers is the true one, and the only person in this borough who saw one of them go is standing in a shop doorway telling me about it and did not tell me at the time."

"Do you want me to say I told you."

"No," said the man in his thirties. "If you say that, then you've been asked something about it, and then next time you see that lane you're a woman watching a lane."

The woman who works in a shop went to put her box in the cold room and came back and shut the front and put the shutter half down, which she does when the light goes.

"There's a bit of that polythene on the kerb inside the bottom gate," she said. "I saw it off the top of the lane when I was dragging my bin out. I didn't go and get it."

"That's not the same as the other two," said the man in his thirties. "The other two are a whole gate each, and that's a bit of the wrapper off one of them, and a bit of the wrapper is not a gate, and anybody who told you it was would be telling you about the wrong thing and they'd be telling you it because it's the only bit anybody could have got hold of."

"I never said it was a gate," said the woman who works in a shop. "I said it was a bit of polythene on a kerb."

"Aye," said the man in his thirties. "So you said it exactly right, and that's the third thing somebody has said exactly right about this lane in two months and I can remember all three of them."

**A BARROW WENT UP THAT LANE AT SIX IN THE MORNING WITH A WHITE THING ON IT AND CAME BACK DOWN IT EMPTY, AND THE WOMAN WHO WATCHED IT GO FROM A WINDOW HAS SAID NOTHING TO THE MAN WHO WORKED THAT LANE, AND HE WILL NOT ASK HER, AND THERE IS A YARD NEAR THE TOP OF THAT LANE WITH A GREEN MARK ON THE BRICK IN IT AND NOTHING ELSE.**
