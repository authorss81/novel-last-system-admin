# Chapter 0667 — The Open Drawer

Monday the thirteenth of September 2021, in this borough. He is in a unit off the industrial estate from first light until midday with a shoebox on his bench, and the whole of the morning is a drawer he does not shut and a road he does not drive up.

**A MAN IN HIS EARLY FORTIES WORKS ALL MORNING ROUND AN OPEN DRAWER UNDER A BENCH IN HIS VAN WITH A NEW BRUSH IN A PLASTIC BAG IN IT, AND LEAVES IT OPEN, AND DOES NOT DRIVE UP THAT ROAD.**

He is a man in his early forties. He is on his own all morning. Nobody rings.

---

The unit is cold when he lifts the shutter, and his bench is where he left it on Friday with the shoebox on top of it where he cannot come in without seeing it, and inside the shoebox is a stove element he has carried twice in car parks for no reason.

---
He puts the kettle on the ring at the back and stands with his hands in his pockets while it heats, and he looks at the box without opening it.

He knows what is in it without looking, which is why it is on top and not underneath.

---

He wants to get a kitchen frame ready for a house on the estate without touching the van drawer, because the drawer is open and has been open since the third of August and he wants to prove he can work round it.

---
He lays the frame on trestles and marks the rail where the new bead will go, and he saws outside the line and planes to it, and the shavings curl down onto his boots.

The radio at the bench opposite is on low with talk he does not follow, and a man in his mid thirties at that bench nods to him and gets on with his own work.

---

At midmorning he needs a short stiff brush to clear dust out of a mortise before he glues, and the brush he reaches for in his canvas bag is not there, and he stands with his hand in the empty side pocket for a moment.

---
The stranger's brush with a curl of old egg-coloured gloss in the crimp is in the side pocket of that bag with a leather bottom and a stone in it, in a footwell, since the twentieth of July, and it is not the brush he wants.

He blows the mortise out with his mouth instead and wipes it with his finger, and the joint fits dry and true.

---

He eats bread at the bench with the frame clamped, and while the glue goes off he goes out to the van with the tailgate open to fetch screws, and the drawer under the bench on the passenger side is open as he left it.

---
Inside it flat along the back in a plastic bag is the second wire brush, nine pounds of his own money out of a rack by the window in a shop with a galvanised bucket by the door.

He looks at the plastic bag and does not touch it, because touching it would make it a thing he was deciding about, and he has already decided.

---

The brush in the bag is for a job he is not going to do, because the woman who clears houses put her hand over her mouth in a kitchen on the sixteenth of July and said she would do the gate, and if he goes first he takes that off her and there is no way to put it back.

He has not drove up that road since the seventeenth of June. He knows the gate is done because he stood at his own kitchen window is not true, he knows it because he went past the end once and saw it from the van, and he has not been told and does not need to be.

He shuts the tailgate with the drawer still open inside and goes back to the bench.

---

In the afternoon he fits the bead and pins it and fills the holes, and he sands the frame with the grain and not across it, and the wood comes up pale and clean under his hand.

The man at the bench opposite asks what the frame is for and he says it is for a house on the estate that wants a cupboard made good, and the man nods and does not ask more.

He is glad of work that has a beginning and an end and a price, because the shoebox on top of his bench has none of those now.

---

He looks at the shoebox while the filler dries, and he thinks about the kitchen at number twelve where he said nothing on the sixteenth of July to a woman who asked him how much is that, and decided it in about four seconds while he was still looking at the drops, and still thinks it was the right thing.

He said about ten minutes for a job that is not ten minutes. He said fifty pound to an old man at a gate in July, a figure he has never charged anybody, and did not correct it.

Both sentences are still true in his head and neither of them needs another sentence after it.

---

He packs the tools at midday and sweeps the shavings into a pile with a brush that is his own and lives on the bench, and he puts the frame on the rack with its face to the wall.

He does not open the van drawer to put anything away. He puts the canvas bag in the footwell with the stranger's brush still in the side pocket, and he leaves the under-bench drawer open under the bench.

---

A drawer that is open is a thing he has not finished with, even when he has finished with it, and he knows that is not sense and he leaves it as it is.

He drives out of the estate with the windows down and goes the long way round so he does not pass the end of that road, because passing the end would make it a thing he did on purpose.

---

He stops for fuel on the ring road and while the tank fills he looks at the shop with the bucket by the door across the way, and he does not go in, because there is nothing in there he needs.

The woman who clears houses bought hers out of a bucket by that door on the twenty-sixth of July and neither of them knows the other did, and that is how it should stay.

---

He gets back to the unit and locks the van with the drawer still open inside it, and he puts the shoebox back on the top of his bench where he cannot come in without seeing it.

---

The table in a kitchen at number twelve stands dead flat on a shim planed and sawn flush under the window-side foot, and a tradesman can see that from six feet away, and he is the tradesman who can see it, and he does not go to look.

---

No Wednesday has been booked. Nobody has asked why the second line is what it is. The gate at number twelve is standing open against the wall of a coal shed with both bolts in an apron pocket.

---

**AND A MAN IN HIS EARLY FORTIES HAS GOT THROUGH A MORNING'S WORK WITHOUT SHUTTING AN OPEN DRAWER WITH A NEW BRUSH IN IT AND WITHOUT DRIVING UP A ROAD HE HAS NOT DROVE UP SINCE JUNE, AND THE SHOEBOX WITH A STOVE ELEMENT IN IT IS STILL ON TOP OF HIS OWN BENCH WHERE HE CANNOT COME IN WITHOUT SEEING IT.**

(End of chapter 0667)
