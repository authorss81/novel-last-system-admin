# Chapter 0382 — His Last One

Wednesday the tenth of January 2018, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past nine at night, a passage in a block of flats, and a landing off it, in Tideglass.

**FIVE WORDS WERE SAID IN A PASSAGE IN THAT BLOCK ON THE MORNING OF THE TENTH OF JANUARY BY A MAN IN HIS SIXTIES GOING OUT TO A STOP, AND HE SAID THEM THINKING OUT LOUD AND NOT TO ANYBODY, AND A MAN IN HIS THIRTIES GOING IN HEARD THEM AND TOOK THEM AS BEING ABOUT A MAN WHO LIVES ON THE SECOND FLOOR OF THAT BLOCK AND WAS RIGHT, AND THE MAN WHO SAID THEM WENT HOME THAT DAY THINKING THE OTHER MAN KNEW WHICH MAN HE MEANT, AND HE DID NOT, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO OF THEM HAS EVER PUT IT RIGHT.**

The block is six flats and it is on a side street, and the passage is the width of one person, with the meter cupboard on the left and eight or nine letter boxes on the right, and the stair goes up off the end of it with a rail on the open side and a light on the newel that is on all day and has been on all day for years.

The man in his thirties has been into that block about once a week since November doing small jobs, and he is known in that passage by sight and not by name, which is how a man is known in a passage.

The man in his sixties goes past that block on his way to a stop about four times a week and has been going past it for about six years, and he has been into that passage and up that stair twice in that time, both times because somebody he knew used to live in it.

---

It was about twenty past eight in the morning on the tenth of January.

The man in his thirties came in off that side street at about twenty past eight with a bag of screws and about four feet of conduit, and he had been in that block the week before and the week before that and had four more things in that block booked for the weeks after.

The man in his sixties came down that stair at the same time. He had been up two floors to see a man there — not the block, another one, about four streets away — and he had come back out into that passage and was going to the stop.

They met at the end of that passage, where the passage turns and the stair comes down into it, and there is about two foot of space there and no way to pass anybody without one of them going first.

The man in his thirties went first. He went up that stair one step and turned side-on to let the other man come off it, because that is what a man does on a stair.

And as he went past, the man in his sixties said, without stopping, without turning his head, and about half into the passage, "He won't get another one."

That is five words.

He was not talking to the man in his thirties. He had been on the top floor of a block four streets away and he had been sitting in a front room for about an hour and a half and that man had had a hand done in the summer and had not got the use of it back and had had a second thing go last month, and the man in his sixties had been turning that over in his own head since about half past seven that morning while he made his tea, and the five words had come out of him on his own somewhere between that front room and that stop, and had come out of him at the moment when somebody was going past him in a passage.

He went on out of that passage and out of that street and up the road to his stop, and he got on the bus, and he did not think about those five words again that day.

---

The man in his thirties went up that stair to the second floor with his bag of screws and his four feet of conduit.

On the second landing there is a man who lives in the second flat on that side, and the man in his thirties has put two small jobs in there since November, and he has been in and out of that flat four times, and the man in there is one who does not go out much in that part of the year and has not worked since the summer.

That man said a thing to him about the middle of December. It was at the end of a job, on the landing, and it was not much of a conversation, and the man in his thirties remembers it exactly because of what was said in it.

That man said he had been at that trade a long time and that he was not going to be asked again, and that whatever came next he would take what came, and he said it lightly and he went back into his flat.

The man in his thirties took those five words in the passage as being about that man, and it is correct. They are about that man, as far as anything in that passage was concerned, and it is the only person in that block that they could be about that anybody in that passage had been talking about in a fortnight.

He carried his bag up the second flight, and did the two things he had gone in to do, and came down at about ten past nine.

The man in his sixties was not there and has not been in that passage since, and if the man in his thirties had said to him in that passage at about half past eight that evening, "Who did you mean, then," he would have had to stop and think, and the answer would have been a man of about his own age in a block four streets away, and he would have said a name, and the man in his thirties would not have known it, and the thing would have been over in about four seconds and neither of them would have been any the wiser about the other.

What the man in his sixties went home with was this: that a man in his thirties had come up that passage at about twenty past eight and gone up the stair, and had said nothing, and had let him past, and had heard what he said.

He went the whole of that day believing that the man who went up that stair had understood him. There was nothing remarkable in that. A man says something out loud on his way to a stop and a man goes past and does not say anything, and a man who says nothing back is a man who has taken it in, and that is what a man in his sixties has thought about every time he has ever said anything out loud and had it taken in.

He did not put the other half to himself once. There was no moment that day or after it on which it occurred to him that the man who went up that stair might have taken those five words as being about somebody in that block and about nobody else at all, and that the reason he had not said anything back was that he had not been the person being talked about.

What is said while somebody is going past on a stair is not a message to him and not a report and not a thing anybody is set down against. Nobody was asked anything that morning. Nobody has been asked anything since.

The man in his thirties has not said anything about those five words to anybody, and has not thought about them as being about anybody, and if it were put to him he would say that a man on a landing in this borough has said to him that he is not going to be asked again, and that he believes him, and that is all he took out of that passage.

**THOSE FIVE WORDS WERE SAID ONCE, IN A PASSAGE, AT TWENTY PAST EIGHT IN THE MORNING, AND THE MAN WHO SAID THEM THOUGHT HE WAS TALKING ABOUT A MAN FOUR STREETS AWAY AND WENT HOME THINKING THE MAN WHO WENT UP THAT STAIR KNEW IT, AND THE MAN WHO WENT UP THAT STAIR THOUGHT THEY WERE ABOUT A MAN TWO FLOORS ABOVE HIM AND WAS RIGHT, AND NEITHER OF THEM WILL EVER MENTION IT AGAIN AND NEITHER OF THEM WILL EVER KNOW.**
