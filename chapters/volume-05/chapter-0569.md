# Chapter 0569 — The Flat Below

The Wednesday of the third of June 2020, and the nine days before it, on the ground floor of a house that was divided into four flats in about 1990, in this borough.

**SHE HAS PAID HER QUARTER OF WHAT THAT HOUSE COSTS TO RUN SINCE 2015, AND SINCE THE TWENTY-FIFTH OF MAY A MAN WHO LIVES IN THE FLAT BELOW HERS HAS BEEN LEAVING A FOLDED NOTE ON THE FLOOR IN FRONT OF HIS OWN DOOR AT SEVEN IN THE MORNING, AND IT IS HALF OF WHAT SHE PAID THE MONTH BEFORE, AND ON THE THIRD OF JUNE SHE LEFT ONE IN FRONT OF HIS DOOR AND HE FOUND IT AT NINE AT NIGHT.**

The house was built about 1890 and there were two rooms downstairs and two rooms up when it was new, and there are four flats in it now and one front door.

He is at the back of the ground floor and she is at the top. Her door is at the top and his is the first on the right inside the front door, and anybody going out or coming in walks past his door whether they want to or not.

They have both been there a long time. He is a man in his mid sixties. She is a woman in her mid fifties. They have said good morning to each other every single morning since either of them got there, and neither has ever put a question to the other about anything.

What the gas costs that house is divided four ways, and since 2015 she has paid her quarter on the same day every month out of the same account, and nobody has ever looked at that account except the woman who has to.

On the twenty-fifth of May he put a folded five-pound note on the floor in front of his own door at about seven in the morning and went to work.

---

She came down at about half eight and saw it on the way past.

She picked it up, went up, and put it inside her own door, and she did not say anything about it to him and he was not there.

He put another one there on the Wednesday and another on the Thursday.

She took the Wednesday one in as well. The Thursday one she left.

On the Friday morning he was standing in the corridor at about ten to seven and the Thursday one was still on the floor in front of his door.

He stood there for a moment. Then he went to work and left it where it was, and it was still there when he came in at about nine that night, and it was still there on the Saturday, and it was still there on the Sunday.

She had not taken it in and she had not put it anywhere else. It was where he had left it, and he could see it every time he came in, and neither of them has ever said one word to the other about a note on a floor.

On the Wednesday he put out another one.

---

At about half eight on the third of June she came down with her cardigan on and nothing over her arm, and she went past his door, and she stopped, and she bent down and picked up the note that was lying there, and then she stood in the corridor for about as long as it takes to count to five.

Then she folded the one that was already folded in her cardigan, folded it again the way he folded his, and put it down in front of his door, and went out.

He came in at about nine.

She was in the flat above with the door shut and he heard her about and he came down and he saw it before he had his own door open.

A folded five-pound note on the floor in front of his own door, folded in half, and nothing on it.

He stood in the corridor and looked at it and then he went in and shut his own door and stood where he had always stood in that part of the place with it still in his hand for a while.

He put it in his wallet.

He put another one down before he left that morning, on the floor in front of his own door, folded in half, in the same place.

He has not asked her anything and she has not said anything, and there is nothing in either flat to say, and there is going to be nothing.

**NINE DAYS ARE THE WHOLE OF IT AND SHE TOOK TWO OF HIS INSIDE HER OWN DOOR AND LEFT THE THIRD ON THE FLOOR, AND ON THE THIRD OF JUNE SHE PUT ONE DOWN INSTEAD, AND NOTHING HAS PASSED BETWEEN THE TWO OF THEM ABOUT ANY OF IT AND NOTHING IS GOING TO.**
