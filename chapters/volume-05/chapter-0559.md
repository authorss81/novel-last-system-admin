# Chapter 0559 — The Ground-Floor Door

The Friday of the seventeenth of April 2020, from about half past seven in the morning until about a quarter past eight, in a block of flats off another street in this borough.

**SHE HAS SWEPT THE RUN OF FLOOR OUTSIDE A FLAT ON THE GROUND FLOOR EVERY MORNING SINCE THE MARCH OF 2015 AND NOBODY ASKED HER TO AND NOBODY HAS EVER ASKED HER WHY, AND HE HAS COME BACK UP PAST HER FOUR MORNINGS A WEEK SINCE THE SEPTEMBER BEFORE LAST AND HAS NEVER SAID ONE WORD.**

The block is three storeys and there are eighteen flats in it. It was divided up in about 1994 and everything about it is from that year, including the air off the entry.

There are two on the ground floor and eight on the first and eight on the second. The one at the near end on the ground floor has been shut since about the March of 2015, and there is nothing on the door, and nobody in that block has a key to it, and nobody has asked what is going to happen to it.

She is a woman in her early forties and she is in the flat next to it on the ground floor. She has been there since about 2011.

She does it with a stiff brush that is her own and a small plastic pan, and she does it at about half past seven, and it takes her about six minutes, and she does it outside that door and along the run of floor to the front door and no further.

The woman who had that flat in 2015 asked her once, in the January of that year, whether she would sweep the outside of it while she was away. The woman said it did not matter either way. The woman went in the March and there was no conversation with anybody about it after that, and the sweeping carried on, and by the end of the summer there was nobody in that block who did not know that the woman in the next flat did that.

Nobody has ever said anything to her about it. Nobody has ever told her to stop either. It has not been discussed in that building once in five years, in any direction, by anybody, and she has never brought it up herself.

**HE HAS BEEN STANDING ON THAT CORRIDOR SINCE THE SEPTEMBER BEFORE LAST AND SHE HAS KNOWN IT FOR ABOUT TWO MONTHS, AND NOT ONE WORD HAS PASSED BETWEEN THE TWO OF THEM.**

He is a man in his mid fifties. He is at the top of that corridor and has been there since about 2013.

He comes down at about half past seven on the mornings when he has not slept, which since the September before last has been about four mornings a week, and he goes down because the air on that ground floor is better than the air on the second, and then he comes back up.

He comes back up slowly. He has never given a reason out loud, and there is nobody in that block he would give one to.

She has known he was standing there for about two months. She worked it out from the sound of him on that corridor, which is a particular sound, because he stops. He comes up the last stretch and then he stops for about four seconds and then the rest of it, and she has been in her own front door with her boots off for about two months and has been able to tell the difference between that stop and an ordinary one.

She has not gone out and looked. She has not said anything from where she was. She has put up with the four seconds about twice a week for two months and has not mentioned it to anybody, and the two months are the whole of what she knows about him.

On the Friday of the seventeenth she was about four minutes later than usual, which she put down to the cold.

She had the pan at her own door and she did the run outside number one first, the way she does, and she had about a minute of it left at the front door when he came round the angle and stopped.

Neither of them said anything.

She finished at the front door and then she stood with the pan in her hand and did not go back in. That is the whole of what happened on that morning and it took about nine seconds.

He did not say anything either. He stood there and she stood there, and then she put the brush down on the floor and left it, and went in, and shut the door behind her without shutting it all the way.

He was still there when the door moved.

He stood there for about a minute after that, and then he went up. He has been coming down since, on the mornings when he has not slept, and he is still on that corridor for the four seconds, and he has not said one word about the brush or about the nine seconds or about anything else, and neither has she, and nobody else in that building has any notion of it.

---

**THE BRUSH IS STILL ON THE FLOOR OUTSIDE THE FRONT DOOR AND HE WALKED ROUND IT TWICE ON HIS WAY IN, AND SHE HAS NOT PICKED IT UP SINCE THAT MORNING, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS ASKED THE OTHER FOR ANYTHING, AND HE IS STILL ON THAT CORRIDOR FOR THE FOUR SECONDS.**
