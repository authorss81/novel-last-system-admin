# Chapter 0472 — The Wall Behind the Cupboard

Tuesday the twelfth of February 2019, from about two in the afternoon until about half past three. One street, one flank wall and the ground behind it, in Tideglass.

**HE HAS BEEN RIGHT ABOUT THAT BOUNDARY SINCE 2014, HE PUT IT INTO WORDS ONCE IN A KITCHEN LAST SEPTEMBER, HE WAS TOLD NOT TO PUT IT INTO WORDS AGAIN, AND HE HAS NOT, AND SHE HAS DECIDED NOT TO MAKE HIM A SECOND TIME, AND NOTHING BEHIND HER KITCHEN HAS SHIFTED BY SO MUCH AS A HAIR.**

She is on the ground floor of the second house in that row and has been there about six years, and the right-hand wall of her kitchen is the boundary between her house and the house next door, and it goes up past the window to the eaves and about nine inch past them, and the house next door is a house of the same age and a foot narrower and about four foot further back, and the result of all that is that its side leans.

Not much. About half an inch over the height of two floors, which is the sort of thing you would never notice if you were not looking for it and would think of as a builder's slack if you were.

He is not a builder. He is in his thirties, he does building work and odd jobs, he keeps a place off Corrance Lane, and about nine people in this borough would tell you he is better than most.

He noticed it in the summer of 2014, in about four minutes, standing in her back garden with a cigarette he did not smoke, because the gable of the next house is tieing into that boundary and the tie is doing nothing at all, and that is the only thing in that row that is actually wrong.

What it does, in the long run, is close that gap in a way that will not close.

That is the plain part and it took him a year to be certain of it. It is not a dramatic thing. It is about nine inches of movement over something like eighty years in a wall that was already tying when he was born, and what it means is that one day in about the middle of this century the three or four courses at the top of that flank wall stop being held by anything and the gable of the house next door comes down on her kitchen.

He has worked out the arithmetic four times and got the same answer three times.

He is right.

---

Here is the whole of what he is and is not able to do with it.

He cannot hand it to anybody in a shape anybody would act on. He has tried. He has tried three times, in three different ways, and he has not been to a solicitor and he is not going to be to a solicitor, and he cannot put a piece of paper in front of a person at the council because a person at the council will want to know how he knows, and how he knows is the entire of his problem.

The reason he knows is that he has been inside that wall twice.

The first time was in 2013, when the roof of the house next door was stripped and re-covered, and the man doing it took the plaster off the outside of her gable and found a gap in the tie, and he was in that gap for about an hour with a torch, and nobody in that house knew he was in there and nobody in her house was home.

The second time was in 2016, when the chimney on the house next door came down and was not put back for about ten weeks, and he went up the scaffold at four in the afternoon with nobody in that house, and he put his hand in the head of the tie, and there was nothing at all in it.

Both times he was paid nothing. Both times he told nobody. And both times he was up in that wall in a house that was not his and not his job with nobody in the house knowing, and that is the thing he cannot get past the front of.

If he says the wall is carrying that gable, then the next four things anybody asks are: how do you know, when did you last see it, who was with you, and what were you doing in there. And he has got a good answer to the first three and none at all to the fourth.

He said it on the seventh of September 2018, in that kitchen, at about half past seven in the evening, with a cup of tea he had not asked for in his hand.

He said, "That wall of yours is holding up the side of theirs."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "It'll hold for fifty years. I just think you should know it's happening."

And she said, "All right," and took the cup, and put it down on the worktop, and then she stood there and looked at him and said the thing that has been sitting in that kitchen for five months.

She said, "I'd rather you didn't say that again."

And he said, "All right."

And he has not said it again.

---

What she meant by it, and what she is not going to explain to him, and what she has been through since about half past seven on that evening four times.

What she heard was not a warning. She heard a man she had had four jobs done for over six years tell her that the house she lives in is going to come down on her kitchen, and then tell her not to say it again.

What she has is three things she could do about it and she has added each of them up twice.

The first is a solicitor, which is about two hundred and fifty pounds for a letter and about six weeks, and she has not got it, and she is not going to get it, and the letter would not do anything by itself anyway. A letter tells a person that she has a problem. It does not move a wall.

The second is a survey, which is more money than the first.

The third is to knock on the door of the house next door and ask to look at that gable with the man who owns it, and the man who owns the house next door was unpleasant to her about a bin in 2017 in a way that she could still describe, and she is not going to stand on that doorstep and ask him for anything.

And there is a fourth thing, and it is the one she has been through four times, and it is the only one that costs nothing.

She could ask him.

And she has decided not to, and the reason is that asking him would be asking him to be the man who told her.

She has had four jobs done for that man in six years. She has never once got a bill she did not expect and she has never once had him turn up when she did not want him. He is the only tradesman she has ever had who has never once made her feel like she was being sold something, and she has wondered about that, and she has worked out that it is because he does not need to be, and that is a thing she could not get out of anybody else.

And if she says to him, do you mean it, and what would I do, and how do I know how long — then she has asked him to be something he is not, which is a person with a standing, and he has told her, in his own way, in one go, in a kitchen, that he is not.

He has no standing here. He does not own that wall, he is not that wall's surveyor, he is not anybody's anything in that row, and the whole of what he knows he got by going into a gap in somebody's house at four in the afternoon when nobody was in.

And she will not ask him to put his name to it a second time, and she will not ask him to sit in a room with a solicitor, and she has decided all of that in about four minutes on that evening and she has not moved on it since.

He has not said it again.

He has also not stopped being right about it, and he has not slowed down for it, and he has not driven past that house fewer times.

He has a job at the end of that row on the eighth of February. He was in that back garden at about ten past two with a tape out and he put the head of it against that boundary wall at about shoulder height and he took it down to the top and he wrote the two figures in the back of a book, and the two figures were the same ones he got in September.

Then he went and did the job.

He is going to be in that back garden again in the April, when there is more of the day in it, and he does not intend to say anything to her, and she will not ask him, and in about thirty years there is going to be a thing that happens in that kitchen that neither of them will have mentioned.

**ON THE TWELFTH OF FEBRUARY A MAN CAME OUT OF A GARDEN AT THE BACK OF THAT ROW AND WENT DOWN THE SIDE OF IT AND CARRYING A BOOK WITH TWO FIGURES IN THE BACK OF IT THAT WERE THE SAME FIGURES AS THE ONES FROM SEPTEMBER, AND HE DID NOT GO IN AT ANY DOOR.**
