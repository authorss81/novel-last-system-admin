# Chapter 0594 — Three Things, One Borough

The Saturday of the nineteenth of September 2020, from about nine in the morning until about half past nine at night, in this borough.

**SOMETHING WAS SAID IN THIS BOROUGH ON THE MONDAY BEFORE LAST AND NOTHING HAS COME OF IT THAT CAN BE PUT DOWN ANYWHERE, AND THE BOROUGH IS EXACTLY AS IT WAS, AND THREE THINGS IN IT ARE STILL GOING THE WAY THEY WERE GOING IN JUNE, AND NONE OF THE THREE HAS ANYTHING TO DO WITH THE OTHER TWO OR WITH WHAT WAS SAID.**

Here is the first of the three, and it is a thing about a door.

There is a door on the ground floor of a building on a parade of shops in this borough, and it has been sticking since before the middle of July. It sticks in the middle, about a foot above the handle, and it is not the handle and it is not the surround and it is not the lock. It is the door, and nobody has found out why.

On the Saturday of the nineteenth of September it stuck about four times in three hours.

The man in his late fifties who works in that building lifts it with his hip. He has been doing that for about two months. He does it without slowing down and without appearing to notice, and about a dozen people went through that door that day, and three of them noticed, and two of them said nothing, and one of them mentioned it to somebody on the way home, which is the whole of the chain of consequence.

Nobody has reported it. Nobody means to. The man who lifts it with his hip and the other people who have noticed it and the person who owns the building, who knows, all regard it as a feature of that door.

---

Here is the second of the three, and it is a thing about a fitting in a covered walkway.

There is a fitting in a covered walkway between two buildings in this borough that comes on when it gets dark. It has been coming on at the wrong time since the June. Not wrong by an hour. Wrong by about an hour and a bit, so that in the middle of September it comes on at about half past seven on a Saturday evening while there is still proper daylight in the sky behind the buildings, and it stands there on its own above an empty walkway for a good while before anybody needs it.

It has a sensor in it. The sensor in it is not faulty in a way anybody could describe. It is a sensor that was set, or has ended up, on a timing that does not match the month it is now in, and there is no adjustment anybody can make at the top of a ladder.

A woman who walks through that walkway twice a day has said to about four people that it comes on too early, and has never once said it to anybody who could alter it, and it has been coming on too early all the way through the summer, and it will go on coming on too early until somebody in this borough with the ability to change it looks, and no such person has looked.

---

Here is the third of the three, and it is a thing about a door as well, in another building, about half a mile off, where nothing whatever to do with the first one happens.

There is a row of four public lavatories in a block off a side street, and the second cubicle door has not shut since the June. It will stand open about a hand's width and it will move if anybody goes past it hard, and there is a ring on the back of it, and a person who goes in there and pulls the door to behind them does not know that the door has not shut until they turn round.

The man in his early sixties who locks that block up at about ten at night has pushed it to with his foot for three months and has never once reported it, and the reason he has never reported it is that it is not a lavatory that anybody goes into in a hurry, and because he is sixty-one and has worked in this borough since he was twenty, and because the council answers the phone on a Wednesday.

Those three sit in a borough of a hundred and thirty thousand people, and no road runs from any of them to either of the others.

The first door is on a parade with shops on it. The second is between two buildings behind that parade. The third door is off that side street. There is no chain that joins them, and there is no person in this borough who knows all three, and there could not be one, and the man who lifts the first one with his hip and the man in his early sixties who pushes the third one with his foot have not heard of each other and would not see any point in it.

And here is the fourth thing, and it is the only one of the four that is about somebody saying something.

On the Monday before last, a woman in her late thirties said four words to a man in his early thirties outside a shop, in a town about thirty miles from this one, and she has not told anybody in this borough a single thing about it and is not going to.

What was said was ordinary, it cost her nothing to say, and it has not been repeated and it is not going to be, and no part of it has reached this borough in any form.

By the Sunday of that week nothing had come of it. By this Saturday a fortnight later, nothing had come of it. There is nothing to measure and nothing to put in a minute and nobody in this borough who is waiting to hear about it, because nobody in this borough knows it happened.

And the one other thing in this borough, which is the thing somebody has been trying to put a name to since the March, is still exactly as nameless as it was in the March.

It is not that anybody has stopped. There is no reason. It is very likely nothing at all. It is probably a timetable nobody has ever looked at, and the woman who has been turning it over has done it again this week and got exactly as far with it as she did in March, and has decided, the way she decides most weeks, that it is very probably nothing, and that decision is not a discovery and it is not a defeat, and she will make it again next week.

It is about half past nine on a Saturday night in September in a borough of a hundred and thirty thousand, and a door on a parade is sticking, and a fitting between two buildings came on about an hour and a bit too early and has not gone off, and a second door in a block off a side street is standing open a hand's width, and four words were said on a Monday in a town about thirty miles off, and nobody in this borough is doing anything about any part of it, and nothing in this borough has started.
