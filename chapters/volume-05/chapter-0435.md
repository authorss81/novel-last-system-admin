# Chapter 0435 — Folded in Three

Wednesday the twenty-second of August 2018, and about the six hours in the middle of it, Ashlay Street from end to end, in Tideglass.

**A BLANKET FOLDED IN THREE AND DONE UP IN STRING WENT ACROSS THAT TOWN IN THE ARMS OF TWO PEOPLE WHO HAD SPOKEN TO EACH OTHER ONCE, IN JUNE, FOR ABOUT TWO MINUTES, AND NEITHER OF THEM KNEW THE OTHER ONE HAD ONE, AND NEITHER OF THEM PUT IT DOWN AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID ONE WORD.**

The street is about a quarter of a mile long and goes down from the bus stop at the top to the postbox at the bottom, and there is nothing on it that anybody would stop for. Sixteen houses, nine of them terraced, then a row of four with a gap for a passage, then eight more, and a corner shop at the bottom that shuts at half past five and sells everything.

The buses are every twenty minutes at the top and the walk down is longer than waiting, which is why the people who use that stop use it for the town and not for the shops.

The man in his forties was given it in the second week of June. He was in his van outside number thirty-one at about half past two in the afternoon with the back doors open, and a man in his sixties came out of the gate and carried it down his own garden path in one arm, held against his chest the way a man holds a thing when the other arm will not go above his shoulder, and put it into the back of the van.

He said the woman who used to be in the top flat at number twenty-two was at Brackness since the first week of July and would be there a while, and would she want a blanket. He said it standing on the pavement with the gate shut behind him, and he did not say anything else, and he did not ask whether the man in the van was going that way.

The man in his forties said right.

That was the second week of June. It is now the twenty-second of August. The blanket is in the back of that van under a bag of offcuts, and it has been there for ten weeks, and he has driven onto that road at Brackness twice, once in the second week of July and once in the last week of August, and on both occasions he has sat in the car park for about ten minutes with the engine off and then driven back out of it.

The reason is not the place and it is not the woman. It is that there are thirty-odd houses up that road and he does not know which one she is in, and the man in his sixties did not say, and there was no reason on that afternoon for the man in his sixties to have said, and there has been no other occasion on which it could have come up.

He has not telephoned. There is no number. He has not asked in the shop at the bottom of Ashlay Street because he has not been in that shop since April, and the woman who stands behind that counter has been in that shop since before he came to this town, and he is not a man who goes into a shop to ask a question.

The blanket is under a bag of offcuts, and as far as his work is concerned it could be anywhere at all.

---

The woman at number eighteen was given hers in the first week of July by the woman who lets the ground floor flat at number twenty, who had it out of a cupboard in the passage of that flat and could not get it up the stairs to the top flat because of the turn on the half-landing.

She carried it down to number eighteen on a Monday afternoon and left it inside the front door and knocked, and when the woman came to the door she said, "This is for the woman at Brackness, if you would," and that was the whole of what she said.

The woman at number eighteen said she would.

The blanket has been folded in three and done up in string since that Monday and has been in a bag under the stairs at number eighteen, and she has taken it with her twice.

In the second week of July she took it on the bus in the middle of the morning and stood outside a desk in a corridor at Brackness from about midday until about half past twelve, and a woman behind the desk said the family were away that week and did not know which week they would be back, and she asked whether she should leave it, and the woman behind the desk said that would be all right.

She did not leave it. She carried it back down the corridor and got on the bus and put it under the stairs again.

In the last week of August she took it again and stood at the same desk from about midday until about one, and the same thing happened in a different voice, and she did not leave it.

She has not been offered anything for the bus and would not have taken anything. There is a shop at Brackness that sells a paper about what is happening in the hospital and she has bought it twice and thrown it away both times without reading it, because the woman who wrote in it has a photograph of the whole street in it.

She is not a young woman and there is nobody in that street she could ask about the family at Brackness, because the woman who lets the ground floor at number twenty does not know either. She said so, and added, in the way of a woman who has already been told once, that she had put it to somebody and had been handed back the same nothing.

---

They went past each other at about ten to twelve.

He was coming up Ashlay Street with the blanket under his left arm, done up in string, the string pulled round twice and knotted at the top. She was going down it with the same kind of thing under her right arm, done up in string, the string pulled round twice and knotted at the top.

There is a width of about nine foot at that point. He was on the offside and she was on the near side, and the bus was coming up behind her and about four minutes late, and neither of them changed pace and neither of them moved to the other side of the road.

He looked at her. He did not see the blanket. He was looking at the shop signs because he was going into the shop at the bottom of that street for the first time since April, on the chance that the woman behind it would know which house at Brackness a woman who used to be in the top flat at number twenty-two was in.

She looked at him and saw a man in a van's fleece with something under his arm going into the corner shop, and put him where she puts about nine other men in a year, which is the place where men who are going somewhere go.

Neither of them said anything. He asked for a packet of washing powder and a bag of apples and paid for both, and the woman behind that counter had never heard of Brackness and could not help him, and he told her it did not matter and walked out to the van.

She got on the bus. The driver did not keep the door open for her and she put her hand on the bar beside the door and went up.

There were four more days in that week and then the week came to its end.

The blanket is still in the back of that van, under a bag of offcuts. The other one is still in a bag under the stairs at number eighteen. Neither of them has asked anybody anything about it. The man in his sixties who gave him his has not mentioned it since the second week of June and is not going to, and the woman who lets the ground floor at number twenty has told nobody she got it out of that cupboard, and the woman at Brackness does not know that two people in a town have carried something for her and neither of them was asked to.

**A BLANKET IS CARRIED DOWN ONE SIDE OF THAT STREET AND A BLANKET IS CARRIED UP THE OTHER, AND THE STRINGS ARE KNOTTED THE SAME WAY, AND NEITHER OF THEM EVER LEARNS THAT THE OTHER ONE CARRIED ONE.**
