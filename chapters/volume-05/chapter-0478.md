# Chapter 0478 — The Pot in the Covered Way

Thursday the fourteenth of March 2019, from about nine in the morning until about half past four. A clearance, and the fourth day of it, in Tideglass.

**A MAN HAS BEEN EMPTIING ONE HOUSE INTO A VAN FOUR DAYS RUNNING AND ON THE FOURTH DAY HE PUT A FUCHSIA IN A PLASTIC POT DOWN ON THE FLOOR OF A COVERED WAY THAT FOUR OTHER HOUSEHOLDERS HAVE TO WALK DOWN, AND WENT BACK IN FOR A BOX, AND A WOMAN AT THE FAR END OF THAT WAY CAME OUT TWENTY MINUTES LATER AND CARRIED IT INSIDE, AND HE HAS NOT ASKED HER.**

The house is at the top of a street and it has been shut since the September before last, and the man emptying it is not the man who owned it.

That is the arrangement. The family are in another county and there are four of them and no two of them agree, and one of them found him and paid him and told him the job was to empty every room and not to do anything else at all. He is on his own in it for the fourth day and there is nobody in the borough he is doing it for and nobody who is going to come and watch him do it.

The van is on the road outside and it is about fourteen foot off the top of the street, and the way to get between it and the house is along a covered way that runs down the side of four houses to a yard, and the family did not think about that in March of 2017 and have not thought about it since.

About nine foot of that way is covered and the rest of it is open to the sky. At the yard end there are four doors and the last one belongs to a woman who has been there about twenty-two years and has never spoken to the man in the house and has never wanted to.

---

The first two days he carried everything straight out to the van.

On the third it rained from about ten until about two and he found that he was making four trips to the front room to get through one, and that there is a thing in a house like this where you stop being able to make your legs do the last of the stairs at about two in the afternoon, and he stopped making them do it.

So on the fourth day he put things down in the covered way.

That is all it was. He worked out a place where he could stand a box for about a minute and a half while he went back in for the next one, and he used the flat part of that covered way about nine foot in from the street, and he stacked a bit of it, and it was raining again on and off all day and everything he put down got wet.

At about half past ten he came out with a fuchsia in a plastic pot.

It is a fuchsia about four foot high on a stem and it is not doing what a fuchsia is supposed to do and it has not done anything all summer. It is the one thing in that house he has looked at twice on his own account.

He put it down against the wall of the covered way on the dry side, at about half past ten, and went back in.

He was in the front room for about twenty minutes with a box of books that is not finished.


She came out at about twenty past twelve because she could not get past.

That is all. There were four boxes across the width of that covered way and she was going to the yard and she was not going to be able to get through the middle of them without turning sideways, and turning sideways in that way with a box in each arm is not something a person does, so she stood there and looked at the boxes and then she went back in and got a coat.

Or: she had been out already, and the boxes were not there when she went out, and they were there when she came back. Same thing.

Either way she came back at about a quarter to one and there was a fuchsia in a plastic pot standing against the wall with water in the saucer and the pot itself wet through on one side.

And here is the thing she has gone over since, and it is not a difficult thing and it has taken her the whole of the afternoon to be honest about it.

She has never done that man's work for him. She has not carried anything of his anywhere. She has not spoken to him and he has not spoken to her and in four days the two of them have exchanged one sentence, which was him asking her whether the water in the yard was off on a Sunday and her saying she did not know.

What she did was pick the pot up with both hands, put it through her own door, and stand it on the floor inside the glass where it would not be walked over.

And that is all it was. A thing had been left standing outside in the rain in a way that was going to kill it, and she had a floor about two foot away, and she put it there.

That is the account she is going to keep, and she is going to keep it because it is true, and because it is small enough, and because the other account is about twelve times bigger and there is no part of it she is going to say out loud.

The other account is this. She has lived at the end of that covered way for about twenty-two years and in four days a stranger has made it his place to put things down. She has walked down that way about six times a day for twenty-two years and there is not one single thing anybody has ever left in it.

And she has looked at that fuchsia and seen a man decide something about it, and what she saw was a man holding a pot of a dying plant and standing still with it for about a second before he put it down, and that second is a thing she did not expect to see and has not been able to put down since.

She put it on her own floor. She gave it about a cup of water out of the kitchen at about six that evening and she did not think about that either.

She has not mentioned it to him and she is not going to. She is not going to say *I have got your plant* and she is not going to say *that thing needed bringing in*, because both of those are about him, and about him is not a thing she is going to do.

---

He came out of that house at about four with a bag and she was at her own door.

He put the bag in the van and turned round and did a count of what was left in the front room by looking at it through the door, and his eye went down the covered way because there was something at the end of it in the dark.

There was a pot with a plant in it about nine foot inside the glass at the last door in that way.

He looked at it for about a second and a half.

It is not his. He knows it is not his before he has finished looking at it, and what he has got at the end of that second and a half is that a fuchsia in a plastic pot has been in that covered way since before he started, because there has been one there all week, and he put everything he emptied in the van and the van is on the road.

What he has also got, and has had since about the third of March, is the sentence he uses about the things in that house.

It is one sentence and he uses it about everything, and it is that a thing that has been in a shut house for a year and a half and is being taken out of it is a thing that wanted to be gone.

He has used it on about four hundred things. He used it on a piano. He has never said it out loud to anybody and it is not a lie in any way he could be caught out on, and it has done the work of about four hundred decisions for him in four days.

He used it on the fuchsia at about four o'clock on the fourteenth of March, standing in the wet at about nine foot inside a covered way, and it took about two seconds, and he got back in the van and drove the four miles and put the bag in his own back room.

**ON THE FIFTEENTH OF MARCH A WOMAN TOOK A CUP OF WATER OUT OF HER OWN KITCHEN AND PUT IT DOWN ON THE FLOOR INSIDE HER OWN DOOR IN FRONT OF A POT THAT IS NOT HERS, AND SHE DID NOT PUT IT BACK OUTSIDE IN THE RAIN, AND THE MAN WHO HAD LEFT IT THERE WAS FOUR MILES AWAY WITH A VAN THAT HAD NOTHING IN IT.**
