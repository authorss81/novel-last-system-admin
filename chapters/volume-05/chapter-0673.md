# Chapter 0673 — Up The Stairs With A Torch

Tuesday the twelfth of October 2021, in this borough. She is in her own house from dark until late with a torch, and the whole of the night is a hatch she does not open and a sound she listens for.

**A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-EIGHT GOES UP HER OWN STAIRS WITH A TORCH ON A TUESDAY NIGHT TO LISTEN FOR A CISTERN WEEPING AT THE FAR SIDE OF A JOIST WITHOUT GOING UP THE LADDER, AND COMES DOWN AND SITS AT HER KITCHEN TABLE.**

She is a woman in her late seventies. She is on her own all night. Nobody knocks.

---

She empties the bowl under the bath tap after tea, with the folded cloth in the bowl and the tap dripping onto it while she watches, and she puts the bowl back with the cloth in it.

---
The brown mark on the bathroom ceiling is still there with a hard edge, and in the evening light it looks wider by a finger's width at the front edge, towards the landing and the front room beyond it.

She wants to hear whether the cistern is still weeping without going up the ladder, because going up would make it a thing she had tried and failed at, and listening from below is a thing she can do.

---

She gets the torch from the kitchen drawer where the string and the fuses and the tea bags live, and she tests it against her palm and it is bright.

---
She goes up the stairs of her own house in her slippers with her hand on the rail, past the half landing where the rail turns, to the top landing in the dark.

The hatch with the fixed ladder is shut above her, and the paint round it shows the line she saw in September, faint and dark at the edge nearest the front.

---

She stands on the top landing with the torch off and listens, and the house ticks round her the way houses do at night, and the tap drips below through two floors.

---
Then she hears it, thin and high and steady, a cistern weeping at the far side of a joist, the same sound she heard in August standing a foot and a half higher on a stool from the front room.

She does not go up the ladder.

---
She cannot turn the supply off from downstairs because she does not know where the pipe comes up and never has, and she cannot ask anybody to get at it and has not since before Christmas.

---

She comes down backwards one step at a time with the torch on for the treads, and at the half landing she stops and looks at the ceiling where the landing meets the front of the house.

---
There is now a damp patch showing on the front room ceiling, small and pale round the edge with a darker middle, just inside the door where she stands in the doorway to look.

The rush-seated chair is against the wall by the window.

---
The sideboard has nothing on it. The brush is not in here. The brush is on the passage floor in the middle of the runner with the handle across the walking line.

---

She stands in the front room doorway for about a minute with the torch off, looking at the patch, and she does not go in.

---
She has not sat in there since the sixteenth of July because there is now a question in that room, and she has never in her life been able to be a person being asked about a thing, and water coming through the ceiling does not make it a room she can sit in.

She goes back to the kitchen and puts the torch back in the drawer with the string and the fuses.

---

She sits at the kitchen table under the window on the green chair with the cushion on it, with a cup she does not drink from, and the gas envelope face up square with the edge, pencil side up.

---
The gate is first because it is the only one she actually wants. The second line is a room with a chair in it and nobody has asked her what is wrong with it.

Four of six are done and nothing has been crossed off, because there is no pencil in that house.

---

The front door is not on the latch. The passage light is on. The gate is standing open against the wall of the coal shed with both bolts in an apron pocket.

She sits until the cup goes cold, and then she washes it and puts it on the drainer and wipes the cloth flat.

---

**AND A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-EIGHT HAS HEARD WATER SHE CANNOT STOP AND HAS SEEN IT COME THROUGH INTO THE ROOM SHE WILL NOT SIT IN, AND HAS COME DOWN WITHOUT OPENING A HATCH, AND THE ENVELOPE ON HER TABLE IS STILL FACE UP SQUARE WITH THE EDGE.**

(End of chapter 0673)
