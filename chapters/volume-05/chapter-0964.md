# Chapter 0964 — Down The Corner Slot

Monday the sixteenth of December 2024, at the angle where the store wall meets the office wall with the strip between them in wind. Down a corner slot.

**THE TWO WALLS MEET AT AN ANGLE AND A RAINWATER PIPE RUNS DOWN THE INSIDE OF IT, WHICH LEAVES A SLOT DOWN ONE SIDE OF THE PIPE AND ANOTHER DOWN THE OTHER, BOTH OF THEM SHUT AT THE TOP AND AT THE BOTTOM AND OPEN AT THE SIDE, AND THE HAND GOES DOWN INTO ONE OF THEM WITH THE THUMB LAID ALONG ONE WALL AND THE FIRST AND SECOND FINGERS ALONG THE OTHER, TO FIND OUT HOW FAR DOWN IT GOES AND WHAT IS AT THE BOTTOM OF IT.**

The store and the office are two buildings of the same row with about two feet of flags between them, and the two of them were built at different times and they do not stand square to each other.

The angle between their walls at that corner is about ninety degrees and a half, and it is closed along its whole height by a rainwater pipe on clips, and the pipe is against one wall rather than in the middle of the angle. The store is corrugated sheet on battens and the office is brick, and they were built about forty years apart, and the two walls came together at that corner without anybody measuring anything, which is why the angle is not square and why the pipe sits against one side of it and not in the middle.

Both of those walls run down to a flags floor and both of them were built on the same ground, and neither has moved, and the two feet between them has been the two feet between them for as long as either building has stood. The strip between them is not a passage and it is not used as one. It is the side of the store that gets the sun for about two hours in the middle of the day and the rest of it is not used at all.

Which leaves a slot down each side of the pipe. The one against the store wall is about an inch wide, the one against the office wall is about half, and both of them are shut at the top by the pipe's own bracket and at the bottom by the flags. That pipe has been on that corner since the office was built and it is a lead pipe of the old kind with a hopper head at the top of it, and it is fixed to the brick on three clips and the middle clip has come off, which is the whole of what is wrong with that corner and the whole of what is not. Nothing is leaking out of it, nothing has come down the wall under it, and the paint on the store side of the pipe has not run anywhere, which is what a pipe that is only standing off a wall looks like.

Nothing has been packed behind that pipe and no joint on it has been made good since it went on.

---

The hand goes down into the wider of the two slots with the thumb on the side of the pipe and the first and second fingers on the face of the store wall, and it goes down as far as the angle will take it.

The point of that arrangement is that the thumb and the fingers are on two different surfaces and they are told to go as far as they can together, so what comes back is not how far the hand can reach and it is not how wide the slot is, and it is the distance between two ends of one hand.

The thumb is against the pipe and it is stiff and it does not move. The two fingers are against the brick and they go down the wall to about the height of a man's knee and then they come to the place where the wall has been repiped at some point and there is a short length of lead let into the fall of the pipe above them. That joint is the bottom of that slot. It is a lead collar wiped round a joint and it is bigger round than the pipe above it and the pipe below it, and where the collar comes down the angle comes in on it and the two fingers cannot get past it.

The collar is hard and stiff and has a bead of solder round it at the bottom, and the bead is the only part of that corner anybody can put a hand on from outside, and nobody has. Below the collar the slot goes on down to the flags and is two inches deep in a socket, and the two fingers do not reach that far in one go because the pipe is not straight there and the slot narrows to nothing at the bottom.

Behind the pipe the angle is dry. The render on both of those walls has gone hard and pale and drawn back from the arris on the store side, and where the two walls meet there is a line of that render no thicker than a coin, with the brick behind it and nothing at all in front of it. Lead is stiff for its weight in the way that anything that weighs a great deal and can be bent by hand is stiff, and it is the pipe that is stiff and not the collar, because the collar is thicker, because it was wiped round the joint where the two lengths meet, and because it is the one thing on that corner that was ever dressed.

Three clips on a pipe of that length is one clip more than it wants and one clip less than it ought to have, and the middle one has come off the brick and left two nail holes in the render with nothing in them. The clips are the ordinary pressed kind with a bit of lead behind them where the screws go into the brick, and the middle one of the three has come away with the plug still in the render and the screw still in the plug, and both of them have been on that wall since the office was built.

At the top of that pipe there is a hopper head, a lead box about a foot square, with a grille let into the front of it, and the hopper has been painted the same colour as the pipe and the paint has gone off round the grille where a hand would go. A slot down the side of a pipe is a place where nothing can get in and nothing can get out, and it is there because two walls were built forty years apart and a length of lead was afterwards put up the angle between them by whoever put up the gutter. Nothing at that corner has been repiped and nothing at that corner has been packed out, and the two slots down either side of that pipe are the same width at the top as they are at the bottom except at the collar.

Two fingers against brick and a thumb against lead go down that slot together until they come to a lead collar, and there is nothing under that collar a hand can reach, which is not the same thing as there being nothing under it.

---

She is on the strip of flags between the two buildings, side-on in a gap two feet wide, at the near end of it and a good distance from the corner.

She stands there with her hands down and looks along the side of the store at nothing in particular, and once she turns her feet and stays otherwise exactly as she was, and the wind in that gap is doing the same thing to both ends of it. She goes into neither building and comes no further along the strip. She leaves again by the way she arrived, and the two feet between those two walls is two feet wide again.

The thumb on the pipe and the two fingers on the brick go down that angle together and stop at the same place, and nothing on that corner is going to make them go any further, for the sake of a lead collar that has been there since the office was built.
