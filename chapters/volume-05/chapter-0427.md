# Chapter 0427 — The Same Building

Friday the thirteenth of July 2018, an hour and ten minutes apart, three storeys of one building off a side street, in Tideglass.

**THE MAN IN HIS FORTIES WAS ON THE TOP OF THAT BUILDING FROM TEN PAST TEN UNTIL TWENTY TO TWELVE AND THE MAN IN HIS THIRTIES WAS IN THE TOP FLAT OF IT FROM HALF PAST TEN UNTIL ONE, AND NOT ONE OF THE TWO HAD ANY IDEA THE OTHER WAS ON THE PREMISES, AND THE ONLY PERSON WHO DID IS A MAN ON THE GROUND FLOOR WHO HAS NOT TOLD ANYBODY AND IS NOT GOING TO.**

There are three flats in that building and a flat roof over the top of them with a parapet about two foot high all round, and the parapet is brick on three sides and a leaded coping on the side that faces the yard.

In the June a man put three scaffold boards across from that parapet to the gable of the building opposite, about nine foot away, so that he could get at the top of a chimney stack that is ten foot off that roof and cannot be reached any other way without a tower. He worked on that stack for two days in the second week of June and finished it and came away, and the three boards stayed where they were because they are his and because the stack is still in use and there is no other way up to it.

The flat roof is flat in the way that about nine roofs in this borough are flat, which is to say that it is not.

---

The man in his forties was up there at ten past ten on the Friday morning with his own van at the kerb outside and the back open, because he had come to take the boards off and take them away and had not given himself a reason to be on the roof until he was on it.

The reason he had actually come, which he had not said out loud to anybody including the woman who asked him where the work was, is that the stack on that roof wants repointing in the September and he will have to be up there again, and a man does not put three boards across nine foot of air in June for one job and then take them away in July and put them back in September. He had come to see whether the boards were still sound. He had also come, and he would not have admitted it, to stand on a roof above a street in this borough for most of an hour doing nothing that anybody would pay him for.

He went up the tower at about ten past ten, which took him three minutes, and got himself out over the parapet at about a quarter past ten, and lay down sideways on the boards and worked his way back along them to the far end.

There was about six weeks of pigeon droppings on the boards, and three weeks of rain on the two that had been in the shade, and he took his time about it. He turned both the middle boards over on the parapet, which is where you do a thing like that, and looked at them, and they were sound, and he put them back.

At about half past ten, working on his knees at the far end with his head about four foot below the top of the chimney stack, he let go of a screwdriver.

It went over the parapet and down the side of the building and hit the flags at the bottom of the front area in a way that made a noise, and he lay exactly where he was and looked down over the edge of the parapet at it and could see it from where he was, lying on the flags about two foot out from the front of the building with the sun on it.

He did not go down for it. The tower was in the corner on the other side of that roof and going back to it meant crossing a roof that is not flat, on a wet morning, alone, to pick up a screwdriver off some flags, and the screwdriver was four foot down and visible and not going anywhere.

He went back along the boards and got onto the tower and came down it and shut the tower and left the three boards lying across from the parapet because he had not actually come to take them today. He had come to see whether they were still sound, which was not a thing he had told anybody he was going to do.



At about the same time, in the top flat of that same building, the man in his thirties had the front of a kitchen unit off and was standing in a kitchen two feet by eight with the carcass of it drawn out from the run and a heap of screws on a cloth on the floor.

The woman who lives in that flat goes out at about half past eight and comes in at about one and had left the way in open behind her, as she always does at that hour, because it is only a short way up from the front area and the building has never had anything taken out of it that anybody could remember.

He had been in there since about half past ten. He had the old carcass out and the new one standing on end against the front of the room waiting, and he was doing the same thing he does on every job of that kind, which is to get all of it right and to be slower at it than the price allows.

The man in his forties was on the top of that building for an hour and ten minutes and the man in his thirties was inside the top floor of it for two hours, and the only part of the building that both of them went near was the same fifty foot of structure between the roof and the kitchen, and neither of them ever had any reason to know anything about the other one.

---

The man in his sixties lives in the ground-floor flat of that building and has lived there since 1991.

His back is to the front area. He has a settee in the front room with his back to the chimney breast and he has looked at that chimney breast and at that front of that room so many thousands of times that he could tell you what is on it. He came here in 1991 when the building was a different set of flats with a different name over the top of it and he has been the only person on the ground floor since about 2004, and nobody has ever come and knocked, and there is a bell that has not been wired since before he came.

On the Friday he came in from the front of the building at about half past ten, which is when he has come in at the end of that week for six years, and he shut the front of it behind him and put the light on and sat down and then got up again about a minute later and went to the window of that front room, which is the only window in the ground floor that looks out at the front.

He looked out at a white van standing at the kerb with its back open and both doors up, with a folding tower leaning against the front of the building and nobody by it.

He went on looking.

At about ten to twelve he saw a man come out of the top of the tower and go across the roof and lean over the parapet and stay leaning there for about three quarters of a minute with his head down, and then he came back the way he had gone, and about four minutes after that he heard something hit the flags outside, a single hard noise, and he went to the window again and saw a screwdriver lying in the sun about two foot out from the front of the building.

He looked at the screwdriver for about twenty seconds. He then looked up at the top of that building, and there was nothing to see up there, and he went and made a cup of tea.

He did not go out and pick it up. He did not go upstairs. He did not knock on the top flat to ask whether anybody wanted a screwdriver. He drank his tea with the net curtain open about a foot, and at about a quarter to one he saw a man come out of the front of that building with a bag over his shoulder and go down the side of it and out of the front area, and then the white van went away up the street and was gone.

He has not mentioned any of it to anybody, and there is nobody in that building he could mention it to, and he is not going to raise it with the woman upstairs, and he has not raised it with the man whose van it was, and he did not put his head out of the front of that building at any point in the whole of that morning.



The woman who owns the top flat came back a few minutes before one and stood in the opening of the kitchen and said the room looked about twice as big as it had been, and he said that it was about four inches, and she said that four inches was a lot if you had been cooking in it, and then she said the thing about the tap at the far end, which he had already looked at, and then he carried the old carcass out in pieces, stacked it in his own van at the kerb, and went.

He was on the flags at the bottom of that front area for about four minutes. The screwdriver was still lying there in the sun. He did not look at it and did not see it, and he has never known it was there, and neither of those things is a decision he made.

**HE DRANK IT STANDING AT THAT WINDOW WITH THE NET CURTAIN OPEN A FOOT, AND HE WASHED THE CUP, AND HE SAT DOWN AGAIN, AND HE HAS NOT SAID ONE WORD ABOUT ANY OF IT TO ANYBODY.**
