# Chapter 0237 — The Same Answer

Sunday the first of May 2016, from about half past ten in the morning, a shop on a parade of shops in Tideglass, a room over a shop, and a trade estate four miles out.

**THE SAME ANSWER FOUR TIMES, AND IT IS THAT THEY WOULD SELL TO SOMEBODY ELSE, AND NOT ONE OF THE FOUR HAS EVER BEEN ASKED A QUESTION AND NOT ONE OF THE FOUR IS WITHHOLDING ANYTHING, BECAUSE THERE IS NOTHING TO WITHHOLD.**

Hanna Wray was on the trade counter at half past ten with the lamp on and a box of broken strapping under her elbow, and Jonas Mercer stood at the end of it with a piece of paper and a list on it that he did not read out because he had it by heart.

"You've been at the Pennick end," she said, before he had said anything.

"I've been at the Pennick end."

"Well. You might as well ask it properly then." She put a box down. "Before you go. I'll tell you the four and then I'll tell you the only thing I know, and the only thing I know is worth about four seconds to you. Corrance, Sarn, Lowdale, Marchmont. I buy off two of them and I've done business with both for six years and I have never once asked either of them a question, and I'd go back to them tomorrow and neither of them would think there was anything odd about that, and that's not a thing about them, it's a thing about a trade counter."

"Before I go up there," he said, "you should hear the actual question, because I've had to write it out twice and it sounds like a trick and it isn't. It's this. Our crews have been doing work over the water since the spring. If from the first of June they did none of it, and if every job they'd have taken went to a crew from somewhere else instead, would that put your prices up, and would you sell to those crews?"

"There's your trick," said Hanna Wray.

"Nobody's ever asked them," he said. "That's the whole of it. I could say that in four words and four of them would be lies."

Tomas Renn came through with a crate of rags on his shoulder and put it down and said, without stopping, to nobody, "Still no," and went out the back.

"That's twice now," said Hanna Wray. "And he's not given me a reason either time and I don't know if he has one."

---

Corrance Supply is a room over a shop on a parade, up a stair that smells of carpet cleaner, and there is a trade counter with a lamp over it and a big book open on a stand.

The woman on that counter has been on it a long time and she is the one who keeps the book with the accounts in it, and the yard at Penhale is the biggest single account on that book, and it has been for years.

Jonas Mercer asked the question, the same question, in the ordinary way.

"Course I'd sell to whoever came," she said. "That's not a policy, that's just a counter. If your boys stop coming over the water, the Penhale account gets smaller and the price on the rest of the book goes on a Tuesday instead of a Monday, and I still fill the same amount of paper in the week, and the first person through that door gets it. I'd not know you'd stopped. Nobody would tell me."

"Would you know if the price went up?"

"I wouldn't know and I'm not going to go and find out," said the woman on the Corrance counter, and shut the book, and it was a Saturday in February when somebody last said that to her and it has been a Sunday in May and the answer is the same. "There's a rate on the shelf behind you on a board and I have never read it. It's not mine and it's not my business and a man in a good coat can come up those stairs and read it himself any day of the week, and none of them ever has, and I have never once wondered why."

---

The trade estate is four miles out and there are three of the four on it, which is not an arrangement and is not a coincidence and has been the case for longer than anybody there could tell you.

At Sarn Aggregates there is a weighbridge and a man on it who has been on it a long time and who keeps a kettle in a hut, and Jonas Mercer asked the same question out loud and got the same answer.

"Depends entirely on the work. We've never turned a job away in twenty years. Not one, not for anybody, not for a reason." He nodded at the road. "If your boys stop coming over we lose a bit and the price goes with the work, and we go and find the work. That's it. We are not a party to anything you do with your Tuesday."

"You'd sell to whoever came."

"We'd sell to whoever came. There's no book here with your name in it that anybody's ever asked us to keep. There's a docket, and a docket's where it went and how much, and that's the end of it."

Lowdale Hire is a cabin with a window and a man inside it, and the man in the cabin did not answer it at all.

"I said all of that in the March," he said. "To a woman, at that window, and I said it once and I got the Tuesday out of it and now everybody's had it. If you've come to ask me again you've come for nothing."

"I'm asking once."

"You're asking the same thing once more, which is the same thing." He turned the light down on the desk. "You want the whole of it? Here's the whole of it. Half my turnover in this borough is work that goes out of it on a Sunday and comes back on the Sunday after, because the same crews take it out again the following week, and I've been saying that since the March and I'll say it till one of us dies of it. That's what a Tuesday is. It isn't a scheme. Nobody sat down and made a Tuesday. A Tuesday is a bunch of vans going out and coming back and me putting fuel in them, and if the vans stop I get a quiet week and I put the light down, and if the vans go somewhere else entirely and I never find out, I still get the fuel. That's it. There's no blood in it anywhere and I wish there was, because I'd charge more."

And Jonas Mercer wrote that down, in his own hand, on the page at the back of the notebook that has been in his inside pocket since the summer, and it is about a hundred and thirty words, and none of it was a complaint.

Marchmont's forecourt is on the far side of the same estate and there is a card on a post by the gate. It is a price card, it is for ready-mixed, it is about a tenth above what the shared yard pays, and it has been repainted twice.

There is a man in the office by the gate who reads the card out to anybody who asks and who did it in about four seconds.

"Tenth above. That's the sheet price."

"And if this borough's crews stopped coming over the water, would you be selling more of it or less?"

"More," he said. "There's four counties' worth of road in this region and a week on a Tuesday is nothing. We could double."

"To whom?"

"To whoever walks in." He put the card straight, which nobody had asked him to do. "That's the trade, that. Nobody's coming here to be loyal to a borough. You want a supplier that's loyal, you want a family."

**AND A REGION CAN BE MADE TO GO SLOW BY A SUPPLY CHAIN THAT IS BEING POLITE, AND THE ONLY THING IT COSTS IS A NOTEBOOK AND A WEEK ON A TUESDAY, AND THE PEOPLE WHO WOULD ENJOY IT ARE PEOPLE THIS BOROUGH HAS NEVER MET AND WHO HAVE DONE NOTHING AND WILL HAVE DONE NOTHING.**
