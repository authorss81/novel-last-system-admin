# Chapter 0661 — The Drawer Under The Bench

Tuesday the seventeenth of August 2021, in this borough, and not in it. She is at a house off a smaller road in the middle of the morning and in her own van on a piece of waste ground from about four in the afternoon until about half past five, and the whole of it is a drawer.

**A WOMAN WITH TWO CUTS INSIDE BOTH PALMS DONE FOUR HOURS ON A GATE FOR NOTHING AND WITH NOBODY ABLE TO THANK HER PUT THE BOLSTER SHE USED ON THAT GATE INTO A DRAWER UNDER THE BENCH IN HER VAN AND SHUT THE DRAWER, AND THE SHUT IS THE WHOLE OF THE AFTERNOON, AND THE WIRE BRUSH SHE BOUGHT FOR THAT GATE IS ON A SIDEBOARD AT THE END OF THAT ROAD AND SHE IS NOT GOING BACK FOR IT.**

She is a woman in her late forties. She is on her own from the middle of the morning until about half past five. There is one woman in a house in the morning and she is not in the house in the afternoon.

---

She does the small house first, and it takes an hour and a half, and it is a job worth about ninety pounds, and it is the fourth one this month.

And she does it with a hand open on the outside of the tool bag the whole time because if she closes her hand the one on the left closes on itself and it hurts in a way she does not like.

---

And here is what she is going to do about the cuts, and she does not do it, and it is worth about four minutes of standing in a kitchen in that house.

She has a first aid box in the van. It is a white plastic one with a lid and it has been in every van she has owned since she was twenty-two, and there are plasters in it in three sizes and a roll of tape and a packet of gauze and a thing in a green box for a man who cut himself in a house in Fallowfield in the February of 2019 and bled through a sofa.

And she can use all of it. On anybody.

She has put a plaster on a man in a kitchen at about ten at night in a house in Stretford. She has put two on a woman in a hallway in Hazel Grove. She has held a man's thumb under a cold tap for twenty minutes in a car park in Sale and talked to him about a football match the whole time so that he would not look at it.

And she is standing in a kitchen in a house off a smaller road at about a quarter to ten in the morning of the seventeenth of August 2021 with a first aid box open on the table in front of her, and she is looking at her own left palm.

---

The left one is the worse.

It is a cut about an inch long in the fleshy part below the little finger, and it is not clean, and the edges of it have gone white and puffy where the skin has been wet and then dry and then wet again, and there is grey dust in it from a gate frame.

And the right one is the same cut on the other hand, an inch long, in the same place, from the same lump of old gloss, and it is shallower and it has a plaster on it.

And there is one plaster on it and none on the other one.

And that is the whole of what her hands have come to and it has taken her until this kitchen at about a quarter to ten in the morning on a Tuesday to notice that she has done one and not the other one.

---

And she puts the box down and shuts the lid on it and does not do it.

Not out of manners and not out of a rule. She cannot do it because to do it to herself she would have to be a person who is dealing with a hand, and she is not, and the only way she manages a hand is somebody else's.

That is not a figure of speech. She has thought about it before. She stood in a kitchen in a house in Sale in the February of 2019 and thought about the fact that she could not do this to herself, and she got to the end of it in about four minutes, and what she got to was that she does not know how to be a person in a room who needs something.

Every room she goes into, she goes into it being able.

She has never once in nineteen years walked into a room where somebody might be helped and been the one who was helped instead, and there is no part of her that knows the moves, and you cannot learn the moves at a quarter past ten in the morning in a kitchen in a house off a smaller road.

---

So she gets on with the job.

And the job is a bedroom and a landing and a garage, and the woman who booked it is a woman of about sixty who has been on her own since February and who asks her one question, and it is the only question, and it is at the door at half past twelve as she is leaving.

And she says, "You all right for the stairs."

And she says, "I've had worse."

And the woman says, "You're hurt."

And she looks at her own hand and has to admit that she is, and it takes her about a second.

And she says, "That's a gate."

And the woman says, "Is it."

And she says, "Aye."

---

And then here is the thing that happens at that door, and it is about a second and a half, and it is what she is going to carry round with her for the rest of the day.

The woman does not ask.

That is all. The woman has looked at a hand with a plaster on one side of it and an inch of dried blood on the other side of it, and has been told it is a gate, and has let her go.

---

And the whole of the drive home she is not thinking about the hand.

She is thinking about the fact that a woman let her go.

Because that is the arrangement. That is the arrangement in this borough and it is the only one of the arrangements in this borough that has never cost her anything, and she has had it since she was twenty-two, and it works because nobody asks anybody anything.

And a gate is not a thing you are asked about. A gate is what you say when somebody has already seen that you are hurt and you have to give them a word for it.

And a gate on a Saturday morning at number twelve, with a woman of seventy-eight at a kitchen window and a woman in her late forties on her knees on the grass for four hours, and not one person in that road has ever asked either of them one question about it.

---

And she gets back to the van at about four o'clock on a piece of waste ground behind a row of shops, and she does not go home first, and here is why, and it is the whole of the afternoon.

She puts the tool bag on the tailgate and she opens it.

---

And the brush is not in it.

That is the first thing, and it takes her about two seconds standing at the back of a van. It was in the bag on the Saturday morning and it was in her left hand at half past two, and she has known exactly where it is every day since the seventh of August, and where it is is a sideboard against the far wall of a dark front room at the end of that road, and she put it down there without deciding to, and she has not been up that road since, and she is not going today.

And there is a woman of seventy-eight at a kitchen window in that house, and neither of them has ever said one word about it.

---

And what is in the bag, in the bottom of it, under the chisel and the rubber mallet and the tape, lying across the base of it with the head out of the end, is the bolster.

Her own, off her own board, and there has been one on that board since the winter before last and there is one on it now and there has only ever been the one, and it is the tool that got under the lump, because the lump was on the underside of that frame and a chisel will not go under it and a bolster will, and that is the whole of the reason it came off in about ten pieces and not in a morning.

---

And here is the whole of what is wrong with a tool in a bag, and she works it out standing at the back of a van on waste ground at about four o'clock in the afternoon, and it takes her about a minute.

The bag is for a job.

That is what a bag is. Everything in a bag is in there because of something on the list for the day, and if a thing is in the bag and there is nothing on the list, then a person carrying that bag is carrying a thing they cannot account for.

She knows this. She has known this since a Wednesday of the nineteenth of May, in a footwell, on a different van, with a stone in a bag that he has never found.

She does not think about that and she does not need to.

---

And the gate is done.

That is the other half and it is the half that has been doing the work in her head for the last hour and a half of that drive. The gate is done. The drops are out and the sockets are brushed and the drops are back in the sockets and the gate swings and it does not come in on the path, and it will not bind in the cold now, and there is a woman on that road who will not have anybody back.

And the job is finished.

And a bolster has nothing to be for.

---

And she stands at the back of that van and looks at the bag for a while.

And what she cannot do is shut the bag with it in and go home, and that is the thing she finds out at about ten past four, standing there. The side pocket is where a brush goes on a day, and this is not a day, and there is no day, and a bolster is nine inches of steel and was never going in a pocket.

And what she cannot do either is take it home and put it on a shelf, because she does not have a shelf for tools and she is not a person with a shelf for tools, and a thing on a shelf in her own kitchen would be a thing she would be asked about by a man she has known for nineteen years who is nosy about her van.

And she cannot put it in the bin, and that is not because she does not know what a thing is. It is because a thing you have used on somebody's gate does not go in the bin, ever, and if it did, and if a woman of seventy-eight ever found that out, that would be the end of everything.

---

And there is one place left in that van and it is under the bench on the passenger side.

And that drawer is not a tool drawer. That is the point of it and it is why the drawer is the right place and it takes her about a second to see that and the rest of the afternoon to sit with it.

The tool bag is for a job. The drawer is for everything else.

There are two extension leads in that drawer that have not been used since the van was new. There is a tin of screws. There is a box of trim off a job in Denton in the winter that she was going to use one day. There is a jubilee clip for a part that is not on this van any more. There is a flier for a garage in Sale. There is a chocolate bar that has been in there since the June and has gone soft and she has not thrown it out because throwing it out would mean she was looking.

Nothing in that drawer is for a job. That is the drawer. That is what a drawer is in a van, and it has never been shut since the van was new because there has never been a reason to shut it.

---

And she picks the bolster up out of the bag at about half past four and she looks at it.

And there is a skin of old egg-coloured gloss dried hard on the shoulder of it and there is grey dust in the socket where the handle goes in, and it is not a new bolster and it never was, and it got under four inches of lump on the back of a gate frame in about ten pieces and it did the one thing a chisel in that bag could not have done.

And she thinks: I will keep that.

And it is the only sentence she says out loud in the whole of the afternoon, and she says it to a piece of waste ground behind a row of shops.

---

And then she opens the drawer.

And puts the bolster in it, on its side along the back of it, on top of the two extension leads, and takes her hand out.

And shuts the drawer.

---

And that is the whole of it. That is the Tuesday. A woman with two cuts inside both palms and four hours in her arms that nobody is going to pay her for and no way of ever being thanked for it, and a drawer under a bench in her own van, and the bolster is in it, and the drawer is shut.

And she gets in the van and pulls the tailgate down and starts it, and the drawer does not open again on the drive home, and she does not look at it once, and that is not because she has forgotten it.

**A WOMAN BOUGHT A BAD BRUSH OUT OF A BUCKET FOR A GATE AND GOT THE GATE DONE IN FOUR HOURS FOR NOTHING AND LEFT THE BRUSH ON A SIDEBOARD IN A DARK ROOM WITHOUT DECIDING TO, AND ON THE TUESDAY SHE PUT THE BOLSTER SHE HAD USED ON THAT GATE INTO A DRAWER UNDER THE BENCH IN HER VAN AND SHUT THE DRAWER, AND THE DRAWER IS WHERE THINGS GO THAT ARE NOT FOR A JOB, AND NOTHING IN IT HAD BEEN SHUT SINCE THE VAN WAS NEW, AND IT IS SHUT.**

(End of chapter 0661)
