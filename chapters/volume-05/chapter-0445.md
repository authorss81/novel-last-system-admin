# Chapter 0445 — Thirty-One Steps

Thursday the fourth of October 2018. A weekday morning, from about ten past six until about ten past seven, and then the same half hour again on the Friday, one street, in Tideglass.

**HE HAS KNOCKED THE GRIT OFF THOSE STEPS EVERY WEEKDAY MORNING SINCE HE CAME TO THIS END OF THE BOROUGH, AND ON ONE MORNING A PATCH IN THE MIDDLE OF THE RUN WAS ALREADY CLEAN, AND HE COULD NOT PUT IT DOWN TO ANYTHING, AND HE WENT BACK DOWN THE NEXT MORNING AND DID THE WHOLE THING FROM THE BOTTOM THE WAY HE ALWAYS DOES.**

The street is a terrace of nine houses on the side that faces away from the water, and the building in the middle of it has a green door and a small plate beside the door with nothing legible left on it. It opens at half past seven. There are thirty-one steps up to it, poured in one go, and they are the colour of weak tea, and the treads have a shallow dish worn into the middle of each one where about two hundred pairs of feet have landed in the same place for longer than anybody in that street has been alive.

The crust on them is grit. It comes off the tyres at the bottom of that street, and salt out of the sea two hundred yard away, and it dries into a pale skin about the thickness of a thumbnail at the edges and comes up in sheets in a wet week. You cannot sweep it, because a broom takes the top of it and leaves the underside bonded down. You lift it. That is all the job is.

The man in his forties does that job. He has done it since he came to this end of the borough, and not one person in that building has any idea that any of it happens, and nobody has ever asked him to, and he has not said a word about it to a living soul, and he is not going to. He started it on a Saturday because the step at the house he was living in then was in the same state, and he went at it with a stiff brush and a piece of flat board an inch wide that he had cut off a length of packing timber, and he finished at the top, and it took him most of an hour, and he has been at it ever since.

He is at the bottom of that run at about ten past six. He works from the bottom, always, one step at a time, brush and board, and he lifts the sheet off, puts it in a bag he carries, and does not stop. At the top there is a porch and a mat and the door, and the top three steps are under it and get a lighter crust than the rest because they are in the dry.


On the Thursday of the fourth of October there was a woman standing at the gate of the house next along.

She had a folded pushchair hooked over her arm and a bag of shopping on the brick wall beside her, and she had been there since about ten past six, because there is nowhere to sit on that side and the only thing to do at a gate is stand at it. She had watched him come up the street. She had seen him at that run a good many times in the two years she has lived there. Neither of them has ever said one word to the other. She does not know his name and he does not know hers, and there is no arrangement between them of any kind, and there is not going to be one.

He was about two thirds of the way up when he stopped.

About the twentieth step there was a rectangle of clean concrete about two foot by three, in the middle of the run, and it was clean the way the rest of them were clean. Same colour underneath. Same grain. Not washed and not new and not a different shade from anything.

He stood with the brush in his hand and looked at it for a long time.

---

He went looking through the four explanations that were available to him and he took them one at a time and knocked each of them down.

Rain. It had not rained properly since Tuesday night and it had not rained at all since Wednesday, and a step under rain goes darker and stays darker, and that patch was lighter than the rest and the same tone as the rest with the crust off.

A broom. Nobody had been at that run. The woman at the gate had been at that gate since he came round the corner, and he had watched her the whole time he was on the bottom half, and she had not gone up those steps. The crust standing at the edge of the clean patch is unbroken, and about half of it is still up on the riser above, which a broom would have taken.

His own round yesterday. He had been up there until about ten to seven yesterday morning and he was certain about the order because he has never once done it any other way, and if he had done that patch yesterday he would have done that patch first.

And then the fourth one, which is the one he could not put down and could not push away.

That he has been skipping it. That he has done this every weekday morning for years and has never once watched his own feet, and that somewhere in there he steps over a patch or a run of them, and that he has been getting away with it, and that this is the morning it came out.

He stood there and took that one twice and then a third time and it would not hold either, because he is not a man who skips things. He has cut the heads off about fifty nails in his life because a nail was under his boot in the dark and he was the man who put them in. He does not go past a job.

The patch stayed clean. He did not touch it. He worked the rest of the run in the same order, brush and board, sheet into the bag, and he did the top three under the porch the lighter way, and at about ten to seven he put the bag in the bin at the top of the street and went off down it to his van.

He did not say anything to the woman at the gate on the way past. She said nothing either. She had seen him stop and she had seen him carry on and she had worked out from the way he stood that whatever it was, it was not hers and not anybody's.

---

On the Friday morning he was at the bottom of that run at ten past six and he did the whole of it in the order he has always done it in.

The grit was unbroken the whole way up, including the two steps and a bit where it had been clean the day before. He went past that place at the speed he goes past every other step, and he lifted the sheet off the step above it and the one below it, and he did not break stride and he did not look down.

He was finished at about ten to seven and he went down the street to his van. The woman at the gate was out again with the pushchair folded over her arm, and she watched him do the whole of it, and she said nothing, and neither did he, and the two of them have not said anything to each other on that street since and are not going to.

**AND ON THE FRIDAY MORNING THE WOMAN AT THE GATE STOOD AGAIN WITH THE PUSHCHAIR FOLDED OVER HER ARM AND WATCHED A MAN GO UP AND COME BACK DOWN, AND THEN SHE WENT IN.**
