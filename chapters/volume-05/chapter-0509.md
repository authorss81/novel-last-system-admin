# Chapter 0509 — Two Sacks

The Friday of the second of August 2019, from a quarter past seven until about half past nine, with nothing else in it. A block of flats and the inside of it, in Tideglass.

**SIX YEARS OF A CAN OF WATER UP THOSE STEPS, EVERY MORNING BUT THE SUNDAY, AND IN THE FIRST WEEK OF JULY A MAN ABOVE HER PUT HIS HAND ON THE HANDLE OF IT, WITHOUT BEING ASKED AND WITHOUT SAYING ANYTHING, AND SHE KEPT HER OWN HAND ON THE HANDLE ANYWAY FOR A FORTNIGHT, SO THAT FOR A FORTNIGHT THERE WERE TWO CANS GOING UP AND ONE OF THEM WAS NOT HER CAN, AND ON THE SIXTEENTH OF JULY SHE PUT ONE QUESTION TO HIM AND GOT BACK SOMETHING THAT WAS NOT AN ANSWER TO IT, AND THAT DOOR HAS NOT BEEN OPENED AT HALF PAST SEVEN ON A SINGLE MORNING SINCE THE END OF THAT MONTH.**

The block is thirty-one flats and it goes up three floors and there is no water on the third floor at all. There has not been water on the third floor since the building went up.

There is a tap in the yard at the back. You go out of the front, round the side, about fifty yard, to the tap, fill the can, and carry it back in and up.

The can holds two gallons. It is a galvanised can with a handle soldered on and a screw top, and it is the same can she has carried since 2013, and full it is as near thirty-eight pounds as makes no difference.

She is at number seventeen. She has been at number seventeen since 2013.

She does it six days a week.

Not seven. She is not there on the Sunday, and three people on that floor can see the yard from their own kitchen windows, and not one of them has ever mentioned it.

She does it at about a quarter past seven because the water has to stand about twenty minutes before it is used for anything, and she has done it at that hour for six years and has never once been late and has never once been early.

The man who took it off her is at number eighteen. He is directly above her and he has been in that block since about 2011.


What happened on the second of July is this, and it took the space of a breath.

She came round the side of the building at about a quarter past seven with the can full and got to the bottom of those steps, and he came out of the door of number eighteen in his dressing gown with slippers on and no shoes.

He did not say good morning.

He took the handle.

That is the whole of what happened. He did not ask her whether she wanted him to and she said nothing, and he went up those steps with about half of what she was carrying at the moment his hand closed on it, and she went up with the other half, about four steps behind him, and she has not been able to account for those four steps since.

At the top he held the door and she went past him with the can and set it on the floor of her own entrance, and then he went down and got his coat and went to work.

And that was the Tuesday.


And here is what she did, and it is the part this chapter is about.

She carried it herself the next morning, at a quarter past seven, and he came out and took the weight of it off her again at the bottom of those steps. She carried it again on the Wednesday, and again on the Thursday, and again on the Friday, and again on the Saturday.

She never said no to him and never said yes and never asked him to stop, and he said nothing at all, and for a fortnight there were two cans of water going up those steps at a quarter past seven every morning and both of them belonged to the same woman.

Nobody in that block said anything about it, because nobody in that block has ever had a reason to be on that staircase before about seven in the morning.



And here is what that staircase is, since nobody outside that block has ever been up it.

It is a dog-leg with about nine steps on each turn and a window at the bottom of the first flight that has been painted over since about 2009 by somebody who thought he was helping. The treads are concrete and they are all about the same and none of them is loose.

There are ten flats on that third floor and about seven of them are somebody's grandmother's, and everybody up there has a can and everybody up there has an arrangement.

Number fifteen does hers at about six in the morning. Number twenty-two does hers on a Sunday and not on a Saturday. A man at number twenty does his late at night, torch taped to the front of it, and never before about ten.

Every one of those arrangements has been in place for years and not one of them has ever been mentioned to any of the others, and it was the fifteenth of July before anybody on that floor knew that two cans were going up at a quarter past seven instead of one.

And on the sixteenth of July, which was the Tuesday of the second week, she asked him one question.

She got to the bottom of those steps with the can and he came out and put his hand on the handle, and she said, "Why are you doing this?"

And he let go.

That is the first thing. He took his hand off the handle and put it in his dressing gown pocket and stood there.

And then he said, "It's a heavy can."

And then he went in and shut his door.


She has been through that answer about nine times. It is a true answer, and it is the answer of a man who has been asked a question and has taken the part of it he can stand behind.

She has never got past the middle of it, and the middle of it is that she did not ask him what he was doing.

She asked him why. That is a different question, and there is a version of the whole fortnight in which that is the whole of what went wrong in it, and she has not been able to decide whether she went wrong or he did.


And then on the twenty-sixth of July he stopped.

There was no conversation about it. She came round the side of the building at about a quarter past seven with the can full on the Friday the twenty-sixth and his door did not open, and she stood at the foot of those steps for a minute or so, and then carried it up herself.

She has done it every morning since, six days a week, on her own. He has not come out at half past seven on any morning since.

He has not been out at all, as far as anybody in that block can tell. He goes to work in the morning and comes back at about six and goes up to his own flat and is in.


And here is what this morning is.

She went out at a quarter past seven as she does, round the side, filled the can, and carried it back up those steps, and there was nobody at the bottom of them.

That is the whole of it. It took four minutes and about ten seconds and she has been timing it in her head since the twenty-ninth of July without meaning to, and before the second of July the same walk took closer to six minutes.

The last ten steps she does quicker than she did.

She has worked out that it is not because she is stronger. She has worked out that it is because there is somebody behind her who is not there.


Here is the rest of it on that morning, and none of it is about her.

At about twenty past eight the woman at number fifteen came down those steps with her shopping and stopped where the can was standing.

That is all she did. She put a foot to it and turned it about a foot round so that the handle faced out instead of in, the way it is supposed to face, and she went on down.

And the woman at number seventeen came out of her own flat at about ten past eight to bring it in, and it was the other way round.

And she stood at the top of those steps for about four seconds with her hand on the handle.

And she took it in, and that is the whole of the second of August in that block, and she has not mentioned it to anybody and has not asked the woman at number fifteen whether she moved it, because she knows that she did.

**A WOMAN CARRIED A TWO-GALLON CAN OF WATER UP THOSE STEPS ON THE MORNING OF THE FRIDAY OF THE SECOND OF AUGUST 2019 AND NOBODY CAME OUT OF THE DOOR AT THE BOTTOM OF THEM, AND AT ABOUT TWENTY PAST EIGHT A WOMAN FROM NUMBER FIFTEEN TURNED IT ABOUT A FOOT ROUND AT THE TOP OF THOSE STEPS SO THAT THE HANDLE FACED OUT AND WENT ON DOWN.**
