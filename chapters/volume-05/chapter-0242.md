# Chapter 0242 — Tuesday

Tuesday the twenty-fourth of May 2016, from about eleven in the morning, a shop on a parade of shops in Tideglass, and a kitchen in Marsh Lane.

**THE SAME MECHANISM, RUNNING THE OTHER WAY, ON A BOROUGH THAT HAD DONE SOMETHING GENEROUS, AND IT RAN FOR ABOUT THREE WEEKS, AND WHAT CAME BACK DOWN WAS A WOMAN'S PREDICTION AND NOT A FACT, AND IT WAS NEVER GOING TO LAST, AND THE ONLY PERSON WHO SAID IT WAS COMING IS A MAN WHO SAID IT IN A CAR ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF MARCH AND WAS RIGHT, AND BEING RIGHT ABOUT IT DOES NOT MAKE IT BETTER.**

The price did not arrive on the twentieth and it did not arrive because of the twentieth. It had arrived about a week before that, the way a price does, and it arrived in an invoice, and it came to about nine or ten people at a time and then to a few hundred over the fortnight after that, and every one of them can tell you the exact day it started, and not one of them has the twentieth anywhere near it.

Hanna Wray had it on the trade counter on the twenty-fourth with a delivery docket and a receipt roll and the lamp on.

"Hire," she said. "Not one thing. Hire, and parts, and fuel's gone funny, and fuel only goes funny when there's more of it about than there was, so that's the opposite of what you'd think. And insurance, which I did not expect at all."

"How much?"

"Twenty per cent, near enough, on the one that matters." She turned the roll round. "It'll come back. It always comes back. You want to know what I think happened? Three weeks of crews going out of the borough first, is what happened, and there's crews in the region that used to be ours and aren't ours now, and they buy where they land. That's all. It isn't anybody. There isn't anybody to be angry at, and if you go looking for somebody to be angry at you'll be looking till you're tired, because that's the trick and it isn't even a trick, it's just Tuesday."

A man came in about half twelve and bought four feet of wire and a length of lead, paid for them, and then stood at the counter for a bit.

"I'm a good drain man," he said, to nobody. "That's the whole of me. Nine years and I'm the only one anybody uses in four streets and I've had it to myself and now I'm not going to. Three weeks. Everybody's got a cousin. That's it. Nobody's doing me wrong, they're all right round here, and I went out to two of them on a Tuesday and the first one said it was a bit dear and the second one didn't get up from a chair." He laughed at it. "It's a Tuesday, that's all it is. There's a man on a trade estate four miles out who's been saying that at me since February and he's right, and he couldn't tell me where to put it either."

"Four of them say it," said Hanna Wray. "Four of them can tell you exactly why a Tuesday is a Tuesday, and I have never asked one of them a single question, and I should not have believed a word of it if I had."

---

Marsh Lane is off Peverell Street and there is a woman in the second house who has had a van on the forecourt of the car park at the end of the road every Tuesday since the middle of April, and who hires it out of Lowdale, and who was moving her brother's building materials to a site nine miles out and has been doing it for six weeks.

She was doing it in the kitchen with a piece of paper on the table and a phone face down beside it when Jonas Mercer came to the door, which he did not, and she told him anyway, because he stood in the doorway about four minutes and did not say anything.

"There's no van," she said. "I rang them on Monday and there's no van on a Tuesday. It's Thursdays now, apparently. The Tuesdays went somewhere else and I don't blame them, they've got a Wednesday and a Thursday and two Saturdays and they'd be daft to keep a Tuesday empty for me." She turned the paper round. "That's the same van, mind. Off the same forecourt. Only they wouldn't bring it on a Tuesday, and when they said they would it was a hundred and thirty-six, and the week before it was a hundred and eighteen, and it's the same half-mile of road."

"Did they say that?"

"They didn't have to. It's a market day on a Tuesday. If you want a van on a Tuesday here you pay the Tuesday price, and the Tuesday price is not the same as the Wednesday price, and it isn't anybody's fault, and there is nobody in the world I can ring about it, and I've looked." She put the kettle on. "I've had this job six weeks. I had it because the men building the wall up there went out of the borough on a Monday and came back on a Wednesday and it's been a mess ever since, and I've been ferrying their stuff round in a hired van on a Tuesday for six weeks because that's the only day it fits anybody. And now there's no Tuesday. So I'm doing it in two trips on a Wednesday and it's a whole day out of me and I can't do that, so I paid the Tuesday price, and here we are."

"What happens if it goes on like that?"

"It doesn't go on like that. I can tell you it doesn't, and I'll tell you how I know." She sat down opposite the paper. "It'll come back to about what it was by the middle of June and I'll go back to a hundred and eighteen and be pleased about it, and none of what I've just told you will have been a favour to anybody, and you can put that in whatever it is you're writing down. It happens both ways. That's the bit nobody wants. It went up because we gave a crew to somebody and it will go down because we gave a crew to somebody, and both of those are the same crew, and the price is not a punishment and it is not a reward. It's a Tuesday."

---

Jonas Mercer was in a car park off the parade at about four in the afternoon and he had been there since two and had not gone into a shop and had not been asked anything by anybody, which by then was a fortnight's habit.

He had worked it all out in the March, in a car, on the twenty-seventh of March, on a parade, out loud, to himself and to nobody, in about nine seconds, and he had been right about all of it. Being right had never bought him a single thing in this borough. He had not been asked anything since the eleventh of May and there was nobody he could have said any of it to, and the parade was the same parade it had been in the March, and he got back into the car and shut the door.

**AND THE PRICE WENT UP ON FOUR THINGS AND A WOMAN IN MARSH LANE SAYS IT WILL COME BACK DOWN ON THE SAME FOUR BY THE MIDDLE OF JUNE, AND A MAN WHO IS THE ONLY GOOD DRAIN MAN IN FOUR STREETS IS NO LONGER, AND A WOMAN WHO HAD A VAN ON A TUESDAY FOR SIX WEEKS PAID THE TUESDAY PRICE FOR A VAN ON THE SAME HALF-MILE OF ROAD, AND NONE OF IT WAS DONE BY ANYBODY.**
