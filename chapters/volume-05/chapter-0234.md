# Chapter 0234 — Held

Saturday the sixteenth of April 2016, from about eleven in the morning, a building by the water in Tideglass, and a parade of shops.

**THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN A PERMISSION, AN OBLIGATION AND A CLAIM IS THREE PIECES OF PAPER IN THREE PLACES, EACH OF THEM IN SOMEBODY ELSE'S KEEPING, AND ONE OF THE THREE IS A THING A PERSON MAY DO, AND ONE OF THE THREE IS A THING A PERSON HAS TO DO, AND ONE OF THE THREE IS A THING SOMEBODY ELSE SENT, AND NONE OF THE THREE HAS BEEN ANSWERED.**

Nina Calder comes in about once a month when nobody is asking her for anything, and she was in the records room on the sixteenth of April doing a list of what was left in a store, and she had a cup of tea on the sill and she said the door sticks.

Jonas Mercer stood at the table by the wall and did not open the register, because he never opens it. It is a different book in a hand that has not changed since somebody in this city decided that a register was a good idea, and it is open at the last written page, and it has always been open at the last written page, and he has never once seen it shut.

Nineteen entries. The eighteenth is a door. The nineteenth is a yard light and the maintenance column against it says a bulb, and the bulb is out. Under the nineteenth a line goes down the page and stops about two inches short of the bottom, and there is nothing after it, and it is the only thing in that building that has not moved while an instrument went out of force in it, and that is worse than a relief.

He had a piece of paper in his coat with a date on it and did the sum standing up, in the margin, with a biro, and it came to five hundred and seven, and he wrote it on the piece of paper and put the piece of paper back in his coat, and it was the same sum he has been doing since the summer and he has never had a different answer out of it.

"The west gate's the same as it's always been," said Nina Calder, without being asked, because she was going through the list and it was on it. "Up at seven on a Sunday morning and stops at six on the Sunday morning after that. Six days. It's not a week and nobody ever called it a week, and there's no date on it anywhere in this city, and there isn't one coming."

She went back to the list.

Nobody had put a name in the line on page two of anything. Nobody had renewed anything, or expired anything, or renamed anything, and the working book on that table was a book about two inches deep with a column in it, and the last entry in the column is the fourteenth of September, and the number of things in that column has not been given to anybody and has not been counted.

---

Nell Ardent was on the parade at about twelve with a bag between her feet and a sheet of A4 in her own handwriting, and Jonas Mercer stood with her in the road outside the shuttered shop at the bottom, and neither of them went in.

"Three," she said, and did not read the sheet, and did not need to.

"One's a permission. It's a sheet on a wall in a corridor in a city on the other side of the water, about two hundred people a day walk past it, one line, six inches of type, and it says that nothing is required of any person in order to use any part of that system. The office copy of the same sheet has a second line under it in the same type and I've got both in this bag, in my own hand, off a pen out of my own bag, compared with a ruler, and neither of them is the wall, and the wall is about two hundred people a day."

"And the second one."

"The second one is a sheet on a wall in a room in a town up the estuary, four lines, and a man's name under the second one, and it's been on that wall since about 1994 and it has never been reprinted because there has been nothing to change. It says he is responsible for the works and the clinic and the shelter and the gate and the yard, and that a duty is an act and not a place, and that nothing in it can be given to a person." She had the bag on her knee. "That is a thing a person has to do. It is a sheet in a corridor and a man goes along that corridor about a hundred times a week and does the five of them, and the man of eighty-nine who runs the yard in the next county can do about a third of them and the rest of them are done by a gardener and a woman who sweeps a room, and there is no post in it anywhere and there never has been."

"You've never had a copy of that one."

"He was offered one on the twenty-second of January and refused it, in a yard, standing up, and he was right to, and I was not asked to argue. It's a sheet somebody brought. It is not the sheet on the wall. Those are two different things and he knew that before I did."

"And the third one is in a drawer."

"The third one is in a drawer," she said, "at the end of a corridor, and it came from a city called Alderwick in December under the sixth clause of a permission that is two pages long and in force and has nine names on its schedule, and it is a claim for money, and it is correct, and it has been in this borough since the twenty-sixth of December, and nobody has answered it and nobody is going to, and the woman who has that room has said out loud that she does not know what it is, and she is right."

"That's the three."

"That's the three." She got the bag up off the pavement. "A permission is a thing a person may do. An obligation is a thing a person has to do. A claim is a thing somebody else sent you and you may answer or not answer. And all three of them are correct, and all three of them are in writing, and two of them are on walls eight miles and an estuary apart and have been there for twenty-nine years and twenty-two years respectively, and the third one is a folded sheet in a locked room in a building by the water, and this borough cannot tell you which of the three it would rather had happened, and it is not going to be asked, because there is nobody in it that could be asked."

"That is not an answer."

"It isn't a question," said Nell Ardent, "which is worse."

Nothing in that conversation was minuted. Nobody in this borough wrote either of those two things down anywhere, and there is no minute of the sixteenth of April 2016 in this borough, and there was nobody at that conversation who was sitting as a minute-taker, and the two people who had it were on a pavement, about two feet apart, and neither of them has told anybody else either of the two sentences.

---

The vestibule on the ground floor has three doors off it and a shuttered shop to the left of it and a counter the width of two people, and the door was open because the day was warm for April, and there was a card in the shop window with the shop's own telephone number on it and nothing else on it.

The woman of about sixty-five was behind the counter with her back to the glass and had been there for about nine years and has still not been told anything.

Jonas Mercer stood on the pavement.

He did not go in. There was nothing he wanted, and a person who goes to a counter with nothing he wants is a person who has started using it, and the day that counter is asked what a thing is will be the day it stops being a counter, and it has answered one thing in about a year, twice, in the same words, to a man who wanted to put the low end of Peverell Street somewhere, and nobody was going to ask it anything today. The drawer under it with no key on it and no list on it is shut, and the correct sentence a woman put in it on the morning of the twentieth of May 2015 is in it, and nobody in this borough knows it is there.

The woman of about sixty-five did not turn round, and neither of them said anything, and that is the whole of what happened between them on the sixteenth of April.

**AND THE REGISTER IS AT NINETEEN AND THE LINE UNDER IT IS FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVEN DAYS AND IS STILL BLANK, AND THREE PIECES OF PAPER ARE IN THREE PLACES, ONE OF THEM EIGHT MILES UP THE ESTUARY AND ONE OF THEM ON THE OTHER SIDE OF THE WATER AND ONE OF THEM IN A ROOM AT THE END OF A CORRIDOR IN THIS BOROUGH, AND NOBODY HAS PROPOSED ANYTHING TO ANYBODY, AND FIVE WEEKS AND FOUR DAYS AGO A VAN WENT OUT OF A SHOP ON THIS PARADE WITH THREE PEOPLE IN IT WHO HAD NOT BEEN ASKED, AND ON THE SIXTEENTH OF APRIL A MAN WALKED DOWN THE PARADE PAST THE SAME SHOP WITH A PIECE OF PAPER WITH A NUMBER ON IT IN HIS INSIDE POCKET AND WENT DOWN TOWARDS THE WATER.**
