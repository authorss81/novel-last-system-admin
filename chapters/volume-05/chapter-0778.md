# Chapter 0778 — The Pipe That Goes Nowhere

Monday the twelfth of December 2022, in this borough. He is at his own unit and yard from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a length of pipe put back into a wall and packed, and a wall with nothing wrong on the face of it.

**A MAN IN HIS EARLY FORTIES HAS A LENGTH OF PIPE STANDING OUT OF THE MIDDLE OF A WALL WHERE NOTHING WAS EVER PUT THROUGH IT AND MEANS TO SAW IT OFF SHORT AND CAP IT, THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN PUSHES IT BACK INTO THE WALL AND PACKS THE HOLE OUT AND CLIPS IT, AND THE WALL NOW HAS A PIPE IN IT THAT GOES NOWHERE.**

He is a man in his early forties at the unit on Monday. The woman from two doors down comes through his yard gate in the morning and does the second half her own way.

---

The roller door is up to its bracket on two chains, and the grit that has come out of the bottom of its rail is still lying on the concrete under it. The gates lean against the wall where the frost found them, and what is left of the chalk on them is two marks at one end and a bare square beside them. The latch is hooked back over that gate to the post on the two new turns of wire, and the post is a wall at the top and a wall at the bottom and in between, at the height of his hip, he can still get his thumb into it. The staple on the frame wants April. Out in the yard the hole he filled is standing up proud in the middle of the grass. On the bench is the length of oak with its shoulder out of the square, and under the bench the drawer is shut with the bag for his brush down on the bottom of it. The back wall of the store is dark from the floor up behind the cowl, and it has not gone any darker and it has not gone any lighter.

The pipe is in the side wall of the unit.

It is galvanised, about the width of a mop handle, standing out of the wall at the height of a man's chest, in a run of wall between the roller door opening and the far corner that has nothing else on it. It is about the length of a forearm. There is no elbow on it and no collar on it and no bracket on it. It is a length of pipe with a cut end, painted with the same grey as the wall, and the paint has gone over the edge of the cut end and the cut end is not rusting.

He has looked at it every day since he took this place and has never had a reason to do anything about it. His plan is a hacksaw. He means to put a sheet of card round it, saw it off an inch out from the wall, push a plastic cap over the stub and leave it, because a thing standing out of a wall where nothing was ever put through it is a thing somebody walks into.

The woman from two doors down comes through the gate at the middle of the morning.

---

"You are not sawing that off."

"I am sawing that off."

"You are sawing it off about an inch out and putting a plastic cap on it." She stops beside him and keeps her hands in her pockets. "Have you looked inside it."

"There is nothing inside it."

"You have not looked inside it. You have looked at the end of it from outside, and from outside it is a cut end with paint round it. Look down it."

He puts his eye to it in the cold. The inside of it goes in a long way and darkens, and it is not a hole, because a hole in a wall that size would have a light in it somewhere, or a sky, or cold coming out of it, and this does not.

"It goes nowhere."

"It may go nowhere. It goes somewhere, because it is in a wall and walls are built out of things." She puts the back of her fingers against the paint where it meets the wall. "This is a cavity wall. There is an outer leaf and a gap and an inner leaf, and the gap is about as wide as your hand. In that gap there is no light and no cold and no draught, and there is a pipe going into it, and whoever put that pipe in put it in the gap on purpose, because there is no other reason on this earth to put a pipe into a gap."

"Then it goes nowhere."

"It goes into the gap. That is nowhere you can see and not nowhere at all." She looks down the length of the wall. "And you have not seen any sign on this wall that anything was ever through it, have you."

"No."

"Then nobody will ever know from this side that it is there."

---

That is the argument and it takes the best part of an hour and she wins it in a way he does not expect. He expected her to say do not cut it because it might be something. She says cut it off if you like and put the cap on, and then asks him what he is going to put in the hole in the wall when he has cut it off.

"A pipe is a hole, and a cap is a lid on a hole, and it is the only thing between the weather and the middle of a wall, and it is made of plastic and it is four years before anybody looks at it."

"So what would you do."

"Put it back in."

---

They do it with a lump of wood and a hammer.

She puts a piece of the lump across the end of the pipe and hits it, and the pipe goes in about a finger's width and stops, and then she puts the lump flat on the end and hits it again and it goes in another finger's width, and they go at it from four sides until the whole end of it is inside the wall and there is a collar of old mortar standing proud round the hole it left.

"You have not got it in."

"I have got it in far enough that nobody can walk into it."

"That is far enough, because far enough is all anybody needs of it, and it has to be that far and no more. Leave it where it is until I have the packing."

She cuts a piece of hardwood off a length she brought, squares it to the size of the pipe and a finger's width bigger all round, and drives it in over the end of the pipe with a lump and a piece of waste behind it, going in on the diagonal so that it cannot come back out the way it went in. She cuts it off flush with the face of the wall with a chisel and knocks the arris off with a file, and then she runs a bead of sealant right round the edge of it where the wood meets the paint.

---

And then the clip, which is her own idea. She brings it out of her bag already made, a galvanised pipe clip with a fixing plate on the back of it, the kind that goes over a pipe and has one screw in the middle of the plate.

"It only stands out a finger's width now, but it is a pipe end at head height on a wall everybody walks past, and in a year that end will be bright where things catch it." She sets the clip against the pipe. "One screw in the plate and one in the jaw and it is on there for ever and nobody breaks it off."

"And when I want to get at it again."

"Then you take two screws out."

---

By the end of the afternoon the sealant has gone off and there is nothing on that wall at all. He stands in front of it with the lamp and looks along the run from the roller door opening to the corner. The bare timber is not the same colour as the wall, and in a raking light he can see the shape of it, a patch about the size of his hand with the clip over the middle of it, and nothing standing out anywhere.

That is better than it was. That is a wall.

Then he puts his knuckle on it, either side of where the pipe is, and knocks.

---

The knock is the thing he will remember.

He knocks on the face of the wall about a foot to the left of the pipe and it is a flat knock, the sound of a wall. He knocks again about a foot to the right and it is the same. He puts his knuckle on the wall directly over the pipe and knocks and there is nothing under it at all.

Not a room. Not a floor. Not a joist.

Nothing.

He knocks round about it and every part of it is solid, and there is one place in the middle of a wall, about the width of a hand across, that is not.

She comes and stands at his shoulder and puts her own knuckle on the two spots, and they get the same answer both times.

"So it is in the gap."

"It is in the gap." She is not taking her hand off the wall. "That is what I told you. That is a cavity and there is nothing in it but air and that pipe, and there has been nothing in it since it was built."

He puts his thumb on the timber he packed into the hole this morning. It is hard and cold, and the dust on it is the same colour as the dust on the wall.

She takes her hand off the wall.

"One of us is going to want to open that wall one day."

And he stands in his own unit with his knuckle against a wall with nothing on the face of it and a pipe in the middle of it that goes nowhere, and no mark on it to say it is there, and nobody alive who knows why it is there.
