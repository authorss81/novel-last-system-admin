# Chapter 0823 — Not The Same Hole

Sunday the twenty-fifth of June 2023, at the back of that lock-up from the middle of the morning until the light has gone off that bay, and a fence door hanging by one strap along that boundary is the whole of that Sunday.

**A FENCE DOOR ALONG THE BACK BOUNDARY OF THAT LOCK-UP IS HANGING BY ONE STRAP BECAUSE THE SCREWS HAVE PULLED OUT OF THE END GRAIN OF THE POST, AND SHE MEANS TO TAKE THE LEAF OFF ITS HINGES AND PUT IT BACK ON PROPERLY, AND THE WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK IS IN THE OFFICE ALL DAY WITH THE DOOR SHUT AND IS NOT SEEN.**

That door is about ten feet along the back boundary from where the three bowed lengths of paling went back in June, and it is a ledged gate, featherboard on a frame with a brace across it, four feet by three, and it has been a door since before that bay was a bay.

It has been hanging off the top hinge.

The bottom strap is on the post and it is fine. The top strap is on the post and it is four inches out from where it should be, standing proud, and the two screws that fix that strap to the post are not in the post any more.

They are in the post by about a quarter of an inch of their own shanks. She can put a finger on each head and push and feel the timber move.

---

This is the fault everybody meets and everybody gets wrong.

The screws have come out of the end grain of the post.

That is not a fault in the screws. It is not a fault in the timber and it is not a fault in the strap and it is not anybody being careless. It is what happens when a screw is put into the end of a piece of wood with a strap on the end of it and a gate hanging off the strap.

Wood does not grip a screw in its end grain the way it grips a screw in its side grain. There are no fibres running that way. The screw goes in by splitting the end of the post and it sits there for years perfectly well, and the gate swings on it a hundred times a day, and every one of those hundred times the whole weight of the gate comes on the strap and the strap puts the whole of it on the end grain and out through the face of the post, and the split closes up and the next hundred goes the same way.

Then the gate is out in the wind one day and the strap comes off.

She has seen that door out three times in the four years she has had that yard. It has been put back three times with the same two screws into the same two holes, which is what anybody does, and it has stood about a year and a half each time.

---

So she does not do it the fourth time, and here is the whole of what she has to think about before she does it.

The things that do not work:

A longer screw does not work, because a longer screw into the same hole goes deeper into the post and the post is hollow at that depth and there is nothing down there to bite on.

A bigger screw does not work, because it is the same fault in the same timber with a bigger head on it, and it carries on longer and then it lets go.

Another screw in the same place does not work, and that is the one that gets done most often.

Putting a peg in the hole does work, if the peg is turned to the hole and glued and sawn off flush, and she has done that twice on other gates in other yards and it is a good repair.

What she is going to do on this one is move the strap.

She takes the leaf off its hinges.

A ledged gate of that size is about the weight of a bag of coal and she can lift it, and she does, and she lays it down flat on the ground at the back of that yard on two boards with the strap end uppermost and the bottom of the gate against the front of her boots.

Then she undoes the four screws, which is the first thing she does not have to do twice, and she lifts the strap off the post and puts it down on the gate beside her.

The strap is an inch and a half wide and about five inches long, and it has two holes in it and the holes are an inch and a half apart.

Those two holes are the whole of the argument.

She takes the strap and lays it on the face of the post where it was and then slides it along the post, away from the old holes, until the two holes in it are about an inch and a quarter in from the near edge of that face.

That is as far as it will go, because a strap screwed within a quarter of an inch of the edge of a post is a strap with the edge of the post shearing off in front of it, and it is a quarter of an inch from the edge already.

So the new holes are an inch and a quarter from that edge and about two and a half inches from the old ones.

That is not much of a move and it is exactly enough, because an inch and a quarter in from the edge of a four-inch post is the middle of the face of it, and the middle of the face of a post is side grain with fibres running across the fixing, and a strap in the middle of the face of a post carries a gate until the gate falls down.

She opens the strap up where it is folded round the post and drills through it and through the post at the two new marks, and she puts the same four screws back in the same four holes — the two at the gate end go back exactly as they were, because that end is into the gate stile which is a rail on edge and it has always been sound.

Two screws. And that is the repair.

She puts the gate back up about two in the afternoon.

It goes on in about a minute, and she puts a wedge under the bottom corner and steps back and looks at it.

It shuts.

That is the thing she wanted and it is not the thing that is remarkable about that gate. The remarkable thing about that gate is that for about a year and a half at a time it has hung on one strap and swung open about four inches on its own and had to be pulled to and pushed to.

It shuts because the top corner has come back up against the stop on the post. The strap had stretched that far in the wind and the screws going had allowed the corner to drop, and with the strap properly on, the corner is back against the stop and the gate is square.

She opens it and shuts it three times. It shuts the same way each time.

The woman who books work is in that office with the door shut all day and the shutter is down over the roller door and neither of them is in that yard and neither of them comes out of that building.

A gate on straps goes back up by eye and it goes back up by feel.

You cannot get the gate up to the strap and keep it there and get both hands to the screws, so the first screw goes in with the gate resting on the slack in the strap and its other end propped on a length of timber wedged under it.

Then you come out from behind it and look at the joint between the strap and the top of the gate from about four feet away, from the side, at the height of your own eye. If the gate is hanging on that strap, the joint is a single line. If the gate is leaning back off the strap and doing the work itself, the gap opens at the top and there is daylight in it.

That gap is the whole of a gate. A gate that stands proud of its strap sags off the top hinge inside the first year and then its own weight finishes it.

This one has a single line at it. She puts the second screw in with the gate on that line, and then takes the timber out from under it, and the gate stays exactly where she left it, which is the only test there is.

The last ten minutes of that Sunday is her standing at that gate with it shut, looking at two holes in that post.

They are an inch and a half apart and they are above the strap, not under it, because the strap was moved down the post by an inch and a quarter and the holes it used to be fixed through are now sitting above the line of the strap where it runs.

They are about five inches above the strap line and set well in from the edge of that post, and they are oval and not round, because a screw that has worked its way out of end grain for a year and a half leaves a hole wider across the grain than up it.

They are perfectly clean holes and there is nothing wrong with either of them.

There is nothing going back into them and there is nothing going near them and they are not going to be filled and they are not going to be plugged and there is no reason anybody should ever look at them again.

They will be in that post for as long as the post is up, and the strap above them is an inch and a quarter lower and it is on the middle of the face of that post now, and the gate shuts on it and will go on shutting.
