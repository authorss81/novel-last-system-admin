# Chapter 0297 — A Bath

Tuesday the seventh of February 2017, from about half past two in the afternoon, a first-floor landing in a building by the water in Tideglass.

**A MAN WITH A VAN AND NO SIGN ON IT CARRIED AN ENAMELLED BATH UP THE OUTSIDE STAIR OF THAT BUILDING AND PUT IT DOWN ACROSS A HALF LANDING, AND THE WOMAN WHOSE DOOR STANDS OPEN THREE INCHES CAME OUT AND SAID ONE WORD AT HIM, AND HE SAID RIGHT, AND IT WENT BACK DOWN THOSE STAIRS AND STAYED IN THAT YARD WHERE A CHAIR HAS BEEN STANDING SINCE JANUARY.**

The bath was a steel one, enamelled, cream gone yellow at the ends, about five foot long, with a pipe and a tap still on the outside of it and one of the two taps missing off the other end. It had come out of a house on a road off the Cleeve one in the first week of January, and that house had a new one in by the middle of January, and since the middle of January it had been on the back of that van under a tarpaulin, and it had been to two car boot sales in February and had not been taken at either.

The man who owns that van is at the end of Marsh Lane and does not live at the end of Marsh Lane, and the van has no sign on it, and he was standing in that line on the third of January for a bag of coal that he did not take and whose source he could not say, and he has not seen anybody from that line since and did not want to.

He was in that street on the Tuesday of this week for a job on a shop about three doors from the top of Marsh Lane, and the work took him until about two in the afternoon, and the bath was under the tarpaulin, and he had to move the van at half past two because the shop he had been in wanted its yard, and there was a handcart in that yard with one wheel on it and it wanted two hands.

He carried the bath up the outside stair of the building by the water instead.

---

It is a long stair and there is a half landing about two thirds of the way up, and he went up the first part of it with the bath under one arm and his other hand on the rail, and stopped on the half landing to shift it, and went up the rest of it, and put it down flat across the landing against the wall under the window.

The woman in the room next door along, counting past the fire door, had her own door open three inches at that moment, and she came out into the landing in a cardigan over her nightdress with her hands held in front of her, and she let the fire door come back to on its own hinge and stood against her own frame.

They looked at each other across about nine feet of landing with a bath lying on it.

"No," said the woman in the room next door along.

"Right," said the man with the van, and got his hands under it again.

He said it the way a man says it when he has just carried a thing up a long stair with it and has worked out on the way up that it is coming back down.

"I had a bathroom out at a place off the Cleeve road in January," he said. "That's off it. The house has got a new one in. I've had that on the van since the middle of January and I've been to two of them sales and there's nobody wants a bath."

"That's not for me," said the woman in the room next door along.

"No," said the man with the van.

He shifted his grip and looked along the landing, and then at the shut door at the end of that corridor, which neither of them said one word about.

"So where am I putting it," he said.

"Anywhere but across here," said the woman in the room next door along. "That's the way in past my door, and I'll not get down to that fire door with a bath lying in front of it."

"That's the passage, then."

"They read the meter in that passage," said the woman in the room next door along. "You leave a bath across there, nobody can get at the meter, and the next person down that passage goes over it in the dark and there'll be nobody at this building for a good while after that."

The man with the van stood where he was with a bath in his arms and did not say anything for a second.

"Right," he said. "You've told me the landing's out and you've told me the passage's out. That's not the same as telling me where it goes. I don't think you know where it goes. It's not yours and it never was, and you've not got a place for it."

---

He took it back down. The woman of about fifty-five was in that passage at about ten past three with her bag in her hand, coming in off the street door, and she stood to one side and let him come past with it, and he said excuse me and she said go on, and neither of them said one word about it.

He carried it across the ground floor and out into that yard.

The chair was where it had been put in January, up against the gable wall on the high side of the yard where nothing runs off the building, with a blanket lying over the back of it and the ground underneath gone hard and white. He put the bath down on its side on the far side of that yard, up against the wall under the passage window, where the water that comes off that building does not reach and where the light from the passage does not get in.

"There," he said, to nobody. "That's it, then."

The roller door at the front of that yard is off the side passage and there is a wooden gate at the back of it, and he went out through the gate and down the passage and pulled it to behind him, and there is nothing on that gate and there never has been.

The man who works nights came in at about six that evening and found it there when he came down, and stood in that yard in the light from the passage window looking at the side of a bath, and then went up.

The woman who works in a shop went up that parade on the Wednesday afternoon on her way to the bus stop and saw it through the gate, standing on its side against a wall, and looked at it for a moment and went on to the bus stop, and she has not said to anybody where it came from and does not know.

Nobody in that building asked the woman of about fifty-five whether she wanted a bath, nobody put one past her door, and the hall off the parade, with its own meter on its own wall, is a long walk from that gate and nothing was carried to it.

He did not come back with a second one, and nobody asked him for one, and on the Thursday he was at the end of Marsh Lane with the tarpaulin over an empty back.

**AND THE WOMAN WHOSE DOOR STANDS OPEN THREE INCHES HAS NOT PUT THE WORD SHE SAID TO ANYBODY, AND IF IT WERE PUT TO HER NEXT WEEK SHE WOULD NOT BE ABLE TO ACCOUNT FOR IT, AND NOBODY IN THAT BUILDING IS GOING TO PUT IT TO HER, AND A BATH ON THE BACK OF A VAN IS NOT ANYBODY'S GIFT AND WAS NOT TURNED AWAY AS ONE.**
