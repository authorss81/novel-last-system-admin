# Chapter 0453 — The Sign on the Wall

Wednesday the fourteenth of November 2018, twenty past ten in the morning, for the best part of half an hour. A street off a parade and a corner of it, in Tideglass.

**THEY AGREED, NEITHER OF THEM WROTE ONE WORD OF IT DOWN, NEITHER OF THEM IS GOING TO, AND IT HOLDS BECAUSE NOBODY WROTE ANY OF IT AND NOBODY EVER WILL.**

The sign is on the wall of a corner building where a street off that parade comes out, at about the height of a man's shoulder, and it is a small enamelled plate about eighteen inch by ten with a maker's name and two lines of a firm's address and the word OPEN in the top corner, and it has been on that wall since before either of those two men came to this town.

It is the wrong sign. It is not the firm that has that corner. It has been the wrong sign for about six years, which is as long as one of the two men on that corner has been working out of a place near there, and the other came into the borough the year before him and cannot remember it being anything else.

There is a chip out of the enamel about as big as a halfpenny, and it has gone under the last letter of the maker's name and taken the white with it. The word OPEN is set in a bar of the same enamel at the top, and the bar has run under a rust spot and gone orange. Of the two lines of address underneath, one of them names a street that is not in this town and the other one is only a post office box. It has been like that long enough that nobody in that street reads it any more. They walk past it the way you walk past a post somebody put there and forgot.

---

The man in his forties was at that corner at about twenty past ten on the fourteenth of November, and about twenty yard along the same wall the man in his thirties was standing, and they got there at about the same moment by accident, and then they stayed there.

What they said was about nine sentences and it took about four minutes.

The man in his forties said the sign had been on that wall when he came.

The man in his thirties said it had been on that wall when he came, and that it was about six years ago, and that the firm it names had gone out of business in a place about thirty miles off, and that the plate is on the wall of a building that the firm has never had anything to do with.

The man in his forties said somebody ought to take it down.

The man in his thirties said yes.

Then he asked how the other one knew it had been on that wall as long as that, and the answer was that he had stood there and watched a man in a hi-vis put a notice on a board at that corner about five years ago, and then come back with a folded paper and a roll of tape and take a plate off a wall about ten foot from where the two of them were standing, and write the man's name and the date on the paper and put the paper inside the door, and that the man had not said anything at all while any of that was going on.

The man in his thirties said, "And?"

The man in his forties said, "That was the end of anybody touching that wall."

Then he said, and here is the whole of the problem, that the last man who took anything off a wall on that street was fined for it by a man in a hi-vis, and that nobody since has laid a hand on that wall.

The man in his thirties said, "Who'd you ring?"

The man in his forties said, "There's nobody to ring."

And that is the entire conversation.


Here is why it is going to stay on that wall until it falls off it, and this is the part that holds.

The man whose building it is on is not anybody either of them knows. He is about thirty-five and he has been in that corner about three years and he is in and out between about seven and about six and he does not stop on that corner, and neither of them has ever been in that building and neither of them has ever been in his house.

And the plate is not about a person. That is what makes it different. If it were a man's name on a door, you could go along and say it to him.

Nobody is going to do that. The two of them did not discuss it and they do not need to, because there is nothing to discuss. One of them would have to go into a building, up some steps, and ask a man he does not know to take a plate off his own wall, and explain why, and the explanation would be: a stranger on a corner said so.

There is no version of that in which a man takes a plate off his own wall because a man he has never met said so on a pavement.

And there is no note. Nothing on paper anywhere in this borough says one word about that plate on that wall, and nothing ever will, and if there were not a thing in the world either of these two men would put down.

---

It holds the way that holds.

It held on the Friday, when the man in his forties was at that corner at about eight in the morning, and he looked up at the plate while he was waiting for somebody who was not coming, and it was on the wall, and he did not touch it and he did not say anything about it.

It held on the Sunday, when the man in his thirties came down that street at about noon with a woman and two children and pointed at something else entirely on the building opposite and never looked up.

It held on the Saturday after, when both of them were within about three hundred yard of that corner at the same time on the same afternoon for the first time in about a year, and neither of them mentioned it, and neither of them noticed the other, and it is not going to matter, and that is the thing about it.

There is nothing to hand anybody and there is nobody to hand it to. Two men said about nine sentences on a pavement and went to their vans, and that is the whole of what there is, and it will still be the whole of what there is in ten years.

**THEY STOOD AT THE TWO ENDS OF TWENTY YARD OF WALL AND AGREED WITH EACH OTHER ABOUT A PLATE, AND WALKED OFF IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS, AND THE PLATE IS STILL ON THE WALL.**
