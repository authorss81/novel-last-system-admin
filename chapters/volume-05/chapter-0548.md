# Chapter 0548 — Three Days at Nine O'Clock

The Saturday of the twenty-second of February 2020, and thirteen days of it, in one building on a wide road and in an office off it.

**NINE DAYS WERE WORKED ON THE OUTSIDE OF THAT BUILDING BETWEEN THE SEVENTH OF FEBRUARY AND THE NINETEENTH AND SIX OF THEM HAVE BEEN PAID FOR, AND THE MAN WHO KEEPS THE HOURS HAS KNOWN SINCE THE FIRST WEEK, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED HIM.**

The building is a brewery that stopped being a brewery in about 1998 and is now offices with a name on it, and what the name is does not matter, except that it means the money belongs to somebody else and comes from a long way off.

The work is on the outside. It is thirteen days of it so far and it is the same nine men every day and it is weather work in the sense that a day is a day or it is not, and the man who decides which is a man in his late fifties who has kept the hours in that company for nine years and has never been anything else.

He has a book. It is an ordinary book with a week down the side and ten rows in a week and the ten rows are the ten men, and he writes the days in, and he has never in all that time left a day out.

They started on the Friday of the seventh and finished on the Wednesday of the nineteenth, and in between they worked nine days, and six of them are in the book with a figure against them, and three of them are not in the book at all.

The man in his late fifties has not written those three anywhere. He has not written them in the book, and he has not written them in a list, and he has not written them in anything at all, and he has not told anybody, and he is not going to.


They went in on the Friday of the seventh and the two before that week were wet, and the company were behind, and everyone knew it and nobody said it.

The man in his mid forties is the oldest of the nine on the outside. He has been doing this kind of work for about twenty-two years and he does the top of the long side, which is the side that goes first in a wind, and he has never once been the one who decides to stop.

On the Wednesday of the twelfth it was cold and the wind was coming across the open ground from the water. They were on at seven. At about nine they were told to go home. Not the man in his late fifties told them. A young man came out of a vehicle and said the word to the man in his mid forties, and the man in his mid forties said, "Right," and took his things down, and everybody took their things down.

It happened again on the Thursday and on the Friday.

That is three days. Six days are in the book and paid. Three are not in the book and are not paid, and nine days were worked, and both figures are true, and neither of them is wrong.
The young man who said the word is not a manager. He is about thirty and he is on that job because the man in his late fifties telephoned him at about half past six and said the word to him and he came out and said it to the man in his mid forties and got back in and drove away.

He came out three times in five weeks, on the twelfth, on the thirteenth and on the fourteenth, and he has driven away from a building where nine men are standing in the cold with their things down, and he has not thought about it since the fourteenth and he will not think about it now.

The men themselves did not mind on the day. That is the part that has stayed with the man in his mid forties. They took their things down and they went, and two of them said that it was daft, and one of them said nothing at all, and none of them asked when it would be paid and none of them asked whether it would be paid.

On the Monday of the seventeenth, the first morning after, the man in his mid forties came in at seven and worked a full day, and nobody said anything about the Wednesday before, and he did not either.

---

The woman in her mid thirties works in the office of the same company, on the ground floor, and has been there for six years, and she is not on the outside and never will be.

She worked it out in the second week, without asking anybody, and the way she worked it out was that the man in his mid forties had stopped saying good morning.

He has said good morning to her for six years. He stopped on the Thursday of the thirteenth, in the kitchen at the place where they eat, and he has not started again, and he is not a man who goes quiet for a reason anybody would recognise.

She has not asked him. She has not asked the man in his late fifties either. She has looked at the book, because the book lies open in front of the man in his late fifties all day and nobody has ever thought to turn it away from anybody.

She looked at it on the Friday and worked out that three of the nine are missing.

She is not the person who is paid short and she is not the person who has been paid short, and she is the only person in that company who now knows, and she has not spoken to the two men or to anybody else about it, and she has not written it down.

---

On the Saturday of the twenty-second the man in his mid forties comes in at seven, which he does not have to, because there is nothing left on that building to do that week.

The man in his late fifties is in there with the book. The building is empty and they are in one half of it with the heating on and the windows out of one side, and it is colder in there than it is outside.

The man in his mid forties puts his things down and says one sentence.

"Twelve, thirteen and fourteen were the three."

The man in his late fifties does not look up. He carries on writing, and the sentence goes into the air and stays there.

"That was a fortnight ago," the man in his mid forties says, and that is the second sentence, and then he does not say anything else for about a quarter of an hour.

The man in his late fifties goes on writing. There is nothing to write, because there is nothing left on that building this week, and he is writing the week out anyway, and he fills in what he would have filled in.

They are the only two people in that half of that building, and neither of them has mentioned the three days to the woman in the mid thirties who has seen the book, and the man in his late fifties has still not written the three days anywhere, and he is not going to, and the money for the three days does not exist and is not going to.

**HE SAID TWO SENTENCES IN A HALF-BUILDING WITH THE WINDOWS OUT AND NEITHER OF THEM LOOKED UP, AND THE ONLY PERSON WHO KNOWS ANYTHING ABOUT IT IS ON THE GROUND FLOOR AND HAS ASKED NOBODY.**
