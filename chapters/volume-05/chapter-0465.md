# Chapter 0465 — The Coping at the End of the Cut

Sunday the sixth of January 2019 and the seventeen days after it, from about ten past seven on a weekday morning until about half past eight on the Sunday. A length of pavement and a low wall, in Tideglass.

**SOMETHING THAT HAD BEEN ON A WALL INSIDE THAT CUT EVER SINCE HE WAS FOURTEEN WAS NOT THERE ON A SUNDAY IN JANUARY, AND HE TOOK A DIFFERENT WAY FOR A FORTNIGHT AND THEN TOOK THE OLD ONE BACK, AND THE PERSON WHO OWNS WHAT WAS ON THAT WALL HAS HAD IT INSIDE A CLOSED DOOR SINCE DECEMBER AND HAS NOT TOLD A PERSON IN THIS BOROUGH ANYTHING AT ALL.**

There is a way through the middle of that block, about four foot wide, between the end wall of a row of eight and a boundary wall that belongs to nothing anybody in the row has ever heard of. It is the shortest way from that row to the street behind and everybody who lives in the row uses it and everybody who does not goes round.

He has used it twice a day for nine years.

At about ten past seven in the morning he comes out of the third house, turns left, and goes up the cut, and at about ten to six in the evening he comes the other way. That is the whole of it. The cut takes about half a minute and going round takes about two minutes, and the difference is the thing he has been arguing with himself about since the first week of January.

Six foot of the boundary wall runs the length of the cut on the right-hand side going in. It is old brick, about two foot thick where it is not buttressed, with a coping on top of it that slopes about fifteen degrees, and about four foot up the wall, opposite the third house, there is a dent in the render about the size of a dinner plate where a downpipe has been coming out of it for longer than the row has been here.

The cat sat on that coping.

It was a black cat and it was not big. It sat with its front feet together about eighteen inch in from the corner, on the high side of the coping where the fall takes the rain away from it, and it faced back down the cut, so that anybody coming up towards it saw the front of it and anybody going out saw the back of it.

It was there at about ten past seven, and at about ten past seven it had been there every morning for nine years, and it was there on Saturdays, and it was there on the mornings when it was raining hard enough that the render under the coping was running.

Where it went to, nobody in that row ever found out.

That is the thing about it. It came out of one of the doors on the street behind and it went back into one of them, and to get to the other side of that coping a cat has to come up off the coping on the corner and go down onto the footway of the street behind and walk about four yard and go in. In nine years he has not once been in that street behind at ten past seven in the morning, because he goes to work, and he has never once gone up to that coping to look at where the cat went, because a man does not do that, and because by the time he got there the cat would be gone in about four seconds and he would be standing on a wet corner in a suit looking at a door.

So the cat is a thing that is on a wall, and where it comes from is a thing he has never known, and the two of those have sat together in his head for nine years without anybody ever breaking them apart.

On the morning of the sixth of January it was not there.

---

He was about two foot inside the cut when he worked it out.

What he had done was the thing he does about nine times out of ten, which is look at that coping as he goes past it, without turning his head, the way you look at a place you have looked at every day since you were a boy. And on the sixth of January there was nothing on the coping.

There was no cat on it and there was no dent in it and there was nothing at all on it, and the render under it was running where it always runs, and he went out through the cut and up the street and got to work at about four minutes past eight, and the whole of that morning was gone before he put it anywhere.

The following morning he was two foot inside the cut again, and the coping was empty, and this time it registered.

He stood there for about three seconds in the middle of that cut, which is four foot wide and which you cannot stand in the middle of without somebody in one of those two rows being able to look out of a window and see you, and then he went the long way round.

He went the long way round fourteen times.

The long way is up the front street past the shop that shuts at half past four, past the garage where the door is up on Fridays, and in at the top where the two roads come together. It is about a minute and a half longer going in and about two minutes longer coming out, and it is nine minutes a day, and he did the arithmetic inside about four days and then stopped doing the arithmetic and just did the going round.

Nobody in that row said anything to him about it.

That is worth saying plainly, because it was not that nobody noticed. About four times a day somebody in one of those eight houses looked out of a window and saw a man who has never gone round that cut in nine years go round it. It went on. Nobody remarked on it. There was no point at which it would have become remarkable enough to say something about, because a man walking a different route is not a thing a person in a terrace of eight interrupts their morning to ask about, and because by the end of the first week the man himself had stopped registering that he was doing it, which means the two of them would have had to be quite lucky for anybody to break first.

He went round it on the Sunday, and then on the Monday, the Tuesday, the Wednesday and the Thursday, and then on the Friday and on the following Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday.

On the twenty-first, which was a Monday, he went up the cut.

There was nothing on the coping when he got to it. He went through at ten past seven and out the other side and up the street and got to work about a minute and a half earlier than he had been getting there for a fortnight, and the minute and a half bought him nothing at all, and he noticed that about two hundred yard up the street, and then he stopped noticing it.

He has not gone round since. He goes through that cut twice a day and he looks at the coping and there is nothing on it.

---

She is about thirty yard up the street behind, and she is the second door along from the corner.

She has lived in that building about seven years. Her front room is on the ground floor and its window looks at the corner where that coping is, across about thirty foot of open space where there is nothing at all, not even a car, because people turn into the yard behind that building rather than pass it.

The cat came out of her door.

That is not in dispute and she is not the only person who knows it. It is only that nobody who knows it has ever been in a position to say so to a man who crosses the other end of a cut thirty yard away at ten past seven on a weekday morning and has never once looked over that wall.

She has not let the cat out since the last week of December.

That is the whole of what happened. Nobody has put a question to her about it and no one is going to, and she is not going to offer it. She has been through why twice and got the same answer and she is not going to go through it a third time, and the answer is not a good one, and it does not matter that it is not a good one.

What she has done about it practically is nothing at all. She has not put anything out. She has not left the front door on the latch for it, which she used to do, and she has not left a gap, and she has not told the woman next door, who feeds a cat of her own and who would have had it inside in about four minutes.

What the cat has done about it is find somewhere else, and she knows where, because she has seen it twice from that window, and it is thinner than it was in December and it is not starving and it is not hurt and it is going into a garden at the top of that street by way of a fence that has a place on it where the render has come off.

On both of those occasions she was at that window and on neither of them did she go out of the door.

On the sixth of January, at about ten past seven, she was at that window for a reason that had nothing to do with the cat, which is that she works afternoons and does not go out until one, and the window is where the sun is between one and two.

She saw a man come out of the third house of the row opposite, go left, and turn into the cut. She saw him stop. She saw him stand in the middle of that cut for about three seconds, which from where she was looks exactly like a man who has remembered something, and then she saw him come back out of the cut and go the other way.

She watched him go the long way round.

She sat at that window for a while without doing anything at all, and what she was doing in it was not thinking about the cat.

What she was doing was turning over the fact that in seven years she has never once seen a man stop in that cut, and that this one had, and that she had been looking at that corner from that window for seven years and had never once had a reason to look at the middle of the cut.

She is not going to be able to say when she will next be at that window on a weekday morning. She works afternoons. She is not going to make a habit of being in the right place for it, and she is not going to move to another window in that house to find out whether he goes through, and she has not gone up to the second floor of the building next door to look down that cut from a better angle, and she is not going to.

Which is how it went on being true that neither of them knew what the other one knew.

The last morning of the fortnight was the eighteenth, and the twenty-first was the first morning he went through, and on the twenty-first she was at that window again, for the same reason, at about ten past seven, and she saw him come out of the third house and go left and go up the cut and come out the other end and not stop.

He did not look up. She was at that window, in daylight, in a room with no blind down, at ten past seven in the morning, and a man coming out of a cut thirty yard away with his head about the height of the coping can see that window perfectly well if he is looking, and he was not looking.

On the second of February that coping had nothing on it and she had not let anything out of that door since the last week of December, and the man had been going up that cut twice a day for three weeks, and the two of them were on the same arrangement as they had been on the fifth of January, which is that neither of them knew the other one was in it.

**ON THE SECOND OF FEBRUARY A CAT WENT INTO A GARDEN AT THE TOP OF THAT STREET OVER A FENCE WHERE THE RENDER HAS COME OFF, AND A WOMAN AT A WINDOW THIRTY YARD AWAY WATCHED IT DO IT, AND SHE DID NOT GO OUT OF HER DOOR, AND THIRTY YARD BELOW HER A MAN CAME OUT OF THE THIRD HOUSE AND WENT UP THROUGH THE CUT AND LOOKED AT THE COPING AS HE WENT PAST IT AND CARRIED ON.**
