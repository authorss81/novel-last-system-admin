# Chapter 0335 — The Warm End of a Wall

Sunday the ninth of July 2017, from about half past eight in the morning until about four in the afternoon, a block of flats off a parade, and a street three streets off it, in Tideglass.

**A WALL AT THE BACK OF THE ROOM AT THE END OF THAT FIRST-FLOOR CORRIDOR HAS BEEN WARM TO THE BACK OF THE HAND SINCE THE FIRST OF THE MONTH, AND THE PERSON WHO LIVES IN THAT ROOM HAS MOVED HER BED TWO FOOT ALONG IT, AND THREE STREETS OFF A MAN WHO IS NOT ON ANYTHING HAS PUT A BOLLARD BACK INTO THE ROAD HE TOOK OUT OF IT, AND NEITHER OF THOSE THINGS IS ANYTHING TO DO WITH THE OTHER ONE.**

That block is four storeys and it faces the parade at the front and about nine yards of ground and a wall at the back, and everybody who comes into it comes up the stair on the back of it, which is nine steps of poured concrete gone in about 1988, and there is a rail on one side of it and nothing at all on the other.

That landing is about the length of two doors and a bit, and the floor of it is bare concrete with the skirt along the bottom of the wall painted at some point in the last ten years, and there is a window at the end of it that has not been looked at since before that, and the room at the end of it is the one with the bowl standing under the strip of wet paper against the end wall.

The wall at the back of that room is warm. It is warm in a shape. The shape is about the width of her shoulders and taller than she is, and it runs from about the height of the top of her bed up to about a foot under the ceiling, and it is warm all the way through the day and it is not warm at four in the morning, and she has had the back of her hand on it about thirty times a day since the first of July without meaning to.

It is the wall that is shared with the house behind, which is a house and not a flat, and there is a kitchen at the back of that house, and there is no way from that room to know what is at the back of that wall, and there is nobody in this borough she could ask who would know, because nobody has any more idea than she does what is at the back of that wall.

---

She found it on the first Saturday of July by washing her face. The water from the hot tap had gone cold on her and she put her hands flat on the tiles above the sink to steady herself and the tiles were hot, and the wall beside them was hotter, and she went and put the back of her hand on that wall in the middle of the room and it was the same.

What she has done about it, and nobody has asked her any of this and nobody has been told any of it:

She has moved the bed two foot along that wall, which meant that the light on the flex now hangs over the side of the bed instead of over the middle of it, and she has got used to that in about a week.

She has the window open from about eight in the morning until about nine at night and there is a plastic chair under the window that she puts her feet in and out of, and on the three days since the start of July when it has been over twenty-eight degrees she has had a bowl of cold water on the floor by the bed and put her feet in it, and the water goes warm and she empties it out into the sink and fills it again, and the two jugs go down the stairs and up the stairs twice a day like the two jugs always have.

And the strip of wet paper at the end of that corridor, which has been coming in since the middle of June, has not got bigger. She has looked at it every day. It is the size of a tea tray and it is exactly that size today, and she has not made up her mind about whether that is a good thing or not.

Nothing has been arranged for her. Nobody put her in that room and nobody has arranged one word of it. She has not asked anybody to do anything and nobody has come and offered her anything, and the wall has been warm for eight days and no person in that building has said one word to her about it.

On the Wednesday of the fifth, the woman in the room next door along came out at about ten in the morning and stood in the corridor for a bit.

She had come out because her own room had been hot since the Saturday and the window in it does not take a breeze off the parade the way the one in the room at the end does, which is the only reason anybody has ever had for liking that room.

"There is something on the back of that wall," said the woman in the room next door along, to the end of the corridor, to nobody in particular.

"There is," said the woman of about fifty-five, from her own doorway.

"That'll be the back of the kitchen at number thirty-one," said the woman in the room next door along. "It is a kitchen and they have got an oven on the other side of that."

"Is it," said the woman of about fifty-five.

And that was all of it, and not one word passed between the two of them about a bowl under a strip of paper off the end wall, and the woman in the room next door along went back into her own room and put her own door back to where it always stands.

Nobody in that building has been on that roof since the middle of June and nobody is going up there in July, and the woman of about fifty-five has not told anybody in that building that there is anything to look at, and the woman in the room next door along has said a thing about a kitchen at number thirty-one that neither of them has any reason to believe, and neither of them is going to knock on a door about it.

---

Three streets off, on the Thursday morning of the sixth, the man in his forties took a bollard out of a road.

It stands at the mouth of a yard entrance where a lane comes off a street of small houses, and it is cast iron and about three foot high and it has a collar round it about two inches from the top, and it is not new and not painted, and it had begun to rock. A man walking into that yard with a barrow catches the side of it with his leg. It had come up about two inches out of the tarmac and the tarmac round it had gone soft and open at the joint, and on the Wednesday of the fifth a woman with a bag went past it, hit it with her ankle, and stopped, and said a word, and carried on into the yard.

He was in that street on the Wednesday for a downpipe bracket and he saw her do it, and on the Thursday he came back at about ten with a bar and a lump hammer and a bag of post fixings and about a foot of rubble he had in the van off a job in June.

The work is about an hour and it is not difficult work. You get the collars off with a bar and the whole thing out, and the hole underneath it is about a foot and a half deep and it is soft at the bottom because it is made out of the road, and you dig another foot, and you put the rubble in the bottom about four inches deep and stand the bollard on it and get it plumb with a spirit level off the kerb, and you pour the fixings in dry and tap them down with the butt of the bar and fill the top inch with the tail end of a bag of post fixings and trowel it flat and let the rain at it.

Nobody asked him to do it and nobody in that street knows his name and there are four houses within about thirty yards of that bollard and not one of them has been told that it has been done.

He was about three quarters of the way through when a man who is not on anything came down that street, and stopped, and watched him for about four minutes with his hands in his pockets, and never offered to hold anything and never put a question to him for about three of those minutes.

"Are you doing that for somebody," said the man who is not on anything.

"No," said the man in his forties.

"Right," said the man who is not on anything, and he stood there another minute and then went on down the street.

He has been on nothing since the start of April. He did a job in a kitchen in Corrance Lane in June and there is nothing in this borough he is on.

The bollard is in the road. It is plumb. It has not moved since the Thursday and the tarmac round the top of it is going back to the colour of the rest of that road, and in about three weeks you would not be able to say which one it was, and no woman with a bag is going to hit her ankle on it in August.

And a wall at the back of the room at the end of that first-floor corridor is warm in exactly the shape it was warm in on the first of the month, and the woman who sleeps two foot away from it is in that room on the same terms she was on in February, and she has told nobody anything, and the woman next door along has told nobody anything, and there are three streets and a parade between that bed and that bollard and not one living person knows about both of them.

**A WOMAN PUT HER FEET IN A BOWL OF COLD WATER AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE IN THE AFTERNOON ON A SUNDAY IN JULY BECAUSE THE WALL BEHIND HER BED WAS WARM, AND NOBODY KNEW THAT, AND THREE STREETS OFF A MAN POUNDED THE TOP OF A BOLLARD FLAT WITH THE BUTT OF A BAR.**
