# Chapter 0252 — What It Built

Thursday the fourteenth of July 2016, from about half past seven in the evening, a hall off a parade of shops in Tideglass, and the corridor outside it.

**SIX WEEKS, TWO YESES AND ONE NO, AND AT THE END OF THE SIX WEEKS NOBODY IN THIS BOROUGH COULD PUT A FINGER ON ANYTHING, AND THE ONLY THING THAT HAD HAPPENED WAS THAT FOUR PEOPLE HAD DONE SOMETHING SMALL ON THE SAME DAY WITHOUT ANY OF THEM KNOWING THE OTHER THREE WERE DOING IT.**

The woman of about forty-two who has the key to that hall put the booking book on the table without being asked to and opened it at the July page, and the gas column in it was hers and had been hers for nine years and the Tuesdays in it were the thing she gave in April, and there were twelve of them marked in pencil in her own hand with a tick beside each one, and about half of them had a number of people against them and about half of them had nothing at all against them.

"That's the whole of what I can show anybody," she said. "I don't know who came. I don't take a name at the door, I never have, and I asked and I wasn't going to ask now, and I'd have been within my rights and it would have finished the thing in a fortnight. It's a hall. You come into a hall."

The man in his forties had been in the room and had the carbon book from Penhale with him in a plastic bag, which he had carried up the road himself, and he put it on the table and opened it at the page and showed the column headed with nothing at all, and the gap in the writing about two inches deep, and the one line in it that was not a claim, and that was all.

"Twelve of them," said the young woman of about twenty. "Twelve rooms with about nine people in them at some point, and one line in a book at the bottom of a hill. That is the entire output of six weeks and I have been in every one of those rooms."

"You have not," said Tomas Renn.

"I have not all of them."

"You have not been in one since the twentieth of May," he said, "and I have not said a word in a room since the week before that, and I would like the room to notice that I came for the last hour of this, and that I have brought nothing, and that it is a Thursday."

Nobody got used to it. That is a thing you can say about Tomas Renn and it is the only reason anybody in that room still talks to him, and nobody in that room had worked that out on purpose.

---

The question got asked at about twenty to nine and it was asked badly, which was correct, because a badly asked question is at least a question that came out of somebody's mouth.

"Is any of it working?" said a man from the parade. "That's all. Is any of it doing anything."

Nobody answered it for about a minute and a half, and then Mrs Obi, who is about thirty-six, said:

"No idea. And I'm going to be much more specific than that, because 'no idea' is a thing people say when they've stopped looking, and we haven't stopped. In this borough, at this moment, in July, with about nine people in a room, there is no way on God's earth to tell whether a thing happened in April or not. There is no paper. There is a hall with a pencil tick in it and a column in a book at the bottom of a hill with one line in it that is not a claim, and if you were to go to a magistrate about that you'd be laughed at, and you would deserve to be."

"That's the first honest thing anybody's said in here for a month," said the man from the parade.

"It's not honest, it's arithmetic," said Mrs Obi. "And before anybody starts on the other thing, I'll say it and then I'll not say it again. A compact agreed with people one at a time is a list. I said that anybody who told you it would not be a list was selling you something, and I said it before there was one, and every person in this room can check the date. And nobody has made one. Not a column, not a sheet, not a card in a shop window, not a page in a pad. Nobody has written down the name of a single person who has said yes to anything in this borough since April, and the only reason that is true is that nobody has asked anybody for a name, and the only reason nobody has asked is that we all worked out the same week how it works and nobody wanted to be the one who did it."

She put both hands flat on the table.

"And I want to be careful with the next sentence, because it is the only one I've got. I do not know what that means. I have not found out tonight, and anybody in this room who stands up and tells you they know what a compact that isn't a list is, is selling you something. That's it. That's the whole finding, and I know it isn't one, and I know it's the first time in my life I've run a thing for six weeks and come to the end of it not being able to say whether it happened."

And the room sat with that for about ten seconds and then the woman who has been in that hall about two years put both hands on the table and said: "Then don't tell us what the compact is. Tell us what happened on the twenty-eighth of June."

"Why the twenty-eighth," said the man in his forties.

"Because it was a Tuesday," she said, "and because the boy asked the man off the river in this borough and got a day out of him, and I was in the room when he did it. That's the only date anybody in this borough has ever agreed on for anything and you didn't agree on it, he did, and you didn't notice."

So they went round the room, and it was not a list, and it was not minuted, and it was four things and every one of them came with the person who was in the room saying what they had heard from somebody else and not vouching for it.

The man from the works on the far side of the river had finished at two on the twenty-eighth of June and had come over the bridge at about half past three, and had spent an hour in a house in Marsh Lane helping a woman get a bath out of a second-floor landing that she had been saying since March that she would do and could not do, and had not told anybody in advance that he was going to, and had not told anybody afterwards, and had gone back over the bridge.

The boat crew had brought four feet of pipe and a fitting back from over the water on the Friday and had left it on the kerb at the parade outside a shop that is shut on a Sunday, and had told nobody it was there, and it was still on the kerb on the Wednesday when the woman who has been in that hall about two years put it in the yard of the house behind because she was tired of looking at it.

Somebody had put a pound into the meter of that hall in the last week of June and nobody had seen who, and there had been no sign of it, and the gas had gone on for an evening with about nine people in the room, and it was still not known.

And the man at the bottom of Peverell Street had walked up to the parade on the Friday, bought a length of chain about eight feet long, paid for it, walked back down, and had not given it to anybody and had not put it anywhere anybody could find, and when the woman who has been in that hall about two years asked him about it on the Sunday he said it was for a gate and would not say which gate.

Nobody could connect the four of them. Nobody had arranged them, none of them knew about the other three, and there was nothing whatever that made them the same day except that one man had said a date out loud on a Wednesday morning in the street to somebody washing up in a kitchen, and had not put it in a list, and had gone back to work.

---

Jonas Mercer was in the corridor outside that hall from about half past seven and did not go in, and the door was open about four inches because it always is, and the radiator in that corridor has been out since about 1998.

He had not been asked anything in the whole of the seven weeks since the eleventh of May, and there was nobody in that room or in that corridor who was going to ask him anything that evening, and the notebook he had given away in May was in a handbag in this borough and he had not seen it since and was not going to ask after it.

He stood there and listened to the sound of a hall with about nine people in it deciding that it could not find out what it had done, which is a sound he had not heard before in this borough, and he had heard a good many.

**HE HAD A BIRO IN HIS COAT AND NOTHING TO PUT IT ON, AND HE DID NOT WRITE THE FOUR THINGS DOWN, AND ON HIS WAY DOWN THE PARADE HE WENT PAST THE SHOP WITH THE SHUTTER THAT GOES UP AND DOWN AND DID NOT GO IN.**
