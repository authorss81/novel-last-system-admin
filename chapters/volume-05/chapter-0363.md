# Chapter 0363 — At About Nine

Saturday the twenty-eighth of October 2017, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past five in the afternoon, a hall at the top of a street, and a shop off a parade, in Tideglass.

**A DOOR STOOD OPEN AT THE END OF A MEETING ON A WEDNESDAY AND THE MAN WHO WAS LAST OUT SHUT IT AND WENT HOME, AND ON THE SATURDAY A MAN WHO WAS NOT IN THAT ROOM WROTE DOWN THAT THE PREMISES WERE FOUND OPEN AT ABOUT NINE, AND THE ONLY PERSON WHO COULD PUT A DIFFERENT HOUR ON IT DOES NOT KNOW WHAT HOUR IT WAS EITHER.**

The hall is at the top of a street and is let out by the hour to anybody who rings the man who looks after it, and he has looked after it for about nine years and he is the only person in that borough who has a key to it and he is not paid for it and there is no paper about it anywhere.

He keeps a book.

The book is a hardback exercise book with about two thirds of it used and the covers come away at the corners, and he writes in it when something has happened in that hall, and there is no column on the page and there is no heading and there is no name on anything in it and nobody has ever asked him for it and he has never offered it to anybody.

The most he writes is a sentence and a day. A thing in March. A window left open in a heat. A leak under the back wall that was not a leak.

On the Saturday of the twenty-eighth of October he wrote in it: the door on that hall had been found open on the Wednesday night, at about nine.

There is nothing wrong with that sentence except the hour.

---

The meeting on the Wednesday finished at about twenty to nine and about nine people had been in that room, and the man in his sixties was one of them and had been in it since half past seven.

He has been going to that hall on a Wednesday for about six years and he sits in the same place at the back with his cup, he is on nothing and has never been on anything, and nobody in that room has ever asked him what he does on the other three days.

He stayed back after the meeting because the chairs go up on their brackets and about nine people do not stop to do it on a Wednesday, and he and one other man did it, and they put the last four chairs up at about a quarter to nine and the other man went out through the front and shut that door behind him.

The man in his sixties was alone in that room for about two minutes.

The back door on that hall opens on to a passage and that passage has a door on to the lane at the end of it, and that outer door was standing open about two foot with the chain off and the passage beyond it dark.

He went down the passage and shut the outer door and put the chain on and came back up the passage and shut the hall's own back door and turned the key in it and took the key out and put it in his pocket.

He did not look at a clock at any point in that, and there is no clock in that passage and there has not been one since before he started going.

He went out through the front at about twenty past nine and shut that door behind him with the key and went down the street and got a bus, and he has not been in that hall since.

There were two people in that building on the Wednesday night: him, and nobody. The man who looks after that hall was four miles off at his sister's until about ten and came in on the Saturday morning at about half past seven.

---

He did not see the door on the Wednesday. He has told nobody he did not see it and nobody has asked him.

He saw it on the Saturday morning when he let himself in at about half past seven, and it was shut, and the chain was on, and the key was in it, and there was nothing on that door that told him anything about the Wednesday night at all.

He wrote what goes in the book.

That is the thing about that book. There is one sentence for a door and the sentence is that the premises were found open, and the hour in it is the hour the man who looks after that hall does his round, which is nine, and it has been nine in that book for about nine years and it was nine in there in 2015 and it was nine in there in 2012.

He wrote it in between a window in September and a window in August, and he did not write anything under it, and he put the book on the shelf it lives on behind the door of that hall, and the shelf is not locked and nobody has ever taken that book off that shelf.


The man in his sixties was in that hall on the Wednesday the twenty-third of October and again on the Wednesday the thirtieth.

On the thirtieth the man who looks after that hall was in the room at about half past eight setting out the cups, and the man in his sixties was in his place at the back with his cup, and there were about nine people and a man in his sixties.

"You are in early on a Wednesday," said the man who looks after that hall.

"I am on early all week," said the man in his sixties.

That is all of it.

He has not asked the man who looks after that hall what is in the book. He did not know that there was a book until about three weeks ago, when a woman at that hall said that everything that ever happened in the place got written down, and said it as a thing she was pleased about, and he had a turn of the head about it and said nothing.

He has not asked and he is not going to, because a man who has shut a door in a passage at about a quarter to nine on a Wednesday night and then asks what the man who looks after that hall wrote about the Wednesday night is a man asking to be found out, and being found out is a thing that puts him in a book.

He has not asked and he is not going to, because a man who has shut a door in a passage at about a quarter to nine on a Wednesday night and then asks what the man who looks after that hall wrote about the Wednesday night is a man asking to be found out, and being found out is a thing that puts him in a book.

It is not a large thing and that is what makes it awkward. If it were a window left open in a heat there would be nothing in it. If a man had left the whole of that hall open at nine o'clock on a Wednesday night there would be something in that and he would have a reason to go and stand in that hall. What he did was put a chain on a door, and a man who puts a chain on a door has not been asked to say so by anybody, and there is no shape in that anybody could be caught by.

The hour in that sentence is the only part of it that is wrong, and he cannot put a different one on it, because he did not look and there is no clock in that passage and the man who wrote it was in another town when it happened.

The man in his sixties was at the counter of the shop off a parade at about half past three on the Saturday after and bought a packet of tea and his hands were not steady on the change and the woman who works in a shop gave him the right money back without saying anything about it and he went out into the parade.

The man in his sixties was at the counter of the shop off a parade at about half past three on the Saturday after and bought a packet of tea and his hands were not steady on the change and the woman who works in a shop gave him the right money back without saying anything about it and he went out into the parade.

He has not been near that hall book. There is a woman who rings the man who looks after that hall to put a meeting on and he has seen that book on a table in that hall about nine times without reading a word of it, and he has never once wondered what is in it, and he has not wondered since either, and a man cannot be careful about a thing he does not know is there.

**A MAN PUT A BOOK BACK ON A SHELF BEHIND A DOOR AT HALF PAST SEVEN ON A SATURDAY MORNING AND WENT OUT AND LOCKED THAT HALL BEHIND HIM, AND THE SENTENCE HE HAD PUT IN IT IS STILL IN IT, AND THE DOOR IT IS ABOUT WAS SHUT BY SOMEONE ELSE.**
