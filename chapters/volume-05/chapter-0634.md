# Chapter 0634 — The One He Asked

Tuesday the thirteenth of April 2021, in this borough. He is in his own front room from about half past one in the afternoon and she is on a bus at about ten past two and the whole of it happens on a doorstep and in a kitchen with the door open.

**THE MAN THE FIVE WORDS WERE INVENTED ABOUT HAS BEEN TOLD SHE IS COMING AND SHE HAS COME, AND HE ASKS HER WHY SHE IS COMING AND SHE GIVES HIM THE REASON SHE WANTS IT STOPPED, AND HE TELLS HER THAT HE DID ASK HER, ONCE, AND THAT SHE SAID SHE COULD NOT DO IT.**

He is a man in his early sixties. She is a woman in her late thirties. They have lived next but one to each other for years and have spoken to each other about the weather, a bin, a lawnmower and a lift in the rain.

The bin is being done by the woman at number six now and everybody on that row knows it and nobody has said so.

---

Here is the telephone call, and it was on the Sunday and it did not take long and she has got it in the sequence it went in.

And she said, "Do you know what I've been doing."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "I've been round the row."

And he said, "What for."

And she said, "To say where the five words came from."

And he said, "You didn't."

And she said, "I said they came off me."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "That's not the same as what happened."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "I know it's not. That's the point of it. Nobody else is going to say they came off them."

And he said, "Where."

And she said, "Everywhere."

And he said, "Right," and she heard him put the telephone down on a table, which he does, and she said, "Are you there."

And he said, "I'm here."

And she said, "Can I come and talk to you."

And he said, "About the words."

And she said, "About the words."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "Is that all right?"

And he said, "When."

And she said, "Tuesday afternoon."

And he said, "Come Tuesday afternoon," and he said it back, and he has never once repeated anything back to her in the whole time they have lived next but one to each other, and he has not been able to account for it since.

Here is what he has done since Sunday, and this is the chapter and not the telephone.

On Sunday night he worked out what she had done and what it meant, and it took him about as long as it takes to make a cup of tea.

She has gone round a row in this borough and put five words on herself in front of eight doors, and the five words are about him.

He is a man in his early sixties with a bad back who cannot get a bin to a corner, and there is a woman at number six doing it now, and there is a woman at number eight who has spent a month telling the row that the woman two doors down started it, and none of that is about him and all of it came out of a kitchen in which he was the subject.

And he has decided something on Sunday night and he has not told her and he is going to tell her this afternoon.

He has decided to tell her the one thing.

She came at about ten past two on the Tuesday and she knocked and he had the door open before she knocked, which he noticed and did not comment on.

And he said, "Come in."

And she said, "Can we stand out here."

And he said, "It's about the same either way."

And she said, "I'd rather stand out here."

And he said, "Right."

And he came out and shut the door behind him and they stood on his own step, which is a small step, and she is two inches shorter than him and they had to do the thing they did all through since Christmas, which is one of them looking slightly down.

And he said, "You've been round the row."

And she said, "I've been round the row."

And he said, "Eight doors."

And she said, "Eight."

And he said, "You said it came off you."

And she said, "I said it came off me."

And he said, "Right."

And he said, "Why are you here."

And she said, "Because I want it stopped."

And he said, "Stopped where."

And she said, "At the person it's on."

And he said, "Which person."

And she said, "A woman at number eight has spent a month telling that row it was the woman two doors down. And there's a girl on Kenner Street who's never been in this borough and about four people have said it about her."

And he said, "You don't know about the girl."

And she said, "I know it's going about."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "And that's the reason I came."

And he said, "Is that all of it."

And she said, "That's all of it."

And he said, "No," and he did not say it unkindly and he said it the way a man says a thing he has had since he got up.

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "You've been round eight doors taking the five words off two women and putting them on yourself, and you have not once said why you wanted them on yourself, and I want to ask you that, and I want you to not answer it properly, and I am telling you now that I am going to ask it so that you can't say afterwards that nobody ever asked."

And she said, "Go on."

And he said, "Why did you want it on yourself."

And she said, "Because I want it stopped."

And he said, "That's the reason you gave me on the telephone and it's the reason you gave eight people on a row and it is a true reason and it is not why you did it, and you know it is not why you did it, and I have been thinking about it since Sunday."

And she said, "I know."

And he said, "Say it or don't say it."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "Right," and he nodded at his own gate, and she has never seen him do that before.

And then he said the thing, and it took him about four sentences and he had them ready on the Sunday and he did not use any of them.

And he said, "You said on the first of March that I never ask you."

And she said, "You don't."

And he said, "I asked you."

And she said, "You didn't."

And he said, "In the February. On a Saturday. About a fortnight before."

And she said, "I don't know what you're talking about."

And he said, "You'd have been out."

And she said, "I was at my sister's."

And he said, "That's what I said."

And she said, "You asked me for the bin."

And he said, "I did."

And she said, "I couldn't do it that day."

And he said, "You didn't. You said you couldn't do it that day and I said right."

And she said, "And you didn't ask again."

And he said, "I didn't ask again."

And she said, "Well, there you are."

And he said, "There I am."

And she said, "You're going to tell them that, then."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "You're going to tell them I said no."

And he said, "I'm not going to tell anybody anything."

And she said, "Then why are you telling me."

And he said, "Because you went round eight doors on Thursday and put it on yourself."

And she said, "You don't know that."

And he said, "Every door on this row has had it and about half of them came past this gate on the way home and I stood at the window and let them go past."

And she said, "And you said nothing."

And he said, "I said nothing."

And she said, "Why."

And he said, "Because you're stood on my step asking me and it would have been a different conversation."

And she said, "What would it have been."

And he said, "A short one, and about you, and you'd have gone home and been pleased with yourself about the woman at number eight and there'd have been a girl on an estate still sat on."

They stood there.

She looked at the middle of his gate for a while, and he looked up the row at the parade, and the parade was doing what it does at about half past two on a Tuesday in the middle of April.

And she said, "You've had that since the February."

And he said, "Since the Saturday."

And she said, "And you never said."

And he said, "And you never did either, until the Monday, and you said it to the man in the house with you and he has probably gone over it a good many times by now."

And she said, "He has."

And he said, "Has he."

And she said, "He has four times. He counts them. He counted them on the Monday night and he counted them again on the Thursday and he's counted them since and I've never once asked him to stop."

And he said, "Why not."

And she said, "Because every time he starts it I find out I've said it again, and then I've got to decide whether I meant it, and I meant it the first time and I meant it the second and by the fourth I couldn't tell, and that's the best thing I've got out of this and I'd not give it up for the bin."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "That's what it is. It got worse because I kept saying it. If I'd said it once and shut up about it, I'd have been annoyed at me and I'd have got over being annoyed at me."

And he said, "And you'd have got the bin back."

And she said, "I'd have got the bin back and I'd have been wrong, and I'd have got it back inside a week instead of carrying it about since the first of March."

And he said, "And on Wednesday last week you went round eight doors and told them the words came off you."

And she said, "Yes."

And he said, "So now everybody on this row knows it was you."

And she said, "Eight of them. I said it through two doors and one of them never opened at all."

And he said, "So seven houses."

And she said, "Seven."

And he said, "And none of them know it was half true."

And she said, "No."

And here is what he decided on Sunday night and told her at about half past two on the Tuesday, and he told her the way you tell somebody a thing you have not decided whether you have decided.

And he said, "I'm not going to tell anybody."

And she said, "What."

And he said, "I'm not going to go round the row or round the estate and say the words are true of me. Once is enough, and one is what I did to you, and I'm not going to do a round of it."

And she said, "Why not."

And he said, "Because there's a woman at number eight who's been telling that row it's the woman two doors down, and if I go round and say the words are true of me, she'll have to stop, and she'll stop by saying a different thing. She won't say nothing. Nobody says nothing. So it will turn round into a thing about a man in his sixties who has been saying it about people, which is me, and it will be worse for me than it ever was for her."

And she said, "It's bad now."

And he said, "It's not bad now. There's a girl on an estate about a bus ride away who has never heard of any of us."

And she said, "There's no way to get at the girl."

And he said, "I know."

And she said, "There's no way to get at her at all. I don't know her name and I don't know her street and I've got a man in a van who won't say and a woman on a doorstep who won't say and an old man at the end of a hill who says it came off a man who won't say."

And he said, "That's about right."

And she said, "Then what do I do."

And he said, "Nothing."

And she said, "Nothing?"

And he said, "Go and tell the woman at number eight that it wasn't her and it wasn't the woman two doors down, and that it was about me and me saying it and nobody starting it on purpose. And then stop."

And she said, "And the girl."

And he said, "And the girl."

And she said, "You said that in the same voice."

And he said, "I did."

And she said, "There's nothing I can do about her."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Then I'm not going round again."

And he said, "One thing."

And she said, "Go on."

And he said, "You said on the first of March that I never ask you, and you have said it to at least three people and you have said it about me at eight doors on this row, and I want you to know that I did ask you, and you said no, and I did not ask again, and that is two things and neither of them is the thing you have been carrying."

And she said, "I know."

And he said, "Do you."

And she said, "I've known since about three o'clock this afternoon."

And he said, "Have you said that to anybody."

And she said, "Not yet."

And he said, "Good."

And she said, "It's not good."

And he said, "It's the best of the four things I could have said to you on this step."

And she said, "I've got to go and be wrong out loud to a woman I have never spoken to."

And he said, "You'll not be wrong."

And she said, "I'll be half wrong, which is worse."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "I'll go up to number eight and I'll say that it came off a man in his own kitchen on the first of March and that it was about a bin and that it was not a true thing about anybody."

And he said, "It is a true thing about me."

And she said, "It's a true thing about a bin."

And he said, "That's fair."

And she said, "And then I'll stop."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "And I'll not go round again."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "And I have not got anything to give the girl on Kenner Street and that is the worst of it, and you know it is, and I have been waiting since about three o'clock this afternoon for you to give me something for her."

And he said, "I haven't got anything."

And she said, "I know you haven't."

And he said, "I'll tell you what I've got."

And she said, "Go on."

And he said, "I've got a girl at number five on this row who had it off the woman at number eight and who believes it came off the woman two doors down, and she's been saying it at the parade and she works with a woman who comes off a bus at about half past two, and that's not the girl, and that's the only thing I've got, and it's been in my pocket since the middle of February and it's no use."

And she said, "I'll take it."

And he said, "You don't want it."

And she said, "No. But I'll take it, because it's the only thing anybody's said to me about any of it that wasn't a person protecting themselves."

And he said, "Right," and he went back in and shut the door, and she stood on his step.

---

Here is what she has got, and she has got it on the pavement and she has got it on the bus and she has got it in her own front room at about half past five.

One: she was wrong, and she was half wrong, and the half she was wrong about is the half she has been carrying in three people's mouths since the first of March, and the man it was about has known since the Saturday in the February and has not said a word to anybody.

Two: he did not use it against her. He did not say *you said I never ask and I did.* He said it to her and then he told her to go and be wrong out loud to a woman she has never spoken to, and he has made that his own errand and not hers.

Three: and this is the one she cannot get round, and she has been going round it since half past two.

He has let it stand. He has had a fortnight of a street saying a thing about him that is half true, and he has decided not to go round and clear it, and the reason he gave her is the woman at number eight and a girl on an estate.

And she has spent a fortnight saying a thing about herself in eight doors that is also half true, and she did not do it for him, and she would not have done it if he had not been on the end of the row, and she has not been able to work out whether that makes it worse or just different.

And he is the only person in this borough who has the whole of it and he has split it in half and kept the half that suits her and handed her the other one, and told her to go and hand it out.

And she has not got to number eight. It is about a ten-minute walk and she has been in her own front room since half past five and it is dark.

And there is a girl on an estate about a bus ride away who has got five words said about her by people who have never spoken to her, and the only two people in this borough who could do anything about it are a man in a van who will not say where he got them and an old man at the end of a hill who has given up asking.

**SHE ASKED HIM WHY HE WAS TELLING HER AND HE TOLD HER THAT HE HAD ASKED HER ONCE, IN THE FEBRUARY, AND SHE HAD SAID SHE COULD NOT DO IT THAT DAY, AND HE HAS DECIDED NOT TO GO ROUND AND CLEAR A HALF-TRUE THING ABOUT HIMSELF, AND HE HAS TOLD HER TO GO AND TELL A WOMAN AT NUMBER EIGHT THAT IT WAS ABOUT A BIN AND NOT ABOUT HER, AND THERE IS A GIRL ON AN ESTATE A BUS RIDE AWAY WHO CANNOT BE REACHED BY ANYBODY, INCLUDING HIM.**
