# Chapter 0416 — A Pair of Glasses

Friday the first of June 2018, beginning at about a quarter to ten in the morning and finishing at about half past six in the evening, a shop off that parade, and a yard two streets off it, in Tideglass.

**A MAN WAS ASKED FOR A THING IN A SHOP ON THAT PARADE AND SAID IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS THAT HE HAD NOT GOT IT, AND HE HAS GOT IT, AND HE IS NOT GOING TO BE ASKED FOR IT BY ANYBODY, AND IT IS IN A VAN AT THE TOP OF THAT TOWN AND IT WILL BE IN THAT VAN ON THE MONDAY.**

The shop is hers, sixty yard back from that parade, and she locks it herself; the counter belongs to her from ten; those two hours have not changed in two years; and the only price list in the building is a strip of pencil on the inside of the till, and the day book is a pad on a string.

Behind that counter there is a rack of offcuts, a length of conduit standing on end, and a heap of about a dozen other people's small jobs at the near end of it. A gate at the back goes out into a service yard shared with the shop next door, which belongs to neither of them.

---

On the seventh of May, at about twenty past twelve, the man in his forties was at that counter with a reel of tape in his hand.

The man behind that counter had gone into the back and there was nothing on the counter to look at, so he turned round and looked up the rack at the list of conduit sizes that is taped inside the top of the till, which is the only price list in the building and is in pencil.

He took a pair of reading glasses out of the breast pocket of his bag, and put them on, and read down about eight lines of it with his finger on it, and took them off, and put them back in the bag, and said the size he wanted and paid for the tape.

The man in his thirties was at the same counter about ten foot off that morning holding a box of plugs, and he saw all of it, and neither of them said a word about it then or at any time since.

He mentioned it once afterwards, and not to the man in his forties. He said it to that man's own mate on a job in another town, three weeks later, in a sentence about how a man of fifty cannot read a drawing past arm's length, and the mate said every tradesman's got them off, and that was the end of it, and it has not come up since.

---

He came in that morning, a little before a quarter to ten.

He had a job starting the Monday morning and the shopfront for it is being made up in a workshop two streets off that parade, and he had the drawing for it rolled in a tube under his arm, and there is about a foot of drawing in that tube with the sizes on it and the setting-out of a frame that is going to be a tall thing by the width.

He has worn glasses for about fifteen years and they are the only ones he has and they sit about nine foot from his face all day and he does not see a great deal without them, and on that Friday morning they were lying on the bench at home under a cutting mat where he had put them on the Wednesday.

He came in for a length of quadrant and paid for it and put it under his arm against the tube, and he was about three foot from the counter with his money out when he said it.

"Have you got a pair of reading glasses I could borrow for an hour."

Halfway along that counter the man in his forties had a length of conduit in his hand and most of the rest of it on the ground at his feet.

He did not stop and he did not put the conduit down.

"No," he said. "I haven't."

That is the whole of it. It is about that long and it is not a slow answer and it is not a fast one, and he said it to the length of conduit and not to the man's face, and he was already moving on to the end of the counter with the conduit when he had finished saying it.

The man in his thirties said, "Right," and put his money down and picked his quadrant up and went.

Nothing beyond that passed between the two of them that morning. She was on the counter and heard all of it from two foot away, and has not brought it up with either man and never will.

Nobody has asked him for them again. Not that man, and not the woman, and not the man who is in that shop most weeks and has wanted to since the May and has never said it, and not anybody else in this borough.

Here is where they are.

They are in the side pocket of a holdall hanging on a hook behind the driver's seat of his own van, in the middle pocket of that bag, in a soft case, and they have been there about three years.

They are wire-framed and about half an inch round the lens and the left lens has a scratch across the bottom third of it that does not show in a room and shows in a workshop, and one arm has been re-boned twice with a wire off a coat hanger and does not sit square.

He had them fitted in a shop in town in the March of 2016, and they were right, and he read everything he wanted to read with them for about eight months.

And then one Monday he went into that shop to read a price list off a board on the wall — a plain board, no list on it, a man had to read it with his own glasses like anybody else — and he stood at that board for about a quarter of an hour and read a third of it and then could not do any more of it and came out and stood in the car park.

Put to that shop, what he would have wanted was the pair of glasses that the fitter had just handed him across that counter, and the fitter was dead by then, and the new man said he would have to order the same again in about ten days and take a fortnight for them and it would be the same pair, and there is not anybody in this town who would do it.

So he never went back, and he put those two away in his own head, and he has worn the old pair since, and he has said nothing to anybody about any of it, and there is no other man in this borough who knows a single word of that.

He is aware every single day that there is a pair of reading glasses in a bag about four foot behind his head. He is aware of it the way a man is aware of a thing in a van he has got in and cannot use. He has never suggested to anybody that the bag is available and he is never going to.

And the reason he said no is not about the man who asked. It would be the same in anybody.

It is that a man who has got a thing does not put it in another man's hands, because that is a thing you do once and then the other man knows what is in your van. And after that every conversation either has that in it or is standing on it.

It is a smaller thing than that, as well, and it is the real one. A pair of reading glasses are not a spanner. You can lose a spanner and buy another one this afternoon. You cannot buy those again in four days and you will not buy them at all, and if a man has your eyes in his hand for an hour he knows that, and so do you.

He had a shopfront drawing to set out that Monday and he could not read the setting-out on it.

He got the frame dimension off it, because that is the one number a man can get off a drawing with a rule and by eye and it does not matter. The sizes he could not get. The centres he could not get. The detail of the transom at the head, which is where the whole difficulty in that job is, he could not get at all, and it took him until about twenty to twelve in the morning in a yard with his own eyes at the wrong end of a workshop before he worked out that he was going to do the head the way he would have done it in 2014 and not the way that drawing said.

He did it that way. It is a perfectly good head and it is not the head in the drawing and nobody will know that but him, and he finished the job on the Thursday instead of the Wednesday.

Late that afternoon she was at her own counter when he came in for a length of quadrant trim, and she asked him for a pencil.

"Have you got a pencil I could have."

He took one out of the pocket of his bag and put it on the counter without stopping and went on talking about the trim, and handed her the change, and said no more about the pair in the morning than he would have if the morning had not happened.

And that is where the line is, and it is a line neither of the two of them has drawn out loud, and she is not going to ask him for anything else that afternoon, and he is not going to offer her anything.

On the Monday he is in that van at about ten to eight with his bag off the hook behind him, and the pair will be in the middle pocket of it, and the job is finished and the setting-out is on the bench, and nobody in this borough has asked him for them and nobody is going to.

**HE PUT A PENCIL DOWN ON A COUNTER AND TOOK THE CHANGE AND WENT ON ABOUT THE TRIM, AND THERE IS A PAIR OF READING GLASSES IN A BAG BEHIND THE SEAT OF A VAN AND NOBODY HAS ASKED FOR THEM AND NOBODY IS GOING TO.**
