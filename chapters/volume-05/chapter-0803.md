# Chapter 0803 — The Shelf Under The Window

Wednesday the twenty-ninth of March 2023, in this borough. That whole Wednesday is spent in her own yard at number nine between the middle of the morning and the end of it, and the tub of iron that lives on the middle of her path is what it is about.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES HAS A GALVANISED TUB OF IRON STANDING ON THE MIDDLE OF HER FRONT PATH BECAUSE THERE IS NOWHERE IN THAT YARD FOR IT AND MEANS TO PUT IT SOMEWHERE ELSE, THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING TAKES IT OFF THE PATH AND FINDS THERE IS NOWHERE TO PUT IT AND SO PUTS A SHELF ON THE FLAT WALL UNDER THE FRONT WINDOW, BRACED AND SCREWED INTO SOUND BRICK AND NOT INTO THE SILL, AND THE TUB GOES ON IT AND OFF THE PATH.**

From the front door of that house on the Wednesday, with the middle of the morning well gone, the woman who does her shopping stands on the step with her hands empty and looks at the wall under the window for about a minute before she says anything at all.

---

That tub of iron has been on the middle of her front path since January.

What came out of the middle bay of that path in January — the roots and the grit and the rubble of it — is still in it, and the tub is on the middle of that path because there is nowhere in that yard to put it, and that is where it has stood ever since with a thumb of the bed gone down into the joint around it.

She means to put it somewhere else.

She has been through the whole of that house twice this month except the front room, which stays shut, looking at the places it could go, and the answer is in all of them. There is a bin store across the end with a shovel and a fireguard in it, a lav in the corner, a passage with a meter cupboard in it and about six feet of wall behind the stairs with the fuse box on it, and there is a hundred and twenty feet of pipe with a hopper on it down the side of the house that has not been cleared out since before she came.

---

"So you are putting it somewhere else."

"I am putting it somewhere else. There has to be somewhere else."

"There is not." She has not even looked at it yet. "Go and stand in that yard and tell me one place."

She stands in that yard for a while and does not find one, and comes back in and says there is nowhere.

"Then that is the whole of it. That tub has been in the way of that path for eleven weeks and there is nowhere in that yard for it, and the answer is not to find a place for it. The answer is to make a place for it."

---

They take it off the path first, and that is easy and it is the only easy part of the day.

It comes up off that bed with a bar under the rim — two months of standing in one place has pressed a hollow into the joint it has been sitting in and it lifts out of it as easy as a tray out of a drawer — and they walk it along the path about four feet with the bar under the rim the whole way, because a galvanised tub full of the roots and grit that came out of that path weighs about as much as a full bath and neither of them can pick it up.

They stand it against the wall under the window on the concrete with a bit of card between the rim and the render, so that nothing stands in the way of the wall while they look at it.

---

And then they look at the wall under the front window for about twenty minutes and neither of them puts anything on it.

It is about six feet of flat brickwork on that house, at the ground level, between the front door and the corner drainpipe, and it is the only piece of straight wall that yard has got, and there is about two feet of clear ground in front of it.

"There then."

"There is a sill."

She is looking at the sill. It is a stone sill, projecting about two inches out of the face of the brick, and it runs the whole width of the window and it is not part of the wall. It sits on a couple of courses of brick that have been cut back to take it and there is nothing behind it at all.

"You are not fixing anything into that."

"That sill is what that window sits on."

"That sill carries nothing at all. That sill is a stone laid on a bit of brick to stop a frame of wood getting wet, and it is let in at the ends and it has been cut back from the face of the wall to take it, and if you put a bracket into the end of that sill you are hanging a tub of iron off a stone about four inches deep and about eleven inches long." She puts the flat of her hand on it and it does not move. "Put it in the brick. In the joint, two courses under the sill, where the brick is."

---

The shelf is made on the ground before it goes up.

It is a length of deal about four inches wide and an inch and a half thick with the arris taken off both long edges, and it is not a beautiful thing. They cut it on the trestles and bore four holes in it while it is on the trestles, two for each bracket.

The brackets are made up out of flat iron, three-quarters of an inch wide and an eighth thick, and each one is an upright about three feet six with a short arm turned at the bottom of it at a right angle, and the upright goes up the face of the wall and the arm goes out under the shelf.

Two of them go up the wall four feet apart, screwed into the brick.

Four screws each, into lead plugs driven into the joints, and they go in brass because the iron is going to be wet on that face of that wall every winter for the rest of its life and cut screws in a house wall are the first thing anybody finds in twenty years. They go into the two joints that are sound. She sounds them with the butt of the bar before she puts anything into them, and the third joint across has a hollow chase behind it that goes up from an old pipe and there is nothing at the back of that one but air and half a brick.

---

Then the braces, and that is the part that decides whether it is still there in ten years.

Each bracket gets a diagonal. A length of the same flat iron about three feet long goes from the outer end of the arm, on the underside of it, back and down into a joint in the wall about a foot and a half below the bracket, so that everything that tries to pull the arm of that bracket down is pulling on the diagonal instead, and the whole of the pull comes onto the brick instead of onto four screws.

A bracket on its own into a wall like that is a bracket for a shelf with a box of matches on it. A shelf like that with a tub of iron on it is four foot of lever against nine inches of solid brick, and the four screws of the bracket alone would draw out over a few winters and take about a foot of face off the wall with them.

"That is a brace."

"That is a brace and it goes into brick and not into that sill, and it is not there because it looks as though it is doing something. That is there because a tub of iron comes down on two arms forty inches above the ground and pushes out at the top, and without that diagonal it comes off the wall in about four years."

They put them in and they tighten them and they put the shelf on and the whole thing takes about four hours.

---

Then the tub goes up on it, and that is two people as well.

A galvanised tub of that size is not heavy in itself. It is about as heavy as a full bath, and it is two feet across and a foot and a half deep with no handles on it and nothing on the outside of it to get a grip on, and it has to go up and out and over and land on a shelf forty inches off the ground.

They get it up by putting the bar through the rim on the two sides and getting the two ends of the bar up over the arms of the brackets, and then one of them stands on a block and they walk it up together, and it comes onto the shelf with a bang and it sits on it and does not move.

You cannot get a tub up onto a shelf on your own. It is not a matter of weight. It is a matter of there being two ends of it and both of them want to be up before either of them is up.

---

It is about four hours altogether and it is the middle of the afternoon and she is standing on the flags looking at the wall.

There is a shelf under that window with a tub of iron on it and the roots and grit and rubble that came out of her own front path in January still in it, and the middle flag of that bay is bare for the first time since it was laid, and she can see the work that was done on it in January — the three of them level with the flags either side and the joint close along the whole nine — and she has had a tub standing on top of it since January and she never once looked down at the joint.

The flag at the gate end is still tilting to the rail and it will go on tilting to the rail.

The coal hole on the other side of that house is shut and the load standing in it does not get looked at.

She has been round that wall twice with her own thumb and her own knuckle since it went up, and there are two small brass screws in the sound joint at each end of both brackets and nothing at all in the sill, and the diagonal off the end of each arm has a fixing in the joint below it, and none of that is where a shelf stands on a house and she did not know any of it.

It took four hours and it took two people, and there is nobody at number nine to take the other end of a bar.
