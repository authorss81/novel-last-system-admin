# Chapter 0538 — Three Hours of Saying Nothing

The Saturday of the twenty-eighth of December 2019, from half past eight in the morning until twelve, on a burial ground four miles off and in one high street, and not in this borough at all until the end.

**THE OLDER OF THE TWO HAS HAD SOMEONE IN HER CAR BEFORE ON A MORNING LIKE THIS ONE AND HAS NEVER BEEN SURE SHE SHOULD HAVE DONE IT, AND THE YOUNGER WALKED THE WHOLE ROUND AS SHE DOES EVERY WEEKEND AND GAVE A STRANGER SEVEN SENTENCES IN THREE HOURS, NONE OF WHICH WERE ABOUT ANYTHING, AND THEN GOT OUT AT NOON AND TOLD HER SISTER ABOUT A DOOR.**

The younger is in her mid forties and the older is in her late forties. They are both from a town four miles off, and they are not from this borough, and the town has a slope at the top of it going down to the water and a football ground and a shop that sells the same two things and has done since either of them was born.

Their mother died on the seventh of March 2011, and the third Saturday after that they went to the burial ground together at half past eight, and they have gone every Saturday since, eight years and nine months of them. Nobody asked them to. There is nobody left to ask them.

What they do is the same every time. They take the turning off the top of the town at about ten to nine, because the turning is the one that goes past the little grey building their father went into in 1981 and came out of in 1994. They stop at the second of the four rows. They are at their mother's headstone for about twenty minutes. Then they walk the two ends of the row and their mother is at one end and the rest of the family is spread down the middle in no order anybody chose.

Then they go into the town and walk the high street and do whatever needs doing that anybody has told them about, and then they go to the car and sit in it, and then at twelve the younger drops the younger and goes home.

That is the round. It takes three hours and a half and it has taken three hours and a half on approximately every Saturday for eight years and nine months, and neither of them has ever once suggested changing it.

---

What was different about this one was that the older had a woman in the car.

The woman is in her early forties. She and the older have known each other since the middle of 2017, which came about through a person neither of them would want written down, and she had been invited once before, in the summer, and had said she would come another time when it was not so cold.

She was not expecting a burial ground. She had not asked what the round was, because she had assumed it was a walk.

She said, on the drive out, "Is this all of it, then," and the older said, "That is most of it," and the younger said nothing at all, and the woman looked at the younger and then looked out of the window and said, "Right."



From half past eight to about twenty to ten, the younger did everything she does.

She read the headstone. She did not read it out, which is not a thing either of them has ever done. She went along the row with her hands behind her back and stopped at four of the other stones, and at the fourth one she stood for a while.

She washed the base of it with the cloth and the water she had carried up from the car in a plastic sack, and then she put the wrapping into the plastic sack and carried it back down to the car, and the whole of that took her about three minutes and she did it without asking anybody to hold anything.

The whole of that took about twenty minutes and she did not speak once through any of it.

The woman stood at the end of the row in a jacket that was not warm enough and did not know what she was supposed to be doing. Twice she started something. The first time she said, "This is lovely, isn't it," to the older, and the older said, "It is," and there was nothing to put after that. The second time, about ten minutes in, she turned round and said, "Sorry, can I ask, do you come every week?" directly to the younger, and the younger looked at her, and the younger said, "Sometimes."

That is the whole of what she gave her. She said it in the tone she uses for a stranger at a shop. She then went back to what she was doing and did not say anything else.

---

Then they went into the town, and the three of them walked the high street, and that took the best part of an hour and a half.

The older had a list of four things in her head and got three of them done. She was pleasant the whole time. She kept turning round to the woman and putting her at the front of everything, saying, "You'll want to see this one," and asking the woman what she thought about things, and answering the woman's answers at length.

The woman answered. The woman was good at it. She laughed twice at the right moments and she picked up a thing in a shop and put it down again in a way that made the older laugh, and at one point she and the older had four paces ahead of the younger and neither of them noticed.

The younger was behind them for the whole of it with her hands in her pockets.

Twice the woman waited for her. Once by a shop with a window full of nothing in particular, and once where the street narrows by the shop that sells the same two things. Both times she waited, and both times the younger came up alongside her and said nothing and looked at whatever the woman was looking at, and then both of them carried on.

At about a quarter to twelve, outside the shop that sells the same two things, the younger stopped and said, to the woman, "It's here," and the woman said, "It was all right, was it," and the younger said, "It was all right."

That is seven sentences in three hours and a half and every one of them is about something else.



The woman worked out most of it in the first ten minutes and worked out the rest of it in the car afterwards, and what she worked out was not the thing the older would have wanted her to work out.

What she worked out was that this was not about her. That is the part that made her sit still on a dual carriageway with the heating going and say, "Is that me?"

She had been told, on the drive out, that this was a weekly round the two sisters had been on since their mother died. She had been brought along on it as a guest. Guests do get looked at. She had expected three hours of being looked at and had got herself ready for it in the car.

What she had got was three hours of not being looked at once, and the not being looked at went on after the car as well.

In three hours and a half the younger woman had read a headstone, walked two ends of a row, stopped at four of them, washed the base of one, taken away what was dead and put in what was not, said seven sentences, and none of them had been directed anywhere. Not at the older. Not at her. Directed at nobody in particular, in the tone of a person talking out loud in an empty kitchen while carrying something.

They went back to the car and sat in it and the older counted what she had spent, which she does, out loud, in figures, and the woman said something about the town being smaller than she thought.

At twelve the younger opened the door and got out.

She shut the door behind her. She stood on the edge there for a moment, which is not unusual, and then she turned round and said to her sister, "Your door's not shutting."

And the older said, "It shuts."

And the younger said, "It shuts on the latch and then it swings itself open in the night, and it has been doing that since you had the door done," and got in the car, and drove off, and did not say anything else for ten miles.

The woman sat in the passenger side and looked straight ahead for about a minute and then said, "Is that me?"

And the older said, "That is how she says things."

And the woman said, "Right," which is what she had said at the burial ground, and the older said, "Sorry. She has never been like that with anybody. She is not like that with anybody. In eight years I have brought one person and she has not done that before," and then she stopped, because there was no end to that sentence that would have been true.

**SHE SPOKE SEVEN TIMES IN THREE HOURS AND A HALF AND NONE OF THE SEVEN WAS ABOUT ANYTHING THAT HAD HAPPENED, AND WHAT SHE SAID WAS ABOUT A DOOR, AND THE DOOR SHUTS, AND THE OLDER SAT IN THE CAR AFTERWARDS AND APOLOGISED FOR SOMEBODY WHO HAD NOT ASKED HER TO.**
