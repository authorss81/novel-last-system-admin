# Chapter 0621 — The Woman That Did the Saturdays

Friday the twelfth of February 2021, and the shop opens at half past nine and what happened happened in the middle of the morning, and she was still behind the glass at eight in the evening, in this borough.

**A MAN IN HIS EARLY FIFTIES CAME INTO A SHOP WHERE SHE IS THE ONLY ONE ON THE FLOOR AND ASKED FOR A WOMAN WHO USED TO DO THE SATURDAYS, AND SHE HAS WORKED THERE SINCE BEFORE THE SUMMER, AND SHE HAS SPENT THE DAY WITH HIS QUESTION IN THE SHOP, AND AT TWENTY PAST TWO ON A FRIDAY SHE TOLD HIM THE ONE SHOP ON THAT PARADE THAT SHUTS ON A SATURDAY AND HE WROTE IT DOWN.**

She is a woman in her mid twenties. She works Fridays and Saturdays in a shop on a parade that repairs phones and does unlocked phones and has a man who owns it in a room at the back with a radio on. She is the only one on the floor and she has been since before the summer.

She is good at it. She can tell a fault from a battery in about a minute and she is the one people trust with the thing they left with her.

---

Here is what he said.

He came in a little after the middle of the morning on the Friday and looked round the shop the way a man does when he is looking for a person, which is from the door, over the whole of it, once, and then at the people.

There was one person, and he was a man in his early fifties with a coat over his arm and a van key in his hand.

And he said, "Is she in?"

And she said, "Who?"

And he said, "The one that does the Saturdays."

And she said, "There's nobody on the Saturdays but me."

And he said, "No, no, the one that was on before."

And she said, "There's nobody before me. It's been me since before the summer and there's never been anybody else on the floor."

And he said, "That's not right."

And she said, "That's what it is."

And he said, "She said to ask for her on the Saturdays."

And she said, "What name."

And he said, "I don't know that I've got it right."

And she said, "What do you know of it."

And he said, "It's an L. That's all I've got of it. It could be one word or it could be two."

Here is where she has to say the thing she has been saying to herself all day and getting nowhere with.

She has done the right thing. She has said no to a man who was sure, in a shop that is hers for a couple of days a week, and she has said it kindly, and she has not lied to him once.

And she is also aware, and has been since the middle of the morning, that she is now the place in this borough where a stranger's errand is being kept, in a shop that she has worked in since before the summer.

That is the whole of what the day has been about and it took her until about two o'clock to put a name to it.

Here is what she got out of him, and she wrote all of it on the back of a docket pad in the stockroom at about half past twelve when he went to the shop next door to ask if it was the same one.

One: he has the first letter of her name and nothing else of it, and he cannot tell whether what she gave him was one word or two, and he has been certain of the other things.

Two: the woman used to work on Saturdays.

Three: she told him to come on a Saturday.

Four: he has come on a Friday, to a shop that is not the shop, and he has walked in and asked the only person on the floor.

And the reason she has spent the day on it is not any of the four. It is that a man walked into a shop and looked round it from the door and found her, and she was the person he was looking at, and she has not been able to get that out from under anything all day.

There are four shops on that parade that a person might mean.

There is one that shuts on a Saturday.

There is one that shut altogether in the back of last year.

And there is a unit two along that was a phone place for a long time and is not one now, and everybody on that parade knows it has not been one since long before the summer, and he did not go to that one, and he is in hers.

The man who owns it came out of the back at about one with his tea.

And he said, "What does he want."

And she said, "A woman whose name starts on an L."

And he said, "Not from here."

And she said, "I know that. He came on a Friday."

And he said, "Well, tell him."

And she said, "I could tell him that now."

And he said, "Then what are you doing."

And she said, "I'm standing here."

And he said, "You've been standing there since the middle of the morning."

And she said, "He'll be back."

And he said, "He'll be back at two."

He came back at about two and he had been next door and the shop next door is a hair place and the woman in there had said no to him.

And he said, "That's her."

And she said, "Who."

And he said, "The woman in there's not the one. She's the one who's covering."

And she said, "Covering for who."

And he said, "That's what I said, isn't it. She's covering."

And she said, "For who?"

And he said, "That's the whole thing, that."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "Have you got a bit of paper."

And she gave him a docket off the pad and he wrote an address on it and it is on the other side of the town, in a road she has never been down, and he put it in his pocket.

And he said, "Ah."

And she said, "Are you going to try that?"

And he said, "No. I haven't got the day."

And she said, "You've got the day."

And he said, "I haven't got the Saturday."

And she said, "It's Friday."

And he said, "I'll come on the Saturday."

And she said, "We shut at one on a Saturday."

And he said, "Which shop shuts at one on a Saturday."

And she said, "This one."

And he said, "This one shuts at one on the Saturday?"

And she said, "It's a phone shop. Nobody comes in on the Saturday. The owner does the repairs and I do the front."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "Which shop did you want, then."

And he said, "One that'd be open."

And she said, "Right."

That is the moment and she has been back over it about six times.

He asked her which shop shuts at one, on a Friday, at about twenty past two, in a shop she has worked in since before the summer.

And she answered him. Properly. She said, this one, and told him the hours, and told him the owner does the repairs, and she was helpful and it was the right answer and it was the answer that lost it.

Because there are four shops on that parade and three of them are open on a Saturday, and if she had said nothing, or said some other thing, he would have walked out with one of the three still open, and one of the three still open is not right either, and there is a whole great deal further than that to go.

And instead she has taken a man who is looking for a woman whose name starts on an L to a Saturday at one o'clock in a phone shop that shuts at one.

And he is going to try it.

Here is what she did with the rest of it, and it is not much, because she works.

She sold a couple of things and repaired one and got on with a job on a switch she had been putting off since the morning, and she was better at it than usual, and she noticed that.

At about four she wrote the address on a docket and put it in the drawer with the receipts, and then she took it out and put it in her bag, and then she put it back in the drawer.

At about half past five she went and stood at the front window facing out and looked at the parade.

At about ten to six a woman came past the front and stopped and looked in the window and did not come in, and she watched her go, and she has not worked out yet whether that has anything to do with anything at all.

And she did not tell the man who owns it, and he did not come out again after his tea, and it is not that he would have stopped her.

**HE ASKED HER AT TWENTY PAST TWO WHICH SHOP SHUTS AT ONE ON A SATURDAY AND SHE TOLD HIM, AND THERE ARE THREE OTHERS ON THAT PARADE THAT ARE NOT SHUT ON A SATURDAY AND HE WALKED PAST THEM, AND HE IS GOING TO TRY A SHOP THAT IS SHUT ON THE DAY HE HAS DECIDED HE CANNOT COME.**
