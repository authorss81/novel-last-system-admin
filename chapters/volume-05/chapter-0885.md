# Chapter 0885 — The Cut End Of The Stone

Friday the sixteenth of February 2024, at the gate across the front of number twelve, and from the time the dew has come off the ironwork until the path has gone grey. The space behind the leading edge of a gate.

**SHE HAS A FIVE-BAR GATE ACROSS THE FRONT OF THE HOUSE AT NUMBER TWELVE WITH A PIECE OF STONE STOOD ON END IN THE GROUND BEHIND ITS LEADING EDGE, AND SHE MEANS TO PUT THE THUMB OF ONE HAND INTO THE HOLLOW BEHIND THAT LEADING EDGE AND LEAVE IT THERE AND WALK THE FINGERS OF THE SAME HAND DOWN THE FACE OF THE STILE, AND HER SISTER IS AT THE GATE WITH HER HAND THROUGH THE BARS KEEPING IT OPEN.**

The gate is five bars and two ledges and two braces, painted at some point and not since, and it hangs on the hinge side of the front path from a single long strap.

It shuts a foot and a bit from the wall of the house, and it has shut there every day since it was put there, which is more than most gates manage.

What put it in her mind was the way it shuts.

A gate that shuts comes to a stop. The stop is what the leading edge arrives at, and it can be a post, or a block set in the ground, or a length of wall, and the whole difference between a gate that shuts and a gate that bangs is whether that stop is the same height for the whole run of the leading edge.

She has watched that leading edge for a week and it comes to something for about a foot and a half of its height, and to nothing at all above that.

From the front of the house you cannot see any of it. From the front you see a shut gate and a straight edge and the fence behind it, and whatever is behind that edge is behind it.

That is the trouble with it, and the only way round it is to get a thumb into the gap.

The hollow behind a leading edge is the space between the back of the stile and whatever the gate is coming against, and the stile of a five-bar gate is an inch and a half through, which leaves a thumb and a bit.

She lays her hand on the face of the stile with the thumb up against the back edge of it and pushes the thumb in, and the thumb goes in a full thumb's width, and then she walks the fingers of that same hand down the face of it.

A thumb left in a gap is a marker, and a marker that stays put turns a thing you cannot see into a thing you can measure everything else against. The fingers carry on down the stile, and every inch they go they are telling her whether the gap behind them is still open, and the thumb is not going anywhere.

She starts at the top, under the strap.

The thumb goes in. She walks the fingers down an inch and the thumb is where it was. Fifteen inches, and the edges of the hole the thumb is in stay the same, because the fingers have been passing over those same edges all the way down.

Twenty inches.

Then the thumb will not go any further in, which is not the same as the gap closing.

That is the part that takes a moment to see. The thumb is in a hole that has got narrower, not a hole that has shut. Something has come up behind the leading edge and taken the space out of it.

She takes the thumb out and puts it in again four inches higher up, and it goes in. Two inches higher, and it goes in. She works her way up in half-inch steps until she finds the line where the hole stops being open enough for a thumb and becomes a place a thumb can just get into, and above that line the hole is a proper hole again.

So the space behind that leading edge is a finger's width open at the top for the last few inches of it, and from that line down to the ground it is closed.

The thing that has taken it is a piece of stone.

It is stood on end in the ground at the foot of the fence, a little behind the line the gate shuts to, and the top of it stands about a foot and a half above the ground. It is four inches through, set across the line of the gate rather than along it, so that the gate comes to it edge on.

The leading edge of that gate is two feet high, which is the ordinary height of a leading edge on a five-bar gate. The stone is a foot and a half of it. So the gate shuts on the lower three parts of four of its height and stands a finger's width clear of the stone at the top, and from the path that reads as a gate that shuts.

Sandstone, by the colour of it and by the way the grain has gone pale on the top.

The way past the stone is over it, so she puts the hand over the top and lets the fingers down the far side.

They come on the ground.

And lying on the ground, flat, running back along the line of the fence, there is more of the same stone.

About two feet of it. It is under the grass with an inch of soil over the top of it, and it is the same width as the piece stood on end, and the break across it is the same break.

So the stub and the long piece were one length of sandstone about three and a half feet long, and somebody has cut it across once.

Everything above that follows from that cut, and nothing about it went wrong at the time.

The stop behind that gate was not designed. What happened is that the front boundary was made good along its length with a course of edging laid in the ground, and when that was done there were two feet of sandstone left over, and and the piece that mattered at the gate end was stood on end as a stop and the other two feet was laid in the line of the boundary and covered over, and the gate was hung afterwards to come to it.

Or the two feet went down first and the stub was the offcut, cut off to make the gate shut. One cut either way.

The stone is sound, with no rot in it anywhere, and it has been doing exactly what it was asked to do since the day it went in.

The gate is not wrong. It is a gate shut on an offcut, and it shuts.

The only thing it will not do is carry a twelve-foot ladder through the front, or a wardrobe, or a sheet of ply. A gate that comes to a stop eighteen inches high cannot be opened further than that without the leading edge lifting bodily over the stone, and lifting it takes two hands and a moment of decision, which is why anybody who has ever tried it has ended up going round the side.

The stone has been in the ground a long while and the ground has come up round the foot of it to within an inch of the top of it, and the grass along the fence is thinner over it than anywhere else, because that is where the feet of a gate go when it is lifted and set down again.

---

Her sister comes along the path about eleven with her hand through the bars of the gate to keep it open, and stands there.

She wanted to know whether the front path was going to be dug before the summer, and she was told it was not going to be dug before the summer. Then she said the bins were going back to Wednesdays, which they are not, and the woman at number twelve told her what the card says, which is Thursdays, and her sister took Thursdays and stood in the gate a while longer looking down the path at the front wall, and after that she took her hand off the bars and the gate came to.

---

That stop will be there in the spring and it will be there after the spring.

It costs nothing, and it is not going anywhere, and the only thing that will ever come of it is that the gate is a lift instead of a swing for anybody carrying anything through the front of that house.

The gate will come to the cut end of that stone for as long as the stone is where it is, and the stub will be the piece that goes first, before anyone thinks to look at the long one lying behind it.
