# Chapter 0573 — The Door at Seven

The Saturday of the twentieth of June 2020, and the eight years before it, in two houses in this borough that have never had anything to say to one another.

**HE HAS OPENED HIS OWN FRONT DOOR AT SEVEN EVERY MORNING SINCE 2012 AND STOOD IN IT FOR A MINUTE AND SHUT IT AGAIN, AND THE WOMAN IN THE HOUSE NEXT TO HIM IS UP AT TEN TO SIX AND HAS SEEN HIM DO IT EVERY MORNING FOR EIGHT YEARS AND HAS NEVER MENTIONED IT, AND ON THE TWENTIETH OF JUNE HE DID NOT, AND SHE NOTICED.**

There is about nine feet of ground between the front of one and the front of the other, with a low painted edge running along it that has been painted so often nobody could now tell what it was made of.

He is in his early fifties. She is in his late fifties. Neither of them has ever known how close in years they are, and neither of them ever will.

---

He does it at seven.

He opens the door, he stands in it, and he looks at whatever the weather is doing, and after about a minute he shuts it. Then he goes off down the road for the day.

He has done that every morning since he came into that house in 2012. Nobody has asked him why. What it is for is not something he could answer, and he has never tried, and there is nothing in it that is for anybody else. It is the first thing his day does.

---

She is up at ten to six because she has never in her life slept past six.

The front of her house is cold in the morning so she stands just outside her own door with her mug of tea in both hands, and from there the door of the house next to hers is about nine feet away and she cannot avoid it.

She has never counted it. She could not put a figure on it and has never wanted to, and she has watched it happen every morning for eight years without once finding it odd, in the way that a thing you have done beside a person for eight years stops being odd.

She has never mentioned it. She has thought about mentioning it perhaps twice and both times decided against it, and there has never been anything to put after it.

He has never asked her why she is up at that hour either, and she has never once found him up at it. In eight years the two of them have spoken about the weather, about the men who take the black sacks away, about the service at the end of the road that does not come when it says it will, and about nothing else whatever.

On the Saturday of the twentieth of June she was just outside her own door with her mug at ten to six and at seven nothing happened.

She waited. She does not know what she was waiting for. She finished the tea, and she put the mug in the kitchen, and she did not go out to anything, and at about twenty past she went up and had a bath and got dressed.

At about half nine she was just outside her own door again because she does that, and the door of the house next to hers was shut and the door of its front part was propped open and he was standing in it with his sweater on.

He has got up at six. She knows that because she hears him, every morning, through two walls. He had got up and got dressed and had not opened his own door, and she has no idea why and there is nothing whatever in her that tells her.

She did not go out. She has not been in that garden since the two of them did something with the water butt in 2018, and she was not going out at half nine on a Saturday about a door.

He did it at about ten past seven the same morning, an hour later than he does it, and she was already inside with her own door shut and did not see it done.

Nothing has passed between those two houses and nothing ever will, and the door opened at seven on the morning of the twentieth of June as it had opened every morning for eight years, and the one morning it did not, and the hour it opened at instead, are not a thing either of them can put a shape on.

**AND NOT ONE WORD HAS BEEN SAID ABOUT EITHER HALF OF IT, AND WHAT DID NOT HAPPEN ON THE TWENTIETH OF JUNE WAS THE FIRST MORNING IN EIGHT YEARS THAT NOTHING HAPPENED, AND WHAT SHE DID ABOUT IT WAS NOTHING AT ALL.**
