# Chapter 0976 — Round The Drum Flange

Undated in this draft.

**A CABLE DRUM LYING ON ITS CHEEK HAS ITS LAST TURN OF CABLE LOOSE AGAINST THE INSIDE OF ONE FLANGE.**

In the corner of the bay at the lock-up a cable drum lies on its cheek, on a bed of softwood boards that somebody put down so that the flange would not ring on the concrete. It is about two feet across the flange and two deep, and the boards under it have been indented by the weight of it in a curve that matches the flange exactly.

The drum itself is oak, two flanges and a barrel of staves between them, and it was built for a factory that wound cable onto it and never came back for it. The staves are kept by three rows of round-headed nails in the outside of the barrel, and the nails have gone to rust where the weather has reached them and have not gone at all where the barrel faces the wall.

The cable on it is armoured, and the armour has come away from the core in patches, so that the outside of the roll reads dark grey with a green bloom on the worst of it and bright zinc on the places the last turn is still wound tight. The whole roll is about the width of a man's arm and its outside has taken a set of its own where it has lain, so that the top of it stands a little proud of the two sides.

The last turn has come off the roll and lies loose against the inside of the upper flange, kept there by nothing much. It sits in the corner of the barrel with a hand's width of space between it and the roll itself, and that space is dark and has been dark as long as the drum has lain here.

The flange is damp along its inner face in the part of it that faces the boards, and the oak there has gone black at the bottom edge where the boards keep the air off it. The cable against it is damp too, along the underside of its own weight, and the green bloom on the armour comes up wherever a wet thumb goes across it.

Of the three bays this is the middle one, and its shutter is down, the rail along the bottom of it standing a foot clear of the floor with the gap behind it open to the office. The concrete of the bay is old and has begun to break up along the line of the shutter guide, and a grit of stone and paint has come up and gathered in the corners of it.

Across the yard from that, the office has a door of its own and a window that looks back towards the bay, and from the window the whole length of the apron can be seen, from the weighbridge deck to where the shutter begins. A woman who books work comes and goes between that window and the yard most days and is expected to be where she gives out that she is.

The rack along the far wall carries rolled sections and lengths of timber and a bundle of scaffold tube, and it stands behind the drum so that a person coming in off the yard has the rack on one side and the drum on the other. The boards under the drum were cut from offcuts and are of two widths, and the narrow ones have been laid across the wide ones to keep the barrel off the ground along its whole length.

Behind the bay, above the shutter, the roof light runs the width of it and lets a bar of daylight in across the concrete in the middle of the day. In the morning the bar lies along the boards under the drum and shows the dust on them. At four in the afternoon it has moved out past the drum and lies along the wall instead, and the drum itself is then in shadow from the flange right round, which is why the space behind the loose turn cannot be seen into from the yard.

Rain off the roof light frame goes down the outside of the wall and across the yard and never reaches under the flange, so what is under there is damp from the boards and not from the sky. The boards themselves dry in the middle of a fine day and are wet again by the evening, and the drying happens first at the ends and last at the middle, where the flange is closest to them.
---

The fingers go in behind the loose turn at the top of the drum and are walked right round, keeping to the flange, so that they go from the top of the barrel past the stave line and down the side and round the bottom and up the other side and back to where they began.

Going round once in this way takes about two turns of the body at the drum's edge, and the hand does not reach the middle of the barrel doing it, because the barrel is two feet across and the loose turn is a hand's width out from it. What the fingers find is the inside of the flange for its whole depth, and then the loose turn again, and then the roll.

Round the whole of the flange the space behind the loose turn keeps one width. It is about three fingers wide where the turn hangs away from the roll, and rather less than that where the turn lies over the top of the roll, because the roll has spread a little at the places where it has been sat on and the turn has followed it down.

The flange itself is flat inside and has never been braced, and there is nothing at all between the flange and the loose turn but air and the dust that comes off the cable. Where the dust is thickest the fingers read a soft floor, and where it has been knocked out of the corner by the boots of people coming round the drum there is nothing under them at all but the flange.

Nothing about the space gives. The flange does not move at the top edge and does not move at the bottom, and the loose turn does not stir, and the roll stays where it has flattened itself. The only thing that happens over the whole of the round is that the dust on the flange is redistributed by the fingers and ends up on the barrel instead, which is the one thing a hand does in there and repairs nothing.

The barrel's staves have shrunk unevenly over the years of weather and about two of them on the underside stand a shade proud of the rest, so that the loose turn catches on them as it goes round. The proud stave is enough to lift the turn a quarter of an inch and enough to take it back down again when it has passed, and the whole circumference of the drum is now marked by that one stave in the dust on the flange.

The cable on the roll is of three sizes. The inner layers are thicker and older, with the armour gone from them entirely and the core showing black under the wire, and the outer turn is thinner and brighter and came onto the drum in the last fortnight of the work that put it there. The joint between them is a straight line the whole way round, visible from the yard as a difference in the colour of the roll.

The armour that has come away has not fallen off. It lies back against the cable in curls the length of a thumbnail, and where a curl has lifted there is a small hollow of the core beneath it, and the core in that hollow is pale and dry while everything above it is wet with the bloom.

The bay takes the weight of nothing much at the moment. The shutter has been down since the winter and the boards under the drum have not been moved since they went down, and a stack of shuttering boards stands along the wall behind the rack with its ends squared off and its faces covered in the dust that comes off the concrete when anything is dragged across it.

The woman who books work comes out of the office door and crosses the apron to where the shutter begins and stops there, with the drum ahead of her and the shutter rail behind her shoulder.

"Does the early one still run on a Sunday?"

"It did in the spring."

"Then it is not the same timetable."

"I have not looked at it since Easter."

The talk goes on to whether the shop by the church keeps later now, and the reply is that it shuts earlier on the weeknights than it did, and that whoever printed the card in the window has not taken the old hours off it yet. The bay gets no mention from either of them while all that is going on.

Her hand comes off the shutter rail at some point and drops to her side before it is anywhere near the other one, and after that she stands at the end of the apron with the boards and the drum between her and the rack. The dust that was on the inside of the flange lies now on the barrel of the drum where the fingers put it, and the drum stays on its cheek in that same dark corner with the loose turn lying where it has lain for a year or more.

There is a width of that space the whole way round the flange, and it is the same width at the bottom of the drum as at the top of it.
