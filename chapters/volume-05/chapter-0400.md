# Chapter 0400 — On the Wheel

Monday the twenty-sixth of March 2018, from about nine in the morning until about half past two in the afternoon, a turning off a side street, in Tideglass.

**A MAN IN HIS FORTIES TOOK HIS OWN CHISELS OUT OF HIS BAG IN AN EMPTY GARAGE HE WAS NOT ASKED TO BE IN AND PUT FOUR OF THEM ON A WHEEL THAT WAS NOT HIS AND GROUND THEM PROPERLY, AND A WOMAN WHO WORKS IN A SHOP STOOD IN THAT DOORWAY FOR ABOUT A MINUTE WITH A BAG OF SHOPPING AND WATCHED HIM DO IT, AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID A WORD, AND HE IS NOT TOLD THAT ANYBODY WATCHED.**

The turning comes off a side street and there are nine lock-up garages along it, the oldest ones brick and the newer ones a grey block, and they are about eight foot wide each, and they all go up on a rope except the new ones, and the ropes have not been looked at since the middle of the sixties.

Number two belongs to a man in his sixties who repairs things and is away that week, and is not far away, and came back on the Saturday.

Number four is his as well. It has been standing open since the middle of February, because he carried a table out of it on a hand truck, took it home to his own front room and never carried it back, and he has not shut that door since, and it does not lock, and the key is where it has always been, which is on a nail inside the door of number two.

The man in his forties has had that key for about six years. He has never asked for it. Nobody has ever offered it to him. He has used number four maybe a dozen times in six years and he has always put the door down afterwards and the key back on the nail.

Number nine has a car in it, and the car belongs to the woman who works in a shop.

---

His own place at the top of the town was full on that Monday.

There is a job in a house in Bleam Street that is on until Wednesday, and everything for it is in his own garage in four sacks and a stack, and there is nowhere to stand.

He came up that side street at about nine on the Monday morning with his bag over his shoulder and went along that turning on foot, because there is nowhere to stop a van in it and the man who owns the two spaces on the far side of that turning is not having anybody else on them.

Number four was standing open with the door about two foot down on its rope, and there was a bucket in the mouth of it and a ratchet strap coiled on the ground beside the bucket.

He put his bag on the bench inside and got his chisel roll out of it and set it along the bench and unrolled it.

There are four chisels in that roll. They are his and have been for years. A half-inch, a quarter, a half and a quarter, and a one-inch, all of them with ash handles gone dark and all of them with blades that have been sharpened so many times the bevels are running out.

A wheel is in that garage.

It is a pedestal grinder about thirty inch off the floor with a foot pedal and a water tray, and it came out of a workshop that shut in about 2009 and the man in his sixties has had it for years. He has used it four or five times. He knows the wheel is a bit out of true, and that you have to take more off the left-hand side of a chisel than the right, and he has worked that out the hard way.

He put the water in the tray and turned the wheel over with his hand and put his thumb on it, which is a thing you do and which is not dangerous if you have a rag and you keep the wheel still.

Then he sharpened them.

He does it properly, which takes longer.

He puts the bevel on the wheel and works from the corner in and does not come round the face until the bevel is flat, and he does it on one side and turns the chisel over and does it on the other, and he wets it every eight or ten passes, and he goes back and looks at it with the light off the ceiling and the light off the open door both on the same piece of steel, which is how you see a bevel.

The half-inch took about twenty-five minutes.

The quarter took about fifteen, and it came off it in about eight minutes and then he went round the face for the rest of it, and that is the right way round for a small chisel and he did the half-inch backwards first and does not do that again.

The half and a quarter he had to take the wheel down for, because the bevel is two and a quarter inches and the wheel is a ten-inch and there is not room to get round it. So he laid it on the bench and worked along the edge of the wheel with it at about a quarter of a right angle, which is not how you do it and which comes out the same if you keep it wet and keep your hands out of the way.

The one-inch he did first and he did last, and he did it twice because the first time he took too much off the back and had to leave it a fraction thick.

He wiped each blade down with a rag and put them back into the roll in order and rolled it up. He wiped the tray out, sponged the spill under the wheel off the floor, sponged the bench down and put his bag under the bench, and he put the cover over the wheel and the cover is a piece of hardboard and a brick.

He was in there from about ten past nine until about half past one and he did not speak to anybody, because there was nobody to speak to, and nothing he did was for anybody, and the wheel belongs to a man who is not in this borough this week and who will not know a chisel was put on it.

He got what he came for. He had wanted to do that for about a week and he had not been able to stand the chisel, and he has not said to anybody that he came up there for it, and he has not said it to himself on the way back down that turning either.

---

The woman who works in a shop parks in number nine and had gone in there at about ten past nine for a bag of shopping that was in the boot.

Number nine is four doors along from number four. She came out of hers at about a quarter to ten with a paper bag in each hand.

Number four was open.

She stopped, because it is unusual, and she looked in.

The door is up about two foot on its rope, so a person standing in that turning can see the middle of that garage and not the top of it. What she could see from about ten foot was a man with his back to her, on one knee on a piece of board, going at a chisel on a wheel that was running, with a bucket of water under it, and the light off the ceiling and the light off the open door both on the same piece of steel.

She stood there.

She was standing there for about a minute. She did not put the bags down and she did not step back and she did not go in. He had his back to the door and his right shoulder across the opening and he did not turn round once in that minute, and a wheel running in a room about eight foot across makes a noise you cannot hear over.

At the end of it she walked the four doors to number nine and put her bags in the boot and shut it and drove out of that turning.

Neither of them said anything.

She has not been in number four. She did not go in that morning and she has not gone in since, and she has not said to anybody that a man was in there, and the man in his sixties, who got back on the Saturday, has not been told any of it, and she would not if it came up, and the answer she would give is that number four is not her garage and she does not know who is in it.

He does not know she was there. He was inside that garage for four and a quarter hours and she stood at the mouth of it for about a minute of that, and he was kneeling on a board with a wheel turning six foot from his head, and four doors away a car door shut and he did not hear that one either.

The man in his sixties came back on the Saturday morning and went into number four at about ten.

He was in there for about ten minutes.

The bench was wiped and the wheel was covered and there was a brick on the cover where there is always a brick, and the water tray had been emptied and the spill under the wheel had been sponged up, and there was no chisel roll on that bench.

He looked at the bench. He did not say anything about it, then or after.

He shut the door down, hung the key back on its nail and went out. He said nothing to the man in his forties about any of it, and on that Saturday afternoon, or any time since, the whole of what he has to say about the bench and the wheel is that the spillage under it had been sponged up.

**HE PUT FOUR CHISELS ON A WHEEL THAT WAS NOT HIS IN AN EMPTY GARAGE HE WAS NOT ASKED TO BE IN, AND FOUR DOORS AWAY A WOMAN WITH TWO BAGS OF SHOPPING STOOD AND WATCHED HIM FOR ABOUT A MINUTE, AND HE NEVER FOUND OUT.**
