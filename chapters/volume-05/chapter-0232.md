# Chapter 0232 — Not Counted

Wednesday the sixth of April 2016, from about half past seven in the evening, a hall off a parade of shops in Tideglass, and the corridor outside it.

**A PERSON WHO IS NOT COUNTED IS NOT A PERSON WHO IS HELPED, AND A BOROUGH THAT HAS BEEN KEPT GOING BY PEOPLE WHO WERE NEVER ASKED AND NEVER COUNTED HAS PAID NOTHING FOR ANY OF IT SINCE THE SPRING AND IS ABOUT TO BE GIVEN A BILL FOR IT BY ANOTHER CITY, AND THE SENTENCE THAT SAYS BOTH THINGS IS CORRECT, AND SAYING IT IN A ROOM IN THIS BOROUGH IS HEARD AS MAKING AN OBJECTION.**

The hall is free on a Wednesday for the mothers' group, which was finished at about eight, and it was free at half past seven for the same reason it was free on a Friday in February, which is that nobody rents it and the woman with the key is in it and does not charge anybody for the gas.

About nine people came. They are not a committee and have not agreed to be anything. Mrs Obi, who is about thirty-six, took the minutes on a pad she takes home in a handbag. The man of about eighteen who washes up in a kitchen at the back of the parade was in and had been twice before. The young woman of about twenty who is at a college four days a week was in. A man from the works on the far side of the river who comes when he can. Two others. And the woman of about forty-two who has had the key to that hall for about nine years and who let it out originally by a key and by a notebook she keeps in a drawer, and a card by the door with the names of the week on it.

Jonas Mercer was in the corridor with his coat over his arm. He had come at a quarter to seven and had not gone in and was not going to, and the door was open about four inches, which it always is, because the fire notice is on the wall opposite and somebody has to be able to read it.

Tomas Renn said it at about ten past eight, standing up, and he did not build up to it.

"We've got eleven names on a bit of paper in a county we've not been to, and nine of them are in Hanna Wray's carrier bag, and Hanna Wray is in this room's sister room most Fridays and has never once been asked about them. Not her. Not one of them. And she's not the only one, and that eleven is not the only eleven, and the reason it's eleven is that we don't count, and I want to say the other half, because I only did half and I'm not going to sit here having done half.

"A person who is not counted is not a person who is helped. That's the sentence. It's been the sentence since September and it is not on any wall, and that is the first thing that is wrong with it. And this borough has been getting through since the spring on people who went out of a shop on a Saturday because they wanted to, and did a good job, and got paid, and came back, and were never asked, and were never counted, and have paid nothing towards any of it, because nobody can make anybody pay anything, and there is a sheet of paper with their names on it sitting in a building by the water that half the people in this room have never been inside, and when that sheet of paper is finished, somebody is going to work out what this borough owes for having had eleven good jobs done for nothing. And they will not be wrong to work it out. That is the thing I can't get past. They won't be wrong."

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"That's an objection," said Mrs Obi.

"I know it's an objection."

"You're sitting there saying it's an objection."

"I'm saying it's a fact and it sounds like an objection," said Renn. "I've been in this room long enough to know the difference and I'm not going to be the one who pretends the room can't hear it."

"What are you asking for?" said Mrs Obi.

Renn opened his mouth, and shut it.

Nothing was asked for. That was true and it was the whole of his position and he was not going to be able to put it down in a way that made it sound like something else, and the room waited about ten seconds and then somebody at the back asked whether there was anything else and Tomas Renn said no and sat down.

---

The gas came on about a quarter to nine because the woman with the key put a pound in it, and the room did what the room does, which is go on, and the young woman of about twenty said that the card by the door still said six and there were still seven keys, which everybody in that room had known since February and nobody had mentioned.

And then Mrs Obi said the old one, and she said it flatly, with the pad shut on her knee, and she had been saying it in that room for about two years.

"I want to say the other thing, because Tomas has said a new thing and I'd like somebody to say the old one so it isn't just the new one. We've had a thing on about forty walls since the September. A man's had a pump going in a doorway at the bottom of Peverell Street since the wet week and nobody asked him where the hose went and nobody's ever going to. There's two of them out there in the rain outside the third door with a shovel and a broom, not a word between them, in November, and that wasn't organised by anybody. There's a label went on a shelf in a chemist's and whoever put it there is not in this room. There's a bench moved in a square in a town up the estuary and a shop sign that said the wrong time on it for a year and got put right by a woman with a marker. There's a hose on a lorry in that same town that nobody's ever come for. There's a man of about eighty-six in that town who gets up at a quarter to six and rakes an intake on his hands and knees and nobody has ever asked him and nobody's going to. All of that happened and none of it was asked for and none of it was counted and the town that owns all of those people owns nobody."

Nobody disagreed with her. Nobody in that room had ever disagreed with her about any part of that, and the room went on.

"The region that will send the bill owns nobody either," said the woman of about forty-two with the key, looking at the pad. "That's the same sentence twice. And it's still true, so it doesn't get us anywhere, and I'm not saying it to get us anywhere. I'm saying it because he said a new thing and I wanted the old thing said tonight as well."

**AND NOBODY IN THAT ROOM PUT THOSE TWO THINGS TOGETHER, AND A MINUTE WENT HOME IN A HANDBAG, AND THE HALL WAS LOCKED AT TEN O'CLOCK BY A PERSON WITH A KEY, AND A MAN STOOD IN A CORRIDOR WITH HIS COAT OVER HIS ARM WHO COULD HAVE PUT THEM TOGETHER AND DID NOT, AND WENT DOWN THE STAIRS AND OUT ONTO THE PARADE.**
