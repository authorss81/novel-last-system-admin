# Chapter 0871 — A Hand's Depth Of Chippings

Thursday the twenty-first of December 2023, at the butt base in the back yard at number ten from the time the frost has gone off it until the middle of the afternoon. How deep the chippings have come round it.

**THERE IS A RING OF CHIPPINGS BANKED ROUND THE FOOT OF THE BUTT BASE AT NUMBER TEN AND SHE MEANS TO PRESS THE HEEL OF ONE HAND FLAT AGAINST THEM TO FIND THE LINE WHERE THE GROUND STOPS IT, AND HER SISTER GOES OUT THROUGH THE SIDE GATE WITH A BAG.**

The ring has been there since the base was set. It is about a foot wide all the way round and it is a hand's depth at the top of it where it is banked up against the edge of the base, and the chippings in it are the ones off the front bed with the grass taken off them, which is what chippings are for.

Chippings are not gravel and they are not sand. They are the small stuff that comes off a bed when it is worked, and they are about the size of a nail head and a half, and they have edges on them because they have never been rolled by anything. Gravel has been rolled by water for a hundred million years and has no edges left, which is why a gravel bed keeps its shape and a bed of chippings does not. Chippings banked against a hard edge keep their shape because the pieces lean on each other and lock. Chippings on soft ground do not, and the wind takes the top of them in a dry week and the water takes them in a wet one.

That is why the ring is a foot wide and not six inches. Six inches of chippings banked against a base would be in the water in a single wet week. A foot is the width at which loose broken stone stops being a pile of stone and starts being a slope that behaves.

The base is a concrete block about eighteen inches across with a pot set into the middle of it and the pot is fed off the tap, and the tap has not been touched in a month and is not going to be touched today.

The ring is there so that what comes off the roof goes over it and not down the side of the base. That is the entire purpose of it and it does that job.

What she wants to know is whether it is still doing it everywhere.

The heel of a hand is about three-quarters of an inch thick across, at the heel, where the meat of it is and not at the fingers, and the heel of a hand is the part of a hand that has no business being a depth gauge and is the best one there is.

You put the heel of your hand flat down into a heap of chippings and push, and you go down until either the ground comes up and stops you or the chippings stop you, and there is no third answer. Deep chippings and your hand stops in them and you cannot tell what is under. Shallow chippings and the ground comes up through into the heel of your hand and you can tell that you have got there, because ground is not the same as chippings and a hand knows the difference between them without being told.

So she goes round the outside of that ring with her heel working, at about a hand's width in from the base on every side, and she goes round it twice.

It is the same all the way.

Hand's depth. At the top of the bank where it is built up against the base, deeper. At the outer edge, less. The ring is doing what it was made to do.

Except in one place.

On the side that faces the house, about a third of the way round from the gate, her heel goes through.

It is not that the chippings are shallower there. It is that they are the same and the ground under them has gone down.

The heel of her hand goes through the chippings and keeps going, and what it finds is not the hard face of the ground that the whole ring is sitting on but a soft brown nothing about an inch thick, and at the bottom of it the ground is still there and is not much further away than anywhere else.

So the ground under that one part of the ring has gone down about an inch, and the ring over it is at the same depth as the ring everywhere else, which means the ring over it is a foot and three-quarters deep instead of a foot and a half, and a foot and three-quarters of chippings on soft ground is a ring that is a quarter of an inch deeper into the soft place than it is anywhere else.

She stands there looking at it for a while before she understands what she has found, and the thing she has found is not that the ring is shallow.

It is that the ring is level.

Everything else about that ring is loose stone banked up against a hard edge, and loose stone finds its own level, and loose stone on hard ground stands higher where it is banked higher and lower where it is lower. A ring of chippings round a base is not a designed shape. It is a heap that has been pushed into a ring with a foot and it has settled and the frost has been at it for four seasons and it is level because level is what it does.

Which means that one inch of ground going down under it did not make a hole in it. It made no difference to it at all, because the heap went down with it.

And what that means is that the ring is going to keep being level whatever the ground under it does, and that the water off the roof will run over that ring for ever without noticing, and that the only place on the whole of it where the chippings will ever go is the outer edge over the soft ground, where they have been going since the base was set and where they will go twice as fast as anywhere else.

There is nothing in that yard that would put the ground up under that inch.

Making good a soft place under a ring of chippings means taking the chippings off, taking out the soft stuff and putting hard stuff in and letting it come down, and then putting the chippings back, and that is a job with a barrow and a bar and a day. It also means taking the chippings off a base that has been in the ground since the spring, and putting them all back exactly as they were, and being certain at the end of the day that the ring is still a ring and not a heap with a hole in it.

Nobody has ever asked her to do that and she is not going to do it in December, and she is not going to pretend to herself that it is a thing she would do in June either.

There is no wear on the chippings in that ring at all, which is the last thing she goes round to look at. Nothing goes across a chippings ring. Nothing is set down on it, nothing is dragged over it, and no boot stays on it, and in four seasons of weather it has not been walked on once. It is the only part of the back yard at number ten that has not been touched by anybody going to and fro across it, and the only thing that has ever happened to it is that it has been left.

Her sister goes out through the side gate about eleven with a bag and does not come in again that day.

They do not say anything to each other. The side gate is on the other side of the house from the butt and there is a whole length of wall between the two of them, and the chippings did not move and the base did not move and the ring is a ring.

---

The one place in the ring round that butt where the ground has gone down is on the side that faces the house, and the chippings over it are a foot and three-quarters deep, and they are going to be exactly that deep this winter and next summer and for as long as the ring lasts.
