# Chapter 0233 — Six

Monday the eleventh of April 2016, from about half past seven in the evening, a Methodist hall on Carrow Lane in Tideglass, and the corridor outside it.

**THE SIX QUESTIONS WERE ON A TABLE IN A ROOM AND NONE OF THEM WAS ANSWERED, AND A MAN WHO IS VERY GOOD IN THAT ROOM PUT A VERSION ON THE TABLE THAT WOULD HAVE SETTLED THREE OF THEM AT ONCE, AND IT RESTED ON A PIECE OF PAPER THAT CANNOT BE PRODUCED, AND HE SAID SO HIMSELF, IN FRONT OF NINE PEOPLE, AND SAT DOWN.**

The hall was booked for that Monday evening because somebody had a key and the gas is on a meter and the meter takes pound coins, and about nine people came, and the women outnumbered the men by about three to one, which it always does.

Jonas Mercer was in the corridor. The corridor is about fifteen feet and it has a radiator in it that has been out since about 1998 and a notice about a lost cat from 2014 laminated on the wall. The door was open four inches because the fire notice is on the wall opposite and somebody has to be able to read it.

The man who is very good in that room is in his sixties and is a building estimator by trade and came to the first of these about six years ago and has never once been elected to anything, and he does the thing in there that nobody else will do, which is to take whatever the room has been carrying for a month and put it into a shape.

He took the best part of an hour over the six questions, and he did them in order, and nobody interrupted him.

"One, which of the dated things this borough has done once is the first one somebody will be able to prove, and whether it's proved by a person standing there or by a person who reads about it afterwards. Still open, and there are more candidates now than there were in the autumn, and not one of them can be told from inside.

"Two, which of the eight dated things is the first one somebody decides to keep, and whether it's kept because it was good or because it was expensive. Open. It's been open two years.

"Three, whether the page or the chair is the first one somebody asks a person to agree to. Open. And there's a fourth candidate now, which is two letters that are both binding and neither of which cancels the other.

"Four — which of two opposite shapes this city is going to have to stop, and what a person has to be holding to stop each of them. Open. The second of those two is still nothing.

"Five — the one that's ours. A permission is a thing a person may do and an obligation is a thing a person has to do, and the same act is one of each on two pieces of paper, and one of those two is a sheet on a wall in a town up the estuary with a man's name under it, and he is not the only one who can and he is not the only one who does. Open. And there are three documents in three hands now, and it's still not a question, it's a fact about paper.

"Six — a claim in a drawer at the end of a corridor, made correctly in another city in December, and nobody in this borough can tell whether it's a price, a receipt or a precedent, and two people in two cities know what it is and neither has said. Open. And there's a second claim and a schedule with eleven names on it, and the second of those is worse, and worse is not the same as bigger, and I want that said before anybody starts."

He stopped, and looked at the table.

"I'm not going to add a seventh. There's no seventh. There's a temptation and I'll name it and then leave it alone."

**AND THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THE WALL OF THAT HALL THAT SPRING, printed and framed by a woman in 2013 who has moved away, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A QUESTION CAN BE CARRIED FOR A YEAR.**
> **A QUESTION IS NOT CARRIED BY THE PERSON WHO ASKS IT.**
> **NEITHER OF THOSE IS A CORRIDOR.**

And on the wall behind him, on the same wall as the fire notice, about four feet along, there was a piece of card the size of a postcard with two sentences on it, printed, and it has been on that wall since about the September, and it is the thing that this whole account has been leaning on without ever once needing to change it.

> *A body that cannot require anybody to do anything cannot be told what it will do.*
> *A body that cannot require anybody to do anything is not asked what it will do.*

"I have had a go at settling three of those tonight," he said, "and I want to be honest about it with you, because I've been sitting here for twenty minutes waiting for somebody to catch me, and nobody has, and I am going to catch myself.

"It works. It works beautifully. You take the sheet on the wall in that town up the estuary, the four lines, the one with the man's name on it, and you take the sheet in a corridor in another city, the one with one line on it, and you put the two of them on a table and you say: look, this town holds a duty in public and a person in person, and that city holds a system in private and a person nowhere at all, and the difference between those two is the whole of the argument, and here it is in two pieces of paper, and there it is. And question four falls, and question five falls, and half of question two falls, and we'd have done it in a fortnight and we'd have had a document you could show anybody.

"And I can't produce either of them. One of them is on a wall about four hundred yards from a gate that a man of eighty-six opens by hand at seven every morning, and the other one is about two hundred people a day walking past a corridor in a city on the far side of the water, and between them there is an estuary and a Tuesday, and a Tuesday is the whole of what is in the way."

The room waited.

"Nobody in this borough can produce the first one. I've been told there's a wall in a town up the estuary and a man on a wall and a name under it, and the man who read it refused a copy and said why and was right, and the copy would not have been the sheet on the wall anyway, and he was right about that too. And the second one is a corridor in a city on the other side of the water and it's a woman with a pen off her own bag."

"So it's a story," said somebody.

"It's not a story, it's two documents, and one of them has been read by one man in a corridor and the other by one man in a corridor in the same week, and they're eight miles and an estuary apart, and they were never in the same room and they never will be." He put his hands flat on the table. "And it is not the story that settles it. It's the paper. If you had the paper you could put it in a drawer and it would be a fact anybody could pick up. Without the paper it's a thing two men said, and one of us is the only person who has read both, and he has told me about them in a corridor, and that is not a document, and I am not going to build a Thursday night on it."

"That card's on the wall," said a woman who had been in that hall about two years and who had not come for the six questions and came anyway. "The one behind you."

"I know what's on the card. That's why I'm sat down."

Nobody argued the card out of itself. That has never once happened in that hall and it did not happen that night, and the reason it never happens is that both halves of that card are the same half of the same thing, and a room that is full enough of people who are right about a small thing does not go and pick the card up and try to make it mean the other half.

Somebody in that room wrote for about ten minutes after that, in their own hand, in a book, and nobody read it over their shoulder, and at about twenty to nine the book was shut and went out of the door with the person who had written it, and nobody in that hall has ever asked to see it, and it is not on anybody's book.

Nobody in that room was given a title, a grade, a department, a chair, a seat, a membership, a form or a row on a rota, and there is no minute.

---

At about ten to ten Jonas Mercer went into the building by the water on the way home because he had a key reason to be near it, which was that he did not.

The records room was shut and the building is shut at ten. He stood on the pavement outside it for about two minutes, in the cold, and did not try the door, and thought about a room where a register is open at the last written page, nineteen entries, a yard light with a bulb against it and the bulb out, and a line under the nineteenth that goes down the page and stops short of the bottom with nothing after it, and the whole of what is on that line is a number he does the sum for every month and has never once been asked to do it for and has never once written down.

**AND A BUILDING BY THE WATER WAS SHUT AT TEN AND A MAN STOOD OUTSIDE IT AND DID NOT GO IN, AND NOTHING WAS ANSWERED IN A METHODIST HALL, AND A MAN IN HIS SIXTIES SAT DOWN AT TEN TO NINE AND DID NOT GET UP AGAIN, AND THE LINE UNDER THE NINETEENTH IS STILL BLANK.**
