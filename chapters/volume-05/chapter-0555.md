# Chapter 0555 — The Low Bar

The Friday of the twenty-seventh of March 2020, and most of the middle of the day, in the kitchen at the back of one building off a street of this borough.

**SHE HAS PUT THEM ON THE LOW BAR TWICE A WEEK SINCE 2016 AND HE HAS EATEN THEM TWICE A WEEK EVER SINCE HE CAME, AND HE IS THE ONLY PERSON IN THAT BUILDING WHO HAS EVER SAID ANYTHING TO HER ABOUT WHAT SHE PUTS THEM ON, AND SHE ANSWERED HIM AND WENT BACK DOWNSTAIRS.**

The building is three floors and about thirty people work in it. It does one other thing, which is that anybody who comes in off that street can come up and be told what they are owed by somebody. There is a corridor on the first floor that everybody in the building goes down twice a day and nobody has ever chosen.

The kitchen is at the back on the ground floor. Lunch goes out at half past twelve for anybody in the building who wants it, and about twenty-two of the thirty come, and it is the same meal every day except that it changes on a Monday.

She is a woman in her late thirties. She has been at that stove since the March of 2016 and she does the cooking and the two of them do everything else in that kitchen between them.

He is a man in his early fifties. He came to that building in the October before last and she showed him the job, which is what happened, and she showed him all of it, and there was no part of it she kept back.

The fish cakes are on twice a week. They have always been on twice a week. They go in at about half past ten and they come out at about twenty to twelve, and about half of them are eaten.

She cooks them on the low bar.

That is not a shortcut anybody handed her and it is not a thing she decided on. The low bar takes about half of what the top bar takes, and the top bar takes eight minutes longer to come up to than the hot end does, and there are two of them in that kitchen and about twenty-two people waiting on the other side of a hatch.

She has never put anything on the top bar. Since 2016 she has not. And the reason it has never once come up between the two of them is that there is no point in any week at which either of them makes the decision.

---

He came in on the Wednesday of the fourth of March at about half past twelve and sat down with the rest of them, which he had not done before. He had eaten, and he sat at the end of the long thing under the window, and he had been in that building for two years and had never taken a midday meal with anybody in it.

She had her back to that part of the kitchen and did not see him sit down.

He bit into it and stopped, and sat there with it in his hand for a bit, and then ate the whole of it.

He says he did not think about it again for the rest of that day and he says he thinks about it most days since, and both of those are true and neither of them has been said out loud to a living soul.

Nobody said anything that Wednesday. He ate all of it. There were people either side of him and one of them asked him whether the lift was working again and he said it was not.

On the Thursday he was on the early and on the Friday he was not in at all, and on the Monday he was on the late. There were six fish cake days between that Wednesday and this one, and he had every one of them in front of him in that corridor, and he used none of them, and there is nobody in that building he would tell about the Wednesday.

At ten past one he came out at the hatch end with a cloth over his shoulder and went up.

Five other people were in that corridor, which is what a corridor is at that time on that day. The woman who sits at the front, the man who does the second floor, a young man who started in the January, and two others he could not have named to save himself.

It is about four feet wide. The window at the end of it stands open an inch, and that is the only reason anybody stands still in the middle of it, and that is why there were five of them in there.

He stopped in the middle and said it to the floor rather than to anybody.

"They're hard, those."

She was behind him. She had come up at the same time with her sleeves turned back.

"They've always come out like that," she said.

That was the whole of it. Nobody answered him and nobody turned round. The woman nearest the window said, "They do," and went back to the thing she was doing with her hands, and that is the only time since 2016 that anybody in that building has said one word about the fish cakes.

The young man in the January did not hear it. He was at the far end with his back to both of them and he has said since that he heard nothing at all at that lunch, and he would have said so on the day.

She went back down and washed the hot end out and did the rest of the afternoon, and she did not ask him one thing about it in the four hours before five, and he did not say one word to her about anything except the day and the weather.

---

**THE ONLY THING ANYBODY SAID ABOUT THEM WAS ONE SENTENCE FROM A MAN WHO HAD BEEN EATING THEM SINCE THE OCTOBER BEFORE LAST, AND THE ONLY ANSWER WAS THAT THEY HAD ALWAYS COME OUT LIKE THAT, AND THE NEXT BATCH WENT ON THE LOW BAR AT HALF PAST TEN ON THE SATURDAY MORNING, AND HE ATE ALL OF THAT ONE AS WELL.**
