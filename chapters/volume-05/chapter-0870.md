# Chapter 0870 — Three Cards Deep

Monday the eighteenth of December 2023, at the bench at the unit from first light until the last of the light goes off the yard wall. How deep the top of that bench is.

**THERE IS THE BENCH AGAINST THE WALL OF THE UNIT YARD AND A BUNDLE OF STIFF CARDS OFF THE STORE SHELF AND HE MEANS TO STAND ONE CARD ON END ON THAT BENCH TOP AND PUSH DOWN ON IT WITH ONE FINGERTIP UNTIL IT WILL GO NO FURTHER, AND THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN COMES THROUGH THE NEAR GATE TO THE STORE STEP WITH A BAG AND GOES BACK OUT AGAIN.**

The bench is a scaffold board on two trestles and it has been in that yard since the spring, hard against the wall under the gutter, with the ends of the trestles about a foot apart along the front.

The top of it has gone down in two places.

Not a sag. A sag goes down all along and is a fault in the bench, and a bench with a sag in it gets a new top. This is two hollows, one a foot in from each end of the front edge, and a board that has two hollows in a line a man can put his two feet into at once.

Anything set on that front edge slides to the middle of the board, because the middle of the board is the only part of the front edge that is still high enough for anything to sit on.

He has a rule for it. He has had one since he was at school and it is in the store on the shelf with the string and the pencils and it has not been out of that store since the shop it came from shut.

But it is not going to be got out for this, because a rule is no good across a hollow. A rule is a straight thing and a hollow is the absence of one, and the rule would lie to him along the length of the board exactly as much as across it, and it would lie the same amount every time, which is what makes a lie of that sort so hard to catch.

What he goes back into the store for instead is the cards.

They are a bundle of fifty, stiff enough not to sag, the sort that comes in a bundle and stays in a bundle. A card is a known thickness, and everybody who has ever handled a bundle of them knows it without knowing a number, because a card is the thing you count by and it has been the thing you count by for two hundred years.

He takes about six off the top of the bundle, which is as many as he can get a finger onto, and puts the bundle back on the shelf where the bundle was.

The technique is not to push the card down flat.

A card stood on end and pushed on from above will lean. It leans because the board underneath is not level, and how far it leans is the whole of the reading.

So he stands one card on end on the top of the bench at the left-hand end, with the long edge of the card across the front of the board, and puts the pad of his first finger on the top edge of it and pushes straight down.

It goes down about three cards deep and then the board is under it.

He does the same at the right-hand end and gets three cards.

Then a foot in from the left, on the front edge.

The card stands up, and it is not leaning because he has pushed it over. He pushes straight down on it and it goes over backwards, away from him, about the thickness of a card, and its bottom edge stays on the board at the near side of the hollow and its top edge goes out over the dip.

Four cards.

He does it at the other hollow and gets four cards, and he does it in the middle of the board and gets three, and he does it along the back edge the whole way down and gets three the whole way, because the back edge of that bench is against a wall and nobody has ever leaned on a wall.

He goes along the front edge in between the hollows as well, a thumb's width at a time, so that he knows where each hollow starts and where it ends, and the left one runs about eight inches and the right one runs about nine, and the front edge between them is the highest part of the whole board, which is the other thing that is wrong with it.

Three cards at the ends, three in the middle, three along the back, and four in two hollows on the front edge a foot in from each end.

That is not a fault. That is about forty years of people leaning on a bench.

There is no wear on the top face of that board except in those two hollows, where it has gone from the colour it was to the colour of the inside of a card in about ten years, and the two of them are the same size to within a finger's width, which means they are not an accident. Somebody stood in that exact place for a long time, and then somebody else stood in the mirror of it, and then neither of them ever moved.

He puts his own feet in them to see, which is a thing he had not meant to do when he came out of the store with the cards.

It is an extraordinary thing to feel. The board comes up either side of him like the lip of a trough, and his weight goes straight through the middle of it, and the bench does not move at all, because a scaffold board on two trestles is a stiff thing and a quarter of an inch at the front edge is nothing to it structurally and is everything to a tray set down on it.

A tray is the case that matters. A tray on that front edge goes in. It goes in whichever way up the bench is faced, it goes in at either hollow, and it goes in at the middle as well, because a tray has a foot on it four times and three of those four are standing on a hollow. And a bench where trays go is a bench for putting things down on and taking them off again, and not a bench for doing anything on.

There is a way out of that and it is the way everybody takes, which is to turn the board over. That would put the hollows on the back edge and the flat on the front, and it would double the wear into a third place, and the board would be an inch and a half at the front instead of three-quarters, and it would have to be done in a shed with two men and a pair of trestles, for a bench.

He does not do it and he does not write it down and he does not think about it again, and that is the correct decision and not a deferral, because a bench is a bench and it was a bench before the hollows and it is a bench after them and nothing about it is getting worse this week.

---

The woman from two doors down comes through the near gate at about ten with a bag she has to carry in front of her, and gets as far as the store step, and puts the bag down on the step, and stands there.

"Your step's got ice on it."

"It's had it since Friday."

"Ours is worse."

"Yours is always worse."

She picks the bag up again and goes back out through the same gate, and he does not touch the gate while she is going through it, and the bag never came off the step and he never went near it, and the bench is six feet away from that step against the wall and she did not turn her head towards it.

---

He goes and puts the six cards back on the bundle on the store shelf, and the bundle is where it was, and the bench has four cards of depth in it in two places and three everywhere else.

Three cards, and that bench top has been three cards deep since before there was anything at all in that yard to be set on it.
