# Chapter 0763 — The Slab On The Front Path

Friday the seventh of October 2022, in this borough. She is in her own house at number nine from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a paving slab lifted, bedded and left tilting the other way.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES HAS A SLAB ON HER FRONT PATH THAT ROCKS, A MAN SHE HAS NEVER HAD IN THE HOUSE LIFTS IT AND BEDS IT HIGH AT THE DOOR END, AND THE SLAB IS LEFT ROCKING AT THE OTHER END.**

She is at number nine on Friday with a flag on the front path that will not stop knocking. A man from up the road stops at her gate with his shopping, lifts the slab, beds it his own way, and leaves.

---

She is in the kitchen from early with the fire unlit and the back door shut true and its card at the hinge. The pads stand on the fridge with the first one at the bottom of the stack. The front room door is shut. She has the one bulb on in the ceiling and nothing else lit.

There are three flags along the front path and the middle one has gone loose again since she did them in September. It does not wobble when you walk on it, which is the trouble with it. It goes down with a knock and comes up with a knock when you put a heel on the corner, and she has been stepping over it or beside it ever since she packed those joints in September, and a fortnight ago the woman who does her shopping put her heel on the corner of it on her way out and it went down and stayed down.

She has a bar, a piece of firewood to slide across so the steel does not chip the joint, and a bucket of sand from the pile at the side of the path. She has not told anybody she is going to do it and she is not going to, and she is at the gate with the bar and the piece of wood when a man coming back with two bags in his hands stops outside.

---

"You are going to take that one up."

"I am going to take that one up."

"Wait till I have been in." He puts his bags down on the path beyond the slab. "I am not walking over that with milk and I am not putting them on the step either, because that is your step and I will not have my bags on it."

She waits until he has gone in and shut his door and come out again and stood on his own side of her gate, and then she tells him the whole of it, the knocking and the woman and the bucket, and he looks down at the slab and then at the bar in her hand.

"Give me that."

"You have not asked what I want done with it."

"You have a slab that is loose and a bucket of sand and a bar." He takes the bar and the piece of firewood from her. "You want it bedded and level and not loose. Give me the sand tipped out and be out of the way for ten minutes."

She tips the sand out in a heap on the path beside the slab and steps back along the path to the gate and stands there with her hands in front of her.

---

It takes him longer than ten minutes, because the slab has a lip on the underside where the bedding has run out from under one edge over two winters and the slab has to come up straight and not twist, and he works the bar back and forth under the near edge until the corner comes up an inch, and then he works it under the middle, and then the whole thing lifts and he takes it out onto the path and lays it down face down on the stones.

The bed underneath is what she expected. It is sand gone to a paste in the middle with grit worked down into it, and along the outer edge it is dry sand with a strip of nothing at all, and under the middle there is a hollow about the width of a hand where the slab has been sitting on air all summer without anything under it to stop it moving. The damp has come up into the sand and gone off it and left a grey skin on the top of the bed, and he breaks that skin with the bar before he puts anything back.

"Look at that." He puts his thumb in the hollow. "That is where it has been moving. It has been rocking in the same place all summer and it has made its own hole."

"I know it has."

"I am going to fill that hollow first and then I am going to bed it over the top of it." He looks up the path at the flags. "And I am going to bed it high at your door end."

She comes back along the path.

"You are going to bed it high at my door end."

"You want it high at your door end." He pushes the sand back in with his heel. "Water comes off the step. It always has. It goes along the path to the gate. If the slab lies flat the water sits on the low side of it and the low side is the middle and it goes soft under the middle and you are back where you started in a month. If you bed it high at your door it sheds to the rail and it dries out at the middle."

"There is nothing on that slab."

"Nothing is on it now." He rakes the sand with the bar. "Everything is on it the day it rains."

He beds it and it does go high at the door end. He brings the sand up against the outer edge in three goes, going over it with the bar and then over that with the back of it and then over that with the heel of his boot, so that the slab stands proud of the flags either side of it by the thickness of a thumbnail at the door and sits level with them at the rail. It makes a lip you can see in a light and a lip you can get your toe caught in, and the surface tilts the whole length of the path the wrong way for anything with wheels on it, and the water off the step will go down it in a straight line along the one place where the flags either side are a sixteenth lower than it is.

"There." He knocks the bar against the slab once. "That is bedded."

"That is bedded wrong."

"That is bedded for weather." He knocks it again. "Try it."

She puts her heel on the corner of the slab that used to go down. It does not go down.

She puts her heel on the middle. It does not go down.

She puts her heel on the corner at the rail end and puts her weight on it, and the slab does not go down either, but the other end lifts enough that her weight tips forward and she has to catch herself on the wall of her own gate post, which is the first time she has had to do that in her own front path in eleven years.

"There." He is picking his bags up. "That is not loose."

"That is not loose and it is tilted the wrong way and there is a lip on it that I will catch my toe on in the dark."

"There is a lip on it because you asked me for it to shed." The man picks up his bags. "Do you want it flat or do you want it dry. You cannot have the flat and the dry off one flag, and I have done the dry."

He goes up his own path and into his own door and shuts it.

---

She goes out into the dark with the torch off, because there is nothing to look at that a torch will help with, and she stands on the step and puts her palm flat on the middle of the slab and pushes down and lets go, and then she goes to the rail end and puts her palm flat on that and pushes down and lets go, and the slab is solid where it used to be a knock and it moves under her hand at the far end where there is not enough sand under it to make it solid at all.

She comes in and leaves the bar and the piece of firewood out by the gate where they are, and stands in the kitchen in the dark with her hand still feeling the shape of the far corner of it.
