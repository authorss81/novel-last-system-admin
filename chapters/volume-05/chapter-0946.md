# Chapter 0946 — Behind The Shelled Face

Sunday the thirteenth of October 2024, at the concrete step on the side path at number ten with the leaves going over it. Behind a shelled face.

**THE FACE OF THE RISER OF A CONCRETE STEP ON THE SIDE PATH AT NUMBER TEN HAS BLOWN AWAY IN ONE PIECE UNDER THE NOSING, WHICH LEAVES THAT NOSING STANDING OVER A VOID AS DEEP AS THE FACE WAS, AND THE CLOSED HAND GOES FLAT AGAINST WHAT IS LEFT AND RAPS IT ONCE ALONG ITS LENGTH WITH THE KNUCKLES, TO FIND OUT HOW MUCH IT GIVES AND HOW DEEP THE VOID GOES BEHIND IT.**

The step is one casting and not three, which is the ordinary way a step like that is made, and it was made in one afternoon by a man with a barrow and a plank and about eight bags of cement. The nosing is the top of it and overhangs the riser by about half an inch, and where the face of the riser has gone the overhang is all that is left standing over the front of the step.

The piece that came away is lying on the flags at the foot of the step. It is about the width of the step, a foot high and three-quarters of an inch thick at the middle and thinner at the two top corners, and nobody has moved it since the morning it fell, and the leaves have begun to come to rest on its upper edge where they lie across it.

The closed hand goes flat against what is left standing and rapped once from the low end to the high, the knuckles leading and the whole hand doing it in one go rather than in a row of taps. Along the middle of the step the knock comes back hollow, a short sound with nothing behind it, and at each end where the face is still standing the same knock comes back dead.

That is all the rapping is for, and it is enough. A knock along a concrete face is a way of asking where the concrete is and where the air is, and it is quicker and it tells more than looking, because the part that is still standing and the part that has gone are the same colour from the path and only one of them answers.

The hand goes up into the void under the nosing at the open end of it. The void is the thickness of the face that came off, which is about three-quarters of an inch, and it runs for two-thirds of the width of the step and stops where the face is still standing at the right-hand end and where it is still standing at the left.

The shell of a step is struck off with a float and not trowelled, and a float leaves the face with enough sand at the top to take a nail or a knock without chipping, and this face was struck off the same way as the two ends that are still standing. The whole of the difference between the middle and the ends was in what was in the barrow when the man got to it.

Behind the void the concrete of the step is dark and close and shows its stone. It is a hard, close concrete with a limestone chippings in it and no sand in the face, and where the void's back has been wet and dried more than once there is a bloom on it the colour of rust and about as thick as a coat of paint.

A concrete face goes when the concrete in it is not worth much, and the concrete in a face is always the last that goes in and the least that is measured. This step was poured with good stuff in the body of it, because the body of it is what carries a person, and the face was finished off with what was left in the barrow at the end of the afternoon, which was more sand than a face wants.

A face of sand and cement has very little stone in it, and stone is what stops water getting in and what stops a face from rubbing away. Sand and cement goes to a powder as it wears and lets the water behind it get at the body, and water that gets in and comes out again over a winter takes the face off in a piece rather than wearing it away gradually.

That is why it went in one piece and not by degrees. The face was already thin in substance when it was struck off, and it stood there through four wet winters doing nothing visible at all. What took it was a line of water getting in behind the nosing and washing the powder out of the back of it, and it came away in one piece along that line and not by degrees.

The nosing is not part of that. The nosing is the same concrete as the body and it is not brittle along its top, and the arris of it has been chipped a little by the boot heel of whoever comes down this path most, and it will go on being chipped. The edge where the face broke away is brittle from end to end, and a fingernail takes a grain off it, and the grains that come off are a pale grey and not the colour of the face, which went some years ago.

The broken line of that face was the arris of the riser, which is the arris every step on this path has, and it is the one line along which water gets into a step and the one line along which a step loses. The face broke along it because the arris was the thin place and the thin place is where the water went.

The piece on the flags does not give either. It is a slab of about three-quarters of an inch with a coarse face on one side and a struck face on the other, and it lies where it fell with its own weight and no more, and the ground under it is the flags and the flags have not moved.

---

The mortar under the step is old lime and about two inches thick, hard as a stone and pale as a bone, and it is brittle in a way the concrete above it is not, because lime has nothing in it at all. A bed like that is what a step is set on so that the step does not sit on the ground and crack in half in the first hard winter of it, and every step on this path was bedded the same way and most of them have not moved at all.

Her sister comes through the gap in the front hedge from the lane, with a branch pushed out of the way and nothing in either hand.

"There is less of that hedge than there was."

"There is more of it than there was in the spring."

"Then somebody has cut it back since the spring."

"They cut it back every year and it comes the same every year."

They stand on the path a while with the branch let back into the gap behind them. And neither of them looks at the step or at the piece lying at the foot of it, and the hand was down at her side before they came up off the lane.

A bus came through the lane at a quarter past nine this morning, which is earlier than the timetable and later than it was in the summer. And they have four different accounts of that between them, and none of the four is about the step.

The void under the nosing is as deep as it was and no deeper, and it runs the two-thirds of the step it ran yesterday, and the face at each end of it stands as it stood.

The piece on the flags is where it fell, and the bed of old lime under the whole step is hard and pale and brittle and does not move when a heel goes down on the edge of it.

Three-quarters of an inch of air sits under that nosing with the broken body of the step behind it, and the shell of the old face is lying on the flags below where it fell with a foot of leaves drifted across its top corner, in order that nothing about that step has been made different by anybody having been there.
