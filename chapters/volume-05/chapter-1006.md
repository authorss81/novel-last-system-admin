# Chapter 1006 — The Figure She Comes Up With Herself

Undated in this draft.

**THE HALL AT NUMBER TEN HAS ITS FRONT DOOR STANDING OPEN ONTO THE STEPS.**

The hall at number ten is four feet wide and runs the depth of the house with a stair going up off it on the left and a door into the front room on the right, and it has a row of four iron hooks screwed into the wall on the left at about the height of a man's shoulder, and on those hooks in the last week of the summer went the coats of two women who both go up the river and one that did not belong to either of them. The coats have been on those hooks since then and the summer is not finished with them yet, and the front door has been standing open on and off all day because that house gets no light anywhere else at this hour. The bulb out of the passage is gone. The corner of the passage at the foot of the stair is square and true and level, and it is the only corner on that ground floor anybody in that house has ever looked at.

She is standing in the hall when he comes in, and not in the front room, which is where she had told him she would be, and she has her sleeves turned back and her hands wet, which means she has just washed something. The sash is still out of its own frame and standing against the wall under the window with the long pale scratch in the architrave that she cannot see and that will not come off, and there is a cloth on the sill where she has been wiping the wet corner she has now finished with. She does not offer him the house and she does not stand aside out of his way. She comes about four feet down the hall so that he has to stop, which is a thing she has worked out and is using.

"I have got the figure out and I want you to hear it before you have looked at anything."

"All right."

"No, I do not mean all right, I mean I have worked at this for two days and I want it heard first, because you are going to come in there and put a number on it and I want mine heard before yours is." She had the cloth over the rail of the sash and she was wringing it out as she talked, and she stopped doing that when she wanted the sentence to land. "Sixty. For the sill, and the new piece, and the reveal cut back square at the top corner where the wood has gone, and the board across the opening, and two days, and me taking the money out of the tin on the Friday."

"Sixty is a good figure."

"It is a round figure and it is mine and I have never had a round figure in my life." She hung the cloth on the rail and folded her arms across herself. "And before you start, I have read your face and I know what you were going to do, which is do it and tell nobody, and then tell everybody that you did it. I have had that off two men in this street in the last six years and I am not having it off a third, so the money is going to happen and the money is going to be taken out in front of me, and if you tell me you cannot take it I will find somebody else, and the somebody else will take it, and the somebody else will not care what the reveal is."

---

He let that finish, because it wanted finishing and because he has learned in four days what happens when a woman on this frontage puts a sentence down and a man talks over it. What it finished was not him. It was the arrangement. She had described the arrangement he has run on for ten years, from the other side, in one sentence, without knowing she was describing it, and she had told him to his face that she would not have it, and he could not think of one honest reason why she should have it.

He went into the front room and put his thumb on the underside of the sill at the corner where it has gone soft and pushed up, and the wood gave about the thickness of a card, which is what it gave in the summer and is what it will give until it gives about the thickness of a pencil. He took the sash where it stands against the wall and moved the corner of the frame with his other hand and got a movement at the top corner of the reveal that he did not like and could not get again, so that the corner has to come back to a true line and be cut to it and not to the edge of what is left, and half a day more than anybody told her in the spring is what this job is going to take.

The hardwood for the new piece is the only other thing in it and he did that arithmetic standing up, with his hand still on the frame. He has four foot of it on a rack at the end of his own store at the unit, and it is stock, and stock belongs to the job it was bought for, and the job it was bought for is in another town and has been since the summer and will be wanted back. He could have four foot of it on his own bench by Thursday for nothing. He decided there and then, standing in a front room with his hand on a window frame, that he would buy his own and would not touch the stock, and that decision took him about as long as it takes to take your hand off a handle, and it was the whole of what he brought into that house that morning.

---

"Then get the sash out of my front room and put it in the kitchen where I can get round it." She was already going, and she stopped in the doorway with her hands on the frame. "The day your job wants finishing. You will want the window and the light and the room open. So name me a day and I will have my sister in when you do, because she will be back from the water by then, and I cannot see one reason on this earth why you should do it in a house with nobody in it."

The front room behind him has a fireplace in it and a chimney breast of old brick that has been papered over twice and a floor of bare boards that the woman of the house varnished herself in the spring and has been sorry for since. It has been shut since the summer. It is the only room on that frontage that could be let to anybody at all, and it has been shut for five months by a woman with her sister up a river and a fire that was never lit, and everything he knew about that room when he came in he knew from the other side of a window in the spring.

He could not answer her for about four seconds, and the four seconds were the whole of what he has learned since the summer. It was not that she was being kind. It was that she had taken the thing he wanted, which is to be asked, and had handed it back to him with a day and a witness fixed into it, so that the one job he is likely to be given on this frontage in the next six weeks is going to be done in front of the woman he wanted to ask, and he will not be able to work for that.

"Then it will be done with her in the room," and she went to open the kitchen door and did not look back to see how he took it. He knew the step outside that house had not been swept since the summer, and he was going to sweep it on his way in, and she would hear him doing it, and that was the whole of what he had to give her before the day.
