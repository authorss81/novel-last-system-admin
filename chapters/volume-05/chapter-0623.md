# Chapter 0623 — The Name at the Gate

Saturday the twentieth of February 2021, in this borough, from about ten past seven in the morning until a little after six in the evening.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE THIRTIES ON A PAVEMENT ACROSS FROM A SCHOOL TOLD HIM THAT A MAN HAS GIVEN A NAME AT THE OFFICE BEHIND THAT GATE AND GOT A DIFFERENT ONE WRITTEN DOWN, AND THERE IS NO NAME ON THE OUTSIDE OF THAT BUILDING, AND HE HAS WORKED OUT IN THE LAST HOUR THAT IT IS NOT ABOUT HIM.**

He is a man in his mid sixties. He is the caretaker at a primary school on a road of houses near the top of this borough, and he has done it for a working lifetime, and everybody who has a child at that school knows him and a good many people who have not got a child at that school know him too.

He does not lock up late. He is not a security man and he has never once asked anybody anything at the gate who did not have a reason to be there.

---

Here is the whole of it, and it took about a minute, and a woman in her late thirties told him standing on a pavement opposite with a smoke going.

He was in the front garden of one of the houses opposite with a bag of salt on the paving, because that path gets ice, and he does it when it has been cold, and it had not been, and he does it anyway while there is salt left in the bag.

And the woman said, "You'll be the man from the school."

And he said, "Depends who has come out of it."

And she said, "There's a fella been asking after you."

And he said, "Me."

And she said, "By name."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "He said a name, and I said it back at him to see if I'd got it, and he wrote it down, and it wasn't the same as what I'd said."

And she said, "I've been chewing on it since he was in front of me."

And he said, "Somebody's got it wrong."

And she said, "That's what I thought when he was in front of me. Then this morning I thought, no. Wait."

He has come away with very little, and every part of it is the right part, and that turns out to matter more than the amount.

The gate is the front gate of that school and the office is behind it, and on a Saturday the office is shut and the gate is shut and a caretaker does not go to the gate because somebody has said a thing to him.

He has been the caretaker since before the woman with the smoke was at that school.

He does not use his name at the gate. There is a bell with a plate on it and a letterbox and a sign about the key safe and there is no name anywhere on the outside of that building that a visitor can read.

He knows that. He has known it for decades. Every child at that school calls him by his name every morning of the week and the woman who runs the office knows it and the head knows it and the woman with the smoke said it out loud to him in a front garden across the road not an hour ago.

And somebody has come to that gate on two occasions and said a name out loud, and a woman on the same pavement heard it and thought about it for a week and then asked the one man she could think of.

He put the salt down and he did not pick it up again.

That is worth writing down because he did the whole of the rest of that pavement in his head while he was standing there, and he never once went back to the bag.

One: a man made a mistake. That is the likeliest thing by a long way. Somebody wants somebody who does not work there, or somebody who worked there a long time ago, or somebody with the same name as him in another part of the country.

Two: a man is after him who knows his name from somewhere. He cannot think of a list and he has looked for one. He is sixty-odd and has lived in three houses and worked in four places and the number of people who have got his name is small.

Three: it was the head. And he put this one away after about a second, because the head would not send anybody to a gate on a Saturday, and because the head rings him at home when he wants him.

And then, at about a quarter past eight, standing on a pavement with a bag of salt, he worked out the fourth one and it took the rest of the day off him.

Four: it is not about a man at all.

He worked the morning on that and he thinks it is right, and he has not told anybody that he thinks it.

Somebody has been to that gate looking for somebody who is not the man they asked for, and has been told he is not in, and has come back, and the woman with the smoke has heard the name and it has not been the name of anybody she has ever heard of in her life.

Which means either the man has a name that is wrong, or there is a woman on a pavement in this borough who has heard something that was said to somebody else, or somebody has said a name out loud at a school gate for a reason that has nothing to do with the man who owns that gate.

And every one of the three of them is about somebody else.

And he is standing on a pavement working it out and it is not about him.

---

He went and did the whole of the building.

That is a Saturday, and he did all of it thoroughly, and he has recognised since that doing it thoroughly was the thing he did instead of the other thing.

He did the hall, and the hall roof, and the outside steps at the back that hold water, and the two gates, and the guttering on the south side which has been wrong since the summer before last.

At about half past ten he went and stood at the front gate.

And he stood there and looked at it from inside, which is the side anybody looking in sees, and there is a bell and a plate and a letterbox and a sign and a key safe and there is not one word of his name on any of it.

And he stood there long enough for a woman with a dog to come past twice and look at him.

He had his lunch in the car and he got his phone out twice.

And in the middle of the day he found that he had got a man in his phone who has not been in his phone for a very long time, and his number is on there because somebody gave it to him years back, and he looked at the name of it for a while.

And he did not ring him.

And here is what he has worked out about that, and it is the only thing he got out of the whole day that he thinks is any use.

If he rings that man, then he is a man who has been told his name is being asked for at a gate, going round asking whether it was him, and if it was not him, then he will never mention it to anybody again, including the woman who told him.

And if it was him, then he has spent a working life not being found, and something has found him, and what he does next is a thing he cannot think about while there is still salt in a bag on a pavement opposite a school.

He put the phone back in his pocket and it stayed there.

At about half past three the woman with the smoke came past again with a different smoke and stopped.

And she said, "Have you thought of it?"

And he said, "I have."

And she said, "Well?"

And he said, "Have you said it to anybody else?"

And she said, "No."

And he said, "Do you mind if I ask you something and you think about it before you answer."

And she said, "Go on."

And he said, "Did he give his name or did you give it?"

And she said, "He gave it."

And he said, "And you said it back."

And she said, "I said it back."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "It'll be right. I would have said it again in the street. My mother made me take a parcel back once over a spelling."

And he said, "It might be right. It isn't me."

And she said, "How would you know?"

And he said, "Because if it were me I'd not be standing on a pavement with a bag of salt. I'd have walked up there and opened that gate and found out who was asking."

She went off at about ten to four and he did not say anything else to her and he has been glad about that and has not known why.

He put the salt away at about half past four because the rain had started properly, and it had not been going to rain when he did it, which is not a reason he has ever been able to give anybody.

And he drove home, and his wife was out until about six, and he made a cup of tea and did not sit down with it.

And he stood at his own front window and looked out at a road of houses and worked out that there are dozens of other men in this borough who could be reached at that gate by somebody who has got a name and a reason, and he has never met one of them, and one of them is at a desk and one of them drives and one of them is asleep in this borough at about ten past five on a Saturday afternoon without knowing that anybody has said his name out loud at a gate.

And he is going to be asleep in about an hour too, and he did not get his name said at the gate. Somebody got his name said.

**A WOMAN ON A PAVEMENT TOLD HIM A MAN HAS BEEN ASKING AFTER HIM BY NAME AT A GATE THAT CARRIES NO NAME ON IT, AND HE STOOD AT THAT GATE FROM THE INSIDE AND LOOKED AT EVERY PART OF IT, AND HE HAS WORKED OUT THAT IT IS ABOUT SOMEBODY ELSE, AND HE HAS NOT ASKED HER TO PASS IT ON AND HE HAS NOT ASKED ANYBODY AT THE SCHOOL ANYTHING.**
