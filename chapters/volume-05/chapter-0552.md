# Chapter 0552 — The First Price

The Thursday of the twelfth of March 2020, and the four days before it, in a small place off one street of this borough and in a house four miles away from it.

**HE HAS SOLD FOUR THINGS OUT OF THAT PLACE SINCE THE MARCH OF 2018 AND TOOK LESS THAN THE WRITTEN FIGURE THREE TIMES AND KNEW IT EVERY TIME, AND ON THE FOURTH HE TOOK WHAT HE TOOK OFF THE OTHER MAN ON THE FIRST ONE, AND HE HAS SAID NOTHING TO ANYBODY.**

The place at the end of the road is barely wide enough to turn round in. There is a name on the side of it that is not the name of what goes on inside, and it has been dealing in second-hand since 2016.

The man in his mid thirties runs it. He buys off houses and off clearances and off a man by the water who has more than he can hold, and he writes a figure on a strip and sticks it on the side of the thing, and then he waits.

The other one is a man in his early forties who has been keeping things since he was a boy.

His is the sort of house where a person cannot open a wardrobe door without finding something that was there in 1974. He does not need money out of any of it. He has never once tried to get a better figure out of anybody, and he has never once tried to get a better deal for himself either, which is not the same thing and is what has happened to him three times.

---

The first was a chest of hand tools in the March of 2018. He wrote one hundred and eighty pounds on the strip and stuck it on the side and the man in his early forties came in on the Thursday and read it and asked him to take a hundred and twenty.

He took a hundred and twenty.

The second was a set of trestles in the June of 2019. He wrote a hundred and ten and the man in his early forties said seventy-five, and he took seventy-five, and they said nothing else about it either.

The third was a set of cast weights in the November of 2019. He wrote eighty and the man in his early forties said fifty, and he took fifty.

There is no reason in that. He has never been able to make it into anything else and he has never once said any of it out loud. The other man has thought about that a good deal, on and off, for two years, and he has gone over all four of them in his own head more than once, and he could not find a pattern, and he has not looked for one.

He knows what the difference is. It is two hundred and fifteen pounds across the four of them, and he has that figure to hand and has had it since about the November, and nobody has ever given it to him and nobody has ever asked for it.

What he has worked out, and what has taken the two years, is that the other man is not being unfair. That is the whole of it, and it has taken him a long time to get to it and he has not got past it.

The strip is a number the other man puts on a thing because he wants the thing gone. It is not a price the other man has thought about. He has been taking what the strip says on everything else that has gone through that place, and the four of these are the only four where he has not, and he did it three times out of four and nobody knows he did it.

---

The bench came in on the Monday and it is a bench with a vice on it and it is the best of the four by a long way.

He priced it on the Thursday morning at two hundred and ten pounds and wrote that on a strip and stuck it on the side of the bench, and he went and had his tea.

The man in his early forties saw the strip at about two hours in.

He stood in that place with his hands in his pockets for a while. It is barely wide enough to turn round in and he did the turn round once and then stood still.

Then he went home, four miles off, and counted a hundred and twenty pounds out on the surface where he eats, and folded it once, and came back at about half past four.

The man in his mid thirties counted it and put it where the money goes and gave him nothing back, because there was nothing to give back.

"That's the first price," the man in his early forties said.

"What?"

"That's what I took off you on the first one. The chest of tools, the March of two years ago." He turned it round in his head for a second and did not say the year out loud, because the other man was there when it happened.

The other one did not say anything.

"You wrote two hundred and ten on that."

"I did."

"That was a good week's work in this house."

"That was a bench with a vice on it," the man in his mid thirties said. "You had it for a hundred and twenty."

"You've had a very good week out of me."

The man in his early forties nodded at that, because it was true, and he has thought about it since and it does not stop being true, and it is the sentence he wishes he had not said.

He stood there for about half a minute with the bench between them and the strip still on the side of the bench saying two hundred and ten pounds on it.

Then he said, "I'll have it down Sunday," and went out into the street.

The strip is still on the side of that bench and he will take it down on Sunday because he said he would, and not one word about the other three has passed between them.

**NOTHING OBLIGES THE OTHER ONE TO HAND BACK A HUNDRED AND TWENTY POUNDS, AND NOTHING OBLIGES HIM TO HAND BACK THE NINETY ON THE FOURTH ONE EITHER, AND HE HAS NOT, AND IT IS NOT IN ANYBODY'S MIND TO, AND IT NEVER WILL BE.**
