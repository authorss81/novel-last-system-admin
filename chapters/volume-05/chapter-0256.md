# Chapter 0256 — Nine Years

Wednesday the third of August 2016, from about half past two in the afternoon, a shop on a parade in Tideglass, and the room behind the trade counter at the back of it.

**A PRINTED FORM ARRIVED THAT WANTS A BODY TO SAY WHAT IT IS GOING TO DO WITH SOMETHING, AND THE ONLY PERSON WHO KNEW WHAT THE SOMETHING WAS HAS A LOCK ON IT AND A KEY IN HER HANDBAG, AND A GOOD REASON WAS PUT TO HER BY SOMEBODY WHO WAS NOT THE PERSON WITH A KEY.**

The woman of about fifty-five, who has the room at the end of the first-floor corridor in a building by the water, came in at about half past two with two things in her hand and a carrier bag on her arm, and she put both of them on the counter before she said anything, because she had them in that order in her head.

The first was a printed sheet with a firm's heading on it that said the first-floor corridor at that address would be stripped and made good between the tenth and the fourteenth of September, and that anything left on a sill, a rail, a shelf or the floor itself during those days would be cleared.

"That is a fortnight away," she said. "I've got a bag with two pieces of paper in it that came from over the water in somebody's wallet and I have had them in a handbag on that corridor since the nineteenth of July, and I have nowhere in that building to put them that isn't either the floor or a bin."

Hanna Wray looked at the sheet, and turned it round, and read the small print at the bottom, and put it back on the counter face up.

"The second," said the woman of about fifty-five, and squared the other thing with her finger.

It was a form on a single side, from the county office, with a reference number in the top corner and three boxes in the middle of it, and the boxes wanted to know what material was held at the address, when it was received, and whether the person answering intended to retain it beyond the works. Underneath the boxes it said a reply was requested by a date, and under the date it said that a reply should be provided by the person responsible for the material, or by the officer holding the record.

"I've had that since Friday," said the woman of about fifty-five. "I filled the first box in. I couldn't fill the other two and I have not sent it back, because if you send it back with a half answer on it they write to me again, and if you don't send it back they write to me again, and either way it is me."

"You've filled the first box in," said Hanna Wray.

"What's in the bag. Yes." She said it flatly, and put her hand flat on the counter, and it was not an ask and it was not a claim and it was the first time in this shop that anybody had put a hand flat on this counter about a piece of paper. "That's the only bit of it I know. And the other thing I know is that the paper of that one is in a drawer about six feet from where you're standing, and it's been in a drawer about nine years, and nobody in this borough has ever seen it, and I would like one piece of it in a place where a man with a stripper is not going to find it."

Nobody in the shop said anything for a few seconds. Out on the parade a lorry went past.

Then a young one came in the front for a builder's barrow and Hanna Wray had to go and serve him, and she went out from behind the counter, and that is the only reason any of the rest of it happened in the back room.

---

She came back with the change in her hand and put it in the tin and came through into the back room with the door over, and sat down on the edge of the desk.

"Right," she said. "No."

"That's all right," said the woman of about fifty-five. "I've had it in my head for two days and I got here to say it out loud and I've said it out loud, and if you want to know the shape of it: I've been the one putting my own money into that meter and my own time into that bag for nine years, and there isn't a version of that where a thing of mine goes in a bin in September because a man in a van has a schedule."

"Nine years I've had that drawer," said Hanna Wray. "And I want to say to you before I say anything else that I have never once given that key to anybody, not in nine years, not to anybody in this room in July, not to you, and I am not going to start on a Wednesday afternoon in August. That is not about the paper. If somebody wanted to look at it in March I'd have said no in March."

"I know you would."

"That's not a thing you can know. That's a thing you're saying because it's the answer you'd have given, which is exactly what I said in July and it's the reason I brought it in a handbag in July and I have brought it here today in a carrier bag." She put the roll of paper down and squared it. "So that's the no. That's the whole of the no and it took four seconds, and I'm not going to dress it up with anything after it."

"Then I'd like to ask you the other question," said the woman of about fifty-five, "and I'd like you to know that I'm asking it because I have been getting at it since Friday and I can't get round it any other way."

"Ask."

"Would you ever hand it to somebody. Not today. Not this year. Ever."

Hanna Wray did not answer for long enough that the woman of about fifty-five looked at the floor.

"No," she said. "And then when she looked up, she said: I don't know. That is the honest answer and I have never had to give it to anybody in nine years, because everybody who has ever wanted to look at that drawer has wanted to look at the paper, and you are the first person who has asked me about the key."

The woman of about fifty-five said, "Why is that a thing anybody would want to know?"

"Because it's the one thing I've got," said Hanna Wray, "and I've been carrying it in a handbag. It's not on the hook behind the till and it has never once been on the hook behind the till, because the alarm in this shop has been changed three times in nine years and every one of those three times I was the one who had to be here before the man came, and I'd have been out on a Friday if it had been on a hook. And that's it. That's what it's for."

The drawer under the trade counter was not opened. It has a lock on it and an invoice spike in it and two sheets on top of each other in it, and the sheets are a sheet of A4 with a line and eight places under it, and a sheet of A4 with the same line and this borough's own name under it, and neither of them has been out since the nineteenth of July.

Nobody in that back room was angry, and by the time the woman of about fifty-five had the form in her bag the two of them were talking about a bag of post-fix.

---

The form was still on the counter when she left, and she left it, and picked it up again about a minute later and took it with her in the carrier bag with the two pieces of A4 in it.

"You could answer the first box and leave the other two," said Hanna Wray from behind the counter.

"I could. And they'd ask again, and I'd ask again, and you'd never know any of it was happening, which is how the last year went." She stopped at the door. "There's a card on a wall in the hall on Carrow Lane with two sentences on it about a body that can't make anybody do anything, and I've read it, and it goes both ways, and the second half of that card is why I am taking that form home in a bag instead of leaving it in that building, and I'd like it noticed that I'm not asking you to be on the same side of that card as me. There's nothing on that form for you to be on a side of."

"That is the correct answer to that form," said Hanna Wray, "and I'm not going to give it to them either, and not for the reason on the card. Because I don't know what the correct answer is. That's the difference between the two of us and it took nine years to get it."

**AND THE ALARM BEAT A COUPLE OF TIMES WHILE SHE WAS IN AND SHE DID NOT GO UP, AND ON THE MONDAY THE FORM WENT BACK IN THE POST WITH ONE BOX FILLED IN AND TWO LEFT EMPTY, WHICH IS EXACTLY WHAT SHE SAID WOULD HAPPEN WHEN SHE ASKED.**
