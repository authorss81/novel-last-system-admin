# Chapter 0337 — A Clock Off a Bracket

Sunday the sixteenth of July 2017, from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon, a hall on Carrow Lane, a shop off a parade, and a hardware shop at the top of the Cleeve road, in Tideglass.

**A MAN IN HIS SIXTIES SAID IN A ROOM THAT A CLOCK HAS COME OFF THE BRACKET IT WAS ON AND HE DID NOT KNOW WHICH CLOCK, AND THE SENTENCE WENT OUT OF THAT ROOM TWICE BEFORE SOMEBODY PUT TWO QUESTIONS TO IT, AND ONE OF THE TWO QUESTIONS COULD NOT BE ANSWERED BY ANYBODY WHO WALKED IN THAT STREET THAT AFTERNOON AND WAS ASKED IT.**

The hall is on a meter and it is open on Tuesdays, on Thursdays and on Sundays, and the man who does that hall had the tables out by ten on the Sunday because there is something on that afternoon, and the window at the back of that hall looks over a piece of ground and the side of a building and no part of any street at all.

The man in his sixties was in at about ten with a cup and sat in the split seat. The man in his forties was in at ten with his bag and sat nearest the door, as is.

"I was up at the top of the Cleeve road on Friday," said the man in his sixties, "and there's a clock that's come off the bracket it was on. The one over the shop that's shut, the one with the man's name on the front of it. It's off."

"How do you know it's the one over the shop that's shut," said the man in his forties.

"Because there's one further up and that one's still up," said the man in his sixties. "I looked at both. I did not see anybody take it off and I was not there Friday morning, and I have no idea when it came off, and the man in that shop is not the man that clock was on. It went on a bracket in about the twenties and it has been over a shop that shut in March ever since and the shop before that."

"Did you ask him."

"He is not there," said the man in his sixties. "That is what I am telling you about. He is not there, and there is a man in that shop now and he has been in that shop since March and he could not tell you what was on that bracket when he took the shop, because nobody ever told him, because it was on the bracket before he came and nobody asked him whether he wanted to keep it."

The man in his forties did not write it down and did not say so and did not say anything else about it then.

The man who does that hall put the urn on at about twenty to noon and the man in his forties had a cup of it standing up, because he does that.

---

The man in his forties came into that shop at about half past one that afternoon and put a length of conduit down on the counter he had not asked for the price of, and he said it in about six sentences in his own words, which is what he does, and she stood there and let him get to the end of it and then asked him one question about the shop that is shut, and he told her the name of the shop and nothing else about it.

"That is the second time I have been told a thing like that this month," she said. "There was the firm that has gone over, and there was whatever it is about the miles of road, and now a clock. And I will tell you what it is, it is not noise, because noise is what people say when they have had a bad morning, and a clock is a thing a man can go and look at."

"I know," said the man in his forties.

"You do not want it on the end of the others."

"No," said the man in his forties. "And I am not saying it is not a thing. I am saying there are four things this room has been given and not one of them can be stood next to another one, and if this goes on the end of them then it is a fifth thing and I do not know what a fifth of them is, because I could not tell you in an afternoon what the other four come to and I am not going to stand a fifth one next to them and pretend that is a thing I have done."

"You have not done anything with any of them."

"I have not done anything with any of them," said the man in his forties, "and I have not done anything with this one either, and I did not write it down, and I would not write it down if it was the only one. That is not a rule. There is nothing to have a rule about."

She put her hands flat on the counter.

"You are not going to let anybody count them," she said.

"Somebody counted them once," said the man in his forties, "and got four, and none of the four has gone anywhere since and none of them has turned into a different kind of thing, and a number that does not move is not a number anybody in this borough can do anything with. It is in a room, in about four people's heads, and I have never once heard the other three out loud from anybody but her, and that is how four stays four."

She said that was fair and went back to the till.

---

The hardware shop at the top of the Cleeve road has a trade counter at the back of it and a woman on it two days a week, and the woman who works in a shop goes in there about once a fortnight for a bag of fixings, and she was in there at about half past three on that Sunday because the hall she has been using takes a bag of plugs and a couple of feet of cable and she wanted both and could get them cheaper in there.

The man who works nights was at that trade counter with his coat on and a length of conduit in his hand, which he has been waiting for, and he has been in that borough a long time and has never been in that hall on Carrow Lane in his life.

The woman who works in a shop told him the clock.

She told it to him in about four sentences and she watched him while she said it and he put the conduit down on the counter when she got to the bracket.

"Which clock was it," said the man who works nights.

"That is what he said. That is what the man in his sixties said in that hall this morning and I have just asked that man myself and he said it himself. A clock has come off a bracket at the top of that road. He does not know which one, and there is one further up that is still on its bracket, and the man in the shop that shut in March was not there when it came off and there is a man in that shop now and nobody told him what was over the door when he took it."

"Right," said the man who works nights. "Two questions and neither of them is the one I want to ask."

"Go on."

"Which one it was, first, and then whether it was keeping time when it came off." He put his hand flat on the conduit. "Because if it was right, then a thing that was right in this borough is on a lorry or in a skip or in a man's garage eight miles off, and that is a thing somebody could say to somebody in about three years. And if it was not right, then it was not right for a year or it was not right for twenty, and there is nothing in this town that ever said which."

The woman who works in a shop put her hands on the bag of plugs.

"They could have had it right," she said.

"They could have had it right," said the man who works nights, "or they could have had it wrong, and that is the whole of what I know, and I am not going to guess, and there is not a person in this borough I could ask, because the man that set that clock is dead and has been dead thirty years and the man that sold it is dead too and the man that took it off a bracket did it on his own on a day when he was not working, which is a thing you get when nobody owns a clock, and nobody owned that clock. That bracket is on a building and the building has never been anybody's job."

"What am I meant to tell him," said the woman who works in a shop, and she nodded at the man in his forties, who was at the front of that hardware shop buying a box of screws and had not heard one word of any of it and did not know she was in the building.

"Tell him nothing," said the man who works nights, "and go home."

She said that a man in his forties had not known where the shop that had shut was when she asked him, and he was gone by the time she got to the door, and there is nothing else that anybody in this borough could put next to a clock coming off a bracket.

**THE MAN WHO WORKS NIGHTS PUT A LENGTH OF CONDUIT UNDER HIS ARM AND WENT OUT THROUGH A DOOR HE HAD WALKED IN TWICE THIS YEAR, AND THE WOMAN WHO WORKS IN A SHOP STOOD AT A TRADE COUNTER FOR ANOTHER MINUTE WITH A BAG OF PLUGS AND A QUESTION SHE DID NOT PUT.**
