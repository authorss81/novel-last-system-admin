# Chapter 0657 — Three Doors Down And No Further

Thursday the twenty-ninth of July 2021, in this borough. She is in her own back bedroom from about ten in the morning and she is on the step at number twelve for about a minute of the afternoon, and the whole of it is a roll of brown tape in a coat pocket and two things she could have said and said one of them.

**A WOMAN WHO TAPED A SHEET ACROSS A WINDOW IN MAY AND WAS TOLD NO IN THREE SECONDS ABOUT A GATE WENT UP THREE DOORS ON A THURSDAY AFTERNOON AND KNOCKED FOR THE FIRST TIME SINCE SHE WAS NINETEEN, AND THE DOOR WENT IN ABOUT A SECOND, AND SHE STOOD ON THE STEP FOR ABOUT A MINUTE AND SAID THE ONE SENTENCE SHE HAD COME TO SAY, AND DID NOT GO IN, AND WAS NOT ASKED IN, AND WENT BACK DOWN THE ROAD WITH THE ROLL OF TAPE STILL IN HER POCKET BECAUSE IT HAD BEEN HANDED TO HER AT THE DOOR.**

She is a woman in her early thirties. There is a woman in her late seventies in the doorway with her. There is nobody else on that road that afternoon.

---

She is at number ten with her back bedroom half done and a roll of brown tape in the pocket of the coat on the back of the door, and the coat is on the back of the door because it is the twenty-ninth of July and it is the only coat she owns.

That is the whole reason. Her own back window is not taped and she has been going to do it since the Saturday, and she has had the roll in her pocket since the Saturday, and she has been putting that coat on and taking it off and standing in her own hall with a roll of tape in her pocket for three evenings.

And on the Thursday afternoon she puts it on and goes.

---

And here is what the tape is, and it is the only thing anybody acts on.

The woman in her late seventies put six things down in pencil on the back of a gas envelope on the third of June. The third one of them is *the window in the back room*.

And in May the woman in her early thirties went in there with a brown tape sheet and a blanket and did it, and she did it in about an hour, and she did it because the woman asked her to, and it is the only thing anybody has ever been asked to do in that house.

And it is now the end of July and the window in the back room is taped and the blanket is folded on the mattress, and the tape is a rectangle of brown on a sash window in a room with no fire in it, and the top corner of it has come away from the wood.

Not much. An inch and a half of it, curling, with the paste underneath gone grey, and it has been like that since about the middle of the week.

And it is the corner nearest the door, which means it is the corner that faces the room, which means it is the corner you see when you are standing in the middle of that room doing nothing at midnight.

---

And she can see it.

That is the thing about that house and it has been the thing about that house since May. Number twelve is on a slight rise and the front step of it is a half inch higher than the path, and from her own back bedroom window at number ten, which is at the back, she cannot see it at all.

But she can see it from her own front door. She can see the front step of number twelve from her own hall, because the road bends, and the back room of that house is on the far side, and what she can see from her own doorway is the side wall and the top of the blanket through the glass.

No. She cannot see the tape. She has not seen the tape.

She saw it in May. She put it there.

And she has been looking at a corner of a window in her own head ever since the middle of the week.

---

And here is what she is actually doing when she goes up that road at about half past two, and she is honest enough to know it in her own kitchen afterward and not honest enough to say it at the time.

She is not going up there about a corner of tape.

A corner of tape is a thing she could fix on a Saturday with a stool and a kettle. She could put it right in about four minutes and she would be in and out and nobody would be thanked and nobody would be asked anything.

She is going up there because she is the only person on that road who can knock.

---

Not the woman in her late forties. She cannot knock on that door, and everybody knows why she cannot, and nobody has ever asked her.

Not the man in his early forties. He knocks the way everybody knocks and the door opens for him and he goes in and comes out and nobody has ever asked him either.

Her sister three doors down has a stool she has never asked a question about and would not go up that road if somebody paid her.

And the woman in her early thirties taped a window in May and was told *no* in three seconds about a gate and did not ask why, and that is a standing, and there is no other standing on that road.

---

And so she knocks, at about half past two on a Thursday afternoon in July, three doors down.

Two knocks and a pause and one, and she has not done that on that door since she was nineteen.

And the door is off the latch, and it is off the latch before her knuckles are off the wood, and it comes away from the frame and swings about an inch and a half on a dry hinge.

---

And the woman in her late seventies is standing in the passage behind it.

That is the whole of the answer and it is the answer to the three seconds in May, and there is no other one. The door came in about a second because it was never shut. It has not been shut since before Christmas.

And the woman in her late seventies is standing in the passage with her hand off the door and she is not saying come in.

And she does not say it.

And she is not going to. Not because she is withholding it. Because it is not there. She has not got a way of saying come in to a person on her step who has not asked her for anything, and a person on a step who has come about a corner of tape has not asked her for anything.

---

And the woman in her early thirties does not cross the threshold.

And here is what that costs, because it is the whole of the afternoon.

There is an open door. That is the thing. It is about half past two on a bright Thursday and the whole front room of that house is straight ahead of her through about four feet of passage, and it is the front room, and she has been in it, and it is the only room in that house she has any business being in.

And she does not go in, and nobody stops her, and she is left standing on a step with a decision in front of her that nobody has made for her.

---

And there are about two seconds where she could have said the second thing.

That is what she came up the road for and she will not admit it to herself until about nine that night, and it is the chair.

She has been thinking about the chair since the Friday of the sixteenth of July. The three of them sat in that front room for about half an hour and did not talk about the gate, and in that room there is an ordinary wooden chair with a rush seat standing against the wall by the window, and it is the only chair in that house that anybody has ever sat in without a table in front of it. The green dining chair with the new leg on it is in the kitchen at the table under the window with a cushion on it, and it has been in that kitchen since the last day of May, and the woman in her late forties put it there herself.

So there is a chair in a front room and a green chair at a kitchen table in the same house, and the woman in her late seventies said out loud in front of two people that the front room was the only room anybody sits in.

And the sister heard it. That is the thing. The sister heard that sentence, and she was in the room, and she has been carrying it down the road ever since, and she has not worked out what it is about and she has not been able to put it down.

And she could say, standing on that step, *you've got a chair in there and you sit at the table.*

And it would be a question. It is the only question anybody has for that house since the spring.

And she does not say it, and it is not because the woman cannot be asked. It is because the woman cannot answer a question in a doorway, and because a woman of seventy-eight who has said a thing out loud once in front of two people does not want to say it again to somebody she has not seen since May.

---

And what she says instead is the sentence she came to say, and it is the whole of her, and she says it to the doorframe.

She says, "That tape's letting go."

---

And there are about a second and a half.

And the woman in her late seventies says, "Aye."

---

And the sister does not say anything else, and neither does the woman, and the two of them stand in a doorway and a passage on a Thursday afternoon and about a minute goes.

And here is what goes on in that minute in the passage, and neither of them is doing it at the other one.

The woman in her late seventies is looking at a person in her early thirties and working out how old she is and working out that she is the one who did the window and working out that she has come about the window and not about anything else, and she is pleased about that in a way she cannot put anywhere.

The woman in her early thirties is looking at a face and working out that there is nobody behind it who is going to ask her one question about anything, and that this is the only house on this road where that is true, and that she has been a person who asks for nothing in this house since she was nineteen.

---

And the sister takes the roll of tape out of the pocket of the coat.

And she puts it into the woman's hand.

And she says, "I've got about half of a roll left anyway."

---

And it is a lie about the roll and the woman in her late seventies does not know that it is a lie, because she has no way of knowing anything about the state of a roll of tape in a coat at number ten.

And the woman takes it, and does not say thank you, and the sister does not expect it, and both of those facts are the same fact and it is the first thing either of them has managed about a thing in that house.

And the woman in her late seventies goes down the passage towards the back room with the roll in her hand and a blanket folded on a mattress, and she is going to do a corner of a window about an inch and a half long.

---

And the sister stays on the step.

And the door is open behind her and she could go through it in one step and she does not, and nobody is watching her not do it, and the back room of that house is through the kitchen and the kitchen is straight ahead and the table is under the window with the cushion on the chair.

And she does not go in.

And then, from where she is standing on the step, she looks at the gate.

---

And here is what she can see of it, and it is not much, and it is what everybody sees.

A wooden gate on a pair of drops, painted many times, standing about two arms out from the front step, and it does not shut square. It stands about an inch and a half open at the hinge side and it has been standing like that for a long time, and the front step is stone and there is a lump of mortar out of the joint at the top of it the size of a thumbnail.

And she cannot see the back of the frame from there. Nobody can see the back of the frame from a step. The lump is on the inside of the gate where the drops go into the bottom, and it is the first thing on a piece of paper in pencil in a kitchen three doors away, and it is the only one anybody has asked for.

---

And the door goes.

Not shut. It was never shut. It goes about four inches further in on its own weight as the woman in her late seventies gets to the back room and puts her hand on it to get past a chair, and then it is at about the angle it always is, and the passage is dark, and the front room is dark, and the sister is on a step with a step that is half an inch higher than the path.

---

And she goes back down the road.

And she gets about halfway down it and stops in the road, in the sun, with nothing in her hands, and stands there for about half a minute, and what she is doing is going over the minute again.

She had about a minute with an open door onto the only room in that house she has any business in.

She used all of it on a corner of brown tape.

And the two other things she could have said are still in her, and they are hers, and she has given neither of them away, and she cannot give them now, because she has used the only thing she came up that road with, which was a knock nobody else on that road can make, and she has used it on glass.

---

And at about half past three she is back at number ten with her coat still on and her back bedroom still half done and the roll gone out of her hand.

And she stands in her own front room and looks out of her own front window at the front step of number twelve, which is a good way off and cannot be seen into, and there is a woman in her late seventies in a back room doing a corner of a window with somebody else's roll of tape.

And the front room of number ten is not the front room of number twelve and has never been in anybody's conversation.

**SHE HAD THE ONLY KNOCK ON THAT ROAD AND USED IT ON A CORNER OF GLASS, AND THE DOOR CAME IN ABOUT A SECOND BECAUSE IT IS NOT ON THE LATCH, AND SHE STOOD ON THE STEP FOR ABOUT A MINUTE WITH THE ONLY ROOM SHE HAS ANY BUSINESS IN FOUR FEET IN FRONT OF HER AND DID NOT GO INTO IT, AND SAID THE ONE SENTENCE SHE CAME TO SAY INSTEAD OF THE ONLY QUESTION ANYBODY HAS FOR THAT HOUSE, AND GAVE A ROLL OF TAPE AWAY AT THE DOOR AND WENT BACK DOWN THREE DOORS WITH NOTHING IN HER HANDS, AND THE GATE IS TWO ARMS FROM WHERE SHE WAS STANDING AND SHE COULD NOT SEE THE FAULT IN IT FROM THE STEP.**

(End of chapter 0657)
