# Chapter 0227 — Out

Saturday the twelfth of March 2016, from about half past seven in the morning, a shop on a parade in Tideglass, and a van on the tarmac behind it.

**A PERSON FROM THIS BOROUGH WENT OUT ON A GOOD JOB IN ANOTHER REGION BECAUSE THE JOB WAS GOOD AND THE PARTS WERE ON THE SHELF THAT AFTERNOON AND THE MONEY WAS RIGHT, AND NOBODY ASKED HER, AND SHE IS NOT ON ANYTHING, AND THE ONLY PERSON WHO WILL BE TOLD ABOUT IT IS TOLD BY SOMEBODY WHO HAS ALREADY DECIDED SHE IS A GOOD EXAMPLE.**

The shop is a merchant's and a tool hire counter under one roof, and it has an awning and sacks of aggregate stacked under it in winter, and it is the shop where Tomas Renn is a steward, which is a thing he has been for six years and does not mention.

Jonas Mercer was on the pavement at twenty to eight with a cup of coffee he had not wanted. He was not going. He had said so on the Friday to two people and neither of them had tried to change it, and Nell Ardent had said a sentence about that on the Thursday which he had not written down.

They loaded at ten past seven. Two men Jonas Mercer could not put an age to, in their thirties, one of them with a stubble and a bad wrist who could not carry and the other one who could. And a young woman of about twenty-three called Abby Marlow, who had done a course, and had been on about nine jobs, and had done four of them over there and would not say so if you did not ask her, and who was asked.

"There's a standpipe," she said. "There's a gully that silts up every time it rains and they've had it three times. There's a soakaway that's supposed to take it and takes about a third of it. Eight houses, no council, no landlord you can name, and a man up there who's been paying for it himself out of his own pocket and has run out of his own pocket."

"And the pay," said Jonas Mercer.

"The pay is the rate," she said. "And the rate's the same as everywhere over there, which sounds like nothing, and it isn't nothing, because everything's cheaper where they're hiring out a van and a plant and a skip. I did the sum. It's a good job." She shut the back door of the van. "And I said yes before anybody asked me. I want that on the thing, whatever the thing is. I said yes on the Saturday and nobody asked me on the Saturday and nobody's asked me since and I'm not going to pretend somebody did me a favour."

Tomas Renn came out with a bundle of trench tools under his arm and a key off his belt and did not say anything about the job, and put the tools in, and counted them, and said to the man with the bad wrist, "You don't lift the second board," and the man with the bad wrist said, "I know I don't lift the second board," and that was the entire handover.

**AND THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THE INSIDE OF THAT SHOP'S DOOR, printed on a card and stuck up at about the height of a person of average height at some point in about 2014 by somebody who has never been asked about it, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A CREW IS NOT A LIST.**
> **A LIST IS NOT A CREW.**
> **NEITHER OF THOSE IS A CREW.**

Jonas Mercer stood on the pavement and watched the van go out of the tarmac and turn left towards the bridge, and did not get in it, and had not been asked to and would not have been.

The thing that is true about that and that nobody in that shop put anywhere: there is not one line in this borough that says a crew went out. There is a book at the yard eight miles away with a row in it, and the row has a name in it in a column headed by nothing, and the name is Abby Marlow's, and this borough's shop has a delivery book with three lines in it that say what went out of the back and when, and the delivery book is about sacks.

He stood on the pavement for a while with the coffee going cold, and then went and knocked on the door of the building by the water and did not go in, and then went up the parade and had a second coffee he did want, and then went and wrote nothing down.

The man of about thirty-one who will be told about all of this is in a works office on the far side of the river four days a week and is not in this borough today and was not in that shop, and he does not know that a van went out of it at ten past seven, and Jonas Mercer has not rung him, and the reason he has not rung him is not that he is busy.

Abby Marlow said it herself, to a man on a pavement, at about a quarter to eight, and there was no third person within twenty feet of either of them, and she said it because she had been thinking about it since the Thursday.

"There's a lad in this borough," she said, "who went over there in the autumn and did a fortnight and came back hurt, and nothing came of it, and he's still here. And there's me going Friday, and there's about ten of us now, and there's no list of us, and nobody's asked, and I know that I'm not on it and I know that nobody's counting and I know that when I get back on Friday there'll be no piece of paper with my name on it anywhere in this borough at all."

"That is right," said Jonas Mercer.

"It's not a complaint."

"No," he said. "It isn't."

"It's the thing that stops me sleeping, though, is the other one. Because if you are not counted then nobody is going to help you, and that is not a complaint either, that is just — that is how it works. You are not on it, so you do not get looked after, and that is not a punishment, that's not anybody deciding anything about you. That's just nobody knowing." She shifted her bag onto her other shoulder. "And I like it here. I want that said. I like it here and I would not have had the money and I would not have had the van and I would not have learned off the man with the bad wrist, and if you tell me I've been done a favour by somebody I'll not have it. I'm not counting, so I'm not helped, and I'm not robbed either, and I don't know how both of those are true and I'm going to work it out in a van."

**AND A VAN WENT OUT OF A SHOP ON A PARADE AT TEN PAST SEVEN WITH THREE PEOPLE IN IT WHO HAD NOT BEEN ASKED, AND NOBODY PUT THEIR NAMES ANYWHERE IN THIS BOROUGH, AND THE ONE PERSON WHO WAS GOING TO BE TOLD ABOUT IT WALKED UP THE PARADE AT A QUARTER TO EIGHT AND STOPPED AT THE CORNER AND STOOD THERE UNTIL HE HAD DECIDED HOW HE WAS GOING TO PUT IT, AND HE PUT IT THE WAY A PERSON PUTS A THING HE HAS ALREADY DECIDED ABOUT, AND THE DECIDING HAD BEEN DONE ON THE PAVEMENT AND NOT ON THE CORNER.**
