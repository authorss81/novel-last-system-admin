# Chapter 0534 — An Ordinary Saturday in December

The Saturday of the seventh of December 2019, and most of the day, in four streets and a piece of ground between them in this borough.

**THE ICE IN THE DIP AT THE BOTTOM OF THE RAMP WAS RIGHT AGAIN ON THE MORNING OF THE SEVENTH OF DECEMBER, AND A MAN CAME OUT FOR FOUR MINUTES AND PUT GRIT IN IT, AND A WOMAN WENT ROUND THE WHOLE OF THE BLOCK TO GET TO THE OTHER END OF IT, AND ON THE FAR SIDE OF THE PIECE OF GROUND A WASHING WENT OUT BEFORE EIGHT AND CAME IN WETTER, AND DOWN BY THE WATER, IN A BUILDING NONE OF THEM GOES TO, A COLUMN IN A WORKING BOOK HAS A RULED LINE UNDER ITS LAST ENTRY AND NOTHING AT ALL UNDER THAT LINE, AND IT HAS BEEN LIKE THAT SINCE BEFORE ANYBODY IN THIS BOROUGH WAS ASLEW, AND NOBODY WENT IN, AND THE BOOK WAS NOT TOUCHED.**

There are nine flats on the landward side of the second of those four streets, and the way in to them is up a ramp of about thirty feet, and the ramp has a dip in it at the bottom about four feet wide where the ground has gone down over the years, and that dip holds ice.

For about nine years a man who lives at the far end has gone out every morning at about half past six and put a shovelful of grit into that dip, and on a frosty morning he has gone back twice. He is a man in his early seventies, everybody in the building knows him for it, and not one of them has ever mentioned it, and he has never mentioned it to any of them, and he is not the sort of man who would.

He has been away since the middle of November. He goes to his daughter's at the end of every year, he went on the seventeenth, and he is not back until the second week of January.

On Friday the sixth there was ice in that dip from about eight in the evening until about ten on the Saturday morning. Then rain came for about an hour and took most of it away, and then it went cold again and made a crust instead, which is worse than what it was, and the whole borough was in and out of shops and nobody was looking down at a dip in a ramp.

At about ten past ten a woman of about eighty-one went up the ramp with a frame. She has used one since about 2017. She got to the dip, stopped, stood about ten seconds, and came back down the whole ramp and out along the front of the flats and away across the piece of ground and round, and got herself to the shop end by the only other way out of that side, and it added about six minutes to going to the shops.

She has been doing that a dozen times or more since the middle of November. She has not said to anybody that she has had to do it, and there is nobody she would say it to, because the man who used to do the dip is a man she nods at on a good day and does not manage on the rest.

At about half past ten, by which time she was round the far side of the building and had been for ten minutes, a man who lives in the third flat came out of the building with a shovel of his own and did the dip.

It took him about four minutes. He did the whole width of it and he did it twice over, and he put the grit at the edges and not in the middle, which is not what anybody else in this borough would have done and is what the man who used to do it has done every morning for nine years. He did not know he was doing it that way. He went back in and shut it and did not look up the ramp, because he was not going to stand there and see whether anybody was coming.

A little before midday she went up the ramp again, on purpose that time, to see. The dip was grit, and she went over it and across and round and in. He was in, and she did not knock, and not one of the two of them has any idea that the other went up that ramp inside about an hour of the first.

---

The second small wrong thing is on the other side of the piece of ground and has nothing whatever to do with any of that.

A woman in her forties, in the second of those four streets, put her washing out a bit before eight in the morning, the way she does. At about half past eight it came on and went through in about six minutes, and a little before midday she brought it in and it was wetter than when it went out.

She has not said anything to anybody about it. She has not put it out again in a different place. She has not bought anything, and she did not take the covers in, and when her husband came in at about half past twelve she said she had got it in early and that was the whole of it.

What she has done with it since is that she has gone through the lot of it. Every piece separately, and she has put them away the way she would have put them away if they had come in dry, in that sequence, and she has not looked at any of them twice, and the whole of it took her about as long as making something hot and drinking it, and she did it in the kitchen with the door shut.

---

And that is that. Neither of those two things is about the other one, and neither of them is about anything anybody has ever written down.

Down by the water, in a building none of the four streets goes to, a working book about two inches deep has a column in it, and the column's last entry was made in the middle of September 2015 and there is a ruled line under it for a person to write in, and the space under the line has been empty for one thousand eight hundred and thirty-seven days, and what is written in that column has not been counted for anybody. Nobody went in this week. Nobody counted. The book was not touched and was not opened and nobody in the four streets knows it is there.

There are four accounts of one thing going round this borough that have been going round for longer than anybody in it has been counting, and none of them fits inside any of the others, and nothing new has come along, and no one alive in this borough has said which of those four is theirs, and nothing anybody in these four streets has said this morning puts any two of them side by side.

And the one thing this borough cannot say out loud has not shifted since the middle of August two years ago. The man who was going out to look for it is not going, and has not said so to anybody, and there is not a person left in these four streets who has any reason to go and look.

**THE MAN WHO CAME OUT FOR FOUR MINUTES AT HALF PAST TEN PUT THE GRIT AT THE EDGES AND NOT IN THE MIDDLE, WHICH IS HOW THE OTHER MAN HAS DONE IT EVERY MORNING FOR NINE YEARS, AND HE WENT BACK IN WITHOUT LOOKING UP THE RAMP, AND HE WILL NOT MENTION IT TO ANYBODY, AND THE WOMAN WHO WENT ROUND THE WHOLE OF THE BLOCK TO GET TO THE OTHER END OF IT WENT BACK UP THE RAMP A LITTLE BEFORE MIDDAY AND FOUND THAT IT WAS DONE AND SAID NOTHING.**
