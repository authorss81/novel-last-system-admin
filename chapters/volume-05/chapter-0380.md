# Chapter 0380 — Twice in One Day

Wednesday the third of January 2018, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past eight in the evening, a street at the bottom of the town, and a shop off a parade, in Tideglass.

**TWO SMALL THINGS WENT WRONG FOR ONE MAN ON ONE DAY, AN HOUR APART, AND HE PUT BOTH OF THEM RIGHT HIMSELF AND TOLD NEITHER OF THEM TO ANYBODY. THE FIRST WAS AT ABOUT TEN PAST NINE IN THE MORNING AT THE BOTTOM OF A STREET AND TOOK HIM A MINUTE AND A BIT. THE SECOND WAS AT ABOUT A QUARTER PAST TEN IN A SHOP OFF A PARADE AND TOOK HIM AS LONG AS IT TOOK. A MAN IN HIS THIRTIES STOOD THERE FOR THE WHOLE OF THE FIRST AND STOOD AT THE END OF THE COUNTER FOR THE WHOLE OF THE SECOND, AND HE TOLD NEITHER OF THEM ANYTHING, ANYWHERE, ON THAT DAY OR SINCE.**

The street at the bottom of the town is short and it goes down to where the bus stop is and it has terraces on both sides of it and no pavement on the low side for about thirty yard, so the low side is just a kerb and a gutter and people walk on the high side.

The man in his forties parks his van at the top of it, nosed in, on a Thursday most weeks and on this Wednesday because the job on the low side was a half day and the second one was after it.

It was about two degrees below freezing on that Wednesday and it had been below freezing since about nine the night before, and there was ice in the shade of the high side of that street and none at all in the middle of it.

He was in that van at about ten past nine in the morning with the engine running and the heater on and the bulkhead shut, and the van did not go.

---

It turned over. That is not nothing: a diesel that has been standing since half past seven that morning will turn over, and this one turned over, and it made the noise a diesel makes, and it did not catch.

He let it come back on to itself. He waited about two seconds and turned it again. It did not catch.

He turned it four more times over the next half minute, and the fourth one he held on to, and on the fifth one he had the throttle down about a quarter and it caught and came up to a fast idle and sat there shaking.

That is about a minute and a bit from the first turn to the sound of it running.

There is no fault with a van in that. It is cold, it had been standing, and the fuel is there and the battery is there, and the whole thing is the sort of thing that happens on a cold morning in a town like this perhaps twice a fortnight across everybody who drives in it.

He put the gear in, let the clutch out, and drove off up that street at about a quarter past nine.

The man in his thirties was at the kerb.

He had come out of a house at the end of that street at about ten past nine with a bin under his arm, and he had put the bin out on the kerb, which is the side with no pavement, and had not come back in yet, and he was standing there with his hands in his pockets.

He was about thirty foot from the front of that van.

He watched the whole of it. He stood at that kerb and watched a man turn over a van five times and watch the man put the gear in and drive off, and he did not move, and he did not say anything, and he did not go and offer to help, and he did not go into the house and tell anybody, and he did not look away either.

He has not mentioned it. Nobody put it to him then and nobody has raised it with him since, and he is not a man who goes round a town saying that a van wouldn't start outside the bottom of a street, and if it were said to him on the Sunday he would say that vans don't start in that weather and that is all that is.

He is not a person who has seen a man be helped and has said nothing about it, and nothing was helped, and a man got a van going by himself on the fifth try in about a minute and a bit.

---

The second thing was at about a quarter past ten, over that counter with the machine on it.

He had a list. It was a bit of paper in his coat pocket that he had written at half past six that morning with four things on it, and he came into that shop for the first of them, which was a box of the smaller washers, and then for the second, which was a roll of something, and then he went up the counter and put his card on the small machine by the till.

The machine did not take it.

It did the thing it does when a card is not read, which is two short buzzes and a line of about six words across the top of a little screen about eight inch by two, and it did not beep for approval and it did not print anything.

He turned the card over and put it on again and it did the same two buzzes and the same line of words.

He waited about ten seconds and put it on a third time with the card the other way up, and got the same thing, and then he took the card off it and put it in his pocket and took his money out and counted it onto the counter and the woman who works in a shop took it and gave him his change and put the thing in a bag and gave him the bag.

That took him as long as the van had taken. It took about a minute and a bit, and he was not in a hurry, and the whole of the business was four or five times as long as it is when the machine works.

The man in his thirties was at the end of that counter with a box of something under his arm and had been there when the card went on for the first time.

He was about eight foot from the machine. He was facing it. He saw the card go on and he saw the two buzzes and he saw the screen, and he saw the card go on again and he saw the two buzzes and the screen, and he saw him take the card off and count money onto the counter, and he stood there through all of it and he did not say a word.

The woman behind that counter said nothing either. She rang the money through, she gave him the change, she gave him the bag, she said the washers were on the second shelf down if he wanted to look at them, and she did not ask him what the machine had done and she did not say it was the same as yesterday or the day before.

Nobody in that shop said anything about the van at the bottom of the street, and nobody in that street said anything about the machine in that shop, and the man in his forties did not mention the machine when he left and did not mention the van when he had been in that street an hour before.


He went up the parade and did the rest of the list and finished about four and spent the last two hours of the afternoon in a yard off a side turning a mile and a half from that shop, and he came home at about half past seven.

He has not thought about either of them since, or he has thought about them separately, which is a different thing. The van was a cold morning. The machine was a machine. They were an hour and a bit apart and a mile and a half apart and there was a whole morning in between them, and if anybody in that town had told him on the Friday that the two had anything to do with one another he would say that things break and that it is not a thing anybody arranges.

The man in his thirties has not said a word to either of them, or about either of them, or about the two of them together, and he is not going to, and there was nothing in his head at any point in that day that put the van and the card machine near each other.

**THE MAN IN HIS THIRTIES STOOD THIRTY FOOT FROM A VAN THAT WOULD NOT START AT TEN PAST NINE AND STOOD EIGHT FOOT FROM A CARD MACHINE THAT WOULD NOT TAKE A CARD AT A QUARTER PAST TEN, AND HE TOLD THE MAN WITH THE VAN NOTHING AND HE TOLD THE WOMAN AT THE TILL NOTHING, AND HE HAS TOLD NOBODY ANYTHING ANYWHERE, AND THE MAN WITH THE VAN PUT BOTH OF THEM RIGHT AND TOLD NEITHER OF THEM TO ANYBODY EITHER, AND NEITHER OF THE THREE OF THEM WILL EVER MENTION THE OTHER TWO.**
