# Chapter 0617 — The Shop He Does Not Use

On Saturday the twenty-third of January 2021, in this borough from before eight until a little after six.

**A WOMAN HE HAS SPOKEN TO ONCE BEHIND A TILL IN A SHOP HE DOES NOT USE TOLD HIM THAT HIS WIFE HAD BEEN IN THERE FOR A BULB, AND HE KNOWS SHE HAS NOT, AND HE HAS SPENT THE DAY AT THE BACK OF THAT, AND HE HAS NOT ASKED HIS WIFE.**

He is a man in his late fifties, and has been on the books of one firm on the industrial estate since he was twenty-nine. That firm opens at seven, and he is married and his wife is at home and they have been in the same house a long time.

He went into that shop because the shop he goes to on a Saturday is shut, and has been shut since before the summer, and he has not got round to finding out why.

---

Here is what was said, and he has got it right because he went over it in the car afterwards.

He was in a shop on the parade for a very short time. He wanted a pack of fuses and he went in the one that is open because the other one is not, and there was one woman on the till and she was about to close the whole thing down for the weekend, which he could tell.

And he put the fuses on the till and she said the price of them and he gave her the money.

And she said, "That's the last of them."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "She came in on the Thursday for a bulb."

And he said, "Sorry."

And she said, "Your wife."

And he said, "My wife."

And she said, "She came in on the Thursday. Stood about where you're standing."

And he said, "My wife's never been in here."

And she said, "Hasn't she."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Well, I've served her."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "Twenty-eight watt, I think it was, and a long one."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "You tell her the long ones go at the side, not in the middle. People put them in the middle and then the switch doesn't do anything."

And he said, "I'll tell her."

And she said, "Right," and he went out and shut the door and stood on the pavement.

Now. Take that apart, because he has taken it apart, and it is where the day went.

It is not the bulb. He does not care about the bulb and she will have done about ninety shops on a Saturday.

It is this: that woman had five things about him at once and she had all of them before she ever said any of them, and there is no way on earth she could have had any of them.

She knew he had a wife.

She knew which one, or she had the wrong one and did not think about it, which is worse.

She knew the street, because she said the Thursday and stood where he was standing.

And she knew enough about his kitchen to tell him which way up a bulb goes.

None of that is a thing you can take off a person at a till. It comes from having been in a house.

He stood on that pavement for a good while and then he got in the car and drove to the industrial estate, because it was a Saturday and there was nothing to do at the house.

That is the first thing about the day and it is the thing he has not forgiven himself for.

He had somewhere else he could have gone. He has a brother-in-law in this borough who would have had him on a Saturday, and a mate at the depot who is at a football thing most Saturdays, and there was a woman he used to see who would have been free after one. He got in the car and drove to work, and he stood about in a car park for most of the middle of the day, and that is where he was when his wife rang him at about two.

And she said, "You all right, you said you were going out."

And he said, "I've been out."

And she said, "Where."

And he said, "Nowhere."

And she said, "Nowhere where."

And he said, "The shops."

And she said, "Right."

And she said nothing else and neither did he and he could hear her deciding not to, and that is the part he has gone over most.

Here is what he worked out, in order, and he is reasonably sure of the first three.

One: she knows a couple of faces in this town and a couple of faces know her. That happens in a place this size and it is not nothing and it is not enough.

Two: he has got the wrong wife. That is possible and he can think of two women who would have been in that shop, and one of them has told a story like that before.

Three: she has mixed him up with somebody on a Saturday who looks like him and buys the same thing.

The first three he can live with. He worked out in the car park at about half past twelve that he can live with every one of them.

The fourth one is the one he cannot put down and it arrived on its own at about ten past two while he was sitting in the car.

Four: she has not mixed him up with anybody.

And what she has done is put a woman in a shop who has not been there, and the woman she put there is his wife, and the shop is not one his wife goes to, and the bulb is the right bulb.

There is nothing wrong with any part of it except that it is not true. He could not begin to say what is wrong with it.

He did three things between about two and about five.

He drove past the shop once, and it was shut, and there was a shutter and a light off in the window.

He went back into the shop on the parade next door, which does groceries, and the woman on the till there is a different woman, and he asked her, in the way you ask a question when you have already decided you will not like the answer, whether she knew the woman who was on at the hardware shop at the weekend.

And she said, "Which one."

And he said, "The one on the till."

And she said, "I couldn't tell you. There's three of them."

And he said, "Right."

And he bought a newspaper and went out.

And at about half past three he sat in the car at the bottom of his own road with the engine off and tried to work out whether there was any version of this that did not end with him going home and opening his mouth.

There is not.

---

Here is the version he worked out, and it took him most of the rest of the day, and it is why he has not asked.

He and his wife have been in the same house for a long time. He knows exactly what it sounds like on the other side of a kitchen when a man comes in and says a woman in a shop told him she'd been standing where he was standing.

It sounds like a man in a shop.

And a woman in her mid fifties who says twenty-eight watt and long one and points at the side of a shelf is not doing that. He knows she is not doing that. He has been in that shop once and she was closing up.

But the whole of the thing is built so that he has to be the sort of man who says it, and he is not going to be that man to find out whether his wife was in a hardware shop on a Thursday, and that is what the day has actually been.

He could have asked at half past two. He has worked out that he could have asked at half past two and he has not been able to say why he did not and he is not going to pretend to himself that it was to protect her.

He came through his own front door at about ten past six.

There was a plate out and the heating on and the news on at a volume she would normally have turned up for him and she turned up for him.

And he said, "Do you want a cup of tea."

And she said, "Go on."

He made it and set it down and stood at the far side of the table, and she looked at him over it for a second, and then went back to the news.

And he said, "Have you got a bulb gone?"

And she said, "There's two in the drawer."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "What for?"

And he said, "Nothing."

And she said, "You've never once asked me about a bulb."

And he said, "Have I not."

And she said, "Not once. Not in all the time we have been in this house."

And he said, "Well. There's a thing in the front room."

And she said, "There's a thing in the front room that needs a bulb."

And he said, "It does."

And she said, "Then get one at the weekend."

And he said, "I will."

And she said, "Shop's shut."

And he said, "Then Monday."

And she said, "Right," and turned the news up, and that is how it finished, and he did not ask her, and she did not say where she had been on Thursday, and she did not know that there was anything to say.

**A WOMAN BEHIND A TILL TOLD HIM HIS WIFE HAD BEEN IN THAT SHOP FOR A BULB, AND HE HAS SPENT THE DAY AT THE BACK OF IT AND HAS COME UP WITH FOUR REASONS AND CAN LIVE WITH THREE OF THEM, AND HE COULD HAVE ASKED HIS WIFE AT HALF PAST TWO AND DID NOT, AND HE CANNOT SAY WHY AND WILL NOT SAY IT WAS TO PROTECT HER.**
