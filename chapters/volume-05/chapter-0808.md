# Chapter 0808 — The Dip By The Rear Step

Monday the seventeenth of April 2023, in this borough. She is in her own bay and out in her own yard on that Monday, and a dip in the floor by the rear step that has kept water in it since the autumn is the whole of it.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FORTIES HAS A DIP IN THE FLOOR OF HER OWN BAY BY THE REAR STEP THAT HAS BEEN HOLDING WATER SINCE THE AUTUMN AND MEANS TO TAKE THE FLAGS OVER IT UP, PUT HARDCORE DOWN AND LEVEL IT; THE WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK OPENS THE FAR END OF THE SAME DIP ON THE SAME DAY AND DOES THE SAME JOB THERE, AND WHERE THE TWO AREAS MEET THE JOINT BETWEEN THEM IS THE TRUEST PIECE OF FLOOR IN THAT BAY, AND NEITHER OF THEM EVER FINDS OUT.**

That lock-up, on that Monday. She is in her late forties. The bay is about thirty feet deep and the whole of its floor is engineering flags laid dry on a bed of sand inside a timber frame, so that any part of it can come up and go back the same way it was.

The dip is along the back of that bay, at the foot of the rear step and running away from it toward the far corner, about fifteen feet of it, and about two feet across.

---

It has been there since the autumn and it has kept water in it from the first hard rain after, every time, because that is what a dip in a floor is for.

The water went down out of it in March when the ground went down. What she has now is a hollow in the ground under a floor that is itself straight, and a straightedge laid across the frame at the back of that bay rocks over the middle nine feet of it, and the frame has begun to take a set because everything resting on it is resting on ground that has moved about an inch under one end of it.

The ply on the floor by the office end is over the older of the two hollows and has been there since the winter. She does not go near it. The four pallets are on their bearers against the outside wall and the kerbstone and its two flat bars are where they were.

---

The way a floor like that is made is not a mistake anybody made. It is a floor laid in a workshop in nineteen-something by men who were building for the job they had in front of them and put a beam under a steel column and did not think about forty years, and the floor was a floor and it did what a floor does, and it sat on ground that had not finished going down because there was a house on one side of it and nothing on the other.

That is the whole of the history of that hollow. Nobody did it.

---

The bay floor is one frame and it is in bays, and the frame is a thing you level once and then work to all day.

The frame is sawn softwood six inches deep set in the ground all round the bay, in sections, with the sections butted to each other and the ends of the sections spiked down, and the flags sit on sand inside it. You level a frame from the ends, because the middle of a thirty-foot bay has nothing fixed in it to measure from, and the two ends have the end walls behind them, and that is the whole of the theory.

The two fixed points in that bay are the wall at the roller-door end and the wall at the far end. Everything else in it moves a little in a hard winter.

Anybody laying flags in that bay lays them off one end or the other and gets the same answer to within the thickness of a thumbnail, because the frame was set in one week and by one gang, and neither of them has been out since.

---

The woman who books work comes up the ramp and under the shutter about the middle of the morning.

The shutter goes up over her without a sound and stays up all day. She has the bar and a short plank and a shovel and a bucket and she goes to the far end of that dip, about nine feet in from the back corner, and starts on the flags there because the far end of a dip is where the water leaves a floor.

---

Nobody knows the other one is there.

That woman does not know it and does not think about it. She goes at the floor with the same method she goes at everything, and the method is right.

The flags come up one at a time off the frame, on edge, and go against the wall. The sand comes out with the back of the shovel. Then the ground itself comes out, and it is not hard, and she takes out about a barrow and a half of it before she reaches stuff that will stand a boot.

She puts hardcore in and tampes it with the end of the plank in three goes, and does not ram the last one, because the whole fault in that floor was ramming the last one. Then sand over the top and the frame, and a straightedge long enough to reach from the back wall past the edge of her own work, worked up and down along the line until there is nothing under it anywhere.

She puts her flags back on the frame on the sand, tight joints, and does not hammer them. A flag on a bed of sand wants to be walked on to bring it down, and she walks on them.

She works from the far wall down. That is what she does, everywhere, and she does not know it is a choice anybody makes.

---

The woman of the house is in her own office at the near end of that bay all the morning with the door shut and the book in front of her, because there is a month of a book to bring up to date and it has to be done off the drums, and the shutter is up the whole time and the office window looks out along the length of the bay.

She can see the far end of that bay from where she sits.

She sees a person at the far end of the floor with the flags up and a barrow and a shovel, the way she has seen a person at the far end of that floor every day for years without turning round to look at any of it.

She goes back to the book. She does not turn round again until about the middle of the afternoon, and by then the person has gone and the shutter is down and the nine feet at the far end of that bay is finished and the flags are down on it.

---

She puts the shutter up again and goes to the dip.

She stands at the back of that bay with her hands behind her for a minute before she touches anything, and what she is looking at is the far end of that hollow, which is finished, and the near end of it, which is not.

She does not think anything about it except that it is done and it is good, and that the bed under it is hardcore and not rammed solid, which is the difference between a floor and a raft.

That is the whole of what there is to be thought about a floor somebody else has done in the same building on the same day, and she thinks it, and she goes to work.

---

Her end is about six feet of it, from the foot of the rear step back to where the other work begins.

The flags come up. The sand comes out. The ground comes out, and it is the same soft stuff, and it comes out wet at the bottom because the frame is what kept the water above it and the ground under it has been in the same place since the autumn.

She puts hardcore in in three goes and tamps each one and does not ram the last. She puts sand over it and brings the frame up to the level she has on the two ends of it that have never moved, which are the end wall and the frame at the point where the other end of the dip begins.

That is the point that matters, and she does not know that is what it is. She puts her straightedge down across it and works the middle of the dip up to the straightedge and the straightedge down across the place where her ground ends and somebody else's ground begins, and she takes the ground at that place down a little because the straightedge says it is a little high, and she takes her own sand down with it and fills the low place in her own side.

The two lengths of hardcore meet in the middle of that hollow with nothing between them, tamped at the same angle from both sides, and the sand over the top is one continuous bed and the frame is one continuous frame and the flags go back on it from her side up to his without a joint that either of them made.

---

She works until the light goes off the far wall.

The flags are down all the way along the back of that bay. She walks it with a straightedge and a spirit level and moves them along the line the length of the fifteen feet, and there is no daylight anywhere under them and the level is the same from the wall to the back corner.

Where the dip used to be there is a place where the floor takes a new line, about two thirds of the way along from the rear step, and that is where a fault always sits and it is a fault neither of them put there and neither of them has any way of knowing about.

It is there because that is where the ground gave, and the ground gave there on its own, and the two of them put four inches of hardcore into the whole length of it without either of them looking over the fence at the other, and the last thing a bay floor does is keep the shape of the ground it is lying on.

---

She has not been in that bay in that corner for about a year, and the reason she comes at it now is that a hollow that has stopped keeping water in is a hollow she can finally get a bar into, and a hollow you can get a bar into is a hollow you can put something back into, and she has been looking at a place in her own floor where the water used to stand since the autumn and wondering what was under it since March.

She puts the bar and the shovel and the plank back against the wall where they lean and the shutter goes down on its rail without a sound, and the four pallets are still on their bearers with the blocks off the ground and off the render, and the ply by the office door is exactly where it was, and the kerbstone is under its two flat bars with its rubber folded on top of it.
