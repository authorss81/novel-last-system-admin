# Chapter 0619 — The One Who Has Taken It On

Wednesday the third of February 2021, in this borough. She is in that kitchen from about ten past seven until about half past four.

**A WOMAN IN HER EARLY THIRTIES SHE HAS WORKED BESIDE SINCE BEFORE THE SUMMER TOLD HER AT NINE O'CLOCK IN A KITCHEN FULL OF NOISE THAT SHE WAS THE ONE WHO HAD TAKEN IT ON, AND SHE HAS SPENT THE DAY GOING THROUGH EVERY SINGLE THING SHE HAS TAKEN ON, AND SHE HAS NOT GOT IT, AND SHE HAS NOT ASKED.**

She is in her early forties, and one of two dinner supervisors at a primary school at the top of this borough, and has done it since before the summer, and the other one is a woman in her early thirties who started at the same time and who is better at it.

That last part is not a complaint. She says the other one is better at it the way you would say a person has a good car.

---

Here is what was said.

They were in the kitchen at about nine with the servery hatch up and two hundred-odd children through the middle of it, and the other one was at the far end with the tray rack.

And she said, "You're the one that's taken it on, then."

And she said, "Taken what on."

And the other one said, "It."

And she said, "Which it."

And the other one said, "Oh, don't," and laughed, and put a tray down.

And she said, "No, go on."

And the other one said, "You've not heard."

And she said, "I haven't."

And the other one said, "Then that's what it is."

And she said, "That is not an explanation."

And the other one said, "You'll hear," and went back to the trays.

And that was it. That is the entire event and it took about half a minute.

What she has spent the day on since is finding the thing.

She is good at this. That is worth saying plainly because the whole of the day is carried by it and because everybody in that kitchen would say the same about her. She is good at keeping a building in her head while she is cooking it. She can hold the dinners, the sheets that come in, the two dietary lists, the child who is not to have anything green, the child who is to be left, the fact that the freezer is on the wrong side and the outside door sticks.

So she sat down at about half past nine in the office with a pen and did the only thing she could think of, which was to write down every single thing she has taken on.

Not for the job. Not for this year.

Everything.

Here is the list and it is the whole of the morning and it is longer than it looks.

One: this job, and it was hers before it was anybody else's.

Two: the fact that she is the one who rings the kitchen about the amounts, which nobody decided, and which everybody now assumes.

Three: the freezer, and the outside door, and the fact that she has told three people and been thanked for it by none of them.

Four: a girl in the year below who will not eat in front of anybody and who comes and sits with her.

Five: a boy who has been off for a long time and who everybody at the front office asks her about because she is the one who will say the true thing.

Six: the folder of menus from a couple of years back, which is in a cupboard under the stairs and which she took out one night and looked at and put back.

Seven: the fact that the other woman, the one in her early thirties, has been doing the early shift and she has been doing the late one, and that they have never once discussed that, and that it happened.

Eight: the woman who was here before the other one, who left in the summer, and who took with her the right to be asked things.

And that is eight, and there are not any more of them as far as she can tell at about ten o'clock, and she has been over it more than once.

Here is what she has worked out, which is that none of the eight is it.

It is not the job, because the other woman knows perfectly well she has the job, and a woman who works beside somebody every day does not need to tell them they have it.

It is not the ringing, because the other woman rings it with her and always has.

It is not the freezer, because the other woman has already thanked her for the freezer, in a corridor, with her hands full.

It is not the girl, because the other woman has the other end of the room and the whole of the school knows the girl goes to her.

And it is not the boy, and it is not the folder, and it is not the shifts, and it was not the woman who left.

And she has been sitting in that office with the pen since about ten o'clock and she has got as far as knowing that she would take the whole of it over again if somebody came in and made her.

Here is the shape of it and she has not told anybody the shape of it.

There are only two ways for a sentence like that to be said.

Either it is about her.

Or it is about somebody who used to be there.

And if it is about her, then she has taken something on that she did not know she was taking on, and she will not find out what it is from the room, because that is not how a thing like that works.

And if it is about the woman who left in the summer, then she is going to spend the rest of her working life being a person somebody else says a thing about in a kitchen full of noise.

She got through the middle of the day.

Four hundred and something covers, and the one with the mash on it that always gets put back, and a boy who told her the whole of what his brother did at the weekend while she was scraping a tray.

And at about half past twelve, in the queue, the woman in her early thirties was behind her and said, "You never asked me what I meant."

And she said, "I know."

And the woman said, "You were going to."

And she said, "I was going to at the hatch."

And the woman said, "You never do."

And she said, "I know."

And the woman said, "It's not a row."

And she said, "I know it's not."

And the woman said, "Well."

And she said, "Is it a thing I'd have said yes to."

And the woman said, "Would you have?"

And she said, "Yes."

And the woman said, "Then it's not a row," and got her tray and went out.

Here is what happened at about half past three, because it is the part she will keep.

The kitchen was empty and she was at the hatch and the other one was on the phone at the wall.

And she stood in the doorway with her cloth in her hand while the boy with the trolley came past and went on.

That is the whole of it, and she has been back over that doorway more than over anything else in the day.

She knew, in that doorway, that she had about a minute before the phone went down, and she worked out in that minute that she had it, and she did not use it, and she went out and did the outside door, which sticks, and which she has told three people about.

And here is the reason and it is not a good one and she has looked for a better one all the way home.

If she asks, then it is hers. Whatever it turns out to be, it will be a thing she was told about in a room full of noise and had to think about privately for the whole of the rest of the day.

If she does not ask, then it is still about somebody.

She would rather it was still about somebody.

And that is not noble and it is not even clever and it is the reason she did not use the minute, and she got the outside door open with a shoulder and propped it with a brick and it will stick again by the end of the week.

She went home at about half past four and she has not asked and she is not going to ask, and she has one more thing on the list that came to her on the way and that she has not got rid of.

On the Monday she had done something nobody asked her to do. She had gone down to the shop at the end and bought a box of the biscuits that are the wrong ones and had put them in the servery herself and had told nobody.

It is not it. She knows it is not it. It is a box of biscuits and there is no sentence anybody could say about her in a kitchen that a box of biscuits would answer.

And she has been over it in the car and she cannot put it out of the list either, because she has nothing to put it out with, and because the only thing that sorts a thing like that is standing in a doorway and using the minute.

**SHE WROTE DOWN EIGHT THINGS SHE HAS TAKEN ON AND FOUND A NINTH ON THE WAY HOME THAT SHE CANNOT GET RID OF, AND NONE OF THE NINE IS IT, AND SHE STOOD IN A DOORWAY AT HALF PAST THREE WITH THE PHONE STILL ON THE WALL AND DID NOT USE THE MINUTE SHE KNEW SHE HAD, AND SHE WOULD RATHER IT WAS ABOUT SOMEBODY, AND THE ONLY THING THAT WOULD SORT ANY OF IT IS A QUESTION SHE IS NOT GOING TO ASK.**
