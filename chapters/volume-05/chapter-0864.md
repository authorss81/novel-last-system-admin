# Chapter 0864 — The Mushroom Off The Turf Stack

Monday the twenty-seventh of November 2023, at the turf stack at number nine from the time the frost has gone off the top of it until the street has gone quiet. A mushroom, and the hole it came out of.

**SHE MEANS TO TAKE A SMALL MUSHROOM OUT OF THE SIDE OF THE TURF STACK AT NUMBER NINE IN THE HAND AND PUT IT DOWN ON THE GRASS, AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING COMES IN AT THE FRONT DOOR OF NUMBER NINE AND GOES STRAIGHT OUT AT THE BACK.**

The stack is twelve feet of turf against the front wall, grass side down, the way it was cut off the bed in the autumn and the way it is going to go back into the bed in March.

It is a stack of slabs. Each piece is about two inches thick and about the width of a spade, and they were cut in squares and laid down grass to grass so that the grass underneath was not against the weather. The soil in a turf is not one thing. There is a dark band about half an inch deep under the grass, and then a paler band of the subsoil under that, and the dark band is full of the grain of the old bed and the pale one has almost none of it, and every face on that stack shows both bands the same way round. The top of the stack is the face of the soil. The two long sides are the spade cuts, and a spade cut is a face of soil about two inches deep with the fringe of roots hanging out of it along the bottom edge where the roots used to be.

It was the roots and the tiles and the daisies that came up in the first week of October that went in the bottom of the stack, and there has been a heat on since, and the heat under a stack of turf with a dead bed under it makes things grow in it.

There are three mushrooms on that stack. She has known about them since Saturday.

She is not going to eat one and neither is anybody else. That is not why she is there and it would be a poor reason in a yard in November.

She is there because of the face.

The side of that stack is the face that goes into the bed. When that stack is laid in March the spade cuts go in against the old bed, face to face, and it is those faces knitting together root into root that makes a new bed last four summers. Anything standing on one of those faces is standing on the only part of the turf that has work to do.

A mushroom on that face has taken a piece of the face to stand on.

She gets the first one in the middle of the day, when the sun has come round far enough to put the front wall in light, and she does it in one movement and without any kind of force.

Two fingers, thumb and first finger, on the stem about an inch down from the cap, and a pinch, and a straight pull away from the stack along the line of the stem and not sideways.

That is the whole of the technique and it is not a thing anybody tells you. A mushroom has no root in the sense a plant has a root. What it has is a foot, and the foot is not hooked into the soil, it is simply grown into it and about it, and a mushroom comes out of the ground the way a cork comes out of a bottle, in one movement, with nothing left behind but a hole. Turn it and you leave the stem in the soil and the hole you have made is twice the hole you needed.

It comes away whole. It is smaller than a thimble and the cap is about the size of a farthing and the stem is pale and the top of it is slightly darker, and there is a rim of wet soil round the base where it has been standing in a place that has not been dry since the beginning of the month.

And there is a hole in the face of the turf about the size of a thumbnail.

She puts it down on the grass and she does not throw it.

The grass along that wall is the dead grass off the front bed and it has been lying over since August and under it the ground is the ground the turf came off, and it is the same ground. She puts it down on the grass and the grass takes it, and the grass does not want it, and it will be there in the morning and gone by the middle of the week and none of that is the point. A thing off the face of a stack belongs on the ground the stack came off, and there is nowhere else in that yard that is.

The point is the hole.

She goes and looks at it with her face about eight inches off the face of the stack, the way you look at a thing in the last of the light.

It is a clean hole and it is not round. It is the shape of the foot, which is narrower than the cap, and it goes in about half an inch, and the inside of it shows soil and a few pale root ends and nothing else. It is a hole in the face of a piece of turf about the size of a thumbnail and it is the only damage that face has got after eight weeks of being stacked.

She goes along the rest of that side and looks at the other pieces in the same light, because a hole is worth looking at next to the faces that have not got one. Eleven of the twelve pieces on that side have nothing on them at all. The twelfth has two mushrooms on it, three feet apart, both of them about the size of a pea, and both of them growing out of the joint between two pieces of turf rather than out of the face of one of them.

That is where they belong and that is why she leaves them.

A mushroom in a joint has come up through the gap between two pieces and is fastened to both at once, and it can only take what is in that gap. A mushroom on a face has one piece to take from and it takes from the middle of it, and the middle of a face is the part with no root ends in it, because the roots are all along the bottom edge where the spade did not go near them.

So the three inches under the top of that stack is the best ground in it for a thing that wants ground, and the two on the joint are standing in the joint, and the one she took off had been standing on a face and had made its own.

A hole like that is nothing. A hole like that is not going to get bigger in eight weeks of rain. And in March, when that stack goes into the bed, it goes in with it.

The woman who does her shopping comes in by the front door in the afternoon and goes straight through the passage and out at the back door without stopping, and she does not look at the stack on her way through and she does not go near it when she is out at the back, and the back door shuts behind her.

There are two more mushrooms on the stack and she leaves them both where they are.

They are on the same face, three feet further along, and they are two days older than the first one was, and there is no weather coming that will take them off that face and nothing about them that wants doing. She has seen what a mushroom on that face looks like and now there are three of them and none of them is going to be eaten, and the one that had to come off has come off.

Where that mushroom came off there is a hole in the side of the turf stack about the size of a thumbnail, and it is not going to close over the winter, and in March that turf goes into the bed with the hole in the face of it, and the grass comes over the hole in the first summer and the hole is still under the grass in the second.
