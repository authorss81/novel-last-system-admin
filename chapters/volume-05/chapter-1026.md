# Chapter 1026 — A Bed Short For A Man In It

Undated in this draft.

**THERE IS ONE WINDOW IN THAT ROOM AT TEN AND A CHAIR STANDING OFF THE WALL.**

The room at the front of the top floor of that house has one window in it looking over the front of the street, and no fireplace at all, and a bed pushed up against the wall the window is in, and a chair standing away from the wall by the window with its back to the glass. The boards are the landing boards with the carpet off them since before the summer and the nails of the carpet are still in two places along the skirting where the tacks went in. She has slept in that room since she was a girl and she is on that book, and she has been on it every season since before she could reach the handle of a tap, which is a thing she has told nobody in that house and has told him once.

The bed itself is a foot and a half short of what a man lying on it would want, and the pillow is at the end nearest the window because the other end is where the pipes come up through the floor.

He came up that stair at the middle of the day with the second half of a thing in his mouth and she had got to the window before he came to the house and was standing there with both hands behind her.

"You have come for the answer."

"I have."

"Then you can have it standing up, because I am not giving it to a man standing in the middle of my own room who has not stood in it since the spring."

He walked his hand along the head of that bed and back again without touching anything on it, because he had come into that room to ask a woman for something and had no business making a second set of marks in a house that is not his. She went along his hand with her eyes and did not comment on it, and he understood that she knew exactly what a hand doing that is for, because she has been in rooms with men her whole life.

"You will ask me one thing," she told him, "and then you can go downstairs and not come up about this again this week."

"What thing."

"Who is paying you."

---

It is three words into her first answer to him and it is the whole of the trouble, and it took him a while to get to the bottom of it because he had come up that stair with the answer worked out in his own head and it was the wrong answer.

"It is a woman."

"I did not ask whether it is a woman. On this frontage it is a woman and one of the three of us and everybody knows which."

"Then that is right."

"Then which one."

He stood by the door of that room with his hands where she could see them and did not answer it, and he went over in his own head the four goings he had made up that frontage in a fortnight and worked out that every one of them had ended in a room he could not get out of without somebody in it having decided something about him.

"I am not going to tell you."

"You are standing in my own room in my mother's house and I am on that book and I am the one who would have to stand in a kitchen in this borough for a fortnight if I agree to this, and you are not going to tell me whose money it is."

"No."

"Then I will not come."

"Then I will go and find somebody who does not ask."

"Nobody on that frontage would take that without asking. And you know it as well as I do. You have come up here and asked me because I am the only one of us who is able to turn you down, and I am turning you down now, and you can go down that stair and think of something else."

She meant every word of it, and he stood where she had put him and understood that she was right about the whole of it and had been right about it before she opened her mouth.

---

What brought her round was not the money and it was not him.

It was that he told her what the work was, and he told her the hardest part of it first, which is that the range does not come off a wall with one man in a house. You take the legs off a range and you want a man under it while you do, and the flue comes off the chimney breast and has to hang somewhere else, and the range goes down clear of the brick standing on its own feet on a tray, and then the front of that slab gets cut back until the wash will run off it instead of standing in the corner. Four streets away there is a yard where two men go in on a Tuesday and a Thursday and come out again, and they are the only two he knows of, and that yard has no money in it to pay them and he is not going to ask them.

It was that he told her the work. He stood by that door and he told her about the range coming off the wall on its own legs onto a tray, and about the flue coming off the chimney breast and being carried up and hung somewhere else, and about the edge of the slab coming back to a true line so that the wash runs off the wall instead of standing in the corner of it. He told her that the wall cannot be mended and can only be freed, and that the freeing wants a space behind the fire and two summers of air at it, and that the woman who owns that kitchen is going to cook on that fire every day for the rest of her life and has a right to know all of that before anybody puts a spade in a floor.

"How long is a fortnight of that."

"It is a fortnight for two men and it is more than a fortnight for one, and I am one, and the whole of what I have got wrong about it is that."

"And what will there be in the room while you do it."

"The range off the wall and a tray under it and a gap you could put your arm into behind the fire, and my coat on the hook, and me."

She looked at the bed for a while.

"My sister has not asked you for anything since the spring and she has had two men in this house in her life and neither of them would take a shilling off her and she has never stopped being sick of the pair of them. I have been in that room myself and I have seen what it does to a woman to be in a room where a man is being paid and not know it. That is what I am refusing. Not you. The not knowing."

"Then I have told you."

"You have told me what the work is and I did not ask for that and now I have it. Come and get me when you are actually going to start, and not the morning before, and not with a tray in the yard. I will stand in that kitchen and I will not touch anything and I will go home again, and you will not pay me and I will not let you."

"That is the second thing this week somebody has agreed to stand in a room for me for nothing, and I did not ask for either of them."

"I know you did not. That is the whole reason I have agreed to it."

He came away down that stair with the answer he came for and with something else he had not come for, which was that he had now been handed a fortnight by a woman who has been on that book longer than he has been alive, and she had asked him nothing at the end of it, and he had nothing to give her in return, and he had spent ten years being a man who gave things for nothing and had no idea at all what it was to be on the other side of it.

What she has done in that room is agree to a fortnight for a man who would not put a name to his payer, and he left her standing away from her own window in a room too narrow for the two of them.
