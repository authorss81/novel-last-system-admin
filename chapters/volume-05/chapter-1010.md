# Chapter 1010 — The Same Thing In An Empty Covered Way

Undated in this draft.

**THE COVERED WAY AT THE UNIT HAS A GUTTER DOWN THE MIDDLE OF ITS FLOOR.**

The covered way at the unit is the roofed passage between the two buildings and it runs the whole depth of the yard from the street end to the far gate, and it is about five feet wide from wall to wall where the plaster has come off and rather less where it has not. The floor of it is brick laid flat with a concrete channel down the middle of it that has been cracked across in two places and patched with nothing at all, and the water coming off the roof goes down that channel and out through a hole in the end wall and does what it likes on the yard afterwards. There is a row of iron hooks set into the wall on the north side at a height a man can reach without taking his hat off, and there has been a row of hooks along a wall like that in a yard like that since before anybody working here was born, because there is nowhere else in a builder's yard to put a coat and a builder will put a coat anywhere rather than carry it about. Along the south side there is a bench made of two scaffold boards on two courses of brick, about nine feet long, and it has been in this covered way longer than the man who put it there. It was set square to the wall behind it once and the ground has moved under it since, and nothing on this ground is true to anything now, and not the channel, and not the kerb he has four feet out of.

He came in out of the rain with his coat over his arm and stood in the middle of that way where the water goes past his boots, and put his hand on the bench, and did not sit down on it, and then stood there for the better part of an hour while the yard emptied out above him and the channel ran. Two men went past the mouth of it and into the store without looking in. Somebody put the store door to on its latch. The light in a covered way like that is the same the whole time the weather is on it, and a man can stand in one for an hour and not be able to name one thing that has happened in it except that he is standing in it. The light in the covered way is the light that comes off the wet brick of the opposite wall, so that everything in it is the colour of a wet brick and nothing in it is any colour at all.

What he worked out in there was not a decision. Decisions come later and they are easier. What he worked out was the shape of the last three weeks, and the shape was this. In four days at that yard he had been given a page with his own name at the end of it, a figure that a woman came up with herself, an ask for a wall that he never got to, a name struck through in pencil in a scullery, a woman's request to be taken off a book, and a price for that request that came out of her own six weeks up the water.

Six things. Not one of them was asked of him by anybody who could have refused him afterwards. Three came from women who have never been asked for a thing in their lives. One came from a woman who keeps a book, who has refused him four times since the summer and would have refused him a fifth.

The four refusals came back to him one after another while he stood there, and they were the same four and they were the same woman and none of them had been angry. One was a work he had brought to that office in the spring and wanted entered. One was a work he wanted entered after it was done. One was a request for a second column, and the answer to that was that there had never been a second column. The fourth was him standing in the middle bay with his own work behind him in a bag, asking to be entered at all, and the woman who keeps the book putting the reason out in front of him in three sentences about a crew that came back up the water at the end of six weeks and were not on it again. He had not been angry with her once in four goings, and she had not been unkind once, and the total of those eight conversations was nothing at all, and there is not a clerk in this borough who could put a figure on what that has cost him.

There is a person on this frontage he has not tried yet and the arithmetic about that person took him about two minutes standing in the rain. The sister at number ten has been up the water six weeks and is due back in days. She has been entered on that book longer than he has been alive. She has never in her life been asked for one thing, and she is coming home to a house where her sister has already spent six weeks doing the asking for both of them, and she will walk in and find the front room altered and a man at work in it who was not there when she left.

"Right," he put out loud to nobody in an empty covered way, and heard how it sounded standing in the rain, and did not take it back. "It is the same thing. It is the exact same thing I stood in a coal shed and put into words about a different woman a month ago, and I have come up this frontage four times since and told myself I had learned something."

Then he gave the other half of it aloud, because a man alone in a covered way who has done the first half and stopped there has not been honest with himself, and the other half was that he had learned nothing at all. A woman at number nine asked him this week for the one thing in this borough he cannot give anybody, and she paid for the asking with her own work, and the answer to it came back to him as a door he could go through. Nothing stopped him. There had been a gap in that frontage where everybody who could refuse him was not standing, and he walked up it, and he had a name ready by the time he got to the middle of it. He has thought since about the one thing that ought to have stopped him and there was only one, and nobody could have put it to him in those words, because the woman who keeps the book had already put it to him in the summer in a different set of words, and what he has done since is not fail to hear her. It is hear her, and go round the thing she put to him, and take the going round as though it were a different decision.

That is the part he will carry longest, and he knows it while it is still cheap. A man who cannot be asked by anybody with a right is not a man nobody wants. He is a man who has arranged, over ten years and without ever once deciding to, be a man nobody with a right needs. And the price of a line in a book is not a refusal. The price is somebody you cannot refuse.

He put his hand back on the bench, and the boards were wet through and had no more give in them than they had when he first touched them, and the channel at his feet went on down the middle of that way and out through the hole in the end wall into a yard he has left open on its south side. He can say the sentence and mean the sentence, and what it will take to make it true is to be in a room with somebody in it when he opens his mouth.
