# Chapter 0962 — Inside The Folded Lead

Tuesday the tenth of December 2024, at the flashing where the lean-to meets the back wall with the roof dark over the yard. Inside a folded lead.

**WHERE THE FLASHING AT THE JUNCTION OF THE LEAN-TO AND THE WALL WAS DRESSED IT WAS DOUBLED BACK ON ITSELF, WHICH LEFT A VOID INSIDE IT, AND ONE FINGER GOES IN AND IS TURNED BACK AT THE KNUCKLE UNTIL THE BEND OF IT ARRIVES AGAINST THE FAR SIDE, TO FIND OUT HOW THICK THAT DOUBLING IS AND WHAT IS BEHIND THE LEAD AGAINST THE WALL.**

The lean-to at number twelve is four feet of roof over the back door and the flashing at the junction of it and the back wall is lead, dressed over the sarking of the lean-to and up the brick of the house and out onto the wall a good inch.

Where it turns the corner onto the face of the wall it is not taken straight round. It is stopped about four inches short of the arris and brought round the last of that in two goes, and each go is a fold, and the two folds are a quarter of an inch apart and they are not joined.

A fold is where the lead doubles back on itself so that a length of it can turn a corner without being cut. The lead goes out along the sarking, comes back on itself, goes up the brick, comes back again, and then goes out again across the face of the brickwork, and the two short doubled lengths of it are what get round that corner. A fold of lead is neither a crease nor a bend. It is two lengths of the same sheet lying against one another with the air left in between, and that air is what turns a corner without the lead being cut.

Each of those doubles has a hollow inside it and the hollow is about the thickness of the lead twice over, and nobody has ever put anything into either of them.

---

The first finger goes into the hollow inside that fold, and then it is bent back at its own joint until the crook of it comes against the far side of the fold.

A finger pushed in straight has the width of a nail at the tip and finds the near side of a hollow and stops there, and the crook of a bent finger is about three quarters of an inch across the inside, and it is the crook that comes against the far side and not the tip. The crook comes against lead. Each of the two faces is about as thick as a sheet of card, and between them is nothing at all, so what the crook is against is the back of one leaf of lead and the front of the other one at once, with the thickness of the pair between the pads of it.

Which is the thickness. Lead that is dressed and folded is doubled, and a doubled lead is not thin however hard it is worked, and a man who cannot get at the inside of a fold has no other way of knowing what is in it than putting one finger in and bending it back. Behind the lead, on the far side of the fold, is the brick of the corner of the wall, and the lime between those bricks is gone hard and pale and stands about a sixteenth of an inch back from the arris where it has drawn itself away from it.

The masonry stop is a lead strip let into a raked joint about two inches above the fold, and it is stiff enough to have stayed in that joint for eighty years, and from the yard it is the sole thing on that flashing anybody can see, and it is a strip and nothing more and it goes nowhere at all.

Above the fold the lead goes up the brick of the corner for about a foot and then stops, and it stops because that is where the render begins, and the render was there before the flashing was and it is behind it now. The wall it is up against is solid and it is old and any cavity in it stopped at the plastering decades ago, which is why the folding was done against the brickwork and not against an open stud as it would be on a timber frame.

The lead at that corner has been lifted off and put back once, and the tell is a line in the patina about four inches long where the upper length was raised to get at the joint below it and then laid back over it. Below the fold the lead lies out across the brickwork over a course that has a crack in it, and the crack follows the joint for about a foot and does not go round the corner.

Lead is also the one flashing material that can be worked at a corner in a wet week with nobody on a scaffold, because a length of it is light enough for one person to keep up in the air while the other works at it, and the reason every flashing on every house of that age is lead and none of them is anything else lies in that. The lead is stiff for its weight and soft in every other direction. It is heavy enough that a man cannot carry a length of it out for long in one arm and it is soft enough to be dressed with a mallet and a grozing, which is what anybody is paid for when a corner is dressed.

Above that junction the sarking of the lean-to carries two or three inches of silt in the angle where it meets the wall, which is where water goes when it comes off that roof and finds a corner, and it is the reason the lead at that junction has been sitting on a wet angle for about eighty years. The drip at the bottom of the flashing is cut into the brick below it and not into the lead, which is the whole difference between a flashing that works and one that does not, and the drip on that house was cut by whoever built the lean-to and has not been filled in by anybody since.

A masonry stop is let into a joint cut to the back of the strip, and the strip itself is bedded in nothing whatever, and a strip let in badly comes out in about forty years. That one has been in for eighty. Where the roof above that junction stands, the sarking and the battens behind the lead are the same battens as the rest of that lean-to, and one of the heads along the lowest course is still standing off its batten exactly as it was, and nobody has been up there since the spring.

The lime between the bricks of that corner has drawn itself away from the arris along the whole of the height of it, which is not decay and is what lime does as it goes off, and it is the reason a length of lead dressed into a corner like that can still be lifted out of the joint it was let into.

There is a bird's nest of grass and hair in the crook of the brick two courses above that fold, built in the angle where the sarking meets the wall and packed hard, and it is old and it is not in the fold and it is not in the hollow.

---

Her sister is at the mouth of the back alley with the alley behind her and the yard in front of her, and she does not come into the yard.

She stands there a while, and what goes between them is the postman, and whether a Saturday call is made now, and whether one was made on the Saturday before last, and what happens to anything that arrives on a Friday. All of it is about a postman, and the finger was out of the fold and down before she was at the mouth of the alley. The fold at that junction is doubled lead a quarter of an inch apart at the inside and nothing whatever between the two leaves, and the masonry stop above it is a strip let into a raked joint and the joint in the course below the fold is cracked for about a foot.

A finger that went into that fold and stayed straight would have found the near leaf of the lead and come out again, than after a finger that went in and turned back at its own joint and found the far side of it as well.
