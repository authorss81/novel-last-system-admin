# Chapter 0207 — Not A Party

Sunday the twenty-second of November 2015, from about eleven in the morning, a parade of shops in Tideglass.

**A REGIONAL GROUP THAT RUNS THE WATER FOR THREE TOWNS WROTE TO THIS CITY ABOUT A THING THAT CROSSED AN ESTUARY IN SEPTEMBER, AND ASKED FOR A PERSON TO COME AND SIT IN A ROOM IN ANOTHER CITY, AND HE SAID HE WOULD GO BUT NOT ALONE, AND HE SAID IT OUT LOUD TO THREE PEOPLE BEFORE ANYBODY HAD ASKED HIM FOR ANYTHING, AND WHAT HE SAID WAS THAT HE WAS GOING AS A PERSON WHO WAS THERE AND NOT AS ANYBODY'S DELEGATE, AND THE ARRANGEMENT HAS NO WORD FOR WHAT THAT IS.**

The letter came on the Friday. It is one page and it is polite and it is signed by a woman called Iselin Krogh, who is about fifty-one and is the clerk of that group and is not the person who decided to write it.

The first paragraph thanks this city for the arrangement of the thirtieth of September. The second paragraph says that the matter has been read with interest by members of the group and by the other two authorities that draw from the same works, and that the group thought it useful to hear the account of the person who stopped a clause on the eighteenth of September from the person who stopped it. The third paragraph invites him to attend a sitting on the fourth of December and to sit in the room.

The fourth paragraph is the one he read three times.

*We record you as our correspondent on the above.*

"There is no budget for this," he said, out loud, to his own kitchen. "There is nothing in this borough I can book a seat on and no way of booking one anywhere else either, and there is nothing in this borough that can make me be in a room in another city on the fourth of December, and if nobody makes me then I can decide not to go, and if I decide to go then I am a man who has gone, and the whole of what they have got is a man who has gone."

He read the fourth paragraph again. The arrangement had a word for him in it and the word was not true. He was not their correspondent. He had no body behind him, and there was nothing in this borough that corresponded to anything, and if he said yes to the word then he would have accepted an office in a group three towns away, and an office is a thing that can be required, and he had spent three months in this borough learning what happens to a person who is standing in a hole in a piece of paper when the paper stops.

So he wrote back on the Saturday, on a sheet of his own, and it went in the post on the Saturday, and it said that he would come on the fourth and that he would sit in the room, and that he was not their correspondent and was not anybody's delegate, and that if the group had a word for a person who is in a room and is not standing for anything, he would be glad to have it, and if not, he would be a person who was there.

**AND THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE IN THE STAIRWELL OF THE BUILDING THE CLINIC IS IN THAT MORNING, printed and fixed to the wall at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A LETTER CAN ASK FOR A PERSON WITHOUT NAMING A JOB.**
> **A PERSON CAN SAY YES TO A ROOM AND NO TO A SEAT.**
> **NOTHING AGREED LIKE THAT IS A MINUTE OF ANYTHING.**

Nell Ardent was on the stairs with a box file under her arm. She opens six days and it was not one of them, and she had come in for something that could have waited until Monday and would not.

"I've read it," he said.

"I've had a copy. I don't know who sent it to me and I'm not going to find out this morning." She put the box down on the step. "You're going."

"I'm going on the fourth. I'm going in the room."

"You've said yes to a seat in a room in a city where the water belongs to somebody."

"I haven't said yes to a seat," he said. "That's the thing I want to say to you before I say it to anybody else, so that you have heard it first. I'm going as a person who is there. I'm not their correspondent and I'm not standing for this borough and I'm not standing for the four boxes on your table, and if anybody in that room stands up and says the man from Tideglass, then what they have got is a man in a room."

Nell Ardent picked the box up again.

"That's the first useful thing anybody has said to me since about March," she said, "and it is a definition, and I am not going to tell you what it is a definition of, and you would not be able to use it, and the day either of us starts writing that kind of thing down is the day this borough has an office of it."

"Agreed," said Jonas Mercer.

"I'm not agreeing to anything. Go and get your bus money." She went down the last four steps and out. "And the early one on Sunday is the only one that gets in before the meeting, and it gets in at about ten past."

Sanaa Iqbal was outside the gate of the hospital at about half past twelve with her coat open and her bag over her shoulder, going out to a car park for a sandwich, and she had been on nights.

Before he got there she said, "You have a letter, you are going to the fourth, and you have come to find me to say a sentence, and I am going to let you say it and then I am going to eat a sandwich."

"I'm not anybody's delegate," he said. "I'm going as a person who is there."

"Right."

"That's it. That's the whole thing."

"I know that it is." She got into the car. "I'm not going to be the reason you are not in a room, and I'm not going to be the reason you are in one either, and if I find out afterwards that either of those things happened I'm going to be extremely unpleasant about it, and you can tell them that when you write. Twenty minutes a week, and the paragraph behind it where it has always been, and neither of those has moved since September and neither of them is going to move because of a boat."

"I haven't asked you for either of those things."

"I know you haven't," said Sanaa Iqbal. "That's why I said it in a car park instead of not saying it."

She drove off at about the speed of a person who is going to be late back.

Tomas Renn was in a room over a shop on a Sunday because the room is free on Sundays, and he was alone with a cup of tea and a newspaper he had not read.

"I've got a thing to say to you and I want to say it before you ask me anything, because I know what you're going to do with it."

"Go on."

"You're going to ask me whether you should go, and whether the three of us should go, and whether I would come, and I have got a view about all three of that and I'm not going to give it to you until I have said this." He put the paper down. "You are not a party to it. You are not a party's delegate and you are not anybody's man in a room and there is no word in that letter for what you are going to be when you get there, and you are going to go anyway, and I want that said out loud in this borough by somebody to you before anybody in another city gets the chance to say it for you."

"That is the first time anybody has said that sentence to me before being asked anything," said Tomas Renn. "In nine months. It is the first time, and I have been on the other side of it about nine times, and I have never once been told in advance, and every single time I have got it right, and every single time it has cost me the whole of the evening getting it right on my own in a corridor beforehand." He picked the paper up again. "So yes. Go. And I will come, and I have not said when, and you do not get to decide that either."

They sat there until about four. He did not tell Renn the third of anything and Renn did not ask.

Nobody knew how the group had heard of a sheet of two pages that had gone down on a counter by the water in front of six people on the thirtieth of September and had gone back across the estuary in a plastic wallet. Jonas Mercer went over it twice that afternoon in his own room with the letter on the table and could not get a handle on it, and he put it to Nell Ardent on the telephone at about five and she had been thinking about it since the stairs and had got exactly nowhere, and he put it to Tomas Renn on the Sunday night over a cup of tea that had gone cold and Renn said he had had the same thought at about eleven o'clock and had got nowhere with it either.

So the three of them knew that, and the four of them knew nothing else, and the letter was still on the table with the date on it, and nobody in that room had been asked to be their correspondent.
