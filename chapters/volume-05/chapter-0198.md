# Chapter 0198 — The Low Corner

Monday the twelfth of October 2015, from about ten to five in the morning, the bottom of Peverell Street in Tideglass.

**THE RAIN CAME IN FROM THE WEST DURING THE NIGHT AND STOPPED BEING HEAVY AT ABOUT HALF PAST SEVEN, AND FOUR PEOPLE DID THE FOUR CORRECT THINGS, AND A FIFTH THING HAPPENED THAT IS NOT CORRECT AND IS NOT WRONG, AND NOBODY DIED.**

Jonas Mercer was on the wall at the top of the yard at ten to five because he had been at the end of a road at midnight with a torch that does not work and a bag of sand, having been asked by a woman of about twenty-two who opens a building in the mornings to look at a low corner that nobody in this city has anything to do with. That is the whole of why he was there and there is no arrangement that sent him and he has none.

The run at the bottom of Peverell Street had been at six inches on Thursday. It was at two feet and rising at ten to five, and it had come up the run and spread across the yard and put itself against the bottom nine inches of a close-boarded fence on an old brick base, which is the fence between this yard and the one next door, and the brick base is the part that goes.

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THE WALL AT THAT POINT, printed and fixed to the railings at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **WATER DOES NOT ASK ANYBODY PERMISSION.**
> **A THING DONE AT NIGHT IN THE DARK IS NOT WRONG AND IS ALSO NOT RIGHT.**
> **NOTHING ON THIS NOTICE IS HELD AGAINST ANY PERSON.**

The man of about fifty-seven was already in the yard with his pump on the tail of the van. He had bought two hundred bags on the Saturday morning after the eighteenth and put forty of them across his own doorway on the Sunday night because of a sheet of paper gummed to a shutter that has no address on it, and the rest of them had been in a lean-to at the side of the van ever since, in case. He was stacking them along the inside of the doorway and he was not doing it for anybody and did not say so.

By about five there were five people on that ground and Jonas Mercer was one of them, which is what the borough has: about four or five people who do a correct thing when a correct thing has to be done, and they are the same four or five as they were in May and in June and in August, and there is no way of getting a sixth, because a sixth would have to be somebody anybody could ask.

They were the man with the pump. A man from a drainage firm who had come from up the road with a rod and a bucket and who was putting sand into the low corner where the run turns and building it up above the line, which is the right thing, takes two hours, and is not in anybody's plan. A man of about seventy-seven in the yard next door, who had a workshop on it and a car under a cover, and who was already awake. A man of about seventy-three who had a gate key and who had come because he lives on the next street up and was not asleep, and who held the yard gate open with his arm for the whole of the time and did not put it down. The woman of about twenty-two, who had a torch and a list of doors on the back of an envelope and who went back up the street opening them, one at a time, on her own account, and came down the street twice.

That is the whole of what a rain is in this borough. It is not that nobody is willing. It is that the willingness is in about five people and they are not chosen, they are not on anything, they cannot be sent for, and there is nothing anywhere in this city that could put a sixth person into a yard at ten to five in the morning, and a body that cannot require anybody cannot be told what it will do. Both of those sentences are on a card on a wall in a Methodist hall in this borough and neither of them can be argued out of the other, and this morning the rain tested both of them at once.

**AND AT ABOUT TEN TO FIVE SOMEBODY WENT ALONG THE OLD BRICK BASE OF THE FENCE AND PULLED THREE BOARDS OFF THE BOTTOM, AND THE WATER WENT THROUGH, AND THE WALL STOOD.**

Jonas Mercer was about ten feet away with a spade, facing the other way, at the low corner where the run turns, and he heard it, and he turned round, and there was a light behind a man and a set of boots going along the base of the fence and then there was no light and there was no boots and the water was going through the gap into the yard next door at about the rate it goes through a hole you have put in it on purpose.

He did not see a face. It was dark and it was raining and whoever it was had a hood and a torch pointed at the ground and worked along the base of the wall without hurrying, which is not how a man works at ten to five in the morning when he is in a hurry.

He did not ask. He has not asked anybody anything since the twenty-fifth of February and he was standing in a yard with his back to the water and there was a man behind him who had just done a thing, and there is nobody in this city he could have said it to even if he had.

And the wall stood. That is the part that has to be said first and it is the part everybody who was there will say. The water went through a nine-inch gap at the bottom of an old brick base, four feet of fence, and the fence did not go over, and the pressure came off the two doorways in this yard, and the man of about fifty-seven did not lose the doorway he had sandbagged in September, and at about six the water in the yard was about a foot and a half and it was going out through a hole in a fence instead of in over a door.

In the yard next door it put about a foot on the floor of a workshop. There was a bench in it and a shelf on the wall with his stock on the shelf, and a car under a cover that took the water round the wheels. He came out in a coat and stood in it and would not go back in for another one.

"Nine inches," he said, to nobody, looking at the gap. "That's all that took."

"Leave it," said somebody.

"I'm not going to put it back with the water coming."

Nobody put it back with the water coming. The boards went down the yard in about ten minutes and went under the run and were not seen again, and the man of about seventy-seven said that anybody with a bucket could have done it, which was true, and then said that there is no way on God's earth of finding out who, which was also true, and Jonas Mercer did not ask, and the two people who might have been asked were about forty feet away with their backs to it, one at the gate and one at the low corner, and neither of them turned round, and neither of them has said a word about it since and neither of them is going to be asked.

**AND NOBODY FOUND OUT, AND THAT IS THE CORRECT OUTCOME, AND IT IS ALSO THE ONLY THING THAT MAKES THE MORNING WORK.**

At about eight the woman of about twenty-two came back down the street with the envelope of doors behind her back and stopped at the yard and said that nine doors had been open since about four and that she had not knocked on one of them, and that there is no list of which ones, and that the envelope is in her own pocket. She had a coat on that had not been on her at ten to five, and one of the men at the gate gave her his, and she put it on over the top of the other one and went back up the street.

The yard next door drained at about nine. The water at the low corner went down an inch an hour from about ten and went away properly by about two in the afternoon. The rain stopped being heavy at about half past seven and stopped altogether at about eleven, and there was no more of it that day, and nobody died, and nobody was in a hospital because of the water, and nobody was sent for, and nobody was fetched.

The man of about seventy-seven lost a shelf of stock and said on the day that it was stock, and that he would rather have lost stock, and he was right about that, and he is not going to be asked about it again. The three boards are at the bottom of a run somewhere downstream and will be found in the spring or will not.

The man of about fifty-seven was in his own doorway at about the middle of the morning with his pump going and about six of his own bags left, and Jonas Mercer carried four of them up the street for him because he was going that way and did not say why he was carrying them, and the man of about fifty-seven said nothing and took them, which is two men not saying anything at each other, and there is no list, and there is no hour, and no instrument in this city could turn six sandbags into an act.

At about half past two Jonas Mercer walked up Peverell Street and the water had gone out of the run to about a foot and there was a line of grey on the brickwork of the fence about a foot above where the run was standing, and the sheet of the drawing on the shutter of the lock-up at the top of the street was still on it with its top-left corner lifting, and a man had already been up and put a strip of gummed tape along the top of it, and he was not going to be asked about that either.
