# Chapter 0455 — Nine Foot of Ground

Tuesday the twentieth of November 2018, from about half past four in the afternoon until about six. One length of a street and the ends of two others, in Tideglass.

**A MAN CARRIED FOUR TRESTLES DOWN THAT STREET AND PUT THEM DOWN AGAINST A WALL HE WAS NOT ASKED FOR, AND A WOMAN PUT EIGHT PIECES ON THE SAME NINE FOOT OF GROUND HALF AN HOUR LATER AND TOOK THEM OFF IT AGAIN, AND NEITHER OF THEM SPOKE, AND NEITHER OF THEM EVER WILL.**

Between the back wall of a run of closed premises and the kerb there is a square of hard standing about nine foot by five, and it is the only place on that length where anything can be stood without being in a road.

The wall is the wrong colour. The standing is about two courses of brick laid flat with a cement collar round the edge, laid in about 1994 by a man who has been dead a long time, and it has been the same ever since. The rain runs off it toward the wall, and about half of it gets whatever sun that street gets.

What is on it on an ordinary weekday is nothing at all. That is the thing about it. It is empty every day of the week, from seven in the morning until about ten at night, and it is empty for a reason that is not a rule and not a sign and not anything anybody ever said out loud.

---

A man in his forties has wanted that nine foot since about the end of October, and he has not put anything on it once.

What he has instead is a job that opens at half past seven on a midweek and he has to have the trestles up before the customers come in, and the trestles are four of them and about six foot each, and they live in a gateway two streets off with a man he has never spoken to who lets him stack them against a wall on the understanding that they are there by eight.

The other place is a piece of ground off a lane that is nine foot square as well, and it is four minutes away in a van at that hour, and he has to reverse in off that lane to get at it because there is a post in the way, and the reversing takes about two minutes each way, and the four trestles go up at about ten past seven and come down at about half past four and the whole business costs him about half an hour a day that he does not have.

He has been doing it for about five weeks. He has never once considered walking up there and saying the word. He has not considered it the way you do not consider a thing that would be a conversation.

A woman came back to that street on the second Tuesday in October with a lorry and a man in a fleece and a removal of about eight pieces out of her mother's house, and the house is two streets off and there is no room at the front of it, and the man in the fleece stood in that street for about ten minutes with a cigarette he did not smoke and said, twice, that he would take them round the back if anybody could tell him where the back was.

Nobody could tell him where the back was. There is no back. The nine foot of standing between the wall and the kerb is the only flat ground on that stretch that is not a road.

So he put them on the nine foot. He put about eight pieces on it in about twenty minutes, which is more than it is meant to take, and she stood on the kerb the whole time, and it took them about half an hour to get the lorry round there and away again.

And that was in October. It is the only time anything has stood on that ground all year and it is the reason she now wants it.

She has not said anything to anybody. She has not put a folding table out. She has not knocked on a door. She has her mother's house keys on a ring with nothing else on the ring and has not been back up there to hand them in, and that is not why she wants the ground. She has thought about the nine foot perhaps twice a day since and has not once thought of a sentence that would get her onto it without also being a thing she would have to keep explaining to whoever she asked.

Here is the part that neither of them knows about the other, and it is the whole of the chapter.

He does not know she came back in October. He has not seen her. He has been working in a part of that street he can only see obliquely, at about half past four, from about a hundred and fifty yard up, and what he has seen from up there, twice, is a woman standing on a kerb on her own.

She does not know the four trestles or the gateway or the man who lets them stack against a wall. She has seen one bundle of poles go past on a barrow in the middle of October and has not connected it with anything.

Both of them have the same reason and neither of them has the other half of it. The reason is that the first one of them to put anything down on that ground is a person who wants it, and the second one is a person who has taken it off somebody. Neither of them is willing to be the second. It is not a code and it is not a habit and it is not anybody's arrangement; it is two people independently arriving at the same refusal for the same reason and never once hearing the other one arrive at it.

The light went early on the twentieth of November and that is the only reason anything happened in this chapter.

He came down that street at about twenty to five instead of about half past four, with four trestles under his arm in a bundle, because the job had been slow and he had made an excuse to himself about the light.

The nine foot was empty.

He stood at the kerb for about two minutes and then he put the bundle down on the far side of the standing, against the wall, because he had it in his hands and it weighs more than it did.

Then he stood there with his hands empty and looked at the space in front of the bundle, and he did not put anything on it, and after a while he picked the bundle up again and carried it on up the street to the gateway and put it where it lives, and it took him until about twenty past five to get it in off that lane with the post in the way.

The lorry came back down that street at about ten to six.

She was not in the house. She had been in that street twice since the Tuesday in October and had got the arrangement into her own head and had not told anybody, including the man in the fleece, who was the same one and had forgotten the whole of it inside a month.

Eight pieces. A bed frame, a settee she was not going to take and took anyway, a chest, a table, three boxes, and a long mirror wrapped in a blanket from her mother's house.

The lorry could not get nearer than about ten foot of the wall on that stretch, because of the cars that were already on it, and the man in the fleece put his hands in the air and said, not to anybody, "That's the nine foot, then."

And she said, "That's the nine foot."

And they got the eight pieces off the lorry and onto that nine foot of standing in about twenty-five minutes, which is more room than nine foot of standing ought to be, and the mirror went on last against the wall with the blanket round it, and the man in the fleece stood looking at the whole arrangement for a moment and said it would be two trips next time and she said it would not be a next time.

Then they got back in the lorry and it came away up the street and turned at the top and was gone.

---

The nine foot was empty again at about six o'clock.

It was empty because at about twenty to five a man had stood at that kerb with four trestles in his arms for about two minutes and then carried them two streets off to a gateway, and it was empty because at about ten to six a woman had taken eight pieces of furniture off a lorry and put them on it and then taken them off it again.

The two of those things are not known to each other. There is no man in his forties in that street after about twenty past five and there is no lorry on that stretch after about ten to six and neither of them has been there at the hour the other one was there in ten weeks.

Nothing on that nine foot says any of this. It is a square of hard standing between a wall and a kerb, and it was empty at six o'clock on the twentieth of November 2018, and it will be empty at six o'clock tomorrow, and not one word about it is going to pass between those two people in either direction.

**SHE STOOD ON THAT KERB AT HALF PAST SIX AND LOOKED AT NINE FOOT OF NOTHING AND THEN WENT DOWN THE STREET TO CATCH A BUS, AND TWO HUNDRED YARD UP IT A MAN SHUT A GATEWAY BEHIND FOUR TRESTLES AND WENT HOME.**
