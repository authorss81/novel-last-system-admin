# Chapter 0495 — Going In Before the Shop Opened

Thursday the thirtieth of May 2019, from half past five in the morning until about two in the afternoon. A corner at the top of a street, and the outside of one building, in Tideglass.

**ABOUT FIVE PEOPLE GO UP AN OUTSIDE STAIR OF STEEL STEPS ONTO THE FIRST FLOOR OF A BUILDING AT THAT CORNER EVERY WEEKDAY MORNING, AND A MAN WHO OPENS THE OFFICE AT THE BOTTOM OF THAT STAIR HAS BEEN GOING UP IT AT HALF PAST SIX AT THE SAME TIME AS FOUR OF THEM SINCE THE FEBRUARY BEFORE LAST WITHOUT KNOWING THEY WERE NOT HIS, AND A MAN WHO PARKS HIS LORRY OPPOSITE THAT CORNER HAS BEEN WATCHING THEM GO UP FOR THE SAME LENGTH OF TIME AND HAS ALSO TAKEN THEM FOR THE FIRM'S, AND THE TWO OF THOSE MEN HAVE NEVER SPOKEN TO EACH OTHER, AND THE ONLY THING THAT IS TRUE IS ABOUT THE BUILDING AND IS NOT ABOUT EITHER OF THEM.**

The corner is the top of a street that is not the parade. There are nine houses in it and a joiner's shop and a place that lends out machines, and where the street turns there is a building with a concrete yard in front of it and one office on the ground floor with a door and a single window.

Above that office, on the first floor, there is a stair of steel steps bolted flat to the outside wall, with a small landing at the top of it and a door on that landing. There is nothing written on the landing. There is nothing written on the ground floor either, except a small plate with the firm on it and the shop's telephone number under that, and the number is answered by whoever happens to be in the office.

The stair has a dozen steps and it is painted a dirty green.

The man who opens that office has opened it at half past six on a weekday for longer than anybody on that street has been asking him about it. He does the telephone himself. He has a kettle, a radio, and about four feet of bench along the window that he does not sit on.

He has never once gone up that stair.

---

What goes up it, on a weekday, is this.

A man comes in off the footway at about half past six and goes straight up, and he has a canvas bag and he is the same man every morning. A woman comes up at about five to seven with a folder under her arm and she is the same woman every morning. Two more come up together at about a quarter past seven, and they talk on the landing and then the door shuts and that is the end of what anybody in that street can see.

Nobody signs anything on that stair. Nobody leaves a car. Nobody has ever rung the bell on that landing door, because there is no bell, and nobody has ever knocked on it.

Here is what the man in the office downstairs has made of it in about two years.

Those are his men. They go up at half past six because that is when the yard opens, and they come down at about one or at about four, and he has never asked any of them what it is they do up there because it has never been any of his business and he does not ask anybody what they are doing.

What the man with the lorry has made of it since March is the same thing for a different reason.

He has parked at that corner at about a quarter to seven every morning for six years because it is the only place on that street where you can get a lorry in without being in anybody's view. He has watched four or five people go up an outside stair at a firm he can see a plate for, and he has assumed the way anybody would assume it, and he has gone on his way.

He is a big man with grey at the sides who does not go into that office and has no reason to. He has had a word with the man at the plate twice, both times about something left standing in that yard, and has not had one since.

Here is what happened in the same week, and it is the whole of this.

On Tuesday the twenty-first of May a man who works at the place that lends out machines came across that yard to the man in the office with his hands in his pockets and said that he had been trying to get hold of the man upstairs, and could not, and had had to go up and knock on the landing door to ask whether they were taking work in.

And then he said, as a joke, that he would not have thought to ask, because he would have thought the office knew all about it.

The man with the lorry was in the lorry with the window down about four feet away, and he heard that, and did not answer it, and did not go up.

On the Friday the twenty-fourth of May the woman who does the invoicing for that firm, who is not the woman who goes up the stair but who works in the office four days a week, put her head round the internal door and said that she had been in the building in March to leave a folder and had counted five coats on five hooks on that landing, and she wanted to ask him a question, and the question was whether he knew the first floor had been let.

And then she said, before he had got as far as an answer, that she would have thought he would have known, because anybody would.

He said nothing to that for about a minute and a half. Then he asked her what the first floor let to.

She said: to a man who does not work in it and does not live in it, and he has four in there as well as himself, and it has been going on since the February before last, and you are not to say anything to him because it is nothing to do with the office.

So the man in the office has known for six days that there are five people going up his own stair and that four of them are not his.

And the man with the lorry has known for five weeks that there is somebody upstairs who cannot be got at and that the office did not send him.

And neither of them has said a word to the other, and neither of them has said a word to anybody else, and the reason is not politeness.

It is that a man in the office cannot go up and knock on a door he has never knocked on, because knocking is a thing you do about a thing you are sure of. And a man who parks a lorry cannot stop a man in a doorway to ask him a question about a floor he has no reason to know anything about. Both of those are ways of saying: if I don't ask, then I am not the sort of person who has to be told.

Here is what happened on the Thursday, and it is a wet morning with showers coming through in bands.

The four of them and the man with the bag were taking a bandsaw down that stair at about twenty past eight in the morning, and it would not turn on the landing, so they turned it about end for end and took it down sidewise, and it is a heavy thing and there are five of them and it took them about a quarter of an hour to get it to the bottom step.

The lorry was across the corner.

The man with the lorry had come in at a quarter to seven as he does, and he had to reverse about four feet to get out of the way, and instead he got out and walked forward and stood in the doorway of the office while they brought the saw past him and down onto the concrete.

The man in the office came out of his own office to see what was happening in his own yard, and stopped, because there was a man he had seen every morning for four months standing in his own doorway with a band of rain across the shoulders of his jacket.

That is the first time either of them has been within two feet of the other.

They stayed there about ten minutes, and then a good deal longer than that after the saw was on the ground, because a woman went into the office to use the telephone and neither of them could leave the doorway without it looking like something.

Here is everything that was said, and it is all of it.

"Did you want it out the front."

"No, I'll take it round the back."

"Right."

Then nothing for about a minute, with rain on both of them.

"You're up every morning now," said the man in the office, and he was pleased to have said it, because it was a thing you say to a man you see every day.

And the man with the lorry said: "You'd have known it was your lot, wouldn't you."

And the man in the office said: "I would."

And then the woman came out of the office and said she had left her bag on the bench, and the man in the office went in and got it, and the man with the lorry went back to his lorry and drove off up the street with the bandsaw following him in a lorry with a flat back that had come from somewhere else entirely.

Neither of them has said anything to anybody.

The man in the office has not gone up that stair. The man with the lorry has not said anything when he parks there, and there is a nod now, and the nod began on the Thursday and neither of them can remember agreeing it.

**A BANDSAW WENT DOWN AN OUTSIDE STAIR AT A CORNER IN THAT STREET ON THE MORNING OF THE THIRTIETH OF MAY 2019 AND WENT OUT THROUGH A YARD THAT A LORRY HAD TO REVERSE OUT OF, AND TWO MEN WHO HAD BOTH BEEN LOOKING AT THAT CORNER EVERY MORNING FOR ABOUT TWO YEARS STOOD IN ONE DOORWAY FOR ABOUT TEN MINUTES, AND THE ONLY QUESTION PUT TO EITHER OF THEM WAS ANSWERED CORRECTLY BY BOTH OF THEM.**
