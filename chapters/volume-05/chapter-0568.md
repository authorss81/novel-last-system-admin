# Chapter 0568 — The Piece That Went Over

The Friday of the twenty-ninth of May 2020, from just before twelve until about ten at night, in two gardens either side of two feet of rough grass in this borough.

**SOMETHING CAME OFF A ROOF IN HIS GARDEN IN THE MORNING AND WENT OVER INTO HERS, AND SHE FOUND HER PIECE JUST BEFORE MIDDAY AND HIS OWN WIFE FOUND HIS AT ABOUT ONE, AND SHE TOOK IT ROUND TO HIM AT TEN THAT NIGHT AND HE LOOKED AT IT AND SAID NOTHING AND WENT UPSTAIRS, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS SAID THE WORD SINCE.**

The gardens are back to back with about two feet between them, and there is a paling along each side of that gap. They are both long enough on the ground that neither of them is new. He is a man in his mid fifties. She is a woman in her early twenties and has been in her side about two years.

He has a house with a slate roof over the back door, and about four of those slates were replaced by him in the February of last year with a piece taken off a house that was being cleared two streets off.

He did that on a Saturday by himself. He used three fixings and he was on it about four hours and he told nobody he had done it. The two he took out are still in a heap against the side of the garage and he has not moved them either.

The wind got up at about half nine and went through until about two.

She was doing the third length of her beans just before twelve and she hit something that was not a bean.

It was about a third of a slate, the size of the cover of a record, with three holes in it where nails had been and the edges of it clean where it had snapped.

She picked it up and turned it over and put it down on the paving by her door and went and finished the beans. Then she came back and picked it up again and carried it round.

His wife found the rest of it at about one.

She is in her mid thirties and she had been at the lines at the back and she came out with a bundle over her hip, and there were two pieces on the paving, one of them the size of a hand and one about half that.

She did not think anything of it. She moved them out of the way of her bundle, and she went on, and she mentioned it at about four o'clock to say that there was slate down again and somebody wanted to keep an eye on that roof.

He said he would.

She knocked at about ten.

She had her piece in her hand and she put it down by her own door when he opened and she said, "That came off your roof."

He looked at it. There was a stub of rusted metal in the edge of it, bent over, and three holes in it, and the snapped edge was straight across and pale where it had never been anything but inside.

He did not say anything for long enough that she said, "It's not anything. I only thought somebody ought to know."

"It's all right," he said.

And he shut the door and went upstairs.

---

They have not said the word since. He has not been up on that roof and he is not going to and the slates are where they were and it has been perfectly still there since the wind dropped at about two.

She has not asked what the other piece was. She does not know there is another one and she has not looked, and she has not told anybody, and she has not gone out of her own back door and stood on her own paving to see what that gap looks like from the other side, which she could have done in about twenty seconds.

On the Saturday she did her beans and on the Monday after that she did the second length, and she has not gone near the gap since.

Between those two houses nobody has said anything to anybody, and there is not one thing in it worth the saying, and that is how it will stay.

**SHE BROUGHT HIM ONE PIECE AND HE HAS SAID THREE WORDS BACK, AND HE KNOWS WHICH OF THE TWO IS THE ONE HE PUT IN, AND SHE HAS NEVER ASKED WHICH, AND IT WILL NOT BE SAID IN EITHER HOUSE.**
