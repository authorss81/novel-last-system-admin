# Chapter 0768 — The Wood In The Coal Hole

Saturday the twenty-ninth of October 2022, in this borough. She is in her own house at number nine from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a load of firewood stood on end in a coal hole with no draught in it and going green in the middle.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES HAS FIREWOOD WET AT THE BOTTOM END UNDER A SHEET OF PLASTIC, THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING STANDS IT ON END IN THE COAL HOLE WITH THE SAWN ENDS OUT, AND THE MIDDLE OF THE LOAD GOES GREEN AND FURRY AND IS NOT FOUND OUT UNTIL THE SPRING.**

She is a woman in her late fifties at number nine on Saturday. The woman who does her shopping comes to the front door with the bags, takes them straight in, and does the second half her own way.

---

The fire is unlit and the back door is shut true and its card is at the hinge. There are pads on top of the fridge in a stack and the first one is at the bottom of it. The front room stays shut, and the two stools behind it have had a cloth over them since the summer. There is one bulb in the ceiling on the low setting, and the jug is on the dresser with its lid down and the kettle sitting on the jug.

The wood is out at the side of the house by the back gate, in the same place it has stood since it came off the cart in the summer, under a sheet of plastic that came off the roll then and has gone down on the top of the load and stuck to it since. It is a load of small stuff, mostly, with four or five lengths of something thicker at the bottom that had been lying against the wall of whoever's place it came from.

The bottom of it is green. You can see it from the gate. The bark on the bottom course has gone that dark wet green and there is a black line where the plastic has been lying against it, and the wood at the top of the load is dry and pale and as it was when it came.

She has a saw, an axe, a plastic sheet and a stack of old bricks, and she means to take the plastic off, saw the four thick lengths into short enough to go into the grate, and rebuild the whole of it as a proper stack against the wall of the yard with the rounds out and the flats in and the plastic over it and the bricks on the plastic.

The woman who does her shopping comes up the path at the middle of the morning with two bags in her hands.

---

"You are not going to be carrying that lot in like that."

"I am going to carry it in a barrowful at a time."

"You are not going to be carrying it in at all. Look at the bottom of it."

"It is wet. That is what wet is."

"It is wet because it has been under a sheet of plastic since August with nothing over the top of it but air that is doing nothing." The woman stops on the step. "Take your bags in and get your sleeves up."

---

They do not argue about it until the load is broken down. She lets the bags in through the front door and puts them on the kitchen table and shuts the door, and they go round to the back and take the plastic off, and the woman picks up the bottom course one stick at a time and lays it on the concrete in a row so they can see all of them at once.

There is a lot of green. It is not only the bottom course. It goes up four courses deep at the side that was against the wall, and it is thickest where the plastic lay, and in one place in the bottom of it the sticks have gone into each other and come up as one piece with the bark pulled off them.

"That is not surface."

"That is not surface."

"Then it goes in the house tonight."

"It goes in the house tonight on top and it goes in the house tonight at the bottom, and in a fortnight you will be bringing it out again." The woman stands back off the row. "Wet wood dries from the middle out. Not the ends in and not the bark off. The middle. And it cannot dry at all unless air is going through the middle of it, and you cannot get air through the middle of a heap lying flat on the ground under a sheet."

"Then what do I do with it."

"You stand it up."

---

She does not want it stood up. She wants it stacked and covered, because that is what a woodpile is, and because a heap of wood stood on end takes up twice the floor of a heap of wood laid flat, and the coal hole at the side of her kitchen is about as big as two barrow-loads.

She says all of that. The woman listens to the end of it.

"The coal hole."

"The coal hole is a hole in a wall. You cannot put a woodpile in a hole in a wall and get air through it."

"You cannot put a woodpile in a hole in a wall and get it wet again either." The woman is already walking round to the side of the house. "You have got a lid on that hole. It shuts down flat on the top and it does not let anything up through the middle of a load. Air comes in at the front and stops about a foot in and everything past that stands in its own wet."

"Then what is the point of it."

"The point of it is the ends. Every one of those sticks dries back from the sawn end and the bark comes off the side of it afterwards, not before." She turns round at the coal hole. "You want them on end, packed loose, sawn ends out, and you want the hole no fuller than about two thirds of the way up, because a load you can get your hand into is a load you can turn."

---

They do it for the rest of the morning and it is quick and it is filthy and it works.

The wood goes in standing on end in rows, bark to bark at the sides and sawn end to sawn end at the front, packed loose enough that you can get a hand down between them. They fill it to about two thirds. The woman knocks the front row flush with a spare stick and a lump of wood and stands back and looks down the line of them, and every sawn end in the front is showing and the bark is behind them.

They sweep the concrete. They put the plastic up on the wall by the back gate where it has been for two months. The four thick lengths go under the stairs out of the rain because they will not go in the hole and she is not sawing them in the yard on a Saturday.

"It is good."

"It is good until the first frost." The woman is at the coal hole with her arms in to the wrist. "Then take the lid off it in the frost and put it back on after, and not before, and do not let yourself do it the other way round, because the day you shut a load like this up warm is the day you go in there in March and find it green through the middle and furry and there is no way out of that but taking it all out and starting again."

"You have done this before."

"I have done this before in a shed that breathes." The woman comes out of the coal hole and wipes her hands down her apron. "I have not done it in a hole in a wall in my life and I am telling you what you have not thought of, which is that your hole has a lid and a lid is the difference between a place that dries wood and a place that only stacks it."

Then she goes, and comes back in for her bags, and goes.

---

She has a look at it before dark, because you cannot have a load you cannot look at, and she stands in the kitchen with the lid up and the kitchen light coming through and looks down the line of round faces and they are dry and pale and they look like a wall of coins.

Then she puts her hand into the middle of it.

The first two rows at the front are dry enough to ring when you knock them with a knuckle. The third is warm. The fourth is wet. Her arm goes in to the elbow and she can feel the middle of the load past her wrist, and that is not cold and damp the way the outside is cold and damp. That is warm and wet, and there is something furry against the inside of her forearm.

She takes her arm out and looks at her hand, and on the underside of her forearm there are two small green smears where her hand went past a bark that is coming off.

She puts her hand in again, further, and there is more of it, and it is not two sticks. It is the whole middle of the load from about the fourth row down, and it is going green the way the bottom of the pile went green in August under the plastic, and it has had the same cause and a different name.

She stands at her own coal hole in the dark kitchen with her hand to the elbow in it, and the sawn ends at the front of the load are dry and pale and look like a wall of coins, and she does not take them out.
