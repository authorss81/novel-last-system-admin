# Chapter 0780 — The Stone Offcut On The Back Step

Wednesday the twenty-first of December 2022, in this borough. She is in her own house at number nine from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a stone offcut laid over a hollow in her back step, and the bottom edge of her back door with a step in it.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES HAS A WORN HOLLOW IN THE MIDDLE OF HER BACK STEP WHERE THE DOOR HAS BEEN CATCHING AND MEANS TO RAKE IT OUT AND FILL IT WITH MORTAR, THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING LAYS THE OFFCUT OF HER OWN OLD STONE STEP OVER THE HOLLOW, AND THE DOOR SHUTS A HAND'S WIDTH LOWER AT THAT POINT THAN IT DOES ANYWHERE ELSE ON THE DOOR.**

She is a woman in her late fifties at number nine on Wednesday. The woman who does her shopping comes round the side of the house to the back door in the morning, with no bags, and does the second half her own way.

---

The fire is unlit. The pads are on the fridge with the first one at the bottom. The front room is shut and stays shut. The coal hole at the side of the kitchen has a new lid screwed down at the back of it, and it is shut, and it is not what this morning is about. Two wedges and a spanner are still under the gate at the bottom of the front path, and the gate is still open across it, and the middle flag is bedded high at the door end and drops toward the rail. The heap of mats is under the rail where it has been since the mats came off.

The back door shuts true with its card wedged in at the hinge.

It has shut true with a card in it since the summer, because the door is heavy and the frame has dropped at the hinge side and there is about the thickness of a coin of gap there for the card to take up. She has had the card in and out about forty times and it is a piece of the back of an envelope and it is soft along its fold where she keeps folding it.

The step is one slab of stone, and it has been there longer than she has, and it runs the width of the door with about a hand's width of it either side.

There is a hollow in the middle of it.

The hollow is dished about as deep as a thumb goes and it runs from about the width of a hand in from the hinge side to about the width of a hand in from the latch side, right where the door lands when it shuts. The door has been catching in it since the summer. It does not slam. It comes down and stops about the thickness of a coin above the frame and she has to lift it the last of the way with her hand under the bottom edge, and she has been doing that since the summer.

Her plan is the hollow.

She means to rake the loose out of it with the back of an old chisel, clean it out with a wire brush, and fill it with mortar to within a hair of the top of the stone, and then the door would come down onto a level step instead of into a dish and would shut on its own.

---

The woman who does her shopping comes round the side of the house in the middle of the morning with her cardigan over her arm and knocks at the back door, which she does not do.

"You are not going to fill that."

"I am going to fill that."

"You are going to fill it and it will be better for about a month." She stands on the step and looks down at it. "How deep is the dish."

"About as deep as my thumb."

"And how wide."

"About a hand each side of the middle."

"And what is under it."

"What is under it."

"There is stone under it, and the stone has gone hollow underneath, because the water off this roof has been going across it since it was laid, getting into the dish and washing the grit out of the stone." She crouches on the step and puts the back of her thumbnail into the dish. "You are going to put a skin of mortar in the top of a hole with a trowel in the wet, and it will be the best thing on that step for a month, and then the wet will get behind it and lift it, and you will be picking mortar out of that dish in bits with a screwdriver."

"Then what would you do."

"Nothing to the dish." She gets up off her heels. "Go and get my old step."

---

Her old step is outside against the wall of the yard, where it has stood since the front one came off in the summer.

It is a length of stone about a yard long and a foot and a half across, and it came off the front of the house where it had stood for about forty years, and the man who took the new one off sawed it into three so it would go in the back of his van and gave her the middle piece and kept the two ends.

It is four inches thick at the weather end and about two at the wall end, because a step that has stood with one edge out in the rain for forty years wears on the edge and does not wear on the back of it.

She walks it round to the back door on a sack barrow with a board under it, and they get it up onto the step, and it is heavy enough that the two of them have to lift it.

---

They lay it across.

The offcut is laid across the step over the dish, with the thick end under the hinge side of the door and the thin end under the latch side, and it overhangs the front of the step by about a hand because it is longer than the step is deep.

They do not put anything under it. It sits on the stone at either side of the dish and the dish is under the middle of it.

"That is a stone on top of a stone."

"That is a stone on top of a stone and it is not going anywhere, because that step has got a solid edge at both ends of it and this is sitting on both of them." She stands back and looks at it. "And the dish is not under that stone any more, because there is nothing coming down onto it. It is not in the weather and it is not in the wet and nothing is going to break it."

---

The door shuts.

She stands in the yard and shuts the back door and it comes down and stops.

It catches nothing.

It comes down over the stone and it stops exactly where it stopped before, a coin's thickness above the frame, and the card is still in at the hinge, and she did not have to lift it at all. She shuts it four times to be certain and it shuts four times.

She pushes it to. It goes over the stone and it goes down to the frame and it is done.

"That is better than the door was."

"That is better than the door was." The woman is standing at the side of the step with her hand on the stone. "Do not go anywhere."

---

"What now."

"Now put your hand along the bottom of that door."

She goes and puts the back of her hand along the bottom edge of the back door where it comes down over the stone, and runs it from the hinge end to the latch end.

It is not a straight line.

The bottom edge of the door is out of line with itself. Where it crosses the stone it stands about a hand's width lower than the bottom edge at either end of it, and the step in it is not a soft thing and it is not a dish. It is hard and sharp and it goes the whole width of the door.

"The door is shut."

"The door is shut." The woman puts her own hand on it and finds the same place. "Because that stone is thicker at the hinge end than it is at the latch end, and it has stopped the door a hand's width higher at one end than at the other, and a door that is stopped higher at the hinge end than at the latch end has to swing further closed before the latch comes home. And when it has, the whole bottom edge of it is out of line and the latch has had to go further into the frame than it was ever meant to."

"Then take the stone out."

"I am not taking the stone out. The stone is the best thing that has happened to that step. That stone is on two solid edges and it is not going to be lifted by any wet that comes down a roof, and that dish under it is finished." She takes her hand off the door. "You have not got a door with a step in it. You have got a door that has been shut further than the frame was made for, and the frame is not a thing that adjusts."

---

Then the card is on the step, face up, where it has come out of the hinge side of the door. It has not been there since the summer and she has not missed it.

She picks it up and looks at it and puts it in her apron pocket, because that is what she does with it, and then she stops with her hand in the pocket because there is nothing in the hinge now to put it into.

She puts the back of her hand along the bottom of the door one more time, in the daylight, and it goes down at the stone and comes back up at the ends, and the door shuts on a step that will not go down again.
