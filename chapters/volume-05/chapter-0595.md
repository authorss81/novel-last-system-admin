# Chapter 0595 — Put Down in Writing

The Friday of the twenty-fifth of September 2020, from about ten in the morning until about half past six in the evening, in an office above a shop on a road in this borough.

**A SENTENCE WAS SAID TO HIM IN THAT OFFICE ON THE MONDAY BEFORE AND HE HAS SPENT THE BEST PART OF A WEEK ON IT, AND IT IS IN THE MIDDLE DRAWER OF THE CHEST OF DRAWERS AGAINST THE WALL, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS DONE ONE THING WITH IT, INCLUDING HER.**

There is a shop on the ground floor of that building and it has been there since before either of them came. Above it are nine flats, and the office is at the back of the first floor. It is two rooms with a corridor between them, and the only outside window in it is at the end of the corridor and it looks at the back of the next building and not at the road.

Both of them have been in that building long enough to have stopped being introduced. The money and the forms and the letters that go out with a signature on them are his, and that is because he has been here longer, and he is a man in his mid sixties. The nine flats and the people in them are hers, and she is a woman in her early forties, and that is because she started later and nobody handed it to her. Neither has a key to the other's flat and neither has asked for one.

She deals with the people in the nine flats. He does the money and the forms and the letters that have to go anywhere with a signature on them, and has done since before she started, and neither of them decided that between them.

---

Here is the sentence.

She said it on the Monday before, at about four in the afternoon, standing in the open door of the first of the two rooms with a folder under her arm, and she said it without any weight on it at all, in the way a person says a thing they have been carrying about for a while and have only just found the shape of.

She said, "I'd rather it was in writing than us going over it again."

He said, "Right," and she went out and shut the door behind her, and he sat there for about a minute afterwards.

That is the whole of what was said. He has not asked her what she meant by it and she has not told him.

Here is what he did about it, and it is the only thing that happened in that building that week, and it was done well.

What she meant, as far as he could work it out, was this. In about a fortnight she had been told nineteen separate things by nine of the people in those flats, and had passed all nineteen on to him in about as many days, mostly in the corridor and mostly while she was on her way out of the door. They were the ordinary kind. One man wanted somebody to look at the thing over his own door that had started going on and off. A woman wanted to know whether the shop was taking its deliveries at the back. A man on the seventh had asked three times about the flat above him.

None of them had been dealt with. She had not been able to do anything with any of them, because none of them was hers, and she had been saying so, and being said so to.

What he decided was that if it were written down then it would exist somewhere other than in a corridor between two rooms, and that would be the end of that.

He started on the Monday night at about half past eight. He did it in the evenings and at the weekend and in the middle of the day on the Wednesday when she was out, and he gave it the whole of his attention each time, and it took him until the Friday because he is not a fast hand and because he would not leave a thing out of it.

He wrote the nineteen out one to a page. At the top of each one he put the flat and the date and the name as the person had given it, and under that he wrote what they had said as near as he could get it, and under that he wrote what he had said back, and then he wrote what had been done, and where nothing had been done he wrote nothing and left the space. He did that on purpose, because leaving the space is the honest way of writing down that nothing has happened yet.

On the Wednesday he went down and knocked on three of the nine doors and asked each of the three whether they had said what he had written down. Two of them had. One of them said she had never said anything of the kind in her life, and he was at that door for a while, and then he went back up and wrote her line out again with the words *put to her on the Wednesday and not confirmed* at the end of it, which made it six lines longer than the other eighteen.

That is the sort of thing that takes a week.

By the Thursday night he had the whole of it. He read it through twice and he was pleased with it in the ordinary way a person is pleased with a piece of work that is right. He is competent at this and it showed, and that is the whole of what his competence was for.

He rang two lengths of paper together on the Friday morning, typed a heading on the first and a line at the bottom with the date on it, and put it in a plastic wallet. The wallet went in the middle drawer of the chest of drawers against the wall in the second of the two rooms, which is where the money goes in the months when there is money. He shut it. It is a good drawer and it shuts properly.

She came in at about half past four on the Friday and he told her, standing in the corridor, that it was done and where it was.

She said, "Right." Then she said, "Is it all of them?"

He said, "All nineteen."

She said, "Did you get the one about the flat above seven?"

He said, "It's in. There's nothing under it, because nothing's been done about it."

She said, "No," meaning she had not expected otherwise, and she went into the first room and shut the door, and that was the whole of what was said about it.

---

She has not opened the wallet. He is not going to mention it again, because nobody asked him to and because a thing that is done does not get mentioned, and because he has understood something about that sentence in the six days since he said *right* to it in the open door. The something is that she said it on her way out, and she has gone down to the shop every day since, and the sentence was hers and not his and he has done the whole of it and there is no more of it to do.

He is not a person who stops. That is worth saying because it is the shape of him. He did the work and he did it well and it took him the better part of a week, and if she had come in on the Monday and asked him for a second one he would have started it on the Monday.

Here is what the nineteen are. One is about the thing over a man's own door. Two are about the shop's back door and who has a key to it. Two are about deliveries coming in at half past five and hitting a wall. Three are about rubbish going out on the wrong night. One is about a man on the seventh who has now asked three times about the flat above him. One is about a woman on the fifth who wants to know when the price of milk goes up. Four are about money, two of which are the same money. One is about water going brown in a kitchen on the first. One is about a dog. One is about the shop's hours changing in the new year. One is about somebody else's mother.

That is the whole of what a hundred and thirty thousand people are, on that road, on the list.

And there is no part of it that can be done by anybody. That is not because nine people have not been asked. It is that the nineteen are things nine people said to a woman in a corridor, and nine people saying things is not a thing a person can act on, and she is not going to take them to the shop or to anybody, and he has not suggested it and will not.

That drawer is shut. The paper in it is good paper and the nineteen are all there, with a space left where nothing has been done, which is nineteen spaces and no more.

**HE DID IT PROPERLY AND IT TOOK THE BETTER PART OF A WEEK AND IT IS IN THE MIDDLE DRAWER OF THE CHEST OF DRAWERS AGAINST THE WALL, AND SHE ASKED HIM IF IT WAS ALL OF THEM AND HE SAID YES, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS DONE A THING WITH IT SINCE, AND HE IS NOT A PERSON WHO STOPS.**
