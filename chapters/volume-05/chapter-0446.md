# Chapter 0446 — The Room at the Top

The Wednesday of the tenth of October 2018, and about an hour of it beginning at half past four in the afternoon, the top floor of a building in a street near the water, in Tideglass.

**BOTH OF THEM WANTED THAT ROOM AND HE KNEW SHE WANTED IT AND SHE DID NOT KNOW THAT HE KNEW, AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID ONE WORD ABOUT IT, AND ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF NOVEMBER IT WAS STILL EMPTY.**

She has wanted that room since the middle of August.

A man carried a wardrobe out through that door on the fourteenth and left it standing open behind him, and she was on the stairs, and she looked in. It is a top room with one slope in the ceiling and a low window at the low end and a good deal of light on the wall that does not carry the slope, and there is a bed in it already that belongs to nobody and has been there since the summer.

Her own tenancy runs to the end of March and she has known for about two months that it runs to the end of March, and she has been in the same position before with one of those, and she knows the shape of the last two months of a tenancy that is ending.

She has told nobody. There is a woman in that building who keeps the rooms and comes round on a Monday with a ring of keys on her belt, and on the Monday of the first of October she asked her one question, which was whether anybody had been in to look at that top room since August. The woman said no. She did not ask a second one. She stood on the stairs afterwards for a while with her hands in her pockets and worked out that asking a second one would be the moment it stopped being a thing she had happened to want.

---

The man in his thirties was at the top of that building at about half past four on the Wednesday, because the woman who keeps the rooms had asked him to look at a roof.

There is a job at the end of that street that has been standing since the summer and that he is not doing. He looked at the roof because it is easier to say yes to a roof.

He went up the outside ladder at twenty past four and was up there about half an hour, and what he did up there was walk the slope, put his thumb into two places in the mortar of the ridge where it had gone out, and tell the woman who keeps the rooms when she came up at about five that the roof was not the problem and had not been the problem, and that it would stand another two winters if nobody put a wardrobe on it.

Then they came down together, and the woman who keeps the rooms said she was going to lock up, and that was that.

She was on that staircase at the same moment, coming up with a ring of keys of her own off her own belt, and she said, since the man was there anyway and since she had asked to see that room on the Monday, would he look at it. Would he just look at it. That was all she asked for.

It took about two minutes to get into. The key is an old one and it turns about a third of a revolution and then stops and then goes the rest of the way, and it has to be lifted as it comes.

He stood in the middle of it. He did not go near anything. He looked at the bed, and at the window, and at the wall the light falls on, and at the place where the ceiling comes down, and he said one sentence.

"It's a good room."

She said, "It is."

He went out. He put his hands in his pockets going down that stair, the way he does, and she stayed up there with the door open behind her and the light on in that room until about half past five.


Here is what he worked out, and he worked it out on the flat, and nobody knows he worked it out.

The woman who keeps the rooms had a ring of keys on her belt and that ring does not open that room. The woman who works in a shop had a key off her own belt and did not need it in any way a person is supposed to need it, and she was on that stair at half past four on a Wednesday when the woman who keeps the rooms was shutting the building up at five, and got a man she has had a word with in a shop a hundred times to stand in a room she had already stood in herself.

He stood on the flat with a length of string he had used to measure the ridge and he thought about that for about a minute, and then he put the string in his pocket and came down the ladder.

It was about half past five by the time he was on the pavement. She was still up there. He could not have told you that, because the door of that room gives on a landing and the landing is a floor below the flat and the door was shut, and he did not go up and look.

If he had, he would have found her standing in the middle of that room with her hands at her sides in a house where the light is on that wall at that hour, not looking at anything.

He did not go up and look. He went to his van. And she does not know to this day that any of it was worked out, because a man who has worked out a thing and says nothing is exactly like a man who has not, and she is not in a position to ask him.

---

They have not spoken about that room. Not once, not in a shop, not in a street, not in a fortnight of seeing each other most days of the week. She has not asked him. He has not offered, and there was a fortnight in the middle of October in which he could have said one word in about four seconds and did not, and he has not been able to account for why he did not and has stopped trying to.

The room is still empty. The bed is still in it and belongs to nobody. There has been no conversation between anybody about that room since the Wednesday of the tenth of October, and the woman who keeps the rings of keys still comes round on a Monday, and the key still turns a third of a revolution and stops.

**ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF NOVEMBER A WOMAN WALKED PAST THE TOP OF THAT STAIRCASE AND LOOKED AT A DOOR SHE HAS A KEY FOR, AND SHE WENT DOWN AND ON, AND A MAN ON A FLAT ROOF TWO FLOORS ABOVE HER NEVER KNEW SHE HAD STOOD IN IT.**
