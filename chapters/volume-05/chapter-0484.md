# Chapter 0484 — The Door in the Flank

The Wednesday of the tenth of April 2019, late afternoon and the first of the evening, in Tideglass.

**AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR ON THAT WEDNESDAY A VAN CAME BACK UP THAT PARADE THE WRONG WAY LOOKING FOR A HOUSE AND WENT OVER THE CORNER OF A CONCRETE PLANTER OUTSIDE A SHOP, AND IT STOOD ABOUT FOUR INCH OFF THE WALL FOR TWENTY-NINE MINUTES WHILE PEOPLE WALKED ROUND IT, AND THEN THE DRIVER CAME BACK AND A WOMAN CAME OUT OF THE SHOP AND THEY PUT IT AGAIN BETWEEN THEM IN ABOUT FOUR MINUTES AND HE DROVE OFF AND SHE WENT IN.**

A Wednesday in the second week of April in that borough is a Wednesday.

The shops on that parade that keep all week are open until half past five and one of them until six, and the rest of them shut when they shut, and the ones that have been shut since January are shut, and there is a sandwich bar at the bottom end that does not close at all on a Wednesday, because the man in it has never worked out what time he shuts and the last customer of the day goes when he goes.

By seven the street is a street with a few lamps on in it and about nine people still on it, and it is not late and it is not a Sunday and nothing about it is a day anybody in that borough would describe afterwards.

About nine people were on it between ten past four and about seven.

A man came down at about ten past four carrying a roll of something under his arm, went into a shop, and came out of it with a carrier bag. A woman with a folded pushchair went up at about a quarter to five and was not seen again. Two men stood outside a shop from about five until about twenty past, saying nothing anybody on that street could hear, and then one of them went home and the other went in. A man in overalls came up from the bottom at about ten past five with a tray of tools and went out of the top. A man in his forties came out of a flat over a shop at about half past five, stood on the pavement about a minute looking both ways, and went back in. The woman who works in a shop came to the front of her own shop at about ten to six, put her head round her own doorframe for about two seconds, and went back in. A man with a folding trolley came up at about six with six boxes on it and went back down at about ten to seven without having been into anything. A woman in her sixties who lives in the flats behind that street stood outside for about ten minutes at about half past six and then went in.

And at about a quarter to seven there came up that parade a man who shuts the shop next door to the one that shuts at half past four, and who has always been able to get in there when it is empty, and he shut that shop, and he has shut it for the woman who runs it on a Wednesday for about seven years.

She is at her sister's on a Wednesday. He has never been asked and has never said no, and nobody on that street has ever thought to notice any of that, and he is not on anything.

---

There was one thing go wrong on that parade that evening and it was a van, and this is the whole of it.

In front of the shop next door to the one that shuts at half past four there is a concrete planter about two foot by two foot and about two foot high, with a shrub in it that has not had a flower on it since about 2017. It stands on the pavement against the front of that shop and it has stood there as long as anybody can remember and it is a thing everybody on that street walks past.

The van that came back up the parade at about half past four was going the wrong way and going slowly, and it came over the corner of that planter.

It did not lift it. It pushed it about a foot out from the wall and turned it slightly, and one corner of it went about four inch out on the road side, and then the van went on up the parade and out of the top of it.

Nine people walked round that planter between about half past four and about five.

Nobody stopped. Nobody said anything to anybody about it, and nobody went into a shop and said *they've hit the thing outside*, and two of the people who went round it were annoyed in the ordinary way, and one of them passed a remark about it to the man with the tray, and the man with the tray passed one back, and that was the end of that.

The driver came back at about five. He came back on foot from round the top end because there was nowhere to turn, and he came down the middle of the parade looking at the fronts, and he found the planter, and he stood and looked at it for about three seconds, and then he went into the shop next door to the one that shuts early and asked the woman at that counter whether she knew whose it was.

And she said, "That's ours. I'll get my husband."

And her husband is a man of about sixty and he was in the back, and he came out, and the two of them got hold of it, and they put it back against that wall. It is about half an inch forward of where it was before. The driver said sorry about four times and the woman's husband said it did not matter about twice, and the driver said if the pot went he would come back and pay for another one, and the woman said it would not go, and that was the end of it.

That is all of it. It took about four minutes, nobody has told anybody, and it is not going to turn into a thing anybody remembers.

There is a mark on one side of that shrub about the size of a bumper and it will be there next year.

There is a door in the flank of the last building on that parade and it is painted the same colour as the wall it is in.

It is not locked. It stands open about two inches along its bottom edge, and it has for as long as anybody can remember, and it does not come to because the ground on that side has come up and the bottom of it has ridden up with it.

It is not a place anybody pays to keep things in. It is a door into a ground-floor room that has not been used since about 2011, and there is nothing in that room but a floor, the smell of cold plaster, and about an inch of water standing in the corner nearest the wall. Nothing in it has ever been sat on.

Nobody in this borough has been inside that room in about eight years.

Nobody goes in, and it is not because anything stops them, and it is not because there is anything to be had in there, and it is not because the woman who owns that building has said so, and nobody has ever asked her about it.

It is that getting in there means standing on about two hundred square yard of ground at the side of a building that belongs to that building, and there is no way through the back of it, and a person who has no reason to be standing on that ground does not go and stand on it in the evening to look at a room with a puddle in it.

That is all it is. A door in a wall, a room with nothing in it, and nobody in this borough who wants anything out of it.

---

Down by that water is a building with a passage running off it, and a room at the far end of the passage, and in that room a book has been left lying open on its own page.

That book has been at nineteen entries since the March of 2016. Under the last of them, on the same sheet, is a ruled line about as thick as a pencil, and it runs the whole way across that page, and not one thing has ever been written in it, and on the tenth of April it had stood there, empty, for one thousand five hundred and ninety-six days.

No person in this borough is carrying that number about. Nobody counted it, nobody ruled it, nobody went down that passage, and the thumb that used to hold the page is not coming back.

There are four accounts of one thing in this borough and they are all still in circulation, and a fifth has not turned up, and none of the four has been withdrawn by anybody who holds it.

Nobody in this borough has ever said to another person which of those four is theirs. They have never been laid end to end by anybody. Nobody has ever been asked what any of them is for.

And behind all four of them there is a question, and nobody on that parade asked it of anybody on that Wednesday, and nobody there is going to.

What a person in this borough still cannot do is say out loud what the trouble is. It is the same thing it was in the middle of August and it has not moved since, and what has come of it since then is nothing, and the reason is that there is nobody left in this borough who would go and look for it.

Six weeks and a bit have gone since the twenty-fourth of February. A fortnight is a fortnight and six weeks is six weeks and neither of them turned anything.

**AT ABOUT SEVEN ON THAT WEDNESDAY THE MAN WHO SHUTS THE SHOP NEXT DOOR WALKED UP THAT PARADE WITH THE SHUTTER BAR UNDER HIS ARM AND TURNED INTO A STREET WITH NO SHOPS ON IT, AND A WOMAN WHO HAD LEFT HER OWN COUNTER EARLY WENT INTO HER OWN SHOP AND CLOSED THE FRONT OF IT, AND A PLANTER STOOD AGAINST A WALL WITH A MARK ON THE SHRUB IN IT.**
