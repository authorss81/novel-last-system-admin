# Chapter 0578 — The Words From Outside the Shop

The Sunday of the twelfth of July 2020, from about half seven in the evening until about quarter to nine, in a hired club off a road in this borough.

**SHE GAVE A MAN FOUR WORDS IN A CAR OUTSIDE A SHOP IN APRIL AND ON THE SUNDAY EVENING SHE GAVE HIM THE FOUR WORDS IN A PLACE WITH A DOZEN OTHER PEOPLE IN IT, AND A WOMAN WHO WAS ALSO IN THAT CAR HEARD THEM AND FOUND SHE HAD BEEN ANSWERING THE APRIL ONE WITHOUT REALISING, AND SAID OUT LOUD WHAT HAD BEEN DONE IN APRIL, WHICH IS THE FIRST TIME ANYBODY HAS PUT IT ANYWHERE.**

The club holds about sixty with the seats in rows and about twenty without them. It was taken for the evening by a group that meets on Sundays and has met on Sundays for longer than that floor has been down. There were folding seats in four rows with a gap down the middle, a plank on two upended barrels at the back with an urn on it and a stack of plastic ones, and a woman in an apron who had come in at seven and would not sit down until those were away.

Twelve people were in there. That is all there were. Two of them had come from one street and had not spoken in three weeks for reasons of their own, and about four of the rest had known each other since long before anybody in there was born.

The man in his early sixties had been a member for about two years and sat on the end of the second row because he has always sat on the end of a row.

The woman in her late twenties had come with him in October and had not brought her own seat because there are never enough.

In April the two of them had been outside a shop on a Saturday afternoon and he had asked her to do a thing for him at the end of that month, and she had said she would think about it, and he had said why do you have to think about it, you have never asked me for anything, and she had looked at the display in the glass of that shop and said:

"I'd rather not."

Nothing came of it then. She thought about it that night and decided against it and did not go back into the shop that month.

The woman in her mid thirties was in the back of that car. She had asked him for a lift that afternoon herself, and had heard both halves of it, and had said nothing in that car because it was not hers.

On the Sunday evening at about half past seven the man in his early sixties turned round on his seat and asked the woman in his late twenties to do that thing at the end of that month.

She had the four words ready. She had them ready in her mouth before he had finished asking, because they had been sitting in that spot since April and nothing since had shifted them.

"I'd rather not."

---

The woman in her mid thirties had both sentences in her at once, and for about three seconds she was in April on a Saturday outside a shop looking into the glass with a man's voice behind her saying *you have never asked me for anything*.

That conversation had been over since the Wednesday of that week. She had been in it and she had watched it end and she had said nothing at the time, and nobody in it has ever said out loud that it ended.

She said, "You asked me that in April. Outside the shop."

She said it across four rows of folding seats with the urn going behind her and a dozen people in there and it came out at the volume of somebody putting a name to something.

The man in his early sixties said, "You did, yes."

That was all he said. He did not ask which April. He did not call it a joke, and he did not call it anything else, and he turned back round to face the front of that place.

The woman in her late twenties sat where she had been sitting and looked at the back of the man's neck for about as long as it takes to count to four, and then she got up and walked round the outside of the four rows and out of the door.

She was outside for about a minute and a half. Nobody went after her. Somebody at the back said the things were ready and the woman in the apron filled them and began putting them down the rows, which is what she does.

The woman in her mid thirties did not get up and did not go out and did not turn round a second time. She sat where she was for the rest of it. She had put a thing into a place that had not been in it and she could not see any way of getting it back out that would not make it worse, and she had known that before she opened her mouth.

The woman in her late twenties came back in about a quarter past eight and sat down and had something of whatever was going and joined in with a discussion about something else that was going on at the other end of the second row.

Nobody said anything else about April. It was said, it was in there, and there was nothing whatever to be done with it, and the meeting finished when meetings of that group finish, and the seats were stacked and two men carried them out to a car.

At the door on the way out the man in his early sixties held the door for the woman in her mid thirties and looked at her, and she looked back at him, and neither of them put one word on it, and that was the whole of what they exchanged about it.

Those four words were given a second time in that place in July and the place took them, and the one person in it who knew which conversation they belonged to is the only person who cannot get them back out.
