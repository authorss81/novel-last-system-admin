# Chapter 0224 — A Sunday in Tideglass

Sunday the twenty-first of February 2016, from about ten in the morning, a building by the water in Tideglass, and then a parade of shops.

**THERE ARE TWO DOCUMENTS AND THEY DESCRIBE THE SAME ACT AND ONE OF THEM IS A THING A PERSON MAY DO AND THE OTHER ONE IS A THING A PERSON HAS TO DO, AND ONE OF THEM IS IN THIS CITY AND ONE OF THEM IS IN A TOWN EIGHT MILES UP THE ESTUARY, AND NOBODY HAS PUT THEM TOGETHER AND NOBODY IS GOING TO.**

The building by the water is shut on a Sunday and the records room is on the ground floor, and Nina Calder comes in on a Sunday about once a month, and this was one of them, and she was doing the second floor's water bill out of a carrier bag because it does not keep and it does not matter, and she had a cup of tea on the sill and she said the door sticks and nothing else.

The working book is on the table by the wall. It is the same book. The column is the same column with the same heading over it, and the last entry in it is the fourteenth of September, and there is nothing at the end of it, and it was not emptied and nobody wiped it.

Jonas Mercer did not open the register. He never opens it. It is on the shelf and it is a different book in a hand that has not changed since somebody in this city decided that a register was a good idea, and it is open at the last written page, because it is always open at the last written page, and he has never once seen it shut.

Nineteen entries. The eighteenth is a door. The nineteenth is a yard light, and the maintenance column against the nineteenth says a bulb, and the bulb is out.

Under the nineteenth there is a line, and the line goes down the page and stops about two inches short of the bottom, and there is nothing after it, and it is the only thing in that building that has not moved.

He has a piece of paper in his coat with a date on it and he does the sum standing up, in the margin, with a biro, and it comes to four hundred and fifty-two, and he writes it on the piece of paper and puts the piece of paper back in his coat, and it is the same sum he has been doing since the summer and he has never got a different answer out of it.

**AND THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE IN THE ENTRANCE HALL OF THAT BUILDING THAT MORNING, printed and fixed to a post at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A LINE UNDER AN ENTRY IS NOT AN ENTRY.**
> **A BOOK ON A TABLE IS NOT A FIELD.**
> **NEITHER OF THOSE IS A REMEDY.**

Nina Calder did not ask him what he was doing, and he did not tell her, and about ten minutes later she said, "There's a boat," which meant the first one, and he said he knew, and she said the coffee place by the slip shut at eleven on a Sunday, and he said he knew that as well, and neither of them said anything else for about twenty minutes, and then he went.

---

The parade was the parade. The board at the top of Peverell Street had the corner lifting and had had it since the autumn, and the shape in the middle of it is a square with nothing in it and has been a square with nothing in it since the eighteenth of September, and there is one on the shutter of the shop at the bottom and one on the inside wall of the hall, and nobody was standing in front of any of them.

The permission from Alderwick is in force and has been since the thirtieth of September and runs to the middle of the spring, and nobody in this borough has extended it, shortened it, quoted it at anybody or asked anybody about it, and nothing in this borough has come to an end this winter that anybody was relying on, and there is no season that anybody has said is coming. A boat, a rain, a gully, a wall sheet and a claim in a drawer are not reasons, and nobody has ever been obliged.

In the inside pocket of his coat there is a plastic wallet with a sheet in it that a woman put in his hand in a corridor in another city in December and told him there was no charge for it and no record of who had taken one, and it is one line and it says that nothing is required of any person in order to use any part of that system, and the office copy of the same sheet has a second line under it in the same type, and he has read both and Nell Ardent has copied both out in her own hand and compared them with a ruler.

Eight miles up the estuary there is a sheet of paper on a wall in a room, and Jonas Mercer has read it and has not got a copy of it, because he told the clerk who offered to ring the printer that he knew exactly what happens to a piece of paper like that, and she said right. It is four lines. The third one is *A duty is an act and not a place.* The last one is *Nothing in this can be given to a person.* Under the second line is a name, and the name is a man of about eighty-six who was asked once, in a room, in a spring, and said yes, and has not been asked anything since, and who said a true thing at twenty past six on Wednesday evening to one person and then said he should not have said it, and it was not taken back and it was not written down and Jonas Mercer has not repeated it and will not.

There is a third of them somewhere. Nobody in this borough has seen it and Jonas Mercer is not going this month, and he has got no way of asking for an invitation to it that would not be an arrangement, and he is not going to invent one.

---

The vestibule on the ground floor has three doors off it and a shuttered shop to the left of it, and the counter is the width of two people, and the door is open because the day is warm for February, and there is a card in the shop window with the shop's own telephone number on it and nothing else on it, put there in July by a woman of twenty-nine who wanted to stop being findable, and about thirty strangers have asked the woman who keeps that shop about it since.

Jonas Mercer stood on the pavement.

She was behind the counter with her back to the glass, doing something with a box of something, and she has been there for about nine years and she has never been told anything. He did not go in. There was nothing he wanted, and a person who goes to a counter with nothing he wants is a person who has started using it, and the day this counter is asked what a thing is will be the day it stops being a counter, and it has answered one thing in about nine months, twice, in the same words, to a man of about twenty-eight who wanted to put the low end of Peverell Street somewhere, and it has not been asked about the order or a gate or a rota or a person or a permission or a lapse by anybody, and nobody was going to ask it anything today, and the drawer under it with no key on it and no list on it is shut, and the correct sentence a woman put in it on the morning of the twentieth of May 2015 is in it, and nobody in this borough knows it is there and nobody is going to be told.

He stood there for about a minute and then went on up the parade, and the woman of about sixty-five did not turn round, and neither of them said anything, and that is the whole of what happened between them on the twenty-first of February.

He went up the parade to the bus stop and there was one at ten past and about nine people got on it, and not one of them had been in that building, and he got on it, and it is a bus and it goes where it goes, and there is not one thing that has happened in this borough since the eleventh of November that anybody is going to write down this afternoon, and nobody has died of any of it and nobody has been carried out of anywhere by it.

**AND THE REGISTER IS AT NINETEEN AND THE BULB UNDER THE NINETEENTH IS OUT AND THE LINE UNDER IT IS FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-TWO DAYS AND IS STILL BLANK, AND IT IS THE ONLY THING IN THAT BUILDING THAT HAS NOT MOVED WHILE AN INSTRUMENT WENT OUT OF FORCE IN IT, AND THAT IS WORSE THAN A RELIEF, AND IT IS NOT A RELIEF, AND A MAN WALKED OUT AT ELEVEN O'CLOCK AND WENT DOWN TO THE WATER.**
