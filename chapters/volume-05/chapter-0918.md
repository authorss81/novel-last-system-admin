# Chapter 0918 — Into The Post Hollow

Tuesday the eighteenth of June 2024, at the timber gate post at number nine from breakfast till the shade thins. The hollow at a post foot.

**SHE HAS A TIMBER GATE POST AT NUMBER NINE WITH A HOLLOW AT ITS FOOT AND SHE MEANS TO PRESS THE THUMB DEEP INTO THAT HOLLOW WITH FINGERS BRACED AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING IS AT THE BACK DOORWAY FROM THE YARD WITH A KNOTTED SCARF ON HER ARM.**

The gate post went in with the fence when the yard was divided, and the gate beside it has been re-hung once while the post has kept its seat.

It is a post of oak about five inches square, with the foot sunk in earth and a hollow eaten at its base where the rain stands, and grass roots at its heel where the broom does not reach.

What she wants is that hollow, and there the timber of the post meets the soil it was set in and the damp sits. The crown of a post tells you the weather and its foot tells you the ground, and the two never agree. So the thumb sinks deep in the post hollow braced.

The wood feels cool round the thumb, and the hollow walls catch where rot has softened them. Past the first joint the thumb finds a wad of earth rammed at the back, black and tight, studded with grit and a flint splinter lodged there since the spade work years back.

Side to side across that wad goes the thumb, and the soil gives a quarter inch under the pad and closes up again behind it. What the thumb finds back there is seasoned oak and nothing else: heartwood set deep, darkened outside by the damp, tight in the grain, rooted where it stands.

For all its depth the hollow proves blind. It goes a thumb deep with soil rammed at the back between, and the soil deadens the pull of the gate when the wind moves it.

She pulls the thumb clear and wipes the soil off on the side of the post, then stands at the gate with her hands empty while the shade thins off the yard. The woman who does her shopping comes to the back doorway from the yard with a knotted scarf on her arm and does not untie it.

Scarf knotted on her arm, she filled the doorway, gaze travelling post to lane to post. The plum news came with the look, late ones colouring on the shaded bough, and the rooftop morning accounted for her outing with no word wasted on it.

After a while she loosed the scarf half its length and knotted it again and kept it on her arm, and then she went on down the yard with the scarf still on her arm and left the doorway empty.

---

One morning's work by the fencer put that post below the hollow in, with the foot charred and the earth rammed round it, and the gate went on last with old hinges gone dark. Hinges that have lost their sharp rims wear before they bend, which is why this gate still swings true after all its re-hanging.

Where the sharp rims have gone under the top hinge the leaf sits a hair lower, and the gate hangs a hair low latch-side and a hair high heel-side, inviting draughts beneath. Low-settled, the gate sloughs rail rain in tempests, sluicing a thin gully through dirt at the post foot.

The run at the foot of this post turns aside at the hollow, because the hollow stands a little open and takes the water into itself; what falls in there darkens the earth behind and never reaches the gully.

The oak of the post has gone coarse where the weather lifted the grain out of it, and the hollow walls with it where rot pocked them. The flint chip in the rammed earth is coarse of another kind, all chips and no fibre.

Dew gathers in the open grain of the shaded side and lies there until the sun reaches it, and every hour it lies it takes a little of the colour with it. A grain-raised face clears earliest after rain, while an oil-fed one keeps the wet along its base past noon.

South cheek coarse, hollow dim: the post's bright side outdries its shade, surrendering the crown while north boards keep their moss. Where banked earth climbs the post the moss stands tall, and short where the charring crowds the mouth. A moss mattress of two summers sits on that shaded cheek, looped so a thumbnail combs it and a finger snags nothing free.

Scrape a cushion open in spring and it knits again from the rim inward, the middle staying thin till autumn, and an old one can be told from a young one by that alone.

Once the dust of the yard pales the moss on that shaded cheek takes over, and the turn of June is the turn of the whole year for it.

Storm rain off the gate rail gathers above the post foot and pours together on the hollow. The hollow swallows the middle of the pour and the charred foot sheds the rest outward, and the rammed earth behind drinks till it darkens.

Above the hollow the post sheds its own wet down the cap and off, so the crown dries overhead while the foot stays damp below through winter. Soakings have fallen to this post since paint last touched the gate; the seat stands unshifted to a finger, and the grain drinks deep, winter-stored damp rising to meet summer rain halfway.

The gate has sunk its hinge by the depth of a coin after three winters. That fleck of paint, once level with the post from the yard painting, hangs a whisker lower now.

That fleck outlived its winter because the rail overlaps it, and nowhere else on the gate is there a second mark of any kind, which is the surest sign the leaf has come down straight and not cocked to one side.

---

No second fleck sits on this gate, and the one says the gate settles straight down. What sits at the back of that hollow is packed hard, dark earth with pale root hairs run through it, slack since the winter and stirring again before July comes.

A centipede works that foot in the dark, and the grit at the mouth of the packed earth goes loose under a fingertip without shifting the bank. A pale cast of that kind shows on the shaded face of the oak, and it has not been on that foot since the hinges were oiled.

This gate hangs true: rail ends square in their stiles, hinge leaves square on the post, and the brick foot square to the soil it stands in.

The rails between the posts span seven feet each, tenoned through and wedged with oak, the wedges dark with linseed. One wedge on the middle rail stands a hair proud, driven home last winter when the joint yawed in hard cold that yet shrank the timber. Footings of brickbats under each post foot spread the load, rough-broken ends locking in rammed earth. Paving slabs before the gate rock faintly on their sand when trodden at the corner, settling back with a sigh of grains. The rock has worn a crescent of polish on the sand bed, pale amid dark, marking the tread line.

The heel of the gate carries a brace cut on the angle, with the ends housed and the pins of oak driven through, and the lower pin has worked a hair proud where the gate swings. A pin proud like that takes the weather on its head and goes dark first, while the housed ends stay pale inside the timber.

The charred foot of the post runs rough where fire cracked it, and the brace pins run rough where the auger tore them, and the latch iron runs rough where rust blooms. Rough char and rough pins meet at the hollow mouth, and oil thumbed on the latch nose smooths one bright patch among the rough, a coin-sized shine hands renew every month. Latch shine shows where palms press, bright amid rough, deepening a shade each season.

She steps back to read the post along its length, gate shut to it and hollow keeping its earth below. A crown weathered rough sheds morning rain in runnels, and every runnel scrubs the oak cleaner, for loose meal washes off while tight grain endures.

The post stays where the diggers rammed it, the foot in its earth with the hollow at its heel, and the grass will seed at its toe though nobody in the house ever looks down there.
