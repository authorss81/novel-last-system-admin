# Chapter 0918 — Into The Post Hollow

Tuesday the eighteenth of June 2024, at the timber gate post at number nine from breakfast till the shade thins. The hollow at a post foot.

**SHE HAS A TIMBER GATE POST AT NUMBER NINE WITH A HOLLOW AT ITS FOOT AND SHE MEANS TO PRESS THE THUMB DEEP INTO THAT HOLLOW WITH FINGERS BRACED AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING IS AT THE BACK DOORWAY FROM THE YARD WITH A KNOTTED SCARF ON HER ARM.**

The gate post went in with the fence when the yard was divided, and the gate beside it has been re-hung once while the post has kept its seat.

It is a post of oak about five inches square, with the foot sunk in earth and a hollow eaten at its base where the rain stands, and grass roots at its heel where the broom does not reach.

She wants that hollow, where the timber meets the soil and the damp sits.

A post-foot hollow instructs beyond its crown: sun on the crown, earth fretting the foot.

So the thumb sinks deep in the post hollow braced.

The wood feels cool round the thumb, and the hollow walls catch where rot has softened them.

The thumb travels in past the first joint and meets a wad of earth rammed at the back, black and tight, studded with grit and a flint splinter lodged since the spade work years back.

She rolls the thumb across the wad from side to side, and the soil sinks a quarter inch under the pad and beds again on the way out.

The thumb meets its master in seasoned oak: deep-set heartwood, damp-darkened outside, tight-grained, rooted where it stands.

For all its depth the hollow proves blind. It goes a thumb deep with soil rammed at the back between, and the soil deadens the pull of the gate when the wind moves it.

She draws the thumb out of the hollow and wipes the soil off on the side of the post, and stands with the gate at her side while the shade thins off the yard.

The woman who does her shopping comes to the back doorway from the yard with a knotted scarf on her arm and does not untie it.

Scarf knotted on her arm, she filled the doorway, gaze travelling post to lane to post. The plum news came with the look, late ones colouring on the shaded bough, and the rooftop morning accounted for her outing with no word wasted on it.

After a while she loosed the scarf half its length and knotted it again and kept it on her arm, and then she went on down the yard with the scarf still on her arm and left the doorway empty.

---

One morning's work by the fencer put that post below the hollow in, with the foot charred and the earth rammed round it, and the gate went on last with old hinges gone dark.

Old hinges gone dark keep their swing on a gate the way bright work cannot, because they have lost their sharp rims years past and now wear before they bend.

Where the sharp rims have gone under the top hinge the leaf sits a hair lower, and the gate hangs a hair low latch-side and a hair high heel-side, inviting draughts beneath.

Low-settled, the gate sloughs rail rain in tempests, sluicing a thin gully through dirt at the post foot.

The run at the foot of this post turns aside at the hollow, because the hollow stands a little open and takes the water into itself.

So the open hollow guards its own seat by taking the wash, and the earth at the back drinks instead of letting it run.

The post oak runs coarse where weather lifted the grain, the hollow walls run coarse where rot pocked them, and the flint chip runs coarse where the hammer broke it.

Coarse timber spills the morning dew in beads, and each bead leaves the face a little barer.

A grain-raised face clears earliest after rain, while an oil-fed one keeps the wet along its base past noon.

South cheek coarse, hollow dim: the post's bright side outdries its shade, surrendering the crown while north boards keep their moss.

North-side moss stands tall where banked earth climbs the post and short where charring crowds the mouth.

Two summers of growth felt a moss mattress there, looped so a thumbnail combs it but a finger snags nothing free.

A cushion scraped open in spring knits again from its rims inward, leaving the middle thin till autumn, and the rim-first mending marks it as old growth.

Moss on the shaded side stays deep green after the yard dust has paled, and June reads the turn along the fence.

Storm rain off the gate rail gathers above the post foot and pours together on the hollow. The hollow swallows the middle of the pour and the charred foot sheds the rest outward, and the rammed earth behind drinks till it darkens.

Above the hollow the post sheds its own wet down the cap and off, so the crown dries overhead while the foot stays damp below through winter.

Soakings have fallen to this post since paint last touched the gate; the seat stands unshifted to a finger, and the grain drinks deep, winter-stored damp rising to meet summer rain halfway.

The gate has sunk its hinge by the depth of a coin after three winters. That fleck of paint, once level with the post from the yard painting, hangs a whisker lower now.

Paint flakes away after one winter, so the fleck surviving where the rail overlaps it proves the timber rim guards the paint. The gate shows no further mark of that kind; the lone fleck argues for a straight settlement.

---

No second fleck sits on this gate, and the one says the gate settles straight down.

Soil packed in the hollow lies dark, threaded with pale root hairs that slackened through winter and stir before July.

A centipede patrols the post foot year round, and grit at the earth mouth rolls loose under a fingertip.

Centipede work in a damp hollow shows first as a pale cast on the shaded face of the oak, and the cast has not been on that foot since the oil went on the hinges.

Gate rails butt stiles true, hinge leaves butt the post true out of the wet, and yard bricks butt soil true.

The rails between the posts span seven feet each, tenoned through and wedged with oak, the wedges dark with linseed. One wedge on the middle rail stands a hair proud, driven home last winter when the joint yawed in hard cold that yet shrank the timber. Footings of brickbats under each post foot spread the load, rough-broken ends locking in rammed earth. Paving slabs before the gate rock faintly on their sand when trodden at the corner, settling back with a sigh of grains. The rock has worn a crescent of polish on the sand bed, pale amid dark, marking the tread line.

The heel of the gate carries a brace cut on the angle, with the ends housed and the pins of oak driven through, and the lower pin has worked a hair proud where the gate swings. A pin proud like that takes the weather on its head and goes dark first, while the housed ends stay pale inside the timber.

The charred foot of the post runs rough where fire cracked it, and the brace pins run rough where the auger tore them, and the latch iron runs rough where rust blooms. Rough char and rough pins meet at the hollow mouth, and oil thumbed on the latch nose smooths one bright patch among the rough, a coin-sized shine hands renew every month. Latch shine shows where palms press, bright amid rough, deepening a shade each season.

She steps back to read the post along its length, gate shut to it and hollow keeping its earth below.

A crown weathered rough sheds morning rain in runnels, and every runnel scrubs the oak cleaner, for loose meal washes off while tight grain endures.

The post stays where the diggers rammed it, the foot in its earth with the hollow at its heel, and the grass will seed at its toe though the shade thins off the yard.
