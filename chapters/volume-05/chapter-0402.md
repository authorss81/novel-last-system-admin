# Chapter 0402 — Out of Square

Thursday the fifth of April 2018, from about ten in the morning until about five in the afternoon, a shop off that parade, in Tideglass.

**TWO MEN WENT INTO THE SAME LOCKED STORE ROOM ON THE SAME WEEK AND BOTH OF THEM LOOKED AT THE SAME BUNDLE ON THE SAME RACK AND BOTH OF THEM WORKED OUT THAT THE OTHER ONE HAD BEEN IN THERE, AND NEITHER OF THEM WAS RIGHT, AND THE WOMAN WHO WORKS IN THAT SHOP KNOWS EXACTLY WHY IT IS OUT OF SQUARE AND HAS BEEN ASKED BY NEITHER OF THEM.**

The shop is on that parade about sixty yard back from the front of it, and it sells building supplies, and it shuts at half past three on a weekday and at twelve on a Saturday, From about ten until about five she is on that counter, she shuts it herself, and she has had the keys to that store since the February before last.

Behind that counter there is a store about twelve foot by nine. It has a steel rack the length of one wall, a fire door in the corner, a broom, and a heap of offcuts in the corner by the fire door about two foot high.

On that rack there are about a hundred bundles of laths, banded in twelves, in two rows, the bottom row about four foot off the floor and the top row about six foot, and above the top row there is a shelf about eight foot up with about nine things on it that have not been moved since about 2014.

The band on every bundle is a strip of flat steel about half inch wide with a buckle, and the buckle is always turned to the front of the rack, because you cannot get at the back of it once it is in and it is turned by whoever stacked it.

---

The man in his forties went into that store on the Wednesday of the fourth of April.

He went in to get about six foot of a size he wanted. He went round the end of that counter and through the door and up to the top row with his own key, and she did not go out of her way and did not look up, because he has had that key about two years and it is not a thing anybody says anything about.

He had a piece of lath out and he was measuring it against the top of a bundle with a folding rule when he saw that one bundle was out of square.

He put the rule in his pocket and came out of that store at about ten past ten and went up the counter and paid for the lath and did not turn round at the till.

The bundle on the top of the third stack from the left, the one nearest the fire door, sat about two inches forward of the nine bundles below it and two inches to the right of it, and every other bundle on that rack is flush with the one under it, and the third stack from the left has been in the same place since before Christmas.

That band is not the way the other nine are. Its buckle sits at about two o'clock on the bundle instead of at six.

And underneath, on the top face of the bundle below, there is a clean rectangle about two foot by fifteen inch where the dust is not. The dust on that rack is about a quarter of an inch deep and grey.

He stood and looked at that for about a second and a half.

Then he carried on, and got his six foot, and went out along that counter and paid and said nothing to her.

The man in his thirties went into that store on the Friday of the sixth of April at about half past ten in the morning.

He had come in for a bag of cement. She said the cement is on the bottom row of the second rack and he went and got it himself, because she was on the phone to a trade counter about a delivery.

He came back up to the top row with the bag on his shoulder to look at a size of lath for a job on the Monday, and he put the bag down on the floor to get his hand free, and he saw it.

The third stack from the left, the one nearest the fire door, the top bundle out of square by about two inches, the band turned a quarter of a turn, and the clean rectangle on the dust on the bundle underneath.

He put his hand on the band and turned it back the way the other nine were turned, and he left the bundle where it was, forward, and he did not push it back, because pushing it back would be the other way round from what it was.

He noticed then that he had turned the band, and he left it turned. Putting it the other way would be a man admitting to himself that he had gone up there to look at a bundle, and nothing in the world gave him a reason to go up there looking at a bundle.

He took a bundle off the top of the fourth stack instead, which is one he has taken off a hundred times and which has a pencil mark across the end of it, and he went out, paid, and said nothing to her either.

Here is what either of them would have had to say out loud, and neither of them said it.

It is about the fourth of April and somebody has been into that store and has taken a bundle off the third stack and has put it back two inches out.

There are two people in this borough who come into that shop most weeks.

One of them works at the counter and does not move laths, because a man brings a barrow into that shop on Wednesdays and Fridays and moving a bundle off a rack to get at what is behind it has never been her work and she has not done it in two years.

The other one has a key.

So the one who is behind the counter is the one who did not do it.

---

It was her. Twice. On the question she has put it to neither of them, and neither of them has put it to her.

On the Monday of the second of April she was in that store for about ten minutes on her own, because the man who brings the laths in had left the second row half empty and she wanted to see what was behind it.

She took the top bundle off the third stack and set it on the floor, and she took a box of hinges out of the shelf space behind it that she wanted, and she put the bundle back.

She set it down about two inches forward and about two inches to the right, and the reason for both of those figures is that once a bundle is in on that rack there is no getting a foot behind it, and the only way to put one back without pulling the whole stack out is to let it down where your hand is.

She has done that maybe once a month for two years, and the band has come out turned that way every time, and she has never once thought about the band.

On the Wednesday of the fourth of April she did it again, because the man in his forties was up at that counter and she wanted him at the far end of it before she went through.

She did it while he was in that store himself. The fire door is on the other side of that store and he was at the far end of it at the rack with a rule in his hand, and she came in at the near end with the box under her arm, and neither of them stood in the other's way for longer than about ninety seconds, and she went out the way she had come in.

Neither of the two of them has ever asked her whether anybody has been in that store. Asked, she would say that it is my shop and that the laths go where the man puts them.

Not one of the two is going to be the one to say it out loud.

The man in his forties has a key and has had one for two years and has never once used it to look at anything in that shop, and to say out loud in there that he has been in that store and that something on that rack is out of square is to tell a woman who works at that counter that he goes in and looks.

The man in his thirties has no key, has not been offered one, and does not want one, and telling her he spotted a bundle two inches forward on her rack is a way of telling her he was up on that top row holding nothing at all and having come for nothing.

So it sits there.

Both of them have a reason to believe the other one has been in a locked room, and both of them are wrong, and the woman who works in that shop has the whole of the answer and does not know that either of them is looking for it, and that bundle is on that rack two inches forward with its buckle at two o'clock, and it will stay forward until she moves it again.

**THE THIRD STACK FROM THE LEFT IS STILL OUT OF SQUARE ON THE TOP OF THAT RACK, AND THE BAND ON IT IS STILL TURNED THE WRONG WAY, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO MEN WILL BE THE ONE WHO SAYS SO.**
