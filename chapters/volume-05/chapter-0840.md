# Chapter 0840 — Level Under Boards

Tuesday the twenty-ninth of August 2023, on the path at number ten from midday until the light thins, and a hollow under boards in gravel is the whole of that Tuesday.

**THERE IS A HOLLOW IN THE GRAVEL ON THE PATH AT NUMBER TEN UNDER BOARDS AND SHE MEANS TO RAKE IT LEVEL WITHOUT DIGGING, AND HER SISTER STANDS AT THE YARD GATE WITH A BLANKET OVER HER ARM AND DOES NOT COME THROUGH IT.**

That path is the run of gravel from the yard gate to the shed, with boards laid along it where the barrow goes, to keep the wheels out of the soft.

One board rocks. Under the middle of it the gravel has sunk into a hollow the size of a basin, shallow at the edges and a hand deep in the middle, where rain off the shed has run under the boards all summer.

She does not dig it. A hollow in tired ground on a path wants filling from the sides and not cutting from below, and cutting below a hollow makes a second hollow under the first.

Raking level is done with a rake, a brush, and a bucket of spare gravel from the heap by the fence.

She lifts the boards off first, three of them, and lays them along the grass. Dust rises off the gravel where they were, pale, with the lines of the boards pressed into it.

The hollow shows once the boards are up. The gravel in it sits loose and dry at the top, with dust between the stones, and firm further down where feet have pressed it over years.

She does not break that firm part. A shallow fix works the loose top and leaves what is under it alone.

She rakes the loose gravel from the high sides into the hollow, a pull at a time, brushing after each pull to see the line. The rake lines show where she has been, fine grooves in the dust, and where the grooves run straight the ground under them is near true.

Her sister stands at the yard gate through the middle of it with a blanket over her arm, folded, for airing.

“Leave the gate open, I am only here a minute,” her sister tells her.

“It is open and staying so,” she tells her, and the sister stays at the gate with the blanket, out of the dust, and goes back out with it still over her arm.

The gate is the yard gate of number ten, iron, standing open as it stood in the morning, and the sister stays beyond the iron the whole while with the blanket folded, and the path is never spoken of.

She thinks about the boards while she rakes, how they keep wheels out of soft ground without themselves belonging to the path. Boards are guests that do the work of hosts, laid down for barrows and lifted for raking, and the gravel under them is the true path that must run true whether boards are on it or not.

Sweat pricks her forehead in the midday heat off the gravel, and the rake handle slides in her palms until she wipes them on her skirt and takes a fresh grip.

Board down is the test. A board that lies without rocking on raked gravel will carry a barrow, and a board that rocks will rock worse under weight.

She lays the middle board back over the hollow and puts a foot on each end in turn. The first time it ticks. She lifts it, pulls two more rakes of gravel into the low spot, brushes, and lays it again.

The second time it sits. She lays the other two alongside it, overlapping at the ends the way they were, and walks all three with the barrow empty.

The barrow runs without a dip now, wheels on boards, boards on gravel that gives nowhere.

She brushes the surplus dust off the boards and back into the gravel, and tips half the spare bucket into the hollow edges where the rake had thinned the top.

---

The boards stay where they were, the rake goes against the shed, and the spare bucket goes back half full.

She stands at the gate end of the path when the light thins and looks back at what is there. A path level under boards with rake lines in the gravel round them, and the boards lying without rocking where the barrow goes.
