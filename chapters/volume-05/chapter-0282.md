# Chapter 0282 — Not Claiming It

Wednesday the twenty-first of December 2016, from about a quarter to eight in the evening, a hall off a parade in Tideglass.

**THE SECOND PIECE OF PAPER CAME THROUGH THE SAME DOOR AS THE FIRST ONE, AND THE WOMAN WITH THE KEY HANDLED IT THE OTHER WAY ROUND, AND IT WENT NOWHERE AS WELL, AND A CUPBOARD DOOR WENT DOWN A BACK LANE ON A TROLLEY.**

The stair on the left of that hall goes up to a landing with a fire door on it and a door on the landing, and the light over the foot of that stair is a batten fitting with two tubes in it, and one of those tubes has been out since the summer.

On the Wednesday evening the eighth of December a man had come and put a new one in and had done a test on the one that was out and had told the woman of about forty-two with the key to that hall that it would do another six months, and that the fitting wanted doing in the spring, and she had said that would be in the spring.

The outside light over the door of that hall has not worked since about 2011 and the switch by it turns and nothing comes of it, and on the Wednesday night of this week a man was up a ladder against it with a tester in one hand and had a sheet out of his coat.

The cupboard under that stair has stood open and empty since the beginning of November, and the door that came off it in November is still lying face down on the boards on the wall side, and nobody has picked it up, and it is the first thing anybody coming down that stair in the dark puts their foot on.

At about half past eight on the Wednesday night a woman came down that stair in the dark and put her foot on the end of it and went over her ankle and sat on the bottom step and swore at the ceiling, and the man in his sixties picked the door up off the boards and stood it against the wall at the side of the cupboard so that it was not in the way, and nobody in that hall said one word about it, then or afterwards.

---

The man on the ladder came down at about ten past nine with a sheet, and he put the sheet on the table face up, the way things get put down in that hall.

It was from a firm of electrical contractors and not from the firm of heating engineers that had put the other one there in November.

It was two sides. The top one had the firm's name, a reference, the date, and a figure of two hundred and sixty pounds set against the words fixed wire test and making good, and underneath that a line that ran through the emergency light, the outside light and four sockets in that hall. The whole of the bottom half of it was empty except for a printed line along the foot, and the printed line was for a name and an account, and neither of the two was filled in by anybody.

The first line on the top side said a test could not be booked until it had been confirmed in writing, and there was no such thing in that room as a writing to confirm it in, and nobody in that room had asked anybody for a test.

"That is a lot of money to look at two lights," said the man from the parade.

"It's not for the lights," said the man in his forties. "It's for a piece of paper that says the electrics in here are sound. That's what that is. Everybody wants that bit of paper and nobody in this country has ever asked what it is for until the day somebody wants to move a class of kids into a room in a building they have never been inside."

"There's nobody going to move a class of kids into that room," said the woman of about fifty-five.

"There's nobody going to move a class of kids into any room," said the man in his forties, "and that is not a reason to hand a man two hundred and sixty pound for saying the wiring's all right."

The man on the ladder said that was exactly what it was for, and that he had not come round to look at anything, and that he had put a date on it because he was up that ladder anyway and because somebody had told him in November that a firm had put a figure on a piece of paper in that address and had not come, and that was a bit of a thing to be told, and that he had come because of it. He said that he was not able to say the hall was tested without doing it, and that he was not able to do it without a name, and that he was going to put the sheet where he had put the other one and go.

The letting agent came at about ten to nine on the Wednesday and was let in by the man from the parade because it is a hall and the door is on a catch.

He was in because of a shop two doors along, which that company does own, and which has been empty since the summer, and which he has a set of keys for. He came in out of the rain, and he took his coat off, and he was pleasant, and he asked whether that room was let.

"Nothing to let," said the man in his forties.

"It could be," said the letting agent. "That's a good room. It's got a stair, it's got a meter, it's got a fire door, and for what that costs the one I looked at on the Marshmont side you'd have the lot and a car park. I could put it on in a fortnight. I'd want a fixed wire test first, because I couldn't let a room to anybody without one, and there is one of those hanging about on that table with a figure on it and nobody's name at the bottom of it."

The room did the arithmetic out loud, which took about the time of a cup of tea, and the arithmetic was the same one it did in November: a company in a city over the water with a letterhead and no number anybody here has rung, a trust wound up in about 1991, a meter the woman with the key pays for out of her own money, and a hall that nobody rents because nobody can be shown round it.

"You could let it," said the letting agent. "If somebody could sign a lease."

"There is nobody to sign a lease," said the woman who has the key to that hall. "That is the whole of it. And before you say anything else, I am going to say the thing I said in November and I am going to say it in the same words, because I have thought about it for a month and I have not found better ones."

She said: "I am not having that test done, and I am not refusing it, and I want the room to hear the two of those. I am telling you both now so that whichever one of them somebody in this room goes off to remember afterwards, they have heard the other one as well. I will not be able to tell you which of the two I meant, and that is not a trick, that is what it is."

Nobody asked her which one she meant. The man in his forties said that he had asked the same question in November and that nobody had asked him either.

"It isn't the right way round," said the man in his forties. "What you should have said was, I am not having it done, and leave it at that, and the sheet goes in the bin."

"The bin's a firm with a job number on it," said the woman of about forty-two, "and I have read one of them, and it is not the bin."

The man on the ladder went at about half past ten. The letting agent went at about a quarter to ten and put a card on the table with the name of his firm on it and gave his mobile to nobody in particular, and the sheet with two hundred and sixty pounds at the top of it went on the table face up, next to the meter on the wall, and it was still on that table at midnight.

Nobody put their name on it. Nobody rang the firm that sent it, because there is no number on it. Nobody rang the mobile.

At about a quarter past ten the man in his sixties said he had to go and get his car because it was going to rain again, and that his car was in that direction anyway, and nobody asked him about any part of that and he had not put any of it to anybody.

He came back in with the folding platform trolley out of the boot, which he has never used for anything anybody paid him for, and nobody remarked on that either. He laid the cupboard door flat on the platform in the doorway, wheeled it out and down the step and away down the back lane of that parade, and stood it up against the outside wall of that hall under the pipe, since nobody had said where that door belonged, and he was not going to leave it lying in a lane.

**AND ON THE THIRTIETH OF DECEMBER THAT DOOR WAS STILL STANDING AGAINST THE OUTSIDE WALL OF THAT HALL UNDER THE PIPE WITH THE RAIN RUNNING DOWN THE OUTSIDE OF IT AND NOBODY ON THAT PARADE LOOKING AT IT, AND A MAN WALKED DOWN THAT BACK LANE ON CHRISTMAS EVE WITH A DOG AND WENT ROUND IT WITHOUT GOING NEAR IT, AND ON THE THURSDAY A LADLE OF RAIN GOT IN BEHIND IT AND IT CAME OFF THE WALL AND DOWN ON THE PAVING SLABS, WHERE IT IS.**
