# Chapter 0351 — The Bulb Goes

Sunday the tenth of September 2017, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past five in the afternoon, a kitchen at the back of a parade, a first-floor corridor in a block by the water, and a low wall by a bus shelter, in Tideglass.

**THE BULB IN THE CEILING OF THAT KITCHEN WENT AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MORNING AND THE MAN WHO COOKS IN IT HAS BEEN BUYING THE WRONG BULB SINCE THE MIDDLE OF JULY, AND THE ONLY PERSON IN THAT ROOM WHO KNEW WHICH ONE IT TOOKS STOOD AT THE SINK AND SAID NOTHING, AND AT HALF PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON HE PUT A TORCH DOWN ON A LOW WALL BY A BUS SHELTER AND WALKED AWAY FROM IT.**

The kitchen is reached off the parade by a passage and then by a door with a wire strung across the bottom of it where the flags have gone, and inside it there is a sink under the window, four burners, two doors on the long side, and a table with its edge rubbed pale where half a century of elbows have gone round it, and there is a man who cooks in it. The lad does that kitchen's washing up on four mornings out of the seven and has done since the year before last.

The bulb in that ceiling is a bayonet cap and it takes about sixty watts, and it has a fitting with a ring and two pins and a sprung catch that has to be pushed in and turned.

There are about four shops in this borough that sell a bulb and all four of them sell the small screw cap.

He has been up to the shop off the parade about six times since the middle of July, on a Saturday, about four miles out of his way, and he has stood in each of them holding up a small screw bulb in its packet, and each time he has been told there is not one in with a bayonet on it and not one coming, and he has bought the screw one anyway because the light was out, and put it in, and it has not worked, and the room has been lit by a lamp on the table since the middle of July.

He has never once said the word bayonet in that kitchen.

---

The bulb went at about half past ten on the Sunday morning.

The lad was at the sink with a cloth still in his hand. He had known since the Saturday before last that it was going, and he had looked at it on that Saturday and thought about saying something on the Monday, and he had not said it on the Monday, and he had not said it on any Monday since, and there have been four of them.

The man who cooks in that kitchen came in at about half past ten and put a cup down on the table and went into the cold room at the back and came back and put the light on, which he does about nine times a day, and the room went the colour of a room with no bulb in it, and the lamp on the table was about four foot away and threw a circle about six foot across on the boards.

"That's the bulb," said the man who cooks in that kitchen.

The woman who eats there twice a week was in the chair at the end of the bench with a newspaper and she said nothing at all about it, and the man at the table said that nobody sits in a room for a fortnight with a lamp on a table, and the man who cooks in that kitchen said he had been up to a shop off the parade about six times since the middle of July, and the man at the table said that was a thing, and the man who cooks in that kitchen said it was, and the man at the table said aye.

That was all the talk about it and it took the length of a minute.

Nobody in that kitchen asked the lad anything. Nobody has ever asked him anything in that kitchen. He stood at the sink with the cloth in his hand for about ten seconds after the light went, and the towel on the rail beside him was dry and there was nothing to wipe, and he put the cloth down and wiped a plate that had been dry for about an hour.

The man who cooks in that kitchen went to the cupboard he keeps the spares in, and the spares are three screw bulbs of about sixty watts and one of about half that, and he put the small one in the wrong fitting to see, and it did not go in past the ring.

He said the word for what that is, and then he said, "I've had a look at the back of it," meaning the fitting, meaning the pins, and the lad was at the sink and did not look round, and the man who cooks in that kitchen said, "It's one of the old ones," and shut the cupboard.

He has not asked the lad. The lad has not told him. The three screw bulbs are still in that cupboard and the man who cooks in that kitchen has a lamp on a table under a bulb that is not lit.

He went up the hill at about twenty to one with his bag, the way he goes four days a week, and on a Sunday as well, because he has gone up to that block on the Sundays as well since about June and not one person living in it has ever put a question to him about that day.

The landing is bare concrete and a shade over the length of two doors, and the glass at the end of it has not been washed since before the year. He had the bag on his shoulder and he put his hand into it and took out the torch and then he put his hand back in and did not take it out, and that is the whole of what happened in that bag that afternoon.

The woman of about fifty-five was in that corridor with a jug in each hand at about half past one and she stopped him, and the last words between them had been in August about nothing at all.

"Where are you going to put it," said the woman of about fifty-five.

He looked at her for a second.

"I don't know yet," he said.

"Right," said the woman of about fifty-five, and went on down past him carrying both, and the door at the bottom of that stair came back on its own behind her, and she put nothing to him about where it was going and used nothing he had told her, and she has not raised it since.

She has not asked him why he came up on a Sunday and he has not told her, and neither of them is going to.

---

He was back in that kitchen at about half past two and the light was still only the lamp, and he was at that kitchen door until about four because he had to finish.

There is a low wall by the bus shelter at the top of that road, about two foot high and about nine foot long, with a rail on top of it, and it is the wall people lean on at that stop and it is not anybody's.

He put the torch down on the top of that wall at about half past four in the afternoon, on its side, with the lens facing the road, and it lay there and it was not raining and it was about as good a place as he could find for a thing he did not want.

He did not put anything on it. He left no note and not a word of it has gone past him to anybody, and there is nobody on that road at half past four on a Sunday except two men at the stop and neither of them looked at a torch on a wall.

The man who cooks in that kitchen was told about it at about half past six that evening, by a man who eats there, who had been told by the woman who eats there twice a week, and what the man who cooks in that kitchen said was that if it was that sort of thing then the bulb could have had it in July and he would not be sat there with a lamp on a table, and nobody answered him, and he did not ask where it had been left.

The woman with a key caught the bus at that stop at about ten past five that evening and was stood at the end of the wall with her bag at her feet about nine foot from a black rubber torch lying on its side with the lens facing the road.

She looked at it. She was at that stop about four times a week and had never seen it before and has not seen it since.

She did not pick it up. There was a bus every half hour and the next one was in about six minutes and she could not have carried a torch the length of a pavement for no reason and put it down again, and there is no version of a woman standing at a bus stop with a torch in her hand that ends well.

The torch is on that wall. It was there on the Monday, and on the Tuesday, and it is there now, and the man who cooks in that kitchen has a lamp on a table under a bulb with a bayonet cap that will not take a screw bulb, and the lad washed up four mornings a week and has said nothing about it to anybody.

**A TORCH IS LYING ON A LOW WALL AT THE TOP OF A ROAD WITH THE LENS FACING THE TRAFFIC AND A LAD HAS WALKED DOWN THE HILL PAST IT, AND A BULB IN A CEILING FOUR PARADES OFF WENT OUT AT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MORNING AND WILL NOT BE REPLACED.**
