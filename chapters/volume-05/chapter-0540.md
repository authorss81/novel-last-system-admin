# Chapter 0540 — The Sentence Said on the Wrong Day

The Monday of the sixth of January 2020, and the middle of the afternoon, in a house on a street in this borough.

**THERE IS A SENTENCE HE HAS BEEN SAYING AT HER FRONT DOOR ONCE A YEAR SINCE 2009 AND SHE HAS GIVEN HIM NOTHING BACK FOR IT AND HE HAS NEVER ASKED HER FOR ANYTHING, AND IN JANUARY HE PUT A SECOND SENTENCE AFTER THE FIRST ONE FOR THE FIRST TIME IN ALL THE YEARS OF IT, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS RAISED EITHER, AND ON THE MONDAY HE CAME AND PUT THE FIRST ONE INTO THE AIR AGAIN.**

They have been married for the best part of fifty years. He is a man in his late seventies and she is a woman in her mid seventies, and he has been in a flat of his own about a mile away since the November of 2018, and before that he was in the spare end of this house for six years and before that he was in this house for thirty-nine.

The sentence is this: *Whatever you decide, it is all right with me.*

Once a year, on that date, since 2009. Never on the second, never twice, never on any other day at all. He says it standing at the front of the house, usually while he is taking his jacket off, and then the two of them have the rest of the afternoon.

She has never answered it. Not once, in any of those years. She has not said no and she has not said yes and she has not said nothing deliberately, and there is a difference between the three of those things which only the two of them have ever been in a position to draw.

She has been asked about it. Not by him. She has been asked about it by nobody.



Why it is the first of January is not a thing either of them could explain to anybody, and she has been asked that too, twice, by people who meant well.

The nearest anybody has got to it is that something finished in December of 2008, and that it took from January to April in the year after, and that he decided in the December of 2008 that whatever she was going to decide about it in the new year, he would be the first person in the house to say that it did not matter.

She decided nothing in the new year of 2009. She has never said one way or the other. She has been at the same house, at the same times, with the same man, for the whole of the time since.



What happened on the first of January is this.

He came at about two in the afternoon. He had come at about two in the afternoon on the first of January for years. She made the tea. He took his jacket off and hung it on the back of the thing by the door where it goes, and there was about a minute and a half of the two of them not saying anything, which is how that afternoon has always started.

And he said, "Whatever you decide, it is all right with me."

And she said, "It is all right with me."

Which is not an answer. It is not a refusal either. He has never pressed it and he was not going to start on the first of January 2020.

And then he did not sit down.

That is the part she has since gone back over. In all the years of saying that sentence in that house he has always sat down within about a minute of saying it, because sitting down is what the two of them do afterwards, and this time he stayed on his feet and put his hands in front of him and looked at the fire, which was not lit, and then he said:

"And I have said that to you every year since 2009 and you have never once told me whether it is."

And she said, "You have not asked me."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Then that is why."

And he said, "I know why."

And then he sat down.



That is the whole of the first of January 2020 and there has not been a word about any of it in five days.

They have spoken on the phone twice. Once on the second and once on the fourth. Both times about something else: about the collection from the place at the end of the terrace, and about whether the man in the flat below wants somebody to go to the shops on the Thursday.

He has not telephoned to ask whether she has thought about it. She is entirely sure he will not, and she has thought about that on four occasions since Tuesday.

And then on Monday the sixth of January he came to her door at about half past two.



She was in the kitchen when she heard it and she went to the front door and he was on the step with nothing in his hands.

"You did not come on Friday," he said.

"You said you would not come on Friday."

"I did not say I would come at all."

"You did not."

He came in. He put his jacket on the back of the thing by the door where it goes. She went and made the tea and brought it in and put it on the surface by him and sat down in her own place.

He did not say anything for about four minutes. She did not fill it, because she has never filled it.

Then he said, "Whatever you decide, it is all right with me."

And she said, "It is all right with me."

And he drank his tea.

She asked him afterwards whether he had heard from his sister. She asks him this about twice a year and the answer has been the same for two years, which is no.

He said no. She said that she thought he ought to ring her. He said that he knew. She said that she had said that at Christmas. He said that he knew that as well.

Then there was another four minutes, and then he said, "Is there anything you want from the town," and she said that there was not, and he said all right, and he put his jacket back on and went, at about half past four.



What has happened in this house since the first of January is that a sentence has been said twice in six days and it has been answered twice in the same words both times, and that a second sentence has been said once, and that she has said two things back to it and neither of them was an answer.

What has not happened is that anybody has mentioned it.

She has not rung him. He has not rung her about it. She has not asked her sister whether anybody says things like that in the first week of January and she has not asked anybody at all.

There is one other thing about the six days, and it is the thing that neither of them would call a thing if it were described.

On the second of January, which was the Thursday, a woman in her early sixties at the end of the terrace knocked at her door and asked her to sign for a delivery going to the house next along, which she did, and the woman said, "You all right, you look tired," and she said she was fine.

On the third, the man in the flat below came up to say that the collection was still on the Tuesday and not on the Thursday, which she had already told him on the phone on the second. He came up on purpose about something he already knew.

On the fourth she was on the phone to him, and he was the one who brought up the collection first, because he had not been listening on the second, and then they talked for about six minutes and neither of them mentioned the sentence, and she said afterwards that she had thought about saying it and had decided not to, and she could not afterwards have told anybody why she had decided.

On the fifth she did nothing at all with the day, which is a thing she does about twice a week, and on the sixth he was on her step.

What none of that adds up to is anybody saying anything. It is the whole shape of that week, and it is not a shape she would put a name to even to herself, and she would not describe it to her sister if her sister asked her how she was, and her sister has not asked her how she is, because her sister asks her that on a Sunday.

On Monday evening, after he had gone, she washed the two cups she had used and put them away, and she did not wipe the surface, because she had not wiped it on any of the other days he had come, and because it was not that kind of day.

**HE SAID IT AT HALF PAST TWO ON THE MONDAY, WHICH IS NOT THE DAY HE SAYS IT ON, AND SHE ANSWERED IT IN THE SAME WORDS SHE HAS ANSWERED IT IN SINCE 2009, AND THE SECOND SENTENCE SAID ON THE FIRST OF JANUARY HAS NOW BEEN IN THAT HOUSE FOR FIVE DAYS AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS RAISED IT.**
