# Chapter 0892 — A Finger's Width Of Grout

Wednesday the thirteenth of March 2024, at the frame of the drain set in the concrete at the unit, from the time the flags have dried where the sun has been until the yard has gone dark. Into the crack round a cast frame, a finger at a time.

**HE HAS A CAST DRAIN FRAME SET INTO THE CONCRETE AT THE WALL END OF THAT YARD WITH ITS GRATE IN IT AND THE CONCRETE AROUND IT ONE PIECE, AND HE MEANS TO WALK THE FINGERS OF ONE HAND INTO THE CRACK ROUND THAT FRAME A FINGER AT A TIME UNTIL A FINGER WILL NOT GO, AND THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN IS AT THE STORE DOOR WITH THE DOOR SHUT BEHIND HER.**

That frame is the drain of the store roof.

It is worth saying what that frame is not, because the two get confused. It is not the same thing as a hole in the ground, and it does not go down into a chamber anybody can get at. It is a frame on a pipe, and the pipe goes under the yard and out, and the whole of it is below a foot of concrete.

Everything that comes off the felt on that shed goes down the pipe at the corner and across the concrete and into that frame, and it has been doing that since the yard was laid.

He had looked at it in the autumn because the concrete round it had a line in it, and had decided the line was a crack, and had gone away again.

The line is a finger wide, all the way round, and it is no crack at all.

A crack is where two things have parted. What is round that frame is a joint, and a finger goes down into it and stays in it, which is the difference a finger can feel and an eye cannot.

---

The woman from two doors down comes to the store door from outside about four in the afternoon with the door shut behind her, and she stands on the step.

She wanted the gutter on her side looked at while he was there, because it overflows every time it rains hard enough, and she has had a bucket under it since Christmas.

He told her the gutter itself would be sound and that it is the downpipe that is letting go, and that a downpipe coming away at the hopper puts more water into a gutter than the gutter was ever built to take.

She told him the downpipe was new in the autumn and could not be letting go.

He told her it could be a joint that has opened behind the plaster and not the pipe itself.

She put that as the same as somebody wanting to look at it, and he let it stand, and she went up the path.

The first finger goes down into the joint round that frame and goes in about half an inch and then stops, because the joint is a finger wide and a finger is a finger wide.

The second finger goes in beside it, and it goes in the same half inch, and the joint is exactly that all the way round.

That is the first answer, and it takes a moment to see.

A crack round something standing on the ground opens where the ground moves and closes where the ground does not, and one of the two ends of it is always tighter than the other. A joint that is the same width at four corners was not made by ground and concrete arguing. It was made by a person with a trowel.

So the frame is not lying on the concrete. It is in the concrete.

A cast frame with a flange is meant to be let into a hole cut for it, with the flange sitting on the ground at the bottom of that hole and the concrete poured round the top of the flange, and then the last inch of the gap filled in with the back of a trowel.

And the finger goes in that inch and finds the top of the flange, two inches below the surface of the concrete, running the whole way round with the grout over it.

He walks the fingers round the four sides of that frame to find out whether it was done the whole way, and it takes some time, because getting round the corner of a cast frame with a finger in the joint means lifting the finger out and setting it down again twice.

On the north side the joint runs a finger's width for two feet and then shuts to nothing across the last foot, where the frame runs up against the brickwork.

On the east side it runs the full length of the frame at a finger's width, and the grout in it has gone the colour of a stone.

On the south side it is the same, and this is where the corner pipe discharges, and the grout there has been washed and scoured by everything that has gone down that pipe since the autumn.

And on the west side it is a finger's width for about a foot and a half, and then the fourth finger will not go in.

Not because the joint has shut. Because the concrete there is in two pieces.

There is a line across that yard about four feet from the brick wall, running the full width of the yard, and where that line comes round the frame the joint is different on the two sides of it.

To the east of the line the grout is a shade lighter and harder than the grout to the west of it.

Two buckets of grout, two days, and the same job.

And the fourth finger will not go past the lip on the west side, because the concrete on that side of the line stands about a sixteenth higher than the concrete on the other side of it, and a lip of a sixteenth is exactly where a finger stops.

That is the only place on that frame where a finger is refused, and nothing about the frame is wrong.

The concrete was poured in two goes and one of them came up a sixteenth above the other, which is the ordinary way round a pour of concrete.

One more thing about that joint, and it is the reason it was filled at all.

A frame let into concrete leaves a finger's width of gap round the top of it, and that gap is a hole in the yard about the width of your finger going down to the frame. Everything that washes across a yard goes into it: grit off the concrete, leaves off the line, the fine stuff off the store roof, all of it.

The first winter the joint takes all of that and keeps it. The second winter the grit in it goes hard and the grit behind it goes hard too, and the joint stops being a hole and becomes a filled joint, and from then on it is stronger than the concrete either side of it.

That is what a joint round a frame in a yard is meant to become, and it is why nobody cuts a frame out of a yard without breaking the concrete round it first.

That grout has gone hard in the gap and gone hard behind itself, and it is a finger's width wide and it is going nowhere.

None of it wants doing.

The grout is hard and sound and not cracked anywhere, the concrete round it is sound, and the frame is sound cast iron with the casting marks still on the top face where the mould left them.

The yard falls along its length to that frame, and rises across its width to the brick wall, and those are two different falls and they have been there since the yard was made, and everything that comes off that shed goes down the corner pipe and into that frame because of the first of them and not the second.

That frame will lift out with a bar in ten minutes if it ever has to lift out, and it has nothing to lift out for, and by the day anybody does want it out the concrete will have gone to pieces round it and the grout will have gone with the concrete.
