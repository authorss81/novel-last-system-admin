# Chapter 0229 — As Agreed

Tuesday the twenty-second of March 2016, from about eleven in the morning, a first-floor corridor in a building by the water in Tideglass, and a car park behind the parade.

**NOBODY IS BEING CHEATED. THE SUM ON THE CLAIM IS THE SUM THE REGION AGREED IN 1987, THE WORK WAS DONE FOR THAT SUM THEN, AND EVERY JOB SINCE HAS BEEN DONE FOR THAT SUM, AND THE RATE HAS NOT BEEN REVISED BECAUSE NOBODY WHO IS OWED THE DIFFERENCE IS STILL IN ANY OF THE BODIES THAT AGREED IT.**

The telephone call took four minutes and Jonas Mercer made it from the corridor outside the room at the end of the first-floor corridor, standing with his back to the window, because the room was shut and it was not his room and he was not going to sit in it.

He asked the man in his forties why 1987. He expected to have to ask it twice and he had it in his head written out with a question mark against it, and he did not have to ask it twice.

"Nineteen eighty-seven," the man said. "That's when it was set. I know. Everybody asks that, everybody asks the number first, everybody always wants the year, and the year is the easy part because the year is just a year." There was a noise of a kettle behind him. "The number's the rate for that work and the rate for that work hasn't been touched since the sheet was agreed, and it isn't going to be touched, and I can tell you exactly why and it's not because we're all friends here and it isn't because anybody's nice. It's because revising a rate needs somebody who is short when it doesn't change. That's it. That's the whole mechanism. To move a number you need a person who loses. There is no person who loses. Not one. There isn't a body in this arrangement with a department that would have to find the money. Everybody who agreed 1987 is either dead or has left or has moved on to the next thing, and the next thing took about nine years, so there's nobody left in that seat. So the number sits."

"So the work's being done cheap."

"No, listen to me." The man sounded almost pleased. "That's the bit everybody gets wrong when they come off the train. The work's not being done cheap, the work's being done *at the agreed rate*, and the agreed rate is old, and old is not the same as wrong. It isn't anybody's fault and there's nobody to have it out with. If the pool's short the pool's short and the rate still stands, because a rate that moved when the pool was short would be a rate that could be moved by anybody with a bad month, and then you'd have a rate and not a rule, and a rate is a business."

"And the person who does the work is paid the going rate, not this figure."

"Paid by whoever they work for, at the going rate, and that's the wages, and the wages are wages, that bit's ordinary. This figure — " he tapped something — "that's not the wages. That's not the job. That's what's left over after the wages, and the parts, and the fuel, and the hire, and it goes in the pool, and the pool is what's left, and the pool has been short most months for years and the rate still stands."

"So the people in the yard know the year is old."

"The people in the yard have known the year was old since about 1994," said the man, "and nobody's ever asked us what happens when the year is old, and there's a reason for that as well, and the reason is that there's nobody to ask. It's not that we won't say. It's that a question about what happens when the year is old is a question to a body, and there's no body, and if you put it in this office it just sits on the desk next to the calendar with the picture of the river on it."

At about half past eleven Hanna Wray came into the shop with a carrier bag and put it on the counter and said she had brought them and that if the woman with the ruler wanted them back she could have them back.

There were eleven.

Nell Ardent laid them out in two rows on the counter with the jobs uppermost and the sums underneath, and Jonas Mercer read the sums out loud, which is a thing you do and not a thing anybody does.

"A hundred and forty. A hundred and fifty-two. A hundred and sixty-three. A hundred and seventy-one. A hundred and sixty-eight. A hundred and seventy-four. A hundred and twelve. A hundred and eighty. A hundred and sixty-six. A hundred and ninety. A hundred and ninety-six."

"That's a good week," said Hanna Wray, going. "You should see a bad one."

He added it up twice on the back of an order pad. It came to one thousand eight hundred and twelve pounds, and it came to the same thing twice.

Hanna Wray had been keeping those eleven since the August before last and had never once looked at them as a group, and not one of them was for a job that anybody in that shop would call a bad one, and the biggest of them was under two hundred pounds, and the figure on all eleven of them had been set in 1987 by bodies that have all been other things since.

"Take them," said Nell Ardent.

"You take them," he said. "They're in your bag and they came out of hers and I'd rather you had them."

She looked at him for a second.

"That's not a thing to say to a person who copies things out in her own hand," she said.

"Then I won't say it again," said Jonas Mercer, and went out into the corridor.

---

The corridor is about twenty feet and it has a window at one end and three doors and a fire door, and a tray under the window that is a tray and has a chipped corner on it, and it is empty.

He stood at the window and did the other half of it, and the other half of it is the whole of what he has come here to do, and it took about six minutes.

The sum is small. A thousand eight hundred and twelve pounds since the spring is not money that anybody in two countries has gone without and it is not a scandal and it is not a fraud and no single person in it has been wronged in a way anybody could put in a room. Nobody is being cheated. That half is true and it is the half that everybody has, including him, on a good day, and it is the reason nobody has ever done anything about it.

The paper is correct. Every part of every one of the eleven is correct and it would survive anybody who was careful with it, and that is not a flaw in it, that is what a correct document is.

And so it took him the rest of that to get to the thing, and he did it by taking the tray under the window off the sill and putting it back, twice, which is what a man does with his hands while he is not using them.

The drain is neither of those. It is that every job a crew from this borough does in that region puts a claim against this borough, and there is nobody in this borough to answer it, nobody to measure it, nobody to refuse it and nobody to plan for it, and the arrangement that does it is entirely voluntary and was joined by people who wanted to join it and who are still members of it and who think well of it. One claim. That is the whole shape of it. Hanna Wray can put eleven of them in a carrier bag and carry them home and hand them to somebody who is owed nothing, because there is nobody they are owed to. A person who is hurt on a job over there comes back and there is no one to go to, because the sheet on the wall says a person has no standing in respect of anything recorded under this, and that line is not there because anybody put it there as a trick. It is there so that the yard can never be a business. And a borough that sends crews into an arrangement that works that way has agreed, without a meeting and without a signature, that it has no counterparty.

**AND NOBODY IN TWO COUNTRIES WAS STANDING BEHIND ANY OF IT, AND HE HAD FOUND NOBODY, AND THE TWO HALVES OF IT HAD BEEN IN DIFFERENT HANDS SINCE THE SPRING BOTH OF THEM, AND HE WAS THE FIRST PERSON IN EITHER COUNTRY TO PUT THEM NEXT TO EACH OTHER AND THE ONLY PERSON WHO WAS IN A POSITION TO, AND HE DID IT IN A FIRST-FLOOR CORRIDOR WITH A FIRE DOOR AT THE END OF IT, AND HE DID NOT PUT IT IN A ROOM, AND HE PUT A PIECE OF PAPER WITH A NUMBER ON IT IN HIS INSIDE POCKET AND WENT DOWN THE STAIRS.**
