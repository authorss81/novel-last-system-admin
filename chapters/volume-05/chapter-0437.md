# Chapter 0437 — Eighteen Inch Across

Friday the thirty-first of August 2018, about twenty minutes past ten in the morning and about eight minutes, a corner of ground where two streets come together, in Tideglass.

**TWO MEN STOOD ON THE SAME TWENTY YARD OF TARMAC ON THAT MORNING BECAUSE BOTH OF THEM WANTED A STUMP OUT OF IT, AND BOTH OF THEM WERE RIGHT ABOUT WHY, AND NEITHER OF THEM EVER SAID HIS REASON AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID THE OTHER MAN WAS WRONG.**

The corner is where Ferry Lane comes out into the front of that parade, and both of them slow down at it. The tarmac fans out to about twenty yard across and then goes back to the width of the two roads, and in the middle of the fan there is a stump.

It is a sycamore, cut flush with the road about five years ago, and it is eighteen inch across the cut and slightly domed, and the tarmac was laid round it and has come away from it on the near side in a lip about an inch high. The lip has been walked round rather than over for about a year.

Nobody in this borough has claimed it. There is a notice on a post about nine yard away that has been up longer than the stump has been there and is about something else entirely, and a woman at the far end of that parade has said since 2016 that somebody would come and see to the corner, and nobody has come.

The man in his thirties got there at about ten past ten with a bar under his arm, having thought about that stump on and off for about five months.

The man in his forties was already there.

---

What they said in the first minute was that it is a big one, and the man in his thirties said it needs a bar, and the man in his forties said there was one against the wall, and went and got it.

What the man in his forties did not say is that on the fourth of July, at about half past three in the afternoon, he came round that corner on foot and a woman came out of the parade side in front of him and put her front foot on the lip and went down onto her hands and knees in the road. She stayed there a bit under a minute. Then she got up and went on up the parade.

He was the only one on that stretch of road at the time. He has told nobody. He has driven past that corner about sixty times since and looked at that lip every single time, and he has worked out that it is a trip for anybody, and that everybody who comes round that corner has to see it, and that a woman of that age coming out of a parade of shops with bags in both hands is exactly the person who does not see a lip.

What the man in his thirties did not say is that on weekday afternoons, most weekday afternoons for about two years, a woman has stood at that corner with a folder under her arm about six foot back from the kerb, in the same place, and has not gone into anything and has not spoken to anybody, and has gone up the parade after about twenty minutes. He has seen her do it perhaps thirty times. He has never seen her do it at any other hour.

He has not the faintest idea who she is. What he has worked out, from standing about nine foot from her on two occasions when he had gone up for a can of something, is that she stands there because the wind comes off that junction and there is no wind in that corner. He has no intention of putting any of that in front of a stranger on a morning like this one.

There was a bar against the wall. It is a crowbar, about four foot, and it is not new.

---

They put it in on the far side, where the tarmac has not lifted, and the man in his forties stood on the short end of it and the man in his thirties took hold of the long one, and they pushed.

Nothing happened for a bit. Then something gave, and about a foot square of tarmac came up in a piece about the size of a tray, and the stump moved round and up about half an inch, and then stopped.

The man in his thirties said, "It's got a root on it the size of your arm."

The man in his forties said, "Yes."

That was all either of them said about what was under it. Neither of them said what it was like down there and neither of them had looked. Both of them were looking at the hole the tarmac had come out of, and there was clay in it and about an inch of something in the bottom of it, and neither of them said the word for either of those things.

They got the tarmac back down as well as they could, which is to say they pushed it into the hole and it went in crooked, and there is now a lip on three sides of that stump where before there was a lip on one. The piece they had lifted had a corner broken off it. That corner is in the channel at the kerb about six yard away and it is a piece of tarmac about the size of a paperback and it will be there after they have both gone.

They stood there a while after that.

The man in his forties said that whoever cut it ought to have taken the root out at the time. The man in his thirties said that nobody who cuts a tree out of a road takes the root out at the time. The man in his forties said that was true. The man in his thirties said it would want a machine. The man in his forties said it would. The man in his thirties said not one he had. The man in his forties said not one he had either.

Neither of them said what he wanted it out for.

Neither of them asked the other what the other wanted it out for, and each of them went away believing he had more or less the same idea as the other man. Each of them was wrong about that. Neither of them will ever find out that he was wrong, because there has not been one word between them since about a quarter to twelve and there is not going to be, and neither of them has any reason to think the other man is thinking about him.

They put the crowbar back against the wall the same way it was, handle up, the end in the gap where the downpipe comes out of the brick. The man in his forties went up the parade and the man in his thirties went down Ferry Lane, and the corner is as it was at ten past ten except for the lip, and the stump is still in it, proud of the road by half an inch where this morning it was flush.

**A CROWBAR IS AGAINST A WALL ON THAT CORNER AND THE TARMAC ROUND A STUMP HAS BEEN PUT BACK CROOKED, AND BOTH OF THE MEN WHO PUT IT BACK THINK THE OTHER ONE WAS TALKING ABOUT THE SAME THING HE WAS.**
