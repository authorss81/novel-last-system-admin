# Chapter 0904 — Under The High Corner

Thursday the twenty-fifth of April 2024, at the foot of the front step of number nine until the street has gone quiet. Under the high corner of a flag.

**SHE HAS A FLAG AT THE FOOT OF THE FRONT STEP OF NUMBER NINE STANDING A HAIR HIGH AT ONE CORNER AND SHE MEANS TO RUN THE KNUCKLES OF A CLOSED HAND DOWN INSIDE THE NARROW OPENING UNDER THAT CORNER WITH THE FINGERS FOLDED AND THE BACKS OF THEM LEADING AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING IS AT THE FRONT GATE FROM THE GARDEN SIDE WITH HER ELBOW KEEPING IT HALF OPEN.**

That flag has lain at the foot of that step since the path went in, and the step above it has been repointed once while the flag has stayed where it was set.

Repointing throws pale droppings down the face of the step, and the droppings sit on the flag until the broom takes them, which is why the joint at the foot runs pale.

It is a concrete square near two feet across, its crown rubbed smooth with one tip riding a hair above its seat.

Two feet square spans a soft spot without breaking, while larger flags crack across the middle when the bed gives under one corner.

She wants what the high corner stands on, where the foot never lands and the broom never reaches.

Under a high corner the knuckles learn fastest, because polish marks the tread while stone wedges the lift below.

Daylight under a corner tells the state of the bed without lifting, which is why the hand goes in before the eye looks along.

So the knuckles of a closed hand run down inside that opening, with the fingers folded and the backs of them leading.

The concrete there is cool and rough, with grit in the opening where the broom has pushed it and sand below where rain has washed it in.

The knuckles drop near an inch and meet stone. Not concrete. A flat hard wedge driven under the corner a hand back from the rim.

A hand back is where the weight of the step bears through the flag, which is why the packing sits there and nowhere else.

She walks the backs along it, and it spans near three inches each way before ending, with hammer-broken ends rough to the touch.

Hammer-broken stone shows fresh faces that bite the soil and lock, while sawn stone shows smooth faces that slip in wet weather. Fresh faces glitter with quartz points that catch noon light, while old buried faces lie dull and dark. The glitter fades after one winter underground as soil acids dull each point to grey. Grey points still bite when rubbed, though they no longer catch the eye at noon.

That is why packing stones are broken and not cut, and why this one has stayed where it was driven while sawn ones work out.

What it is, is a packing stone, put there to bring the flag up to the step when the path was set.

The woman who does her shopping comes to the front gate from the garden side with her elbow keeping it half open.

She stood there with the gate on her elbow and looked along the path to the step and then to the street and back again, and she shifted the gate a little wider and let it fall back, and then she went out through it with nothing in her hands and left it half open behind her.

A flag on a packing stands only on the packing and on nothing else at the high corner, because the packing lifts it clear and air goes under the rest.

Concrete flags ring true when tapped where they sit firm, and drum dull where they bridge over air.

True ring sounds sharp and short, because the flag sits dead on its bed and gives nothing back to the knuckle.

Dull drum sounds deep and long, because air under the flag lets it sing a little when struck.

That is how the ear finds hollows the eye cannot see, and why tapping a path tells more than sight along it.

This one rings true at the high corner and dull along the low edge, which says the packing carries the high side while the low side floats on grit.

That is why the high corner never moves underfoot while the low edge rocks a hair when trodden on the end.

She pushes the knuckles further under to feel how much the stone gives.

It gives nothing at all, because stone on earth packs firm and stays firm, while grit on earth moves with every rain.

What makes the corner high is the stone. The flag was set to the step and found to dip at the corner, and the stone was driven in to bring it up.

---

A packing driven like that lasts for years, because stone on soil locks firm and concrete on stone spreads the load.

Soil under a packing sinks a little each winter and the packing follows it, and each year the corner sits a hair lower than before.

Rain gets under the stone where water sits and washes grit out on the path side where feet shake it, which is why packings on the trodden side sink while those on the still side stay high, and why this corner, which feet miss, has sunk least.

That is why the high corner stands only a hair high now, where it stood a finger high when the stone went in.

Flags dip toward the wash, because water runs off the step and under the low edge and takes grit with it.

That is why the low edge has gone down half an inch while the high corner on stone has barely moved.

She turns the knuckles sideways to feel how the bed runs under the flag.

It runs firm under the stone for about three inches and then softens into grit all round, with the grit damp where water sits under the middle.

A bed of grit sets firm under tread the way road metal does, and every boot that crosses seats it a touch harder where the flag bears most.

Road stone locks because each stone turns until its flat side finds another flat side, and then it stops and stays.

Grit under a flag does the same on a small scale, which is why old paths sit firmer than new ones, with each year pressing the bed a little truer.

That is why the centre stays dead while the low rim stirs faintly after rain, as seep lifts it a hair.

What the path wants is grit brushed under the low edge with a thin stick and watered in, with the flag tapped down onto it to seat it.

Grit damped in sinks through the voids and binds, while grit swept in dry lies loose and leaves by June.

The top of the flag is firm underfoot, with no cracks across it and the worn part smooth as glass where feet have polished it.

Feet polish concrete the way hands polish timber, because grit underfoot cuts a little every step until the top shines.

Polish shows where feet land most, which is the middle of the flag and never the corners, and why the middle shines while the high corner stays rough.

The stone under the high corner is firm in the earth, with the top of it worn smooth where the flag rubs and the bottom rough where it bites the soil.

---

She takes the knuckles out from under the corner and brushes the grit off on the flag beside it, and reads the flag from the path for what the surface confesses.

From above the flag looks level and true, with the high corner showing only as a shadow line along the joint.

From the end the high corner shows a hair of daylight under it, where the packing lifts it off its bed.

Both views are true, and the knuckles know which edge will want doing first, which is the low one floating on grit that washes out each rain.

Let the low edge be packed firm with grit watered in, and let the high corner stand on its stone through another year.
