# Chapter 0547 — The Other Name on the Label

The Sunday of the sixteenth of February 2020, and the whole of the day, in one building off a wide road and in a kitchen in another street.

**SHE HAS HAD THE PLACE AT THE END OF THE RUN SINCE THE OCTOBER AND THE WOMAN WHO HAD IT BEFORE HER LEFT IN THE SUMMER, AND ON A SUNDAY A THING ARRIVED WITH HER NAME ON IT AND THE OTHER WOMAN'S NAME UNDER HERS, AND SHE HAS OPENED IT AND CARRIED IT HOME AND SHE HAS NOT ASKED ANYBODY ANYTHING.**

The office is on the second floor of a building that does four other things in the same building. There are nineteen of them on the floor and about half of them are in on a Sunday, because Sunday is when the week gets caught up.

She is a woman in her late thirties. She has been there since the beginning of October, which was the middle of a bad year for the building, and she came in as a temporary and became permanent in November without anything being said about it and without anything being written, because nothing in that place is written.

That place at the end of the run had been somebody else's for a good many years. She knows that much and no more. The woman left in the summer, in July, and did not come back and did not clear her own things out, which everybody there remarks on.

The thing came on a Sunday and was on the floor outside the door at about half past midday, and it is not large, and it is the wrong shape for what came that day before it.

She brings it in and puts it down on the floor by the end of her place, and she looks at the label for about a minute and a half.

Her own name is on it, in the same hand and the same typed letters as her own name would be. Underneath that, on the second line, there is another name, and it is not a person she has ever worked with, and it is not a person she has ever met.

She takes it up to the flat that evening and she opens it on the kitchen surface with a knife, because there is nothing in that kitchen to open a parcel with.

There is half a jumper in it.

It is a man's jumper, or a large woman's, and it is about a third done, and it is a colour she would not have chosen, and the wool is good wool and the needles are good needles and one of the needles is still in the work with about nine inches of it on.

The pattern is folded inside the wool and it has been folded and unfolded so many times that it will not lie flat. There are marks on the pattern that have been gone over twice, and then gone over again.

There is nothing else in the box. No note, nothing with a name on it in handwriting, nothing with a date on it, nothing that says which of the two women it is going to.

She sits down and looks at it for a long time, and then she puts it in the cupboard at the end of the kitchen, on top of a box she does not open, and she shuts the door.

There are a dozen other things of the same shape in that building's store by the time the Tuesday comes round, and none of them has two names on it, and she looks at every one of them.

She is not looking for anything. She has not told anybody that she is doing it and she would not tell anybody if she were asked, and if you asked her why she was doing it she would say it was quicker than going down to the store yourself, and that would be true, and it would not be why.

He has been in that office since about a year and a half ago and everybody there has been given the same afternoon off, and there are about a dozen of that building's own people in the corridor outside the store, and about nine of them are looking along the same stretch of it.

One of them says, "They've sent another one to the wrong floor," and the other one says, "Not with your name on it," and that is the entire conversation that takes place in that corridor that afternoon, and it is about the place, and it is true, and it is not about the woman at the end of the run.

She does not join in. She looks at the whole row and she looks at her own name on a label and she does not say the name under it, and about four minutes later she goes back to her place and finishes the afternoon.


On the Monday she is in early and she is in late, and she does not ask.

She works out over two days how she would ask. She works out that she would have to start with the name, and that the name is the whole of it, and that a woman who has been in a place for two days does not go to a woman who has been in it for six years and ask her whether she ever had a parcel.

On the Wednesday she asks one person in the corridor, and it is the wrong person to ask, and the person is not unkind about it.

"Did you know who was in that place before me?"

The other one thinks about it for a second and says a name, and says, "She'd been there years," and then says that she thinks she left in the summer and that there was a collection going on for her, and then goes on up the corridor to do something else.

She says nothing at all. She stands there in the corridor for a moment longer than she needs to and then goes back in and does her day.

That is all she gets, and it is more than she expected, and it is not enough to ring anybody with, because there is no number on the label and there is nowhere for it to go, and a name is a name and not a place.

She has not rung anybody. She was never going to ring anybody. There is no version of this in which she rings anybody, and the reason is not politeness and is not care and is that there is nothing to say.

---

She does not open that cupboard again for the best part of a fortnight.

That night she does open it, once, and takes the jumper out and puts it on the surface and looks at the nine inches that are still on the needle and at the point where the other woman had got to.

She has not knitted anything since she was a girl. She looks at the pattern and the two rows that are there and she counts the stitches against the pattern, and she does it wrong, and she does not find out until she has taken about two inches off.

She puts it back on the needle and puts it down, and does not put it away this time, and leaves it on the surface of that kitchen with the needles still in it, and that is where it will be when the March comes, and there will be nobody in that flat but her and the thing on the surface.

**SHE HAS ASKED ONE PERSON ONE QUESTION AND GOT A NAME AND NO NUMBER, AND SHE PUT IT DOWN ONCE AND WROTE DOWN NOBODY'S NAME FOR IT, AND THE ONE WHO OCCUPIED IT BEFORE HER WENT IN THE SUMMER AND HAS NOT BEEN HEARD OF SINCE.**
