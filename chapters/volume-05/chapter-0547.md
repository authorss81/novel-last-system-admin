# Chapter 0547 — The Second Name Under Hers

The Sunday of the sixteenth of February 2020, and most of the day, in one building off the long road and in one kitchen four streets away from it.

**THE PLACE AT THE END OF HER RUN HAD BEEN SOMEBODY ELSE'S FOR YEARS AND SHE WENT IN AS A TEMPORARY IN THE OCTOBER, AND THE WOMAN WHO LEFT IT IN THE SUMMER DID NOT CLEAR ANYTHING OUT, AND EVERYBODY ON THAT FLOOR REMARKS ON IT AND NOBODY ON THAT FLOOR CAN SAY WHY SHE WAS GIVEN IT.**

The office is on the second floor of a building that does four other things as well. There are nineteen of them on that floor and about half of them are in on a Sunday, because a Sunday is when the week gets caught up.

She is a woman in her late thirties. She has been there since the beginning of October, which was the middle of a bad year for the building, and she came in as a temporary and became permanent in the November without anything being said about it and without anything being written down, because nothing in that place is written down.

That place at the end of the run had been somebody else's for a good many years. She knows that much and no more. The other one went in the July and did not come back.

The thing was on the floor outside the door at about half past twelve. It is not large and it is the wrong shape for what else had come that day before it.

She brings it in and sets it down by the end of her run and looks at the label for about a minute and a half.

Her own name is on it, in the same typed hand as her own name would be in. Under that, on the second line, there is another name, and it is not somebody she has ever worked with and not somebody she has ever met.

---

By about three in the afternoon there are a dozen things of the same shape in that building's store and not one of them has two names on it.

She stands in the corridor outside the store and looks down the whole row of them. There are about a dozen of that building's own people standing in that corridor and about nine of them are looking along the same stretch of it, and the row is not going anywhere and is not in anybody's way.

One of them says, "They've sent another one to the wrong floor."

The other one says, "Not with your name on it."

That is the whole of what is said in that corridor that afternoon, and it is about the place, and it is true, and it is not about the woman at the end of the run.

She does not join in. She looks along the row and then at her own name on a label and she does not say the name under it, and after about four minutes she goes back to her run and finishes the afternoon.

At about half past four she stops a woman in her early fifties who has been on that floor for years and asks her one question.

"Did you know who was in that place before me?"

The other one thinks about it for a second and gives a name, and says, "She'd been there years," and then says that she thinks she left in the summer, and that there was a collection going on for her, and then goes on up the corridor to do something else.

She stands there a moment longer than she needs to and then goes back in and does her day.

A name is a name and not a place. There is no number on the label and nothing on it that any letter could be sent to, and a woman who has been on that floor since before the summer is not going to go and ask a woman who has been on it for six years whether she ever had anything come.

She was never going to ask anybody anything. It was settled before she asked, and the reason it was settled is not politeness.

---

She opens it at about nine that night, on the kitchen surface, with a knife, because there is nothing in that kitchen to open a thing with.

There is half a jumper in it.

It is a man's size, or a large woman's, and it is about a third done, and it is a colour she would not have chosen. The wool is good wool and the needles are good needles and one of the needles is still in the work with about nine inches of it on.

The pattern is folded inside the wool and it has been folded and unfolded so many times that it will not lie flat. There are marks on the pattern that have been gone over twice and then gone over again, in a different colour, and the second colour is not the first one.

There is nothing else in there. Nothing with a name on it in handwriting, nothing with a date on it, nothing that says which of the two women it is for.

She has not knitted anything since she was a girl. She looks at the pattern and the two rows that are on the needle and she counts the stitches against the pattern.

She has got a row wrong. She has got it wrong in a way that will not show until it is a good deal further on, and the only way she can find out is to take about two inches off and see whether the join closes up.

She takes two inches off.

It does not close up.

She puts those two inches back on the needle and puts it down on the surface, and it is about half past ten.

She goes and stands at the window for a while and then comes back, and picks it up again, and counts the row once more, from the beginning, slowly, because the marks that are on that pattern were made faintly and the ones under them were not.

She has got it wrong again, in the same place, because she is counting the row on the wrong side of the pattern, and there is no way to tell that tonight.

She puts it down. She leaves it out on the surface with the needles in it, and she shuts the door of that kitchen on it, and that is where it will be when she comes in from work on the Monday.

**SHE HAS ASKED ONE PERSON ONE QUESTION AND BEEN GIVEN A NAME AND NO WAY OF REACHING IT, AND SHE HAS PUT HALF A JUMPER DOWN TWICE IN ONE EVENING AND TOLD NOBODY IN THIS BOROUGH THAT SHE HAS PICKED IT UP AT ALL.**
