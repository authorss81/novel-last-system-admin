# Chapter 0225 — One Line

Sunday the twenty-eighth of February 2016, from about ten in the morning, a bus, and a yard gate at Penhale, and then a corridor outside an office.

**THE THIRD OF THE THREE IS THE BEST OF THE THREE AND IT IS NOT HIDDEN AND IT IS NOT RUN BY ANYBODY, AND ANY PERSON IN THIS REGION MAY GO IN THROUGH THAT GATE ON A SUNDAY MORNING WITHOUT ARRANGING IT, AND THE WHOLE OF WHAT ABOUT FOUR HUNDRED PEOPLE HAVE AGREED ABOUT IS ONE SENTENCE, AND THE SENTENCE IS A RULE, AND THE RULE OWNS NOTHING AT ALL.**

Penhale is not a town. It is a bend in a road with a bus stop at the near end of it, and the yard is on the flat part, and there is no church in it and no square and no pub and about forty houses on the road itself and four of them have a van.

The gate is a green gate with no gatehouse. Inside it there is hard standing, and a shed with a corrugated roof that is on a weekly hire from a firm in the next county, and racking that is on a weekly hire from a different firm, and a stack of copper pipe about nine feet long with a strap round it, and two trucks belonging to a haulier, and a bench under the office window with the paint off one end of it, and a folding sign on the office door that says *NO CALLS AT THE YARD — RING THE OFFICE*, and a trundle of empty crates by the fence because crates come back when they come back.

It is not a works and it is not a depot and nobody in it would call it anything. It is a place where things are kept that other people own, and the man who sweeps it does not work for the people who own the things.

Jonas Mercer got off the bus at ten past ten with a bag he did not need to have brought, and there was nobody at the gate to meet him, and about nine people inside it, and he told the first one he spoke to that he had come on his own account and had not written to anybody, and the man said that was all right and did not go and tell anybody else, which Jonas Mercer noticed and thought about afterwards.

**AND THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE IN THAT YARD THAT MORNING, printed and fixed to the office wall beside the door at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **THE SAME SENTENCE CAN BE PUT ON A WALL AND IN A RACK.**
> **THE SAME RULE CAN BE READ BY EVERYBODY AND SIGNED BY NOBODY.**
> **NEITHER OF THOSE IS AN ASSET.**

Above it, framed, at a slight angle, is a sheet of paper the size of a bill. One line, six inches of type, and under it about four inches of white.

*Any part of any system in this region may be used by any person.*

The frame is not level and nobody has straightened it. There is a second of the sheet in a rack of forms inside the office, on top, and it has a second line under the first one in the same type and at the same spacing.

The man with the clipboard came out of the office about eleven o'clock and stood in the doorway with it against his chest. He had nothing to write on and he did not go back in for anything for about half an hour.

A woman of about eighty-six was in the office doorway behind him with a piece of chalk, counting off lengths of pipe on a pallet and putting a tick beside each one, and she did it without stopping and without looking at either of them.

Jonas Mercer was not on anything. He was not a party to the arrangement and had not written to anybody and had been in this borough for a year and a bit and was a stranger in that yard, and nobody had a list on which to put him, and there was no line anywhere for him to sign to get in.

He was in the corridor outside the office for the best part of the morning. The corridor is the ten feet of concrete between the office door and the yard gate, and it is not a corridor, and that is the whole of the geography of it.

The man of about eighty-nine came along it at about half past eleven with a folder under his arm and a mug in his hand and stopped, because a man of thirty-two in a good coat was standing in it with his bag at his feet.

Haldane Perry. His name was on a bit of lanyard round his neck on a piece of string and it said PENHALE SHARED YARD and underneath that, in a different hand, *since 1987*.

"You're all right," he said. "Nobody's expecting anybody."

"I know," said Jonas Mercer. "I didn't write."

"No, I should think not." He drank out of the mug. "You'll be the one off the bus."

"That was the other one."

"Then you'll be off the Monday one, and it's not as good, and the driver's a worse man for it." He said it the way a man says a thing he has been saying for years. Then he nodded at the frame. "You reading that."

"I have."

"Right." Perry shifted the folder. "That's the one everybody reads. That's the one that goes in the paper and on the wall and on the back of the letterhead, and every word of it's true, and I'd put it up in a place with a lot of money in it. And in the rack there's the one with the second line under it, and that's the one that gets signed, and it's not a secret and it's not a different rule, it's the same rule with the bit about standing in it left on, because you need the bit about standing in it when you're filling a sheet in for somebody. Two sheets off one original. 1987. Neither of them's been reprinted because there's never been a reason to."

"What's the second line."

"*A person has no standing in respect of anything recorded under this.*" Perry said it the way a man says a thing he has said a lot. "That's not a warning, that's the second half. When you ring this yard and say put a crew on that, there's nobody to put a name against, because if there's a name against it then the yard's doing a job for a person, and then it's not shared, it's just work with extra steps, and it'd have to be invoiced, and then you'd be talking to a business, and nobody here wants to be talking to a business. So there's no names, and so anybody can ask, and that's the whole trick and it's thirty years old and it's never been wrong."

Somebody called across the yard about a strap. Perry said something back that Jonas Mercer did not catch, and then he went back along the corridor, and about four minutes later he came back with a clipboard and a sheet of paper on it and said, "You're going out with the four, are you."

"No," said Jonas Mercer.

"Right," said Perry, and did not appear to think anything else about it, and held the clipboard out. "Well, you'll want to sign for that then, because it goes back to the office with the crew and there's one of them per job and I've got three jobs on and the lad's off. Sign at the bottom."

Jonas Mercer looked at it before he signed it, because he signs things.

There was a job at the top in a box. Under the box, printed, not written, a figure: **£163**. Under the figure, printed, a line of small type: *sum for the work as agreed, 1987.* And at the bottom, above a long rule, four printed words: *signature of person carrying out work.*

He signed it.

"There's no charge for the sheet," said Perry. "There's no charge for anything, that's the whole point, that's the top one. You'll get the money posted, don't bother with that."

"Money posted," said Jonas Mercer.

"Aye. Well." Perry was already walking. "You'll want to read it. Half of them never do."

---

The yard owned nothing. That was the third of it and it took him until about two o'clock to be sure of it, walking the hard standing twice with a cup of tea somebody had given him and nobody watching him.

The shed was hired by the week. The racking was hired by the week and belonged to a firm whose office is forty miles away. The pipe was sold to the crew and went out in the crew's van and was paid for by the yard out of a pool, and the pool is made of what the members put in, and the members are the yards, and there are nine yards in it, and every one of the nine belongs to somebody. The two trucks on the standing belong to a haulier in the next county and come at eight and go at four and the haulier has never once been asked a question. The office is a hut on a base. The bench is a bench. The crates are the haulier's and the fence belongs to the next door, which is a farm, and the farm has never been asked about it either.

Not one thing in that yard belonged to the arrangement. The arrangement was a sheet on a wall and a rack of forms and a book with a column in it and two people and a telephone, and that was the whole of its capital, and it had been running since 1987 on that.

**AND HE SAID THE THIRD SENTENCE IN THE TEN METRES OF CONCRETE BY THE OFFICE DOOR, TO HIMSELF, IN A VOICE THAT DID NOT GO ABOVE A NORMAL ONE, AND TO NOBODY:**

*A thing nobody owns can be used by everybody, and so can the paper that comes with it.*

A raven got into the racking about half past two and everybody in that yard stopped what they were doing for about a minute and a half, and the man with the clipboard lost his place on a list he was not writing, and a lad of about twenty got a broom, and the bird went out over the fence and the yard went back to it, and nobody in it made a thing of it, and that is the arrangement working.

**AND A MAN STOOD AT A BUS STOP ON THE OTHER SIDE OF THAT GATE AT FOUR O'CLOCK WITH A SHEET OF PAPER IN HIS INSIDE POCKET THAT HE HAD SIGNED BY MISTAKE, AND A SUM ON IT THAT HE HAD NOT ASKED ABOUT, AND A YEAR UNDER THE SUM, AND HE DID NOT KNOW WHAT ANY OF THE THREE WAS, AND NONE OF THE PEOPLE IN THAT YARD HAD ASKED HIM ANYTHING AND HE HAD ASKED NONE OF THEM.**
