# Chapter 0342 — The Last Page

Monday the seventh of August 2017, from about eight in the morning until about half past five in the afternoon, a passage between two buildings off a parade, that parade, and a van in a lane, in Tideglass.

**A LENGTH OF IRON RAILING ABOUT FOUR FOOT LONG ABOVE A PASSAGE CAME AWAY FROM ITS FIXINGS ON THE WALL SIDE AND IS HANGING WITH THE BARS DOWN OVER A FOOTWAY THAT IS ABOUT FIVE FOOT WIDE, AND A MAN WHO WALKS UNDER IT EVERY DAY HAS NOT REPORTED IT, AND A TRAFFIC CONE HAS BEEN STANDING UNDER IT SINCE SATURDAY, AND A MAN IN HIS FORTIES READ A PAGE IN A PAD HE HAS NOT OPENED SINCE JUNE AND FOUND ONE THING ON IT.**

The passage is between the back of a shop on that parade and the gable end of a house, and it is five foot wide from wall to wall and nine foot high where the two buildings come close, and it goes from that parade to a yard at the other end of it where a man keeps a bicycle he does not ride.

There were five feet of iron railing on the wall side of that passage at about seven foot up, made up with two uprights, and it was bolted through with four bolts into the brick, and it is a thing that was put there to stop people coming off a wall and it has done that for about thirty years.

One of those four bolts has turned about a foot and a half in its hole and the plate has drawn out of the brick on the top edge, and the whole five feet has come off the wall and is hanging by one bolt at the far end with the bars down and its own weight on it, and the bottom of it is at about five foot off the ground.

A traffic cone is standing on the footway underneath it, orange with a white band round it, and it has a scuff on one side of the base and a bit of cardboard under it to stop it going over, and it went there on Saturday afternoon, and there is a man on that parade who is about seventy per cent sure he put it there himself, and he has not told anybody, and nobody has asked him.

The footway is five foot wide and a person goes under that railing with about a foot and a half over their head, and there is nothing in that passage that stops anybody walking round it, because five foot is five foot and nobody walks round a thing that is five foot overhead.

There are about thirty people a day through that passage in August and there were about four in that heat on the Monday morning, and every one of them has looked up at it, and not one of them has said one word about it to the next person through, because a thing that is hanging off a wall and has not fallen yet is not a thing anybody in a street wants to be the first one to have mentioned.

---

The man in his forties went under it at about eight on the Monday morning and again at about ten past nine and again at about one, and he has gone under it on the way to that parade since the last week of March.

He looked up at it the first time and the second time. On the third time he looked up at it and said nothing to himself about it, and he has not looked up at it since.

He has a van and a route and about nine stops a day and that passage is between two of them, and a thing that is not his on a wall does not come into a route, and a thing on a wall that is going to come down in the next month is a thing he will be asked about by a person who owns it, and there is no person he can find who owns the wall at the side of that passage, because the side of that passage is the back of a shop on a parade and the back of a house and a wall that has been the back of a wall for longer than anybody on that parade has been trading.

He could have gone into that shop and asked. He has been into that shop about four times since March and the woman behind the counter there does not know the wall is there, and if she did know she would say the wall is not hers, and then he would have to find out whose it is, and nothing he could come back with is better than walking under it.

The woman who works in a shop came out of that shop at about ten in the morning to put a card in the window and stood on the step and looked down that passage for about a minute.

"That's been hanging since Saturday," she said, when he came by at about a quarter past ten. "And there's a cone under it and I would put money on that cone being one of about nine cones in this borough and every one of them put out by a man who was in a van he had no business being in."

"I have walked under it every day since March," he said, "and I am not reporting it."

"I am not telling you to report it," said the woman who works in a shop. "I have had a letter off that street about a hedge and a letter off that street about a street light and both of them asked the same question, which is why is it not been done, and I have got a bit of paper with a date and a number on it and nothing else about it at all, and that bit of paper is the only thing that has ever come back about anything I have sent anywhere and I have never once been told what it means."

"What does it mean."

"It means somebody counted it," said the woman who works in a shop, "and then somebody else wrote the number down, and neither of them is going to tell me which hedge."

He said that was about the shape of it and went on up the parade with his bag, and the cone is still under that railing and the railing is still hanging off one bolt over a footway that five foot wide.

---

He got the pad out of the box in his van at about half past four that afternoon because he was looking for a length of four by two he had written somewhere.

He has carried that pad since January and it is a black plastic cover with a card in a pocket at the back and about nine lines used in it in seven months, and the rest of it is blank, and it is the one sheet of paper in this borough with anything in his handwriting on it and it lives under a seat in a van and he has thought about that more than he has thought about most things.

He went through the pages from the back and there was nothing on nine of them, and on the page before that there was a length of four by two in his own writing and a date, and on the page after it, which is a page he bought blank in June and wrote on once and then never went near again, there was one thing.

It was a lane name in pencil, on the second line down, in the middle of a ruled page with nothing at the end of it and nothing above it.

F E R R I E R   L A N E.

He looked at it for about a minute.

He knows that lane. It is off a road about a mile off and it goes down to a yard and he has driven down it twice that he can remember, once in March and once in May, and there is nothing at the end of it he has ever wanted, and there is no number against that lane and no house and no date and no nothing, and the name is written in his own hand and it is the only thing on that page.

He has driven down it twice. Once in the March he was after a man who wanted a garage roof taken off and stopped doing, and once in May he was going through that road because it is a short cut and he went back the way he came, and neither of those two times was a job that anybody was waiting on, and there is a man at the end of that lane with a stack of pallets and a van and a dog that barks at cars, and he has not been in that yard and has not spoken to that man, and the name of that lane is on a page in his van.

He has no idea what job that was. He has no idea if it was ever a job. He has no idea whether he wrote it in that lane or at a junction off it or at the end of a different day's work with the pad open on the passenger seat and the window down, and there is no way of finding out, because a page in a pad in a van has not got a day on it and there is one pad and not a book, and if he writes on this page again then the thing that is on it now will be under about nine more lines of something else in about four months and there will be no way of telling which of them it was.

A list of nothing is not a document. Nothing has been added to that page and nothing has been taken off it, and no living person is ever going to hear that the name of a lane is on a sheet of paper in a van, and nobody in that lane has any idea he has driven down it twice.

He put the pencil on the shelf of the cab, and he shut the pad and put it under the seat and shut the door of the van and went to the last thing of the day.

**A CONE STOOD UNDER FIVE FEET OF IRON RAILING HANGING OFF ONE BOLT ABOVE A FOOTWAY, AND A NAME WAS WRITTEN ON A PAGE IN A VAN IN PENCIL WITH NOTHING AT THE END OF IT, AND NEITHER OF THOSE THINGS WAS GOING TO BE ANYBODY'S PROBLEM ON THE SEVENTH OF AUGUST.**
