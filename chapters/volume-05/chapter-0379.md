# Chapter 0379 — No

Thursday the twenty-eighth of December 2017, from about half past eight in the morning until about half past nine at night, a street off a parade, and a shop on it, in Tideglass.

**A MAN IN HIS THIRTIES BOUGHT A CHEST FREEZER SECOND-HAND IN OCTOBER FOR A JOB THAT FELL THROUGH AND HAS BEEN TRYING TO GET RID OF IT EVER SINCE, AND ON THE MORNING OF THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF DECEMBER HE OFFERED IT, ONCE, TO A MAN IN HIS FORTIES STANDING AT THE BOOT OF A PICKUP THIRTY FOOT FROM THAT SHOP DOOR, AND THE MAN IN HIS FORTIES SAID NO, AND THE MAN IN HIS THIRTIES WENT ROUND THE PICKUP, PUT IT BACK IN, AND SHUT THE TAILGATE, AND HE HAS NOT OFFERED IT TO ANYBODY ELSE AND HAS NOT MENTIONED IT TO ANYBODY, AND THE ONLY OTHER PERSON IN THAT STREET WAS INSIDE THAT SHOP WITH HER BACK TO THE WINDOW.**

The shop is on a parade a hundred yard off the main one and it is set back about eight foot from the footway with a forecourt in front of it that it does not use, and there are two things on that forecourt, a rack of firelighters and a stack of baskets.

The woman who works in a shop is on that counter from about ten to nine on six days and had come in at about half past nine because the boiler in the back had gone out and she wanted to be in before the kettle went.

The man in his forties was in that street at about ten past ten that morning because he had a roll of insulating tape on a list and he was going down the parade to the shop that keeps a bigger stock of it and had come back past this one empty-handed.

The man in his thirties had his pickup outside that shop at about five to ten in the morning with the tailgate down.

---

The freezer is white, about two foot and a half long by about two foot, and it is the small chest kind with a sliding glass lid and a catch on the lid that has nothing to work it, and it is the only thing in the back of that pickup apart from a coil of lead and a shovel.

He bought it in October off a man who was clearing a house in another town and he paid about sixty pounds for it and it worked when he got it, and the job it was for was a kitchen in a house in the last week of October that did not go ahead after the Monday.

He put it in the back of that pickup and it has been in the back of that pickup since, and he has had it in his yard twice over a long weekend, and he has had it out on a pavement in one place for four hours and taken it back in, and he has had it on a hardstanding at the top of the town with a cardboard on it and nothing written on the cardboard and taken that off it as well.

He is not asking anybody to take it off him. He has not put it on a wall and he has not written a price on it and he has not told anybody in that shop that he has got it.

It was the man in his forties coming past that did it. He came along that street at about five to ten with his hand in his pocket, and the tailgate was down, and the freezer was in the bed with the lid up, and the man in his thirties was standing at the kerb with his back to it having a smoke.

"You've still got that thing," the man in his forties said, meaning it in a way that was a question and not a question.

"I have."

That is all either of them said about it for the rest of that conversation and neither of them has said anything about it since, and they have been in that street together since.

The man in his thirties came round the front of the pickup and the man in his forties moved about a foot to let him and did not help and did not stop him, and the man in his thirties got hold of the freezer at the back and turned it round and lifted it up onto the bed of that pickup and pushed it back against the bulkhead and put the shovel flat on the top of it and pulled the tailgate up and dropped the catch, and the catch took first time.

"No," the man in his forties said. It was before the man in his thirties had finished getting his hand on it, and it was one word, and he did not go on.

"Right," the man in his thirties said.

He shut the tailgate and they stood there for about a second and a half, and the man in his forties then went on up the street to that shop and the man in his thirties went and got in the pickup.

The whole of it took about two minutes and neither of them gave a reason, and there was nothing in that exchange for either of them to be wrong about, and one of them has not thought about it since and the other one has thought about it twice.

---

The woman who works in a shop was at her counter with her back to the window of that shop and the radio on the shelf above the till going, and it was on the local station and she had it low, and there were two people in there at about five to ten that morning and then there was one.

She saw a pickup standing outside her shop with its tailgate down and a man at the kerb, and she saw a second man come along the street and stop, and she saw the first man come round the front of the pickup and the two of them stand there, and she could see from where she was that one of them was holding something and that the other one was not.

She did not turn round. She has raised none of it with either of them and does not intend to, and the reason is not caution: she simply has no way of asking that is not a question about a man she knows nothing about, and a woman who asks a man in a street what he is doing with a freezer in front of a shop has admitted an interest in the answer.

The man in his forties came in at about ten past ten and bought his roll of tape and paid for it and said the shop was on about being out of them and she said they had two left, and that was the whole of that visit from both sides.

The man in his thirties drove off and went and did a job in a terrace at the bottom of the town and came back to that street at about half past four with the pickup still in it.


He has not offered it to anybody else.

Not to the man he was working for in October, who is not the man in his forties, and not to the man in his sixties who he passes most days, and not to the woman at the shop, and not to the man who does that hall, who would have taken anything. He has had two chances since and has not taken either, and the reason is not a decision he made. A man who has been told no once by a man in his forties does not go round a town asking other men, because the second question is the one that costs something and the first one cost him about two seconds.

It is in his yard at the top of the town.

It is behind the low wall at the side of that yard under a piece of hardboard, and it is not on a pavement and it has no sign on it and it is not in anybody's road, and it was there through January and it was there at the end of that month, and nothing has been done about it and no arrangement exists and nobody has been asked anything and nobody is owed anything and it costs him money to keep where it is, in that he cannot get at the other side of his own yard.

He has not mentioned it to the woman who works in a shop. She does not know it exists.

**THIRTY FOOT FROM THE DOOR OF THAT SHOP, AT ABOUT FIVE TO TEN IN THE MORNING ON A DAY WHEN IT HAD BEEN STANDING IN THAT PICKUP SINCE OCTOBER, A MAN SAID NO, AND THE MAN WHO HAD ASKED PUT IT BACK IN AND PULLED THE TAILGATE UP, AND NOBODY ELSE ON THAT PARADE OR IN THAT SHOP HAS EVER HEARD THAT IT WAS OFFERED, AND THE ONLY PERSON IN THAT SHOP WHO SAW ANY OF IT FROM THE INSIDE TURNED ROUND AFTERWARDS AND THERE WAS NOTHING TO SEE.**
