# Chapter 0609 — The Set of Twelve

The Thursday of the tenth of December 2020, from about eight in the morning until about nine in the evening, in this borough.

**HE HAS HAD A SET OF TWELVE NAMES SINCE BEFORE THE SUMMER AND HE HAS GONE OVER THEM NINE TIMES SINCE BEFORE THE SUMMER, AND HE CARRIES THE COUNT AND NOT THE NAMES, AND TONIGHT HE CARRIES IT PAST A DOOR HE HAS DRIVEN PAST FOUR TIMES.**

He is a man in his mid forties. He works in a place that does not have a name on the door and he has worked there for most of a decade, and the set of twelve is about his mother, who is in a home about five miles off.

Here is what the twelve is.

There are twelve people who were at his mother's funeral. He was not there and he has said since the June that he was not there, and there are twelve of them, and he has the names of all twelve, and he has a way of getting at them that takes the best part of a cup of tea on a decent line.

He has gone over them nine times since before the summer.

That is not a thing he has decided to do. He has gone over them in a car park once and then in a kitchen once and then in bed, and there are always twelve, and the counting is now the shortest route between two facts.

Here are the two facts.

One: he did not go.

Two: twelve people did go, and each of them spent money on the flowers or the paper or the car and spent a Saturday getting there, and every one of them found out he had not come a good while later, because he has told none of them in advance and he answered the two calls that came.

---

He has not written to them. That is worth being exact about, because it is the thing he has got wrong in his own head and has corrected himself about about four times.

What he has done is this. In about the June he thought he should ring them all. He got four names out of the list and then stopped, because the fourth name was a man he has not spoken to since about 2014 and he could not work out what the call would be.

Then he decided he should not ring, and he was pleased with the decision.

Then in about the September he worked out that not ringing is a thing he had decided, and a decision has a shape, and his did not have one, and so he went back to the list.

He has been through the list nine times and it has not improved.

Here is what happened today, which is ordinary, and there are three parts to it and none of them is a decision.

The first part was at about eight in the morning, in the kitchen.

His wife was out at about half past seven and the house was quiet and he had made the list on the back of an envelope because he does not keep a book for this and would not think of it.

He got to the fifth and could not get the fifth.

Not the name. The name was there. The fifth is a woman who was in the same road as his mother for about twenty years and who was at the funeral and who has since had a bereavement of her own, and he knew that, and he could not get to which part of that was the reason he had not rung.

So he did the thing he does. He went past it and counted it, which is the same as counting it.

And then he got to the twelfth, which is a man who used to do something with his mother about the volunteer thing, and it is one of nine of these twelve that he cannot get to, and two or three of the three he can get to live right by the ones he cannot, and he has never worked out why.

And then he did the other thing, which he started in about the August and has done maybe six times since, which is that he checks whether the count is still twelve.

It is twelve. It has been twelve every time.

The second part was at about half past four, on the road.

He was in the car and he came down a road of terraces he does not live on and he has driven down perhaps a dozen times since before the summer because the road of his mother is off it, and there is a house on it with a hedge and a gate and a brown front door.

He slowed at the gate.

He did not stop and he did not put his hand on the brake and he was not going to knock, and he knows the woman who lives there because he was at her mother's funeral as a small child and does not know what happened to her and has never asked.

Here is what he has worked out about the slowing, and it took him a couple of months to get to.

He is not looking at the house.

He is looking at the count. He goes past doors and he checks that he still has twelve, and the going past is how the checking is done, and the door is only a thing at which to do it that has not been used for anything before.

He has done this at four doors in this borough. He knows which four. He could draw you the four.

---

The third part was at about eight o'clock tonight.

His wife said, at about half past seven, "Did you ever ring them?"

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Have you decided?"

And he said, "I decided in the summer not to."

And she said, "You said last month you were going to."

And he said, "I said I'd ring them."

And she said, "You said last month you were going to."

And he said, "I've not decided."

And she said, "You've decided and then you've undecided and that's what's happened."

And he said nothing.

And she said, "I've no objection to you doing it, and I have never once said otherwise."

And he said, "I do know."

And she said, "It's not going to change anything."

And he said, "That would be the same."

And she said, "You want it to be the kind of thing where somebody turns up at the end."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "You don't."

He got up after that and went and stood at the front window in the front room for a while.

Here is the part he has not told anybody, and it is the part that the whole of the summer has been about without his knowing it.

He does not want the twelfth name to arrive.

He has been through it nine times and every time he gets to the end he is aware that if the twelfth name comes up in his head in the right way then he has a thing to do. And if it comes up wrong then he has not.

And in a car park in about the August the twelfth name came up in the right way, and he wrote it down, and then he made the list again a couple of weeks later and the twelfth name came up wrong, and he has not been able to make it come up right since and he has not tried to.

He is not going to ring twelve people. He knows that.

He is also not going to have the next one, where there is no twelfth name and nothing to do and the thing has simply stopped.

And the way he has found to keep that from happening, this evening, standing at a window in his own front room, is to do one thing.

He went into the kitchen and he got the envelope off the stack of post and he got a pen and he counted the list again, in order, out loud, alone, and he got twelve, and he put the envelope back on the stack of post.

He will do it again. He knows he will do it again, in a car park, on a road with a hedge and a gate and a brown door, at a time when he has told himself he is going to stop.

**HE HAS TWELVE NAMES AND HE HAS GONE OVER THEM NINE TIMES SINCE BEFORE THE SUMMER AND HE HAS COUNTED THEM OUT LOUD ALONE IN HIS OWN KITCHEN AFTER NINE O'CLOCK AND PUT THE ENVELOPE BACK ON THE STACK OF POST, AND HE WILL GO OVER THEM AGAIN.**
