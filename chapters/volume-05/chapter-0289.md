# Chapter 0289 — A Cushion

Sunday the fifteenth of January 2017, from about four in the afternoon, a room over a shop at the end of a parade in Tideglass.

**SHE LEFT IT ON A CHAIR IN A ROOM ON A SUNDAY AFTERNOON AND DID NOT SAY SHE HAD, AND IT SAT ON THAT CHAIR FOR A FORTNIGHT WHILE THE ROOM WAS OPENED AND SHUT TWICE A WEEK, AND ON THE FOURTEENTH DAY SOMEBODY CAME UPSTAIRS AND CARRIED IT DOWN AND PUT IT SOMEWHERE ELSE.**

The room over the shop at the end of that parade is a first-floor room with a hatch in the ceiling and a stair at the back of the shop, and a man in his thirties keeps it, and he opens the door on a Tuesday and shuts it on the Wednesday, and there is a fire in it on the nights it is open and a jug and six chairs, and a table at the far end with a green baize cloth on it that has been on that table so long the baize is bald in the middle.

There was no fire in it on that Sunday because there was nobody in it.

She had come off the bus at about twenty past three with her bag of books and gone the length of that parade because there was nothing on and the rain had stopped, and she went up the stair at the back of that shop because the door of that stair was open and it is never locked, and she went in and shut it behind her, and the room was cold and smelled of ashtrays and old card.

There was a table with the baize on it, six chairs, and a fire in a grayed-out hearth, and one of those six chairs was the chair nearest the door.

The other five had cushions on them. That one had not, and it had not had one since the summer, and she noticed that when she put her bag down on the table, which is a thing that takes about two seconds and does not mean anything at the time.

She went out at about half past four and got the bus back down to the parade, and the cushion she put on that chair was a square one with a zip cover and no cover on the zip, and it had a grey print on it that had gone the colour of the wall in this weather.

She did not put it on the chair, exactly. She put it on the seat and pushed it back against the table leg with her foot so it would not go over when the door came on the wind, and she shut the hatch behind her coming out, and she did not say to anybody that she had been in the room, and nobody knew.

---

He came in on the Tuesday with his own key, as he does, and he opened the room, and put the fire in, and put the jug on, and about five of them came up between half past seven and a quarter to eight and played one hand and then three more, and one of them said, at about ten o'clock, that the cold was getting into that room from the back, and the man in his thirties said it had been getting into that room from the back since about the summer, and nobody went and looked at the wall.

The cushion was on the chair nearest the door the whole of that night, and two of the five sat on the other five, and one of the five pulled that chair out a foot to get at the fire and pushed it back with the cushion still on it, and at about half past ten they went down the stair and he shut the hatch and locked the room and went.

It was the same on the Tuesday after that, and the Wednesday after that, and the Tuesday after that.

The room was opened and shut twice a week for a fortnight. A man came up with a fire and a jug and five men and a woman came up with a fire and a jug and four men, and the green baize was bald in the middle on every one of those nights, and the cushion was on the chair nearest the door on every one of those nights, and nobody looked at it twice, and nobody ever sat on it, and nobody mentioned it, and it did not gather dust because there was no dust in that room worth the name.

It is not a thing anybody would describe if they were asked to describe the room, and if the man in his thirties had been asked on the Wednesday what was in it he would have said a table, six chairs, a fire and a jug, and he would have been right about all four and would not have mentioned the sixth chair twice.

On the Sunday fortnight after, at about ten in the morning, a woman who works in that shop came up that stair with a cloth in her hand and a can of furniture polish, because the baize on that table gets to a state about twice a year and it is her table in the sense that it is under her shop and nobody has ever told her that it is not.

She was in there about ten minutes. She turned the chairs up on the table the way they go, and she polished the bald bit with her hand, and she put them back down round the table, and the sixth chair was the one nearest the door, and there was a cushion on it, and it had not been there the week before.

She stood with the cloth in her hand for a bit.

Then she took the cushion off the chair, and she did not put it on any of the other five, and she went down that stair with it under her arm, and she went through into the back of that shop and past the trade counter and into the cold room at the back, and she put the cushion on the top shelf in the cold room on top of a bag of flower bulb paper behind the door, where the shelf is about as deep as the cushion is thick and where the light does not get, and she pushed it back until it was against the wall, and she shut that door and turned round and went out and put her hands in the cold tap over the sink for a second because they were hot from carrying it.

Nobody saw her do it. The shop was shut on a Sunday until nearly twelve and she was the only one in it, and she did not mention it at the counter that week, and the man in his thirties came in on the Tuesday and the room was as it was on every other Tuesday, and there was a chair at the table nearest the door with nothing on it.

The woman who had put the cushion there in the January before that got a bus down to the parade at about half past four on the Sunday and did not know any of it, and did not find out in this batch or in any of the ones after it, and was never asked about it by anybody at any time.

**AND THE CUSHION WAS ON THAT SHELF AT THE BACK OF THAT SHOP ON THE FRIDAY OF THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF JANUARY AND ON THE FIRST OF MARCH, AND THE WOMAN WHO PUT IT THERE TOOK THE SHELF THINGS OFF IT IN THE SUMMER AND PUT THEM BACK ON IT IN THE SUMMER AFTER THAT, AND SHE NEVER TOOK THE CUSHION OFF IT, AND IT IS NOT ON ANYTHING.**
