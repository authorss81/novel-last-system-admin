# Chapter 0527 — One More Than the Monday Before

The Monday of the fourth of November 2019, from about ten past nine until about a quarter to twelve, in a long room at the back of a building in this borough.

They sing on Monday mornings. They have sung on Monday mornings since the March of 2000, which is nineteen years, and there is nobody who runs it, and there has never been anybody who runs it, and there is nothing written down about it anywhere, and the two people who have been in that room the longest have both said, separately and years apart, that a Monday is the only reason either of them gives.

The room is at the back of a building on a street that does not go anywhere people want to go. It is long and it is low and it is quite cold until about twenty past nine and then it is not, and there is no way to tell what is on the other side of the far end of it. The floor has been painted so often that it has gone soft at the edges and the paint has come away in a strip along the line where people stand. They stand in it. They have stood in it since about 2003, because two of them decided in 2001 that standing up was better, and by the time it could have been argued about it was too late to argue.

She has been coming since the March of 2000. She is a woman of about seventy-one and she is the second person through that door on a Monday, and she has never once been asked to be anything by anybody.

What she does is count.

She does it in the first few minutes, before anybody has got themselves settled, and she has done it every Monday since the March of 2000, and it takes her between twelve and sixteen seconds, and she does it with her right hand hanging down the side of her leg where the room cannot see it, going along her fingers and coming back to the start every time she has finished.

She has never once said the number out loud. She has never once written it down. There is nothing anywhere in this borough with that number written on it, and there never has been, and if you asked her to give it to you she would have to start again.

In nineteen years the number has been between fourteen and nineteen. It has most often been seventeen. She knows that it has most often been seventeen because she has counted something over a thousand Mondays, and she is not a woman who keeps records, and she has never needed to.

It began the way most of her habits began, which is that she was standing at the back in the March of 2000 with a hand down her leg doing something with her fingers, and a woman beside her said, "You'll never get the start of it in this cold," and she said, "I'd counted you by then," and the woman laughed, and neither of them ever went near it again.

---

A man came into that room on Monday the twenty-ninth of July 2019 and has been in it every Monday since.

He is a man in his late thirties and he came because somebody he works with comes, and he knows nobody else there, and he stands at the back on the left, which is where the new ones stand because that is not where the ones who have been longest are. He has been late twice and early once and he has not been late since August. He is quite good, according to the two people who have told her so without being asked, and he does not know that she was told.

On Monday the second of September, which was five weeks after he had started coming, he said one sentence to her.

They were both standing at the back during the interval. The interval is twenty minutes and it is not a break, it is the twenty minutes in the middle in which about half of them go outside and the other half stand where they are and talk, and the room is half emptied out, and on that Monday he was on the side nearest the way out and she was against the end.

"You count us," he said.

And she said, "I would have thought you'd never notice."

He said, "I have," and she said, "You have not," and he said nothing to that, and they went back in and sang the second half, and afterwards they went out separately.

That was on the second of September and it is nine weeks ago. He has not asked her one question since, and she has not offered him one, and neither of them has mentioned it. She has wondered about it perhaps twice a month and what she has wondered both times is whether he worked it out on his own, which he could have done, because he is that sort of man, or whether somebody told him, and there is nobody in that room who would tell anybody anything, and so it must be the first, and she has decided to leave it there.

---

On the fourth of November she counted eighteen.

Nothing else happened in that room that morning. It happened at about a quarter past nine, with a hand going down the side of a leg, and nobody in the room knew it was happening except the man at the back on the left, and he may not have known it was happening.

She had counted seventeen the Monday before. She has counted seventeen on something near half of the Mondays since 2000, and for about a year the number has been seventeen or eighteen the way an hour is four or five, and it has not come to eighteen in the whole of the time she has been counting, and she knows both of those things about it, and neither of them is written down.

The eighteenth is standing at the front on the right with a woman who has been coming since about 2011.

Nobody brought her in. Nobody said she had come. She is a small woman in her sixties in a green coat and she is standing where somebody who has been coming a long time would stand, and she has put her hands together in front of her the way people do when they do not know what to do with them, and she has not spoken to anybody, and she has not once looked at the woman in her seventies.

The woman in her seventies counted her in.

That is the thing and there is no way of putting it any other way. She counted her in without asking, and she counted her in in the second, before the room had finished emptying in, and she has done that for nineteen years with everybody who has ever come through that door, and nobody has ever asked her how she knows whether a person is to be counted or not, and she has never worked out what she would do if somebody did.

She did not go over. That was the part that took the whole of the interval. She stood where she was with her right hand against her leg and she thought about going over, and she thought about it for most of twenty minutes, and she did not, and when the others came back in and made a noise of getting ready she turned round and faced the way everybody faces.

The man at the back on the left came back in at about two minutes to ten. She does not know where he was between half past nine and two minutes to ten. He had his back to the room for most of the interval and the room was half empty and she was not looking at him, and he has not said since that he was looking at her, and she has not asked, and she is not going to.

They sang at ten. The new one sang, and she was in it, and she was not good, and she was not so far out that anybody stopped, and she came in half a beat behind the woman in her seventies and stayed there, and the woman in her seventies sang as she has sung every Monday for nineteen years, at the front on the left, second in from the end, and she did not listen to the new one, and she was not listening for anything.

That is not a thing she has decided to do. It is what nineteen years of a Monday morning amounts to. She has got to the place where the second half goes the way it always goes and her voice is inside it, and whatever else is happening in that room is a long way off and not in the way.

At half past ten they stopped and about half of them went out into the rain. The woman in the green coat went out with the woman who has been coming since 2011, and the two of them were talking when they went through, and nobody asked the green coat to come back and nobody asked her not to.

The woman in her seventies did not ask her anything either, and she has not asked the man at the back on the left anything, and he did not ask her anything, and she went out at a quarter to twelve and went the long way round because of the rain.

**SHE WENT OUT AT A QUARTER TO TWELVE AND WALKED HOME THE LONG WAY ROUND BECAUSE OF THE RAIN, AND SHE WILL BE THE SECOND ONE THROUGH THAT DOOR AGAIN NEXT MONDAY MORNING, AND SHE WILL COUNT.**
