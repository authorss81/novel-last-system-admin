# Chapter 0373 — An Ordinary Day

Monday the fourth of December 2017, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past eight in the evening, a street at the top of the town, a shop off a parade, and a low wall by a yard gate, in Tideglass.

**THREE PEOPLE SAW A MAN GO PAST THEM ON ONE ORDINARY MONDAY FOR THREE REASONS THAT HAD NOTHING TO DO WITH EACH OTHER AND NOTHING TO DO WITH HIM, AND NOT ONE OF THE THREE OF THEM HAS SAID ANYTHING TO ANYBODY ABOUT IT, AND HE WENT HOME AT HALF PAST EIGHT AND HAS NOT THOUGHT ABOUT IT SINCE, AND NOTHING AT ALL HAPPENED TO HIM.**

The man in his forties is up at about half six most days and he was up at twenty to seven that Monday because there is a fire to make and a van to get.

He had about four hours of work in him that Monday and he did about two of them, and the other two went off a road at about ten past ten in the morning because a woman was not in, and he sat in his pickup at the bottom of a street for about three quarters of an hour in the cold and then went and did something else that he had not meant to do that day.

---

The woman who works in a shop is at that counter at about ten to nine and she has been at it at that hour on a Monday for about six years.

That Monday the man in his forties came in at about a quarter to ten, which is later than he comes in, and he was in there about six minutes, and he bought what he always buys, and he did not buy the other thing.

He has bought the other thing at that counter at about the same hour on a Monday for a good few years. It is a small thing in a small paper bag and it is not for him and he has never said what it is for and she has never asked him and has put it in a bag about as often as she has put anything else in a bag.

That Monday he put his money on the counter and went, and she stood there afterwards for about a minute with her hand on the till key, and what went through her head was not about him. It was that a Monday in the first week of December had gone out differently from a Monday in the last week of November, and that she would have to do the order for the holiday on the Friday and she was a bag short.

She has not mentioned it to him and she has not thought about it since, and she was back behind that counter at ten to nine on the Tuesday and the Wednesday and the Thursday.

The man in his thirties walked up that street a little after ten on the Monday morning with his hands in his pockets and stopped outside a gate on the off side to look at something that is not his, which is a thing that men do on a street.

Across that street there are nine steps up to a door and the man in his forties came out of that door with a folding rule in his hand and went down the steps in two goes and stood at the bottom with the rule against his leg.

He has gone up those steps in one go since before the summer. He is a man in his forties who carries a bag up those steps and has not thought about it, and on the fourth of December he took them in two halves with his hand on the rail, and got to the bottom and stood for a second before he started across the road.

The man in his thirties was on the other side of that road and had been standing there about a minute and a half and saw all of it, and the reason he was standing there was that he had come to look at a gable he is not working on and does not expect to be asked about, and his own business had not started.

He has told nobody he was on that street that morning and he will not, and it is not in him to go round telling people a man took some steps in two halves, and he did not know the man had done it a thousand times in one piece.

The man in his sixties was up on a low wall beside a yard gate, that Monday, with a cup in his hand.

He is out in whatever there is most days when there is something out, and a Monday in the first week of December with low sun on a south wall is a Monday to be out on, and that yard gate is a low wall with a flat top about eighteen inch off the ground and it faces the sun at about the middle of the morning and has done since the building went up.

He had been on that wall for about twenty minutes when the man in his forties came out of the gate on the far side of the road with a folding rule under his arm, and the man in his sixties looked at him for the length of a street crossing.

What he took in was that the man had stopped in the road, and that he had looked up at the sky, and that he had gone on across with his head down after it.

That is what a man in his sixties takes in from a distance of thirty yard. He has been looking at streets out of a window and off a wall for about thirty years and a man who stops in a road and looks at the sky is a thing you notice and it does not stay with you, and it is not about the man at all and it did not even stay for the length of the crossing.

He drank his tea and went home at about a quarter to twelve and has not been on that wall a great deal since, and he would not be able to tell anybody what the man looked like.

---

At about ten past ten that Monday the man in his forties was on the off side of that street, and the woman who works in a shop was at her counter, and the man in his sixties was on a low wall, and the man in his thirties was on the other side of that road looking at a gable, and not one of the three had any means of finding out that the other two were out that morning.

He did about two hours of work that Monday in a kitchen at the bottom of the town, on his own, and he did it well, and he came off at about half past twelve and was paid in cash and went and had his lunch standing up.

He was at the shop off the parade at about half past four for a box of screws and about twenty minutes, and he spent the back half of that afternoon in a yard off that road, and came away from it a few minutes short of seven, and he was in his own front room from about half past seven with his bag on the floor and the heater on and a paper he had not opened.

Nothing happened to him.

No one spoke to him about any of the three of them, and no one has spoken to him since, and he has not the least idea that there were three people out on that Monday who saw him go past and that each of them had a different reason, and if a man in that town told him about it on the Wednesday he would say that a Monday is a Monday and go and make his tea.

**HE CAME IN AT HIS OWN DOOR AT ABOUT HALF PAST EIGHT ON THAT MONDAY AND PUT HIS BAG DOWN BY THE HEATER AND STOOD FOR A SECOND IN THE MIDDLE OF HIS OWN FRONT ROOM, AND HE DID NOT PUT THE LAMP ON FOR ABOUT A MINUTE.**
