# Chapter 0630 — The Thing She Said Out Loud

Thursday the twenty-fifth of March 2021. She is in this borough and the man she is talking to is in this borough and the whole of it happens on four steps outside a church at about half past two in the afternoon in the sun.

**A WOMAN HAS DECIDED TO GO ON DOING A THING SHE HAS DONE FOR A MAN FOR YEARS, AND SHE HAS DECIDED ALSO TO SAY SO OUT LOUD TO SOMEBODY WHO DID NOT ASK, AND WHEN HE ASKS HER WHY SHE IS TELLING HIM SHE GIVES HIM THE TRUTH, AND IT IS TRUE AND IT IS THE CHEAPEST THING SHE HAS EVER DONE.**

She is a woman in her early sixties. He is a man in his mid thirties. He does not know her and she does not know him and they have both been sitting on the steps of a church for a good while before either of them says anything.

The thing is a Wednesday afternoon in a front room at the top of this borough and she has been going for most of a working life and she does not get paid and nobody has ever mentioned paying her.

---

Here is what was said, and it was not much, and she started it.

He was on the step with a cup from the shop and she was on the step with a bag and she said, "Can I ask you something odd."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "Did you ever do something for anybody that nobody asked you to do, and go on doing it after you stopped enjoying it."

And he said, "I don't know."

And she said, "That's all right."

And he said, "Why."

And she said, "Because I've decided to go on doing it and I've decided to say so out loud, and I've not got anybody to say it to."

And he said, "Say it to me, then."

And she said, "Would you think less of me."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Why not."

And he said, "Because it isn't a crime."

And she said, "It's not that it's a crime."

And he said, "Then what is it."

And she said, "I want somebody to know that I know what I'm doing."

That is the whole of it up to there and he put the cup down on the step beside him and he did not pick it up again for a while.

And he said, "Go on, then."

And she said, "There's a man at the top of this borough and I go on a Wednesday afternoon and sit with him from about two until about half past four, and his wife is in a home and he is not a man who wants anybody in his house, and he says a good deal less than he used to."

And he said, "How long."

And she said, "Most of a working life."

And he said, "Since when."

And she said, "Since before I had a car."

And he said, "And you don't get anything."

And she said, "I don't."

And he said, "His daughter knows."

And she said, "His daughter knows I go and she has never once said a word about it and she has never once stopped me and last year at Christmas she gave me a box of biscuits and I have still got them."

And he said, "Then go on."

And she said, "I am going on. That's what I came out here to say."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "You can go."

And he said, "I'm in no rush."

And she said, "There's more, if you don't mind."

And he said, "Go on."

Here is the part she has been trying to get to since about a quarter to three and could not get to until he said the thing he said.

He said, "It's a strange thing to carry, doing somebody else's Wednesday."

And she said, "It is."

And he said, "Most people I'd know would have let it go by now."

And she said, "Most people you'd know wouldn't have started."

And he said, "That's a different way of putting it."

And she said, "It is the same way of putting it."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "Can I tell you the actual reason and you will not believe me."

And he said, "Try me."

And she said, "Because somebody asked me. Once. Years and years."

And he said, "Asked you to do it?"

And she said, "No."

And he said, "Asked you for what, then?"

And she said, "That is the whole of it and I have never said it to anybody."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "It was a Thursday and I was a young woman and I had been in his kitchen a good while and I was putting the kettle away and I said I'd have to be going, and he stood up."

And she said, "And he said, will you come next week."

And he said, "That's it?"

And she said, "That's it."

And he said, "That's not a reason."

And she said, "I know."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "It wasn't *will you come next week*."

And he said, "It was what, then."

And she said, "It was that he stood up."

And he said, "He stood up."

And she said, "He had not stood up the whole time I had been in the house. He had been sat in that chair since I came in and he had not stood up once, and he stood up, and then he said it, and then he sat back down."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "And I have had a lot of people be kind to me. My own sister has been kind to me since I was a girl. A woman I worked with paid my bill when my husband went. My neighbour has had my bins out when my back's been bad."

And he said, "Yes."

And she said, "And none of it is the thing."

And he said, "I can believe that."

And she said, "You shouldn't be able to, and you've just said that too quickly, and that is the second time you've said something too quickly to me this afternoon."

And he said, "Sorry."

And she said, "It's all right."

And he said, "I've only just met you."

And she said, "I know. That's why I can say it and why you'd not have believed it."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "That's the true reason and I have said it out loud and I have said it to a man I met a short while ago on a church step, and I would have said it to you sooner if I'd had anybody sooner, and I want you to know that I have never said it to anybody."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "Now the part I have not worked out."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "I said it out loud because I decided to, and I did not decide to because I wanted telling. I decided to because I have noticed that nobody knows."

And he said, "Who?"

And she said, "That's the other half. Nobody."

And he said, "His daughter."

And she said, "His daughter knows I go. Nobody knows why I go."

And he said, "Why does it matter?"

And she said, "It doesn't matter."

And she said, "It matters to me."

She went at the words she had not got to, and she said them, and they were this.

A woman she knows had a lad in at the top of this borough who "never asks for anything," and it was not the woman who started it, and it had been going round since before Christmas, and she had heard four or five of them and had agreed with the last one, and one of them was a woman at number thirty who has never in her life asked a neighbour for a bottle of milk and thinks that is a form of character.

And the man on the step had said it too. She had heard him say it to himself, twice, in about a minute, in a voice that had the shape already on it, before she had said anything about anything.

And she said, "There's a sentence going round this borough and it's about a man who never asks anybody for anything."

And he said, "Is there."

And she said, "You said it."

And he said, "When."

And she said, "Twice. You've been sitting here saying it under your breath about a man on a wall and I've been listening to you do it for about a quarter of an hour."

And he said, "Have I."

And she said, "You did. It came out at about the same time both times and both times it was the same words in the same order, and I have heard that order all through since Christmas."

And he said, "I don't know where I got it."

And she said, "Neither do I."

And he said, "It isn't about anybody."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "Then why is it said."

And she said, "Because a thing that's not about anybody is easier than a thing that is."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "I have been saying it too."

And he said, "I know."

And she said, "You don't, I haven't told you."

And he said, "You said you had."

And she said, "I said I had heard it. Hearing it is not the same as saying it, and I have said it, and I have said it to his wife in a kitchen once and to the woman at the chemist's twice, and both women thought I was talking about the same man and we were talking about two different ones and it made no difference to either of them."

And he said, "And you don't want to be doing that."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "So I've stopped."

And he said, "Since when."

And she said, "Since the middle of February. I have not said it since then and I am not going to start again on a church step."

And he said, "You haven't said it since."

And she said, "Not once."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "I'm not finished."

And he said, "I know you're not."

And she said, "And here's the thing that's going to sound daft. You don't need to stop saying it. I want that on the record before you get to the bit where I tell you something."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "Because that sentence is not true."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Not of anybody. It is not true of one single person in this borough and I have looked, because that is what I did instead of stopping, and I have been at about it ever since."

And he said, "How do you look."

And she said, "You don't. You hear a thing said four times and you find out whether it fits anybody you know and if it doesn't fit anybody you have never met then it's a sentence and not a fact."

And he said, "And did it fit anybody?"

And she said, "It nearly fits a man I know and it does not fit him at all, and there is a woman on a row who stopped doing a bin over it before the month was out, and I do not know that for a fact, I know a woman said it."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "So."

And he said, "So."

---

Here is what she has, and she has it on the steps and she has had it on and off all day and she has been to the parade and back.

One: she has said the true reason out loud for the first time and she said it to a stranger who gave her the time of day.

Two: saying it did not make it lighter. She has thought it would and she has been wrong about that, and she has been wrong about that in the past before and has not mentioned it to anybody.

Three: what she has got instead is that she has now said, out loud, to a person who did not ask, that she is going to go on doing a Wednesday afternoon at the top of this borough for a man with his wife in a home, and that is a thing said in front of a witness. Before today it was a habit. It is not a habit now. It is a thing she has said she is doing, and she is not going to get to be somebody who has not said it.

Four: and she has not told anybody else, and she is not going to, and she has got a rule about that now and the rule has no exceptions in it, and the reason she made the rule is that she has realised what she did on this step was cheap.

It was cheap and it was the only place it was going to happen and she knew that while it was happening.

And here is what she would not have put to a man on a step before this afternoon.

One of the women on that row might have stopped doing a bin over it and might not know why she stopped. There might be a man on another road who put it into somebody's mouth on purpose and has been sitting on his own front step ever since waiting to see whether it came back. There might be a woman in a queue on the parade who has had it in her mouth for a while now and has not been able to account for it.

She does not know whether any of that has happened and she has not tried, and she is fairly sure she does not want to.

What she knows is that she has heard a sentence four times and been annoyed by it, and that is the whole of what she has done about it, and she would like somebody to know that being annoyed by it is not nothing.

**SHE HAS BEEN GOING TO A MAN'S HOUSE ON A WEDNESDAY AFTERNOON FOR MOST OF A WORKING LIFE AND HAS DECIDED TO GO ON, AND HAS SAID IT OUT LOUD FOR THE FIRST TIME TO A MAN ON THE STEPS OF A CHURCH, AND WHEN HE ASKED HER WHY SHE SAID IT SHE TOLD HIM IT WAS BECAUSE A MAN SHE SAT WITH FOR AN AFTERNOON WHEN SHE WAS A YOUNG WOMAN STOOD UP BEFORE HE ASKED HER, AND SHE HAS SAID IT OUT LOUD AND IT HAS NOT GOT ANY LIGHTER.**
