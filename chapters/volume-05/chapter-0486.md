# Chapter 0486 — The Top Deck

Friday the nineteenth of April 2019, that evening only, from about half past eight until about half past ten. Five decks and a ramp, in Tideglass.

**ON THE FRIDAY OF THE NINETEENTH OF APRIL A MAN WITH A CLIPBOARD AND A RING OF KEYS PUT FOUR CONES AND A NEW BARRIER ACROSS THE RAMP AT THE TOP OF THAT CAR PARK, AND A MAN WHO HAD BEEN DRIVING UP TO THAT TOP DECK AT TEN O'CLOCK ON A WEDNESDAY NIGHT FOR ABOUT THIRTY YEARS WAS STANDING AT THE BOTTOM OF THE RAMP AT HALF PAST NINE THAT FRIDAY LOOKING AT THEM, AND HE DID NOT GO UP.**

It is a car park with five decks and a ramp on the west side and it has had about two hundred spaces in it since it was built, and on a Wednesday night at about ten there are about nine cars in it.

He has been one of those nine since 1989.

The first year he went because he had been told to go up it and look at something from the top. The second year he went because the first year had been worth it. Somewhere in there, and he has never been able to find the year, it stopped being something he was doing and became the thing that happened at the end of a Wednesday.

He drives the four miles across. He parks on the fourth deck because the fourth is where the ramp ends and the spaces are widest. He takes the stairs the rest of the way because he has never trusted that ramp, which was not new in 1989 and has not been touched since. He goes to the barrier at the north-east corner of the top deck and stands against it and looks out over about two hundred yard of that part of the town and does not smoke and does not use a telephone and does not go in the four minutes it takes to walk to the corner and back, which he has timed.

On a clear night you can see the water from up there. On a wet one you can see the lit part of it. He has never had a night when he could see nothing at all and on about nine of those nights the whole thing got put off to the next week, and it was never put off twice.


What he has never told anybody is that he does not know what it is for, and that the not knowing has never once made him consider stopping.

He has gone at it about a thousand five hundred times. He has never written it down, never said it out loud to anybody, and never had anybody in this borough ask him about it, and the two of those facts are about four months apart in his life: the first one is how he came to be a person who does that, and the second one is the reason it has stayed that way.

If somebody had asked him at any point in thirty years, he would have said he liked it, and that would have been true and would not have been the answer.

She asked him on the tenth of April.


She worked at the hospital up at Cleeve and finished at about half past nine, and she drove into that car park at about a quarter to ten on a Wednesday night, and had done for about two years, and had come to expect him on the top deck the way she comes to expect the fourth deck being empty.

It had not been anything. That is the honest description and she had it as a fact for about a year before she turned it into a question.

Then in February her hours changed and she was on nights until the middle of April, and before that, in the last week of March, a man in her work had said a thing about somebody in his forties who drove four miles across a river on a Wednesday night to stand on a car park, and he had said it as a joke and had not known who he was talking about, and she had sat there and worked out that there was only one man it could be, because she had seen the car.

She asked him on the tenth of April at about nine minutes past ten.

---

She said, "Can I ask you something and you can say no."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "Why do you come up here?"

And he stood there for about four seconds.

What he had ready, and what he had had ready since she came round the corner of his own car, was that she would ask about the water, or ask how long he had been coming, or ask whether it was not a bit cold. Three questions he could have answered and gone.

That is not the one she asked.

He said, "I don't know."

And she said, "You don't know."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "It's not a reason. I want to say that. It's not a reason I've got."

And she said, "It's all right. I wasn't after a reason."

And he said, "That's all right."

That is the whole of what was said and it took about ninety seconds and neither of them has mentioned it to anybody.

---

Here is what he gave her, and he has been over it since and he would give her the same again.

He did not say that he loved it. He did not say he came to think about his wife, who died in 2016, because he does not do that and because it would have been about her and he did not want it to be. He did not say that there was money in it, because there is not.

What he said, in the end, was the thing that is actually under it, and it came out in about nine words and he did not plan it.

He said that if he did not go up then the Wednesday had not happened, and that without the Wednesday he would not have had a thing to have got through the week with.

And she said, "Right," again.

And after a bit she said, "That's not a reason either."

And he said, "No. It's not."

And she said, "It's a feeling."

And he said, "Yes."

And she said, "Well. Now you have said it."

And she went down the stairs and he stood at the barrier for another four minutes and then went down and drove the four miles across.

Two things had to be true for him to open his mouth on that car park, and both of them were about her rather than about him. One was that nobody else had ever done it. The other was that he had read her well enough in nine seconds on that Wednesday to be certain of where it would stop. The thirty years had no bearing on it at all.

She has not been back. It has been nine days. She has not written, and she will not, and that is neither a coldness nor a falling out, and the two of them are entirely content with the way it stands.

He has not been back either. He did not go on the seventeenth. That is the first Wednesday missed since about 1994 and there is nothing on his own account that stops him going on a Wednesday except a barrier and four cones and a man with a clipboard who said the top deck was off from the first of May.


He was standing at the bottom of the ramp at about half past nine because he came anyway, without deciding to, and then stood there for about nine minutes.

The cones were on the ramp itself, not at the head of it. The new barrier was not up yet. There is another one chained at the bottom by the office and it has been chained there since the building went up.

The man with the clipboard had come and gone. There was a date chalked on the concrete by the office door and a man's name under it, and neither of them had anything to do with him.

Above him, on the fourth deck, there was a car with its boot open and a man getting something out of the back of it, and after about two minutes he shut it and got in and went down the ramp, and from the bottom you cannot see what is at the barrier end anyway, which he knows because he has walked up that ramp about a thousand five hundred times.

**A MAN STOOD AT THE BOTTOM OF A RAMP IN THAT CAR PARK AT HALF PAST NINE ON THE NINETEENTH OF APRIL 2019 WITH FOUR CONES IN FRONT OF HIM AND A BARRIER THAT WAS NOT UP YET, AND HE DID NOT GO UP, AND NOBODY ELSE IN THAT BOROUGH KNOWS THAT HE HAS EVER DONE IT.**
