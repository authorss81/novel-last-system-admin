# Chapter 0583 — Four Words Apart

The Friday of the thirty-first of July 2020, from about nine in the evening until about half ten, in the back room of a pub off a road in this borough and on the pavement outside it.

**A MAN PUT A SENTENCE INTO A ROOM WITH THE MACHINE GOING AND TWO WOMEN CAUGHT A DIFFERENT HALF OF IT AND BOTH WROTE IT DOWN AND THE TWO WRITINGS CAME OUT FOUR WORDS APART, AND THE FOUR WORDS THAT CARRY THE WHOLE OF IT ARE IN BOTH OF THEM, WORD FOR WORD, IN THE SAME ORDER.**

The back room has a serving hatch through to the front and about nine tables pushed together and a machine in the corner that takes money and plays about four hundred things, and at nine on a Friday in the end of July it is going hard enough that a person has to put their head close to another person's ear to be understood at all.

They have a table by the wall at the near end of it. One of them is a woman of sixty-two and the other is a woman of sixty-five and they have been coming in on a Friday for long enough that neither of them has to look up when the machine starts.

The man is in his early fifties. He has come in on his own and has been at the bar for about an hour, and at about half past nine he came over to their table, which he has done perhaps four times in as many years, and put his hand on the back of the empty chair and leaned down.

He put it into the noise with his face about a foot from the woman of sixty-two:

"You can tell her it was you that asked and she was never told."

Then he straightened up, put two pound notes on the table, said "That's all," and went back to the bar.

---

The woman of sixty-two was looking at his mouth. That is her habit and she is not proud of it. She had him for about the whole of the sentence and she got the shape of it and the last four words.

The woman of sixty-five was listening past the noise to the words themselves, because that is how she has had to do it in that room for two years, and she got the whole of it and the last four words.

The woman of sixty-two had a fiver in her purse and turned it over and wrote on the back of it in biro:

*You can tell her it was you that asked and she was never told.*

The woman of sixty-five keeps a small book of her own in her coat for exactly this sort of thing, because there is a woman about three streets from there who is going to have to be told something on Monday and she would not want it on a telephone from somebody who has caught it once in a room with that going on. She wrote on the left-hand page of it:

*You could tell her it was you that asked at the time and she was never told.*

They put both away without looking at each other's.

They were not hiding anything from one another. Neither of them had a reason to think the other had got it wrong. The woman of sixty-five had been in that room every Friday for two years and had caught a great many people putting things into that noise, and the woman of sixty-two had been in that room longer than that and had caught fewer of them and more of the mouths.

---

At about half past nine they went out and stood on the pavement because it had got cooler, and the woman of sixty-five said she was going to ring the woman on the Sunday, not the Monday, because the Monday was the day she went to the shops.

The woman of sixty-two turned her fiver over and put it in the front of her cardigan, and then she stopped, and she got it out again.

"Have you got yours?"

The woman of sixty-five got the book out of her coat and opened it on the wall of the pub with one finger holding the page.

The woman of sixty-two read it twice.

"It's all there. That's the lot of it."

"It is," the woman of sixty-five said. "Yours is missing a bit at the front."

"There is no bit at the front."

"There's *could* and there's *at the time*. That's all the difference. Three words and a modal."

They stood there and worked through it, and they did it carefully, because that is how they do everything.

Those three words and that modal are the whole of the disagreement. *Could* is not *can*, and *at the time* is not nothing. Neither of those can turn a thing into a different thing, because what the sentence does is entirely in the four words at the end of it, and those four words are in both writings, in the same order, with a full stop after them.

They were both certain. That was the remarkable thing about it and neither of them mentioned it. The woman of sixty-two had watched his mouth make the whole sentence and the woman of sixty-five had her own two years of not being able to trust anything said at that volume in that room, and they had both arrived at the same four words from opposite ends.

The woman of sixty-five wrote out the wording she was going to use on the Sunday on the back page, tore the page out along the seam, and folded it twice, and put it in her coat pocket.

They went back in and stayed until about half ten. The man was at the bar and neither of them went over to him.

**THE TWO OF THEM DIFFERED BY FOUR WORDS AND ALL FOUR OF THEM WERE IN THE PART NOBODY CARES ABOUT, AND THE THREE WORDS THAT CARRIED IT ARE ON TWO PIECES OF PAPER IN TWO POCKETS, AND BOTH WOMEN WOULD SWEAR TO WHAT THEY WROTE.**
