# Chapter 0583 — Four Words Apart

The Friday of the thirty-first of July 2020, from about nine in the evening until about half ten, at the back of a pub off a road in this borough and outside it.

**A MAN PUT A SENTENCE INTO A PLACE WITH THE JUKEBOX GOING AND TWO WOMEN CAUGHT A DIFFERENT HALF OF IT AND BOTH WROTE IT DOWN AND THE TWO WRITINGS CAME OUT FOUR WORDS APART, AND THE FOUR WORDS THAT CARRY THE WHOLE OF IT ARE IN BOTH OF THEM, WORD FOR WORD, IN THE SAME ORDER.**

The back of that pub is one long place with a hatch through to the front and about nine sets pushed together along one side, and a jukebox against the far end that takes money and plays about four hundred things. At nine on a Friday at the end of July it is going hard enough that a person has to put their head close to another person's ear to be understood at all.

They have the two nearest the door because they always do. One of them is a woman of sixty-two and the other is a woman of sixty-five, and they have been coming in at the end of the week for long enough that neither of them has to look up when it starts.

The man is in his early fifties. He has come in on his own and has been at the bar for about an hour, and at about half past nine he came over to them, which he has done perhaps four times in as many years, and put his hand on the back of the empty seat and leaned down.

He put it into the noise with his face about a foot from the woman of sixty-two:

"You can tell her it was you that asked and she was never told."

Then he straightened up, put two pound notes down, said "That's all," and went back to the bar.

---

The woman of sixty-two was looking at his mouth. That is her habit and she is not proud of it. She had him for about the whole of it and she got the shape of it and the last four words.

The woman of sixty-five was listening past the noise to the words themselves, because that is how she has had to do it in there for two years, and she got the whole of it and the last four words.

The woman of sixty-two had a fiver in her purse and turned it over and wrote on the back of it in biro:

*You can tell her it was you that asked and she was never told.*

The woman of sixty-five keeps a small pad of her own inside her jumper for exactly this sort of thing, because there is a woman about three streets from there who is going to have to be told something on Monday and she would not want it put to her on the phone by somebody who had caught it once in a place with that going on. She wrote on the left-hand half of it:

*You could tell her it was you that asked at the time and she was never told.*

They put both away without looking at each other's.

They were not hiding anything from one another. Neither of them had a reason to think the other had got it wrong. The woman of sixty-five had been in there every Friday for two years and had caught a great many people putting things into that noise, and the woman of sixty-two had been in there longer than that and had caught fewer of them and more of the mouths.

---

At about half past nine they went out and stood in the road because it had got cooler, and the woman of sixty-five said she was going to ring the woman on the Sunday, not the Monday, because the Monday was the day she went to the shops.

The woman of sixty-two turned her fiver over and put it in the front of her jumper, and then she stopped, and she got it out again.

"Have you got yours?"

The woman of sixty-five got the pad out and opened it flat on the low stonework outside with one finger along the fold.

The woman of sixty-two read it twice.

"It's all there. That's the lot of it."

"It is," the woman of sixty-five said. "Yours is missing a bit at the front."

"There is no bit at the front."

"There's *could* and there's *at the time*. That's all the difference. Three words and a modal."

They stood there and worked through it, and they did it carefully, because that is how they do everything.

Those three words and that modal are the whole of the disagreement. *Could* is not *can*, and *at the time* is not nothing. Neither of those can turn a thing into a different thing, because what the sentence does is entirely in the four words at the end of it, and those four words are in both writings, in the same order, with a full stop after them.

**THEY STOOD IN THE ROAD AND WORKED THROUGH IT AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID THE ONE SENTENCE THAT WOULD HAVE SETTLED IT, BECAUSE THERE IS NO SUCH SENTENCE AND BOTH OF THEM KNEW THAT AND WERE RELUCTANT TO BE THE ONE WHO SAID IT FIRST.**

They were both certain. That was the remarkable thing about it and neither of them mentioned it. The woman of sixty-two had watched his mouth make the whole sentence and the woman of sixty-five had her own two years of not being able to trust anything said at that volume in that noise, and they had both arrived at the same four words from opposite ends.

The woman of sixty-five wrote out the wording she was going to use on the Sunday on the other half, tore it away along the fold, and folded it twice, and put it away.

They went back in and stayed until about half ten. The man was at the bar and neither of them went over to him.

**THE TWO OF THEM DIFFERED BY FOUR WORDS AND ALL FOUR OF THEM WERE IN THE PART NOBODY CARES ABOUT, AND THE THREE WORDS THAT CARRIED IT ARE IN TWO PLACES THAT ARE TWO STREETS APART, AND BOTH WOMEN WOULD SWEAR TO WHAT THEY WROTE AND NEITHER OF THEM WILL BE ASKED.**
