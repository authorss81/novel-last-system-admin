# Chapter 0831 — The Mend Still In It

Tuesday the twenty-fifth of July 2023, in that office cabin for the whole of that Tuesday, and a window frame that is coming out whole is the whole of it.

**THERE IS A WINDOW FRAME IN THE OFFICE CABIN AT THE UNIT AND HE MEANS TO TAKE IT OUT OF THE WALL WHOLE WITHOUT BREAKING WHAT IS ROUND IT, AND THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN IS IN THAT YARD AT THE START OF THE DAY AND IS NOT THERE FOR THE FRAME.**

That cabin is timber on a low brick base with a felt roof, and the window is in the side wall looking along the frontage, a wooden frame in a brick opening with a sill below and a lintel above.

In June he squared that frame where it stood, moved a tab three inches along the lintel into sound brick, and cut through the two sides and joined them with two hardwood pieces the width of the frame and four bolts. The leaf has shut without a knock since, with a quarter inch of daylight along the head left open on purpose.

He starts with the leaf out on a blanket on the bench, then the architrave off inside with a thin bar, then the screws out of the tabs.

Here is what he has to look at in the middle of the morning with the frame loose in the opening.

The mend comes out with it. The two hardwood pieces and the four bolts are in the sides of the frame where he put them, flush, painted over once, part of the sides now and not plates on top of them. From the front the frame looks like any old frame with a thick side. From the back the bolts show as four round heads with washers under them, sunk square.

That is the stop, and it is not where he thought it would be.

Whether he opens it up again or leaves it. A mend in a frame that is coming out asks for a look at the wood under it, and one kept shut asks to stay shut. He runs a finger along the joint between hardwood and old softwood and feels no lip in it, and he puts a thumb on each bolt head and tries it for movement and there is none.

He leaves it. It is now part of the frame rather than part of a repair, and a frame taken out whole wants to stay whole. To open it would be to make two frames out of one, and there is nothing in front of him to argue for opening sound wood.

The woman from two doors down is in that yard at the start of the day with a bucket for the tap by the gate, and does not stay past the first hour and is not there for the frame and does not look at it twice.

“Have you had the rain in your back room?” she tells him.

“Not since the felt went back,” he tells her. She goes out through the yard gate with the bucket and the yard is his again.

---

The rest of the day is easing and lifting.

He runs the knife round the outside where paint has bridged the joint, so the frame comes free without tearing the brick face. He taps wedges in at the sill and at the head, takes the weight on his shoulder, and walks the frame out of the opening square, a little at a time, until it is clear.

The sill below and the head above are not touched in any of it. The opening stands empty with the brick showing square on three sides and the felt behind it, and the frame goes down on a stack of battens at the side of the yard on sacking, level, with nothing under it but air.

When the light goes thin he stands at the cabin and looks at what is there. A window frame is on a stack at the side of the yard with the mend still in it, the two hardwood pieces and the four bolts flush in the sides, and the quarter inch of daylight still along the head where it was left open on purpose.
