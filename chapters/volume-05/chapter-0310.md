# Chapter 0310 — Twice

Thursday the thirtieth of March 2017, from about half past ten in the morning, a yard behind a shop on a parade in Tideglass.

**A BED FRAME WAS PUT AGAINST THE WALL OF THAT YARD ON A THURSDAY MORNING AND WAS STOOD ON ITS SIDE THERE FOR A FORTNIGHT, AND FOUR WEEKS LATER A DIFFERENT BED FRAME WAS STOOD AGAINST A WALL IN A YARD THREE DOORS FURTHER UP THAT PARADE BY A MAN WHO HAD FORGOTTEN HE HAD DONE IT IN JANUARY, AND THE WOMAN WHO SAW BOTH OF THEM FROM BEHIND A COUNTER NEVER SAID A WORD ABOUT EITHER.**

A small iron bed frame, single size, cream, gone orange at every joint where the paint has thinned on the knuckles, with a sprung base and a head and a foot and one slat missing out of about fourteen, is a thing that comes out of houses four or five times a week in this town and it comes out of them in the back of something.

The yard behind that shop is about the size of two cars, with a roller door on it off the side passage and a wooden gate at the back, and nobody in that building holds a key to that gate, and nobody in it has ever been asked to hold one, and the man who puts the bins out has never once stopped in that yard to ask what is standing against a wall in there.

---

He had it on the van since about the middle of the month, standing on its side on the boards with a blanket thrown over the foot of it, and it came out of a house off the Cleeve road where a woman was having a back bedroom done and had asked him to take it and he had asked where to and she had said it could go.

On the Thursday of that week he was in the parade and there was nowhere in the van that it would lie flat with a door in it, and he put it over the back of the van in the lane and had it down in that yard in about six minutes and stood it against the gable wall on its side with the head end up, and the foot end about three foot out into the yard.

"That's going in your yard," he said, to the woman who runs that shop, who was on the shop floor with a cloth in her hand.

"It's not our yard," said the woman who runs that shop.

"I know it's not yours," said the man in his thirties. "That's why I'm telling you about it. I asked the woman on the Cleeve road and she said it could go, and I asked her where to, and she said anywhere, and anywhere I know is a yard with a wall in it and nothing done to it in it. It'll go in the skip on the other side of the month and you'll not see it."

"And you'll not come back for it."

"I've got another job on the Friday and another one the week after," said the man in his thirties, "and there's a bed frame in every yard on this parade and in every one of them there's about four feet between it and the wall, and I'm not spending a morning in a fortnight turning one round in a yard I don't know the gate on."

He put his bag in the van and went.

The woman who works in a shop stands at that counter four days a week and there is a door behind the counter with wired glass in it and through that glass you can see the roller door and the wall on the left of that yard, and the frame was against that wall from the Thursday, and she looked at it about four times a day for a fortnight, and it was still there on the Friday, and it was still there on the Friday after that.

She did not go out into that yard once. She did not ask the woman who runs that shop where it had come from, and the woman who runs that shop did not ask her, and the man in his thirties came down that parade about nine times in the fortnight after and did not go through that gate and did not turn it round.

It went in the skip at about twenty to six on the fourth Monday, with three pram things and a bike wheel and somebody's television, and the man who puts the bins out said it had gone with about nine other things and that he had not known a bed frame was a thing you could put in a skip.

---

The man of about twenty-eight has a unit off the parade with a bench in it and a chair and a heater and no bed, and since about the middle of January there has been a small iron bed frame in that unit, cream gone orange at the joints, one slat missing, used as a bench with a folded blanket on the seat of it and two milk crates in front.

On the Sunday after that he took it out into the yard of a shop three doors further up that parade, because the roof over that unit leaks onto the top rail of it where it sits, and he stood it on its side against the brick with the head end in and raked the wet leaves out from under the foot end with a stick, because a barrow would not go under a bed frame on its side.

Nobody saw him do that.

On the Monday the landlord's men came with a skip and took everything in that yard that was not a fixture and nobody asked them to and nobody told them to, and the bed frame went in it with about six bags of somebody's building waste, and the man of about twenty-eight went in on the Tuesday morning, put his coat on, and looked at the wall, and did not say one word to anybody about it, and has not said one word to anybody about it since, and he did not know that the man in his thirties had got one out of a house off the Cleeve road three weeks before and stood it against a wall three doors back the other way.

The woman who works in a shop saw the second one from the parade on the Sunday afternoon through a gate on its latch, and she stood on the pavement and looked at it long enough to see that it was a bed frame, and she did not once think about the one behind her own shop that had stood against that wall for a fortnight, and she went on to the bus stop.

**AND THE FIRST OF THOSE TWO FRAMES IS IN A SKIP FOUR DOORS BACK AND THE SECOND OF THEM WENT IN A SKIP THE SAME MORNING, AND A WOMAN STOOD ON A PAVEMENT AND LOOKED AT ONE OF THEM FOR A SECOND AND A HALF WITHOUT THINKING ABOUT THE OTHER, AND A GATE ON A LATCH HAS STILL NEVER BEEN LOCKED.**
