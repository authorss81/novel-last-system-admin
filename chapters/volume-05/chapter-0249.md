# Chapter 0249 — A Day

Wednesday the twenty-ninth of June 2016, from about ten in the morning, a parade of shops in Tideglass, and the top of Peverell Street.

**THE ASK WAS PUT TO PEOPLE AND NOT TO ANYBODY, ON THE MORNING IT WAS PUT, ONE AT A TIME, OUT LOUD, WITH NOTHING WRITTEN DOWN ANYWHERE, AND TWO PEOPLE SAID YES, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO SAID YES TO A THING.**

The man of about eighteen who washes up in a kitchen at the back of the parade, four nights and a Friday, was on the parade at about ten in the morning on the Wednesday because it was not one of the four nights and it was not the Friday, and he had got into the habit of being on the parade in the mornings and nobody had ever asked him to stop doing it.

He did not make a list. He had thought about making a list on the Sunday and had not done it, and the reason he had not done it was not a principle. It was that he did not have anybody's names except the ones he already knew from standing in rooms.

He asked about five people and he asked them the same way, standing in the doorway, no preamble, and the way he asked it was not a good way and he knew it was not a good way while he was doing it.

"Big favour to ask. If there's a job in this borough on a day — not a flood, nothing like that, I'm not talking about a flood, I'm talking about a floor — and you're in this borough that day, would you come and do it."

The first person said, "What day."

"That's the thing," he said. "It's not a day yet. I'm asking if you'd come on a day. I haven't got a day."

"Then I can't tell you."

"You can tell me you don't know either."

"That's not the same as telling you no, that's telling you I don't know, and there's a difference, and you're the first person I've asked in about two weeks who hasn't said no to me inside a second, so I'll tell you what I think," said the man at the first doorway. "I think if you came and knocked on my door about half seven on the day and said there was a floor, I'd probably come. Whether I'd come at half seven in the morning is a different question and it's got an answer in it that I haven't got. I do about eight months of the year on a contract out of town. Ask me in September."

"That's a yes for a day."

"That's a yes for about half of the days between now and September and that's a lot more than I expected to say out loud to somebody washing up in a kitchen."

---

The man from the works on the far side of the river was on the parade at about half past ten coming back from a job, and he is one of the people who comes to the halls on Carrow Lane when he can, and he had come to the one in April and to the one in May, and he had been in the room on the twentieth of May when a woman of about thirty-six gave up this borough's first call in four sentences and he had been the first person to ask whether that was the thing that went out of force in November.

He listened to the whole of it with his head on one side.

"What's in it for me," he said.

"Nothing," said the man of about eighteen. "I'm not offering you anything. There's no money in it and there's no list afterwards and your name doesn't go anywhere, because there is nowhere for it to go. That's not me being straight with you, that's me telling you what I actually have, which is a question and a Wednesday morning."

"What's reciprocal."

"It's not. Not yet. If you come on a day, nobody is going to be able to do anything for you on a day, and I can't fix that, and I looked for about a week for something to put on the other side of it and I haven't got one." He put his hands in his pockets. "I'm telling you it's not reciprocal because the second I pretend it is, this becomes a thing with two halves, and a thing with two halves gets a name, and the name gets a person under it, and then it's the other thing."

The man from the works on the far side of the river thought about that for a while, standing at the kerb with a bag of tools in his hand.

"Right," he said. "The twenty-eighth. I finish at two on the twenty-eighth, I'm over the bridge at about half three, and if anybody in this borough needs a second pair of hands that day I will come down and I'll not charge you and I will not tell anybody I did it. That's not a favour either, because you'd do the same and I'd want you to."

"That's a day."

"That's a day," he said, "and it's a Tuesday, and I'll tell you the whole of it now so you don't have to work it out. It's one day, and the day after that I'm in Leeds till Wednesday, and the day after that I've no idea. That's your right of return. That's the size of it. One Tuesday and a man who finishes at two."

---

A third person said no, and meant it, and it took about nine seconds.

A fourth said, "Ask me on the day, not now," and when the man of about eighteen asked why, said, "Because now's a promise and on the day it's a job, and I'd rather be a man who did a job."

A fifth was not asked, because there was no fifth, because the man of about eighteen counted the people he knew and there were four he could have asked and he had asked all four, and the one person he had not asked and had not intended to ask was the man at the bottom of Peverell Street, whose name was in a drawer in a kitchen four doors up from where he was standing, and who had been asked something in May and had answered it and had never been asked anything since, and who would have been the fourth name on anybody's list and was on nobody's.

"That's four," he said, to Hanna Wray, at the counter at the back of the shop, where he had gone in to get a pen and did not use it. "You know all four of them."

"I know three of them," said Hanna Wray. "The fourth is the man who read that price card out to you in May in about four seconds and put it straight afterwards, and I've never spoken to him in my life, and I could not tell you his first name and neither could he tell you the rate."

He put the pen back where it had been and went out.

**HE PUT THE PEN BACK WHERE IT HAD BEEN AND WENT OUT, AND IT WAS A WEDNESDAY, SO THERE WERE NO DISHES IN IT FOR HIM, AND HE WILL BE ON THAT PARADE IN THE MORNINGS UNTIL SOMEBODY STOPS HIM, AND HE WILL NOT HAVE A LIST.**
