# Chapter 0530 — The Year He Cannot Say

The Wednesday of the twentieth of November 2019, from about ten to six in the morning until about twenty to one in the afternoon, in a house in this borough.

**ONE WORD A NIGHT IN WHITE CHALK ON A MIRROR THAT NOBODY HAS CLEANED SINCE THE MARCH OF 2008, AND ON THE EIGHTH OF NOVEMBER HE PUT IT TO HER AND SHE SAID SHE WOULD THINK IT OVER, AND ON THE FIFTEENTH SHE LEFT IT WHERE HE COULD REACH IT AND WENT OUT, AND IT IS THE FIFTH DAY AND HE HAS NOT STOOD NEAR ENOUGH.**

They are a man in his mid forties and a woman in her early forties, and they have been living at the same address since 2008, and there is nothing in either of their lives that anybody outside that house would think to ask about, and neither of them is unhappy.

He goes out at half past seven. She is at work until about six on a Wednesday, which is her late day, and so the mornings are the only part of the week in which the two of them are in the same building without a reason for it.

The place is a house of the kind that is all the way through, so you can hear somebody in another part of it. That is most of what is known about the arrangement between them. He said once that it was convenient and she did not disagree with him and neither of them has said anything on the subject since.

What he asked her on the eighth of November was whether he could see the thing she writes down.

Here is the thing she writes down, and it is the only reason any of this has a shape to it.

Since the March of 2008 she has put one word down every night, in white chalk, on the mirror over the small basin in the small bathroom at the back. One word. It goes low, and it goes in a different place every night, and the place moves about nine inches at a time and comes back round, and in all the years since, the bottom third of that mirror has gone into a field of small writing that goes up into the top left, where the paint has lifted.

Nobody has cleaned that mirror since the March of 2008, and that is neither of those things, and nobody in that house ever arranged it. It is only that neither of them goes into that bathroom at night, and the woman who used to come in on a Thursday used to clean it, and she has not been coming since the summer.

Once a year she reads the year back. That is all the ritual amounts to and it takes her the best part of an hour, and she does it in the last week of November, standing up, in the sequence the words went down in, going back through the year in a straight line from the bottom left up and across. She did it last in the last week of November before. She has not done it this year and it is five days off.

He has never been in that bathroom at night and has never asked to be. The asking is what he did on the eighth of November. It took him a fortnight to get to it and he did it standing in the kitchen on a Friday evening with his outdoor things over his arm.

He wanted one thing out of it. In the middle of October a woman he works with asked him, out of a corridor, nothing more than that, how long he had been where he is now, and he said a number, and the number was wrong by about a year, and he has not been able to put the number down since. That was all he wanted. Not the years. One year.

He has been wrong about a great many things for a long time and has never once been troubled by it. He is not sure now what month it was in that house, or which of the two of them mentioned the place first, or what they were both doing in the year before it, and he could not put any of that to a living soul without sounding like a man making an excuse for something. What he could not carry was a number he had given to a stranger in a corridor and had got wrong. A number is a different kind of thing. A number is the sort of thing people repeat.

He did not say that. He said, "Can I see it?" and she said, "I'll think about it," and both of those were said in the ordinary way, and neither has been raised since.

What she did on the Friday was go out. She said she was going to the shops and did not say which and he did not ask. She went at about half past ten and was back at a quarter past midday, and before she went she was in the small bathroom, and he was in the kitchen and heard the water run and nothing else.

He has not stood close enough to read one word of it in five days.

---

This morning he was up first, as he is most mornings. He came down at about ten to seven and put the outside water on and stood in the kitchen drinking it, which he has done since he was a boy and which is the one thing either of them does here before about eight.

He went into the small bathroom at about ten past seven.

There was no reason for it. There is nothing of his in there and nothing he needs in there, and he has been in that bathroom fewer than a dozen times since 2008, and what he has done since Friday is not go in.

This morning he went in.

It is a small bathroom, and the mirror is as wide as the tiling behind the basin, and it is old, and it has a black rim all round where the silver has gone, and the writing on it is white and small and it is not a thing you look at as a whole. That is the thing to understand about that mirror. It is not a thing you take in at a glance. There are about four thousand words on it and they are legible one at a time, at about ten inches, and no quicker.

He put his hands in the basin and looked at it.

The words are not in a language. He had known that without ever having checked it, and he checked it that morning by looking for about a minute at the part of the field she had done most recently, which is the part he can pick out because it is the least smudged, and it is not any language he can read, and it took him four goes to be sure of it, and then he understood that it is not a language at all, and that no amount of standing in that bathroom is going to get him a year.

What he did in the next while is not something he would be able to set out, and he has not tried. He stood about a foot back from it, which is the distance at which you cannot, and he looked at it for a while, and somewhere in the middle of that he put his right hand out and touched one word in the middle of the field, to see whether it would come off.

It came off. It came off onto his finger and it was chalk and it had been on there since some night in 2008, and where it had been was a clean patch about the size of a thumbnail, and he wiped his finger on the side of the basin and put the chalk down on the little ledge where it lives, squared up against the edge.

Then he did the other thing he came in for, which was nothing he would be able to account for, and he came out.

She was in the kitchen with one sleeve pulled down over her hand, waiting.

"You're up," she said.

"I am," he said.

She stood where she was with her outdoor things over her arm and began to say something about the time and then did not say it, and he carried his own water across to the other end of the kitchen and had to move her glass out of the way to get to the cold, and that was the two of them, at about a quarter past seven in the morning, on the fifth day.

"It's still up," he said.

"Yes," she said.

"What time are we going?"

"Half past four," she said.

"Right," he said.

That is the whole of what was said in that kitchen that morning, and she has not told anybody it happened, and he has not either, and there are two people alive who know it and one of them was the one who said it.

---

He went to work at half past seven, as he does, and she does not go out until a quarter to four, and they have gone at different times for years and neither of them has ever arranged it.

He got in at twenty to one. The house was cold and he put the water on again and stood in the kitchen with it, and he did not go into the small bathroom, and there was a word on that mirror that had not been there when he left, because she had been in there at some point in the afternoon.

It was in a new place. About nine inches along from the one she had put down the night before.

He knows the way it goes now. He has been shown it by the clean patch his own thumb made at ten past seven that morning, which is a place that has been left out of the sequence for good, and it is the only mark either of them has ever made in it.

**SHE PUTS ONE WORD DOWN EVERY NIGHT AND HE PUT A CLEAN PATCH THROUGH THE MIDDLE OF IT WITH HIS OWN THUMB, AND ON FRIDAY SHE WILL FIND THE HOLE IN THE FIELD AND WILL NOT ASK HIM ABOUT IT, AND IT IS THE FIRST THING THAT HOUSE HAS TAKEN OFF SOMEBODY.**
