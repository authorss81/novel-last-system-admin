# Chapter 0923 — Along The Hairline Crack

Sunday the seventh of July 2024, at the cracked concrete plinth at the lock-up while the street stays quiet past eight. Along a hairline crack.

**SHE HAS A CRACKED CONCRETE PLINTH AT THE LOCK-UP WITH A HAIRLINE ALONG ITS EDGE AND SHE MEANS TO DRAW THE HEEL OF THE HAND ALONG THAT CRACK WITH FINGERS LIFTED AND THE WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK IS AT THE FRONT ARCH FROM THE CONCRETE WITH A PENCIL BEHIND HER EAR.**

The plinth was cast with the bay when the lock-up went up, and the doors over it have twice been re-hung while the plinth stayed put. It is a plinth of pale concrete about nine inches high, with the edge sharp and a hairline running its length, and sand in its lower end where the yard wash lodged it.

She wants that hairline, where the weather gets in and the grit settles. The poured face of the plinth belongs to the yard, and the hairline along it belongs to the ground underneath. So the hand-heel travels the plinth hairline fingers clear. The concrete feels cool under the heel, and the crack lips catch where grains line them.

Run the length of a hand and the heel comes up against sand packed at the lower end, pale and sharp, with a grass seed wedged where the wind left it weeks past.

She bears the heel left, then right against the sand, and the grains yield a quarter of an inch under the bone and pack again as the heel comes back. Back of that hand the concrete gives nothing but itself: poured dense in the yard, seasoned on the outside by rain, hard as a hearthstone at the core.

Long as it runs, the hairline ends eyeless: a finger of depth, sand rammed at the foot, the grains bedding the rim for crossing feet. She draws the heel off the crack and dusts the grains off on the side of the plinth, and stands with the bay at her back while the street wakes.

Pencil behind her ear, the booking woman gains the front arch off the concrete, leaving everything folded.

"Is the bay shut past dinner?"

"It shuts itself at four."

"My late peas have filled out."

"They fill soonest against the sunny fence."

She shifted the pencil from one ear to the other and kept it behind her ear where the dust could not reach it, and stood in the arch with the pencil still behind her ear, and then she took the pencil on across the yard with the arch left empty behind her.

Concrete wants a job and a plinth has had three of them. It is where the spare goes when a bay is shuttered, and the mouth of the lock-up is where it sits till it is wanted for a kerb.

Neighbouring bays lie dark and oiled across one face from a winter under engines, while this plinth cures pale and dry, swept by the broom all winter.

Pale-cast concrete sinks into the earth it sits on, while dark-cast stuff perches atop. The dark casting turns rain with a sealed skin, and the pale casting sips a little before drying, settling into the dirt till the dirt grips it.

---

Where boots have crossed the plinth the dew stands in pearls, and every pearl that falls off it leaves the crown a shade barer. Seasons have gone coarse with the rim of that plinth and the lips of the hairline with them. The sand at the foot is coarse in its own way, every grain of it standing on its own.

Heel-roughened concrete abrades gently with each crossing toward brightness, brightest mid-plinth where treads fall, dim toward the tips. Oil on a face keeps the damp in it till midday, and bare concrete under the same rain has been dry an hour. East crown coarse, crack in shadow: dawn dries the bright side ahead, night damp lingering west till supper.

Drought dusts the plinth crown over, and rain packs it dense again, each packing driving slack sand off and leaving solid mix. The hairline travels a palm's length and a nail deep, closing at its far tip where both lips press cheek to cheek on a dust film.

Meeting dust lifts away over a season or two and gets brushed home, while open-crack sand knits and stays put. Ants keep the crack archive, dense where sand rides highest under the rim, sparse where the core pinches the mouth.

Warm-end ants ferry grains out at sunrise and crumbs in at dusk, paving their tunnel sides with grit a fingertip reads but a nail cannot pry. Spring-cut runs refill rim-first, channels last, an outside-in mending that stamps old work. Door shade nurses the damp after the yard pales with dust, and June marks the shift along the sill.

Shower water off the doors gathers above the high end of the plinth and drops together on the crack. The crack drinks the middle of the drop and the rim sheds the rest along, and the packed sand narrows the run.

Beyond the crack the plinth sheds its own wet straight off its end, so the tip dries overhead while the crack stays damp along through winter. That weather has beaten on the plinth since the previous door painting, its seat true to a finger's width.

The doors have polished their runner down by a coin's width across the run of three winters. That fleck, once arch-flush from the lock-up painting, lies a shade lower now.

What is left of that fleck sits under the lintel where the doors shut across it, and the bay carries no second mark anywhere on it, which says the doors have come straight down all along. Hairline sand lies wan and keen, sown with dusky seeds dormant in cold months and due astir by August.

The spider that lodges in that hairline has the run to itself, and the grit at the sand mouth shifts loose under a fingertip that only brushes it. It gives itself away in silk across the shaded rim, and there has been no silk over the high end since the oiling.

Door ledges meet boards clean, runner bars meet lugs clean out of the wet, and yard flags meet soil clean.

The hasp on the near door carries a leather washer under its plate, tawny hide that swells in rain and grips the plate tight. The plate screws bite through hide into timber, slots all aligned north-south by the fitter's habit. One slot bears the bright scar of a slipped driver, a silvered nick the rust has not claimed. The arch over the bay spans in soldier bricks, upright soldiers shoulder to shoulder, their feet on stone skewbacks. A soldier mid-arch sits a whisper forward, nudged by settlement, casting a needle shadow at noon that creeps a finger along the plinth by one o'clock, a sundial no mason intended yet faithful to this one wall, keeping better time for the plinth than sun does for the lane, and the doors mind it, hesitating a breath at noon when the shadow crosses the crack.

Bolts through the door frames run an inch proud inside, nutted with square nuts gone dark. Boards leaning spare inside the bay wait their turn on the bench, pine sides silvered at the arrises. Stickers between the spare boards lift each course a finger, oak laths that drink the damp first. The top sticker cups upward, bellied by seasons of sun on its crown and shade beneath. Bends in the runner bar ease the doors round the bay curve, shallow arcs forged in, bright on the wearing flank.

Boards grained the concrete while it was green, and that graining is coarse under a palm to this day. Sand struck the arch bricks coarse, and the soffit of the lintel is coarse where the float skipped. Soap rubbed on the pencil shelf takes one thumb-wide tablet out of it, a grey slate the rain inks and rinses yearly. Tablet smoothness shows where figures are worked, sleek amid rough, hollowed a hair by the pencil.

She surveys the plinth down its length from the arch, doors closed overhead and crack hoarding sand below. A crown weathered rough divides morning rain in sheets, and every sheet scours the concrete cleaner, for loose meal parts off and close mix stands its ground.

The plinth bears where the yard packed it, and the sand stays in its lower end, yet the doors run above it.
