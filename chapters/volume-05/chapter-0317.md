# Chapter 0317 — A Length of Four Inch

Thursday the twenty-seventh of April 2017, from about ten in the morning, the first floor of a building off Cleeve Street in Tideglass.

**A MAN IN HIS THIRTIES HAD A LENGTH OF FOUR INCH PIPE STUCK UP THROUGH A CEILING ON A LANDING WITH ONE END ON A STEP AND THE OTHER ABOUT SEVEN FOOT IN THE AIR, AND THE JOB AT THE END OF IT WILL NOT GO ALONE, AND THE ONLY PERSON ON THAT LANDING THAT MORNING SAID SHE WAS NOT GOING TO BE THE ONE WHO HOLDS IT.**

The building is one of four off Cleeve Street and it goes up three floors, and on the first floor there is a landing with a fire door at the bottom of the stair, two flat doors, and a step up into each of them about nine inches high, and the carpet on that landing came up about a fortnight ago because the woman who lives in the top flat has had a man in with a gauge on the hot water, and it is rolled in two rolls against the wall by the top flat's door.

The soil stack on that landing goes up through the ceiling of the ground floor flat and comes out in the middle of the landing floor about a foot in from the top flat's door, and it is a four inch, and it is an old cast iron one with a hopper into it out of the top flat's bathroom, and in the week before that, a man from a firm that does that work had gone down at the bottom of that stack in the ground floor flat and fitted a new length onto the top of the old one and had gone, and had left a length of open pipe about three foot up out of the floor of the landing with a hopper on the side of it, and the last man through that building left the water off at the stop and nothing else.

The man in his thirties had been up at about half nine to look at it.

What he had in the bag when he came up that stair was a length of four inch, about two foot, and a coupling, and a length of hemp, and a caulk iron, and a spirit level, and, in the other bag, four foot of scaffold tube, which is in that van because a man cannot go out in a van in this town without something long in it.

You cannot put a coupling onto a length of pipe that is standing up in the air at the end of itself. The joint has to come to you, or the pipe has to be held while you make it. One man can hold a length of four inch in a vice low down and work up to it, but not on a stack that comes out of a floor six foot under a landing and points at the ceiling.

So he got the top of the old stack cut off with a multitool — that is the only part of the job he could do alone — and then he got it up through the hole in the ceiling on a cord, and it came out into the light on that landing, and the two of them, the pipe and the hole, are what you are looking at.

He stood it on its end. Its bottom end is on the step up into the top flat, and its top end is about seven foot in the air, and it is leaning about two foot off the vertical against the wall, and it has a hopper on the side of it two foot up, and there is a length of ceiling plaster dust on the landing about a foot across where the hole came through.

---

The woman who works in a shop came up that stair at about ten past ten with her cardigan over her arm, because she is in the top flat on that landing and had heard a noise.

She stopped at the bottom of the flight.

"What's that."

"That's the stack."

"Which stack."

"The one you had done last week at the bottom," said the man in his thirties. "Your man cut a length off the top of the old one and didn't cap it, so I've had to cut another one off and there's nothing on the end of it now. So I've got a length of new four inch up through your ceiling with a hopper on it and no joint on the end of it."

"Can you do that?"

"I can do that in about a quarter of an hour," said the man in his thirties, "with two. One of us holds the top of it at seven foot while the other makes the joint underneath, and it takes four minutes to caulk a four inch if the hemp's right and it takes twenty minutes if it isn't. I want you to hold that."

"What do I hold it with."

"Your two hands," said the man in his thirties, "and something under the top of it, a bit of board, because the weight of it is at the top of it and if you let go of it the top end comes down and it goes through your ceiling again and I've had that hole out once today."

The woman who works in a shop stood at the bottom of that stair and looked up the flight at a length of four inch pipe standing on a step.

"I'm not doing that," she said. "I'm not standing under that holding a pipe in the air while somebody's got a caulk iron in the joint. That's what those three steps are for. I can see the top of that pipe from here and I can't see the end of it."

"You'd be holding the board."

"Get the man who did the bottom of it," said the woman who works in a shop. "He comes on two days a month and he does the same two. He was here on the twenty-fifth and he'll be back on the twelfth, and he'll do the top of it as well as the bottom of it, and it'll be his own joint and it'll be right, and that's how it was done in the first place."

"He's not coming today."

"He'll be back on the twelfth."

"I can't leave that in your ceiling until the twelfth."

"Then take it out of my ceiling."

"I can take it out of your ceiling. That's the other thing I can do in about ten minutes with two hands, and then you have an open stack with a hopper going down it and a hole in your landing, and whatever's in that bathroom goes down four floors onto a length of open pipe."

They stood on that stair.

"There's a hole in that ceiling either way," said the woman who works in a shop. "There's a hole in there now with a length of pipe sticking out of it and it has a hole in it that will get blocked in about six weeks and then you'll be back, and he'll still be the one who comes on those two days, and it'll be his job and he'll do it and put a piece of plaster in your landing for about twelve pounds' worth of somebody else's mess."

"That is exactly right," said the man in his thirties, "and that's the reason I'm not doing it on a step in my own time on a day off with you holding one end of it. Because if I do it now and it isn't right, you'll have a leak in your ceiling in a fortnight and you'll have to find somebody to come and look at it, and you won't have my name for anything because there is no piece of paper anywhere with my name on it, and I'll have done it for nothing and you'll have paid twice."

"So you'd rather leave a pipe in my ceiling."

"I'd rather leave a pipe in your ceiling than put a fault in it," said the man in his thirties, "and I'd rather be honest with you about which of those two is happening, and it's the first one."

---

A man came up that stair at about half past ten with a folded trolley under his arm and a set of steps in his other hand, and he had come to move a fridge out of the flat at the bottom of that stair for a woman who has moved out and is owed nothing and is not the woman who works in a shop.

He got the fridge up those steps on the trolley with one wheel at a time and it took him about four minutes, and the landing was not wide enough for two jobs, and the man in his thirties stood against the wall with his hands in his pockets and did not say anything to him about a pipe, and the man with the trolley did not offer to hold anything, and neither of them looked at the other one for the whole four minutes.

Then the man with the trolley went back down the stair with the fridge on it and the door at the bottom of that landing shut behind him, and that was the whole of what either of them ever had to do with the other.

"You could have asked him to hold it," said the woman who works in a shop.

"He had a fridge on a trolley and the width of that landing," said the man in his thirties, "and I did not know him and he did not know what I was doing up here and in about four minutes he'd have been gone. You don't get a man's arm out of a man carrying a fridge up a stair on a working morning. And he wasn't in my hire and he's not on my list and he'd have wanted paying for the four minutes, and I'd have had to write it on something."

The woman who works in a shop did not say anything to that.

What happened after that was that the man in his thirties got the cord back down the hole, undid the two screws holding the hopper, lifted the length of four inch back out of the ceiling, and took the pieces of the ceiling that came off it in his hand and put them in a bag and gave the bag to her to put in her bin.

He left the old stack capped, because he had a soil cap in the bag, and he put the cap on and tapped it home with the handle of the caulk iron.

So there is a stack on that landing with a new length on the bottom of it, capped at the top, and a hole in the ceiling about five inches across with a length of old plaster round it, and a length of four inch, a coupling, a coil of hemp and a caulk iron standing against the wall of that landing in a roll of carpet that is not on that landing to be a carpet, and a step up into the top flat with nothing on it.

Nobody gave anybody anything, and nothing was asked, and no arrangement was made with anybody, and neither of them said a word to anybody else about it in the four days that followed.

**AND IT WAS ARGUED ON A LANDING IN A BUILDING OFF CLEEVE STREET, AND ON A STEP UP INTO THE TOP FLAT, AND ON A STAIR THAT SOMEBODY ELSE WENT DOWN CARRYING A FRIDGE, AND A WOMAN WHO WORKS IN A SHOP CLOSED HER OWN DOOR AND A MAN IN HIS THIRTIES WENT OUT INTO THE STREET, AND NEITHER OF THEM WENT BACK UP.**
