# Chapter 0668 — The Stool Against The Wall

Friday the seventeenth of September 2021, in this borough. She is in her own house at number nine from dark until late with her sister at the table, and the whole of the night is a stool she does not move into use.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES LEAVES AN ASH STOOL AGAINST THE WALL OF HER OWN FRONT ROOM OPPOSITE THE WINDOW WHERE IT IS NOT A PLACE TO SIT, AND DOES NOT GO UP THAT ROAD.**

She is a woman in her late fifties. She is at home all night. Her sister is at the table for part of it.

---

She comes in from lates with her coat damp and her shoes loud on the tiles, and the house smells of soap and toast, and her sister looks up from a form from the council spread on the kitchen table.

---
They do not talk about the form at first. They talk about the bus being late and the rain on the windows, and she puts the kettle on and hangs her coat on the back of the door.

Her back door is the one she fixed herself with folded card packed at the hinge, and it shuts true now without lifting.

---

She wants to get the kitchen straight without making the stool into a thing to sit on, because the stool has been against the wall in the front room since August and that is where it is looked at.

---
She wipes the worktop and the sill over the sink, and she sweeps the floor into a pile by the back door and leaves the pile there for the morning.

On the fridge is a pad with six things on it in the order she would do them, with the first one crossed off, twice, in her own hand.

---

Her sister tells her, in the middle of something else about the council form, that a light has been put in at number twelve, and then unasked that the woman up that road could not have done that herself.

---
She stands at her own sink with a cloth in her hand and listens without asking, because asking would make it a thing she was pursuing.

She asks one question and it is only a place, and her sister tells her, and she does not ask more.

---

She works out, standing there, what she worked out on the twenty-third of August, that the man who has been in that passage is the man who made her stool, because the woman at number twelve told her on a step in May that he made it himself on a bench in a unit off the industrial estate.

---
Three rails oak and one ash. She knows the pale one by sight now without going to look.

She has never gone up that road. She is still the woman who has never gone up that road.

---

She goes through to the front room with the dusting cloth to go round the stool where it stands against the wall opposite the window, with a chair on one side of it and a settee on the other, where it is not a place to sit.

---
Her sister stays at the kitchen table with the form, and does not follow, and does not give the stool an age or a name.

She dusts the rails and the seat and the feet without lifting it right off the floor, rocking it a little to get under each foot, and she puts it back square to the wall.

---

Against that is the thank you she cannot get at, for a stool given for nothing by a stranger she has never asked a question of, and carried out of a kitchen into a front room because the kitchen is where you use a thing and the front room is where you look at one.

---
She stands back with the cloth in her hand and looks at it between the chair and the settee, and it looks right there and it looks wrong to sit on.

She does not sit on it. Nobody sits on it. That is the whole of its use.

---

Her sister calls through about a line on the form, and she goes back to the kitchen and reads the line over her sister's shoulder and says what she thinks it means, and her sister writes it in.

They drink tea at the table with the form between them, and the talk goes to ordinary things, to rotas and to a curtain rail at number ten that wants mending.

She does not mention the stool. Her sister does not mention it. It is in the next room against a wall and they both know it is there.

---

At late night her sister goes back to number ten with the form in a bag, and she bolts the back door and checks the front, and she goes through to the front room once more before bed.

The stool is still against the wall opposite the window. The chair is on one side of it and the settee is on the other.

She turns the lamp off in there and leaves the door open a little, the way she always does.

---

She gets ready for bed with the radio on low, and she thinks about the woman at number twelve eating at a kitchen table under a window on a green chair with a cushion on it, and about a gate standing open on a road with nobody on it.

She cannot get at the thank you for the stool and cannot ask anybody anything, and asking her sister one question about a place in August was the nearest she has got.

She is still the woman who has never gone up that road, and she will still be that woman in the morning.

---

She writes nothing down. The pad on the fridge stays as it was, six things with the first one crossed off, twice.

---

The woman at number ten mends what is hers without knocking up the road. The woman who clears houses keeps a drawer shut in a van. The man who built the stool leaves a drawer open in another van.

None of them is in this house tonight, and none of them has been told any of this.

---

**AND A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES HAS GOT THROUGH A NIGHT WITHOUT MOVING A STOOL FROM WHERE IT IS LOOKED AT INTO WHERE IT WOULD BE USED, AND WITHOUT GOING UP A ROAD SHE HAS NEVER GONE UP, AND THE STOOL IS STILL THREE RAILS OAK AND ONE ASH AGAINST THE WALL OF HER OWN FRONT ROOM.**

(End of chapter 0668)
