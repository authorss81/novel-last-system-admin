# Chapter 0625 — The Bin on a Wednesday

It is Monday the first of March 2021 and it is in this borough, and it starts before half past six in the morning and does not finish until about nine at night.

**A WOMAN HAS DECIDED SHE IS NOT GOING TO PUT HER NEIGHBOUR'S BIN OUT ANY MORE, AND HER HUSBAND ASKED HER WHY IN THEIR KITCHEN AND SHE TOLD HIM THE MAN HAS NEVER ONCE ASKED HER, AND THE REASON IS NOT THE REASON.**

She is a woman in her late thirties. He is a man in his mid forties. They have the end house of a terrace off a road that runs up off the top of a parade, and the bins on that road come out on a Wednesday morning and go back on the same night.

The bin next door has a red lid that does not shut all the way and it does not matter that it does not shut, because it has not been shut properly since before they came.

---

Here is the whole of what was said, and it did not take long, at the kitchen table at about a quarter to seven in the morning with the radio on.

And he said, "You'll not do it."

And she said, "I never said I wouldn't."

And he said, "You'll not do it."

And she said, "I'm not doing it."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "It's not a big thing."

And he said, "I didn't say it was."

And she said, "Well."

And he said, "Why."

And she said, "Because he never asks me."

And he said, "He never asks you."

And she said, "Not once. Not ever. It just goes out."

And he said, "You've never said."

And she said, "I've never had to."

That is the whole of it. It went like that.

Then there was the bit after, which she has been over since the middle of the morning, and the middle of the morning is when she noticed there was going to be one.

And he said, "You want me to say something to him."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "You don't want me to say anything to him."

And she said, "I've not said that."

And he said, "You've said the whole thing by saying no."

And she said, "Have I."

And he said, "It's the same as saying yes. You say no and I go out there, and if he asks me how I'm getting on I say your woman's done with your bin."

And she said, "You wouldn't say that."

And he said, "I might."

And she said, "You wouldn't."

And he said, "No," and got up, and put his plate in the sink, and stood there.

And he said, "What do you want me to do."

And she said, "Nothing."

And he said, "That's the third time you've said nothing to me before I've got my coat on."

And she said, "I know."

And he said, "Right."

And he went to work and she stood in the kitchen with the tap running.

---

Here is what she has got, and she worked it out while the water was running and she has been back over it all day.

She has made a decision about a bin.

That is the whole of the act. There is nothing else in it. A red-lidded wheelie bin stands at the kerb on a Wednesday morning and it does not get pushed out on this occasion, and that is the entire event and she has decided it and she decided it about nine at night the night before.

The rest of the day was not about the bin.

The rest of the day was about the sentence.

And she wants it on the record that she has got a reason and that it is not a made-up one and that it is true as far as it goes, because she would not have said it otherwise and she is not the sort that makes reasons up.

It is true that he has never asked her.

Not once, in the time they have been at this house. He has come out on the Wednesday morning and he has got the bin and he has taken it down to the corner and he has come back up, and he has said something back, and she has said don't be daft, and that is the arrangement and it is a good arrangement and she has never once minded it until this year.

But what she told her husband in the kitchen is not the whole of what she was thinking about at nine o'clock the night before, and she has not been able to get the rest of it to fit anywhere, and the rest of it is this.

He came out on the pavement last week and stopped.

He stood at his gate and he stopped, on a Saturday, just before midday, and he said, to the woman two doors down, that he would have to get somebody to do his bin because of his back.

And she was at her own gate. Not at his gate. Her own gate, next door but one, with the washing out, and she was close enough to hear all of it, and she carried on pegging.

And that is what she was thinking about at nine o'clock on the Sunday night, and that is not what she said in the kitchen at a quarter to seven on the Monday morning.

One: she heard it and she said nothing.

Two: she has never once said nothing about a thing and then gone on doing it. That is not a rule. It is simply what has not happened before.

Three: and this is the one that took until the middle of the morning to get to, and she would not have got to it if the radio had not been on. If he has asked somebody else to do it, then the bin was never hers. It was something that stood at the end of a row until a woman came out and took it. And the day he asks somebody else to do it is the day it stops being a kindness and starts being a job she has been doing without knowing she had it.

And she has never been given the job. Nobody ever gave it to her. She took it up, on a Wednesday, before they came, or after, and she cannot put her hand on which, and neither can he, and that is exactly the arrangement that cannot now be ended without somebody saying something.

And she is not going to be the one who says something.

So she said *because he never asks me*, in a kitchen, to her husband, at a quarter to seven on a Monday morning.

Here is what she did with the rest of it.

She took her own bin out at about a quarter past seven, which is early and which she has not done before, and she left it on the pavement at the bottom of the row with the lid up because the catch on it has been broken since before they came.

She went to work. Monday is one of her fixed days and so are Tuesday, Thursday and Friday, and the days in between are hers to do what she likes with.

And at about half past ten a woman she works with asked her why she looked as though she had not slept.

And she said, "I didn't."

And the woman said, "You look like it."

And she said, "I had a night."

And the woman said, "Anything good."

And she said, "It was quiet."

And the woman said, "Right," and went back to what she was doing, and she has not mentioned it again, and that was the whole of what happened at work.

At about half past three the man next door came out.

She was on the other side of the road with the shopping and she watched him stand at his gate with his hand on the bin, and then go back in and shut the door.

He did not knock. A man stood at his own gate with his hand on his own bin, looked at it, and shut his door, and there is a woman on this street who would have had it out in under a minute.

At about five she came back up the row and the bin was still at the kerb.

At about quarter past six he came out again with a coat on and stood next to it, and she was at her gate and he knew she was at her gate because he looked over.

And he said, "Morning."

And she said, "Mr—" and stopped, because she does not do that, and neither does he, and they have not done it since the summer.

And he said, "I can't get that down today."

And she said, "It's the back, is it."

And he said, "It's everything."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "I might get someone."

And she said, "You said that on Saturday."

And he said, "Did I."

And she said, "You said it to the woman two doors down."

And he said, "I don't know her last name."

And she said, "You said it on Saturday, on the pavement, out on the footpath, and I was out."

And he said, "I don't remember doing that."

And she said, "You don't have to."

That is where she has got to and she has been over that exchange about nine times since.

She did not say she had heard him. She did not say *you said it on Saturday and you said you might get somebody.* She gave it to him and she let him find it out himself that she had been listening at her own gate, and then she let him off it with *you don't have to*, which is the kindest thing anybody has said to him all day and she did it without deciding to.

And he said, "I'll do it Thursday."

And she said, "All right."

And he said, "It'll be out of time."

And she said, "It'll be out of time."

And he said, "Are you all right with me."

And she said, "What?"

And he said, "You never used to."

And she said, "You never used to ask."

And he said, "Ah," and went off down the row with his coat done up, and she went in and shut her gate.

He got it out on the Thursday and she watched from the upstairs window and it was out at about half past seven, which is early, and it was not tidy, and it went down to the corner and came back up.

Then she did the rest of the week.

And this is the part she will not be able to put down cleanly, and she has been over it since Friday and it is the part her husband would call being daft about a bin.

On the Thursday night, a good while after he came in and put his plate down and before the news was on, she said, "You didn't say anything to him."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "You didn't say anything."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Was that because of me."

And he said, "Which part of that."

And she said, "All of it."

And he said, "I had my coat on at seven and I was not going to walk back down there."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "Is there a thing you wanted me to do."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "That's the fourth."

And she said, "I know."

And he said, "If there is a thing, tell me what the thing is and I'll do the thing or I won't do the thing, but I can't do nothing four times because you've told me nowt."

And she said, "There's no thing."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "He never asks me."

And he said, "You've said that to me twice now."

And she said, "I know I've said it twice."

And he said, "I'm not having a go. I'm telling you I heard it."

And she said, "I know you heard it."

And he said, "Right," and turned the sound up.

So she has said it four times. She has been back and checked. Not in one go, and not because anybody asked her to, and she did not know she was doing it.

One: in the kitchen at a quarter to seven on Monday morning.

Two: on the Wednesday, to the woman at work, though not in so many words, and she does not think that counts because she did not say the rest of it.

Three: to him at the gate at quarter past six on the Wednesday, in a different shape, when he asked her whether she was all right with him.

Four: on the Thursday night, word for word, in the same kitchen, with her hands flat on the table.

And she has said it four times to a man who cannot do anything whatever about it, because the man she is talking about does not live in the house and has not asked her for anything and is not going to.

At about half past nine he came up and turned the sound down and said he was going to bed.

And she said, "Are you all right with me."

And he said, "You keep asking me that."

And she said, "You've been quiet."

And he said, "I've got the last hour."

And she said, "Is that a no."

And he said, "That's a no," and he said it kindly, which she had not expected, and she sat in the kitchen after he had gone up with the plates still on the table.

The thing she has not told him is that she would have gone on doing it.

That is the part. That is the actual finding of the day, and it came at about six o'clock on Wednesday when she was standing at the gate and he was stood at his and neither of them said anything, and what she worked out was that she has now done a thing out of a complaint, and a complaint is not the same as a reason, and if he had come to her gate on the Monday morning and said *would you put my bin out, my back's gone* then she would have said yes and meant it and never mentioned it again.

And she is not going to say that to anybody, and she is not going to go round and tell the row.

And she is not going to write down that she has said a thing four times, and she has thought about writing it down, and she would not have had the sentence in her head at all if he had not asked her four times what the thing was.

**SHE DECIDED IT ON SUNDAY NIGHT, AND SHE TOLD HER HUSBAND ON MONDAY MORNING THAT HE HAS NEVER ONCE ASKED HER, AND THE MAN NEXT DOOR PUT THE BIN OUT ON THE THURSDAY EARLY AND UNTIED, AND SHE WATCHED IT GO DOWN THE ROW FROM AN UPSTAIRS WINDOW, AND BY HER OWN COUNT SHE HAS SAID THE REASON AS MANY AS FOUR TIMES AND COULD HAVE GIVEN A BETTER ONE AT ANY POINT IN THE WEEK, AND THE BETTER ONE WAS THAT SHE WOULD HAVE SAID YES.**
