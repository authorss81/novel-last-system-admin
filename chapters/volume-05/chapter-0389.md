# Chapter 0389 — Two Rails

Tuesday the sixth of February 2018, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past nine at night, a yard behind a row of workshops, in Tideglass.

**A LENGTH OF RAIL ON TWO SET OF BRACKETS WENT UP INSIDE AN OUTBUILDING AT THE BACK OF THAT YARD ON THE MORNING OF THE SIXTH OF FEBRUARY, AND THERE IS ANOTHER LENGTH OF RAIL ON ANOTHER SET OF BRACKETS INSIDE THAT SAME BUILDING ABOUT THIRTEEN INCH ABOVE IT AND THAT ONE WENT UP IN THE MIDDLE OF JANUARY, AND NEITHER MAN KNOWS ABOUT THE OTHER ONE, AND A WOMAN WHO HAS THE LETTING OF THE SHOP ALONGSIDE THAT YARD WENT IN THERE ON THE AFTERNOON OF THE SIXTH, STOOD IN THAT BUILDING FOR FOUR MINUTES, AND WENT OUT HAVING SEEN ONE OF THEM.**

The row is six units, and the yard behind it is about seventy foot wide and the full depth of the units and it is hardstanding, not concrete, and there is a gate in the middle of it off a turning that nobody uses for anything but going in. Two lorries stand in it. There is an outbuilding along the back of it about thirty foot long and twelve foot deep, with a felted roof and a corrugated skin, and a door on the front of it that does not shut, and it has been in that yard longer than anybody there can remember.

The man in his forties trades out of the front of the second unit and has the use of the outbuilding. He has had it for about three years and there is nothing in it that belongs to anybody and nothing in it that is his.

The woman has the letting of the fourth unit, has had it since 2018, and goes in that yard when the collection is, and when the man who owns the third unit leaves something in that outbuilding for her, which he does perhaps twice a year and always in a box with his name on the side of it.

---

The first rail went up in the middle of January.

The man in his forties went in there on the Thursday of the fifteenth with about eight foot of slotted rail and two of the brackets that hook over the top of a course of brick, and he put them on the brick at the back of that building at about five foot off the floor and hung the rail off them and tapped it level with the heel of his hand.

The brackets are the kind that go over the top and tighten with a screw underneath, so there is no hole in the brick at all, and the whole thing comes off that wall in about a minute if anybody ever wants it to.

He put three hooks in the rail. They are ordinary coat hooks screwed into the rail itself, and he has hung a coil of cable and a bag of bibs and a coil of cord on it, and that is the whole of what that rail is for.

He is a man of a certain height, and a man of a certain height does not get a box out of a cupboard to reach a bag of bibs. Five foot is the right height for a man of a certain height and he is right and he would say so.

The second rail went up on the morning of the sixth of February.

The man in his thirties had been in there on the Saturday before with a tape and he had stood and looked at that building for about ten minutes and worked out what he was doing, and what he was doing was a bench.

He is building a bench in there. Not to work at, because it is not his and the light is not good enough, but to put things on, and the one thing he has to put things on more than anything else is sheet material, and he has four sheets of it leaning against the brick at the back of that building where they have been leaning since the middle of January because the floor of that outbuilding is not level and they will not stand up on their own.

He wants the rail above the top of the bench and out of the way of the sheets.

So he took his own rail and his own brackets on the Tuesday, at about eight in the morning, and he put the two boxes the fittings come in down on the hardstanding and stood on those, and he screwed his two brackets into the brick at about six foot two above the floor and hung the rail off them.

His brackets are ordinary, and they go in, and there are four holes in that brick with a plug in each one.

---

They are thirteen inch apart.

The first rail is at about five foot. The second is at about six foot two. They are both on the same four foot of brick at the back of that building and they are both about five foot long and they run in the same direction, and from where a man stands in that doorway you cannot see one of them at all unless you go in.

There is a stack of four sheets of plywood leaning against the brick in front of the lower one, and the upper one is behind the top of that stack, and it has nothing on it.

Neither of them has seen the other's.

The man in his thirties went in there on the Saturday, and on the Tuesday, and on the Thursday of the week before, and the man in his forties had his rail up through all of that with three hooks in it and a bag of bibs hanging on it. The man in his thirties went in and put the sheets back against the brick on the Saturday and would have had his back to it. He did not turn round. There is nothing on a rail that says it is the only one, and a rail does not make a sound.

The man in his forties has been in that outbuilding four or five times since the fifteenth of January and the top of his hooks is about five foot three and the second rail is at six foot two, and a man cannot see a rail that is over his own head, and he has not looked at the top of that brickwork since he took a coil of cable off those hooks in January.

She went in there at about half past two on the afternoon of the sixth.

The man who owns the third unit had left a box in there on the Friday with his name on the side of it, and it is always in there when he says it is, and she goes and gets it. There is no arrangement about it and neither of them rings the other about it; it is a thing that is left and a thing that is taken and it has happened about twice a year for nine years.

She let herself in with her own key to that outbuilding, which she has had the whole nine years and which is the only reason the man who owns the third unit is able to leave things in there.

She was in there for about four minutes. She got the box out and put it on the floor by the front of the building while she took her coat off her other arm, and she stood up and looked about the way you do when you have been in a building you have not been in for a while.

She saw a rail with brackets on it and three hooks in it and a bag hanging off one of the hooks and a coil of cable on another.

That is what she saw. She did not see the second rail, and she did not know there was a second rail, and she has not been back in there since and would not see it if she went, because the sheets are leaning in front of it and the light in that building is from one high window at the front and a rail thirteen inch above another one at the back of a dark outbuilding is not a thing anybody sees.

Neither of them will raise it, and there is nothing there worth raising.

The man in his thirties, if the man in his forties ever found out that there was a rail above his, would not call it a mistake. It is above a bench that is two foot deep and it is out of the way of four sheets of plywood and it is a perfectly good height for what it is for. It happens to be in the same four foot of brick as a rail that is five foot off the floor, and that is what happens when two men use the same outbuilding and neither of them is the owner of it.

The man in his forties, if he found out, would not call it a mistake either. It is a rail on brackets at six foot two for a man who is building a bench, and it is not in the way of anything of his, and a man who had been told that there was a rail over his rail would say it was a bit close and then go out.

There is no version of either of them saying anything about it and neither of them is going to go and look.

**SHE WENT BACK IN THERE IN THE FIRST WEEK OF APRIL FOR ANOTHER BOX AND STOOD IN THAT BUILDING FOR FOUR MINUTES AND DID NOT LOOK AT THE BRICK AT THE BACK OF IT AT ALL, AND THE MAN IN HIS THIRTIES HAS BEEN IN AND OUT OF THAT YARD A GOOD MANY TIMES SINCE THE SIXTH OF FEBRUARY.**
