# Chapter 0605 — The Wood Up Three Flights

The Friday of the twentieth of November 2020. It begins at about ten in the morning and it is not finished until about half past four in the afternoon. In this borough.

**HE CARRIES A BAG OF WOOD UP THREE FLIGHTS TO A FLAT ON THE TOP FLOOR AND HAS DONE SINCE BEFORE THE SUMMER, AND TODAY HE IS TOLD BY THE MAN WHO OWNS THE BLOCK THAT THE FIRE IS NOT WANTED, AND HE CARRIES IT UP AGAIN ANYWAY, AND HE CARRIES IT UP ON THE SATURDAY AS WELL.**

He is a man in his late thirties. The woman he takes it to is in her early fifties and she is on the top floor of a block of flats about eight hundred yards from where he lives, which is not far, and which he has been walking since the June.

The wood is his. He gets it from a yard at the back of a row of shopkeepers and he pays by the half-bag, and he buys it early in the week and it stands in his own back yard until it is dry, and a bag takes about three goes up the stairs on his shoulder and about the same coming down with his other hand on the rail.

It is not a favour. That is worth saying first. She asked him in June whether he knew anybody, and he said he could do it, and he has never asked her for anything and she has never offered.

Here is how the wood is done, because the doing of it is most of what he knows and it is not complicated.

You do not bring wood green. A green log smokes and it puts a black mark on a chimney plate and it will not catch when it matters. You bring it as dry as the yard will give you, and then it stands in your own yard under something with a roof on it, and it stands there until it has got the weight gone out of it, and you find that out by breaking one across your knee and listening to it rather than by looking at it.

That is the whole of his method and he has never written it down and nobody has asked him.

---

The block is three storeys and the top floor is up the last flight, which is the one everybody puts off because the flight turns at the top and you cannot get a bag round the turn with your shoulder under it. He has been up this flight maybe two hundred times since the June. He takes the bag on his shoulder to the turn, puts it down on the landing, turns it round on the ball of his foot, takes it on the other shoulder, and goes the last six steps.

He was on the second landing at about half past ten this morning.

She had the door open before he got to the top because she heard the bag on the rail.

And she said, "Put it there. Mind the step."

And he said, "It's all right."

She has a mat and a bit of bare board by the door and she has been putting it there since June and the board has gone the colour of tea.

He put the bag down and she shut the door behind him while he was still on the mat, and stood in the hall in a dressing gown with her hair up, and said, "The nephew came."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "He came on the Monday. He came all the way up and he looked at it and he said what my brother said, which is that the gas man can come on the Monday and the fire isn't wanted."

And he said, "Is that the brother's?"

And she said, "That's the brother's. He wrote it down on a bit of paper and I couldn't read half of it."

He had his hand on the bag.

And he said, "Where'll he want it?"

And she said, "I don't know. Down the stairs, I suppose."

He did not open the bag in the hall. He took it through to the alcove by the radiator, which is where it has been since June, and he undid the tape at the top and turned the bag over onto its side and took the wood out the way he always takes it out, which is the driest at the bottom and the dirtiest on top, because the bottom is the one that got used.

The alcove holds about two thirds of a bag standing up and the rest lies along the wall under the window.

He put it in the same way he has put it in every single time since the June. He stood the round pieces on their ends at the back, because they hold the heat better and they go out slower, and he put the split pieces in front of them on the flat with the cut face out, and he left a space at the left-hand end for the kindling tin, which is a sardine tin with a bit of card round it.

She watched him do all of it from the doorway.

And she said, "There's no need to bring it if it's not wanted."

And he said, "It's not been wanted since the June. You never said it was."

And she said, "Well."

And he said, "Are you using the fire today?"

And she said, "I had it on before you got to the top."

He went down the six steps and turned the bag round on the landing and took it on the other shoulder for the flight, and he was near the bottom when he saw the man who owns the block coming out of the door of the office at the side.

The man is in it for as much as he owns the block, and that is a set of keys on a ring and a way of standing about eight feet away from people.

And he said, "You're the one with the wood."

And he said, "I am."

And the man said, "Her nephew was up on the Monday and he's not coming back. Gas man's coming at ten on the Monday and after that there's no fire in that flat."

And he said, "Right."

And the man said, "There's an inspection on the Tuesday after. Flue. If it's been lit there's nothing to look at, but if it's been lit and nobody said then somebody asks."

And he said, "I've not touched the flue."

And the man said, "I know you haven't. I'm telling you for the reason I'm telling you."

And he said, "Right."

And the man said, "There's no money in this."

And he said, "There isn't."

And the man said, "It's just a flue, that's all that is. You want to stop after Monday, stop after Monday."

And he said, "I'll see her Saturday."

And the man said, "She'll not know you're not coming unless you tell her."

And he said, "I'll tell her Saturday."

Here is the part that matters, and he did it in a few seconds on that landing and he has not been able to account for it since.

He went back up. Not to the flat — he did not knock and he did not go up the last flight. He went up the first flight to the landing on the second, and put the bag down on the landing, and stood there with his hand on the tape.

There is a landing on the second with a window in it and a bike on a chain and a smell of somebody's cooking off one of the doors, and he stood in it and worked out, quite calmly and one after the other, the following four things.

One: the block has a flue and the flue has an inspection on the Tuesday and a man coming round with a pad on a clipboard. A flue is a thing, and he has been feeding it since June without knowing there was an inspection coming.

Two: if the fire stops on the Monday then she will be cold, and she is on her own, and the boiler has not worked since June, and the gas man is coming to a flat that has a flue in it and no fire.

Three: it is not his fire and it is not his flat and he has never once asked her a question about it.

Four: he has no way of telling the difference between the first three and the fourth, and all four of them are reasons, and he could give any one of them out loud and it would be true enough that she would not push it.

And then he thought, on that landing, with his hand on the tape: she has not asked me once why.

That is the sentence. Not the wood, and not the Tuesday, and not the inspection. She has asked him nothing for months, and a woman who asks you nothing is a woman who has decided something, and he has been reading that as being about the wood, and it is not about the wood.

He picked the bag up and carried it up the last flight and knocked, and she opened it.

And he said, "Saturday I'll fill the log basket as well."

And she said, "That's not wanted."

And he said, "I know," and he put the bag down on the board by the door and he went down the last six steps and turned it round on the landing and went down the flight.

---

He went home at about twelve and put his coat over the back of a chair and stood in his own kitchen.

He lives alone and there is a wood stove in his own kitchen that he bought in the March and has used since, and the two bags under the sink are his, and the wood in them is the same wood and it came off the same yard, and the difference between his and hers is however long it has stood in his own yard under a roof.

That is what he is carrying. It is not a sack of logs and it is not a favour. He is carrying the standing of it, and there is nowhere on earth for that to go, and he has known all of that since the June, and he has gone up that flight about two hundred times without once stopping to work out what the running total is.

He sat down and then stood up again because sitting down is when he would have thought about it properly.

He went to the yard and got his own barrow and loaded the last of the driest at the back and got the tarpaulin across it and weighed the four corners down with two bricks, and did that competently, because he can do that.

And then he went in and washed his hands and had his lunch standing at the board and he looked at the phone on the table and there was a text from a man about a van and a date and about money, and he read it and put the phone down and did not answer it, and he would not be able to tell anybody afterwards why.

He came back at about half past four in the afternoon.

It had gone dark early and there was a yellow light coming off the parade at the bottom of the road and the last bit of sky over the roofs behind it was a colour he does not have a word for.

He carried it up at a quarter to five.

She was not in, and the door was shut, and he put the bag on the board by the mat, where it has been each single time since June, and he knocked anyway and stood there while he counted to about ten and went down.

And going down he thought, at the turn, with his hand on the rail: whatever she asks me on Saturday, the answer is the same, and the answer is that I will be here on Sunday as well, and the reason I will give her is that the wood will be there for the nurse, and there is no nurse, and I have known that since the June.

**HE CARRIES A BAG OF WOOD UP THREE FLIGHTS TO A FLAT ON THE TOP FLOOR AND HAS DONE SINCE BEFORE THE SUMMER, AND THIS AFTERNOON THE MAN WHO OWNS THE BLOCK HAS TOLD HIM THE FIRE IS NOT WANTED AND HE HAS CARRIED IT UP ANYWAY AND PUT IT DOWN ON THE BOARD BY HER MAT AND KNOCKED ON A SHUT DOOR, AND HE WILL BE THERE ON THE SATURDAY AND ON THE SUNDAY, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED HIM WHY.**
