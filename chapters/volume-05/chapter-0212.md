# Chapter 0212 — An Ordinary Sunday

Sunday the twentieth of December 2015, from about two in the afternoon, a street four hundred yards up from the slip in Ravensmere.

**TWO SMALL CORRECT THINGS HAPPENED ON ONE SUNDAY AFTERNOON IN A CITY THAT IS NOT THIS ONE, AND NEITHER PERSON WAS ON ANYTHING, AND NOBODY FOUND OUT, AND IT IS THE THIRD TIME IN THREE MONTHS THAT A CORRECT THING HAS BEEN DONE IN A STREET BY SOMEBODY WHO IS ON NOTHING AND NOTHING HAS COME OF IT, THE FIRST BEING A DOORWAY KEPT DRY WITH A PUMP AND THE SECOND BEING A GULLY CLEARED WITH A BAR, AND THE ONLY PERSON WHO KNOWS BOTH OF THESE HAPPENED WALKED DOWN THE SAME STREET TWICE.**

The group does not sit on a Sunday. There is nothing on the fourth of December's papers for a Sunday and there is nothing for the Sunday after, and nobody had given Jonas Mercer anything to do in this city and nobody was going to, and on the Monday after this one he would get a boat back across the water.

The weather was flat and grey and not cold and the town was doing what a town does on a Sunday in December, which is a great deal of nothing with a church on it and two buses and a queue outside a chemist's.

The queue outside the chemist's had about seven people in it and Jonas Mercer was not one of them and stood about a yard to the side of it, the way a man stands.

The man of about eighty-four was in it, third from the front, with a basket on his arm and a folded newspaper under it. He was in front of a woman with two children and behind a man in a fleece. He read the shelf label on his right as he got to it, and then he read it again, and then he said, to nobody in particular and quite clearly, "That's the wrong price."

The woman at the counter said, "Which one."

"That one." He put his finger on it without touching it. "It's the same bottle in the window and the window is ninety-one pence and that is on the shelf at a pound four, and I've had it in this basket since October, and it's not a shelf and a window, it's a shelf and a window in the same shop."

It was, in fact, a different bottle. The one on the shelf was the larger one and the one in the window was the smaller one, and the man of about eighty-four was wrong about that, and everybody standing behind him waited to find out how wrong he was.

The woman at the counter said, "That's a big one and that's a small one, love."

"Then it's a bad label," he said. "And you'd not want me to take the small one and pay the big one's money, and you wouldn't want the next person down this queue to do it either, because you haven't got time to check every time and neither have they." He lifted the basket a little. "Put a card on the big one."

There was about four seconds of the shop not doing anything.

Then she said, "We're not doing cards on a Sunday."

"Then do it on a Monday," said the man of about eighty-four, "and do it before anybody's paid, and I don't mind. I'll be in on the Monday at about ten."

"I'll put a label in," she said. "I can't do a card."

"A label's a card," he said. "A label's a card with a price on it. I'll take the small one for the small money and you put the label in and nobody's been done out of anything."

He took the bottle and he paid the price on the window and he went out, and the queue went on, and the two children looked at the bottle, and the woman at the counter wrote a note on the back of her hand and put the bottle in a different place on the shelf where she would not forget about it, which is a system, and it is the whole of the system.

Nobody saw the man of about eighty-four again that afternoon. Jonas Mercer did not go after him and did not speak to him. Nobody in that shop is on anything and neither is he, and nobody is going to find out.

Jonas Mercer walked up the street to the bus shelter at about twenty past two because the bus was due.

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THE INSIDE OF THAT SHELTER THAT AFTERNOON, printed at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A TIME PRINTED SOMEWHERE IS NOT A TIME GIVEN TO ANYBODY.**
> **A SHEET ON A WALL IS A SHEET ON A WALL.**
> **NEITHER OF THOSE IS A PROMISE.**

The woman of about sixty-nine was already sitting on the bench with a bag of shopping on her knees and a folded timetable in her hand, and there was nobody else at the shelter and there was not going to be anybody else, because it was Sunday and it was the wrong end of a route.

"The four o'clock's not at four," she said, to Jonas Mercer, without any preamble at all. "It's at about ten to."

"How do you know."

"Because that's what it says." She put the timetable down on the bench and turned it round so he could see it, and it was the printed one from a shop in the third town, and it was a different year's, and she had a pencil out and a mark against the Sunday services in it. "Look at that. That's a timetable. It hasn't been a timetable since they changed the Sunday service and nobody reprinted it, and it's in three shops in this town and it's not in the shelter, and the shelter's got the old board up with the old times on it, and the board and the timetable are not even out of the same year, which I don't think anybody has worked out."

"The driver would tell you," said Jonas Mercer.

"The driver's been driving it since the change," she said. "He knows. He doesn't say. You get on and he goes at ten to four and he looks at you in the mirror once and you can say what you like after that." She put the timetable back in her bag. "And I am not going to stand in a shelter and shout at a bus driver about a printed sheet on a Sunday, and neither are you, and the four o'clock is at ten to and it will be at ten to until somebody who owns the printing says different, and I have not got an idea who that is and neither have you."

The bus came at about ten to four and Jonas Mercer got on it and she got on it, and neither of them said anything else, and the driver looked in the mirror once at about the second stop.

Jonas Mercer got off near the slip at about half past four, and the light had not changed and the water was where it had been in the morning and there was nobody on it.

**AND THE SECOND THING HAPPENED AT ABOUT TWENTY PAST TWO AND THE FIRST THING HAPPENED AT ABOUT TEN TO THREE, AND THEY WERE FOUR HUNDRED YARDS APART ON THE SAME STREET, AND JONAS MERCER WALKED IT TWICE, AND HE TOLD NEITHER OF THE TWO PEOPLE ANYTHING AND NEITHER OF THEM KNEW ANYTHING ABOUT THE OTHER, AND BY MONDAY MORNING THERE WAS A LABEL ON A SHELF IN A CHEMIST'S AND A TIMETABLE IN A BAG AND NOTHING ELSE ANYWHERE IN EITHER CITY.**

The chemist on the corner put a label in on the Monday at about ten past nine, and it says the same as the window, and nobody on that street has asked anybody about it since.
