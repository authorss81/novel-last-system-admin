# Chapter 0411 — The Cage

Tuesday the fifteenth of May 2018, starting at about half past nine in the morning and running until about ten to seven that evening, a shop off that parade, and an unnamed lane at the end of another street, in Tideglass.

**A WOMAN BEHIND A COUNTER IN THAT SHOP PUT A WIRE BIRD CAGE INTO A MAN'S HAND ON ONE MORNING IN MAY BECAUSE SHE HAD NO MORE ROOM AND NO MORE BAGS, AND HE CARRIED IT IN HIS VAN FOR THIRTEEN DAYS AND PUT IT DOWN AGAINST THE INSIDE OF A BRICK PIER AT THE BOTTOM OF A LANE HE WAS IN ONCE IN FEBRUARY, AND SHE HAS NOT COME LOOKING AND IS NOT GOING TO AND NEITHER OF THEM WILL RAISE IT.**

That shop is the one about sixty yard back from the parade, and she shuts it herself, and that counter is hers from ten in the morning, and it has been the same two times for two years.

What she has been losing to since the second week of May is the room at the back of it. There is more going into that room than will ever come out of it, and she cannot get at it, because a heap of offcuts stands in the corner by that fire door that goes up about two foot and that has to be shifted first.

On the Monday of the fourteenth she took nine things out of that store and put them in the yard at the back in a pile by the wall, and covered the pile with a sheet of polythene weighted with two lumps of block, and it was still raining lightly on the Tuesday.

---

She was doing the Monday and the Tuesday of that week in that yard.

The thing on the top of that pile on that Tuesday morning was a birdcage. It is a dome standing on a base, about eighteen inch high and fourteen across where the base sits, painted green over white, and the green has gone chalky and flaked on the dome where the weather got at it in about 2009.

The door of it is on the front and it is a frame of wire with a hinge on one side that does not do anything, because the pin of the hinge is not in it and it has not been in it since about 2015. The door will lift out about half an inch and no further and it will not stay there. There is a dish clipped inside on the left and a perch across the back of it, and a door in the top of the dome with a ring on it and no hasp on it, so that nothing can be shut inside that cage and nothing ever was.

There is a ring-mark in the white paint of the base about the size of a shilling and about a year old, and the bottom of that cage has a bit of dried something in the grooves of it that comes out of it in three or four little hard pieces.

It was in that store since the last week of February, when a man brought six things into that shop that he had out of a house and could not keep and would not take home, and she put them in that store because there was nowhere else, and it has been at the top of that pile of nine things since the Monday of the fourteenth.

He was in that shop at about half past ten on the Tuesday for a length of conduit and a roll of DPC and a box of plugs he did not need that morning.

He came in and put his bag down on the boards and went to the end of that counter and said, "Can you cut that conduit in two, there, and there."

She had about six feet of a bundle of conduit and about three hundred weight of parcel tape and the bag of nails from the morning and about nine other people's small jobs on that counter in front of her, and she had no more bags, and the bin at the end of that shop had a flattened paper bag in it and the bin was full.

She said, "Hold that, would you," and put the cage into his hands.

He took it because his hands were there.

It weighs about four pounds. He stood holding it in his left hand with his right under it while she measured the conduit and marked it, and she went and got a sheet of newspaper to put the cut pieces on, and then he had to move it to his other hand so that he could get his money out.

He put it down on the floor at his feet while he paid, and she looked at it on the floor and said, "That came in with six other things on Friday and I have been standing in a yard in the rain about them for two days."

He said, "Is that one yours."

"No," she said. "Nobody's. That's why it's been in the yard."

Then he picked it up, dropped it in behind the seats of his own van, and that was all either of them ever said about it.

The man in his thirties was standing about ten foot off at the middle of that counter with a box of plugs when she put that cage into that man's hands. He looked across at it. He said, "There's a door on that thing that comes out."

"There is," she said, "and it doesn't."

And he said, "Right," and paid for his plugs and went.

That is the whole of what any of the three of them said about that cage on that morning, and not one of the three has come back to it since, and it will not be raised.

It lived in his own van for thirteen days.

There was no use for it in him and nothing in that week, and nothing the week after either, that would make a man stop and consider a thing like that. It lay on the boards behind that seat, on its side, with the dome jammed against the door, which is the only way it goes.

It moved about four times a day. It went on the seat when he wanted the front of that van. It came off the seat when he wanted the middle of that van. It came out entirely for one job, on the Friday of the twenty-fifth, and was put in a passage and stood against a wall for the best part of a day, and then went back in.

He knocked plaster off one corner of that base on the first day and rubbed it away with his thumb, and that is the only time in thirteen days that he laid a hand on it.

He did not take it home. He lived in two rooms and there is no room in that, and he did not offer it to anybody and he did not think of offering it to anybody.

On the Monday of the twenty-first he saw a man in a fleece put a kettle on a trestle table in the middle of a road and he thought about it for about a second and then thought about something else.

On the Wednesday of the sixteenth he was in a shop in a town eight miles off and there was a display of ironmongery in the window and a wire thing about the size of his head on a stand in it, and he stood outside that window for about a minute, and then went and bought his screws and came back and the cage was where it was on the floor of his van, and it was in that van on the Wednesday.

He has not taken that cage out of that van since the Friday of the twenty-fifth. It is in there now.

He set it down at ten to seven on that Friday, the twenty-fifth of May, and it took him about a minute.

He had been at the bottom of a lane off a side street that afternoon, for a job at the end of it, and that job ran about five minutes over and the light had gone at about half past five and he was about twenty foot from his own van with the last of that day's stuff in his arms.

There is a yard at the bottom of that lane, and it is not shut, because nothing at the bottom of that lane has been shut since before 2011, and the man who owns that yard is a man of about fifty-five and he has not been down that yard since about the middle of May, and there is nobody in that yard on that Friday evening in May at about a quarter to seven.

He was in that yard on the tenth of February for one morning, for a job at the far end of it that was off before lunch.

He stood the cage against the inside of the brick pier at the gate, upright, with its base on the flags and the dome under the hood of that gate so that nothing can get at it out of the weather, and it stood up on its own.

He stood and regarded it for about a second, and then carried his stuff out to his van and drove off.

He has not been down that lane since. He does not know that a gate stands open at the bottom of it and has stood open for years. He did not ask the man who owns that yard whether anybody was in, and he did not go and find out, and he did not tell anybody where he had put it.

The woman who works in a shop has not been down that lane since about the middle of March. She does not know there is a lane there, or a gate, or a yard, and put to her she would have to be told, and she would say she has never had anything out there and she would be right.

---

**HE STOOD IT UP AGAINST THE INSIDE OF A PIER UNDER THE HOOD OF A GATE THAT STANDS OPEN, AND IT IS STILL THERE, AND THE OWNER OF THAT YARD HAS NOT WALKED PAST IT TWICE.**
