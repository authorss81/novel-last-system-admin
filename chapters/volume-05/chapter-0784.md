# Chapter 0784 — The New Kerb On The Flower Bed

Tuesday the tenth of January 2023, in this borough. She is in her own house and yard at number twelve from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a new kerb bedded in cement along her flower bed, and a wall going dark behind it.

**A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-EIGHT HAS THE WOODEN KERB OF HER FLOWER BED COME AWAY FROM THE WALL SO THE BED FLOODS, AND MEANS TO PUT A BRICK ON THE KERB AND WEIGHT IT DOWN, HER SISTER LETS A NEW LENGTH IN AND BEDS IT IN CEMENT, AND THE BED IS A RAISED BED NOW AND THE WALL BEHIND IT IS TAKING EVERYTHING THAT COMES OFF IT.**

She is a woman of seventy-eight at number twelve on Tuesday. Her sister comes through the yard gate in the morning with a length of board under her arm, and does the second half her own way.

---

The stove is lit and the green chair is up at the table with the gas envelope face up square along the right-hand edge of it and the taped box under its cloth on the far side. The coal shed is shut and the padlock is open in the hasp. The gate is propped back against the wall where it has been since September. The kitchen sash has been a hand's width down in its own frame since the summer and is not touched. Through the back window the grass heap is still banked against the shed wall with the wet-tea stripe up the brick behind it, and the yard wall is capped for three feet above that heap and bare for four at the coal shed end, and neither of those is any part of this morning. A brush lies across the runner in the passage and she steps over it.

The flower bed runs along the yard wall under that capped stretch.

It is about eight feet long and four feet wide and somebody made it before she came. It has a wooden kerb along the front of it, a length of board about as deep as a hand stood on its edge with its foot in the ground and its top level with the soil, and the same board goes along the near end of it and turns the corner.

The kerb has come away from the wall.

It is not leaning over. It is standing up perfectly straight and about a finger's width away from the wall along the whole of its length, and the soil behind it has been going out from under it since the summer. The gap between the back of the kerb and the wall will take two fingers, and the ground in it has gone down about as deep as your hand, and there is a trough along the front of the bed where the soil and the leaf mould and the grit off the wall have been going under it and out into the yard.

The bed floods.

She has known it floods since before the summer. She has watched the water come off the top of that wall and down the face of it and stop at the kerb and stand along the whole of it, and the bed is a trough the length of the wall, and when there is a wind off the lane with rain in it the water comes over the kerb and out through the gap at the back and takes the top of the bed with it.

Her plan is a brick.

She has a brick and she means to lay it along the top of the kerb the whole length of the bed and weight it down, and put a couple of screws through it into the kerb and a wedge under the back of the kerb where it has come away from the wall, and that will keep it where it is and stop the water going over it.

---

Her sister comes through the yard gate in the middle of the morning with a length of board under her arm, which is the first time she has come in that way in a year.

---

"You have not used that gate for months."

"You have not been in that yard for months." Her sister turns round on the flags with the board under her arm. "Put your coat on."

---

They go and look at the kerb.

Her sister stands over the gap behind it and runs her fingers down the back of the board the whole length of it, and her thumb goes in to the first knuckle for most of it and further at the near end where the end kerb meets the side kerb.

"That has not come away from the wall."

"It has."

"It has not. It has gone soft along its whole length and the soil behind it has gone out from under it, so there is nothing behind it keeping it out any more. It is not leaning. It has nothing to lean on."

---

"So it wants keeping up."

"It wants taking out." She gets down on her knees in the bed and puts her hand down behind the kerb. "And you cannot put a brick on a piece of firewood and you cannot wedge it, because the wedge goes into the ground behind it and the ground behind it is nothing but the same stuff that has washed out. You would be wedging it into air."

"Then it wants a brick on it."

"It wants a new one."

---

She has the board under her arm and she stands it against the wall in the yard to show them what it is.

It is a length of sawn softwood board about seven feet long, an inch and a quarter thick and about six inches deep, dressed on the face, with a good straight arris on the top. It came out of a shed she took down herself in the autumn.

"That is a kerb and a bit over, and it is six inches where your old one is four."

"That is deep."

"That is what a kerb for a bed against a wall is. Yours is four inches and there is four inches of soil behind it, and a board four inches deep in four inches of soil keeps nothing up. Six inches is the board and there is soil behind it to the depth of the bed." She looks along the wall. "And you are not laying it on the ground. Nobody lays a kerb on the ground."

---

They take the old one out.

It comes out in three pieces and it is soft along its whole length in the ground line. Where it has been in the ground it is black, and where it has been above it is grey and gone furry on the wet side along about three feet.

They dig the ground behind it out to the foot of the wall until they are down to something hard, and they clear the old trench out and barrow the spoil round to the front, because there is nowhere else for it to go.

Then her sister digs a footing.

The footing is a trench along the line of the old kerb, cut as deep as her hand plus the depth of the new board, and cut hard against the face of the wall, so the new kerb stands hard against it and not a finger's width off.

She mixes cement in the yard in a barrow by the coal shed, not in the shed, which is shut, and not through the tap in the kitchen, because there is nothing in that yard that wants a hose. It takes three mixings and it is thick and strong, and it goes into the trench about half of the way up, and the new kerb is stood in it and bedded all round with more.

---

The bed is where it should be.

It is better than it ever was. She puts her hand on the top of the new kerb and pushes and it does not move and it does not give, and the soil behind it is now four inches higher than it was, the top of the bed is level with the top of the kerb, and the six inches of board has gone into the ground with four inches of it standing out.

"That is a kerb."

"That is a kerb bedded in cement against the foot of a wall, and it will be there when neither of us is." Her sister is washing the trowel off on the flags. "And that is what I said the last time and you have not heard it and you are not going to. A kerb on the ground is a thing that keeps soil in. A kerb bedded in cement against a wall is a thing that keeps soil in AND water in, and it is the second half of that job that is the whole reason you have one."

---

Then it rains in the afternoon, which is the only bit of luck either of them gets all winter, and they stand in the yard door and watch it.

The rain comes down the top of the coping on that wall and down the face of it and it does what it has done since the summer. It runs down the wall and it comes to the kerb.

It does not go over the kerb. It cannot, the kerb is higher than the soil now. It does not go under the kerb. There is nothing under it but cement.

So it goes into the bed.

It goes into the bed along the whole eight feet of it and it stands there against the wall, because the bed is a trough with a kerb at the front of it and a wall at the back of it and nothing anywhere for it to get out of, and the wall behind it is the only warm thing in that corner and the wet is going straight onto it.

They stand there for ten minutes and neither of them says anything.

Then they go out in it and she puts the back of her hand flat on the face of the wall above the new kerb, and the wall is cold, and when she takes her hand away there is a mark the shape of her hand on it, darker than the brick either side of it.
