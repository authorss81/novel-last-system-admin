# Chapter 0349 — A Bird Box on a Path

Wednesday the thirtieth of August 2017, from about half past nine in the morning until about half past four in the afternoon, the low end of a street, a shop off a parade, and that street again, in Tideglass.

**A MAN HAS SAID AT THAT STREET FIVE TIMES THAT HE WILL MAKE A BIRD BOX AND SCREW IT TO THE GABLE END OF A HOUSE AT THE LOW END OF IT BEFORE THE END OF SEPTEMBER, AND ON TUESDAY A DIFFERENT MAN CAME WITH A LADDER AND TOOK THE EXISTING ONE OFF THAT WALL AND LAID IT FACE DOWN ON THE PATH, AND ON WEDNESDAY MORNING THE FIRST MAN SAID IT FOR THE FIFTH TIME AND DID NOT DO IT.**

The street comes off the Cleeve road and stops. There are nine houses on it and a gable end at the bottom of it facing about nine foot of waste ground, and on the gable end of the house at the low end there is a bird box about the size of a shoebox on two screws, and it has been on that wall since somebody put it up and nobody in that street knows who or when, and it is painted the same colour as the render and it has a chip out of the front lip the size of a thumbnail.

There is a job on that gable which is not the bird box. It is about four foot of mortar joints on the south corner that have gone soft, and the man in his forties put a price on it in June and it has not been given to him, and he has chased it twice, and the woman who lives in that house told him in July that she was getting three other people to look at it.

The box has been on that wall through all of that and neither of those men has ever mentioned it.

---

He said it in the road at the low end of that street at about half past nine in the second week of July, to the woman who lives in the house, who was standing at her gate.

"I will make you a box and screw it up on that gable before the end of September," he said, and she said that would be nice, and neither of them has mentioned it since and she has told no one.

He said it in a yard at the top of a street in the last week of July, to a man in his forties, over a length of batten, and he said aye, and went on with the batten.

He said it on a parade in the first week of August, to a man in a fleece, outside a shop, and the man in a fleece said right and drank his coffee and went on down the parade.

He said it at a counter in a shop off that parade in the third week of August, to the woman who works in a shop, with his bag on the step, and she said that he had said that in July as well, and he said it was the third time and she was at liberty to keep count.

He had the pad of work notes in his hand when he said it that last time, with the elastic off, and she watched him put it back in the bag without writing anything on it, and she said, "You are not going to write that down."

"No," said the man in his thirties. "I have got a line in there for the letters in that shop at the bottom of the Cleeve road and I have not opened it since the summer before last, and I am not starting now."

On the Wednesday morning of this week the man in his forties went down that street with a ladder and a flat bar and a paint brush, at about half past two in the afternoon, and he was let in at number nine, and the woman who lives there had gone out.

The box is on two screws about nine foot up. He got it off with the flat bar in about a minute and a half, and the two screws came out of the render and about two foot of the head of each one came away with the plaster, and he put the screws in his pocket.

He put the box down on the path at the foot of the wall face down, the way a man puts a thing down when he cannot see where the ground is, and it is lying on the path at the foot of that gable now, face down, with the open side against the paving, and the woman who lives in that house came in at about half past five and stepped over it.

What has gone round that street is not that a man is going to make a bird box. What has gone round it is that somebody is going to have that wall done.

Two men are the source of that and neither of the two of them ever had it off him. The first of them got hold of it at a stand in a market four miles off in the second week of August and carried it to the man in his forties, and the man in his forties carried it in turn to a man in a fleece further up that road in the third week of August. No name went with it at any point along that line.

The man in his forties heard a man say somebody is going to have that wall done, and he had a quotation of his own on that wall from June that has not been given to him, and he put the two of them together at about half past two on the Tuesday, and the only thing in that arithmetic that was a mistake is the man in the forties being sure that the man in a fleece knew which wall.

It was the right wall. It is the only gable on that street that anybody has said a word about.

He came back on the Wednesday at about half past ten to do it and the box was lying on the path face down, and he stood and looked at it, and he did not put it back.

He is not going to put it back. A man in this borough who puts a thing back on a wall he has been told is being done is a man who has accepted the job, and the woman in that house is getting three other people to look at it, and he has chased it twice. He picked the two screws up off the path where they had fallen out and put them in his pocket, and he did the four foot of pointing on the Wednesday afternoon with nobody watching, and it took him about two hours, and he came away at about half past four and the job is not his and he has not charged anybody for it and has not mentioned it.

The box is still on that path. The woman who lives in that house came in over it on the Tuesday evening and moved it to the side of the path with her foot, and on the Wednesday morning she came in over it again and it went back to the middle, and she has not touched it again and has not asked anybody about it.

---

On the Wednesday morning he was at the bottom of that street by twenty to ten with his bag, and he was standing at the gable end before the man in his forties came back, and he said it for the fifth time, to a man who had not asked him for it and had not said one word about any of it.

"I will have a box made and up on that gable before the end of September," he said.

The man in his forties had the ladder over his shoulder and he put it down in the road and looked at him.

"Who is that for," he said.

"That is for whoever wants one," said the man in his thirties, and after that he turned about and looked down at the path, where a bird box was lying face down at the foot of a wall with two screws gone out of it and about two foot of plaster off around each hole.

He said nothing else at all.

He has not done it. It is the thirtieth of August and the month runs out on the thirtieth of September and there is a bird box on a path and two screws in another man's pocket and four foot of pointing on that wall that nobody has paid for.

The woman who works in a shop had him standing at her counter for about six minutes at about half past four that afternoon and he told her all of it, the fifth saying, the two screws, the box on the path and the four foot of pointing, in that order, and she listened with her hands flat on the counter.

"You have said that five times."

"That is five," said the man in his thirties.

"And you have never written it down."

"Not once."

She said right, and he paid for a bag of plugs he had come in for, and she said, "It will still be on that path on Saturday," and he said, "It will," and went out into the parade.

**A BIRD BOX LIES FACE DOWN ON A PATH AT THE LOW END OF A STREET WITH TWO SCREWS GONE OUT OF THE RENDER ABOVE IT, AND A MAN STOOD UNDER THAT GABLE AND SAID FOR THE FIFTH TIME THAT HE WOULD PUT ONE UP, AND HE DID NOT.**
