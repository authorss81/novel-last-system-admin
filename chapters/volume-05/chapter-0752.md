# Chapter 0752 — The Yard Wall

Tuesday the twenty-third of August 2022, in this borough. She is at her own lock-up from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a booking talk kept in the yard outside over a van and boards.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FORTIES STANDS WITH THE WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK IN THE YARD OUTSIDE THE LOCK-UP, TALKS THE VAN AND THE BOARDS OVER THERE, AND DOES NOT OPEN THE DOORS TO SHOW HER IN.**

She is a woman in her late forties at the lock-up on Tuesday. The woman who books work meets her in the yard in the morning, stands by the yard wall with her, and goes no further than the wall.

---

She gets to the lock-up in the morning with the white van tidy in the yard and the sun on the yard wall. She does not open the doors at once. She opens the van instead and looks in at the boards and the bolster shut in the back, the rag bag tied, the blanket set aside, the card work in its place. She shuts the van doors and tries them, and then she stands by the wall with her hands in her coat pockets, waiting, because the woman who books work is due and today the talk will be outside.

The woman comes through the yard gate with her bag over her shoulder and stops when she sees her by the wall.

"You are out."

"I am out." She stays by the wall with her shoulder to it. "You can stop here. Today the wall is the place."

The woman looks at the lock-up doors standing to behind her.

"Since when is the wall the place."

"Since today." She does not move from the wall. "Today we speak here and the doors stay to. What did you bring."

The woman pats her coat pocket where the slip lives.

"The Wednesday." She nods. "It went as booked and the woman is pleased. I came to tell you."

"I am glad of it." She stays with her shoulder to the wall. "You did not need to come for that. You could have left word."

"I wanted to see you." The woman looks past her at the van. "You have the van tidy."

"I have." She glances at it without leaving the wall. "Boards and bolster shut. Rag bag tied. You can see it from here and no closer is needed today."

---

"You will not open the doors."

"Not today." She shakes her head. "The lock-up is the bench and the box and the drawer, and today I want a morning where no one stands inside it with me, not even you."

The woman leans on the wall a little way from her.

"What did you want to speak of, then."

"The boards." She nods at the van. "How they went in and out, and how the bolster sat, and how the van ran. I wanted you to hear it here with the sun on the wall, and not inside with the bench behind us."

The woman considers this.

"They went well."

"They did." She folds her arms. "And the blanket stayed set aside, and the card work stayed in the van, and the box sat with its corner standing, and I did not open it, and that is the whole of the Wednesday."

"You never opened it."

"No." She looks at the doors standing to. "I put my knee under it once and it settled, and I left it, and today I do not go to it. Today the wall is as far as either of us goes."

They stand there a while with the yard bright round them and the lock-up shut behind them. A pigeon goes over the wall and drops beyond the road, and another follows it, and the yard settles again. The van stands tidy with the sun on its side.

---

She looks back toward the van where the sun sits on the roof and the side doors show shut.

"You leave the doors to." The woman nods at the lock-up.

"I do." She stays by the wall with the sun warm on her shoulder and her hands in her coat pockets, and the yard bright and empty round her, and the van ticking as it cools. "Standing to and not wide, to keep the sun off the box. From here you can see the gap, dark and narrow, and no closer is needed today."

"You will open after I go."

"I will." She nods. "Wide to the dim and the bench, and the drawer shut under it and the box behind it, and I will look in to mark that all is as I left it. But not with you beside me. Today the wall is where we stand."

The woman looks at the road where cloud moves over the houses.

"You keep the pad in your coat." She nods at her pocket.

"I do." She pats it without taking it out. "Ruled lines and a date on one, and the rest bare. Today no line is ruled and nothing goes on. Today the pad stays in my coat."

"You are steady today."

"I am here." She gives a short laugh. "Here by the wall is enough for a Tuesday with a job done and the van tidy."

---

"You are short today," the woman shifts along the wall.

"I am short." She gives a short laugh. "I am short because the job is done and the slip is in your pocket and there is nothing to settle, and still we are here by the wall talking as if there were."

"What now."

"Now the next." She puts her hand on the wall where the sun warms the brick. "Another house and another van load, when it comes. Until then the van stands and the doors stay to."

"You will not show me the bench."

"No." She shakes her head. "Today no bench. Today the wall, and then you go, and I open up alone."

She looks at the yard gate where the road shows beyond it, quiet in the sun.

"You will run the van after I go." The woman nods at it, half smiling.

"I will." She stays by the wall with the sun warm on her shoulder and her hands in her coat pockets, and the yard bright and empty round her, and the van ticking as it cools. "Run it to hear it and switch it off. That is the rest of the morning."

"You love that van."

"I keep it tidy." She pats the wall where the sun warms it. "White and shut and ready, with boards and bolster in the back. That is enough love for a van."

The woman pushes off the wall.

"Then I will go."

"You will." She stays by the wall. "And the Wednesday stands done, and the van stands tidy, and the doors stay to until you are through the gate."

The woman goes through the yard to the gate with her bag over her shoulder. At the gate she turns.

"You never asked what was on my slip."

"I did not need to." She smiles without leaving the wall. "You came to the wall and you stopped there, and we spoke of boards without opening the doors. That is enough."

The woman nods and goes out through the gate and up the road. She stays by the wall until the steps go from hearing. Then she goes to the lock-up doors and opens them wide to the dim inside, looks in at the bench with the drawer shut under it and the box behind it with the corner standing, and nods once as if to mark that the inside is as she left it. A pigeon goes over the wall and drops beyond the road, and another follows it, and the yard settles again.

---

She pulls the doors to all but a hand width to keep the sun off the box, gets into the van, runs it a moment to hear it and switches it off, gets out again and stands at the doors looking into the dim at the bench with the drawer shut under it and the box behind it, with the yard wall bright behind her and the morning sun still warm on it, and the van ticking as it cools beside her while the pigeons settle back on the warm brick of the wall.
