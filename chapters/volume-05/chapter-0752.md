# Chapter 0752 — The Window In The Greenhouse

Tuesday the twenty-third of August 2022, in this borough. She is in her own yard and her own lock-up from morning until dark, and the whole of it is a sash window off a dead man's bungalow that is going in the roof of her own greenhouse and will not be coming back.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FORTIES REFUSES A WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK AN AFTERNOON TWICE AND TELLS HER WHY ONCE AND WILL NOT SAY IT AGAIN, REFUSES HER A WINDOW OFF A JOB SHE DID IN JUNE TWICE AND TELLS HER WHY ONCE AND WILL NOT SAY IT AGAIN, PUTS THE WINDOW INTO THE ROOF OF HER OWN GREENHOUSE ON HER OWN, AND TELLS HER NOTHING AT ALL ABOUT ANY PART OF IT.**

She is a woman in her late forties at the lock-up on Tuesday. The woman who books work comes to the doors in the morning, stands at the bench with her, and stands outside the greenhouse with her.

---

The greenhouse is along the side of the yard under the eaves of a wall that is the back of somebody else's coach house, and it is an old glasshouse on a brick base that came with the yard.

Everything that comes off a house that has no other use goes in it. There is a wc pan on its side under the far bench, and a sink. There are four glass doors stacked against the wall at the back that will go on somebody's outbuilding one day and never do. There are two lengths of guttering full of water because the guttering is not connected to anything. There is a cold frame lid, and a set of blocks.

And there is a gap in the roof of it about the width of her two hands, where a pane came out years ago and was never replaced, and the rain comes in there and goes down the inside of the far bench and out through a hole in the brick base into the yard.

There is a sash lying on the second bench that will cover that gap exactly.

---

It came off the front of a bungalow off the ring road in June. The man had died in the winter and the widow was still in that house and did not want the front of her own house opened up.

It is a good sash. It is painted and it is sound and it has lain on that bench since the day she brought it back, and she has been in here twice since June and moved past it both times.

She has not touched it and she has not told anybody it is here, including the woman who books work, who has been in this yard every week since April and has never once gone past the doors.

---

The woman who books work came up the yard about the middle of the morning with her bag on her shoulder and a great deal of speed on her.

"I have got one for the afternoon." She puts her bag down on the boards. "Just one. I have been sitting on it since Friday."

"Then it is for this afternoon."

"That is not what I mean and you know it is not what I mean." The woman keeps her hand on her bag. "There is a woman in a home and her son has been to look at the house twice since June, and he wants the back of it done before he puts it up, and the job is tomorrow at first light. He is coming at about three this afternoon to look at it again. If you did it this afternoon he finds it whole. If you do it tomorrow he finds it half done and gets it finished before he puts it up."

"Then do it this afternoon."

"No."

"You have not said why."

---

"I have taken jobs where the man is not in the room," she says. "A man stood in a back room watching a woman empty it is not a man who goes home that night and tells his wife the room is clear. It comes back at me inside a month with a question in it. If I start that back at one o'clock I have a man stood in it at three."

"You have taken jobs with men in them."

"Not standing in the room they are in."

"Then he comes at three and you are in the room with him."

"Then he comes at three and I am in the room with him," she says, "and that is the one reason and I am not giving you a second one to see if you like it better."

The woman picked her bag up off the boards.

"Then he sees it half done tomorrow and he rings me."

"He rings you tomorrow and you tell him it is done, because it will be. I will be in that back at first light and I will have finished it before he gets there."

---

"There is a thing in this yard you have not asked me about," she says.

"What thing."

She walks her out to the side of the lock-up where the greenhouse is and points at the second bench without going in.

"A window is in my greenhouse."

The woman did not blink.

"That is the one off the bungalow."

"The one off the bungalow."

"That has been on your van since June."

"It has been in my glass since June. It came off the front of that house because the widow did not want the front of her own house opened, and it was not on a list, and I am not sorry about it."

"It is on a list. I put it on a list."

"You put a house on a list."

"The window was on the house," the woman says. "It is the whole front of that bungalow, and when that son comes he will look at his mother's front and see a hole where a window was."

"Then he will see a hole."

---

"And there is a hole in the roof of that glass," she says, pointing. "There has been one since the spring. That window goes in it and it fits it exactly, and I am putting it there this evening before the light goes, and that is all that window is doing."

"That is a hole in a shed."

"That is a window on a greenhouse roof," she says, "and it is the only one of the four of us, you and me and the woman in the home and the son, who is going to be there in five years. That is the whole of my case and I am not making it a second time."

---

"You will not give it back."

"No."

"Not even if I bring him."

"No."

"Not even if I bring him and he stands in that yard in his good coat and looks at you."

"No," she says. "It would be worse if he stood in that yard. A window that a man has watched me refuse is a window I have to think about every time I go in that glass, and there is enough in that glass already."

"Then I am going to the woman in the home on my own."

"You are going to tell her before you go, and not on the step," she says. "And you are going to tell her that the back of her house is being done tomorrow at first light, and that the son is going to stand in a half-done back at three this afternoon, and that the man who does it is a woman of her own age who has been doing it for twenty years."

"That is a mouthful to give a woman in a home."

"It is better to hear it than to find it out tomorrow." She goes in and opens the lock-up. "And I am not turning more jobs down this year because you have had a bad week. There is a Tuesday at the end of next month off the ring road with a landing on it that nobody wants, and I want it."

---

The woman goes at about the middle of the day. She stands at the gate for a second and looks up and down the lane, the way people do when they have just been refused twice, and then she goes off up the lane and does not look back.

The greenhouse is hot with the glass and the brick, and the sash is lying along the far bench with the sun coming through the one pane that is left in the roof above it.

She takes it by both ends and brings it out and lays it down on the boards outside the lock-up doors, face up, where the light is coming out sideways from under the roller door.

---

There is nothing on the wood.

Not a maker, not a number, not a mark of any kind. It was sanded pale under the paint and it would have stayed that way for ever.

She gets the pencil out of her apron and writes the address on the underside of it in pencil, where it will be wet for a week and then dry for the rest of whatever there is.

It takes her a moment to get round it, and she has to shift the far end twice to reach the bottom rail, and the pencil goes on in three places because the paint has gone hard in the grain. When she has done it she wipes her hand down the front of her apron and looks at it.

She carries it back into the greenhouse and gets it up onto the roof on her own, one end at a time, and it goes in over the gap and it does not slide and it needs nothing to keep it, and from underneath there is daylight in a long thin line where there has not been daylight since the spring.

She comes back down the yard and shuts the greenhouse door and turns the key in it, which is the only lock on this yard.

Then she crossed the yard towards the lock-up and about halfway across stopped.

The light coming out from under the roller door was throwing the whole glasshouse up on the wall behind her with one bright square in it where the new sash is, and she stood in the middle of her own yard and looked at that for a while.
