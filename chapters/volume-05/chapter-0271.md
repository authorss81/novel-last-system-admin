# Chapter 0271 — A Queue

Wednesday the twenty-sixth of October 2016, from about half past three in the afternoon, a shop that shuts early on a parade in Tideglass, and a hardware shop four doors down.

**TWO PEOPLE WENT INTO THE SAME SHOP ON THE SAME AFTERNOON IN AUGUST AND SAID THE SAME SENTENCE, AND NEITHER OF THEM KNEW, AND IT CAME OUT IN A QUEUE FOR A KEY CUT SIX WEEKS LATER, OUT OF A MAN WHO HAD BEEN TOLD BY SOMEBODY WHO IS ON NOTHING.**

The young woman of about twenty went into the shop that shuts early at about half past three because it was still open and because the woman of about fifty-five had said on the Thursday before, in a room over a shop with her bag on the sill, that the door of that room does not shut.

"Does it."

"It doesn't shut," said the woman who runs the shop. "It's an old door and it's got a swollen bottom and the frame's out at the hinge side. You can get it shut if you wind it up but you can't get it latched, and it bangs all night in a wind and it lets the heat out of a room with a radiator in it. There's a hasp and a staple and a screw eye in a bag in the back and I have had them since about the summer and I have not put them in, because that's not my door and I don't do another person's door for nothing."

"I'm not asking you to."

"No," said the woman who runs the shop. "You're not, and you'd not take them if I did, and that's the reason I said no. You'll take them off a shelf in a shop that shuts at one and you'll pay for a bag of post-fix with your own money, and if you don't, then in a year somebody else will, and that's how it happens."

The young woman of about twenty did not say why she had asked and there was nothing to say, and she paid for a bag of post-fix and went out, and the shutter came down behind her at twenty to four.

---

There is a key cutter in the hardware shop four doors down, and it is the only one in that part of the parade, and on a Wednesday at half past four there are four people in a queue for it, and Tomas Renn is behind the counter working the machine, and nobody minds the wait because he will talk while he does it and there is nowhere to go.

The man at the back of that queue was the man behind the counter on the parade, who is in that shop about once a fortnight for a bulb and has been since the summer.

He was behind a man with a car key and in front of a woman with a handbag and a man with a van key in a piece of card, and he said, to the man with the car key, in the voice of a man telling you something he was told a fortnight ago and has not thought about since:

"That's the second one, that was."

"Second what."

"Never mind — the other one," said the man behind the counter on the parade. "I had a man in my shop on the afternoon of the twenty-fifth of August, about half two, stood at my counter, and he said he hadn't got anybody, and he meant it, you could tell he meant it. And a woman was in about an hour after him, in the same shop, and she said she hadn't got anybody as well, and she'd come out of a room at the end of your parade. And the woman who runs that place gave each of them a bit of something off the shelf and let them both go, and neither of them knew the other one had been stood in that shop, and they never will, and I know it because she told me, and she did not tell me for any reason at all, she told me because she was stood in a shop with nobody to tell."

The man with the car key said, "I couldn't tell you."

"Neither can I," said the man behind the counter on the parade, and got his bulb, and went out into the rain and put the bulb in the pocket of his coat and forgot about it, and came back out for it in about a minute and went off again.

The young woman of about twenty was behind him in that queue with a handbag of books on her knee, and she heard the whole of it, and she did not move and she did not make a noise, and nobody in that shop asked her anything about anything, and Tomas Renn worked the key and said nothing to anybody.

---

She told the man of about eighteen at about ten past nine that night, in the kitchen at the back of the parade, with plates, and she told it flat and quickly and got it out in about a minute and a half.

"Two people," she said. "Same shop. Same afternoon, in August. Neither of them knew the other had been in, and both of them were told they hadn't got anybody, and a woman who is not on anything gave each of them a bit of something off a shelf and has not told anybody since except one man, and he told a queue."

The boy put a plate in the rack and lined it up with the last one.

"Right."

"You can say something else."

"I can say there's no arrangement," said the boy. "There's no arrangement and there's no list and there's no somebody who thought of it up, and you can put those three things in any order you like and it comes out the same every time. A man said a thing to a shopkeeper, and a woman said a thing to the same shopkeeper about two months later, and the shopkeeper took something off a shelf and gave it her, and nobody asked the man to say anything, and nobody has asked her anything since."

"Somebody should have told him."

"Nobody's going to," said the boy, "and I'm not, and you'll not, and if I was to he'd have to say something back to me about a hasp and a staple, and he hasn't got a thing to say back to anybody about a hasp and a staple." He turned the tap off. "There's a lamp in a corridor and you paid him ten pound for it in August and he has not told anybody that, either. There's four boxes went up two flights of a narrow staircase in August because a man took a barrow out of a shop in daylight and nobody arranged that. There's a chain on a gate at the bottom of a street that nobody has ever walked down to look at. None of them went anywhere."

"I didn't say it was going anywhere," said the young woman of about twenty. "I said it happened."

"It happened on the twenty-fifth of August," said the boy, "and it is the twenty-sixth of October, and I have had six plates in that rack for about four minutes and you are stood there holding a bag."

She put the bag down under the table and started on the plates.

The boy did not ask about it that night, and has not asked about it since, and has not been to the shop that shuts early, and does not know what was in the bag or what was in it for.

**AND THE RACK WAS FULL BY TEN AND STOOD IN THE RACK TILL HALF PAST TEN, AND THE BULB WENT IN THE BIN OUTSIDE HIS OWN SHOP IN ITS PAPER BAG BECAUSE HE CAME BACK OUT FOR IT, AND THE QUEUE OF FOUR WENT HOME IN THE RAIN AT ABOUT TEN TO FIVE.**
