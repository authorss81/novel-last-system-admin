# Chapter 0858 — The Worm On The Flags

Saturday the fourth of November 2023, on the flags in the yard at the unit from the time the frost has gone off them until the light has gone off the wall. There is a worm on them.

**THERE IS AN EARTHWORM OUT ON THE FLAGS IN THE YARD AT THE UNIT AND HE MEANS TO LIFT IT ON THE FLAT OF A CARD AND SET IT DOWN ON THE SAME FLAG A FOOT CLEAR OF THE TRODDEN LINE, AND THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN STANDS AT THE FAR GATE WITH HER BACK TO THE YARD AND DOES NOT COME IN.**

The frost came on the Friday night and it was a proper one, the kind that gets into the ground rather than sitting on it, and it went in the morning about the time the light got round to the yard wall.

An earthworm comes up after a frost and it cannot go back down.

That is not a habit. The worm has been down in the cold ground all summer where the soil is about the same temperature as itself and it has been eating it and casting it. A frost goes through the top two inches of that soil and takes it below freezing, and the worm cannot burrow into ground that is solid, so it comes up and lies on the surface until the frost comes out of the ground again, which in this yard in November is about the middle of next month, or until it dies, whichever is first.

This one has been up since the Saturday morning and it is on the flags in the middle of the yard where the light is.

He has been stepping round it since about ten. You cannot walk a yard without a worm on the flags being something you walk round, and that is all it was for the first hour, and then he started looking at it properly and it stopped being something to walk round and became a thing that is on the flags and will not come down.

---

He reads it where it is before he goes near it, and the reading is what decides everything after.

A worm that has just come up out of the ground is pale. A worm that has been out is dark. It is less a colour than a deepening, the same colour as before with the pigment burnt into it by light and air, and the darker it is the longer it has been lying in the open.

This one is dark right along its length and black over the front half. It has been out of the ground for days, not hours, and it is not going to find a way back into it from the middle of a yard in November.

And it is not going to do that on a flag. A flag is a stone laid on sand. There is nothing under that flag a worm can go into but the sand, and the sand is under the flag, and what is under the sand is stone, and a joint between two flags is no more than a pencil width and full of grit off the yard, and a worm cannot get a ring of itself down into a quarter of an inch of grit whatever is at the bottom of it.

So it is going to lie on that flag until it is dried out, and it is going to be there for a week or the whole winter, and on this yard it is going to be walked on every time somebody goes in and out of the store.

He goes inside and gets a card.

Not to be careful. Because a hand closes. A hand on a worm is a hand that is wet and gritty and it closes, and it does not close gently, because the thing that makes it close gently is knowing what is under it, and a worm under a hand in a yard in November is a cold slimy nothing that is going to be a shock. He has done it before and got a worm and a mess of grit in the lines of his hand and a worm that was no longer a worm.

---

The card is an old one off the shelf in the store, the kind that comes in a bundle of fifty and is stiff enough not to sag.

He slides it flat under the worm from the side, not under the middle, and brings it up about a quarter of an inch off the flags, and the worm comes with it. It does not wriggle off. It goes rigid first, the way they do, every one of them, and lies on the card in a curve about as long as his hand and does not move for about twenty seconds.

Then the wave starts.

He has got the card in both hands, out at the length of his arm so that he can see the whole length of it, and the wave goes along the animal from the front end to the back end and back again four times before he has marked ten of them.

Twenty seconds is longer than it looks and it is the whole of the reading. A worm on a cold card goes stiff first, and then the stiffness comes off it from the back end forward, and the animal has to get that out of it before it will move at all, and how long that is depends how long it is. It is not frightened and it is not dying. It is cold and it is working something out. The body gets longer and thinner and shorter and thicker. It is the only thing on that card that moves, and it moves properly, which answers the only question he had about it.

He looks at the ring at the back end. It is swollen and darker than the rest and it is pressed down against the card, and that is where it is going to lay, and it has been laid several times already today, and there are two pale marks on the card either side of it where the castings have been left off.

It is not dying. It is inconvenient and out of place and it is going to be all right if a joint is within a yard of it and nothing steps on it.

So he takes the card down to the flags and finds the place.

The trodden line is the length of yard everybody uses. It runs from the gate to the store door and it is about a foot and a half wide, and it is worn paler than the rest of the flags because it is the only part of the yard that gets feet.

He puts the worm down on the same flag it came off, which is the one by the wall on the house side, and a foot clear of the trodden line, and about four inches back from the joint between that flag and the next one.

Not on the grass. The grass along that wall is dead since August and it is lying over and it is dry underneath, and a worm put on that grass is a worm put on a shelf. The flags are down on soil and they are the last damp thing in that yard, and the soil comes up into the line between them. A worm cannot go down a joint. It travels along one, because the line where two stones meet is a groove with soil in it, and that groove is the only road it has in this yard.

He gets the card right down to the stone so it does not drop the last half inch and turns it over on its edge, and the worm comes off onto the flag and lies still for a moment with the ring at the back end working, and then it goes, slowly, along the line between that flag and the next, and does not go in a straight line.

It does not go in a straight line because the grain of a flag is not flat. There is a lie in the surface of every stone that has been laid on sand, and a worm follows it, and it went to the joint because the joint is the only place in that yard where the lie is deep enough to fall into.

He watches it go with his hands down at his sides and does not move his feet.

The woman from two doors down comes out to the far gate in the middle of the afternoon with a bag of something under her arm and goes to the gate and stands at it with her back to the yard, and she does not come in and she does not turn round while she is there.

"You'll want it shut when you're through," he says.

"No. Leave it."

She shifts the bag to her other hand.

"There was a light burning in the hall at the end house at two this morning."

"There was."

"It's on the mains. You can hear it from here at night."

"You can."

"My husband had the hall light on all last winter."

"Then he wasn't the only one."

She stays at the gate a while longer with her back to the yard and then goes out through it, and the gate comes to behind her.

He goes and gets the card off the flags where he put it down and puts the card back in the bundle in the store.

And the flag has two marks on it this evening where it had none this morning. There is the trodden line down the middle of the yard, which was there before he came out and will be there tomorrow. And there is a damp ring on the house-side flag about the size of a saucer, where the card lay against the stone, which will be gone by tomorrow and which is not going to be missed by anybody including him.
