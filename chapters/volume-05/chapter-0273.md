# Chapter 0273 — A Knack

Tuesday the eighth of November 2016, from about half past seven in the evening, the Methodist hall on Carrow Lane in Tideglass.

**HE DID TWO THINGS IN FRONT OF EVERYBODY AND NOBODY MADE ANYTHING OF EITHER, AND ONE OF THE ELEVEN SAID SOMETHING KIND TO HIM, AND HE DID NOT KNOW WHAT TO DO WITH IT AND WENT HOME.**

There were eleven of them in that hall, and every one of them had walked in from a wet parade or a wet lane, and there was a jug of tea on the table and a plate of biscuits that had come from the shop that shuts early, and it was the week after half term and half the people in that borough were working a day they did not normally work, and there was a dog outside the door for the first hour and then it went and did not come back.

He came in at about ten past eight with his cardigan over the back of a chair, which is where he has put it every week since the summer, and nobody looked up, and he stood at the back of the hall the way he stands at the back of a room.

Mrs Obi was at the front with her hands round a cup and nothing else in front of her on the table. The man from the parade had not brought anything. The woman who has been in that hall about two years had come off a late and had a paper of chips in her pocket. The man in his sixties had a cardboard tube under his arm that had a drawing in it. The man in his forties was late. The woman of about fifty-five had her bag. A woman who works in a shop came in about half an hour after that and was the only one who said sorry for being late, twice, to two different people.

---

There was a trestle table down the middle with the plate of biscuits on it, and the man in his forties was leaning on that table with his elbow when the leg went.

It did not break. It came out of its bracket, and the whole table tilted about two inches, and the plate went off the end of it and hit the boards and did not break either, and about half the teas on that table went over at once, and there was a noise in that hall that Mrs Obi said afterwards was like a man falling down a stair.

The man in his forties was up off his elbow and had both hands under the table before the plate had finished bouncing.

The lad had a spanner in his hand. Not a big one, a ring spanner, and he had had it in his pocket since about half six because there is a tap on the cold feed in the kitchen at the back of the parade that has been crying for a fortnight, and he cannot stop crying about it, because nobody has asked him to and nobody is going to.

He got down onto one knee, put his shoulder into the underside of the table leg, found the bracket with his fingers, and had it back in and locked in about four seconds without ever having moved the table.

He got up and went back to the back of the hall.

Nobody stopped talking. The man in his forties said "Christ," and picked the plate up off the boards, and the woman who has been in that hall about two years said, "That is the second time that table's done that," and the conversation went straight back into the middle of whatever it had been, and the lad stood at the back of that hall with a spanner in his hand and nobody said one word to him about it for the rest of the night.

---

The second thing was at about half past nine, and it was a bag.

Somebody's paper bag of shopping had gone out of the door in the draught — that door opens both ways and it is on a catch that has not held since the summer — and had gone out into the wet parade and was about two foot from the step, and everything in it had started to go.

He was through that door and out and back before anybody had finished a sentence about the price of a boiler. He came in with the bag held up in one hand by the string, so that nothing in it went, and he put it down on the floor by the chair of the person it belonged to and said, "That was yours going out of the door," and went back to the back of the hall.

On the way in he put his other hand flat on the door frame as he came through, the way anybody does, and it stopped about a foot open instead of hitting the wall, and it stayed like that, and the draught in that hall went from a cold spot at the door to nothing at all, and nobody in that hall said anything about that either.

That was the two of them. There was nothing else. Neither of them took more than about half a minute together and half of that was the door, and neither of them was done because anybody asked.

At about twenty to ten, with the tea still being mopped up off the table and nobody still talking about the price of a boiler, the woman of about forty-two with the key to that hall came past him on her way to the meter by the door with her coat over her arm, and stopped, and looked at his hands, which were grey to the second knuckle off the spanner and the door frame and a bit of biscuit.

"You've done that before," she said.

"No."

"Somebody taught you."

"No," said the boy, and then, because she was standing there waiting for an answer: "There's nobody to teach me. I watch a thing and then I do it wrong about nine times."

She laughed at that and said, "Somebody did."

And then she went and shut the gas off at the meter by the door, which she does, and put the padlock on it, which she does, and did not say one other thing to him all evening, and he did not say one other thing to her.

He stayed until the chairs went up at ten past ten, because he does that. He carried four of them, because the man from the parade had carried four on the first of September and had not mentioned it and neither does he, and he went out into a rain that had set in at about nine and stood on the step for a second with his cardigan over his arm, because that step is the width of the hall and there is nothing to come out under.

Nobody asked him one thing all night that was a question about him. Nobody offered him one thing at all, and nothing was written down, and the thing he asked in June is still the thing he asked in June and it was not asked and it was not answered.

**AND THE SPANNER WENT BACK IN HIS POCKET ON THE STEP, AND THE TABLE LEG WAS STILL LOCKED IN ITS BRACKET ON THE THURSDAY AND ON THE FRIDAY AND ON THE MONDAY, AND THE DOOR ON THAT HALL STILL CATCHES AND STILL DOES NOT HOLD.**
