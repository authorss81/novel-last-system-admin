# Chapter 0607 — The Steps He Does Not Take

The Tuesday of the first of December 2020, starting about half past eight in the morning and running to about half past five in the afternoon, in this borough.

**HE GOES PAST A TURN IN THE ROAD EVERY MORNING AND EVERY EVENING AND HAS DONE SINCE BEFORE THE SUMMER, AND THE TURN IS NOT THE WAY AND HE HAS MEASURED THE WAY ROUND AND WILL NOT PUT A FIGURE ON IT, AND HE HAS TODAY WALKED PAST IT AND GONE ROUND AND SAID NOTHING TO ANYBODY.**

He is a man in his mid thirties. He teaches in a school in another part of the borough and he drives there, and the road he drives in on has a turn off it past the church, and the turn takes you to the estate, and the estate is where he does not have to be going.

---

The turn is not a shortcut and it does not save anything. He worked this out the first time he took it, which was one morning in about the May, and he has never since pretended to himself that there is a reason for it.

Here is the position in full, and it has not changed and he could draw it.

He lives on a road of terraces a short way back from the turn. He works at a school at the other end of the borough. If he goes straight down the road and turns left at the lights at the bottom of it, he is at the school gates. If he goes down the road and takes the turn to the estate, and goes along the estate road past the shops and the primary school and the row of flats with the drying green, and comes out at the far end onto the road that goes back past a garage and a church hall and joins the lights, it is longer.

Longer. He has measured it, twice, by the clock in the car. He does not know what the figure is to the minute and has decided that he would rather not know it than know it badly.

So it is longer, and there is nothing at the end of it, and he has been doing it since before the summer.

Here is what he has never told anybody, which includes the woman he has been seeing since the October, and she sits in the passenger seat most of the time and has never asked why the clock on the sign changes at the turn.

Here is this morning.

He was out of the door at half past eight and it was dark and there was a hard frost on the roofs and on the bonnet of the car at the end of the row, and he had to clear the inside of the windscreen before he could see the road at all.

And she said, "You've got that thing again."

And he said, "What thing?"

And she said, "The wipers."

And he said, "They're fine."

And she said, "They're not. You've done them again."

She was right. He had done them again. He does it constantly without noticing and then does it when she is in the car, and she has never once been unkind about it.

And he said, "Sorry."

And she said, "It's all right. I've got to be out at nine."

And he said, "Mm."

And she said, "Are you going the long way?"

And he said, "I'm going the long way."

And she said, "Fine," and turned the radio up, and that was the whole of it, and she does not know why, and he did not tell her, and he was grateful in a way he is not going to look at directly.

The turn is a left with a give-way and a post box on the corner and a notice for a fete on the green in the summer, which is now down and has been down since September.

He knows what is on the estate road because he drives it.

He knows the shops because there is a shop that does keys and a shop that does groceries and one that is a takeaway with a shutter that is down about half the time.

He knows the primary school because the children come out at about ten past three and about twenty of them come out on the side that faces the road and stand about six deep on the pavement and there is a woman on the gate with a clipboard.

He knows the row of flats with the drying green because there is a woman in the ground floor flat on the corner who puts bedding out on the green and brings it in and it is never down long, and he has seen her about four times and has never seen her face.

He knows the garage and the church hall and the road that comes out at the lights.

That is the whole of what is out there. He has been past it every working morning and most evenings since the May and he could not tell a single person what is at the end of it, and there is nothing at the end of it.

---

Here is what he thought about this morning at the lights, and he has thought it before, and this time he got further with it than he has ever got.

The first thing is that it is not about the estate. He has driven round estates in three other towns he has worked in and he does not do this.

The second thing is that it started not long after the school changed the way it does the mornings. The gates moved. They used to be at about half seven and now they are at about ten to eight, and he is at the gates by about ten to eight either way, and there is a gap in the middle of the morning where he is not anywhere.

The third thing is that he has worked out, in the car, at the give-way, that the gap does not exist. There is nothing to be lost between the two. He worked it out properly, once, over a stretch of driving in the summer, and then he did not want to have it again.

The fourth thing is that he has not told anybody that he worked it out.

That is where he gets to every time, and this morning he got one step past it and then stopped, and the step was this.

If the gap does not exist, then he is not filling it. He is not on a longer route than he needs to be on. He is going somewhere he has never named, off his way, on purpose, every working morning and most evenings, and there is no reason given for it because there is no reason and he has looked for one.

And he thought, at the give-way, with the post box next to him: I would have to be able to say what it is, out loud, to somebody.

And then he went round the estate road and past the shops and past the flats with the drying green and out at the far end by the church hall, and the bedding was out on the green again, and he went back to the lights at about ten past.

Here is the rest of the day and there is not much of it, because he teaches.

He taught until about half past twelve and then had a bit of time and then taught again until about twenty to four, and then marked for about an hour, and it is marking that has done it, because marking is the only part of the job where he is not responsible for being right in front of anybody.

The head asked him at about half past one whether he was all right for the rest of the week and he said he was, and there was no reason for the question that he could find, and he has still not gone back and thought about whether there was.

At about half past four he put his coat on and went out and got into the car and sat in it.

Then he reversed out and went down the road and took the turn.

That is the part. He had a choice at the bottom of the road and he had made it every morning since before the summer, and by then it was properly dark and there was a fog down on the estate green, and the bedding on it was a white sheet at the far end, and he took the turn and went round and came out at the lights later than that, and turned right for home.

And the thing he has noticed, which he noticed this afternoon and has not yet had any way to put down, is that the turn is harder to come out of on the way home than it is to go into on the way in.

He has never tried the other one. That is what he is saying. He has never once, since the May, come back down the estate road and turned out onto the road of terraces instead of carrying on to the lights. It has never occurred to him, or it has occurred to him and he has not done it, and he cannot tell which, and he drove home with that.

He put the car on the drive at about half past five and the frost had gone off the roofs and he went in and she was doing something to a sleeve at the table and he did not tell her, and she did not ask, and he has still got the turn.

**HE TAKES A TURN THAT IS LONGER THAN THE WAY AND HE HAS MEASURED IT TWICE AND WILL NOT PUT A FIGURE ON IT, AND HE HAS WORKED OUT THAT THE TIME IT SUPPOSED TO BE FILLING DOES NOT EXIST, AND HE COULD TURN OFF THE ESTATE ROAD ON THE WAY HOME AND HE HAS NEVER DONE IT ONCE.**
