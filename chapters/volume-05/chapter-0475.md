# Chapter 0475 — Nine Foot of Bramble

Wednesday the twenty-seventh of February 2019, from about half past seven in the morning until about six in the evening. A day, and the rain that came down in the middle of it, in Tideglass.

**THE RAIN CAME DOWN ON THAT BOROUGH FOR THREE HOURS ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF FEBRUARY AND UNDER ABOUT FOUR YARD OF GREEN WOOD AT ONE END OF A HUNDRED-YARD WAY THE GROUND WAS ALREADY MUD, AND A MAN AND A WOMAN STOOD SIX FOOT APART IN IT FOR TWO MINUTES AND SAID FIVE WORDS BETWEEN THEM.**

It is not a public way and there is no sign anywhere. It is about nine foot wide at its narrowest, between the end wall of a row of fourteen and a boundary wall that belongs to a yard on the other side, and it runs the better part of a hundred yard from one street to the next, and for about fifty of those hundred yard the whole of the width of it is flagged and swept and clean.

Then there is a bit of about nine foot where it is not.

What is in it is a privet. It has been in that bed since before anybody living came to the row, and in November the beds on both sides were cut back and this one was not, and the cutting that was made of it was put in the bed and has gone into a hedge, and what the hedge has done since is spread about four foot across the flags and closed over the top of it at about four foot off the ground.

Under that, in the last week of January, there was still a way through. You could go down it if you turned sideways and put your hands on the wood on each side. In the last ten days it has gone from that to nothing. There is no going through it. You can stand at one end and look along it and see daylight, and about four foot in, the green has closed.

She has put her head round it twice. She has not gone in. She is not going to.

---

He is at the top end and he has been there about nine years.

He goes out at half past seven for a place four miles off and is not back before six, and his is one of about twenty houses that use that way because it is a hundred and ten yard shorter than going round. He has never been the person to do anything about that way. He has used it the way you use a piece of pavement, which is that it is there.

What he has done about it is worked it out, more than once, and not done it.

The working-out is short and it finishes every time. There are about twenty houses at the two ends. He is one person. If he goes in with a billhook on a Saturday and cuts four yard of privet back to the boundary, then on the second Saturday somebody will put it forward again, because the person who put it forward in the first place does not know why there is a nine-foot gap, and if the gap is not nine foot it is not a gap, it is a hedge, and a hedge in that place is not an argument anybody makes on a Sunday.

He has a bad back. He is a man who has never in his life done a day's work outdoors that was not on somebody's job and paid for, and he is aware that if he were the one who cut that wood back he would be, for the rest of how long he lives, a person in that row who does things in that passage. He has put a number on that as well and the number is worse than the one about the billhook.

She has done the same working-out and hers finishes further down.

She is at the bottom end and she has been there about six years and she has done other people's gardens in this borough for about nine of those years, and she has two hands that are better than his and a hip that is worse, and she goes down that way about four times a day.

What she has worked out is that if she is the one who cuts it, then she is the one who cut it, and the cutting will be seen, and by about 2023 the bed will need doing again and the whole of that row will look at it and know whose it was.

And there is a second thing under that, and she has not told herself the second thing plainly, and the second thing is that the bed is not hers. She rents the house. The row is not hers. In six years she has put a wash on, hung things, and taken in the top step, and there is not one thing about the outside of that house that anybody could point at and say she had ever intended to be there.


Here is what each of them did with the sum, and it comes to the same number both times, and neither of them has the smallest suspicion that the other one has been through the same arithmetic.

What it would come to for him to be the one who asked: about four seconds of saying it, and then eight years of a woman in his own back yard every year.

What it would come to for her to be the one who asked: about four seconds of saying it, and then as many years as she stays of a row in which she is a person who has cut a hedge in it.

Each of them has added the other's figure up and found it cheaper.

On the twenty-seventh of February it rained from about two in the afternoon until about five.

She was out in it at about three because there is a bird in the eaves of the garage at the bottom of that way and she wanted to stand under it with a broom to see whether it was sitting on anything, which is a thing she does and which has never once worked. She got wet to the waist in about nine minutes and she was stood at the bottom of that way with her hand on the privet when he came through it.

He had come back early. That is the whole of why he was there. His place had finished for the day and he had gone home and come out again at about half past two to take something to a man about two doors off, and he came back down that way because it is a hundred and ten yard shorter and because it was what he always did.

She had a broom in one hand and there was a plastic bag over the other one and her coat was off, and he stopped about six foot off and said, "Is it open?"

And she said, "No."

And he said, "Right," and stood there.

That is the whole of what was said in that passage on the twenty-seventh of February, and it took about nine seconds, and neither of them has told anybody it was said.

They stood there in that rain for about two minutes. The ground under the privet had gone to mud and had been going to mud for about nine days. He could see that from where he stood. He looked at the mud and then he looked at the four foot of green above it and then at her, and she had her back to him and was holding the bag up so that the rain was not going into it, and she said nothing.

What he thought about in those two minutes, and he has not got further than it, is that if he had said a different thing — if he had said *I could do it* — then it would have been done, and it would have been done badly, because he would have taken the top off and made a gap that a child could get through and the side would go within two years, and then the gap would be a fault, and the fault would be his and it would be visible from both rows.

What she thought about in those two minutes is that he was going to offer, and she got ready to say no, and then he did not offer, and that she was not relieved, and that she has been ready to say no since the middle of January and has been ready to say no every day since, and that the readiness has started to take up more room than the thing it is a readiness for.

---

At about half past six he came back down that way with a carrier bag and his coat over his arm and he did not go in.

There is about four foot of it he could have got through in January and there is not now, and he has stopped thinking of it as a thing that is blocked and started thinking of it as a thing that belongs to somebody, which is not true and is a great deal easier.

She went in at about ten past eight that night, and the ground under the wood was still mud, and she put her shoes on the step and looked at it and thought that by the middle of April it would have dried out and sat down and the whole of that wood would be four foot lower than it had been in January.

And by the middle of April she will not be here, because the lease is up in June, and not one person in that row has been told about the lease, and the lease and the wet ground are not going to be what gets that wood cut back.

**ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF FEBRUARY A MAN STOOD IN THAT RAIN AT THE TOP OF A BLOCKED PASSAGE AND SAID ONE WORD BACK, AND A WOMAN STOOD AT THE BOTTOM OF IT FOR TWO MINUTES WAITING FOR SOMETHING SHE COULD HAVE SAID NO TO, AND NO PERSON IN THAT ROW HAS BEEN TOLD ONE THING ABOUT WHAT EITHER OF THEM IS STANDING IN.**
