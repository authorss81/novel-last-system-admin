# Chapter 0518 — You Did the Right Thing

The Thursday of the twelfth of September 2019, the whole thing over between two and half past four, at two houses and the street between them, in this borough.

A man said three words to his brother in a kitchen in 1996 and then did not say them again for twenty-three years, and the brother never asked, and the brother's wife has spent twenty-three years being careful not to need them. On Thursday she heard them said to somebody else in a different kitchen.

---

The two brothers are called nothing by anybody. They are a man of about sixty-four and a man of about sixty-one and they have been in the same borough for their whole lives and they have never once in that time been in an argument, and both of them have said to other people, at different times and in different words, that they do not have arguments.

What happened in 1996 is this.

Their mother was ill. The older brother had a share in a firm of about nine men that had been going for two generations, and the younger brother had been in it for a good many years, and in the summer of 1996 the older brother was told that the firm was going and that there was a way of keeping six of the nine and that two of the names on the list were not going to be on it.

The younger brother was one of the two.

The older brother put his own share into what was left so that the younger brother could stay on, and the younger brother stayed on for another two years and then in 1998 he went into something else, and in 1999 their mother died, and everything since then has been ordinary.

And in the kitchen of the older brother's house, in the September of 1996, the older brother said, "You did the right thing."

There was no other sentence. He said it with his hand flat on the wood and then he filled the kettle and they watched something on a set with the sound off.

The younger brother has never asked about it. Not once, in twenty-three years. He has thought about it, and by his own account he has thought about it a great deal, and he has never asked, and he has never told his wife that it was said.

His wife knows that it was said. She has known since 1997.

He told her in the November of that year, in their own kitchen, after about a year of not telling her, and he told her because a woman she works with said something at a leaving do about a family that had given something up, and he came home and put it to her that night and she said, that is not us, and he said, it is, and then he told her.

Twenty-three years. She has never asked him why he told her then and not before. She has never asked him what he thought he had given up. She has never asked him whether his brother meant it.

What she has done with it is keep it, and the keeping has had a shape, and the shape is that she has never once, in twenty-three years, treated that family as a family that had made a sacrifice.

She has thought about it every time there has been a bill come through that she has paid herself without saying anything about. She has thought about it and she has gone on paying, and she has told nobody that that is what she has been doing, and her husband has never once asked her to.

Here is how she came to know that the words were the words, and it was not on the twelfth of September, it was on the fourth.

They were in the older brother's kitchen because the older brother's wife is not well, and there were four of them and a young man there, a man of about thirty-one who does something at the works and who has a mother who is not well either.

The younger brother and his wife got there at about half past one. They ate. They talked about nothing. The young man had been on the outside of something and had given up a post in another town that summer to be near his mother and the older brother had said that he could come and work with them for a while and sort himself out.

And at about two in the afternoon, standing at the side of the worktop with his plate in his hand, the older brother said, "You did the right thing."

The younger brother's wife was carrying three plates across that kitchen and she stopped where she was.

Nobody saw her stop. The older brother had his back half turned and was talking to the young man, and the young man said, "Well, I don't know about that," and the older brother said, "You do," and put his hand flat on the wood, and said it again, "You did the right thing."

She put the three plates down on the surface by the door and she went out and sat in the sitting room for a quarter of an hour.

She went over it in the sitting room and what she went over was not the words. The words are three words and she has had them for twenty-three years. What she went over was the hand.

The older brother had put his hand flat on the worktop before he said it, and he had put it flat there in that same kitchen in the September of 1996, and she was in that kitchen with her coat still on, and she was not to find out for another fourteen months what had been said in it.

She sat there until she could go back in, and she went back in, and she picked the three plates up and carried them and nobody said anything about it.

On the Thursday of the twelfth of September she did not do anything about it either, for about two hours.

They were there from about two until about four. The young man left at about half past three. The younger brother and his wife walked home at about four.

Neither of the brothers said anything in the street. The younger brother does not know that his wife stopped in that kitchen on the fourth of September and went out and sat down, and he has not known since, and there is no reason on earth why he should.

They got to their own street at about ten past four and their own house and their wife put her handbag down and said she was going to put the tea on and went into the kitchen.

And then she stopped, and she turned round, and she said, "That is what you said to me."

Her husband said, "What?"

She said, "On the fourth. To the young man. That is what you said to me."

And he said, "Ay."

And she said, "You never told me that was the sentence."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "I have had it for twenty-three years as something you said about the firm."

And he said, "It was about the firm."

And she said, "You don't know what you said about the firm."

And he said, "I did, though."

He did not deny it. Since then neither of them has raised it. It is a quarter past four on the twelfth of September, and that is everything there is.

He put the plate down. There was a plate in his hand because he had been carrying the tea things through and he put it down on the surface beside her, and he did not apologise, and he did not explain, and he said, "There's more in the pot."

And she said, "There is."

And she did not leave the kitchen. She stood there for a while, and he stood there, and he filled two cups and brought them to the surface and she took hers and said, "Right," and they took them into the sitting room and watched something with the sound off, which is what the older brother had done on the fourth and what he had done in 1996.

And here is the part that she has not said to anybody and is not going to, and it is the reason she is not going to ask him anything about it, and it is not the reason anybody would guess.

If she asks him what he meant in 1996, he will be sorry. He will be genuinely sorry, in about four seconds, in the way he does things, and he will say that he did not think of it like that, and he will say that he was not trying to be clever, and he will offer to say something else.

And that is the thing she will not have.

She has wanted to know since the fourth of September whether those three words were a judgement. If he says he was sorry, then they were a judgement, and she has spent twenty-three years on a family that gave something up and she will have to start again.

If he says nothing, they were something else, and she still does not know what, and she can go on being where she is.

And she has worked out, sitting in the sitting room with a cup she is not drinking from, that she has been protecting a sentence she has never been told the meaning of, for twenty-three years, by never once asking.
