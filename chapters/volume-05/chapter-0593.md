# Chapter 0593 — What She Added

The Tuesday of the fifteenth of September 2020, from about half past two in the afternoon until about nine in the evening, in a kitchen in this borough.

**IT WAS SAID TO HER A WEEK AGO ON A WEDNESDAY AND IT CAME BACK TO HIM THIS AFTERNOON IN THE WORDS OF A THIRD PERSON WHO HAS ADDED ONE THING TO IT, AND HE HEARD HIS OWN SENTENCE AND CANNOT WORK OUT WHICH PART OF IT IS HIS, AND HE STOPPED WANTING TO KNOW ON FRIDAY AND HAS NOT BEEN ABLE TO START AGAIN.**

The man is in his mid forties. The woman is in her mid thirties and has a sister in her mid thirties on the other side of the county, and the two sisters have not spoken since the June, and neither will say why in front of anybody. The man and the woman have been married six years.

The person it comes back through is a woman in her early sixties who has been a friend of both of them for about as long as they have been married and who passes things on. She is not a person who would do it on purpose. She is a person who has never in her life held anything in, and who has never once in her life considered that a thing she had been told might be a thing she ought to be careful with.

---

Here is the sentence.

He said it to her on the Wednesday a week ago at about half past four, in that kitchen, and there was nobody else in the house. He said:

"I've not said a word to your sister and I'm not going to."

It is an ordinary thing to say to a person. It is what a person says when a family is not speaking and the other person wants it known that the not-speaking is not his doing.

He said it once. He has not said it since. He did not say it with any weight on it at all.

---

Here is everything that happened to it, in sequence:

On the Friday the woman in her mid thirties was at her mother's and the woman in her early sixties was there, because the woman in her early sixties is at that mother's most Fridays and has been since about 2005.

The woman in her mid thirties did not say the sentence. She has not been near the sentence. What she did was say, in the ordinary way, that he was being decent about it, and that her sister was being stupid, and that she was tired of the two of them.

And the woman in her early sixties said: "Well, he says he hasn't said a word and he isn't going to."

That is what went out of that kitchen. It is not what he said. It has two halves to it that were not in it when he said it — *he says* and *and he isn't going to* — and it is a different sentence, because his was about a thing he had done and hers is about a thing he means to go on doing, and a person can be the first and not the second, and a great many men are.

It came back this afternoon.

He and the woman in his mid thirties are in that kitchen at about half past two and the woman in her early sixties rings. She rings about a completely different thing, which is a hall that has been let for a funeral on a weekday and whether the woman in her mid thirties can come.

At the end of it, having said nothing else about anything, the woman in her early sixties said, out of nothing at all, in the voice of a woman who is passing on the day's news:

"And he's still not said a word to her, has he."

He said, "Right."

That is all he said. He said "Right" and then he said he would ring her back about the hall and he did not ring her back about the hall, and he did not think about anything else for about twenty minutes.

Then he thought about it, and here is the exact shape of the problem and it is worth setting out because it is a shape a great many people have been in.

His sentence, which she had heard and which he knows she heard, because he said it in a kitchen with the two of them alone in it and she did not argue with it and she took it completely straight, was: *I've not said a word to your sister and I'm not going to.*

The sentence that came back was: *he says he hasn't said a word and he isn't going to.*

Those are not the same. His was a claim about the past and about his intention and hers is somebody else's account of a claim, put in by another mouth, with two pieces added to it, and one of the two pieces — *he says* — is a softening that was not there, and the other — *and he isn't going to* — is nearly in his own words and not quite, because he never said *to her* and she said *to her*, and the difference between *your sister* and *her* in a kitchen with the two of them in it is the difference between a person being defended and a person being handed round.

He knows it is not his. He cannot prove which part is not his. And that is the whole of what has happened to him since a quarter past three on this afternoon.

Here is why he cannot prove it, and this is a real thing and not a complaint.

If he rings the woman in her early sixties and says, *I never said to her* — and the woman in his mid forties's wife is the woman in her early sixties's friend and the woman in his mid forties's wife knows perfectly well what he said, and the woman in her early sixties will say, well, you said the same thing, and he will have to say no I didn't, and the woman in her early sixties will say what difference does it make, he has not said a word, and that is what he said.

It is not a lie. That is what makes it unusable. Nobody in that triangle is lying to anybody. The woman in her early sixties has changed a sentence the way water changes a shape, and the man in his mid forties cannot point at the place where it changed, because there is no such place, and the woman in her early sixties is not a person who would ever be asked to find it.

And the woman in her mid thirties has not seen any of it. She has heard both. She heard him say it in the kitchen a week ago and she heard the woman in her early sixties say it this afternoon and she has not noticed that they are not the same, and he cannot show her, because to show her he would have to explain what he said, and what he said was *I've not said a word to your sister and I'm not going to*, and if he says that back to her she will say *and you haven't* and then they will be in it.

He stopped wanting to know on the Friday.

That happened at about seven in the evening on the Friday, over a bin bag and a length of time of about a minute, and what happened is that he worked out that he could ring the woman in her early sixties and put it right, and he got as far as the door with his coat on, and he stood there, and he thought about the woman in her mid thirties saying *and you haven't*, and about how much of that woman's week is now made out of not having that conversation, and about how much of his is, and he took his coat off.

He has not rung anybody about it. It is not that he has decided not to. It is that he can no longer construct a version of the phone call in which he comes out of it with anything he wants, and the wanting went on Friday, and it has not come back since, and it did not come back this afternoon either, and he noticed that it did not, and then he had dinner.

**IT WENT OUT OF HIS MOUTH ON A WEDNESDAY AND CAME BACK THIS AFTERNOON WITH A SOFTENING AND A NAME IN IT, AND NOBODY HAS SAID ANYTHING UNTRUE, AND HE COULD PROVE IT IN ABOUT FOUR WORDS AND HE STOPPED WANTING TO ON FRIDAY, WHICH IS A DIFFERENT REASON FROM THE ONE HE WOULD GIVE.**
