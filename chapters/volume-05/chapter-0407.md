# Chapter 0407 — Four Posts

Friday the twenty-seventh of April 2018, starting at about half past eight in the morning and running until about half past five in the afternoon, a yard off a street off that parade, and a shop on that parade, in Tideglass.

**A MAN SPENT A DAY IN THAT YARD PUTTING FOUR POSTS UP FOR A WASHING LINE, AND A WOMAN BEHIND A COUNTER ON THAT PARADE TOLD HIM AT HALF PAST FOUR THAT ANOTHER MAN HAD SAID AT LUNCHTIME THAT HE WAS DOING IT, AND HE SAID NEITHER YES NOR NO, AND NEITHER OF THOSE TWO HAS RAISED IT SINCE.**

The house is in a terrace of five off a street that comes off that parade. The yard is behind the middle of the five, it is about twenty foot deep and about twenty wide, and there is a coal house against the left-hand side of it that is a brick box with a felted roof and a door that does not close, and the rest of the bottom of it is hardstanding with a grass strip about three foot wide against the back fence.

A washing line in that yard is not a difficulty. It is a difficulty because the man who lives in that house is seventy-one and does not dig and has a bad hip, and in March he put it to his granddaughter, asked her whether there was anybody about who would put one up, and that is how it came to be done and it is the only way anything gets done in that terrace.

---

He was in that yard from about half past eight.

The posts are galvanised, about six foot six out of the ground, and they are not a thing you knock in. Each one goes into a hole two foot deep with a half inch of gravel in the bottom of it and about three inches of lean-mix around it and a galvanised base plate bedded in, and you set them in a line at about five foot six off the ground and you check the whole run with a line and not with your eye.

He had dug the four holes on the Thursday evening in the rain, which he should not have done, and on the Friday morning the fourth one had about two inches of water standing in it.

He mixed the first three in a barrow and set them. The first one went in straight. The second went in straight and about ten minutes later he looked at it from the back fence and it was out by about a quarter of an inch over its length, and he knocked it out and did it again with a drier mix, and the second time it held.

The third one he had the same trouble with and the same cause. That post is the one nearest the coal house, and the coal house is built on a raft and the ground under the third hole is about four inches of hardcore and then made ground, and a post bedded in that with a wet mix leans.

He knocked that one out at about twenty to twelve and took all of it out with a bar and a shovel, because you cannot put a fresh one in on top of a wet one, and he went and got four bags of post fixings from the merchant on the Cleeve road and made it up again with a stiff mix, and he set that post at about twenty past one and it stood.

By about half past two all four were in and set and he had the line run at five foot six and it was dead straight over the whole twenty foot of that yard and he had checked it from the coal house and from the back fence and from inside the kitchen window of that house, which he could see by standing on the coal house roof.

A woman came out of the back door of that house at about ten past ten.

She lives in it and she is the granddaughter and she is about thirty and she was on her tea, and she came down the two steps and stood about four foot off the third post and looked at it.

She said, "Is that going to take a wet sheet."

"It'll take a wet sheet," he said. "That line's not string. That's the proper line, the stuff you get in a coil. You can put a wet double sheet on that with a full bucket in the middle of it and it'll come down the same. What you can't do is a wet sheet and a wet towel and a wet duvet, because that's about ninety pound of water in a coil and every washing line in this country is a coil."

"Right," she said.

"It's not a question of the posts. Posts are easy. It's the coil. A coil in a line that hasn't been out of its wrapper for a year in this weather will go over about half past seven in the morning and take the whole run with it, and you will find it in the neighbour's hedge at about eight."

He did not tell her that. He told her that she should not hang a wet sheet on it in the evening, and to bring it in for anything heavy.

"Right," she said again, and went in, and he carried on.

That is the whole of what was said to her. He has been in that yard twice since, and she has not asked him for anything else, and nobody in that house has put a name to him and he has not put one to himself.

The man in his forties went down that street on the Monday of the twenty-third of April, at about half past two in the afternoon, for a door frame that was going into the first house along.

That street is about a hundred yard long and has a parade end and a blind end, and from the parade end you can see a long way down it, and the back of the middle of that terrace is the top of the fourth house from the parade end, and it is the only place on that street where there is anything to see at all.

He was stopped at the parade end, at the corner, waiting to cross to put a trolley in the back of his van, and he looked up that street the way a man does.

There was a man in that yard with a bar and a shovel and a barrow, and a string line across the yard, and about four feet of the end of a galvanised post standing out of the ground at the top of it.

He stood there about twenty seconds. He did not go up that street. He did not see the man's face and he does not know who it is, and there are about nine men in this borough who put posts up for a living and he could not have picked one of them out of nine.

At about ten past four on that same Monday he was standing in a shop off that parade with a length of conduit in his hand, and he said, to nobody in particular, in the ordinary way a man fills a counter, that somebody was pegging out four posts at the back of a house off that street.

The woman who works in a shop was on that counter. She was at the middle of it, cutting a length of something off a roll, and she said, "Which man."

"Whoever he is," he said. "He's got the line up. It's that little terrace."

And she said, "That's the man who did the shopfront on our parade in March," which she knew because she had had the shopfront in her own shop put in, and he said, "Is it," and paid for his conduit and went.

That is the whole of it. Four seconds of it, at about ten past four, on the Monday, in a shop.

He was at that counter on the Friday at about half past four, buying a length of galvanised line to put on the four posts, and she rang his stuff through and put it in a bag.

He said, "You'd not mind if I was here on the Wednesday with the line up."

"No, that's all right," she said. "You'll do whatever you want with it."

That was not what she was going to say. She waited until he had the bag, and then she said, "The man who was in here on Monday with the conduit, that's the man in his forties. He said somebody was pegging out four posts at the back of a house off that street."

He did not say anything for about two seconds.

"Did he," he said.

"He did," she said. "He said somebody was pegging out four posts at the back of a house off that street, and I said which man, and he said whoever he is."

"And that's it."

"That's the whole of it," she said. "It came in on a length of conduit at about ten past four."

He picked the bag up off the counter and said, "Right," and went out into the parade.

He did not say yes and he did not say no and he did not ask her which man she meant, and she did not tell him that she knew, and that is where both of them have left it, and nobody has asked either of them about that, and nobody is going to.

He drove back down that street at about five o'clock and got that yard on his own and put the line up on those four posts, and it is up now, and it is a proper line and it is dead straight over twenty foot.

He did not think about what she had said once he was in the van, and the only reason he is writing this down at all is that a man who has been told what he is doing by somebody who was not there, and has said nothing about it, has not been caught by anything, and nobody will put him on the spot about it.

---

**HE SAID RIGHT AND WENT OUT INTO THE PARADE WITH A LENGTH OF LINE UNDER HIS ARM, AND THERE ARE FOUR POSTS IN THAT YARD AND A LINE ON THEM AND A COIL THAT HAS NOT BEEN OUT OF ITS WRAPPER SINCE MARCH, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED HIM ANYTHING AND HE IS NOT GOING TO TELL ANYBODY.**
