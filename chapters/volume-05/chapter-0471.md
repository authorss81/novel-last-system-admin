# Chapter 0471 — The Machine at the Back

Tuesday the fifth of February 2019, from about nine at night until about half past nine. One building and the floors above the ground floor of it, in Tideglass.

**TWO WOMEN LIVING ONE ABOVE THE OTHER HAVE EACH WANTED SOMETHING IN THAT BUILDING TO BE QUIET AND NEITHER EVER SAID SO TO THE OTHER OR TO ANYBODY, AND A MAN WITH A UNIT AT THE BACK CHANGED IT IN JANUARY, AND BOTH OF THEM FOUND OUT, AND NEITHER WILL SPEAK TO HIM ABOUT IT OR TO EACH OTHER.**

It is a building of four floors. There is a shop on the ground floor that shuts at half past four, and behind that shop there is a unit, and the man who has that unit has been there since 2016.

The unit is about twelve foot by nine and it is not a shop and it is not a store and it is not anything anybody in that building has ever asked him about. He goes in there in the mornings, and there is a machine in it, and at about half past nine at night the machine starts.

It takes about twenty-two minutes.

It is not a loud noise. That is the thing that has kept it going as long as it has. It is a thump about every four seconds, quite low, with a sort of rattle under it, and it comes up through the floor, and the woman on the first floor hears it as a series of small knocks, and the woman on the second floor hears it as a machine.

The woman on the first floor has wanted it to stop for about two years and nine months.

She moved in in the summer of 2016. She is on a rota with an early start and she is in bed by about half past ten, and from the first week in that flat the noise was there, and she put two things against it, which are a pillow over her head and a fan on a timer, and the fan worked well enough that she stopped noticing it after about a fortnight.

She has never said it to anybody. Not to the woman above, not to the man at the back, not to the man who owns the building and whom she has spoken to twice about the bins.

That is not because she is a quiet person. She is not. She has complained about the man on the first floor's bins in a voice that carries, and about the man upstairs's children at half past seven on a Sunday, and about a man in the car park who cannot reverse. She has a mouth on her and she uses it on about nine foot of a stairwell and nowhere else.

She has never said one word about that machine, and she has been asked about it exactly once, and the asking was in the October of 2017, and she gave the answer that is the easiest answer in the world to give.

A woman on the second floor asked her, on the stairs, whether that noise at night was the machines.

And she said, "Oh, I don't know. I sleep through everything."

---

The woman on the second floor started noticing after Christmas.

She has been in that building since about July and she wakes at the slightest thing, and what she has is not a machine in a unit at the back. What she has is a man on the first floor who comes in about half past twelve and is in his kitchen for about an hour and puts things about.

She has never said that either.

The thump came into what she had already decided was a man's kitchen about three weeks after she moved in, and she put the two things together the way anybody does, and she is quite sure of the putting together and quite wrong about it, and it does not matter because the machine is the noise.

It is the noise from about the first week of December that she started wanting to stop. It is not a big thing. It is a thing that makes you lie and listen for twenty-two minutes instead of lying and not listening for twenty-two minutes, and after about three weeks of it she worked out that she had started lying there earlier so that she would not be caught by the start of it.

And she has not said it. And there is one person in that building she could say it to, and she has worked out about nine times since December that she could go down one flight and stand on that landing and say the sentence, and she has not.

What happened on the twenty-ninth of January is that he changed the timer.

He set it about four months ago because the machine is no use to him before about nine and because a machine going for twenty minutes every night at half past nine is two hundred pounds a month of a thing he is not using.

On the twenty-ninth he found the part of that machine that makes the thump, which is an arm about the size of his hand, and he had it off and he had a spare, and it took about an hour, and while he had it off he looked at the timer and put it back a quarter of an hour later.

He was not thinking about anybody. He was not thinking about the women. He was thinking about an arm of metal and about a quarter of an hour.

She on the first floor worked it out on the Sunday night, because on the Sunday night there was no thump, and there has been no thump on a Sunday night since the last week of December and she has put that down to Sundays.

She lay there from about half past nine and she listened for it, and it did not come, and she lay there for about a quarter of an hour listening for it.

The woman on the second floor worked it out on the Thursday, and she worked out more than the other one, because the thump came at about ten to ten instead of half past, which took her two evenings to be certain of, and by the time she was certain of it she had already been certain for four days that the thump had started at the back of that building and not in the kitchen of the man below.

---

By the fifth of February both of them knew and neither of them knew that the other one knew.

That is the part that has done the damage, and neither of them could tell you afterwards why it is the part that has done the damage.

They are two women who have lived one above the other for two and a half years and have spoken perhaps ninety times, most of it about the bins and about a parcel, and there was never anything between them, and there is not going to be anything between them.

And now there is a thing they both wanted, and neither of them said, and a man at the back of that building did it in a week with a spanner.

What the woman on the first floor has not said and is not going to say is that she is worse off than she was at the end of December.

That is not a joke and she has not put it to herself in that shape. What she has is this: for two years and nine months the noise was a thing that was happening to her and that she was enduring, and enduring it was the largest single fact of her life as a person in that building, and now a man she has never spoken to has taken it away and there is nothing to endure, and nothing to endure is not a relief. It is a hole where a thing was, and she is lying in it.

And she cannot put any of that in front of the woman upstairs, because putting it in front of her would be admitting to a total stranger on a landing that she has been lying awake for two years and nine months over a noise that had stopped before either of them said anything about it.

What the woman on the second floor has not said is that she is angry at the woman on the first floor.

Not about the machine. About the two years and nine months.

She has done about an hour of that. She went back through it in December, when it first started to get to her, and she found that what she could not get past was not that man at the back. It was that a woman has been living directly underneath that noise since 2016 and has said nothing about it, ever, to anybody, including the person who is now the only other person in the world she would want to say it to.

And what she is not going to do, on a landing or anywhere else, is say that to her.

There is a man on the third floor of that building who has been leaving it at about ten since 2015 and who comes down past that unit four times a week, and he has never once had a reason to look at that door, and he has never opened it, never knocked on it, and nobody on earth is going to question him over any part of it.

There is a man on the fourth floor who has a television on at about the same time as that machine used to be and has never spoken to anybody about that either.

**AT ABOUT HALF PAST NINE ON THE FIFTH OF FEBRUARY A WOMAN CAME DOWN THE STAIRS OF THAT BUILDING WITH HER BAGS AND WENT OUT, AND A WOMAN ON THE FLOOR ABOVE HER HEARD THOSE BAGS, AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID ANYTHING, AND AT THE BACK OF THAT BUILDING A MACHINE SAT WITH A NEW ARM ON IT.**
