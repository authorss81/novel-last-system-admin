# Chapter 0627 — The Woman Who Told Him

Wednesday the tenth of March 2021, in this borough. She is in the flat by a quarter past six in the morning and she is still at the kitchen table at about seven in the evening, and the whole of it is one road of houses and one thing she has decided.

**A WOMAN WHO HAS SPENT HER LIFE BEING THE ONE WHO KNOWS HAS DECIDED TO STOP PASSING ON WHAT OTHER PEOPLE TELL HER, AND A MAN IN THE HOUSE WITH HER ASKS HER WHY SHE CAME HOME AND TOLD HIM A THING ABOUT A BIN, AND SHE GIVES HIM A REASON THAT HAS COME TO HER THROUGH TWO OTHER MOUTHS ON THE SAME ROW AND THAT SHE GIVES AWAY WITHOUT KNOWING THAT IT IS A SENTENCE.**

She is a woman in her mid thirties. He is a man in his late twenties. They have the top of a house that has a shop at the bottom of it, on the same row of twelve as the man at the end who cannot get a bin down to the corner.

The woman two doors from that end is the one who put it to her on the Monday, and this is the only part of the chapter that matters and she gets to it about two thirds of the way through.

---

He was eating off a plate on his lap with the television on low, and she had not been in the room for a bit and came in and put her bag on the end of the table, and he said, "You'll have been round at number nine's."

And she said, "I have not."

And he said, "You've had your coat on."

And she said, "I've been to the shops."

And he said, "You've had your good coat on."

And she said, "It's the only coat."

And he said, "Right," and he looked at her for a moment, and she knew what was coming and she let it come, because that is what she does and because on the Monday she had already decided.

And he said, "What did she want."

And she said, "Nothing."

And he said, "You were gone a good while for nothing."

And she said, "She wanted to tell me a thing."

And he said, "And you let her."

And she said, "I let her."

And he said, "What thing."

And she said, "That the man at the end of the row can't get his bin out and he's asked her to see if you'll do it."

And he said, "He asked her."

And she said, "He asked her."

And he said, "So he hasn't asked you."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "Then it's got nothing to do with you."

And she said, "He's asked her to ask me."

And he said, "That's not the same as asking you."

And she said, "I know it's not."

And he said, "So why are you telling me about it."

And she said, "Because I said I would."

And he said, "You said you'd tell me about bin men."

And she said, "I said I'd tell you about the row."

And he said, "Right."

And he put the plate down on his knee and said, "Go on."

And she said, "What?"

And he said, "You said you'd tell me about the row and you've told me about a bin, which is the row, and you said something else when you came in and you haven't said it yet, and I'm not going out."

And she said, "There's no something else."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "I want to stop doing it."

And he said, "Stop doing what."

And she said, "Telling people things somebody's told me."

And he said, "You want me to help you."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "I can help you."

And she said, "I know you can. That's not why I'm telling you."

And he said, "Then why are you telling me."

And that is where she said it, and it came out in the same tone she had used for everything else all evening, which she has noticed since and which she has not been able to account for.

And she said, "Because he asked."

---

Here is what is actually true and she did not say any part of it.

The woman at the end of the row is a man in his early sixties and she has known him by the shape of him since she came into this flat, and she has never had a conversation with him above the state of the weather and the state of the road.

He has not asked her for anything. He cannot get down the row to the corner and he has not tried to hide that. He has not asked her, and he has not asked the woman next but one either, and the woman next but one has not spoken to him since before the summer as far as she knows.

What he did was this. He went to the woman two doors from his end, on the Saturday before last, at about midday, and he asked her to see if the woman at the top would do it.

And the woman two doors from his end came up to this flat on the Monday morning with her cardigan on over her housecoat and sat on the end of this table where the man is sitting now, and she said, "He asked me to see if you'd do his bin."

And she said, "Right."

And the woman said, "You don't have to."

And she said, "I know I don't."

And the woman said, "It's not me being daft. He came and stood in the road and asked me to ask you."

And she said, "He could have asked me."

And the woman said, "He could have."

And she said, "Yes."

And the woman said, "Well, I'll tell him I have."

And she said, "Don't tell him I've said yes."

And the woman said, "You've said yes."

And she said, "I know I have."

And the woman said, "You'll want to think about it."

And she said, "I've thought about it."

And the woman said, "You've had about a minute."

And she said, "I've been thinking about it since Saturday."

Here is what she decided in that minute and has not been able to put down since.

She will do the bin. That is settled and it was settled on the Monday and she is not going to take it back.

What she has decided against is the rest of it. The part where she is the woman on this row who knows what is happening at the end of it. The part where she goes to the woman at number eight and says have you heard, and to the woman at number nine and says have you heard, and holds it and gives it out and is the one people come to.

She has done that since she was about nine. She has done it with her mother about her aunt, and with the woman at the shop about the woman at the launderette, and with the men on this road about the man at number ten. She is not a gossip. She would not use the word for herself and she would not use it for anybody else. She simply knows things and the knowing does not stay with her.

And she has decided to stop.

And she got to the reason for it, which she has not said to anybody, and the reason is not noble and she has made her peace with that on about half an hour.

The reason is that being the one who knows has never once cost her anything. She is not old and she has her forties coming and that is the point of it, and it has never cost her anything, and there is no arrangement in the world where that is going to keep being the case. She would rather find out while it is still cheap.

That is the true reason. It has nothing to do with a bin.

And what she said to a man eating off a plate on his lap was: because he asked.

Here is what happened with the rest of the day.

She went down at about half past two to tell the woman two doors from the end that she would do it.

And she said, "I'll see to his bin."

And the woman said, "You don't have to."

And she said, "I know I don't."

And the woman said, "Have you told him?"

And she said, "No."

And the woman said, "You'd better tell him."

And she said, "I know."

And the woman said, "Right," and went in.

And she went up again and did not do the bin herself. She went to the end of the row at about half past three and knocked, which she has not done since she came into the flat, and he came to the door in a cardigan and did not know her and did not say so.

And she said, "I'm at number six."

And he said, "Aye."

And she said, "I'll see to your bin on Wednesdays."

And he said, "I didn't want to put you out."

And she said, "I know you didn't."

And he said, "I asked her to ask you because I couldn't stand at your door."

And she said, "I know."

And he said, "You do."

And she said, "We've all got on with it."

And he said, "I have."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "It's a Wednesday thing."

And she said, "I know what day it is."

And he said, "I mean it's not mine. It's got my wife's writing on it. It was hers."

And she said, "I know it was."

And he said, "I couldn't tell you the colour of it."

And she said, "You don't have to."

And he said, "That's good of you."

And she said, "It's all right."

And she said, "I'll do it Wednesday."

And he said, "Right."

That is the whole of it and it took about a minute and a half and she has been back over it about nine times.

He said, *I asked her to ask you because I couldn't stand at your door.*

And she said, *I know.*

And she knew.

Here is the thing she was not ready for and got on about half past four on the row outside the shop with her bag in her hand.

She knew before she knocked. She had known it since the Saturday and she had known it in the kitchen on the Monday morning with the woman sitting on the end of the table.

A man who says he cannot stand at your door, and asks somebody else to go, is a man who has a reason for not standing at a door.

And he has not given her the reason and she did not ask for it and she is not going to.

But she has said *because he asked*, out loud, in her own kitchen, to a man who eats off a plate on his lap, and she said it in the tone she uses for the weather.

And that is the finding of the day and she has been going round it since about five.

The reason she gave was not a reason for anything. If he had said to her at his own door, at about half past three on a Wednesday, *would you put my bin out, my back's gone* — which is the whole of what he wanted and is about four words long — then she would have said yes and meant it and there would have been nothing to think about, and she would have done it on the Wednesday and it would have been a bin.

And instead there was a man in a doorway who could not stand at a door, and a woman in a cardigan on this table, and a Saturday, and a card.

And what she has in her kitchen now, at about seven in the evening, is a sentence that has come to her through two people and that she has given away to the third without once having a thought about it.

*Because he asked.*

That is four words and it is not hers. It has been in two mouths on this row before it got to her and she has heard it and she has used it, and she used it to a man as a reason for saying a thing she has actually decided on her own.

And he did not even hear it. He had gone back to the plate by then.

At about a quarter past seven she said, "You'll want your tea."

And he said, "Go on then."

And she said, "It isn't about the bin."

And he said, "I never thought it was."

**SHE TOLD A MAN IN HER OWN KITCHEN THAT THE MAN AT THE END OF THE ROW HAD ASKED FOR HER, AND THE WORDS CAME TO HER THROUGH TWO OTHER MOUTHS AND SHE GAVE THEM AWAY TO THE THIRD BEFORE SHE KNEW THAT THEY WERE WORDS, AND SHE HAS DECIDED TO STOP BEING THE ONE ON THIS ROW WHO KNOWS, AND THE ONLY PERSON SHE HAS SAID IT TO IS A MAN EATING OFF A PLATE ON HIS LAP.**
