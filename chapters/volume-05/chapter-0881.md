# Chapter 0881 — A Skin Of Stones

Friday the second of February 2024, at the edging heap against the wall at number twelve from the time the shade has come off the top of it until the flags have gone grey. The gaps between the stones on the top of it.

**THERE IS SIX FEET OF EDGING STOOD AGAINST THAT WALL UNDER A LOOP OF BLUE STRING AND SHE MEANS TO PUT A HAND FLAT ON THE TOP OF IT, PALM DOWN, AND PUSH DOWN BETWEEN TWO OF THE STONES UNTIL IT COMES ON THE STONES BELOW, AND HER SISTER IS AT THE FRONT WINDOW FROM OUTSIDE WITH A HAND ON THE GLASS.**

The heap has been there since the spring. It has settled from six feet to five foot six and the string over the top of it has a foot of slack out on the flags at each end, and none of that has moved and all of it has been found out with a hand on the ground.

What has not been found out is the heap itself.

You cannot see into a heap of broken stone. That is not a complaint. It is the arrangement. From the gate you get the outside of it: six feet long, four feet at the base, five foot six high, the string over the top with its slack out on both sides. And there is no way at all from outside to tell what the inside of it is, and the inside of it is the part that decides what happens to it.

She is seventy-eight and she is not going to turn that heap over to find out, and nobody is going to dig it out in February.

So she does the only thing there is.

She puts a hand flat on the top of it, palm down, across two stones and in the gap between them, and pushes down.

That is the whole of the technique and it is worth saying why it works. A hand on top of a heap tells you what is on top of a heap. A hand pushed down between two things tells you what is behind them, because the fingers go into the gap and the gap is the only place in a heap a hand is welcome.

The first gap takes her about half an inch, and takes the same as the second, which is what loose broken stone always gives at the top of a heap.

The second one takes the same. Across four feet of the top of that heap there is not one place where the stones are touching, and where two stones lean against each other they lean on their corners and there is a wedge of nothing between them going down.

She finds the widest of them, which is up near the wall end where a long piece lies with its edge up, and she sets the heel of her hand on the top of that piece and puts the fingers down on either side of it.

The fingers go down.

They go down about three inches and then they come on stone.

Three inches. She can get three fingers in beside that long piece and then there is more stone under her fingers.

And between the two of them there is nothing.

She turns her head to get her eye down the gap, which is not easy at that angle and at that age, and what she can see down it is not more stone.

It is the side of another stone about four inches off, and it is dark down there, and it is not in contact with anything.

A thumbnail goes into that gap and there is nothing on the other side of it.

That is the finding, and it has been sitting in that yard since March.

Six feet of edging was tipped into that corner off a barrow and it was not built. It was tipped. And a heap that is tipped is not a wall and not a stack and not a solid of anything. It is the outside of something. Every piece that came off that barrow landed where it landed and stayed where it landed, and the pieces that landed on their sides stood up and the pieces that landed on their ends lay over, and none of them were placed and none of them were bedded and nothing was shaken down.

So the whole of that heap is about three inches thick at the top, it gives a hand the same answer at every point along four feet of it, and after that there is air.

And the air is a large thing, and it is not a fault. Six feet of broken stone weighs a great deal and it is standing up in a corner of a yard without a wall behind it on one side and a lean-to on the other, and the reason it is up at all is that a heap of loose broken stone is a good deal stronger than people think. The pieces lock against each other and the heap stands on its own lock.

It is only the top three inches of it that is anything like a surface.

There is an obvious thing to be done about a heap of air, and the obvious thing is to put stone in the air.

It is not a day's work. It is a barrow and the bar and about four feet of the front of that heap taken down a course at a time and put back up the way a wall is built, with the big ones at the bottom and the small ones over the top, and the string off and on so that the heap does not spread while it is being done.

It would also undo four months of settling, which means the heap would go back up to about six feet and the slack in the string would go back under the bottom of it, and the whole arrangement would be as it was in March.

And it is a job for two people, because a four-foot face of loose stone gives way on you all at once if you take it down in the wrong order, and she is seventy-eight, and the arm that goes under a stone went on a different bed last March.

So the air stays where it is.

Which brings back the string.

She has been thinking since last week that the string was doing something and it turns out that the string is doing one thing and one thing only, which is stopping the heap spreading.

A loop over a heap of stone with its ends tucked underneath keeps the whole of it against the wall. It has no strength at all against anything coming down through it. There is nothing to come down through, because there is no hole in the top of that heap for anything to come down through. The stones are not touching.

So the string is stopping the outside of a heap from going anywhere and it has got nothing whatever to do with the air in the middle of it.

Which means the thing she has been half expecting for four months is not going to happen, and the thing that is going to happen is worse.

What comes off that wall — the rain on the front of the house above that heap, and there is a good deal of it in an ordinary month — does not go into the heap. It goes down the wall behind it, along the three inches of stone and down to the ground behind, and out from under the bottom of the heap on the flags at the wall end.

And it has been doing that since March, and there is a flag at that end of the yard where you can see the grit going out across it in a wet week, and nobody in that house goes to that corner of the yard.

What comes off that wall gets nowhere near the top of that heap, because there is nowhere in it for water to go.

---

Her sister comes to the front window from outside towards the end of the afternoon with a hand flat on the glass and stands there.

The window has been up since the summer and she puts her face round the edge of the opening and looks along the room. She wants the small pair of shears and they are not in the drawer in the front room and they have not been in that drawer since the spring. She stood at the window with her hand on it until the light had gone enough that she took it off, and then she went round the side of the house, and the heap is at the far end of the front of the house and there is a whole width of building between her and it.

---

So the heap goes on being what it is.

Down through that heap there is three inches of stone and then air, and no water going in at the top of it because there is no way in.
