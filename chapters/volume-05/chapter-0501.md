# Chapter 0501 — She Was Not Supposed to Hear

The Sunday of the twenty-third of June 2019 from about half past three in the afternoon until about six, and then the Wednesday morning of the twenty-sixth until about a quarter to nine. A kitchen, the passage beside it, and a front room, in Tideglass.

**A MAN SAID ONE SENTENCE TO HIS OWN FATHER IN A KITCHEN ON THE AFTERNOON OF THE TWENTY-THIRD OF JUNE 2019 WITH THE DOOR OPEN AND A PERSON IN THE PASSAGE CARRYING FOUR PLATES, AND THE FATHER HAS NOT THOUGHT ABOUT THAT SENTENCE SINCE, AND THE SON HAS THOUGHT ABOUT NOTHING ELSE FOR THREE DAYS, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO MEN KNOWS THAT ANYBODY ELSE HEARD IT.**

The house is number four of a row of six at the end of a street off the parade. It is a small terrace house and the kitchen is at the back and there is a passage running from the front door past the foot of the stairs to that kitchen, and the kitchen door stands open because the house gets almost no sun in it between October and March.

The son is a man of about forty-five. He does something at a place four miles off that is not a trade anybody can describe in one sentence. The wife has been with him since 2008 and works three days at a place in town.

The father is a man of about seventy-six who has lived alone since 2013 and who comes to that house on Sundays and has done for about six years.

---

Here is how it happened, and it is about a sentence and about nothing at all happening.

The son cooked it, which is what he does, and they ate it at about half past four, and the old man had two helpings and said so, and the son was pleased about that in a way he did not show.

And then the old man got up and put his plate on the table and said, "I'll just wash up."

And the son said, "Leave it, I'll do it."

And the wife said, "I'll do it," and took four plates off the table and went out through that kitchen door into the passage, and went about nine foot down it towards the front of the house to get the hot water.

And the two men were at the table in the kitchen with the door open and about nine foot of passage between that door and the front door, and a woman in that passage with a stack of four plates, and they said what they said.

---

Here is the whole of the exchange, and it is about a minute and a half, and it has been described here exactly.

The old man said: "I'll not come next Sunday if you want the room."

And the son said: "It's not the room."

And the old man said: "Well, it's the only thing I'd have thought of."

And the son said, and this is the sentence: "Dad. I've been at this for two years and I want to be able to tell you that I'd rather you didn't come on a Sunday, and the reason I have not told you is that I could not think of the sentence."

And the old man said: "You've told me now."

And the son said: "I have."

And the old man said: "Right," and got up, and the son said, "I do want you to come. I want you here. I have never once wanted you not to be in this house and that is not what that was."

And the old man said, "I know that," and went out into the passage, where his daughter-in-law was standing with a stack of four plates, and said, "Right, I'll be off then," and let himself out of the front door and went up the street.

The woman stood in that passage for about a minute afterwards and then went and did the washing up and said nothing about any of it and has said nothing since.

Here is what each of the three of them did with it, and none of the three of them knows what the other two did.

The old man forgot it by the middle of the week. That is not a figure of speech. He has a way of putting a conversation down when he leaves a room, and he did it in the passage that day, and by the Wednesday morning of the twenty-sixth of June there was nothing at all in it for him, and he would have told anybody who asked that he had had a good Sunday and that the roast was a bit small.

The son has not put it down. He has thought about it every day since and he has got to the point where he is not sure whether the thing that happened in that kitchen on Sunday was a thing he did or a thing that happened to him, and he has not worked that out, and he has noticed that not working it out is the thing he has been doing with it.

The woman has not mentioned it to either of them and is not going to, and the reason is not loyalty to her husband.

It is that if she tells him what she heard, then she is a wife who heard her husband say a thing about his father, and if she tells his father, then she is a woman who has carried a thing about a man in her own house, and she is not going to be either of those. And she has also worked out, in three days, that there is a third thing underneath both of those, and the third thing is that she wishes the kitchen door had been shut.

Because a thing said in a kitchen with the door open is a thing that both men can go on denying, and they are both going to, and she is the only one of the three of them who cannot.

What happened on the Wednesday morning is this.

The son telephoned his father at about a quarter to nine because he wanted to ask whether the old man wanted a lift on the Sunday, and the old man was in.

He said, "You're all right, are you? You were a bit quiet on the phone, last week."

And the son said, "I'm all right."

And the old man said, "You'll not want me Sunday, then."

And there was about four seconds of nothing on the line, and it is not a thing the old man can remember, and the son can remember every one of those four seconds.

And the son said: "I'll see you about one, Dad."

And the old man said: "Right, I'll be ready."

And they said something else about a thing that is coming in the post, and the call ended after about two minutes.

The son put the telephone down in the front room and stood there for a while. He did not go into the kitchen. He did not go out.

And here is the part that is not about him either.

The woman came into that front room at about five to nine with her coat on and said she was going, and stopped, because her husband was standing in the middle of the room not doing anything.

And she said, "You've gone grey."

And he said, "Have I."

And she said, "You have," and then she said, "What did he want?"

And he said, "He thought I didn't want him on Sunday."

And she looked at him for about two seconds and said: "Did you tell him you did?"

And he said: "I said I'd see him about one."

And she said, "Right," and went, and shut the front door, and did not look back, and she has not raised it since and she is not going to.

**A MAN STOOD IN THE MIDDLE OF A FRONT ROOM ON THE WEDNESDAY MORNING OF THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF JUNE 2019 AFTER HE HAD PUT A TELEPHONE DOWN AND DID NOT GO INTO THE KITCHEN, AND A WOMAN CAME IN WITH HER COAT ON AND ASKED HIM TWO QUESTIONS AND WENT OUT AGAIN.**
