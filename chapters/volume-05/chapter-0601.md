# Chapter 0601 — She Asked Somebody Who Asked Somebody

The Wednesday of the twenty-eighth of October 2020, beginning at about ten in the morning, in a first-floor flat in this borough and at a house in a road about a mile off it, and running until about four in the afternoon.

**SHE WAS TOLD A THING ON THE MONDAY BEFORE AND THIS MORNING SHE ASKED A PERSON TO PASS IT ON, AND THAT PERSON ASKED ANOTHER PERSON, AND THAT OTHER PERSON DID EXACTLY WHAT WAS ASKED OF HIM AND TOOK IT ROUND TO A HOUSE IN THIS BOROUGH AND LEFT IT WITH A WOMAN WHO IS NOT THE PERSON IT WAS FOR.**

The man who told her is a man in his early twenties. The woman who passed it on is a woman in her late thirties and she is his sister. The man who carried it is her brother, a man in his late thirties. The man who pointed the way is a man in his late forties who works in the shop on that corner and was in it on his own. The woman who took it in is a woman in her mid forties and she lives in a different part of the borough entirely.

The thing is a set of keys.

Here is the thing that was said.

The man in his early twenties said it on the Monday before, at about half past nine in the morning, in the queue, and he said it to the woman in her late thirties and not to anybody else, and she is the only person he spoke to that day about it.

He said, "You could give these back to my mam for me."

And she said, "When?"

And he said, "Any time. She's not using them."

And she said, "Right. I'll drop them in."

And he said, "Cheers."

And they got on the bus, and that was the whole of it.

Here is the difficulty, and it is that he did not say which set, and there are two.

His mother has two sets. One is the set for the house he grew up in, on a road about two miles from where he lives now, which nobody has lived in since the March except his mother, and she is there some nights and not others. The other is for a flat she has had for about two years, in another part of the borough, and she is in it every day of the week except Wednesdays.

He did not say which set and she did not ask, and she did not ask because he said *any time* and *she's not using them*, and a person who says those two things is handing over two sets of keys that are both doing nothing on a Wednesday.

Everything the woman in her late thirties did about those keys happened in about three quarters of an hour this morning, and this is all of it.

She went home at about half past ten and got both sets out of her own bag, because she has been carrying them since the Monday before last, which she has not told anybody either, and because she thought about them for a fortnight and has not settled it.

She took the older one, which is on a ring with a fob on it and has been since she was at school, and put it in the front of her bag.

She took the newer one, which is three keys on their own, and put that in her coat pocket.

And she went out at about a quarter to twelve and she went to neither.

What she did instead was ring her brother at about ten past twelve from the corner and ask whether he was going past his mother's on the way home this evening.

He said, "About six this evening."

And she said, "Can you take these round for me?"

And she said which set, and she said the older one, the ring with the fob, because the fob is the one his mother recognises, and because it is the one she was handed first on the Monday before last, and because she had picked it up out of the bag this morning without thinking about it, which is the actual reason and is not a reason she is going to say out loud.

He said, "Right."

And she said, "It's the one with the fob. Last but one past the shop."

And he said, "I've not been past there in a bit."

And she said, "You can't miss it. It's got a front garden."

And he said, "I've got it."

And she said, "Don't ring. She's not expecting you."

And he said, "Right."

And they both hung up, and that was the whole of the asking.

What he did about it, at about six this evening, was this. He had the bag out of his pocket in the street and the ring round his finger and the fob on it, and he walked to the end of that road and turned and went up it counting, and he found the house at the last but one past the shop on the corner.

It has a front garden that has not been done since the nineties and a door that has not been painted since the eighties and a window on the ground floor about the width of the door.

There is a shop on that corner and the man in it is a man in his late forties and he was putting the shutters half down when the brother went past, and the brother stopped and asked him which way it was, and the man told him, and said, "Second one. The one with the bit of a garden."

And the brother said, "Right," and went up the road, and the man in the shop went on with the shutters and has not thought about it since.

The brother knocked.

A woman in her mid forties came to the door. She is not his mother. His mother is a woman in her late sixties and this one is younger by a good many years.

She said, "Yes?"

And he said, "Is this the one with the garden?"

And she said, "It is."

He had the ring in his hand by then and he put it up where she could see it without saying anything about it.

And he said, "Is there a woman here who has a set of keys with a fob on it?"

And she said, "Keys?"

And he said, "I've got a set I was asked to bring."

And she said, "Bring them where?"

And he said, "Here. I was told to bring them here."

And she said, "Who from?"

And he said, "My sister."

And she said, "Your sister?"

And he said, "I've got a ring with a fob on it and I'm to leave it."

She looked at the ring and then at him and said, "I'll take them."

And he gave them to her and she put them on the shelf by the door, and he said, "She'll know which ones they are."

She said, "Right," and shut the door, and he stood on the step for a second afterwards and then went, and he did not look back, and he did not go to the next house, and he has not mentioned it to anybody.

---

Here is what happened on both sides of it, and neither side is a mistake.

The woman in her mid forties has a set of keys on a ring with a fob on it, on a shelf by her door. Her own are on a hook in her hall, and they are the same make and the same fob, and they have been since the June, because the ones she was given in the June had come off their fob.

The woman in her late sixties has been at the flat this week and is at the house tomorrow. The flat key on that fob was in a coat pocket in a kitchen about two miles away, where the woman in her late thirties had put it this morning before she went out, on the table by the toaster, with the other two loose ones beside it.

And the man in his early twenties gave two sets of keys to one person on a Monday in the queue and said *any time* and *she's not using them*, and both of those were true, and the two of them had to be, because on a Wednesday she is not using either.

She asked her brother and he did exactly what she asked him and took it to a house a mile off and knocked and left it with a woman who is not the person it was for. Nobody has told anybody anything.
