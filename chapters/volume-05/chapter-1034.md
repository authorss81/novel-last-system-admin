# Chapter 1034 — What She Is Going To Ask Two Men

Undated in this draft.

**A CHAIR STANDS AGAINST THE WALL IN THAT ROOM AND NOTHING ELSE IS IN IT.**

The room over the kitchen at number nine is a small cold box with one window in it giving on that back yard and a single chair under the window against the wall, and no bed in it and no fire and no shelf, and in that whole house there is nowhere a person can sit and not be asked a question. The woman of that house goes up there when the front room has the fire out in it and the kitchen has somebody else's washing stood up in it, and she has spent a fortnight in that room with nothing to do except the one thing she cannot get anybody else to take on, and on the afternoons when the fire goes out in the front room downstairs she sits up there and looks at a wall, and nothing whatever in this borough would come up that stair behind her and help.

That one thing is a range. It has gone out twice on her since the week she came down the river, and she has been feeding a house off a fire that will not stay in, and she has not asked anybody, and she has not told anybody she has not asked anybody either.

He came up that stair and she was in that room with her hands in her pockets and the window open about four inches, and the back yard below it is short between two walls and gets about an hour of the middle of the day and no more of it.

"You have come to bring me something and it is bad news, because you have been in this house four times since the spring and three of those were you being refused."

"I have come to tell you that I have begun and I cannot finish."

---

"Cannot, or will not, and there is a difference between the two of us on this frontage and I know which one it is from where I am standing."

"Cannot. That range wants two men under it, and there is a tray made and a length of flue to buy and no money for the flue and no two men I can pay to put under it. I have been to that yard four streets away twice this week and every time I came away with the same thing in my head, which is that a shut season is a shut season whatever page it is on. So I am going to end up as a man who has taken a woman's money for a fortnight of work and has not done a single day's work on it, and I cannot think of one thing that makes that better than being a man who turned it down."

"You cannot think of one thing?"

"I can think of one and it is not much of one. If somebody else does that fortnight, then it gets done."

She turned round at that.

"Put that again."

"I told her if somebody else does it, then it gets done."

"Somebody else, and not you."

"Not me. I have no money for a second man and there is not one woman on that frontage I would put that in front of."

"No." She took her hands out of her pockets. "I have been sitting on that top step for two days waiting for a thing to come to me, because nothing has come to me since the season shut, and my name was on that book before I could reach the handle on a tap, and I know exactly what it feels like to be inside a room with a tradesman in it and not know the price of him. And you have just handed me the one thing I have wanted for a fortnight, which is that somebody is going to take that wall off its brick, and you have told me the man will not be you."

"You are not going to do anything about it."

"I am going to do one thing about it, and you cannot stop me, and I want you to hear it out before you try."

"You are going to ask two men what a fortnight of a man's time costs a woman."

"I am going to put that question to two men, and I am going to tell them it is a range coming off a wall at a house at the back of that frontage, and the woman has the money and the woman is not going to ask anybody at all, and I am going to find out what two men in this borough think a fortnight costs a house that cannot bring itself to ask."

"And when you have put it to them?"

"Then I carry whatever they tell me to that house, and if it comes to more than she has, she will find the rest the way women on this frontage find the rest, which is not out of a purse." She got up, went over to that window and put a hand on the frame of it. "My entire plan, and it took me two days on that top step, and you have had the news for ten minutes." She stopped and looked at him. "And you are not going to be in that room at any point."

"That is not a thing you are able to arrange, and you have been at it for two days already and I have seen you at it from the bottom of that stair."

"I know two men who have done work in that street for nothing and been turned away by women who would not have either of them inside a house they had not asked for myself. Not one of those two has ever been asked what a thing costs a woman, and that question has been sitting outside that house since the spring with nobody carrying it. That is the question, and I am the one who can put it, and a woman can put a question like that wherever she likes."

---

"Then hear one thing from me first."

"Go on."

"My name is in two women's heads in this borough and it is on no piece of paper anywhere in it. One of them keeps a book and will not have it because nobody asked her for it, and one of them took it and made nothing of it and has not mentioned it since, and I am telling you the whole of that because you are about to put a question to two men and I would rather you did it with all of it in your head." He took his hand off the back of that chair. "I am not giving it to you, and I am putting it in these words so that you cannot come back on this in a fortnight telling anybody they were never told."

"You are not going to give it to me."

"Not today. A name does not get anywhere in this borough unless a house asks for something, and you have not asked a house for anything in your life, and if you did it would not be for this."

"Do not tell me not to use it."

"I am telling you not to use it."

"Then I will use it." She got up off that wall. "Not to get at a page. To make two men believe a woman stands behind this and not a man talking out of his own head, and all I shall do with it is that, and you can have it back inside a week if you want it."

"Then you are not going to see it back in your life."

"No."

He went down that passage and out of that front door and did not shut it behind him, and the range at the back of that house has not come off its wall, and the tray a man made for it is standing on its edge against a wall in a store, and that tray has no work to do until two men exist.

His name is in three women's heads in that borough now and one of them told him to his face that she means to use it, and there is not one piece of paper anywhere in it that carries him.
