# Chapter 0254 — Ordinary

Saturday the twenty-third of July 2016, from about eleven in the morning, a building by the water in Tideglass, a parade of shops, and a hall off the parade.

**THE BOROUGH HAD AGREED TO SEND ITS OWN CREWS OUT OF ITSELF BEFORE ANYTHING INSIDE IT GOT DONE, AND ON A SATURDAY IN JULY ABOUT A THIRD OF THE MEN WHO DO THAT WERE EIGHT MILES AWAY AND EVERY PERSON IN A HALL THAT AFTERNOON COULD NAME EVERY ONE OF THEM, AND NOT ONE PERSON IN THAT HALL COULD SAY WHEN ANY OF THEM WAS COMING BACK, AND THAT IS WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE WHEN IT IS HOLDING.**

Nina Calder comes in about once a month when nobody is asking her for anything, and on the twenty-third of July she was in the records room with a cup of tea on the sill and a list of what was left in a store.

"The door sticks," she said.

Jonas Mercer stood at the table by the wall and did not open the register, because he never opens it.

He had a piece of paper in his coat with a date on it and did the sum standing up in the margin with a biro, and it came to six hundred and five, and he wrote that on the piece of paper and put the piece of paper back in his coat, and the register was at nineteen and the nineteenth was a yard light with a bulb in a maintenance column and the bulb was out, and under it a line went down the page and stopped about two inches short of the bottom, and the whole of what is on that line is a number he does once a month for nobody.

"That's the same as last month," said Nina Calder, without being asked, because she was going down the list and it was on the list.

"It's the same as every month."

"It's the same as every month," she said, and went back to the list, and the door stuck, and he was out of the building by twenty past eleven and down the parade by twelve.

---

The parade was the same parade it has been since before any of this, and the shutter on the shop went up at the minute it always goes up, and the window card with the shop's own telephone number on it and nothing else on it was in the window where it has always been, and the woman of about sixty-five was behind that counter, and nobody has ever gone in and told her anything and nobody is going to today.

A man behind a till on the parade asked Jonas Mercer, as he came past, standing with his shoulder against the door frame, what a two-inch swept bend went for in the catalogue, and Jonas Mercer told him, and it took about four seconds and it was correct, and the man said "cheers" and wrote it on the back of a receipt and did not ask him anything else.

It was the first thing anybody in this borough had asked Jonas Mercer since the eleventh of May, and it was a price, and it took four seconds.

Tomas Renn had a crate of rags on his shoulder at about half past twelve and put it down on the kerb and wiped his hands on his apron.

"Two vans are out."

"Which two."

"Boat and one off the engine." He looked up the parade. "I know where two vans are. I'd like that noted, because a fortnight ago I knew where one and it was a novelty, and now it's a Tuesday."

"Nobody's asked you to be noted."

"I know that," said Tomas Renn, and went inside, and shut the door.

There was a white van with a scabbed corner on the forecourt at the end of the road and it was off Lowdale, and it had come back at about eleven, and the woman in the second house of Marsh Lane was not in it and was doing her brother's materials in one trip on a Friday for the first time since April, and had said in June that it would come back to about what it was by the middle of July and had been right.

At the bottom of Peverell Street the door of number forty-one was open a hand's width and there was a length of chain round the back of the house on a gate, and the man who had bought it would not say whose gate it was, and nobody had asked him again since the Sunday, and the pump is still in the doorway and the stopcock is off and nobody had asked him about the stopcock.

---

The hall off the parade is free on a Saturday afternoon because nobody rents it and the woman who has had the key to it for about nine years is in it, and the gas is on a meter, and she had put a pound in before anybody came because there is no reason on earth to stop now there are nine of them.

About nine people came and every one of them was on nothing.

That is the sentence that describes the room and it is not a complaint. Mrs Obi, who is about thirty-six, was at the front with the pad shut on her knee. The man in his sixties, who is a building estimator, was sitting down, which is unusual. The young woman of about twenty was in. The man of about eighteen, who washes up in a kitchen at the back of the parade, was in. The woman of about fifty-five, who has the room at the end of the first-floor corridor in a building by the water, was in. The woman who has been in that hall about two years was in. A man from the parade. And a man in his forties who had come for the last hour of it and had not brought anything.

The man from the parade put his hand up and asked whether anybody was going to write any of it down, and the young woman of about twenty said there was nothing on the table to write it on, and he said, "You've not got a pad," and she said, "I've not got a pad," and that was the entire minute of that afternoon and there was no minute.

They did not do the six questions. Somebody suggested doing the six questions and the man in his sixties said, "No, and the reason we are not is that we did them in April and we did them again in July and neither time answered one, and the only difference between the two evenings is that in July I put a wrong one in the room on purpose and the room let it stand, and if we do the six again in August we will be a hall that meets about the six questions, and that is a committee, and everybody in this borough knows what a committee is."

They talked about the price of a fitting, the weather, a man in the third house in Corrance Lane whose roof had come off in a wind in April and was still off, and the eight places on a sheet in a drawer, and the four yards at the bottom of a hill, and a boiler in the Methodist hall on Carrow Lane that anybody could have had for nothing since about February and had not been taken.

And then the man from the parade said the thing that was in everybody's mouth and nobody had wanted to say first.

"That's the fifth Tuesday running I've not known where a van in this borough was," he said. "Not a fortnight. Five Tuesdays. And I've stopped minding, and I want to be careful here, because I know how this sounds, and I'd like it in the room that I have stopped minding and I have not made my peace with it, and those are two different things, and the difference is the whole of it."

Nobody argued with him and nobody agreed with him and the young woman of about twenty wrote nothing down, because there was nothing on the table to write on, and nobody had brought anything, and that has been true of every one of these rooms since the first of them.

At about half past four most of it went out into the parade in the ordinary way, and about four stayed, and the woman of about forty-two who has the key to that hall asked the man of about eighteen, at the side of the table, with the gas still on:

"Are you still after that thing you asked."

"I'm still after it."

"Anybody said anything to you about it."

"No."

"Right," she said, and put the key in the drawer, and shut the drawer, and that was the end of it.

**AND THE LINE UNDER THE NINETEENTH ENTRY IN A REGISTER IN A BUILDING BY THE WATER IS SIX HUNDRED AND FIVE DAYS, AND A PARADE WENT ON BEING A PARADE, AND A VAN CAME BACK AT ELEVEN, AND A MAN AT NUMBER FORTY-ONE HAS A CHAIN ROUND THE BACK OF HIS HOUSE ON A GATE HE WOULD NOT EXPLAIN, AND NOBODY HAS ANSWERED THE QUESTION, AND THE GAS WAS STILL ON.**
