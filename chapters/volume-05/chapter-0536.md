# Chapter 0536 — The Number She Has Not Written Down

The Thursday of the nineteenth of December 2019, from about four in the afternoon until about ten at night, in a flat on the third floor of a building in this borough.

**SHE HAS RUNG HIM EVERY WEEK AT HALF PAST SEVEN IN THE EVENING SINCE 2008 AND HE ANSWERED FOR YEARS, AND IN THE JUNE OF 2019 HE WENT SOMEWHERE ELSE AND POSTED HER NOTHING, AND ON THIS EVENING SHE PUT TEN DIGITS INTO THE PHONE OUT OF HER OWN HEAD AND GOT A STRANGER, AND SHE PUT IT DOWN AGAIN WITHOUT SAYING WHO SHE WAS.**

She is a woman in her early fifties. She has been at that flat since 1998, on her own since the flat below hers was emptied out in 2016, and she is not a woman who has ever had to ring anybody about anything.

The call is on that evening every week. It has been on the same evening every week since 2008 and before that it was at nine, and the change to half past seven came in the March of 2011 because she started working Saturdays and has never gone back.

For most of those years there was somebody on the other end. Not always the same somebody in the sense that anybody would call it, but there was a man on the other end for most of those years, and he was on the other end more often than not, and in the year 2013 he was on the other end fifty times, which is a figure she has never said out loud to anybody and has not thought about since.

She cannot remember which June it was. That is not a thing about her memory. She has three things in that June in her head and the first one is a date at the end of a long week, and the second is that she was not in the borough for any part of it, and the third is that she rang the number on the Friday and a voice she did not know said the number had gone in June and asked whether she wanted the new one, and she said she would ring him and he said he was going that weekend.

He moved on the Friday. He went on the Friday. The last time she rang him on that number it was the fourteenth of June and he said he was going that night and he said she would hear from him and she said all right.

She never heard from him. Not because she rang and got nothing. She has rung that number every Friday since then and it has never been his and it has never been hers to change.

---

What she rang him on the Friday before this one about was the piece of post she sent him for his birthday.

There is no need to be dramatic about that. It is what it was. It is what anybody sends. It had the address he had been at since 2014 on it, which she had by heart and never thought about, because she had not needed it thought about since June and there was no reason on earth for her to have checked whether he was still there before the middle of October.

It came back in the last week of October. It came back with a sticker across the front and a short word stamped on it and one of those printed marks at the top. She turned it over and looked at the back and there was nothing on the back.

She put it in with the other post that is not going anywhere and she did not ring him, because she worked out inside a few minutes that she did not have a new number, and then she worked out inside a few more minutes that she did not want to hear a stranger say the number had gone again.

She rang him twice on the Friday before this one and it was the first time since the June of 2019 that a voice on that number was his.

She rang at half past seven. It rang out. She put the phone down and stood in the kitchen for a minute and then she picked it up and rang it again, and this time he said his name.

He said his name the way a man says his own name when he is checking who he is talking to, and she said it was her, and he said he was glad to hear it, and she said she hoped she was not catching him at a bad moment, and he said she was not.

She asked him whether he was going to be in this borough over Christmas.

He said no. He said it quickly and he did not name a town to her, and she said that was all right and she was not asking, and he said no, she was asking, and she was, and he said he was sorry.

Then he said, and she has gone over this part more times than she will ever admit to, "You have still got the old number, haven't you." And she said that she had, and that she had had it since the June. And there was a pause on the line long enough for her to look at the time in the middle of the cooker hood, and then he said, "Then write this down," and he said a number.

The number is ten digits. She knows it is ten digits and she knows the first four of them and she has the middle three. She is not certain about the last three and she has not been certain about them since Friday.

She wrote it nowhere.

That is the thing and it is the whole of the thing. She did not get a pen. She did not put it on anything. He said, "Write this down," and she said, "I have got it," and he said, "Right," and then the call went on for about four minutes longer and they said nothing that either of them would remember in a week.

She thought about writing it down on the Saturday, and on the Sunday, and on the Monday, and she did not do it on any of those days either.

---

On the Thursday she rang it at ten past six.

She was in the kitchen and it had been dark outside for about a quarter of an hour and she had been standing there doing nothing in particular since about half past four, and at ten past six she picked the phone up and put the ten digits in from memory, which she had been holding in her head since the Friday and had not put it on anything.

It rang four times and a woman said, "Hello."

The woman said it in the way a woman says hello from the front of her own house, with somebody behind her.

She did not say anything at all. She put the phone down.

Then she stood there and worked out what had happened, and the working-out took about ten seconds, and then she picked the phone up again and rang the number she has had written in the back of her own book since the June of 2019, and when he answered she asked him for the last three of them one at a time, to be read back to her.

The man said them. He read them back to her twice because the first time she had it down wrong as well, and she said that she was sorry about that, and he said there was no need for that either.

And then he said, "You will ring me on Friday, then."

And she said that she would.

And he said, "You have not rung me on Friday for six months."

That was true of the six months and not of the last Friday, and she let it stand, and there was about two seconds on the line in which the two of them were both in the other person's kitchen, and then he said something about a place where he had to be at seven and put the phone down.

She did not ring him on the Friday of that week. She will ring him tomorrow at half past seven and he will be there and they will talk about nothing for twelve minutes or more.

What she did not do, on the Thursday, after she had got the number right, was put those ten digits back into her head and ring them to see whether he was at that end of it. She had thought about it. She had the phone in her hand and the ten digits in her head and she stood there and did not do it, because she worked out that if she rang it and he was out, she would have gone through the whole thing again, and because there was a version of the afternoon in which she rang it and got him and the two of them talked for a quarter of an hour about nothing, and she did not want that version, and she has not been able to explain to herself since why she did not want it.

The ten digits are still in her head and they are still there this evening. She has not written them on anything and she has not told anybody them, and she will be able to give them back to him tomorrow at half past seven without looking at anything at all.

**SHE WENT THROUGH TEN DIGITS IN HER HEAD AT TEN PAST SIX ON THIS EVENING AND GOT A STRANGER BECAUSE THE LAST THREE OF THEM ARE THE ONLY THREE SHE WAS NEVER SURE OF, AND SHE HAS NOW HAD THEM RIGHT AND HAS NOT PUT THEM BACK IN AGAIN, AND TOMORROW AT HALF PAST SEVEN SHE WILL GIVE THEM BACK TO HIM.**
