# Chapter 0308 — A Hundred and Ninety

Thursday the twenty-third of March 2017, from about half past five in the afternoon, a parade of shops in Tideglass.

**A MAN WHO SELLS RADIOS SAID A THING ABOUT A WATER BILL TO A WOMAN ON A PARADE AT ABOUT TEN PAST SIX AND SAID A FIGURE WITH IT, AND A MAN AT THE BUS STOP HAD A DIFFERENT FIGURE FOR THE SAME BILL OFF A DIFFERENT PERSON, AND THE TWO FIGURES WERE BOTH ABOUT ONE HOUSE AND NEITHER OF THEM WAS WRONG ON PURPOSE, AND NOBODY PUT THEM TOGETHER.**

The parade is about half a mile long end to end, the way you walk it, and the bus stop is at the bottom of it where the parade runs into Peverell Street, and the shelter there is a bus shelter with a roof and a side and a bench in it and a timetable in a plastic case with the top corner gone soft, and the number that stops there has been reading the same thing on that display since about the middle of January.

The man who sells radios had his own shutter half down behind him, which is how it stands most days, because the shop is open and the shutter is not for the winter.

The woman with a shopping bag was coming down the parade at about ten past six with the shop in front of her shut and the window lit, and the man who sells radios was standing outside his own half-down shutter with a cigarette he was not going to finish.

"I hear you've had a bill," he said. "In the second house of Marsh Lane."

"Who's been saying that."

"Somebody at the post office," said the man who sells radios, "and I'm saying it as well now, so that's two of us. It's over two hundred, and it's been over two hundred in two quarters running, and that's not a meter you've misread. That's a landlord with two supplies coming off one kitchen and one of them billing for a house the other one's already billed for."

"Is it?"

"Ask him which one of them he read," said the man who sells radios. "That's the question. Don't ask him for the money. Ask him which meter he read and watch what he does with his hands. And if he says he read both of them then he's read a meter that isn't there, because there's one on the outside of that house and the other one's in the cupboard under the stairs at the old place, which he hasn't got a key to and hasn't had since about November."

The woman with a shopping bag stood on that pavement.

"He's told me the meter was changed," she said.

"He's told you the meter was changed," said the man who sells radios, "which is the same as what you said, and it's the same as the water going through a tap twice a day, and it isn't a lie, it's just not the whole of it. Whatever's on the paper is what he's entitled to charge. The rest of it isn't on the paper and it isn't going to be."

The woman at the counter of the shop that shuts early was on the far side of that window with a cloth in her hand, and she heard the whole of that and said none of it and went on wiping the counter.

---

The man of about thirty had been at that shelter since about five with his bag between his feet and the display saying that the bus was due in a number that had not moved since he got there.

He was the sort of man who has one coat and reads the figures on the bottom of things, and he had worked out by about half past six that he was going to be standing there until long after dark, and he had decided to be the sort of man about it.

The woman with a shopping bag came down to the shelter at about ten past six with her bag in her hand and stood on the other side of him, and about two minutes afterwards he said, "Did I hear you say two hundred on the way down there?"

"You did."

"Two hundred what."

"Pounds. On the water. Over two hundred, in two quarters running, and it's the second house of Marsh Lane and I live in it," said the woman with a shopping bag, "and I've not said a figure to anybody else in this street and I shan't."

"You've said one to two of us now."

"You've said one to one of me now," said the woman with a shopping bag, "so that's even."

The man of about thirty laughed at that, and she did not, and then the two of them stood there.

"I'm not arguing with you," he said. "I'm asking you a straight question because I've had the same bill described at me in February with a different figure on it, and the figure I was given was a hundred and ninety, and the man that gave it to me was talking about your house, and he said it twice the same way, which is why I've kept it."

"Who's that?"

"A man I work with, and he's gone to work in a town about thirty miles off since the middle of March," said the man of about thirty. "And he wasn't talking to me about your bill. He was talking about a bill he'd heard of in that street, and he was in a doorway on a weekday in February, and he had a figure on him. A hundred and ninety. And I have not got a piece of paper on any of it and I couldn't show you anything if I wanted to."

The woman with a shopping bag stood and looked at the road.

"Then one of you is talking about a different house," she said, "or one of you is talking about a different quarter, or one of you is wrong, and I am not standing at a bus stop working out which. I've got the paper. You want to see the paper, you come and look at the paper, and I'm not carrying it about."

"I wasn't asking to see the paper."

"You were telling me you had a figure about my house from a man who's left the county."

"I was telling you I've had a figure," said the man of about thirty, "and I've had it for about five weeks, and I'm a man who'll go on saying what he was told. I'm not going to pretend I've not got it, because a figure is a figure and I only know the one I was given and I'd rather say that than make one up now to be even with you."

---

That number came down the parade at about ten to seven and stopped, and the man of about thirty got on it and put his bag on his knees, and the woman with a shopping bag watched it go and then went back up the parade on foot.

At about half past six the next evening the man who sells radios was outside his own shop with the shutter half down and the woman with a shopping bag was not there, and he said the thing about the two hundred pounds to a man who had come in for a battery and did not know her and was gone in four minutes.

On the Saturday the woman with a shopping bag went down that street past that shop and the man who sells radios was in and the shutter was half down, and he said nothing to her and she said nothing to him, and he does not know to this day that there is a man in this borough holding a hundred and ninety pounds about her kitchen.

**AND A BUS CAME DOWN A PARADE AT TEN TO SEVEN AND WENT ON WITH A FIGURE ON IT THAT WAS NOT THE ONE SAID ON THE PARADE, AND THE WOMAN WALKED BACK UP IT ON HER OWN FEET, AND THE FIGURES HAVE BEEN GOING ROUND THIS TOWN FOR FIVE WEEKS AND SIX DAYS WITHOUT EVER BEING IN THE SAME ROOM.**
