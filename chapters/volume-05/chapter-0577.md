# Chapter 0577 — That's No Trouble

The Tuesday of the seventh of July 2020, from about nine in the morning until about half past ten, in a small workshop off a road in this borough.

**A SENTENCE SHE HAD BEEN SAYING FOR YEARS WENT OUT OF HER MOUTH IN THERE ON THE FRIDAY BEFORE AND ON THE TUESDAY MORNING IT WENT OUT OF HIS, AND HE HAS BEEN IN THAT WORKSHOP EVERY DAY SINCE SHE STARTED COMING AND HAD NEVER ONCE USED IT, AND SHE WAS STANDING SIX FEET AWAY WHEN HE DID, AND HE SAID HE MIGHT HAVE SAID IT ANY TIME IN HIS LIFE, AND SHE CANNOT SHOW ONE PERSON IN THAT BUILDING THAT HE DID NOT.**

The workshop is two bays with a partition down the middle of it. There is a pillar drill, a long wooden top along the side with something bolted to the end of it, a trough, a small extractor that comes on when the door is open, and a ring on the wall in the corner with a kettle standing on it. It smells of grease and hot dust and has smelled like that for as long as anybody has been in it.

The woman in her early fifties has been in and out of that workshop since about 2009. She does not have a key and does not want one. She comes in when there is something to carry and she leaves when there is not.

The man in his mid forties has been in there six days a week since before she started coming in. He has about ten words a day, most of them about sizes.

The woman in her late forties has been coming in since the January, twice a week, and does the same work as the other one and is better at it.

On the Friday before, the woman in her late forties had been holding a shelf up while the man dealt with something behind it, and could not get her grip, and the woman in her early fifties put her hand on the other end and took the weight without being asked and without stopping what she was saying.

"That's no trouble," she said.

She says that. She has said it for as long as anybody in that building has been listening, to everybody, about fetching things, about holding things, about waiting, about a great many subjects.

She has not thought about it since the Friday. She was talking about the front of somebody's house.

---

On the Tuesday morning at about nine the woman in her late forties said she would go down to the counter and get a part, and the man in his mid forties said, without looking round from where he was:

"That's no trouble."

The woman in her early fifties was on the other side of the partition with her back to both of them, taking a piece off a bar of something.

She stopped.

It was not a shock. She wants that understood afterwards. It was closer to being handed something on a tray. He had been in there every day since she started coming in and he had said that to nobody. She has been here when the woman in her late forties left at noon and when she came back at one. She has stood at that top for longer than either of them has been able to say.

She stood where she was for about four seconds and then she carried on with it.

The woman in her late forties went and got the part and was gone about twenty minutes. Nothing else happened in there. The extractor came on and went off. The man in his mid forties took a piece off with a file and did not blow the dust off his hands, which he always does.

At about half past nine the woman in her early fifties put the bar down and said, "You never used to say that."

He said, "I might say it."

"That's what I mean."

"You can't tell me what I say," he said. "I might have been saying it before you ever came in here."

That is a fair thing for him to have said, and she has been over it since. She has gone back through the whole of it in her head and there is not one morning she could put her hand on. She cannot say that he took it off her. She also cannot say that he did not, and she knows perfectly well that those are the two halves of it, and that a person only has to hold one of them to be finished.

The woman in her late forties came back at about ten with the part and neither of the other two said a word to her about any of it, and she has not asked and is not going to, and she is the only person in that building who has heard the sentence twice and can do absolutely nothing with it.

At about half past ten the woman in her early fifties went out the back for some air and stood in the doorway with her arms folded.

She did not go and ask anybody else. There is nobody in that building she could ask without telling them all of it, and all of it is only about eight words.

**SHE HAS NOT FOUND OUT WHETHER SHE GAVE HIM THOSE WORDS OR HE HAS ALWAYS HAD THEM, AND SHE IS NOT GOING TO, AND THE ONLY THING SHE IS CERTAIN OF IS THAT SHE CANNOT PUT HER HAND ON ONE MORNING OF IT.**
