# Chapter 0797 — The Two Bars Under The Shutter

Saturday the fourth of March 2023, in this borough. The whole of that day in that lock-up is one roller shutter off its drum and cleaned along its track and back on again, and it takes two of them and a bar.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FORTIES HAS A ROLLER SHUTTER THAT JUDDERS AT THE HEAD BECAUSE ITS GUIDES HAVE BEEN PACKING WITH GRIT SINCE THE AUTUMN AND MEANS TO CLEAR THE GRIT OUT WITH A BRUSH AND A MACHINE, THE WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK PUTS TWO BARS UNDER THE BOTTOM RAIL AND STANDS ON THEM AND HAS THE CURTAIN LIFTED OUT OF BOTH GUIDES, AND THE DRUM AND THE WHOLE LENGTH OF BOTH GUIDES CLEANED, AND IT RUNS UP AND DOWN WITHOUT A SOUND.**

She is a woman in her late forties and has been the length of that building's life. The woman who books work comes up the ramp and in at the head of the bay in the middle of the morning, through that shutter, while it is up and doing what it does at the head of it.

---

That shutter has been sitting at the bottom of its own guides since the middle of January, shut, and it is the whole front of that building.

It goes up about twice a day and it comes down about twice a day and both of those are wrong. Going up it comes off the drum in the first foot and gives two hard knocks and then a noise like a saw going through a board for as long as it takes the curtain to get its back down to the head, and then it comes up straight. Coming down it comes down straight. It is only the top of it that is bad.

She has known what it is since November. Grit comes off the yard and off the road and off the tyres and it goes into a track and packs there, and the bottom rail rides up in that grit until it is lifting the curtain off the bottom of the guides and letting it touch itself on the way in. She means to run a brush and a machine along both guides from the floor and get it out.

She has a brush and a machine. She has not been able to get at the head of either guide since November, because the head of either guide is eight feet off the ground and inside the mouth of the housing.

The office door is on its padlock at the front. The fan heater is on the windowsill of the office and has been since October. There is a bucket under the housing at the head of the bay for the last of the grit off the floor, and it went into that bucket three weeks ago and has not been emptied since, and there is a week of sweepings standing in the bottom of it. A bar of square steel tube leans in the corner by the office door and there are three lengths of scaffold batten against the ramp wall. The van is nose to the back wall with its tray bedded on two brackets and a length of green conduit cut off square lying under the front of it, and neither of them looks at it.

---

She runs it up for the woman who books work and it does the thing it does at the head.

"There."

"I have heard it," the woman who books work says, coming up the ramp behind her. "That is not the bottom of it."

"It is the bottom of it. You can hear it at the bottom of it."

"That is the bottom of the curtain and that is the slats knocking together in the first foot. Listen to where the noise stops."

The woman who books work stands under it and listens to it go up and it goes on being bad for about two feet and then it stops.

---

"It stops there because that is where the drum starts," she says. "And everything under that point is straight and everything above that point is not, and you have been cleaning the wrong two thirds of it."

"The grit is all down the bottom."

"The grit is all down the bottom because the grit falls. That does not mean the grit is only at the bottom." She is looking up into the mouth of the housing. "Your bottom rail runs in that guide from the floor to eight foot. The head of that guide is where it is tightest, because it is the highest part of the bend and everything the curtain drags in gets pushed up into it. And your drum is the one thing on that shutter that has never had daylight on it."

"You cannot get at the drum."

"You cannot get at the drum and you cannot get at the head of either guide, and both of those are where it is." She turns round. "How long is that bar by the office door."

---

They get the shutter's weight off its drum and off both guides, and that takes two of them and a bar each.

The shutter goes down first, all the way, so that the drum stands empty in its brackets with nothing wound on it, and that takes a minute and a half and makes that judder going down.

Then the two of them go under the bottom rail of the curtain with the two bars.

The bottom rail is a bar of iron across the bottom of the slats and it runs in the channel of the guide, and there is one bar lying in the corner by the office door about eight feet of square steel tube and another one behind the shelving. They put them in under the bottom rail at each end, crossways, and stand on them. Standing on them lifts that bottom rail up out of the two guide channels and takes the whole weight of the curtain off the drum and off both guides at the same time, and while it hangs there the bottom of each guide is open to the floor and the drum in its brackets above it is free to be got at.

Then the woman who books work tells the woman of the lock-up to stand still on them, and she goes over the curb and gets a brush and a bar and a bucket.

---

She does the bottom of the two guides first, from the floor, while the other one stands on the bars.

At the bottom of the left-hand guide there was a hollow about the thickness of a thumb worn in the channel of the metal itself, with a roll of grit lying along it, and that hollow is the second half of the noise: the bottom rail rides a quarter of an inch high in it, and a rail riding high lets the slats above it swing sideways and touch each other. She gets the roll out with the end of the bar, and she sweeps the two feet of channel out from the front of the bend to the back of it, and she does the same on the other side, and the two of them are not the same because the shutter was hung a bit out of true and the right-hand one has been getting more of it since somebody reversed a lorry off that ramp in the autumn.

She puts what comes off that in the bucket, and she does not put it with the crescent of grit that is trodden into the floor where the rack stands, which was swept to in the autumn and which is going to stay exactly where it is.

Then the head of it, and this is why the two bars matter.

There is a length of scaffold tube leaning at the head of the bay with a rag wired round the end of it, and she puts it up into the mouth of the housing and works the rag round the drum with it. The drum is a steel barrel on a shaft and about four inches of it, and there is a band of grit packed right round it where the curtain has been lying on it and coming off it again every time that shutter has moved since the autumn. It comes off in rings and it is the only part of that shutter that has never seen daylight.

The noise is made in the mouth of the housing and there is very little of it in there at all. She cannot get her hand up there and she is not going to try for it. She goes right round that drum and right round the mouth of the housing with the rag on the end of the tube, working from the curb, and she does not touch the shaft — everything hanging off that shaft has the whole weight of that curtain through it — and she brings down about a hatful of grit that has been packed there since before the middle of January.

---

Then the bars come out and the curtain goes back down into the guides.

They go under the bottom rail at each end again and lever it down into the two guides and take the bars out from under it, and the curtain hangs in them exactly as it hung before, and the woman who books work stands clear of it, takes the chain in her hand, and goes.

It comes down without a sound. It goes all the way down and the bottom rail comes into the floor of the guides and stops and that is the end of it, and there was no noise in any part of that.

Then she takes the chain and puts it up to the head, and it comes up off the drum and it does not knock and it does not saw and it comes all the way into the housing and sits there.

---

"Nothing."

"Nothing at all," the woman who books work says. "It is not the drum and it is not the roller and it is not anything to do with the motor. It has been a dirty shutter."

"I knew it was grit."

"You knew it was grit and you knew it was up high, and there is no way to that head of that housing from the floor with a brush and a machine, and that is not a fault and it is only what it is." She is putting the bar back against the wall by the office door and there are two of them there now where there was one, because the other one came out of the shelving and it is not going back in there. "That shutter wants somebody standing on something for as long as it takes to get under it, and it is not a thing a person can be doing and standing on at the same time."

---

They go down the ramp and out through the gate, and the shutter is down behind them and there is nothing in it to be heard.

The woman of the lock-up stands at the foot of the ramp with her hand still on the chain and listens to that shutter sit at the top of its own housing without saying anything at all.

There are two bars by the office door where there was one. She did not have a spare and she has one now and she has never used it and she knows what it is for, and she knows that the next time the wind gets up that wall and the rain drives across the yard at it, that is a morning she will have to stand on one of those bars while somebody she has not got takes a brush to the head of that housing.
