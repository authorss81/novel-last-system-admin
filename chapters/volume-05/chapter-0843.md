# Chapter 0843 — Dust Round The Leg

Sunday the tenth of September 2023, round the grit rack at the lock-up from morning until the dust hangs, and a leg deep in dust is the whole of that Sunday.

**THERE IS A GRIT RACK AT THE LOCK UP WITH DUST DEEP ROUND ONE LEG AND SHE MEANS TO SWEEP ROUND IT WITHOUT EMPTYING IT, AND THE WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK IS BEHIND THE SHUTTER WITH THE SHUTTER DOWN AND IS NOT SEEN.**

That rack is the open frame by the back wall where grit is kept for paths, with four legs and boards between, full to the top with sharp grit.

Dust has drifted round the near leg all summer, off the yard and off the heap, lying in a soft ring half a hand deep, with grit mixed through it where the heap has spilled.

The leg stands in it without harm. Wood in dry dust does not rot, but dust piled round a leg keeps damp against it after rain, and damp kept there eats the foot of the leg through winters.

She does not empty the rack. A full rack wants a day and a crew to move, and sweeping round a leg wants only a brush and a pan, and the grit above stays where it is.

She starts with a stiff brush, a pan, and a bucket for the sweepings.

The ring round the leg she brushes back first from the wood, a stroke at a time, down to where the flags show. Dust rises in a soft cloud and hangs in the still air of the bay, pale in the light from the door.

Under the dust the flags are dark where damp sat longest, and the foot of the leg shows a tide mark half a finger up, where last winter reached it.

She does not touch that mark. A shallow fix clears the dust and leaves the wood as it found it, and paint or treatment is another job.

She works round all four sides of the leg, brush into the corners where the boards meet the wood, pan after to take the sweepings. The dust comes away with grit in it, sharp among the soft, and she tips each pan into the bucket without sifting.

The shutter stays down through it. Behind it the office is dark, and the woman who books work is in there at her desk with the shutter down over the front, heard once as the shutter rattles when a lorry goes past on the road.

The rattle is the shutter in its guides, loose in the wind of the lorry, and nothing through it except the sound of a chair and a drawer, which is a day going on behind a shutter and not a talk.

She thinks about the leg while she sweeps, how wood stands in dust year after year asking for nothing except to stay dry at the foot. The rack is full and useful and the leg is buried, and nobody sees the burial until a brush finds the tide mark of last winter. Seeing in time is the whole of upkeep at a lock-up.

Dust coats her sleeves to the elbow and tickles her nose, and she sneezes twice into her shoulder and goes on sweeping.

She sweeps the whole bay round the rack after the leg, so the leg does not stand in a clean ring with dust heaped round it. The brush lines show where she has been, fanning out from the leg to the door.

Heap is the end of sweeping. Dust swept wants somewhere to go, and the heap goes beside the leg on a sheet, for the path, where sharp and soft together do no harm.

She lays the sheet down by the wall and tips the bucket onto it in a neat pile, dust with grit through it, and folds the corners of the sheet over.

---

The brush goes on the rack, and the pan goes on top of it, and the bucket goes back empty.

She stands in the door of the bay when the dust hangs in the light and looks at what is there. A swept pile of dust beside the grit rack leg on a sheet with the corners folded, and above it the rack still full, grit to the top of the boards.
