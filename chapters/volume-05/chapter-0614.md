# Chapter 0614 — Three Drawings and a Tape

The Tuesday of the fifth of January 2021, from about nine in the morning until about seven in the evening, in this borough.

**SHE HAS THREE DRAWINGS OF A BATHROOM THAT HAS NOT BEEN BUILT AND HAS NOT BEEN COVERED, AND THE MAN WHO DREW ALL THREE IS A MAN IN HIS MID SIXTIES WHO TOOK HER MONEY FOR TWO OF THEM, AND TODAY HE HAS ASKED HER WHETHER SHE IS GOING ANYWHERE WITH IT AND SHE SAID SHE WOULD RING HIM, AND SHE WENT HOME AND DREW A FOURTH ONE HERSELF.**

She is a woman in her mid forties. She has lived in the same house for years and the house has a bathroom in it that came with the house and has not changed since, and she has been on about this since the summer before last, and there is nothing wrong with any of it that needs doing.

Here is what the three drawings are.

The first one is from about the summer before last and is in pencil and is on a sheet about the size of a tablecloth, and it is the whole of a bathroom with nothing left out. It has the door on the left and the window on the far wall and a bath across the back and a basin and a toilet and a cupboard over the cistern and it has everything drawn to scale and it has the light going the right way.

She paid for it.

The second one is from the summer and is in ballpoint on the back of the first one, and it is the same bathroom, and it has one thing different on it, and the thing is a wall moved.

The third one is from a few months back and is in ballpoint on a fresh sheet and it is the same bathroom again with a second thing different on it, and by that one the door is not on the left any more.

There is no fourth one in the folder until this evening.

---

The man who drew them is in his mid sixties and he draws them for money and he has a first floor flat above a shop on the parade that has his name on the door of it that she has never been inside, and he has drawn for about four hundred people.

He is very good. That is not a warm opinion. The thing he does is that he will sit in a room with somebody for an hour and a bit and then draw what is in the room with the things that are not in the room already picked, and the first one of these three is a drawing of a bathroom he has not seen the door of.

She can tell you what he is thinking about the whole time. You are looking at a drawing and you can tell what the man making it is thinking about the house. He is a good listener. He asked her about the light about nine times.

And he gave her the price of three at the start of the summer and she paid for two.

Here is what happened today.

She rang him at about ten in the morning because she had not rung him for some time and he had sent her a card on the Friday and she had not opened it until the Saturday and the card said that the price he gave her was good to the end of March, which was not true of anything, but he meant it kindly.

And he was pleased to hear from her. That is the thing that is difficult to write down.

He asked whether the fitter had been back and she said no.

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "I've not been in touch."

And he said, "I know."

And she said, "I've not been able to think about it."

And he said, "Right," and then he said, "Are you going anywhere with it at all?"

And that is the question, and she has never had it put to her before, and she did not have an answer.

Here is what she said, and she said it at about ten past ten on a Tuesday morning on the telephone and then spent the rest of the day.

She said, "I will ring you."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "I don't want to waste your time."

And he said, "I'm not worried about that."

And she said, "It's not that I don't like it."

And he said, "That's all right."

And she said, "I'd like you to know that I think it's the nicest thing anybody has drawn for me."

And he said, "Well. It's the nicest thing I've got in the folder."

She went out about half an hour later.

She has a car and she drives to work and she did not go to work, which she had not done for some time and had told nobody about except the one person she had to tell, and she told him on about the Friday.

She drove to a retail park on the far side of the borough and she parked it and she went in and she walked round the second-floor showrooms for a while.

There is a bathroom in about every one of them and they are all different and every one of them is nicer than her bathroom and every one of them is wrong for her bathroom in some way she could name at a glance.

She has got very fast at that. That is what two summers of drawings does. She walked round that retail park for a couple of hours and she turned down about nine things and the reasons she gave herself were all real and none of them was the reason.

The reason is that she does not want to walk into a shop and be a person who wants something. That is the whole of it.

She came back at about one and she did not go in and she sat in the car at the bottom of her own road with her hands on the wheel and worked out four things, and the four things are in the sequence she got at them, and this is the honest one and not the tidy one.

One: she has been sitting on this since the summer before last and nothing has happened in all that time and nothing was going to happen, and she had worked that out without knowing it.

Two: if she goes and looks at one of the nine things she will buy it before the month is out, and it will be a nice bathroom, and it will be wrong in a way she will be able to see by about the next weekend, and then she will be a person who spent three thousand pounds on a bathroom she does not like, which is a worse position than the one she is in now, because the money will have gone.

Three: if she rings him and says no, then the three drawings go in the folder and the folder goes in the cupboard and that is the end of it, and she would rather not do that.

And four: and this is the one she was not expecting. She would rather draw a fourth one than do either of the other two.

She has never drawn anything in her life. She has never drawn a plan and she cannot draw a plan and she could not draw a room to scale if somebody paid her.

What she has done is spend two summers looking at the same bathroom with somebody who knows how to draw, and she can hold the lot of it. She can hold the door and where the door wants to go and the wall that would have to come out and the pipe that is in the wrong place and the window and what the window does at about four in the afternoon in a room that faces that way.

She says she cannot draw it. She cannot describe it either. She tried to describe it to herself in the car and got about a third of the way and gave up on it.

She went in at about two and she got the folder out of the cupboard where it lives, which is a plastic wallet in the bottom of a cupboard in the front room with the holiday things in it.

The three drawings came out and she put them on the table and looked at them and she was looking at three drawings of the same room done by one man.

Then she got a sheet of paper.

Here is what she did and it took her until about half past four.

She did not draw a plan. She wants to say that again in her own head, because she is going to say it to nobody, and she wants it to be true while she says it.

She drew the room as you look at it when you walk in.

She did the far wall first, because that is the one with the window, and she got the window in the wrong place and knew it within about a second of having it in and did it again. She did not use a ruler for anything. She used the edge of a credit card for the tiles and then a different edge for the wall, and at one point she used a shoe.

She got the bath in roughly right and she got the window in about the right place, and she could not get the door at all, because the door is the thing she has wanted to move for two summers and it is the one thing she has not been able to hold in her head in the right order.

So she drew the door twice, once where the door is when you walk in, which is on the left, and once where the drawings have been trying to put it, which is not on the left, and the two of them cannot both be there, which means the fourth drawing has both doors.

She looked at that for a bit.

And then she got a pen and crossed the left-hand one out.

---

And here is where the afternoon stopped being ordinary.

She stood at the table with a pen in her hand and looked at the four pieces of paper and she understood what she has.

It is not a set of drawings. A set of drawings is something you take to somebody and somebody builds.

It is a way of not deciding, done properly, in more detail each time, with a new thing changed on each new one, so that there is always a fresh reason not to pick up the phone.

And she has been doing it since the summer before last with real skill, and she is good at it, and there is not one person in her life who knows she does it.

That last part is the part she went and sat down about.

She sat in the front room with the folder on her knees for a while.

She thought about her daughter, who is in her late teens, who said something about a bathroom once and meant it as a joke, and who has not said anything about it since.

She thought about the man in his mid sixties, who has drawn about four hundred of these and who is not going to be harmed by any of it, who will be pleased to hear from her, who will say *right* and put the phone down.

She thought about her husband, who is not her husband any more, and about how she told him the price and he did not say anything at all about it, and about how she has been carrying that silence as somebody else's opinion ever since.

Then she got the paper back.

She folded the first drawing along its fold so the door that was on the left went behind the one she had drawn, and she left the fourth one on the table on top of it.

And she got the tape measure out of the drawer where the tape measure lives, which is the drawer with the string and the spare keys and a spare bulb, and she measured the bathroom.

She has never measured it.

It is nine foot by six foot and the door is on the left coming in and the window is on the far wall and the low point of the floor is under the cistern where the stopcock is, which is the thing that would cost anybody money who did not know about it, and she did not know about it until mid-morning today, and she is going to have to say that to somebody.

Here is what is in this borough at about seven o'clock tonight.

There is a plastic wallet in the bottom of a cupboard in the front room with three drawings of a bathroom in it that has not been built and has not been covered, and the price on them was good to the end of the March and was never going to be.

There is a man in his mid sixties above a shop on the parade with a folder in a drawer of his own and a good drawing of somebody's bathroom in it, and he does not know that the woman has drawn a fourth one and he will never know.

And there is a woman in her mid forties at a table in her front room with a tape measure beside a drawing she cannot do to scale, and she has a figure in her head for that floor that nobody else in this borough has.

**SHE HAS THREE DRAWINGS OF A BATHROOM SHE HAS NEVER BUILT AND SHE HAS DRAWN A FOURTH ONE HERSELF IN PENCIL WITH TWO DOORS IN IT AND ONE OF THEM CROSSED OUT, AND SHE HAS MEASURED THE ROOM FOR THE FIRST TIME AND IT IS NINE FOOT BY SIX AND THE LOW POINT OF THE FLOOR IS UNDER THE CISTERN, AND SHE IS GOING TO HAVE TO SAY THAT TO SOMEBODY.**
