# Chapter 0477 — Half an Inch Apart

Friday the eighth of March 2019, from about ten past two in the afternoon until about four. Two people, and a frame that has been measured, in Tideglass.

**THE HINGE STILE OF THE GROUND-FLOOR FLAT ON THE UPPER SIDE OF THAT STREET HAS NINE SETS OF SAWN MARKS UP IT AT EXACT HALF-INCH STEPS AND TWO MORE UNDER THEM THAT ARE WIDER AND DEEPER, AND ON THE EIGHTH OF MARCH A MAN CAME TO PUT HIS HANDS ON THAT FRAME BECAUSE HE HAD A NEW ONE TO CUT, AND A WOMAN CAME OUT OF THE DOOR HE WAS STANDING IN AND STAYED, AND THEY TALKED FOR ABOUT TEN MINUTES.**

The building is four storeys and it is one of nine like it, and the ground floor of each of them opens straight out onto the pavement, and the frames on that side were all hung in one week in the summer of 1974 by a man who kept the keys for all nine of them and who has not been in this borough since about 1979.

He has come to measure one of them.

That is the whole of the job. He is hanging a frame in a shop at the other end of the town on Monday and the one he has been given is out by about a quarter of an inch, and the way to find out what a frame in that building is, without a rule and without arguing with anybody, is to go and put your hands on one of the originals, because nine houses in a row fitted in the same fortnight by the same man come out the same inside their frames to within a hair.

He has a small steel rule in his back pocket and he does not use it. He puts his hands on the rebate and his thumbs on the shoulders and he knows it, and he wrote nothing down for about a minute and a half, and then he took a stub of pencil out and wrote a figure on the back of a bit of paper in his pocket.

---

The marks are on the hinge stile, the side you do not open it from, at the height where nobody puts anything.

Each of them is a short saw cut across the width of the stile, about three inch long, about a sixteenth deep, and a second cut a quarter of an inch below it, and a pencil figure beside it in the same hand.

The nine of them start about two foot eight from the floor and they go up to about five foot six, and from the first of the nine to the last of the nine the step is the same every time, which is half an inch, and the figures beside them are all in the same hand and all end in the same two letters, and the pencil has not been touched since about 1974 because nobody has ever had a reason to do anything about a pencil.

The two lower ones are not any of that.

They are about a foot below the first of the nine and about five inch apart from each other. They are wider and deeper, they go across the stile at a slight angle, there is no figure beside either of them, and the wood round about them has gone furry where somebody sawed at it with a dull blade and without any care at all.


She came out at about half past two because she heard somebody talking.

That is the whole of the reason. She has been in that flat for nine years and she has not heard a voice in that doorway since the man who sold the place on it stopped coming in about 2011, and a person saying two or three words into a door frame is a thing a person gets up for.

She was in the doorway when he said it.

He said, "That is a hand's width out. Sorry."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "I have not got my own off it. It is all right, I will work it out."

And she said, "It is a big hole in it," meaning the doorway, because he was standing in it.

And then he said the thing.

What he said was, pointing and not touching, at the nine: "Who did those?"

And she said, "I do not know. They were here."

And he said, "They are in steps."

And she said, "Are they."

And he said, "Half an inch, every one, and they are off a rule, and the figures are all in one hand with the same two letters at the end of them, which is a man writing down where a child is, and he did them in one go, because you do not get nine marks a rule apart unless you have the rule lying on the frame and you are reading off it."

And she said, "What about those two?"

And he said, "Those two are not any of that," and stopped, and put the pencil back in his pocket.


He was right about the nine.

He was in that house in 1974, on the last two days of the fitting week, seventeen years old, with a saw and a pencil behind a man who used those marks so that any joiner who ever came into that street afterwards would know at a glance which frames were his and which were not.

He was never told what the marks were for. He did them. It took about two days across the nine houses. At the end of it there were marks and nothing else anywhere — no sheet, no list, no second line under anything.

He also knows something he has not said, and what he knows is that the two lower marks were made after his, and by somebody else, and in that same room, and over the top of his own, and on the side of them that faces the floor.

That one stays in his pocket as well, and what is keeping it there is about four inches of painted wood on the twelfth of April.

When the old frame comes out, the nine go out with it. The two go out with it. And the woman will not have been told there was anything in that frame to lose, and he is the man who came and measured it.

He is not going to be the reason a thing a boy made with a dull blade at about six comes off a piece of wood and goes on a lorry to a yard, and he is not going to say one word that would start that happening, and he is not going to say one word that is not true to keep himself out of it.

---

She is right about the two.

Her son is twenty-one and he is in another town and he was six when he made them, on a Sunday in the summer of 2009, with a hacksaw blade, because she had said something at the table about how much he had grown.

He cut them in about ten minutes, both of them, at the same place on the stile, and then he stood back and looked at them, and he did not cut them out.

He was not five. He was six, which is the entire distance between a boy cutting his own marks and a boy cutting through another man's, and it is why he went over the top of somebody else's without noticing, and she had not seen the other nine either, and she has been carrying that since 2009 without knowing she was carrying it.

He came out of the shop at ten past two and she came out of her flat at half past two and neither of them put one word about the other's nine or the other's two to the other one.

At about twenty past three she took her telephone out and made a picture of the two marks and put it away again. She has not looked at it since. She does not know what it is for and she is not going to show it to anybody.

At about four he put the figure in his pocket and went back to the shop and wrote it on the end of a piece of offcut and cut a length of frame to it, and he did not write the marks down.

**ON THE TWELFTH OF APRIL THAT FRAME CAME OUT OF THE DOORWAY AND WENT ON A LORRY AND CAME OFF IT AGAIN, AND A MAN AND A WOMAN STOOD ON A PAVEMENT ABOUT NINE YARD APART AND WATCHED A VAN GO OFF WITH A PIECE OF JOINERY IN IT, AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID WHAT THE OTHER ONE HAD TOLD THEM.**
