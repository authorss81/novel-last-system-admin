# Chapter 0787 — The Post And The Two Wedges

Friday the twentieth of January 2023, in this borough. From the first hour of it until the lane has gone dark he is in his own yard at the unit, and a gate post and the two turns of wire on it take all of it.

**A MAN IN HIS EARLY FORTIES HAS A GATE POST THAT LEANS IN BETWEEN TWO TURNS OF WIRE AND MEANS TO DIG IT OUT AND DRIVE IT BACK DOWN PLUMB AND PUT ANOTHER TURN OF WIRE ON IT, THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN DIGS IT OUT AND PACKS IT TO THE BOTTOM DRY AND PINS IT PLUMB WITH TWO HARDWOOD WEDGES CUT OFF FLUSH AND PUTS THE TWO TURNS BACK WHERE THEY WERE, AND THE POST DOES NOT MOVE AT ALL, AND BY THE SPRING IT IS GOING OVER AT THE HEIGHT OF HIS HIP AGAIN FOR WHAT HE DID TO IT AFTERWARDS.**

He has been in his early forties for some years and the unit is his, and the woman from two doors down comes in at the end of the afternoon while there is still light in the lane, and she does not knock, because that gate is standing open.

---

It is the one Friday in that month with any light in it, and the unit is cold from the floor up and the roller door is up to its bracket. The stool that folds in two is against the paint where it has been since the summer. The rack of boards is along the wall behind it with nothing off it, and in the corner by it a box has its lid on and nothing nailed on it. There is a roll of wire in the corner of the office with about a fathom left on it. The oak length is still on the bench where he put it in September, the shoulder of it out over the planed face. The store door is shut on the rail that went in in January, and there is a quarter of an inch along the head of it, and the store is not what this is about. The staple is not done on the frame and the latch is hooked back over the gate.

The post is the thing between those two gates.

He put both hands on it in November and it moved. He has put both hands on it since. It moves about the width of a thumbnail at the height of his hip, which is neither the black part at the bottom nor the sound part at the top but the grey part in between, and that grey part is the whole of what is wrong with it.

There are two turns of galvanised wire on that post. One is a hand's width under the top and one is a hand's width up off the ground, and they are tight, and they do nothing in the middle, because a wire at the top of a post and a wire at the bottom of it stop the post going up and stop it going down and stop it coming out.

His plan is three parts and it is the plan of a man who has been told the answer once.

Dig the post out. Get it down to the bottom of the hole, drive it back in plumb on the same line, and put a third turn of wire on it in between the other two, so that the middle is caught as well as the two ends.

---

She comes in at the end of the afternoon and walks straight up the yard, because the gate is standing open, and it stands open for the reason it has stood open since the spring, which is that it has to be lifted to be shut.

He is up against the post with a spade in his hand when she comes round the corner of the unit.

"You are going to put another turn of wire on it."

"I am going to put another turn of wire on it."

"You are going to put a third turn on it in the middle." She stops at the gate. "And what does the middle of that post do."

"It leans."

"It leans because the middle of it has got nothing round it. Have you ever put a wire round the middle of anything that was packed."

He does not answer that.

"Not once," she says. "And that is the whole of it, and you worked it out in November and you have not taken it any further."

---

So she digs it out.

And she has to break the collar of old concrete off the bottom of it first, because that collar is what the post is standing on and it goes about a foot up out of the ground. It was put there years before he came and it has been under the ground ever since. She knocks it off in pieces with a cold chisel and a lump hammer and puts the pieces in a heap by the wall, because they are good hardcore and because nothing wet is going into that hole this week.

Then she gets the post up on a lever with a bar and a block of wood under it. It comes out whole, and the hole under it is about a foot and a half deep and full of the inside of the ground that has been in and out of it for years.

Then she digs that hole out another foot. She goes down with a spade until the blade is going into hard stuff and cannot go any further, and that is the bottom of the old footing, and the spoil comes out and stands in a heap by the wall with the broken collar on top of it.

"Now the wire."

There is a coil of it left over in the corner and she does not use it. She puts the post down on the grass with its top end up and gets a cold chisel and a small hammer and goes at the two turns of wire where they cross the grey middle of the wood.

That wire is rusted into the post in about a dozen places. It has been there since before he came and it has gone into the grain and it does not come off in pieces, and she works it off an inch at a time with the corner of the chisel, ringing the head with the hammer to get the corner under the wire, and every inch of it leaves a bright line in the wood behind it.

"You are marking him up."

"I am getting it off him." She turns the post over and starts on the other side. "And when it is back on, it is going on over those lines."

---

Then she packs it, and it is dry from the top to the bottom.

The bottom of the hole goes in first, with the biggest stuff. There is broken stone in that yard in a heap by the wall and it is not rubble, it is lumps about the size of two fists, and she stands them in on end and hard against one another, so that there is nowhere at the bottom of that hole for anything to come up into. Then hardcore over that, in layers, and she rams every layer with the back of the bolster until the handle rings, and the broken collar goes in with it.

The sides are packed in layers as it goes up, not at the end. She stands the post in the hole, and takes the loose out so that there is a hand's width of space all the way about it. Then she packs that space in with the same stuff in layers and tamps every layer, going up as far as the old collar went, and when that is level she puts the last of the loose earth from the heap in over the top of it and tamps that as well, so that the ground comes up flush with the rest of that yard exactly as it did before she started.

---

Then she pins it.

She stands the post in on the same line as the gates and gets it near enough plumb with her eye, and then she stops, because a post that is near enough plumb with the eye has not been made plumb.

She has two hardwood wedges in her bag. They are about nine inches long, an inch and a half across the thick end, and they came out of a piece of oak that was a horse's feed box.

Before she drives anything she hangs a line. She drives a nail into the top of the post on the other side of the gate, which is sound and has been in that ground since before he came, and she hangs a bob off the end of it about a foot above the ground where both faces can be seen at once from the step of the office door. Then she takes the slack out of that line with a nail pulled out of the scrap heap until it hangs dead of its own weight, and does not touch it again.

Then she brings the post to the line and not the line to the post. She stands him in the hole and leans his post over until the face of it is dead against that string, and looks along it from the foot of it to the head of it and knows at once that it is out, because one end of it is in and the other end of it is out. She drives the first wedge in beside the post on the side that is out, one end first and up against the wood about two thirds of the way up. Then she knocks the post back the other way with the heel of her hand, reads the string again, and drives the second wedge in on the far side about a third of the way up. Then she goes back and forth on the two of them, alternating, a fraction of an inch at a time, and she reads the string between every pair of blows, and she does not stop until face and string are dead together where the post enters the ground and where it comes out of it, twice running.

Then she cuts the wedges off at the ground.

That is the part that matters and she does it with a bolster and two blows each, because a wedge standing up out of the ground beside a post is a thing that gets kicked and a thing that works loose. She pares the end of each of them flat with the chisel, packs the ground about them and tamps it.

And she does not put one turn of wire more on than there was.

She puts the same two back. One a hand's width under the top and one a hand's width up off the ground, and she twists them tight with the bar through the loops and chops the ends off, and they go on over the bright lines the cold chisel left, and that is where they go.

---

The post does not move at all.

He puts both hands on it at knee height and pushes and it is a wall. He puts both hands on it at the height of his hip where it used to give him the width of a thumbnail and pushes, and it is a wall there too, and he hangs his weight on the gate with the gate shut behind him and nothing gives anywhere.

"She has killed that post," he says to himself.

The staple on the frame is still not done and the latch is still hooked back over the gate and the gate is still open.

---

Inside a month he has stopped leaving the gate standing where the frost left it.

He has done it gradually. It starts with the cold mornings, when the frost takes the ground and the gate has to be lifted to come through, and then with the wet ones, because a gate left standing open in the wet is a gate with a hinge swinging in water. Then it stops being a thing he does and becomes a thing he cannot help doing, and by the middle of February he shuts that gate hard every single time he comes through it. It goes over onto the frame with a bang you can hear from the lane, and it does not go home, and he kicks it and leaves it.

And he has never once taken in that the gate is hung off the top of that post and that he stands on the wrong side of the handle.

---

And by the spring the post has begun to go over again at the height of his hip.

It does not go the way it went before. It does not give and come back. It leans, and stays leaning, the way a thing leans when there is nothing loose left in it anywhere to move, and the lean is neither at the bottom nor at the top.

The packing she put in that hole was put in dry and it is packed hard and there is no give in it anywhere from the top of it to the bottom, and the ground under it comes up every winter the way it always did, and what used to move in that post was the middle, where the middle had room. The middle has no room now. It is packed on both sides and it is rammed and it is wedged and it is wire, and so the whole of the load goes into the wood itself, and it goes into it at the one place where the wood is grey and has never dried.

He finds it in March with his own hands at hip height, the way he found it in November, and the post gives him the width of a thumbnail again on the first push and about twice that on the second, and it does not come back, and he stands in his own gate in the dark with his palm against it and knows exactly what he has done and cannot name it.
