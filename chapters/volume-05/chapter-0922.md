# Chapter 0922 — Through The Weep Hole

Wednesday the third of July 2024, at the foot of the yard wall at number nine before the heat settles on the bricks. Through a weep hole.

**SHE HAS A YARD WALL AT NUMBER NINE WITH A WEEP HOLE THROUGH ITS FOOT AND SHE MEANS TO HOOK THE LITTLE FINGER INTO THAT HOLE WITH THE HAND CUPPED BELOW AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING IS AT THE BACK GATE FROM THE LANE WITH A BASKET LID IN HER HAND.**

The yard wall went up with the house when the lane was cut, and the bricks above it have been repointed once with old grout gone hard while the foot has kept its seat.

It is a wall of red brick about seven feet high, with a weep hole left through the third course and a pebble at its lip where the wash lodged it.

She wants that hole, where the water leaves the earth behind the wall and the air gets in.

A bored foot argues beyond its brick face: daylight outside, bank thrust inside.

So the little finger hooks through the wall weep-hole.

The brick round it feels cool up the finger, and the hole walls catch where grit lines them.

The finger travels through the wall and meets damp earth at the back, dark and close, with small roots in it and a potsherd wedged where the diggers left it years past.

She bends the fingertip skyward, then earthward, into the bank, and the soil dips a quarter inch under the nail and firms again on withdrawal.

A buried stone bars the way. A field pebble set deep behind the wall with its face smooth from water and its back in the soil, standing where it was laid.

The hole in the end does not run empty. It runs through to earth banked at the back between, and the earth beds the wall against the push of the bank when the rains come.

Finger freed from the hole, damp thumbed away on brickwork, she takes her stand bricks-adjacent as lane heat brews.

Down the lane the shopping woman reaches the back gate, basket lid in hand, always keeping it outside.

Lid in hand at the gate, her gaze went wall-ward, lane-ward, wall-ward. By and by she flipped the lid and clasped it, then walked on, lid in grip, gate agape behind.

The bricks above that hole were laid in a week by the men who built the yard, with the courses level and the perpends flushed, and the weep went in last with a wood plug that rotted out within two summers.

Old grout gone hard bears its joints through rain past the point where new work fails, having dried out years since and turning to crumbs rather than giving.

---

Where the crumble has gone under the second course the joint runs open along the wall, and the bricks above have dropped a hair at the middle and risen a hair at the ends, which is why the draught gets through the hole.

A wall settled low like that casts rain off its coping during storms, and the cast water cuts a shallow scallop in the dirt at the brick foot.

Storm wash at the brick foot bends away at the weep mouth, where the open hole swallows the bank water.

So the open mouth guards its own seat by letting the wash through, and the bank behind settles instead of pushing.

The wall brick runs coarse where weather pocked the face, the hole walls run coarse where grit scored them, and the lip pebble runs coarse where field water rounded it.

Night damp quits coarse brick in drips, each drip stripping the face a little barer.

A weather-pocked face clears soonest after rain, while a soot-glazed one keeps moisture along its footing till noon.

South-facing and bank-shaded, the coarse wall face outdries the shade, baring the crown while north joints stay mossy.

North-joint moss stands tall where damp lingers on the brick and short where noon sun strikes.

Two summers of growth weave a moss blanket there, meshed so a thumbnail traces it but a finger lifts nothing whole.

A cushion torn in spring closes again from its rims inward, with the heart shutting last, and rim-first closing marks it as old growth.

Wall shade nurses the moss after the lane dust pales, and June marks the turn along the whole run.

Storm water off the coping centres on the weep bay and falls together on the mouth. The hole takes the middle of the fall through to the bank and the lip throws the rest clear, and the pebble stops what the lip throws.

---

Coping-shed wet sluices down the brick face past the weep, washing the courses while the foot stays boggy behind all winter.

That drenching has belonged to the wall since the gate's previous painting, its footing unshifted a finger's width.

The coping has compacted its bed by the width of a coin in the course of three winters. Yard-paint fleck, gate-post level when wet, now rests a hair low.

A single winter powders the paint away, yet the fleck surviving beneath the coping overlap proves the brick rim its guardian. Elsewhere the wall is unmarked; the lone fleck points to the coping coming straight down.

Banked earth behind lies dark, laced with pale rootlets that slackened through winter and wake before August.

Year round a woodlouse circuits the weep, earth-mouth grains rolling loose at a fingertip.

The tenant first declares itself in pale bloom on shaded brick; no bloom has filmed that foot since the wall was washed.

Gate rails marry stiles fair, hinge leaves marry the post fair out of the wet, and lane setts marry soil fair.

The latch keeper on the post mouths a frog-mouthed slot, iron lips that guide the bar home in darkness. The bar tip wears a chamfer bright on its underside from nightly seating, and the keeper floor dishes beneath it. A gate wheel under the free stile trails on the lane slabs, rubber-tyred, leaving a faint black ghost on wet days. The ghost fades by noon, re-inked each evening the gate swings, a diary of comings the rain keeps better than chalk. Swallows nest under the coping near the weep, mud cups that breathe with the broods inside, and the parent birds skim the lane for flies at dusk, scything the cool air the weep exhales off bank earth, tireless till the light fails, and the weep sings faintly after cloudbursts, a reed note the bricks hum, heard only with an ear to the weep.

Steps up to the back door rise three treads in bullnose brick, the nosings rounded by a century of feet. Flints knapped into the risers glitter where quartz veins catch noon, each flint set with its flat face out. Grit beds along the wall foot grade from pea to pinhead downhill, the grade marking every cloudburst in miniature deltas. The deltas fan across the path after storms and dry to pale fans the broom erases.

The coping bricks along the top ride in a row of headers, with the joints flushed and the frogs down, and the third from the gate rocks a hair where the joint opened. A coping loose like that lifts in hard weather and settles back crooked, while the tight ones beside it stay close through winter and through every rain after.

The brick stretchers run rough where kiln kisses pocked them, and the frogs run rough where thumbs pressed them, and the coping joints run rough where the trowel serrated them. Rough stretchers and rough frogs build the wall foot together, and limewash brushed on one header smooths a hand-sized patch within the rough, a pale square winters fade a little yearly. Wash patch shows where the brush rested, faint amid rough, thinning toward the weep.

She paces the wall endlong from the gate, brickwork shuttered above, weep hoarding its earth beneath. The bond runs Flemish-wise, headers with stretchers, the weep course keeping gauge.

A crown weathered rough parts morning rain in curtains, and every curtain rinses the brick cleaner, for loose meal runs off and close clay stands its ground.

The wall drains where the hole lets it, and the pebble stays at its lip, for the bank pushes and the mouth runs.
