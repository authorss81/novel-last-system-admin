# Chapter 0322 — An Upright Cleaner

Thursday the eighteenth of May 2017, from about ten in the morning, a parade in Tideglass, and a building by the water.

**A MAN WITH A VAN CARRIED AN UPRIGHT VACUUM CLEANER UP AN OUTSIDE STAIR INTO A BUILDING AND LEFT IT ON A LANDING AND WENT, AND A WOMAN USED IT ONCE ALONG TWENTY FEET OF CONCRETE AND PUT IT IN THE CORNER BY THE FIRE DOOR, AND SHE IS NOT THE WOMAN IT WAS TAKEN FOR AND HE NEVER FOUND OUT THAT IT HAD BEEN USED.**

It had been on the boards of that van since the second week of April.

He had emptied a house on the Cleeve road for a woman who is now living a long way off with her sister, and everything in that house had gone on the van over a day and a half except the vacuum cleaner, which was in a cupboard under the stairs with about half a bag of somebody's hair in the filter.

He had asked the woman from the letting agency about it in the yard, with the loader running, and she had said take it, because she was not going to take it and she was not going to put it on a skip and she was not going to have it in a house with nothing in it.

So it went on that van and it is a good machine, an upright, about four foot and a bit off the floor with the handle folded down, with a lead about fifteen foot long and a cracked cuff on the machine end of it, and it works, and he had run it once in the yard of that house on the Cleeve road to see whether it pulled.

It does not fit anything he carries. It has to go across the boards on its side and everything else has to go over the top of it, and there is nothing he needs that is coming in a van that he is going to make room for it for.

---

The woman who works in a shop came out of that parade at about ten past ten with a bag in each hand and stopped, because there was a van at the kerb with its back open and a man in it arranging something that was not arranged.

"That's the machine," he said. "I want to ask you about that building."

"What about it."

"Whether there's one person living in it on the first floor," said the man with the van. "That's all. I'm not going in and I'm not knocking on anybody's door with it. I've had it in there since April and I'd like it out of there."

The woman who works in a shop looked at the building by the water from where she was standing.

"There's one," she said. "End of the corridor on the first floor. And I'll tell you now that I'm not going up there with you and I'm not telling you anything about her, and she'll be the one that says whether she wants it, and not me."

"That's fair."

"You'll want the street door open, though," said the woman who works in a shop. "That stair's a landing stair and it's about two foot nine wide the whole way up it, and I'd not want a woman of about fifty-five doing that on her own with a thing the size of a perambulator. I'll open the door for you and I'll take an end of it up those stairs. I do want to be straight with you, though, that I'm doing it because that stair is tight and not for any other reason."

"I understand that."

They went up. The machine went up on its side in two goes, the first one up to the second tread and the second one the rest of the way, and the woman who works in a shop held the bottom end of it and the man with the van held the top, and neither of them said anything while they were doing it.

The woman of about fifty-five opened the street door with her coat already on.

"That's a hoover," she said.

"It is."

"I haven't had one of those since about nineteen eighty-eight."

"It's an upright. It's not new and it's not been done anything to. It works. I emptied a house with it in the cupboard in April and the woman said take it and I've had it in the van since." He was standing on the bottom step. "I want to say why I'm bringing it, and then you can say no."

"Go on."

"In the third week of January I was in the yard at the back of this building at about half past three on a weekday afternoon with a torch on a pipe, and I looked up, and there was a light on in a room at the front on the first floor in broad daylight. And I've been thinking about that light since January, and I've had this thing in a van since April. That's the whole of it. I'm not asking you for anything."

She looked at the machine and then at the woman who works in a shop and then at the man with the van.

She said it was a big thing to take up a stair for a hoover, and he said he was aware of that, and he said he had asked at the parade first and had been told there was one person on that floor, and he said that if she said no he would take it down and put it back in the van, and she said it was going up or it was going back down, and she said put it on the landing and not in the room, because her room is about nine foot by nine and she has got a bed and a chair and a table and there is nowhere in that room to stand a thing the size of a chair without being in the way of everything.

He put it against the wall on that landing with the handle up and the lead looped over the handle, and it stayed where it was, and he came back down the stairs, and the woman who works in a shop came down behind him, and he shut the street door, and the three of them were in the street, and the woman of about fifty-five went back up that stair and shut her own door.

Nobody said anything else and the man with the van went back to his van.

---

She ran it on the Sunday morning.

There was sand on that landing. There had been sand on it since some building work finished at the end of March and the wind had been bringing a bit of it in off the parade for five weeks, and it had got into the junction between the concrete and the skirtings and along the bottom of the fire door, and it had got into the gap where the concrete meets the metal threshold of the top flat's door, and a broom does not get into any of those places.

She took the brush off the front of the machine and put the tube on the cuff, which was cracked and had to be held at the crack, and she started it and it made a noise like somebody clearing their throat in another room, and it came up off the concrete with about half a bag of grit in the filter by the time she got to the fire door.

The lead is about fifteen foot and the landing is about twenty-six foot, so she did it in two goes and put the plug in the socket at the top for the second one.

The woman in the room next door along came out while that noise was going on, because that noise is not a noise anybody makes on that landing, and she stood against her own frame in the way she does and looked at the machine, and the woman of about fifty-five said that a man had brought it and that it was not hers and that she was not going to keep it in the room, and the woman in the room next door along said aye, well, and went back in.

Then she put it at the far end of that landing against the wall by the fire door on its side, because the wall there is not straight and it does not sit flat against it, and she put the lead on top of it so that a person going past could not catch it.

It is still there.

She has not used it again. The filter is full of sand off that landing and the cuff is cracked where the machine end of the lead goes in, and if she had wanted to use it again she would have had to tip the filter out into the yard and she did not do that, and the machine is in a corner by that fire door at the far end of the landing and everybody who comes up those stairs goes round it.

The man with the van was down that street two days after that, and the woman of about fifty-five was at the gate at about half past ten with her coat on, and he put a hand up and she put a hand up, and neither of them said one word, and he has not been back up that street since, and nobody in that borough has ever told him what happened to the machine or that anybody ran it, and the woman who works in a shop did not tell him, and the woman in the room next door along has not mentioned it to a living soul.

**AND A MACHINE WENT UP A LANDING STAIR IN A BUILDING BY THE WATER ON A THURSDAY IN MAY BECAUSE A MAN ASKED A WOMAN AT THE DOOR WHETHER SHE WANTED IT, AND SHE RAN IT ONCE AND LEANED IT OVER IN A CORNER THAT EVERYBODY GOES ROUND, AND HE HAS NOT BEEN UP THOSE STAIRS SINCE.**
