# Chapter 0398 — Stair Rods

Saturday the seventeenth of March 2018, from about ten in the morning until about half past four in the afternoon, a room at the end of a passage, in Tideglass.

**A WOMAN CAME INTO A ROOM AT THE END OF A PASSAGE ON THE AFTERNOON OF THE SEVENTEENTH OF MARCH AND ASKED A MAN IN HIS FORTIES FOR FOUR BRASS STAIR RODS HE HAD BEEN KEEPING SINCE 2015, AND HE PUT THEM INTO HER HAND INSIDE ABOUT TEN SECONDS AND SAID NOTHING BEFORE, DURING OR AFTER, AND SHE WROTE NOTHING DOWN, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS PUT ANYTHING BACK TO THE OTHER ONE SINCE.**

The passage is off a side street and it is about four foot wide and about thirty foot long, and it runs between the back of a row of shops and the side of a building, and it is where the bins stand and where a man parks when he is short of room, and there are six doors off it. Four of them are to workshops at the back of that row and two of them are to a building that has flats on two floors and a yard at the end of it.

Of the two doors at that end of the passage, that room is the first on the left. It is about twelve foot by ten and it has one high window with the bars painted in, and a bench against the far wall with a vice on it and a set of drawers under it, and a concrete floor that was poured about 1962 and has been painted over twice.

The man in his forties has had that room for four years. The woman who works in a shop's father had it before him and had it for nine years before that, and he had his own bench in this borough before it and could not keep the two at once, and that is the whole of why he is in here and not in his own front room, and there is nothing in writing anywhere and no money in it and he is on nothing.

There is a ledge in that room. It is about four foot long and ten inch deep and it is at about five foot off the floor, and it is not a shelf: it is what is left of a chimney breast that came out in about 1968, and the brick was left where the breast came down, and it is part of the building and it is not anybody's.

---

What is on that ledge is about two feet of it.

From the left: a jar of screws with the lid off and a spoon in it, a coil of about twenty foot of green wire, a folding rule about a foot long with both ends gone and the joints loose, and a carrier bag with the top of it rolled down, and in the bag four brass stair rods.

The stair rods are the old kind. Each one is about a foot nine and a quarter long and about three-eighth of an inch thick, with a turned end that drops into a socket cut in the tread, and the four of them were in a packet wrapped in brown paper when they were new, and they have been in that bag on that ledge since the twelfth of May 2015 and they have never been out of it.

They came out of a house off the parade in a bag with about nine other things.

He was taking up the hall floor of that house and the stair had to come up as well, because the treads were going at the nosing and the rods were under the carpet on the stairs, and you cannot lift a stair rod without taking up the tread it sits in, and taking up that tread means taking the rods out.

They came up with the boards. He put them in with the offcuts because there was nowhere to put them, and he carried them back in his van at the end of that week and he put the bag on that ledge and he has not moved it since.

There was no exchange with the woman of that house about them at all, and no question was asked of her and no answer was given.

That house has been empty since November. It is being done out in April. She has the keys of that house and the job of emptying it, and the men come on the second of April for the rest of it.

She has been in that room twice before the seventeenth of March.

She was in it on the twenty-fourth of February about a length of pipework for a flat above that building. The man in his forties was in it. She was shown the room and could see the bench and the vice and the ledge in about thirty seconds.

The rods were on that ledge then, in that bag, at the right-hand end, the same as they are now.

She did not ask for them that day. She looked at the ledge the way you look at a ledge and then she looked at the vice and the bench, and she did not think about them for three weeks.

She thought about them on the Thursday of the fifteenth, in a car park, for about a minute, and what she thought about was that the bag had been going grey with dust on the top of it and that it had been there long enough for that.

She got as far as working out that she could ask him for them, and as far as working out that she would not know what to say if he said they were not hers, and that is the whole of what that minute was.

On the seventeenth she came down that passage at about twenty past two with the man in his thirties, who has a job in that yard and had come out of it to open the door for her.

He was in that room for about nine minutes.

---

She did not come in with a bag and she did not write anything down.

She stood in that room and said, "Are those the rods off the stairs at that house."

That is the whole of what she said about it. She did not say which house, because there is one house in this borough whose hall floor he took up and whose stair rods are in a bag on a ledge in a room at that end of a passage.

The man in his forties was at the bench with a chisel and a block of wood and about a foot of sash cord in his hand, cutting a rebate for a new toe to a door he is fitting in a house in Bleam Street on Monday.

He put the chisel down.

He went to the ledge, took the bag off it, opened it, took the four rods out, and put them into her hand.

It took him about ten seconds from the time she finished the sentence to the time her hand closed on them, and there was no pause anywhere in it, and he did not look up at her while he was doing it.

He said nothing about where they had been, or how long he had had them, or what they were. He did not say that he could have said something to that woman in 2015 and did not. He did not apologise and he did not explain and he did not put it as a joke and he did not ask her whether she wanted the bag.

He said, "Right."

She said, "That's all I wanted."

Nothing else was said about it in that room.

The man in his thirties was on that landing with his bag over his shoulder. He kept his back to the room. Since then he has put none of it to either of the two of them and is not going to, because there was nothing in it for him and he was in the doorway for none of it.

She put the four rods in a carrier bag of her own and took them out to her car, and they are in that car now.

She has not rung him. She has not written to him. She has not told the woman whose house it was that the rods are back, because that woman is in a home and is not told anything about that house. She has not told the man who works nights, who was in that yard on the seventeenth and said nothing to anybody. She has not said anything about it at the counter to anybody.

He has said nothing to anybody. He has been in that room about thirty times since the seventeenth and the bag is still on that ledge with the jar and the coil and the rule in it, and it is lighter by about two pounds and a bit.

The rods have not been put back in the bag and the bag has not been put back against that wall and the man in his thirties has not been asked what he saw, and the asking is not going to happen from either side.

**SHE CAME DOWN THAT PASSAGE AT TWENTY PAST TWO WITH A MAN WHO HAD A JOB IN THAT YARD AND HE PUT FOUR BRASS RODS INTO HER HAND INSIDE ABOUT TEN SECONDS, AND THE MAN AT THE DOOR WITH HIS BAG DID NOT TURN ROUND.**
