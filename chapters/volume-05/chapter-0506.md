# Chapter 0506 — Nine Benches

The Wednesday of the seventeenth of July 2019, that morning only, and not for longer than an hour and a half. A garden with a path through it, and the whole length of that path, in Tideglass.

**A WOMAN HAS WALKED THE LENGTH OF THAT PATH EVERY SUNDAY MORNING SINCE 2007 AND PUT NINE BENCHES BACK WHERE THEY BELONG, AND A MAN WHO GOES IN THERE ON SUNDAYS HAS COUNTED HER ROUNDS FOR YEARS AND HAS NEVER SPOKEN TO HER, AND SHE WAS NOT THERE ON THE SEVENTH OF JULY NOR ON THE FOURTEENTH, AND ON THE SEVENTEENTH HE CAME AT HALF PAST EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND SHE WAS ALREADY THERE, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS SAID ONE WORD, AND HE HAS TOLD NOBODY THAT HE CHANGED THE HOUR FOR IT AND SHE HAS TOLD NOBODY THAT SHE CAME EARLY BECAUSE SHE HAD BEEN AWAY A WEEK.**

The garden is about two hundred yard long along a path and about thirty yard wide, and nobody owns it and nobody has to look after it, and there is a railing round three sides of it and a brick wall along the fourth that stands about four foot higher than the others and goes on past the end of the path.

There are nine benches in that garden and all nine of them stand on the path, and eight of them face the flower border and one of them faces the wall, and nobody has ever known why.

She is sixty-four or thereabouts and has been in this borough since 1979 and has been on that path since 2007.


Here is what the round is and nobody in this borough could tell you about it.

She goes in by the railings at the wall. She walks the length of the path and she looks at nine benches.

Seven of them are where they go.

One of them is on the grass, or turned round, or has been shoved along about a foot so that it is not opposite the border.

One of them is facing the wall.

And she puts them back. That is the whole of it, and she has never once done it in a hurry and she has never once done it with anything in her hands, and it takes her about twelve minutes.

On a Sunday in August last year a man who goes in there on Sundays counted it. He has counted it about thirty times since and he has a figure, and the figure is twelve minutes, and he has never once said that figure out loud to anybody until this July.

She has never known that there is a man in that garden who counts.


He is a man in his middle sixties who retired from the water and goes in there most Sunday mornings at about ten because he cannot sleep and because there is nowhere else to sit at ten on a Sunday.

He has been going in for about nine years.

What he has done is not watched her. That is not what it is and he has been careful about that for a long time. What he has done is arrived at ten and found the benches in the state they are in, and gone round them, and sat down, and gone home, and known that somebody had been there before him.

He worked out who in about the second year.

She comes in the second year. She has come in every Sunday since. He has never been in there on the same part of the round as her, because she is there and gone by twenty to ten and he is there about ten, and in seven years the two of them have been within thirty yard of each other perhaps two hundred times and have never had a sentence.


What happened in July is this.

She went away on the Monday the eighth and came back on the Saturday the thirteenth.

That is all there is to say about it and it is not the point. The point is what a Sunday morning is for, and she has had four hundred of them, and she went away for one week and came back, and on the Sunday the fourteenth she stayed in the house, and she has not managed a reason for that in three days.

Then on the morning of the Wednesday the seventeenth she got up at half past six, which she does not do.

She was in the garden at about half past eight and she had not meant to be there before nine.


And he was there before her.

He had not been in that garden on a Wednesday in seven years and he had not been in it before nine since about 2014, and he had got there at about ten past eight and had stood at the railings by the wall for four minutes without going in, and then had gone in and walked to the far end and come back and looked at the nine benches.

The seventh one was on the grass.

He stood looking at it for the best part of a minute.

Then he lifted it — it is a cast thing and it weighs more than a man expects — and carried it up onto the path, and set it down opposite the border, where the other seven are.

And then he did it, and he did it in four seconds and he has not stopped being able to explain why.

He put the eighth bench where it goes. He put the last one where it goes. And then he went back to the seventh and moved it two feet along, so that it was opposite the gap in the flower border rather than opposite the border itself, where all the other eight are.

It is a two-foot difference. A person who did not know would not see it in a year.

---

And then she came round the wall.

She came at about twenty to nine, and she came up that path at the pace she comes at, and she got to about the fifth bench before she had to stop.

She stood there for about three seconds.

Then she went on to the seventh one.

She stood in front of it for about four seconds, and she looked at it, and she looked along the line at the other eight, and then she looked back at the two feet.

Then she walked on to the eighth and on to the last one, and both of those were as they should be, and she came back to the seventh and picked it up and carried it two feet along and set it down opposite the border.

And she wiped the place on the paint where her hand had been with the side of her other hand, which is a thing she does.

And that is the whole of what happened in that garden on the morning of the seventeenth of July.


He was on the second bench at that point and he had been there for four minutes and he had a cup in his hand.

Here is what each of them did, and neither of the pair has any idea how the other one handled it.

He watched her do all of it out of the corner of his eye without turning his head, and his hands did not move, and his cup went about half an inch down and stopped.

And when she put the seventh one back he worked out — and this took him about half a second and it went through him like something cold — that she knew. That a person who has not touched a bench in seven years does not stop at the fifth one.

She knew, and she corrected it, and she did not look round, and she did not say anything, and she walked on.

And what he has been carrying since about twenty to nine on that Wednesday is that there is a woman in that garden who can tell, from where a bench stands, who moved it and when and how, and that she has let him go on being a man who comes at ten and looks at nine benches, and that she did that on purpose and in front of him and without a word.


And here is hers, and she has not said it to anybody, and she is not going to.

She has done that round since 2007 and nobody in this borough has ever known about it. She checked that in about 2011, in a way that you check a thing, by mentioning it once over a kitchen and watching the face of a woman she likes.

So for eight years she has been doing it as a thing that happens to a garden.

And on the seventeenth of July she found out that there is a man in that garden who has been counting.

Not watching. Counting, and knowing the time it takes, and never once being there while she is there, which is the part that got her.

What he has been doing is being reliably late. He comes at ten because she is gone by about twenty to ten, and he has been coming at ten for about seven years without knowing that he was doing it on purpose, and she worked that out on the Wednesday morning between the fifth bench and the seventh one, standing still, and then she picked the seventh one up.

And what she is carrying, and has been carrying since about twenty to nine on that Wednesday, is that she has been given a thing by a man who has never said one word, which is that somebody has been counting the whole of it.


And then the rest of it, on that morning, from about twenty past nine.

He got up off the second bench at about half past nine and went and stood at the seventh one and looked at it for about a minute, because that is what a man does.

She was at the far end by then, doing the last one.

He put his hand flat on the back of it. The paint is gone in one place about two hands wide and it has been gone for years and she has never once tried to do anything about that.

Then he went out by the railings and went home, and he did not go back, and he has not been back since.


**A WOMAN CARRIED A BENCH TWO FEET ALONG A PATH IN THAT GARDEN AT ABOUT TWENTY MINUTES TO NINE ON THE MORNING OF THE WEDNESDAY OF THE SEVENTEENTH OF JULY 2019 AND PUT IT DOWN WHERE THE OTHER EIGHT WERE, AND A MAN WHO HAD BEEN SITTING ON THE SECOND BENCH FOR FOUR MINUTES WITH A CUP IN HIS HAND DID NOT TURN HIS HEAD.**
