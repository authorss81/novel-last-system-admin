# Chapter 0716 — Two Bells On One Line

Friday the eighth of April 2022, in this borough, and not in this borough, because the house is off the ring road and he is in it from first light until dark. The whole of it is a floor lifted and a mobile he does not take out of his pocket.

**A MAN IN HIS LATE TWENTIES LIFTS A FLOOR IN A BACK ROOM OFF THE RING ROAD AND LETS HIS OWN MOBILE GO IN HIS POCKET BECAUSE THE WOMAN WHO BOOKED IT IS THE ONLY PERSON WHO CAN ASK HIM FOR A SECOND THING, AND FINDS THAT THE BELL IN THIS HOUSE IS UNDER THE STAIRS AND THE TELEPHONE IS IN THE HALL, AND TELLS AN EMPTY PASSAGE AND NOT HER.**

He is a man in his late twenties. He is at the house all day, in the back room and then the passage. The woman who booked it comes at the end of the day. He speaks to her at the door.

---

He starts with the boards because the boards are the whole job and everything else is carrying.

The back room is a box with a window that will not open and a door that has been taken off its hinges and is leaning outside in the passage. He starts at the far corner from the door because the far corner is where the boards always come up whole, and he slides a steel bar under the first one and lifts it a hand's breadth and the nails come up with it, bright at the ends, and he puts the nail in the hollow of his hand and goes on.

He has been doing this as long as he has had the shop. He does doors and kitchens and floors and a cupboard door is a quarter of an hour if it behaves itself. Four or five houses a week when it is like this.

His van is on the drive outside with the tailgate up and the boards are going into the back of it as he works, and the tailgate is the only thing on that van that shuts properly.

---

The mobile goes at about the middle of the morning and he knows before he looks at the pocket who it is.

He leaves it going.

It is not a thing he does to be rude. It is that the woman who booked this job is the only person this week who can put a second thing in front of him, and if he takes that call while he is standing on a floor with the boards up he will be on that call for the rest of the morning, and she will hear the boards coming up in the background, and by the middle of the afternoon there will be a second thing, and a second thing on a morning like this is another house and another morning and he will be here until dark over a floor he was promised the length of a cup of tea.

So he stands still with the bar under the board and lets it ring in his pocket, and it stops, and a good while later it goes again, and he lets that one go too, and he does not feel good about either of them and he does not feel bad about them either, which is the part of it he would not be able to explain to anybody if anybody ever asked him, which nobody is going to.

He puts the nail in the hollow of his hand and goes on lifting boards.

---

The bell goes in the passage while he is under the window.

It is not loud. It is the flat sound a bell out of a wall makes, one ring, and it comes from behind him and below, and he stops with his hand flat on a board and listens.

Then it goes again, and again, and it is three rings and a gap and then it starts, and it is coming from under the stairs.

He gets up off his knees and goes out into the passage with the bar in his hand and stands there.

There is a bell on the wall under the stairs at about the height of a person's shoulder, a small round one, screwed to the plaster, with a name on the back of the cover that he can half read because he is standing over it.

And the telephone is in the hall.

He goes and stands in the hall doorway and looks at it and then he comes back out to the bell and looks at it again, and then he goes down the passage to the front door and stands at the step with the door open on the bars, and he can hear the bell the same from out here, which it would be, because it is not far.

He has never been in a house where a telephone and a bell were two separate objects in two separate rooms. He has done sixty kitchens and a great many floors and he has wired nothing in his life and he would not know where to start.

What he does know is that one bell is not a fault. A house with one bell and one telephone is a house where you can hear it from the kitchen and you cannot hear it from the back bedroom, and anybody who ever wanted that got it that way on purpose.

And that is not what this is, because there is a second bell and he has not found it, and it is not under the stairs because that is the one he is looking at.

---

He looks for the second bell for the rest of the morning and finds it twice.

The first time is at half past the middle of the day, when he is standing on a stack of boards and he hears it start up, and he goes out and walks the passage end to end with his ear turned into each room as he goes, and it stops before he gets to the end.

The second time is later, and this time he gets it, and he gets it by stopping moving.

He is in the back room on his knees with his ear on the boards and the boards are up and the void under them is hollow, and the sound comes up out of the floor from under the far boards, not out of the wall.

And he lifts two more boards and there it is, a second bell, a small round one with a name on the back of the cover, screwed to a joist under the boards with two long screws, and it is wired with bell wire that runs under the floor and out through the wall by the skirting.

He puts his hand flat over it to stop it. He does not think about putting his hand over it. He just does it, the way you put a hand over a tap.

Then he takes his hand off, because it starts again almost at once, and he gets his thumb and forefinger on the wire where it comes out of the bell and keeps them there, and the bell goes on ringing very quietly with the wire pinched.

He kneels there on the boards with his hand pinched on a wire for as long as he can stand it and then he lets go and the bell stops and he does not know what he has done or what it means and he puts the board back over the hole and screws two nails in it and leaves.

---

He tells the empty passage late in the afternoon, standing at the front door with the boards stacked on the drive behind him.

"Bell under the stairs," he says. "Telephone in the hall. And there's one under the floor at the back, screwed to a joist."

The passage does not answer him.

"And I've not rung anybody," he says. "And you're not getting that off me for nothing, so don't ask."

He shuts the door and goes out to the van and puts his hand in his pocket and the mobile is off and he looks at the screen and it is off, and he puts it back without ringing anybody back, which he is not going to do either, because ringing her back is the same call with the same person in it and it will do the same thing to the afternoon.

---

She comes at about ten to five and he is in the passage with his coat on when she knocks.

"You've been on that floor a long time."

"Boards were nailed down by somebody who did not want them up," he says. "That's the whole of it. They were hidden."

"Right."

He looks at her and there is a place where he could put it. It is a small place and it would take about the length of one sentence and he has got it ready, and what stops him is not that he does not want the work, because he would want the work.

What stops him is that if he says the words bell and telephone in that passage then he has looked at a thing he is not asked about, in a house he is not building anything in, on a day he is not paid for, and then the next time he comes here he will be a man who has been in and had a look at the wiring, and he does not want to be that in a street off the ring road where he does not even know the name of, and he has nearly finished a job here and would like to be able to come back.

So he says: "Floor's up. Boards are stacked on the drive. There's about a dozen of them in the kitchen, you can have them or not."

"Anything else?"

"No."

"You said it was a quarter of an hour."

"I said it was a quarter of an hour if the boards came up," he says. "They didn't."

She pays him on the step and takes the card off him without reading it, because it is the same card every time, and he says the same thing he says every time, which is that if anything is not right he should ring the shop, and the number on the card is the shop's number and not his, which he has never mentioned and is never going to.

Then he is in the van and the doors are shut and the boards are on the drive in a stack with a strap over them and the house is shut and he is going.

---

At the roundabout he stops for the lights and there is nothing in front of him at all.

He puts his hand in his pocket and takes the mobile out and looks at it in his hand. There is nothing on it. He puts it back and waits for the lights.

The boards are stacked on that drive under a strap and she will have them or not have them, and either way they go back over the hole in the back room, and the bell is under the hole, and it is not his, and he has already put two nails in a board over it and he is not going to take them out.

There is a camera on the phone in his pocket. It would have taken about a second, and there is nowhere to put the picture that is not somebody else's drawer or his own, and a picture of a bell in a floor would be a picture of a floor he is not building anything in.

He said it out loud to a passage once, and the passage did nothing with it.

He goes through on the green, and the boards are on that drive, and the two nails are in.
