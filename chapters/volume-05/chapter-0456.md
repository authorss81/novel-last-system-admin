# Chapter 0456 — What the Boxes Told Him

The Sunday of the twenty-fifth of November 2018, from about ten past ten in the morning until about ten to one. The ground floor of one building and a doorway in it, in Tideglass.

**HE CAME ACROSS ONE THING ABOUT HER BY ACCIDENT IN THE MIDDLE OF A KITCHEN HE WAS NOT SUPOSED TO BE STANDING IN, HE HAS TOLD NOBODY, HE HAS NOT CHANGED ONE THING ABOUT WHAT HE DOES OR SAYS, AND SHE HAS NO IDEA HE KNOWS ANYTHING AT ALL.**

There is a man in his forties who trades out of a yard off a street on the other side of the water, and there is a woman in this borough he has built things for nine or ten times over about six years, and the two of them have a way of doing business that neither of them has ever examined.

He goes where he is sent. She is told the day. He does the job, she pays him, he is gone. She has never once offered him anything to drink and he has never once asked, and that is not coldness. It is a thing settled between them by about ten repetitions, and neither of them has ever been in a room with the other one in it for longer than the job takes.

---

On the Sunday of the twenty-fifth of November he was sent to a kitchen on the ground floor of a building off that parade, and the job was about nine feet of tiling where a splashback had been taken out and the wall behind it was bare block.

The woman who had asked for it was not in. She had left a key with a neighbour and the neighbour had gone up at ten and left that door on the latch, which is the whole of the security of half that street.

He had been in that building before, twice, both times for about two hours, both times to look at something and not to do anything.

The kitchen was small and it had not been cleared before he came.

That is the whole of it. There was a worktop with nothing on it, a cooker with four rings and one of them missing, and a floor with a scuff line across it where a table used to stand, and nothing on the walls at all. There was nothing in the sink. There was a mark on the door frame at about the height of a man's shoulder where something heavy has been carried through it often enough to take the paint off.

He did the nine feet. It took about three hours with a wet saw he had brought up on a trolley because of the stairs, and the wall behind was solid and true and the old adhesive came off clean, and there was nothing about that job that needed anything out of him except the work.

At about ten past ten he had the last strip out and was bedding the bottom edge when the door at the back of that kitchen, which is not a door to outside and is a door to a store, was swinging about four inch on the draught coming out of the front of the building.

He did not open it. He put the trowel down and went across and shut it, and shutting it took him two seconds.

What was on the other side of it was a store about six foot by four, and there was no light in it.

On the floor, against the far wall, under a stack of three others, there were about thirty boxes. They were the kind removals men bring, meant to be the bottom of a stack and not the top of one, and they had one firm's name printed on them in the second line and a set of numbers printed on the end of each, and the two nearest had their tape already cut and their flaps already up.

That told him she had already got them in. That is all it told him. Somebody who has taken about thirty boxes off a lorry and stacked them in the store of a flat, where there is nothing else stored and no room for anything else, is a person who is living in that flat and not moving out of it.

He shut the door. He finished the splashback. He cleaned the mortar off that floor with a bucket he had brought for the purpose. He went up and gave the neighbour the key back and said the wall was done and would want grout in the morning.

And here is the thing he could not put down afterwards, and it is not the boxes.

The two nearest boxes had their tape cut and their flaps up, which means the last person to have touched them was a person who unpacked them, and a person who unpacks thirty boxes into a store six foot by four does not do it in a morning.

The inside of the top one was not dusty, and there was a ring about four inch across in the dust on the box under it, and a ring like that is what a jar standing in the same spot for a fortnight leaves. There was no jar in that store, and there is nowhere in a store six foot by four to stand one.

He stood in that kitchen with a wet saw in one hand and worked out that somebody has been sitting down in that store to work, for about three weeks, and has carried the jar out again every time, and has not put one single piece of that furniture anywhere else in a flat whose ground floor has nothing on it.

That is all of it. It is a jar and about three weeks and an empty flat, and it is not a fact about anybody, and he is not going to make it into one.

He knows how that reads and he has been over that as well.

A woman in a flat with about thirty boxes in a store and nothing else on the ground floor, and a man who is in that flat some evenings when he is not there, is a thing a man can put a name to in about a second, and he could put a name to it in about a second, and that is exactly why he is not going back into that store with a torch.

What he has done instead is the other thing, which is the ordinary thing, which is to keep the same face.

He had been back the previous Wednesday, the twenty-first, because the grout had come up white under the edge near the window. He was in that kitchen about fifty minutes with the back door about four foot off the back of his left boot and it was shut the whole time, and she came in while he was cleaning up and said, "You got the boxes down, then," and he said, "No," at which point she said, "Right," and went out of the kitchen, and the draught shut that door all the way through the whole of it and neither of them looked at it.

---

And that was four days ago, and he has not told a single person in this borough, and he is not going to, and the not-telling is not loyalty and is not care and is not anything he could put in a sentence if anybody asked him.

What it is is that he has decided, on his own, standing up, with a wet saw in his hand, in about four seconds, that there is one room in that flat he is not going to be asked into, and that he has arranged that himself, and that arranging it himself is the only part of it that is his.

He has not changed one thing about what he does or says. He has not changed the days he goes. He has not changed what he says when he arrives. If she telephones him tomorrow about the grout he will say the same three sentences he said on the twenty-first and go on the twenty-eighth and the fifth, and there will be nothing in his face at all, and there will be nothing in his face at all on the day she finds out he was in that store, and she will not find out for a long time, and by then it will be a thing about a wet saw on a Sunday morning and not about a room.

**HE CLEANED UP AFTER HIMSELF AND CARRIED THE BUCKET AND THE SAW DOWN THE STEPS OF THAT BUILDING AT TEN TO SIX THAT SUNDAY, AND THE STORE DOOR WAS SHUT BEHIND HIM AND HE HAS NOT PUT A HAND ON IT SINCE.**
