# Chapter 0372 — A Stone on the Landing

Thursday the thirtieth of November 2017, from about half past ten in the morning until about half past nine in the evening, a stair in a block of flats, and a landing on it, in Tideglass.

**A PIECE OF AN OLD HEARTH ABOUT A FOOT AND A HALF ACROSS AND FOUR INCH THICK CAME UP THAT STAIR ON THE THURSDAY MORNING AND WAS PUT INTO THE HANDS OF A MAN IN HIS FORTIES WHO HAD ASKED FOR NOTHING AND WHO COULD NOT FIND OUT WHOSE IT WAS OR WHO IT WAS FOR, AND HE HAS KEPT IT IN THE HALL OF HIS OWN FLAT SINCE AND NOBODY HAS ASKED HIM FOR IT.**

The block is eight flats and it is a converted building with a stair in it that turns once on a half-landing, and the stair is about three foot six across with a hand on the wall and a rail on the open side, and the half-landing is about five foot by seven and there is a window on it that nobody has cleaned since the summer.

The man in his forties has the flat on the right on that half-landing. His front room is about twelve foot by eight and it opens off the landing through a door that stands open most of the day because he is out most of the day.

The woman who works in a shop has the flat on the left on the ground floor, and she comes in at about ten to six every evening and goes up past that half-landing four times a day, and she and the man in his forties have said good afternoon to each other about four hundred times since June and have never had a conversation.

---

Two men came up that stair before eleven that morning with a stone between them.

It came out of a hearth on the second floor, where a woman had wanted a boiler put in and the man who came had found a stone under the front edge of the old hearth that is not a slab and is not a piece of anything, and it is about a foot and a half by a foot and a half and about four inch thick and it weighs about a hundred and thirty pounds.

They had it up the first flight and round onto the half-landing shortly before the half hour, and then they could not get it round the turn onto the second flight, because the turn is a right angle and the stair is not wide enough to turn a stone of that shape in it with a handrail in the way.

The man in his forties was standing in his own doorway at that moment with his bag in his hand, about to go down to the shop off the parade, and he said, and this is the whole of what he said, "Hold that end for a minute."

He took the end of it that was nearest to him and had both hands on it and his back against the wall of the half-landing, and they got it round in about a minute and a half with the man in front going up backwards and the man behind watching the corners.

Then they got it up onto the top flight and set it down on the landing at the top in the space outside the first flat, and the man who had been at the front end took his hands off it, straightened up, and said that the man in that flat was not in and had not been in since the Tuesday, and that they would come back on the Monday and take it down again.

Then he said, "Get it inside of you," meaning put it off the passage, and went down the stair with the other man, and the stair is about three foot six across and the stone is a foot and a half, and a stone left in the passage of that stair is a stone that nobody can get past in either direction.

The man in his forties stood there with his hands still on it.

He carried it into his own hall and set it down against the wall opposite his own door and about three inch off the skirting.

It took him a step and a half to turn it. It took him a whole step and a half and he had to get his shoulder under the edge of it, and the edge of it is not dressed and has a crust on it about the thickness of a thumb where it has been in a hearth for a hundred years and nobody has ever dressed it or wanted to.

He has got a hall about five foot by nine and a stone in it that is a foot and a half square, and it stands against the wall that his front room door is on, so that a man coming in that door has to go round it, and it is a quarter of the width of that hall.

He could have put it in his front room. He did not, and he has been asked about nothing and has offered nothing.

He cannot give it back. There is nothing on that stone and nothing on the man who came up that stair to mark it, and the only thing he has got is a first floor in that block with a door on it, and that door has not been answered since the Tuesday, and the woman who wanted a boiler put in it is not in and has not been in since the Tuesday, and nobody in that block knows that he has got the thing.

If he carried it up there on the Monday and put it on that step, he would be standing on a landing with a stone he cannot explain, and somebody would have to take it, and the only somebody would be a woman who does not know him.

He has not done that and never will.

---

The woman who works in a shop came up that stair at about ten to six that evening with a bag of shopping over her arm and put her key in the door of her own flat and then stopped on the half-landing because there was a stone in the way of the only clear line from the top of the stair to her own front door.

It is a foot and a half square and it is against the wall and there is about two foot nine of hall left beside it, and anybody carrying a bag of shopping down that stair can get past it if they go sideways and everybody who has gone past it since the thirtieth of November has gone past it sideways.

She went past it sideways, and she put her bag down inside her own door, and she came back out at about half past eight and went up the stair on her way to a bin and went past it sideways again coming down, and she said good evening to the man in his forties's door and there was nothing in his hall light and he did not answer and was not there.

She has not mentioned it. She did not mention it on the Friday, and she did not mention it on the Sunday when her brother-in-law was in her front room for an hour and a half, and she has not mentioned it since, and it is still against that wall.

He has not mentioned it to anybody. He has had two men in that hall on the Saturday about a fireplace of their own and they came in at the door and went round it, and he has not said one word to either of them about what is against that wall.

**THE STONE IS AGAINST THE WALL OF THAT HALL ON THE HALF-LANDING ON THE THURSDAY OF THE FIRST WEEK OF DECEMBER AND ON THE FOLLOWING TUESDAY, AND IT HAS BEEN WHEELED ABOUT FOUR INCHES TO THE LEFT ONCE, BY NIGHT, BY SOMEBODY, AND NEITHER THE MAN WHO PUT IT THERE NOR THE WOMAN WHO WENT PAST IT HAS EVER MENTIONED THAT TO ANYBODY.**
