# Chapter 1028 — One Thing Before Anything Else

Undated in this draft.

**THE NEW ROW UNDER THE SHUT ONE IN THAT ROOM HAS BEEN RULED ACROSS AND NOTHING IS IN IT.**

The office at that yard is a single room with a window giving on the frontage and a shutter down over the top half of that window, which is not one of the shutters on the bays. A desk stands under the window with the chair behind it and a lamp on the desk that is not lit, and there is a book on the desk lying open at this week with the pages squared up against the edge of it. Above that week in that book there is one ruled column, and underneath the week that has shut there is a second row ruled right across the page, and it is ruled clean and it is empty and it has been empty since before the season went.

What that room is for in the winter is that the shutter comes down at one end of the day and the book stays where it is, and one woman comes in and goes out again without turning a page, and everything a person might want to put into that page waits until the weather turns. He has been in that room four times since the summer and every time he came out with a request and not with an offer, and this is the fifth goings and it is the first one in which he has come with nothing to ask for.

That column has been ruled on and filled in and ruled under and filled in every season since before the war, and every line in it came off somebody who had been asked to be there, and he has spent ten years making sure that he was never going to be one of the lines in it and he has managed it, and it is the single piece of arranging he has ever managed.

The distance from the door of that room to the near side of that desk is about four foot and it is a short walk, and he made it three times while she was getting her coat on behind the desk, and on the third occasion he stopped in the doorway and stayed there, which is the only thing a man can do with a floor like that between him and the thing he has come for.

"You have come up here to ask me for something."

"I have come up here to hand you something, and I have not done that in my life, so I am going to put it into words badly and you are to stop me at the place where I get it wrong."

She came round that desk and stood on the other side of it and did not sit down, and he put both hands flat along the front edge of the wood and left them there for the whole of it, because he could not get a sentence out about the only fault he had ever kept to himself with his hands anywhere on it.

---

"Four feet of the brick kerb round the weigh pit at the unit came out of its bed before the summer turned and I took it out myself with a bar and my thumb behind the blade, with nobody in that yard and nobody after me. I have kept it to myself since. I have had the bed of it opened out clean to the bottom along that whole run since as well, which is the right first job and the one that wants doing before a stone goes near it, and what it wants into it afterwards is dressed kerb and lime, and that yard has not had a penny for either of them for a long time. The cover that was lying over the corner of that pit is leaning against a wall inside their office where I stood it this morning, and nobody coming in off that frontage in the dark could put a boot through that hole and find anything under it but air. I am telling you because I have told nobody at all, and because I cannot go on carrying it, and because there is a book in this room."

He got to the end of it and she let him get to the end of it.

"A column takes work somebody asked for."

"You are asking me to enter a fault that you put in yourself, on your own ground, with your own bar, and nobody asked you for it."

"That is not why I am standing here and you know it is not that either."

"Then give me the reason."

"The reason is that the column takes work somebody asked for, and you are asking me for it, so the minute I enter it, it stops being a fault and becomes a request." She laid her palm on the cover of the open book without touching the page. "And if you had not asked me it would still be a fault, and I have no column for a fault, and there is nowhere at all in this borough that I can put one."

She gave him all of that in two sentences and took any softness out of both of them, and he stood on the near side of that desk and understood that she had not turned him down out of any feeling about him whatever, and that she was the only person in four streets who had just put a thing about himself into words that he had not thought of before.

---

"Then give me something else."

"Ask it."

"What does a woman have to do to get a job put down against her own house."

She looked at him for longer than she had looked at him at anything else that day.

"A house sends for it."

"Not a woman."

"A house. A woman is not a house. I can put a man's name in that column against work a house has asked for, and I cannot put a woman's name there against work a woman has agreed to out of her own pocket, and the difference between those two is not a small one, and it is the only thing I have got that I am certain of. When a woman in a house wants a man in her kitchen for a fortnight she comes into this room and asks me, and I ask her what it is for, and then the row goes in. I have never had that conversation. I have been behind this desk a long time and I have never had it once."

He had come up that yard to give a thing away and he was standing there being handed one instead, and he worked that out standing there with his hands in his coat. He turned his head towards the shutter as though the window were worth looking at, and what was behind it was a yard with nothing happening in it, and he did not tell her why he had asked and he was not going to.

"Does a house ever send for anything it does not want."

"Never. That is all of it, and no second half of it is coming to anybody."

He took his hands off the wood and put them into his coat and he did not take the four feet of brick anywhere with him, and going down that frontage with it still in him was the hardest of the three days he had had, because he had come out of that room with nothing done and nothing asked for and the only thing he had got was an answer to a question he had not meant to ask yet. And he left that row ruled across and empty exactly as he had found it, and the lamp on that desk went on being unlit with the shutter half down over the window above it, which is what that room looks like for a quarter of the winter.

She went round to the far side of that desk and put the pen away and left the book lying open at this week, and it will stay like that until somebody comes into that room who wants something put down in it.
