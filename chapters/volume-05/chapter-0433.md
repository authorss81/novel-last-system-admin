# Chapter 0433 — The Print

Wednesday the fifteenth of August 2018, and again on the Monday five days after, a first floor over that parade, in Tideglass.

**HE WENT INTO THE BACK ROOM AT HALF PAST ONE TO PRICE A RANGE HE WAS NEVER GOING TO SELL, AND HE CAME OUT WITH A PICTURE FRAME IN HIS HAND, AND HE DID NOT SAY WHY TO ANYBODY BECAUSE THERE WAS NOBODY THERE, AND ON THE MONDAY HE PUT IT BACK IN A DIFFERENT ROOM OF THAT BUILDING AND NEITHER MAN MENTIONED IT.**

There are two rooms over that shop and they have been store rooms since the shop was fitted out. They are reached from the yard at the back by an outside stair, and the wire along one side of it has been loose since about March and everybody uses it and nobody fixes it.

The woman who works in a shop has been offered about sixty pound for whatever is in them by a man in the town eight miles off who has taken on a shop in that town and wants a back room for himself.

She did not know whether that was a good offer or not, because among the things in those two rooms there is a range, and a range is the only thing in either of those two rooms that is worth a second van.



She opened them herself on the Monday and left them unlocked, and told him what she wanted, which was a figure and not a valuation: whether a cast iron range with two hot plates and an oven under it that is about eighty years old and has been out of use since before she took that shop is worth going back across eight miles for or whether it is not.

He said he would look at it and tell her, and he did not tell her when, and she did not ask.

He went up at about half past one on the Wednesday with a tape in his pocket and a small torch.

The back room is about twelve foot by nine — he measured it, twice, because it is the way his head comes up — and it has a shelf screwed along the left side at about five foot off the ground, a window on the outside wall, and a hundred and thirty-odd separate objects on that floor and on that shelf and in the corner.

The other room is smaller and is full of paper.

He went through all of it in about three quarters of an hour without hurrying, which is the only way he can do it. He wrote nothing down. He priced about six things in his head that he was fairly sure of, and one range that he was not, and he did not photograph anything and he did not measure a single object, and when he came to the range he crouched in front of it for about a quarter of an hour with the torch and worked out from the maker's name on the back plate that it came out of a Belfast foundry in about 1904 and that it is worth about four times what the whole of that room is worth to the man who offered.

He was standing up off his heels at about twenty to two with the torch in his hand when he saw the frame.

---

It is a wooden frame about ten inch by eight, dark, with a bevel and a slip of glass in the front of it, and the slip of glass is broken in three places and the putty round the back of it has come away at one corner and there is a loose splinter about a quarter of an inch long standing up out of the bottom of the frame.

It is standing on that shelf face in against the wall between a bag of door furniture and an enamel chamber pot with the lid off it.

There is a print inside it. It has gone brown at the edges and the top left corner of it has gone completely, so that about a fifth of the sky in the picture is not there any more.

In it there is a man in a cap standing outside a building, and a woman beside him with her hand on her apron, and neither of them is looking at the camera, and the man is looking slightly down at the woman and the woman is looking at nothing at all. Behind them is a low building with a doorway in it and a hand-lettered board above the doorway that cannot be read.

He looked at that frame for about a minute without touching it.

Then he picked it up with two hands and turned it round, and the back of it is a piece of hard board with four tacks in it and a smear of old glue, and he turned it round again and looked at the print for another half a minute, and then he went down the outside stair with it in his hand and put it on the front seat of his own van face up, which is the way a man puts a thing down when he is not thinking about the way he is putting it down, and which is the opposite of the way a man puts a saucer down.



Here is the reason and it is the only one and he has never said it to anybody.

It is not that it is worth anything. It is worth about four pound at a shop in the town eight miles off and it is worth nothing to him. It is not that the man in it is anybody he has ever met. He does not know whether the man in that cap is anybody at all, he has never seen that building, he has never heard a name to go with it, and he will never find out, and there is not one thing in that print that anybody in this borough could put beside any other thing.

It is that he has been in about two hundred rooms in this borough in twenty-two years that were being cleared, and he has watched people carry photographs out of them in their arms and in carrier bags and in the seats of vans, and put them in with the plaster and the nails and the broken glass, and he has never once said a word about that on any of those occasions and he is not going to start now.

And there was a room in March where a woman of about seventy carried a dozen of them down a stair one at a time and put them in a black bag in the road, and every one of those was a face, and he stood on that road for about four minutes and did nothing, and it has not been a thing he has thought about once in five months, and it came back up that outside stair on the fifteenth of August with no help from anybody.

He is not a rescuer of anything. There is nobody in this borough who has been rescued and there is nothing in this account that is ever going to be one. He took a picture frame out of a store room that is going to be emptied on Friday for about sixty pound, and he is going to have to do something about it eventually, and he has no idea what, and he has not asked himself the question once.

He shut his van and went and did two other things and then went home.

---

He was back in that building on the Monday the twentieth of August at about half past four in the afternoon for a job in the ground floor and the two rooms behind it that was going to take two days and that he had quoted that morning.

The shop was shut and the man in his thirties was in the ground floor of it with the old counter pulled out and the new one coming in in sections, and he was on his own with it at about half past four on a Monday because the woman who works in a shop had gone home at four.

The man in his forties stood on the front step of that shop at about half past four with a bag over his shoulder and did not open the front of it, because it is shut.

The man in his thirties came across the floor, unlatched it, pulled it open about two foot, and stepped back and went to the other end of the counter and picked his tape measure back up.

That is how he got back in afterwards. There was no question asked and there was no answer given and neither of them mentioned the yard or the outside stair or the two rooms over the shop or anything that has ever been on that shelf, and it is his belief, and it is wrong, that nobody has opened those two rooms since about 2011, and nobody will ever put him right.

They worked in that building for about two hours and said about nine sentences to each other, and every one of the nine was about the counter.

At about half past six he carried the last section in and put the man in his thirties's tape measure on the top of it and said the price of the two days and went.

On his way out he went up the outside stair, which is not what a man does on his way out of a shop, and put the frame on a shelf in the room at the back, standing on its edge, at about four foot off the ground, where there is a window and about three times the light there was on the other shelf, and where it will be the first thing anybody sees if anybody ever goes up there and looks, which nobody will, because nobody has yet gone back up those stairs with the man in the town eight miles off, and the sixty pound has not been taken, and nobody in that shop yet knows what that range is really worth.

The frame is on that shelf. It is face in against the wall, and the tacks at the back of it are showing, and the slip of glass in the front of it is still broken in three places and the splinter in the bottom of it is still standing up.

**HE CAME DOWN THAT OUTSIDE STAIR AND WENT OUT ALONG THAT PARADE, AND THE MAN IN THE THIRTIES WENT ON WITH A TAPE MEASURE, AND NOT A WORD OF IT HAS PASSED BETWEEN THEM.**
