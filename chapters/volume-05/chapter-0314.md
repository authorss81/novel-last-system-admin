# Chapter 0314 — The Seventeenth of April

Monday the seventeenth of April 2017, from about ten to eight in the morning, a parade of shops in Tideglass, a building by the water, and a hall off the parade.

**A BOOK WENT BACK ONTO A SHELF FOUR INCHES ALONG FROM WHERE IT HAD BEEN STANDING, AND AT HALF PAST SEVEN THAT NINE PEOPLE WERE SITTING IN A HALL WITH A METER RUNNING, AND FOUR SENTENCES HAD BEEN CIRCULATING AMONG THEM SINCE THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY, AND NOT ONE PERSON IN THAT ROOM ANSWERED ANYTHING.**

The rain came in off the estuary from about half past seven and it was the sort that does not come down hard, it just hangs in the air and soaks into the shoulders of a coat, and by about nine the boards along that parade had gone through to wet and stayed wet.

The shop that shuts early went up at about ten to eight, which is roughly the time it has been going up at since the middle of March, and the woman who runs that shop stood on the pavement in front of it with her hands down in her pockets while the roller came, and next door to that a radio shop keeps a separate shutter of its own, which on that Monday was down to about the height of a man's chest, with a man in behind it and a set on the counter and no sound on it.

The man who comes down with the shovel did not come down that parade until about two in the afternoon and he did no work at the bank end that morning, and the bin for gritting that stands at the top of Peverell Street was where it always is with a skin over what is left in the bottom of it and a hole punched clean through one side of the bin about the size of a closed hand.

A white van came up the low end of Peverell Street as far as the second house on the left and stopped there, and a man got out of it, and stood in the rain looking up the front of a building for about a minute with his hand on the roof of the cab, and then got back in and drove off back the way he had come, and nobody waved, and the only two people who saw any of it went inside.

At the end of Marsh Lane the van with no sign on it was parked outside a house with its back to the wall, nothing in the back of it, and a tarpaulin bundled down over the top rail, and on the count anybody keeps of it that is most days of any week, with the odd day it is not there at all.

---

In the building by the water, the woman of about fifty-five came down that ground-floor passage at about twenty to eight with an empty jug in each hand, because it was a Monday and there is no water anywhere in that building until the first person down has put the stop tap back on.

The strip fitting over her head lit when she put her hand on the switch by the meter, which is where that switch is and has been since the summer, and the jug filled in about four minutes and she carried the two of them up that outside stair one at a time and let herself in.

Somebody put the register back on a shelf in that building at about half past eight that morning.

It had been out of its place since a box of files came in off the corridor on the tenth of March and a binder was leaned against it by a woman on a stair who did not know what was under it. On the Wednesday of that week she took the binder off the shelf again and carried it about two feet along to the next bay, and the book went with it for an inch and stopped, and she pushed the two of them square again and went out.

The door on that bay does not shut over the catch and never has.

That register stands at nineteen entries. The last thing anyone has entered in it is a lamp belonging to a yard, its bulb entered under a heading kept for maintenance, and that bulb was reported out about November two years ago and no one has been near it since, and below that last writing the paper carries on by itself for the length of two fingers and then leaves about two inches of itself unused at the foot of a bound book about that thick.

The number of days between the day that lamp was entered and this Monday morning is eight hundred and seventy-three.

Nobody in that room looked at any of it. There were two people in that room and both of them were in a hurry, and on the corridor outside there is a door with a shelf behind it, and on that shelf there is a bag with a piece of string going round it twice, and the bag has not been moved an inch since before the winter.

---

By half past seven that Monday there were nine of them in that hall and the meter was on, and that gas has been paid for out of one woman's own pocket since the summer and not out of anything else.

The woman of about forty-two with the key had hung her coat over the back of a chair near the fire. The man in his sixties had taken the seat by the window with a cup of tea going cold in his hands. The man in his forties came in halfway through the evening with a bit of paper folded small in the pocket of his coat, and there is a number on it, and nobody has seen it. The woman of about fifty-five got there before he did. The man who works nights stood by the door in his coat. The woman who works in a shop had a chair turned so that her back was to the glass. The young woman of about twenty was at the far end of the table with both hands round a cup and is at a college four days a week until the end of April. The man from the parade was in before half past and had not taken his coat off.

The grate in that hall is full of old ash and nobody has lit a fire in it since somebody decided not to, and the folding table down the middle of the room has a two-foot length of scaffold board screwed across its legs at the one joint that has opened, and that board came off a skip somebody put out on Cleeve Street in the summer, and it is what is holding that table up.

Four of them, and there have been four of them lying on that table since the twelfth of January, and the four on it now are the four that were on it then, and nobody in this borough has ever found a way of laying any one of those four alongside any of the other three, and nobody at that table has ever managed to put into words what those four amount to between them, beyond the fact that there are four of them and that they are lying on a table in this room.

"There's still four," said the woman who works in a shop, and turned her chair round. "Nobody's put a fifth one on and nobody's taken one of the four off, and nobody's said where any of them came from, and the one about a street is still about the same street it was about in January."

"That's all of it," said the man in his forties, without looking up off the boards.

"It is all of it."

The man from the parade had both hands round his cup and said out loud, to the table and to nobody, that one day somebody is going to lay a hand on the one that counts, and that it will not be a man off any council because there is no council in this town and has not been one for years, and that it will not be the men who did the things either, because those men are gone, and that it will be a piece of paper that somebody puts away in a folder somewhere and forgets about. He said that he does not much mind what it turns out to be and that all he wants is to be in the room when it does, and he drank his tea after it.

The woman who works in a shop said that a man had stood at a shelter at the top of Marsh Lane in the wind in February saying the first of the four, and that a woman doing a count at a door had told him she had heard it before him, and that since then nobody has produced a fifth of anything, and that nobody at that table has produced one either.

"Which would be the thing," said the man in his forties.

"That would be the thing," said the woman who works in a shop.

The man in his sixties said that there was something going on up at the bank with the rain in that lamp, and nobody at that table asked him anything about the lamp.

That gas ran out somewhere around ten past ten, and the padlock went on the case after that, and they put the chairs up, and the woman of about fifty-five was one of the last two out of the door, and the light in that porch was burning when the door opened and cut out about a quarter of an hour afterwards, when the lamp at the corner outside did the thing it does.

**AND THE GAS IN THAT PORCH RAN OUT AT TEN PAST TEN AND A PADLOCK WENT ON THE CASE AFTER THAT, AND A SHELF DOOR WENT BACK AND DID NOT CATCH, AND THE SHUTTER CAME DOWN TO THE HEIGHT OF A MAN'S CHEST ON A SHOP ON THAT PARADE, AND THE RAIN WENT ON ALL DAY.**
