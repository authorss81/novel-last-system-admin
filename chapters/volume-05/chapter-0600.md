# Chapter 0600 — The Office in the Next Borough

The Thursday of the twenty-second of October 2020, from about half past eight in the morning until about half past four in the afternoon, in a town about thirteen miles from this borough.

**HE WENT THERE ON THE TUESDAY BEFORE LAST WITH A PIECE OF PAPER HIS MOTHER-IN-LAW GAVE HIM, AND HE WENT AGAIN ON THE THURSDAY, AND HE IS THERE AGAIN TODAY, AND ON ALL THREE VISITS HE HAS SPENT THE WHOLE OF THE WAITING ROOM, AND THE WHOLE OF IT HAS BEEN ABOUT SOMETHING ELSE.**

He is a man in his mid thirties. The woman who sent him is a woman in her late fifties and she is his mother-in-law and she lives with them.

The thing the paper is about is a sum of about two hundred pounds that she thinks she is owed for something in the March.

---

Here is the piece of paper.

She wrote it out for him on the Monday a week ago on the back of a bill, and she wrote it in pencil, and she underlined the address at the top, and she had been to a place with a name on it in the August and had been told to write.

The three lines are: a name, an address in this town, and the words *ask for Wendy*, with a question mark after it, because she was not sure.

He asked her on that Monday what the two hundred pounds was for.

She said, "The bench."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "They took it off me when they did the porch."

It was a good bench. She said that much too, and he said it would be in the paper somewhere, and she said it would not be because they hadn't got it, and he said, "Right," and that was the whole conversation. It was the fourth time he had been told about the bench and he knew as much about it now as he had on the first.

Here is what happened on the Tuesday before last.

The building is a low one on a road of retail places with a car park behind it, and the office is on the ground floor at the back of it, past a corridor with a row of chairs down one side.

There are four people ahead of him and there are about nine behind. A man comes out of the door every twenty minutes or so and says a name, and the people who have been called stand up and go in and come out with different expressions and some of them stop in the corridor and use their phone.

He was there that Tuesday from about ten to about ten past one.

At about twenty past ten a woman in her late fifties came out of the door having been in there for about fifty minutes and she sat down again in the fourth chair, and he was next to her, and he did not say anything for about ten minutes. She had a handbag on her knees with the strap over the flap, and she kept putting her thumb under the flap and taking it out again.

Then she said, "Did they tell you to come back?"

And he said, "They haven't called me."

And she said, "You'll not get called. They don't call people, they just go out and if you're not out you go back in."

He said nothing to that. He got his phone out to look at the time and put it back in his pocket without unlocking it.

And she said, "I came in the August for a different thing and I'm here for the different thing. That's three times. I've got a piece of paper with the first thing on it and I've not asked about the first thing."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "They've changed how it works. You go in and you tell them the thing, and then they go out, and then you go in again and you tell them the second thing, and if you say the second thing first they get the first thing wrong. I watched a woman in here lose it over that."

He said, "Right," and then, "How long did you say to wait?"

And she said, "About two hours. It's about two hours."

And they said nothing else for a while, and he read the piece of paper four more times, and the *ask for Wendy* with the question mark after it, and he could hear the door going every twenty minutes.

Here is what happened last Thursday.

He got there at about ten again on the Thursday and there were two ahead of him that day, and the same man was at the door, and he said a name at about a quarter past ten and a woman went in.

He was not out of the place again until about one.

The room has a desk and two chairs and a window with the blind down, and a woman behind the desk, and she had the piece of the March on her screen when he came in, and it was not his piece of paper.

He gave her the paper. She read it. She said, "There's no Wendy here."

He said, "There's no Wendy there. That's what she wrote."

And she said, "Have you got anything with the date on?"

And he said, "The March. She said the March."

And she said, "Right, well, that's not going to be on it. We don't keep the March."

He put the paper back on his knee. She turned the screen a few inches out of his line of sight while she typed, which he took for the answer.

And she said, "Have you got the old porch?"

And he said, "I've not been in the porch."

And she said, "No, I mean the old porch. The one you had before. Because they'd have taken it off the old porch and the old porch would have it on."

And he said, "Right," and she said, "You'd want to ring the people who had the old porch."

And he said, "I'd have to find out who they were first."

And she said, "We're not the ones who take things off. We're the ones who give them back. That's the second office, that's the one over the road, but they're shut at the weekend."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "Are you going to say you're from her?"

And he said, "She said to say I'm from her."

And she said, "Well if you say that they'll not help you, you'll have to go back to the start. Just say you're after the porch."

He said he would say he was after the porch. She said that was all right, and then he said, "The bench?"

And she said, "What bench?"

---

Here is what happened today, and it is the third time and it is the same.

He got there at half past eight because he thought it would be quieter. There are two people ahead of him and a young woman on a bench at the far end with a laptop and two children. The man at the door said a name at about ten past ten and a young man went in.

He has been here since half past eight. It is a quarter past two now.

The woman in her late fifties from the Tuesday is in the second chair. He said good morning to her and she said good morning and said nothing else, and they have both been there over an hour, and there is a different man at the door today, and he is going in less often.

Here is the whole of what he has learned in two hours this morning, and he has not had a turn yet.

A man came in at about half past twelve and was in there for about an hour and came out and said, to nobody, "Same as last time," and sat down. A woman was in there for about half an hour and came out and stood by the door for a while and then said, "There's nobody here you can speak to," to a man beside her, and he said, "There's a woman in there," and she said, "She reads a screen." And they both stood there.

He has read the piece of paper about nine times. He has written *the people who had the old porch* on the back of it in his own handwriting this morning, under her pencil, which he was not going to do and did.

And the door has not gone for his name.

Here is the thing that is worth putting down, and it is still the only one in the building at half past two on a Thursday: he is not angry, and he is not even especially tired, and he has not once thought about the bench. He is thinking about a bench on a porch in another borough that was taken off in the March, and about the fact that he has now been to this room three times for it and has spent the best part of three mornings in a corridor, and about a man who came out just before midday and said *same as last time* in a voice that is the voice of a man who has come here more times than he can count.

The second office over the road is shut at the weekend, so he will go on the Tuesday, and he will not say he is from her.

**HE HAS BEEN THREE TIMES AND HAS SPENT THE BEST PART OF THREE MORNINGS IN A CORRIDOR AND HAS NOT BEEN TOLD THE NAME OF ANYBODY WHO CAN HELP HIM, AND THE ONLY THING HE HAS LEARNED IS HOW TO ASK, AND HE WILL NOT BE ABLE TO USE IT, AND ON THE TUESDAY HE WILL GO TO THE BUILDING OPPOSITE.**
