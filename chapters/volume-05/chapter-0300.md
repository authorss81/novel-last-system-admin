# Chapter 0300 — Two Things

Sunday the nineteenth of February 2017, from about ten past ten in the morning, a yard at the back of a shop in Tideglass.

**A WOMAN WENT INTO A YARD BEHIND A SHOP LOOKING FOR A BARREL FOR A BACK DOOR AND CAME OUT OF THAT YARD WITH TWO THINGS INSTEAD OF ONE, AND SHE PUT ONE OF THEM IN THE SHED AND SAID NOTHING TO ANYBODY, AND FOUR DAYS LATER A MAN CAME INTO THAT YARD FOR SOMETHING ELSE.**

The shop she works in opens at one on a Sunday and she had come in at a quarter to ten with her coat on because the back door of that shop has had a barrel in it since the summer that turns over about a third of the way round, and anybody can get that door open from the inside with a coin and from the outside without one.

She had a locksmith's card in her pocket with a figure on it and a number on it and she had been carrying that card in her pocket since the second week of November.

The idea she had in her head on that Sunday morning was that a shop like that has a tin of oddments in a shed behind it, and that if the last man in before her had been anything at all he would have put a barrel in a tin.

Behind that shop there is a yard about fifteen foot by twenty, with the bins going out through a roller door that is up, and milk crates that stack along a wall in the rain, and a wooden shed with a door on it, and a bench inside the shed and nothing else of any account in it.

She went out into the yard at ten past ten in a coat with no buttons done up.

---

The shed door was shut and it was not locked, because nothing in that yard is locked and the woman who runs that shop has never once had a key to it and has never once been asked for one.

Inside the shed there was a bench against the back wall, a shelf over the bench about a foot deep, an old milk crate with a dead torch in it, and about three inches of leaf on the floor.

There was no tin.

There was a wooden crate, about two foot by three, with a division down the middle of it and a rope handle at each end, and it was standing against the outside of the shed wall under a sheet of dpm that had come off it in the wind and was folded over the alley fence at the back of the yard.

She did not see that from the doorway. She saw it when she went round the corner of the shed to put a milk crate down and stood on it.

She went back in for a bit of a knife to cut the dpm tape, and when she came back out she had the lid off the chest before she had decided to have the lid off.

There was about four inches of assorted hinges in it. T-braces and parliament hinges and a strap hinge and about nine loose screws in a tobacco tin, and the whole lot of it was galvanised and had gone a very slightly dull white the way galvanised steel does in a wet winter.

She shut the lid and stood there with her hands on it.

Then she went back into the shed, and picked up the old milk crate, and turned it out on the floor, and put the dead torch back in it and put it back on the bench, and then she went over the bench inside that shed and had a proper look for a tin along the shelf and under the bench and behind the bench, and there was no tin, and there never had been one, and the man she thought of had been to that shop in November and had done a thing over the door and had not left a barrel and had not been asked for one.

She carried the chest into the shed and put it on the floor against the left-hand wall under the shelf, with the hinge end towards the door, and pushed the milk crate in in front of it, and shut the shed door and pulled it to behind her, and went in and had her tea.

---

The barrel was on the shelf above the desk in the office, which is a different place and a different sort of place, and it was the right barrel, and it was the size that fits that door, and there was a bit of pencil on the shelf beside it saying *back door* in a hand not hers.

She did that door herself at about half past twelve with a screwdriver and about ten minutes, and the door now takes a key from both sides, and the locksmith's card went into the inside pocket of that coat and has not been out since.

There is one other person who works in that shop on a weekday and he was not in on the Sunday, and there was nobody else in that building to say it to, and the chest stayed in the shed.

On the Thursday of that week the man in his thirties went into that yard at about ten past ten in the morning with about nine feet of capping in his hand and went round the back because the man in that shop was taking a delivery and the side gate was open.

He wanted a length of batten about six foot long, and he knew there was batten in the back of that shed, because he had put some there himself the last week of November when he had done the over-door and taken the old one off, and it had gone in the rain and he had thrown a sheet of dpm over it.

He went round the corner of the shed.

There was no batten in that yard, and no dpm over anything, and no crate against the outside of that wall, and the dpm was over the fence at the back of the yard with the tape cut off it, and the leaf on the floor of the shed had been swept to one side with the toe of somebody's boot.

He stood in that yard for a minute with a length of capping in his hand and then went out to the front of the shop and asked the man in that shop whether he had ever had any batten at the back of there, and the man in that shop said he had not had anything at the back of there in his life, and the man in his thirties said right, and put the capping in the van, and came back in a week with his own batten on his own time.

**AND THE CHEST OF HINGES IS STILL ON THE FLOOR OF THAT SHED UNDER THE SHELF WITH THE MILK CRATE IN FRONT OF IT, AND HE HAS NEVER ASKED ANYBODY IN THAT SHOP ABOUT ANYTHING THAT WAS EVER PUT IN THAT SHED, AND SHE HAS NOT SAID, AND THE FOOTFALL ON THE BOARDS OF THE SHED DOOR HAS NOT BEEN LOOKED AT BY EITHER OF THEM.**
