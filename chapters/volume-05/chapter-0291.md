# Chapter 0291 — A Wheelbarrow

Saturday the twenty-first of January 2017, from about twenty past nine in the morning, a yard at the back of a shop that shuts early, in Tideglass.

**HE HAD PUT SOMETHING IN THAT YARD IN THE SUMMER AND A WOMAN HAD GONE IN NOVEMBER AND A MAN HAD MOVED IN, AND THE SAME THING CAME BACK UP THE YARD ON A SATURDAY MORNING AND NOBODY IN IT KNEW IT, INCLUDING HIM.**

The yard at the back of that shop is about twenty foot by thirty and it is where the rubbish goes and where the pallets stack and where the man in his thirties has tipped more things over the last three years than anybody in that street could put a figure on. There is a roller door at the front of it off the side passage and a wooden gate at the back and a shed along the left-hand wall that is a shed in the sense that it has a roof on it.

A woman had been living in the flat over that shop since the spring before last and had gone in November, and her furniture had gone out on two lorries in the second week of November with a man on the Cleeve road moving it, and she had not left a forwarding anything, and the landlord — a company in a town sixty miles off that does not come — had let the flat to a man who moved in on the sixth of January with two cases and a bicycle.

The man who had moved in was in his twenties and worked somewhere with a van and was out all week, and on the Saturday mornings he was in the yard in a coat without a coat on.

The man in his thirties came into that yard at about twenty past nine on the Saturday with a length of batten over his shoulder, because the shop needed a batten and he had a batten, and he went round the back because the roller door was shut and the gate was open.

There was a wheelbarrow standing against the wall by the shed with its handles up on the wall.

It was a builder's barrow, one wheel, galvanised, with a flat tyre on it and a wooden handle worn pale and shiny on the top where a thousand hands had gone, and the tray had a dent in the front left corner that had been pushed in and beaten out more than once.

The man in his thirties put the batten down on the concrete.

He stood in that yard and looked at that barrow for a good deal longer than he would have stood looking at anything.

He went over it. He put his thumb on the dent in the corner and pushed it and it moved, which told him it had been beaten out and not left, and he turned the barrow over on its side and looked at the underside of the handles where the maker's name is stamped in raised letters, and he could not read it, and he put his thumbnail in the stamping and got most of it.

He put the barrow down on its legs and stood up and looked at it again and said, out loud, to nobody, "That'll be a Landis," and then stopped, because he had not said the next part of it out loud, and the next part of it was that he had never in his life had a Landis, and there was only one builder's yard he had ever fetched anything off at that sold a Landis, and it shut in about 2011.

He stood there a while longer with his hand on the rim of the wheel.

---

The woman who runs that shop came out through the kitchen at about a quarter to ten with her tea and stopped in the doorway.

"That's been there since I came back in September," she said. "I asked him about it in November and he said it wasn't his. I said whose is it, and he said whoever it belongs to will come for it one day, and I said I'd had that off a house that burnt down at the back of the Cleeve road, and he said aye."

"He'd be right about that," said the man in his thirties.

"Would he."

"He'd be right if he'd had a barrow and lost track of it," said the man in his thirties. "And that'd be an honest thing for it to be, and I'd not want it to be the other one."

"What's the other one."

"Nothing," said the man in his thirties. "It's a barrow."

The man who had moved in came in through the gate at about ten past ten with a bag of shopping and stood in the middle of that yard in his coat without a coat on, and the three of them had a conversation about a barrow over about six minutes, and none of the three of them established anything.

The woman who ran the shop said the barrow was in the way of the oil tank and had been in the way of the oil tank since September and that the man on the insurance wanted to know what was behind the door because of the oil tank. The man who had moved in said the tray had a dent in the corner and that the dent was the same one he had beaten out in the summer in a yard about four miles off, and that if it was the same one then somebody else had had it off him first, and that he was not going to be the one to say. The man in his thirties said he had never had a barrow off anybody and had never seen that one in his life.

That was the last of it, and it was said in the time it takes to put a kettle on, and the man in his thirties picked the batten up off the concrete and went in through the kitchen door, and he shut that door behind him.

The man who had moved in put the barrow out in the yard for another week, because it was in the way of the tank and because he had not wanted it in the flat, and then on the Saturday after that he came in with it at about ten past four and put it in the back room of that shop with the shelves of stock in, and turned it on its side against the wall behind the door, and the wall it went against is a wall that is about four foot off the floor where the ceiling slopes, and the barrow went under the slope and out of the light.

He shut that door and put a crate of bulbs on the floor in front of it so he would not catch it with a sack of something when he came in with a load.

Nobody in that shop has opened that back room and looked behind that crate since the last week of January, and nothing in that shop has a list on it, and there is no part of that building anybody would ever have cause to look behind a crate of light bulbs in.

**AND THE LANDLORD, WHICH IS A COMPANY IN A TOWN SIXTY MILES OFF THAT DOES NOT COME, SENT A LETTER ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF JANUARY ABOUT THE OIL TANK AND SAID NOTHING WHATEVER ABOUT ANYTHING BEHIND A DOOR, AND THE BARROW IS STILL ON ITS SIDE UNDER THAT WALL.**
