# Chapter 0483 — The Top Corner of That Ground

Saturday the sixth of April 2019, and the days either side of it. A row of twelve and the ground behind it, in Tideglass.

**THE FIRST HOUSE IN THAT ROW LET AN ACRE AND A BIT OF GROUND BEHIND THE TWELVE IN MARCH, AND A WOMAN WHO HAD BEEN AT ITS DOOR THREE TIMES SINCE JANUARY HEARD ABOUT IT ON THE SIXTH OF APRIL, AND FOUR MILES OFF A MAN PAID FOR TWO MORE HIVES AND PUT THEM IN THE BACK OF HIS OWN VAN, AND NOT ONE WORD OF EITHER HALF OF THAT REACHED THE OTHER.**

There is a row of twelve in that part of the borough that runs in a curve, and behind it there is ground, and the ground goes back about a hundred and ten yard and is higher at the far end than it is at the row.

It belongs to the woman in the first house.

She has had it since about 2004 and it came with the house and she has done nothing with it in the fifteen years since except mow a strip about the width of a car along the front of it, twice a year, with a small machine that will not start the second time in any given summer.

The way in is off a public path at the top end. There is a hole in the wall about three foot wide where a gate used to be and the wall was never mended, and everybody in that part of the borough has walked through that hole at some point since 2004 and nobody has any arrangement about it.

He is in his fifties and he is at number seven and he keeps bees.

Not many, and not for the money, and he has never sold a jar to anybody in this borough that he knows of. He had two hives and he has one, because the second one died out in the first week of February and he has not replaced it.

He wanted the top corner of that ground since about 2015.

Not the whole acre. The top corner, about thirty square yard, the highest part, the part where nothing has ever been and where nothing could be seen from the road. He asked the woman in the first house three times.

In the summer of 2015. Again in 2017. And in the third week of October last year, which is the last time.

The answer was no each time and the no was not unkind. The first time she said there were bees already in that row and that was that. The second time she said her own daughter had been allergic as a child and she did not want to think about it. The third time she said she was going to let the whole thing and would not be having anybody's anything on it, and that was in October.

And in the last week of November she found him in the front of his own house and told him the man from the town had signed, and she said it kindly, and she said she was sorry, which she was, and she said she hoped he would get something else.

He said, "Right." And shut his own front door, and did not ask her again, and has not been to her door since.

He has not told anybody that he stopped asking. He has not told the woman at number three, who lives six feet of brick wall from him and whose kitchen he has been in about four times a summer since 2017, and he has not told his own sister, and there is nobody else in that row he would tell.

What he has done is nothing about it at all, which is the part that is not like him and which is the whole of what happened.

---

The woman at number three is in her fifties and has been in that row since about 2016.

She is the one who saw it. In the last week of July last year he came down the row with two long boxes in his hands off the back of his own van and put them down on his own front step, and she was on her own step at the time with a cup, and she said, "You moving in?"

And he said, "No. They're bees."

And she said, "You have bees at number seven."

And he said, "I have bees at home. Those are the two I can't have at home."

And she said, "Right."

And then he said the thing, and it is the reason she is standing at that door in January, and in nine months neither of them has said one further word about that strip of ground.

He said, "There's no point in anybody asking, mind. She's said no to me three times. If you ever hear anybody in this row saying they're going to ask her — no. Don't."

And she said, "I wasn't going to ask her anything."

And he said, "Right," and carried the boxes in.

She has been over that sentence every day since and what she has got to is that he did not say *don't ask for me*. He said *don't ask*, and she heard the first one, because she has spent her whole life hearing what people mean, and the difference between those two things is about four months wide and she has been standing in it since January.

She went in the second week of January.

She told him she was going and he looked at her for about two seconds and said, "Don't."

And she said, "I know."

And she said, "I have not said I was going to ask for you. I said I was going to ask her whether that ground had ever been let and who to. That is all it is. You can do what you like with the answer."

And he said, "Right."

And she went.

What she asked for was not the acre. She made that clear on the doorstep, twice, in the first fortnight. She asked for the top corner, about thirty square yard, for him, and she said his number out loud on that step, and she said she had known him since 2016 and that was all of her interest and that she was not doing it for herself and that she would not be doing anything about it in this row.

And the woman in the first house said no.

The second time, in February, the woman said no again and then said the thing that has put the woman at number three where she is now.

She said, "I've had nobody ask me about that ground in about twenty years."

And she said, "Not the allotment people, nobody, nobody at all."

And she said, "Whoever it was, they stopped a long time before you came."

That is the whole of what was said on that doorstep. It was true, and there was nothing in it that was not true, and the woman at number three came away from it and stood in her own front room for about ten minutes and then sat down.

The third time, in the third week of March, the same answer.

And then in the second week of April the woman in the first house put her head out of her own front door as the woman at number three was going past with her shopping and said, without any other conversation, "It went in March. The whole thing. To a man from the town who wants it for the tip and the parking."

And the woman at number three said, "Right."

And the woman in the first house said, "I'm sorry. I did say no."

And the woman at number three said, "You did," and went on up the row with her shopping.

What the woman at number three has not done since the second week of January is tell the man at number seven any of it.

She has not told him she went three times. She has not told him what the woman said about nobody ever asking. She has not told him she was told *don't* and went anyway, and she has not told him about the sale.

What she has told herself about that is that she did it because he said *don't* and then said *right*, and that those two together are a man who has given up and is being polite about it, and that a man who has given up and is being polite about it cannot be told that somebody went to a door three times on his account, because there is no version of being told that does not cost him something he has not got.

And underneath that, which she is not going to look at, is that she does not know whether she did it for him or because she was told not to.

She has been getting that wrong on and off since the second week of January. There is a version of her in which she went because a man said *don't*, and that is the version she has been telling, and it is the version that lets her go on living at number three without having to look at the fact that she went to a door about a strip of ground that is not hers for a man who is not hers either.

And the second week of April was not that. The second week of April was a man coming out of his own front door at about nine o'clock in the morning with his coat on, and the woman at number three was standing in her own front room, and she waited until she heard his own door shut.

---

At about half past ten on the sixth of April he was in a shop four miles off.

What he bought was two hives, a smoker, a veil, and a pair of gloves, and he paid for all of it, and the man behind the counter said he was the second person in that shop that week who had bought bees, and he said, "Right," and carried it out himself in two goes.

He had a long one in the van and the two hives went in sideways with the smoker and the veil round them, and he got home at about ten past twelve and put them at the back of his own house in about nine foot of ground between his own wall and the next one.

He had one hive there before. Now he has three.

He has read enough to know that three is too many for nine foot. He also knows that the woman at number three has a back window looking at about four foot of that ground, and that she will be the one to notice first, and that he does not care, and he has not got as far as why he does not care.

The corner he wanted was thirty square yard at the top of a slope where nothing was and where he would not have had to explain himself to anybody at all, and what he has done instead is buy three hives for nine foot of ground behind a terrace in a row where he has lived since 1988.

He put them down at about ten past twelve and stood looking at them and thought, and did not say to anybody, that he had stopped asking in November and that nobody had told him to.

**A MAN STOOD IN FOUR FOOT AND A HALF BEHIND HIS OWN HOUSE ON THE SIXTH OF APRIL 2019 AND LOOKED AT THREE HIVES HE COULD NOT AFFORD TO KEEP AND AT A WOMAN'S BACK WINDOW TWELVE YARD AWAY, AND HE CAME IN OUT OF THE SUN AT ABOUT A QUARTER PAST TWELVE, AND A WOMAN IN A HOUSE IN THE SAME ROW STOOD IN HER OWN FRONT ROOM AND HEARD HIM AND DID NOT GO OUT.**
