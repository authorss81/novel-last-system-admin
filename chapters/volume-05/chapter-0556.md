# Chapter 0556 — Four Floors Down

The Thursday of the second of April 2020, and the thirty days before it, in one block of flats a short way off a street of this borough.

**SHE HEARD IT EVERY NIGHT FROM THE WEEK SHE CAME IN 2011 AND HAS NEVER SAID ONE WORD ABOUT IT TO ANYBODY, AND IN THE FIRST WEEK OF MARCH IT WENT AWAY AND HAS NOT COME BACK, AND ON THE EIGHTEENTH HE CAME BACK INTO THAT BUILDING AND STOOD IN THE CORRIDOR FOR TWO MINUTES.**

The block is three storeys and there are twenty-four flats in it. It was put up in about 1963 and the water is in the ground under the floor of the ground-floor corridor and comes up a riser in the middle of it.

From about half past nine at night, in that building, for as long as anybody can remember, you could hear water knock about two floors down.

Not run. Knock. Four or five goes, hard, from under the floor, and then nothing, and it was over in about ten minutes, and it had started every night for years and it started every night when she came.

She has been in the flat on the second since the March of 2011. She is sixty-two, and there are about nine people in that block she speaks to.

She worked out inside a month that it came from below her, and that whoever it was loudest for was whoever had a front door nearest the riser. She has never gone down and stood under it. She has never tried anything with it. She has never once raised it with anybody in that building or outside it, and not one of the nine people she does speak to has ever heard her mention it.

What she has never got to the bottom of in all that time is whether she would be glad if it stopped.

He is a man in his late fifties and he has been in the flat next to hers since about 2016. He went away on the sixteenth of February to a place about two hours off and came back on the evening of the eighteenth of March.

He had come to that flat in about 2016 and had never mentioned the knocking either. The reason is that she had never mentioned it, and neither of them ever worked out how that had come about between two people who live six paces apart and use the same door.

On the evening of the eighteenth he came in at about seven and was back out in that corridor inside two minutes, because he had something to check on the first floor, which was not anything he had to check.

He stood in the middle of that corridor for about two minutes.

He was waiting for it without knowing that he was waiting for it. He has said since that he did not know it was a thing he had been hearing all those years until the second he stood there and it did not come, and he has said that to nobody at all.

**HE STOOD THERE FOR TWO MINUTES ON THE EIGHTEENTH WAITING FOR A SOUND HE HAD BEEN HEARING THROUGH THAT FLOOR SINCE HE MOVED IN AND HAD NOT KNOWN HE WAS WAITING FOR, AND NEITHER OF THEM PUT ANYTHING TO THE OTHER.**

The last time anybody in that block heard it was the night of the fourth of March. It did not come on the fifth, and it did not come on the sixth, and by the Saturday nobody was expecting it, and by the following Wednesday nobody in that building would have been able to tell you what you were describing if you asked them.

She noticed on the night of the fifth. She was in and it was twenty to ten and there was nothing, and she sat there for about half an hour with the television on and nothing else on.

She has not mentioned it. She has not put anything under nine doors and she has not asked the man next door and she has not gone down to the ground floor to find out whether anybody else had noticed it either. There is not one arrangement in her head that would ever take her there.

There are two of the nine she speaks to in that building. One of them is on the ground floor and one is on the third, and both of them are in at night, and between them they have covered every evening for about as long as she has been there, without a gap. On the Friday of the sixth of March she was in the shop with the one on the third and she got as far as the first two words of it. She said, "Have you ever" and the other one said, "Two pound twenty, if it's the milk," and she said that it was, and that was the whole of that as well.

Since the eighteenth they have come out into that corridor at the same hour four times.

On the nineteenth he was in with his door open and she went past and neither of them looked at the other. On the twenty-sixth she was out in her jacket and he was coming back in and they passed and said nothing. On the first of April they were both out at about ten and stood about a metre and a half apart for about three minutes and said one sentence between them, which was about the weather.

On the second of April she came out at ten to ten.

"Why are we both standing out here?" she said.

"Goosebumps," he said.

"It's not that cold."

"It's not where I live," he said.

That was all of it. Both of them knew what the other one had said and neither of them asked a second question, and they went back in at about the same moment without agreeing to, and there has been nothing since.

She has thought about the two minutes he stood there on the eighteenth. She has thought about it more than about the knocking, which is the part that has surprised her, and she has not been able to work out whether that is because of him or because of her.
