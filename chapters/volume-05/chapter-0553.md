# Chapter 0553 — The Notice, and the Date on It

The Monday of the sixteenth of March 2020, and from about half past nine in the morning until about half past two in the afternoon, in three parts of this borough.

**A MAN IN HIS MID SIXTIES SAID SOMETHING OUT LOUD IN THE SEPTEMBER AND TWO PEOPLE CARRIED IT, AND IN MARCH HE WENT AND READ A DATE FOR HIMSELF AND FOUND HE WAS FIVE MONTHS OUT, AND HE GAVE THE DATE TO BOTH OF THEM ANYWAY, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS ASKED HIM HOW HE KNEW.**

The footpath runs from the estate down to where the buses used to stop and on to a shop that shuts at four, and it is the way nine or ten people in that part of the borough take to the shops, and it is a made path with posts down both sides of it.

He is a man in his mid sixties. He has lived in one of the houses on the estate since 1988 and he is not a man anybody in that part of the borough thinks of as knowing anything.

In the September of last year he said, in a place where about nine people were standing, that the path would be shut in the new year. He said it the way people say things that have already been decided somewhere else, and there were nine people standing there, and two of them took it as a thing that was going to happen.

The two of them are a woman in her late fifties who has taken the short way to that shop for about nine years and a man in his late twenties who works at the shop and has taken it for about three.

Neither of them has asked him where he heard it. Neither of them has asked anybody. That is not a mark of respect. It is that a man in his mid sixties saying a thing like that in a place like that is not somebody you ask.


He went on the Thursday of the twelfth of March, at about ten in the morning, and walked up the path, and there was a notice on the post at the top of it, and the notice is the size of a sheet of wallpaper and it is printed and it is fixed on with tape.

It says what it is meant to say. It says the path will be closed from the fourth of June for works, and it gives a length of time, and it gives no reason beyond the word works, and it has a name of a company on it and nothing else of anybody.

He stood and read it. He did not photograph it, because he does not have anything that photographs, and he did not write it down, because he has never written anything down in his life.

The date on it is the fourth of June.

He had said the new year. He was five months out, and he had been right about the rest of it, which is that the path is going and that it is going because of something decided somewhere else.

There are four other notices on the posts down that path and they are about three years old and about two of them are for things that happened.

He read all of them. He stood at the top of that path for about ten minutes and read four notices and one of them told him the date the path was re-laid and one of them told him not to park and the other two told him nothing that is still true, and he took none of them down and did not take a photograph of any of them, because there is no one he would send a photograph to.

Nobody asked him to look. He came up on his own on the Thursday because it had been five days and he wanted to see whether there was anything up yet, and there was.

The woman in her late fifties has not been to the top of that path since. She has gone down it on four mornings since the twelfth of March and on each of them she has looked at the notice on the post at the top of it, and on each of them she has read the date again, and the date has not changed.

The man in his late twenties went up on the Wednesday and put his hand flat on the notice, which is a thing a young man does, and read the whole of it including the part about the length of the time, and it told him nothing that the Monday had not told him.

The man in his mid sixties has not been back. He has not been up that path since the Thursday of the twelfth and he has not mentioned it since, and he has not been asked, and if he were asked he would say the same thing he said in the September of last year, which is that it is going.

---

He told them on the Monday, at about midday, in the place where the nine people were on the day in September, and there were four of them, and both of the two were there.

"June," he said. "The fourth of June."

The woman in her late fifties said, "Right," and the man in his late twenties said, "That's months off, then," and the man in his mid sixties did not correct that either and did not agree with it.

Then the two of them walked it. They walked it at about two in the afternoon, together, the long way, round by the row of shops and down the other street and back in by the field, and it took about twenty minutes longer than the path, and they did not say one word about why they were doing it, and the man in his late twenties was a bit silly about it and the woman in her late fifties was not.

The woman said, "It'll be a mud thing, that'll, won't it."

The man in his late twenties said, "Aye."

That is the whole of what was said about it, and it was said on a made path in about ten seconds, and then they came back in and the woman went home and the man in his late twenties went back to the shop.

Nothing has been written to anybody. Nobody has been asked anything. The path will be shut on the fourth of June and about nine people will have to go the long way and two of them have been to see it and one of those two has said one sentence about mud.

**HE SAID IT WRONG AND WAS RIGHT AND HAS SAID NOTHING SINCE, AND THE ONLY TWO PEOPLE WHO CARRIED IT WALKED IT ONCE AND SAID ONE SENTENCE ABOUT MUD, AND THE NOTICE IS ON A POST AND IS IN WRITING AND NOBODY HAS SENT A REPLY.**

Down by the water there is a building that none of those three parts of this borough reaches, and in that building there is an old book about two inches deep, and a line was ruled under the final entry in a column in it a long time ago and nobody has put anything under that line since, and that gap measures one thousand nine hundred and thirty-seven days.

**HE SAID IT IN THE SEPTEMBER AND HE WAS FIVE MONTHS OUT AND RIGHT ABOUT EVERYTHING ELSE, AND TWO PEOPLE WALKED THE LONG WAY ONCE AND SAID ONE SENTENCE ABOUT MUD, AND THE PATH IS SHUT ON THE FOURTH OF JUNE AND NOBODY HAS ASKED ANYBODY ANYTHING.**
