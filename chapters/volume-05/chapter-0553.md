# Chapter 0553 — The Fourth of June

The Monday of the sixteenth of March 2020. Most of the day, on one made path between an estate and a row of shops and in the places between them.

**HE SAID SOMETHING OUT LOUD IN THE SEPTEMBER OF LAST YEAR IN FRONT OF NINE PEOPLE AND TWO OF THEM CARRIED IT FOR SIX MONTHS WITHOUT ASKING HIM ONE QUESTION, AND HE HAS NOW READ A DATE ON A NOTICE AND GIVEN IT TO BOTH OF THEM ANYWAY, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS ASKED HIM WHERE HE GOT IT.**

The path runs from the estate down to where the buses used to stop and on to a shop that shuts at four. It is a made path with posts down both sides of it, and about nine or ten people in that part of the borough take it to the shops because it is shorter than the road.

He is a man in his mid sixties. He has lived in one of the houses on the estate since 1988, and he is not a man anybody in that part of the borough thinks of as knowing anything.

In the September of last year he said, in a place where about nine people were standing, that the path would be shut in the new year.

He said it the way people say things that have already been settled somewhere else. Nine people were standing there and two of them took it as a thing that was going to happen.

The two of them are a woman in her late fifties who has taken the short way to that shop for about nine years, and a man in his late twenties who works in the shop and has taken it for about three.

Neither of them has asked him where he heard it. Neither of them has asked anybody else. That is not respect. It is that a man in his mid sixties saying a thing like that in a place like that is not somebody you ask.

---

He went on the Thursday of the twelfth, at about ten in the morning, and walked up the path, and there was a notice on the post at the top of it.

The notice is the size of a sheet of wallpaper. It is printed. It is fixed on with tape that has gone yellow at the corners.

It says what it is meant to say. It says the path will be closed from the fourth of June for works. It gives a length of time. It gives no reason beyond the word works. It has the name of a company on it and nothing else of anybody.

He stood and read it.

He had said the new year, and by the new year he had meant the new year, and January to the fourth of June is five months.

He was right about the rest of it. The path is going, and it is going because of something that has been decided somewhere else by people who will not be asked, and everything he said in that September was true except the month.

There are four other notices on the posts down that path and they are about three years old and two of them are for things that already happened.

He read all of them. He stood at the top of that path for about ten minutes and read four notices, and one of them told him the date the path was laid again in the first place, and one of them told him not to park, and the other two told him nothing that is still true.

He took none of them down. He has nothing that photographs and he has never written anything down in his life, and there is not one person in this borough he would send a photograph to.

Nobody asked him to look. He came up on his own on the Thursday because it had been a week and he wanted to see whether there was anything up yet, and there was.

The woman in her late fifties has not been to the top of that path since. She has gone down it four mornings since the twelfth and on each of them she has read the date again on the way past, and the date has not changed.

The man in his late twenties went up on the Wednesday and put his hand flat on the notice, which is a thing a young man does, and read the whole of it including the part about the length of the time.

The man in his mid sixties has not been back up it and has not mentioned it since, and if he were asked he would say the same thing he said in the September of last year, which is that it is going.

He told them on the Monday at about midday, in the place where the nine of them were in the September, and there were four of them, and both of the two were there.

"June," he said. "The fourth of June."

The woman in her late fifties said, "Right."

The man in his late twenties said nothing at all, and stood there with his hands in his pockets, and did not look at the man in his mid sixties, and did not look away either.

Then the two of them walked it. They walked it at about two in the afternoon, together, the long way, round by the row of shops and down the other street and back in by the field, and it took about twenty minutes longer than the path.

Neither of them said one word about why they were doing it.

The woman said, "It'll be a mud thing, that'll, won't it."

The man in his late twenties said nothing, and walked on, and did not answer her, and she did not ask him again.

They came back in by the field and the woman went home and the man in his late twenties went back to the shop and shut up for the day at four, and nothing has been written to anybody, and nobody has been asked anything, and the path will be shut on the fourth of June, and about nine people will have to go the long way.

He is half right, and he told them anyway, and one of them said one sentence about mud and the other one said nothing at all.
