# Chapter 0865 — The Width Of The Top Board

Thursday the thirtieth of November 2023, in the yard at the unit from the time the boards have come out of the damp until the light has gone off the store roof. One board off the top of the stack and how wide it is.

**THERE IS A BOARD OFF THE TOP OF THE STACK OF SPARE BOARDS IN THE YARD AT THE UNIT AND HE MEANS TO CLOSE ONE HAND ACROSS ITS OWN KNUCKLES WITH THE BOARD BETWEEN THEM AND TAKE THE WIDTH OFF THE KNUCKLES, AND THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN STANDS OUTSIDE THE NEAR GATE AND DOES NOT COME IN.**

The stack has been at the wall end of the yard since the summer, eleven boards on the flat with the offcuts on top of them, and it has been rained on from the north side since the middle of September.

There is no wear on it at all, because nothing has ever crossed it and nothing has ever been dragged along it. What is on it is three months of weather standing on one face, and from the gate end of the yard it looks like the best board in the stack.

That matters more than it sounds. A board laid on the flat in this country takes what the weather gives it on the face that is up, and gives it back out of the same face, and after a fortnight of wet the up face has swelled and the down face has not. The board is still the same board. It has simply got a top that is higher than its bottom, by an amount you cannot see at all and can find out in a second with your hands.

He has been meaning to find that out since the Friday.

The question underneath it is a longer one and it is not going anywhere this winter. The frame over the store door is the last thing in that building that was made by somebody else, and the lintel on it came out of the old place when the doors came out, and the frame round it is two uprights and a head of nothing in particular, and it is sound and it is not square. Whether anything is ever rebuilt across that opening is not a decision anybody has made, and a man does not make it for himself in a wet November because he has a board in his hand.

What he wants to know is narrower than that, and it is the only part of it he is going to get.

Will one of these boards go across.

---

His hand is the rule. Everybody carries one and it is the only rule in this yard that costs nothing and never gets lost, and the part of a hand that is worth anything for a board is the row of knuckles across the top of it, with the thumb outside and the first finger inside. A knuckle does not lie to you the way a stretched tape does, and it does not lose its shape on a cold morning the way a folding rule does, and it is the same width on both sides of a gate whichever way you happen to be standing.

He takes the board off the top of the stack and puts his left hand up in the air with the back of it towards him and takes the board in between, closing on it at the knuckle line rather than anywhere else.

The knuckle across the top of a hand is about an inch and a half from the tip of the thumb to the first joint of the first finger, and it is the widest part of a hand that is not the palm, and it is the part that does not lose its shape when a man closes it. He closes on and the board stops him at a hand's width and a bit over.

He does it at four places along the board, about a foot in from each end and twice in between, because a board is not one width all the way and you want to know where it stops being one. All four come out the same, at a foot and a bit over, to within the width of a thumbnail.

Then he stands there and looks at it.

The width of a board is not the interesting part of it. Any board can be measured and the answer goes in the same drawer as the answer for every other board in the country. The interesting part is that he does not know what he is measuring it against.

---

The opening is what he has to find, and he cannot put his hands round it, and he does not try.

What he can do is stand at the store door and take it in two halves. He puts his hand up against the left upright, thumb on one edge and first finger on the other, and closes, and gets a hand's width and a bit. Then he moves across to the right upright and does the same, and gets the same, and the two of them with the space his hands did not cover in the middle make an opening about a foot and a half across.

He has been in and out of that doorway for years. He would have said a foot and a half without doing any of that.

A board a foot and a bit over, an opening a foot and a half, and there is a little over two inches coming back at each end.

That is the answer and it is the answer he expected, and he stands in the doorway with the board in his hand and finds that he is pleased about it in a way that has nothing to do with the door.

Then he goes back to the stack and does the thing he should have done first.

He measures the board again at the two ends, which are the two places he left out of it.

At the end that has been out in the weather for three months it is a foot and two and a quarter. At the end that has been against the wall it is a foot and a bit. A quarter of an inch over eleven feet, which is nothing to a man and everything to a board, because the two inches at each end of that opening are the whole of what is available and a quarter of an inch of them has gone already.

So the board goes across, and it goes across with the swollen end at the wall side, and if it goes across with the swollen end at the door side it does not.

The woman from two doors down comes to the near gate at about two in the afternoon and stands on the path side of it with her coat done up to the neck and does not come in.

He is on the other side of the yard with the board back on the stack and his hands in his pockets.

"Your side of the road out yet?"

"Not two. Ours came off about eleven."

"Ours has come and gone twice."

"Then you're the one to ask."

She stands there a while longer with her hands inside her coat and looks at the stack along the wall rather than at him, and then she goes back down the path and the gate is where it was.

He does not follow her and does not go near the gate while she is going, and she does not ask him anything and he does not tell her anything, and the gate is a gate and shuts.

What he has is a number.

There is no list. There is no sheet with a column on it for the width of a board against the width of a door, and there is nobody he could give a number to who would be the person to use it, and a man who writes a figure down for no one ends up keeping it in his head, which is a worse place for it than the stack.

So it goes in his head, and the board goes back on the stack with the swollen end at the wall side, which is the only thing the day arranged that it arranged itself.

And if anybody ever takes that frame out, they will want the wide end of that board in first, and the two inches over at each end are at the thin end only, and not one word of it is written down anywhere in this yard.
