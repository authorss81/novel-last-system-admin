# Chapter 0730 — The Ladder Under The Window

Friday the third of June 2022, in this borough. She is in her own house at number twelve from first light until dark, and the whole of it is a front room she goes into and does not sit in.

**A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-EIGHT STEPS OVER HER BRUSH TO LOOK INTO HER OWN FRONT ROOM, SEES THE FOLDED LADDER UNDER THE WINDOW AND THE TELEPHONE SITTING SILENT ON THE LOW TABLE, SPEAKS TO HER SISTER ON THE STEP ABOUT THE ROOM, AND COMES BACK TO HER KITCHEN TABLE BEFORE DARK.**

She is a woman of seventy-eight at number twelve on Friday. Her sister stands on the step near dark and she speaks to her out there.

---

She does the bowl at first light and the sill after, one pass with a damp cloth, and then she sits under the window with her cup.

On the left of the table under its cloth rests the box with tape round it. The gas envelope is on the right with its six things in pencil, nothing crossed off. The ballpoint pen is in the drawer, dry, where she put it back in May.

She does not open the drawer. There is nothing to write, and a dry pen in a shut drawer is better than a dry pen on a table.

---

At the middle of the morning she goes down the passage with the washing for the back room and stops at the front room doorway.

She steps over the brush with its handle across the runner, the way she has stepped over it since the autumn, and she looks in without going in at first.

The room is cold. The rush chair stands against the front wall where nobody sits. Under the window, folded flat against the wall where it has been since the seventeenth of December, is the step ladder with four treads shut true, tape round the second rail and chalk on the third tread. She knows it by feel more than by sight now.

On the low table by the door frame the telephone sits silent on top of its box with the flex to the socket, dust on both. She looks at it and leaves it where it is, and goes in two paces to be sure the window is shut, and then she comes out again without sitting down. She has been into this room twice before and sat in it neither time, and this makes a third time the same way.

---

She spends the middle of the day between the kitchen and the yard.

The coal shed stays shut with its padlock open in the hasp. The gate stays propped back against the brick. The passage light burns on, as it has since June, and she leaves it burning.

She eats standing up at the kitchen table, which she never does when her sister is there, and wipes the crumbs away with her palm.

---

Her sister comes to the step at dark with her bag over her arm and looks past her down the passage.

"You went in," her sister says. "I can tell by your shoes. There is dust on them."

"I looked in," she says. "The window is shut. The ladder is where it was. I did not sit down."

"What for, then?"

"To be sure," she says. "A room you do not sit in still wants looking at, or it stops being part of the house."

Her sister looks at the doorway and the brush across it and then back at her.

"You could bring a chair in there," her sister says. "The green one from here."

"No," she says. "The green chair lives under this window. The rush chair lives in there. I will not mix them."

"Then what?"

"Nothing," she says. "I looked and I came away, and that is enough for June."

She sees her sister to the gate with the apron on and stands there till her sister is off down the road with her bag on her arm, and then she goes back in and pulls the front door to the width of her hand, and sits under the kitchen window with the cloth squared over the taped box and her hands round a cold cup.
