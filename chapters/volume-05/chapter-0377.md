# Chapter 0377 — Before Nine

Saturday the sixteenth of December 2017, from about half past nine in the morning until about half past ten at night, a shop off a parade, and a room at the back of it, in Tideglass.

**TWO MEN IN THIS BOROUGH BOTH WANT TO KNOW ONE THING, WHICH IS WHETHER THAT SHOP IS OPEN BEFORE NINE IN THE MORNING, AND BOTH OF THEM HAVE BEEN IN THAT ROOM AT THE BACK OF IT, AND ON ONE OF THE AFTERNOONS OF THAT FORTNIGHT SHE ANSWERED IT, OUT LOUD, TO SOMEBODY ELSE, IN THAT ROOM, IN FRONT OF BOTH OF THEM, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO MEN HEARD IT AS AN ANSWER TO ANYTHING, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS ASKED HER, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS ASKED THE OTHER.**

That shop has been shutting its counter at half past three on a Saturday since before the summer, and behind the counter there is a hatch through the wall into a room with a table in it and four chairs round it, and the room is not on any plan of that building and no more than four people have ever been in it at once.

The woman who works in a shop is on that counter from about ten to nine on six days and is in that room from about four on a Saturday, and she has been doing both of those for about six years.

The man in his forties comes in off that parade on most days. He buys a coil of wire, plugs, or a box of screws, and he is at that counter at about ten past ten in the morning on one day of the week and at four o'clock in the afternoon on another, and he does the morning on one day and the afternoon on another, and the rest of it depends.

The man who works nights finishes at about four in the morning, sleeps until about two, and goes into that parade at about half past four most days, and has walked into that parade at that hour for the best part of six years now, and he carries two sacks of ice out to his car in the back of it every time.

---

The thing the man in his forties wants to know is whether he can get into that shop at half past seven in the morning.

He has a job that starts at eight most days in December and the job is a half hour off, and there is a set of outside lights he has been asked to get for the front of somebody's house, and that shop is the only one in this borough that keeps the kind he wants, and they are on a rack at the back in a brown carton and the carton is behind the door of the back room.

He has wanted to ask since about the middle of November. He has not asked. He is not a man who asks, in the sense that asking a shopkeeper to let him in the back of her shop at half past seven in the morning is a thing that has to be arranged, and arranging it makes it something, and he does not want it to be something.

The thing the man who works nights wants to know is also whether that shop is open before nine in the morning, and for a different reason, and the reason is that he has started, since the beginning of December, to go into that parade at about seven in the morning instead of about half past four in the afternoon, because he cannot sleep after two and four hours is too long to lie there, and he has been going into that parade at seven and walking up it and coming back, and he does not know whether there is anybody in that shop at seven, and he has twice walked past the front of it in the dark and seen it shuttered.

He has wanted to know since the beginning of December. He has not asked either. He has never once asked anybody for anything in that shop in all that time and does not intend to begin, and there is a version of asking that is only asking if you are in a position to be turned down, and he is not in a position to be turned down at half past four in the afternoon with two sacks of ice on the counter.

---

She said it on the Monday, in that room, at about twenty past four, and there were three other men in there.

She was at the hatch with her sleeves up over the elastics and the man with a van had come in the back way about four minutes before and had been asking after a size of washers he had had off her in the summer, and she said that she would not have to go out for them until the middle of the week, and then she said, without anything coming before it or going after it, that she was in there from about half seven on the mornings and that anybody who came before nine was no use to her because the door was shut and she was not coming through the middle of a shop in her coat.

She said it to the man with a van. She said it in the way a woman says a thing she has said before and does not think of as information.

The man in his forties was at the other end of that table with a cup. The man who works nights was at the window end of it with a carrier bag of ice at his feet.

Both of them heard it.

The man in his forties heard that she was in at half seven and not that the door was shut, because a man in his forties is interested in a person being there and not in a door. He put down his cup about two minutes later and said something else about a length of conduit and did not come back to it.

The man who works nights heard it. He was at the window end of that table and he was not listening, and what he took in was that she was annoyed about something, and being annoyed at the end of a working day in front of three men is a thing a man in that position does not follow.

He did not hear the door. He has never known whether that door is shut before nine or open before nine or propped with a bit of wood, and he is not a man who will stand outside a shop in the dark at seven in the morning trying a door.


Neither of them has asked her.

It is not that either of them decided not to. It is that in six years and four months neither of them has been in a position to put the question, and a question a man has never asked and has not prepared is not a thing that comes out at a table with three other men sitting at it.

What they have instead is this. The man in his forties could ask the man who works nights. He does not know that the man who works nights wants to know. And the man who works nights could ask the man in his forties, because the man in his forties comes in that shop five days a week and the man who works nights has seen him at that counter more times than he could count, and a man in his forties will answer a question without being asked it directly.

He has not done that either. He has been in that room about ten times since the beginning of December and the man in his forties has been in it about six of those, and neither of them has ever put the other one on the spot about his weekday mornings, and one of them was about to, at about half past four on the Thursday, and the woman who works in a shop put the kettle on, and by the time it had boiled neither of them was on the thing.

And the fact is still there. She has said it twice more since the Monday, both times to other men, both times in that room, both times with the words in them. It was said on the Friday to a man who wanted a bag of sand by eight the next morning, and it was said on the Saturday to a man who asked her what her Christmas hours were, which was a question about something else and got an answer about something else.

The shop is in at half seven. The door is not open before nine. Both of those are still true, and neither of the two men has either of them, and either of them could have had both of them by asking one person one question, and there is no arrangement of any kind standing between them and it.

**ON THE SATURDAY OF THAT WEEK SHE WASHED THE JUG AND PUT THE KETTLE ON AT ABOUT FOUR AND SAID, TO NOBODY IN PARTICULAR, THAT SHE WAS NOT BACK IN ON THE MONDAY UNTIL HALF NINE BECAUSE SHE HAD SOMETHING TO DO BEFORE, AND THE MAN WHO WORKS NIGHTS WAS AT THE WINDOW END OF THAT TABLE AND HEARD IT AND DID NOT PUT IT WITH THE OTHER THING HE HAD HEARD, AND HE WILL BE IN THAT ROOM AGAIN ON THE WEDNESDAY, AND THE MAN IN HIS FORTIES WILL BE AT THAT COUNTER ON THE TUESDAY, AND NEITHER OF THEM IS GOING TO ASK.**
