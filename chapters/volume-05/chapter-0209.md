# Chapter 0209 — What Everybody Agrees About

Friday the fourth of December 2015, from about half past nine in the morning, a works office at the second pumping station past the bridge in Ravensmere.

**THE FIRST OF THE THREE REGIONAL SYSTEMS WORKS, IT IS CHEAP, IT IS CORRECT, IT HAS NOT FAILED, AND EVERY PART OF IT IS HELD BY SOMEBODY PRIVATELY, AND NOT ONE PERSON IN IT IS HIDING ANYTHING, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE PUT TWO SENTENCES NEXT TO EACH OTHER IN A CORRIDOR AND THEN WISHED HE HAD NOT.**

The water comes off the intake at Ravensmere, goes through the filters, and is delivered to three towns, and it has done that since before anybody alive was on the group, and the pressure at the far end of the third town is good enough that the man who runs the clinic there has not had to ask for a tank in nine years. A standpipe at the edge of the second town runs from about six in the morning until about nine at night and costs a person nothing and asks nothing of them. There is a night rota on the wall with eight names on it and four more under it in brackets, and Jonas Mercer stood and read every one of them and did not learn a thing about any of them. The works was built before anybody in that room was born, the filters were renewed some years back, and the bill went down when they were.

Jonas Mercer saw all of it between half past nine and about one o'clock and it was the best-run public thing he has ever stood inside.

**AND THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE IN THE LOBBY OF THAT WORKS OFFICE THAT MORNING, printed and fixed to a post at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **USING SOMETHING IS NOT THE SAME AS HAVING A SAY IN IT.**
> **A READING BELONGS TO THE THING THAT WAS READ.**
> **NEITHER OF THOSE IS FALSE.**

There were about fourteen people in the room and a table the length of it and printed paper on the table face down in front of every chair, which is a thing Jonas Mercer had not seen done anywhere except once, in a room over a shop in his own city, on the eighteenth of September, and he did not say that out loud.

Iselin Krogh sat at the end nearest the window and did the whole of the first twenty minutes and gave nothing away, because there was nothing to give away. She is about fifty-one. She said the following things and every one of them was true.

The group is three authorities and about six staff and it exists to keep the three towns on one set of terms.

Nothing in it asks a person to give anything up.

Nothing about it is secret. The terms are on the wall behind her and there is a copy on the table in front of every chair and she said she would get him one now and did, and put it in front of him face up, which is the opposite of what the sheet had been lying.

A person who uses the standpipe and gives nothing is not being taken advantage of. A person who uses it and gives nothing has exactly the same rights as a person who uses it and gives everything, and that is deliberate, and she said the word deliberate out loud, and nobody at that table disagreed with it.

"What we cannot do," she said, "is tell you what happens to a reading. That is not a thing we have kept back, it is a thing that is not ours. The asset is not ours. In this town the works is a company's. In the second town the mains are a trust's. In the third the pumping station is municipal and the other two are not, and the two that are not have both changed hands inside ten years, and every time one changed hands nothing was broken and nothing was promised and the obligations stayed exactly where they were."

"Whose obligations," said Tomas Renn.

"The region's," said Iselin Krogh. "Which is a word that means the three of us and nobody else, and I will put my hand to that as a description of a legal arrangement and I will not put my hand to it as a description of a place." She turned the top sheet over and pushed it about three inches towards him. "That is the sheet. It is the same sheet in all three towns. It has been the same sheet since the year before the filters."

Jonas Mercer read it. It is a good sheet. It is clear and short and it does not waste a word and it is in plain type and everything on it is true.

It is also, all the way down, a set of things a person does not have to do, and then, at the bottom, in the same type and the same size and with the same amount of space above it, one sentence about what a person has not got.

> *Nothing is required of any person in order to use any part of this system.*
> *A person has no standing in respect of anything recorded under this.*

He read it twice. There was nothing between those two lines. There was nothing in the whole of the document that said who kept the readings or what was done with them or who else could look at them, and he looked for that for about ten minutes, and there is no way to look for the absence of a thing in a document and find it, and that is the whole of what he learned on the fourth of December.

The man of about thirty-one who comes to a canteen hatch at ten to three on Fridays in Tideglass was in the corridor outside that room at about half past eleven with a paper cup of tea from a machine at the end of it, and he is not a party to anything, and Jonas Mercer had not seen him since the thirtieth of October.

"I didn't know you came over here," he said.

"I've come over four times since August," said the man of about thirty-one. "On a boat Friday morning and back Sunday night, and I have not told anybody about any of it, so don't tell anybody, and you're not going to, I can see."

"I won't."

"Right." He drank his tea. "I've been stood in this corridor for twenty minutes reading the sheet on the wall, and there's two lines at the bottom of it, and they might as well be printed one above the other because that's how they're printed, and the top one says you don't have to give anything, and the bottom one says you've got no say in whatever it was that got given."

"Yes," said Jonas Mercer.

"That's two lines."

"That's two lines."

"And they've been two lines since before the filters."

"They have."

The man of about thirty-one looked at the floor of the corridor, and then at the ceiling, and then he put his hand flat on the wall next to the sheet, which is not a thing anybody does with a wall.

"I'd rather I hadn't said that," he said. "Not because it's wrong. Because I've just said the only thing anybody's said about that wall in about ten years and I'm in a corridor, and I don't know who heard it, and I can't un-say it, and I'm going to have to go back into that room in twenty minutes and sit down."

"You don't have to say anything in there."

"I know I don't," said the man of about thirty-one. "That's the other thing. There's nothing in that room I have to say, and nothing in that room I can say that changes the sheet, and I have come four times on a Friday and I have said nothing four times, and I have got a good job on that side and a better one on this side and I am the only person I know who is in both and I have never once said that out loud anywhere, and I have just said it to you in a corridor, and I'd like to go home."

He went back into the room and sat down and did not say anything else for the rest of the morning, and Jonas Mercer did not go in.

Nobody in that room had lied to him and nobody in that room had hidden a single thing. He had come across an estuary for a sheet of paper and he had got it, and it had two lines on it, and one of the two was a door and the other one was a hand on it, and the two were printed six inches apart on the same sheet by the same people on the same day, and everybody at that table thought it was a good sheet, and one of them had said the word deliberate out loud in front of him.

At about one o'clock the group broke for lunch and nine of the fourteen went out into the rain. Iselin Krogh stayed in and gave him the copy of the sheet in a plastic wallet and said, "That is yours. Take it with you. There is no charge for it and there is no record of who has taken one."

The man of about thirty-one went out at the same time as the rest of them, with his paper cup still in his hand, and got on a bus, and Jonas Mercer watched him through the glass and did not go after him, and the water at the far end of the third town was better than the water at this borough's end, and the standpipe ran from six until nine, and the bill had gone down when the filters were done.
