# Chapter 0520 — The Part They Agree About

The Tuesday of the twenty-fourth of September 2019, from half past ten in the morning until two in the afternoon, at two houses and on a bus, in this borough.

**THEY CAN GIVE THE WHOLE OF THE EIGHTH OF MAY WITHOUT LOOKING AT ANYTHING AND THEY CANNOT AGREE ON ONE SENTENCE SPOKEN DOWN A TELEPHONE AT HALF PAST TEN, AND NEITHER VERSION IS WRONG, AND IT HAS BEEN RUNNING FOR FOUR AND A HALF MONTHS WITHOUT EITHER OF THEM RAISING IT, AND ON THE SUNDAY BEFORE LAST ONE OF THEM PUT THAT QUESTION TO A THIRD PERSON AND WAS ANSWERED THAT SHE ALREADY KNEW WHICH WAY ROUND IT WAS, AND THAT PERSON HAS SAID NOTHING SINCE.**

They have known each other since 1988 and they have never had a row in their lives, and anybody in this borough who knows the two of them will tell you the same thing about both of them, which is that neither of them has ever been unfair to anybody.

---

Here is what happened in May, and it is set out here running forward, because both of them can give the whole of it without looking at anything.

On the Wednesday of the eighth of May a woman called Petrie, who is a friend of both of them and is not a relation of either of them, had a thing done that was done in the hospital in the town and that is not a thing anybody should have to do on their own.

The older of the two, who is a woman of about sixty-two, took her in her own motor at about ten and stayed in the hospital from about ten until about one.

At about half past ten the younger of the two, a man in his late fifties, was sent for. He got to the hospital at five to twelve.

Petrie came out at about one and went home with the older woman, who stayed until about four in the afternoon and then went home because she had a dog.

The younger man was not in the hospital when Petrie came out. He had gone at about quarter past twelve.

Both of those things are agreed. Neither of them has ever been disputed by anybody, including Petrie, who was under something for part of it and has said that she remembers the middle of it badly and the ends of it well.

Here is the one thing they do not agree about, and it is one sentence long, and it has been running since the middle of May.

The woman says she rang him at about half past ten and told him to come.

The man says she rang him at about half past ten and told him she was on her way and that he should wait for her.

Neither of them has ever said that sentence to a third person. He has never told anybody. She has never told anybody. They have not had it out, and they have not avoided each other either, and they have been in the same room more times than either of them has counted since the eighth of May, and neither of them has raised it.

Here is why neither of them is wrong.

The woman had been asked by Petrie on the Sunday before, in a kitchen, with a cup in her hand, to be the one who would go in and sit, because the woman is the one Petrie trusts with a hospital and has been since 2003. And the woman had said she would.

She had not said to anybody on the Monday or the Tuesday that she was going to do it. She had not told her husband. She had not told the younger man. On the Wednesday morning she drove Petrie to the hospital and parked and went in, and then at about half past ten she telephoned the younger man, and what she has said ever since is that she told him to come.

The man had been told nothing before that telephone call. He has said that he knows this because he was at his own desk and his own telephone was on the desk in front of him and it was on the loud setting the way it is, and he was on a job, and he finished what he was doing, and it was nearly half past ten when the telephone went, and there was no possibility of him knowing anything before that.

The woman has said that she does not know that any of that is true, and she does not say that it is not true, and she has never once accused him of not listening. She says, when anybody has ever come near it, and nobody has, that she may simply have said it in a way he heard differently.

That is the whole of it. It is not a quarrel. It is two people who both did everything correctly and who cannot both be right about a sentence spoken on a telephone at half past ten on the eighth of May, and who have both gone on being exactly the same to each other for four and a half months.

And on the twenty-second of September the woman telephoned a third person about it.

The third person is a woman of about fifty-eight who was at the hospital that day in a different capacity altogether and who has known both of them since the eighties.

She was telephoned at about four in the afternoon. The woman said, "Can I ask you something about May."

And the third person said, "What about it."

And the woman said, "When I rang him on the Wednesday, did I tell him to come, or did I tell him I was on my way."

And the third person said, "I knew you'd ask me that."

And the woman said, "You did?"

And the third person said, "I've known which of you was right since about half past ten on the eighth of May."

And the woman said, "Which was it."

And the third person said, "I'm not going to say that."

And the woman said, "Why not."

And the third person said, "Because you'd take it and use it, and so would he, and you're both wrong and you're both right and if I say the words one of you is going to be somebody who has been let down by me instead of by the other one, and I won't do that."

And the woman did not say anything for a while.

And the third person said, "Are you all right?"

And the woman said, "I've been all right since May."

And the third person said, "Yes," and then the woman heard nothing for a few seconds, and then the third person said, "It's not the sentence, you know. It's what you'd both do with it."

And then the woman said, "All right," and they stopped talking about it, and they talked for another nineteen minutes about the front boundary on the corner, which is not being cut and should not be cut at that time of year.

Here is the whole of the twenty-fourth of September.

The two of them have been in the same house since one o'clock. It is the younger man's house and the reason is a heating plant that has been making a noise since August, and there is a man coming on Friday, and the older woman came to be there when the man came, which is what she has always done and what everybody expects.

Between about one and about two they sat in the kitchen and they talked about the heating for ten minutes and then they did not talk about the heating.

And at about twenty past one the man said, "I've been thinking about a thing."

And the woman said, "Have you."

And the man said, "The May business."

And the woman said, "I thought you'd never bring that up."

And the man said, "I've had four and a half months of it and there's a man coming on Friday, so I thought I'd get it out."

And the woman said, "Go on, then."

And the man said, "I'm not going to say it, though."

And the woman said, "Why not."

And the man said, "Because I was on the desk and the telephone was on loud and I was nine minutes into something, and I will say that every day for the rest of my life and it will not be any use to you."

And the woman said, "I know it won't."

And the man said, "You might have said it to me. That's all. You might have said it on the Monday."

And the woman said, "I might have."

And the man said, "Why didn't you."

And the woman said, "Because I didn't want to be somebody who had said it to you on the Monday. Because then you'd have come on the Wednesday, and I wanted to be there."

And the man said, "Right."

And neither of them said anything for a while.

And the man said, "That's it. That's the whole thing. I've not got a grievance about you."

And the woman said, "I know you haven't."

And the man said, "It's that you were in that hospital at half past ten and I was at a desk, and there's no way of ever finding out what you said, and I've had to stop needing to know."

And the woman said, "When did you stop needing to know."

And the man said, "About June."

And the woman said, "I've not."

And the man said, "No," and got up and went over to the thing on the wall and stood looking at it, and came back and sat down again, and neither of them has raised any of it since.

At about ten past two the man went out to the front and stood in his own drive for a minute and then came back in and said, "Is there anything you need me to do before Friday."

And the woman said, "There's a stopcock outside that drips."

And the man said, "Which one."

And the woman said, "The one by the garage. It's been going since the summer."

And the man said, "I'll have a look at it."

And the woman said, "You don't have to."

And the man said, "I'll have a look at it," and did not look at it, and the man who comes on Friday looked at it, and it is a washer and it is nine pounds, and she paid it and told him afterwards, and neither of the two of them has ever mentioned it.

**HE TOLD HER ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF SEPTEMBER THAT HE GOT THROUGH WITH IT IN JUNE AND SHE SAID THAT SHE HAD NOT, AND NEITHER OF THEM WAS WRONG, AND HE WILL NOT ASK AND SHE WILL NOT TELL, AND THE MAN WHO CAME ON THE FRIDAY CHARGED HER NINE POUNDS FOR A WASHER.**
