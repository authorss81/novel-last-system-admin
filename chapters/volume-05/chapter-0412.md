# Chapter 0412 — Four Foot of Marble

Saturday the nineteenth of May 2018, beginning at about half past nine in the morning and ending at about ten past four in the afternoon, a yard off a road off the Cleeve road, in Tideglass.

**TWO MEN WALKED PAST A MARBLE CHIMNEY PIECE IN A STORE AT THE BOTTOM OF THAT YARD TWICE EACH ON ONE AFTERNOON AND NEITHER OF THEM PUT A HAND ON IT, AND ONE OF THEM HAS A RULE HE HAS NEVER SPOKEN ALOUD TO ANYBODY, WHICH IS ABOUT LOCKED ROOMS AND HIS OWN VAN, AND HE HAS NOBODY TO SAY IT TO, AND THE OTHER ONE HAS NOBODY TO HOLD A BOARD AND IS NOT GOING TO BE THE ONE TO GO LOOKING, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS ANY IDEA WHAT THE OTHER ONE'S REASON WAS, AND IT IS STILL AGAINST THAT WALL.**

That yard is reached by an archway between two houses off the Cleeve road. There is a building at the top of the yard and about nine hundred square foot of hardstanding and a brick workshop building standing along the west side of it, thirty foot by twenty-two, with a pitched felted roof and a wide plank door on a raised ledge and a bar that drops into two staples in the jambs.

That door is not locked and has not been locked this century, and the bar is down, and the key hangs on the back of that door itself, and about five people in this borough have ever stood in that store and every one of them has noticed the same thing about that bar.

The man who owns that yard and that building is away and has been away since the first week of May and comes back at the end of the month. He built a workshop on that site in about 1994 and he has let it out in bits ever since.

In that store, against the north wall, there is a chimney piece.

---

It is a marble one and it is the plainest thing in that building.

The shelf measures about four foot across, stands three foot ten, and projects about five inch off the chimney breast behind it. It is one piece of white marble with a grey veining in it that comes up out of the bottom left corner and off the top, and there is a roundel cut in the middle of the frieze, and there are two small square panels either side of the opening, and the jambs are about seven inch wide and the inside of the opening is about three foot two by three foot two.

Under it is a piece of slate the width of the shelf and an inch and a half thick. It was taken out of a house in Terrace Street twenty-eight years back, wrapped in straw and timber offcuts, and two men had it up against that north wall inside half an hour, and one of those two was the man who owns that yard and he has never once mentioned it to anybody.

It comes in at about three hundred and twenty pounds.

It is not a thing anybody in this borough has asked about, and it is not a thing anybody in this borough has told anybody else about, and it is not in anybody's mind on any day except the days that both of these two men have been in that building.

The woman who works in a shop was in that store in the second week of January, buying a bag of wall fixings, and it stood against that north wall then, and she looked at it for about as long as anybody looks at anything in a store, and she said nothing to the man who owns that yard then and she has not said anything about it since.

He was in that yard at about ten on that Saturday.

The man in his thirties was in there for a bag of cement, which is on the pallet against the east wall with the sand above it, and the man in his forties was in there for about twenty foot of scaffold tube, which is on a rack along the south wall and is not that shop's and is not anybody's in particular.

They did not come in together. The man in his thirties had been let in by a man who owns a shop on that Cleeve road and who has a key and who opened the door and stood in it and let him in and went, and the man in his forties let himself in at about ten with his own key, which he has had since about 2012 and has never asked for and which nobody has ever offered him and nobody has ever mentioned to him.

The man in his thirties was in and out of that building in about twenty minutes. He got his bag of cement, and he went to the rack along the south wall for a look at the tube to see if there was any that was not bent, which is a thing a man does and is not anything to do with what he came for.

The man in his forties was in there about ten minutes after that, and he came up the yard to the door of that store, and he was in there about half an hour, and the man in his thirties was not in it and was not on the yard and had gone up the street.

The man in his forties has a rule about that store and he made it a long time ago and he has never said it out loud to anybody.

It is not the rule he keeps about a bolt and a nut. That is a different one, and it is about leaving things where you found them.

This one is that nothing comes out of a locked room and into his own van, and he made it a long time before the church hall at the top of a street in the next borough had a thing taken out of it that was in a locked room, and he has never had the rule brought up because there is nobody to bring it up.

He has been in that store about four times a month for six years. He has never once gone in there for nothing. He has never once gone through that room past the north wall. He goes up the middle of it and down the middle of it and along the south rack and out, and he has gone past that thing about thirty times.

It cannot be carried down by two. It wants two underneath and a third up above them, and he has not got the two, and getting them means standing in a street saying out loud to three people that there is a marble surround at the far end of it worth having and about three hundred and twenty pounds of it.

He would have to say that out loud to somebody. He has nobody to say it to and he is not going about looking for one.

And underneath that, which is the part that has never moved and which he would not have said if he had been asked: it came out of a house. It stood in a room with a fire in it and a floor and a chimney and a family in it, and it is in a crate in a store now, and if he puts it in his own van then in a year somebody in this borough will be stood in a room looking at a gap in a chimney where it was, and he will know.

They passed each other at about twenty to twelve.

The man in his forties was coming out of that store with the tube under his arm and the man in his thirties was coming down the yard from the shop on the Cleeve road with a bag of cement on his shoulder, and one of them said, "You could stand that there," which is a thing you say to a man carrying that, and the other one said, "It'll go," and that was the whole of that as well.

The man in his thirties was on that yard at about half past three. The man in his forties was in that store at about half past three, getting a piece of batten out of the corner by the door that he had gone in for.

They passed each other again in the same place, and the man in his forties said, "Have you got a hand free," because the door of that store is heavy and it is on a ledge, and the man in his thirties came back up the yard and took the bottom of that door while the man in his forties got the batten out and shut it.

And that is all.

The man in his forties was the last one out of that yard on that Saturday.

He was out at about ten past four. He shut the plank door, and got the bar out of the way and dropped it into those two staples, and put the key back on the back of that door, and went up the yard to his van.

The marble chimney piece is against the north wall of that store, on its slate base, in line with the wall, and it has not been moved a sixteenth of an inch since about 1990.

The man in his forties will not take it, will not say that he cannot, and will raise it with nobody.

The man in his thirties is not going to take it either, and the whole of his reason is that he has nobody to hold a board, and he has no intention of being the one who goes looking, and this borough has nobody in it he could go to, and that is the whole of his reason and it is not going to change.

Neither of the two of them is ever going to find out what the other one gave as his reason.

---

**HE SHUT THE PLANK DOOR AND DROPPED THE BAR INTO THE TWO STAPLES AND PUT THE KEY BACK ON THE NAIL, AND FOUR FOOT OF MARBLE IS STILL STANDING AGAINST THE NORTH WALL OF THAT STORE AND NEITHER OF THOSE TWO MEN IS GOING BACK IN THERE FOR IT.**
