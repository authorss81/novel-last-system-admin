# Chapter 0434 — Nothing to Report

Tuesday the twenty-first of August 2018, from about four in the afternoon until about half past nine that night, and rather more than a hundred yard of that parade, in Tideglass.

**THAT PARADE WAS BUSIER ON THAT TUESDAY EVENING THAN IT HAS BEEN SINCE THE END OF MAY, THE SMALL THINGS ON IT WERE THE SMALL THINGS, ONE OF THEM WENT WRONG AT SOME TIME AFTERNOON AND WAS PUT RIGHT IN ABOUT NINETY SECONDS BY A WOMEN WHO SAID NOTHING TO ANYBODY, AND NINE PEOPLE WENT HOME ALONG IT.**

The parade is a bit over a hundred yard from the bank end to the shutter at the top and it is not a parade anybody comes to. It has a bank on it, a shop that shuts early, a shop that does not, a place with a counter in it, a lock-up with a roller shutter on it, and about nine other things, and the nine of them change according to the month and none of them is here for anybody else.

There is one pub on it that shut in the March and is now a place to get a key cut and a loaf of bread.



There is a lock-up at the top end, between the shop that shuts early and the shutter, with a roller shutter on it that has been up every day since about March.

There is a man in it. He is in there from about ten in the morning to about nine at night, and he has a bench in there and a table and a small heater and a radio, and he repairs things that are not worth repairing and sells a small amount of what comes off them.

On the Tuesday the twenty-first of August he was in there from about ten until about nine, and the shutter was up the whole of that time, and something over thirty people went past that opening in the course of the day and not one of them looked in.

Nobody on that parade knows what he does in there and nobody has asked and one woman behind a counter on that parade has said to a man in her forties that there is a man in that lock-up and he could not tell you one thing about him.

Nobody in this borough has spoken to that man in about seven months, and he has not asked anybody for anything, and nobody has knocked on that shutter, and he is not waiting for anybody and is not going to be asked anything.

---

Nine of them were out on that parade between about six and about nine that evening and not one of the nine was on it for any of the other eight.

A man went up at about ten past six with a carrier bag and came down at about ten past six with the bag empty. Two women came up together at about half past six, looked in the windows of the shop that does not shut, and went down again without going in. A man in a high-visibility jacket came up at about twenty to seven, stood outside the shutter for about a minute looking up at it as though it were a building he had not seen before, and went down the road. A woman came up at about ten to seven, went into the place to get a key cut, and was still inside at about eight. A man came up at about eight with a dog and stood at the top end for about ten minutes talking to nobody, and then went back down. A woman and a boy came up at about a quarter past eight and went in and came out. A man who works nights came up at about half past eight and went into the shop that shuts early and came out of it and went on up the road.

That is eight, and the other one is inside the lock-up with the shutter up, and he does not count because he never came out of it.

---

The mat outside the shop that does not shut is a coir mat about two foot by three foot and it belongs to the shop and it is put out on a Monday morning by a man who comes on the Monday with nine other things in a van and does not come again until the next Monday.

On the Tuesday evening of the twenty-first of August it was not on the step.

It was about six yard up the parade, in the gutter against the kerb, on its side, with the back of it up. There is a rubber backing on the back of it and there is a black line across that backing about a foot from one end, about two inch wide, which is the print of a tyre, and there is a smear of oil along the gutter beside it where it had been lying.

It had been on that step on the Monday. It was put there and nobody looked at it and nobody counted it and there is nobody in this borough who would know whether it was on that step on the Monday.

At about ten to five she came out through the front of her own shop in the middle of serving somebody, because the light outside had gone the colour that means it is going to rain and there was about an hour of it left in the day.

She saw it in the gutter from her own step.

She went up the parade at a fast walk, on the pavement, and got it up out of the gutter with two hands and put it up against her hip, and it is about the weight of a bag of coal.

She carried it back down and put it on her own step, in the right place, with the front of it where the front of it goes, and she put her hand down the face of the coir to take the grit off it, and she went back inside and finished serving the person she had walked out on.

She did not look for a tyre. She did not look for a van. She did not ask anybody at anybody's door.

Nobody in this borough can be told. The man who puts that mat out comes on a Monday morning with nine other things in a van and has no idea it is missing, and will never know it was ever off, and if it had stayed in that gutter until Thursday he would have seen it from about fifty yard on his way in and put it back himself without stopping. No one has ever put anything to him about that mat. He was not questioned on the Monday and nothing is going to be put to him, and there is nothing to put, because a mat on a step is a mat on a step and it was not and now it is and that is a thing that took ninety seconds and about two thirds of a minute of somebody's time and was not worth putting a word in anybody's notebook.



At about half past six that evening two men were standing outside the shutter of the lock-up at the top end of that parade, and one of them said the thing he says, which is that he knows what it is, and the other one said, "What?"

"You know what it is," said the first one.

"No I don't," said the second one.

"You do," said the first one, and then the two of them stopped talking, and one of them is the reason a fifth answer has not joined four others that have been going round this borough since the autumn before last.

There are four of those and there have been four of them for most of this year. One of them has been in far more mouths than the three others together and has been carried like that since about the October before last. None of the three others has been in a mouth since February. Not one of the four will sit next to another one of the four, for the plain reason that they are four answers to four different questions wearing the same coat, and everybody who has ever put two of them side by side has come away with nothing to set one against.

Nobody here has said out loud, this year or any year, what question any of them is an answer to. It has never been written down anywhere. On the twenty-first of August it was still only a question, and a question nobody on that parade was asking anybody.

Nobody in this borough holds the answer to it, or the question, or the day it was first asked. Nobody has taken any of the four back. Nobody has added a fifth.

In a building by that water there is a room with a shelf in it and a book standing open on the shelf, and the last line written in that book is the nineteenth, and there is a ruled space under the nineteenth that nobody has ever put anything in.

When the twenty-first of August came, that space had stood open for one thousand three hundred and sixty-four days, and that room was shut on that day and on the day before, and there is nothing under that line and nothing on the rest of the page either.

There is no twentieth line and there is a person in this borough who could write one and has not and was not asked to and has not been near that building since the end of March. Nobody went up that corridor on that Tuesday. Nobody put a figure on that space. It is the same page it was at the end of July and it will be the same page in March.

And there is one thing in this borough that nobody living in it could put into a sentence if you stopped them in the street and asked them to describe it, and it was there at the end of July and it is there now, and it has not been touched and nobody has mentioned it and it is not going to be mentioned by anybody.

**SHE WENT BACK INSIDE AND FINISHED SERVING THE PERSON SHE HAD WALKED OUT ON, AND THE MAT IS ON THAT STEP, AND THE LOCK-UP CAME DOWN AT NINE, AND NINE PEOPLE WENT HOME ALONG THAT PARADE.**
