# Chapter 0270 — The Bill

Thursday the twentieth of October 2016, from about ten in the morning, the second house of Marsh Lane in Tideglass.

**A FIGURE ON A PIECE OF PAPER WENT UP, AND NOTHING THAT HAPPENED IN THIS BOROUGH PUT IT THERE, AND SHE SAID SO OUT LOUD AT HER OWN KITCHEN TABLE, AND ONE MAN HEARD HER SAY IT.**

The man had come about the screen over the bath. The bath had been going without one since the house was built and had one now standing on end in the passage in three pieces with the tape still on the cardboard, and he had a tape measure out and the scribe block in his pocket, and he said the wall by the window was out by most of an inch, which is normal, and he said the wall by the door was a true one, which is a gift, and he said he would bring a scribe and a block of plastic on the Wednesday and be there about eight and would not be late.

While he was in the bathroom with the tape over his teeth she picked the envelope off the top of the boiler and sat down at the kitchen table with a cup of tea and her reading glasses, which are the ones from the chemist in the parade and not the ones from the shop.

It is a water bill. It has a date on it, an account number, a strip that has come off the back of the envelope where it was gummed, a standing charge, a rate per unit, and a printed line near the foot with a cross reference to a page and a page number and a free telephone number for anybody who wishes to query it.

The standing charge was up by about two pounds and something. The rate per unit was up by about two and a quarter pence. There was a line on it she had not seen on one of these before, about the supply having changed hands in the summer.

"That's nowt to do with us," she said, out loud, to the man in the passage, with her thumb on the bottom of the page, "and it's still got my name on it."

---

He came out with the tape in his mouth and put it on the table beside the bill, which he did not mean to do and did not pick up again.

"Who's us."

"Everybody on this street and the end of it and up the parade and over the bridge, and all of you, and the man who does the drains." She turned the bill round so he could see the line near the foot without picking it up. "They've had a takeover. That's what that is. There's four counties over the water on the same letterhead since about June and they've been going up one at a time. I did a tap in a house in Sarn in August and the woman had the same letter with the same thing on it and she'd brought it round to show me because she'd nobody else to show it to."

"So it's not this borough."

"It's got nothing to do with this borough and it's got nothing to do with me either, and I'm telling you because you've been shown the paper." He looked at it and nodded at the line. "That's the trade side of it is what you're looking at there is a house. The rate on a job out of the yard at Penhale has not moved since about nineteen eighty-seven and it is not going to, and that is a different sheet of paper with a different name at the top of it, and it has got nothing to do with you either, and I can hear you wanting to be sure of that."

"I wanted to be sure."

"You can be sure of it. That one's steady. That one hasn't moved since the year I was twenty. This one isn't steady and it isn't going to be, and there's nobody in this borough has any say in it at all, which is the part that will keep you up."

"I won't keep me up. I've been wrong once and I've been right once and I'd rather do neither again."

He got the tape and the pencil and started marking the tiles by the bath, and after a bit she said:

"I was right about a thing in June."

"Were you."

"About an hour, under the stairs, and a man off the river gone before four," she said. "And I was right, and I said the date before anybody asked me for it, and I have had it since, and I have not been wrong about a single thing since, and that is the best run of my life and it is not a run that anybody would put on a card."

"Now you've been right about a water bill."

"Now I've been right about a water bill," she said, "and I would rather not have been. There is a man up this street who did the drains and his legs are about what they were in February and he is not getting them back, and back would be four, and he knows it and so does everybody on this street, and there was nobody to ring about that either and there was nobody to ring about this. So when I see a number go up on a piece of paper I want very much to be a woman who is wrong about it. That is all. That is the whole of the want."

"Can't be," said the man. "Somebody's bought them and the bill has got to go up and the meter does not care whose name's on it, and there is no version of this where you ring them and it comes back down, because they will tell you they didn't, and you will believe them, because they are telling you the truth."

"I know they didn't," she said. "That's why I said it out loud and not to myself."

---

He had two of the tiles marked and the scribe block held on the bath and was washing his hands when she said:

"There's a number on the bottom of it for anybody who wants to query it."

"There is."

"Should I ring it."

"Who'd you ring?"

She sat with that for a second and then said, "I don't know."

"You've a telephone."

"I've had a telephone since the spring," she said, "and I have not had one call on it that anybody wanted. The last one was a man about a survey of a wall in June and I said no to that one as well, and it was the only one I ever said no to, and it took me a fortnight to say it."

He wrote the date on the outside of the three screen pieces in marker so that she would know which way round they went, and told her Wednesday was about eight and that he would not be late, and he was gone by a quarter to twelve and shut the door on the latch, which is the only door on that street that shuts properly.

The bill was on the table when he left and it was on the table at two o'clock and it was on the table at six, and it had not gone into the folder with the others, and the standing charge on it was up, and the cross reference to the page was correct, and the number at the foot of it was a free one.

**AND THE SCREEN LEANED AGAINST THE PASSAGE WALL IN THREE PIECES WITH A DATE WRITTEN ON THE BACK OF EACH ONE IN MARKER, AND THE MARKER WAS HER OWN OUT OF THE SIDEBOARD, AND THE KETTLE WENT ON AT SIX AND SHE SAT DOWN AT THE TABLE AGAIN AFTERWARDS.**
