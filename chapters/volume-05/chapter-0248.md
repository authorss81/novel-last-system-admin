# Chapter 0248 — Back Down

Sunday the twenty-sixth of June 2016, from about half past eleven in the morning, a shop on a parade of shops in Tideglass, a trade estate four miles out, and a kitchen in Marsh Lane.

**THE PRICE CAME BACK DOWN AND IT CAME BACK DOWN FOR THE SAME REASON IT WENT UP, AND THE SAME REASON IS THAT A CREW OF THIS BOROUGH WENT SOMEWHERE AND CAME BACK, AND NOBODY DID ANYTHING, AND NOBODY IS A HERO AND NOBODY IS A VILLAIN, AND THE ONLY THING THAT GOES UP AND DOWN AROUND HERE IS A TUESDAY.**

Marsh Lane is off Peverell Street and the woman in the second house had been doing two trips in a hired van for a fortnight, and had paid a Tuesday price for a van on a half-mile of road in order to do it in one, and on the Sunday she did it in one.

"A hundred and eighteen," she said. "Back to a hundred and eighteen. Same van, same forecourt, same half-mile, and I rang them on Thursday and it came down about what it was, and I've been sitting here since Saturday morning waiting to feel pleased about it and I don't."

"Why don't you feel pleased about it."

"Because I'm not paid for feeling pleased," she said. "And because I'm the only one who got that bill. Everybody in this borough who's been hit by it was hit through somebody else's invoice and the woman in Marsh Lane is the only person in this borough who's seen the number, and I'm telling you the number came down the day the boat came back and I had nothing to do with the boat coming back."

She put the receipt on the table, face up, the way people put a receipt down when they have already had this conversation with themselves about six times.

"And it came down because the same thing that put it up has stopped," she said. "That's the bit. It went up because we gave a crew to somebody and it has come down because we gave a crew to somebody, and that is the same crew, and there is not one single thing anybody in this borough has done that has made it either way, and if anybody comes round and tells me they got it down for me, you tell them where to put it."

---

The trade counter at the back of the shop on the parade had the lamp on and Hanna Wray had a delivery docket and a roll and the invoice spike, and Jonas Mercer stood at the end of it because that is where he stands now.

"Penhale's smaller."

"Is it."

"It's a smaller book," said Hanna Wray. "Not much smaller. The size of the account is down about a sixth off where it was in April, and I could not tell you a single one of the men who caused that, and if you asked me in March I'd have told you the same thing about a different size. Everything else on that book is the same and the price on everything else is the same, and that's the sentence I gave a man in a good coat on a Sunday in March and I gave it to him before he'd finished asking."

"What's the rate on it."

"There is a rate. It's on a board on the shelf behind you and I have not read it and I am not going to read it for a man standing at my counter." She turned the roll round. "What I have is a book where the biggest account got smaller and nobody has been sacked and nobody has come and told me anything, and that's the twenty-sixth of June and I want it written down that I said in May it would come back to about what it was by the middle of June and that I did not do one thing to make it."

"You did say."

"I said it to my own counter. That's the audience I've got."

A man came in at about half past twelve and put four feet of wire and a length of lead on the counter and paid for them and then did not go.

"I'm the drain man," he said. "Same as last time. Everybody knows what I am."

"You've had your book back."

"I've had about half of it back," he said. "That's the honest version and I'd rather say it than have somebody say it for me in September. I had four streets to myself for nine years. When they came in in May I went from four streets to two and a half, and I said out loud in this shop that I'd not be having it, and I was right and it happened, and the price they went out to was fair and their work was decent and one of them is a nicer man than me. Since the boat's been back, one of them has stopped coming out, because the lad who does the other street has gone to work on a job out of town and his cousin's taken it. So I've got two and three-quarter streets."

"Are you back."

"I'm back to about what I had in February," said the drain man. "I am not back. Back would be four. And that's the part of this I don't get back, and it isn't anybody's fault and nobody's done me wrong, and there is no arrangement in this county that gives me that street, and I'm not going to be one of the two that starts saying so out loud in a hall on a Sunday, because a man who says that is a man who has started going in."

He picked his change up and went out.

The trade estate is four miles out and it is about half an hour in a car at that time of a Sunday, and three of the four companies are on it, and Jonas Mercer drove out to it because going out to it is what he does now.

At Sarn Aggregates there is a weighbridge and a man on it who has been on it a long time and who keeps a kettle in a hut, and he had been asked the same question twice in the spring by a man in a good coat and answered it the same way both times.

"Never turned a job away," he said. "Twenty years. Not one, not for anybody, not for a reason. We're lighter and we're not worried. The price goes with the work, that's the whole of the trade, and you don't need me to tell you what that means because you had it off a man in a cabin in March and then you had it off four of us and then you had it off a woman in a kitchen, and that's five people, and there isn't anybody in this county left to tell you."

"That's the fifth time."

"That's the fifth time and it's still true," said the man on the weighbridge, and put the kettle on.

---

The forecourt card on the same estate said MARCHMONT and the price per litre on it was about a tenth above what the shared yard pays, and it had been repainted twice, and the man in the office by the gate read it out to anybody who asked and put it straight afterwards, and nobody had asked him since the first of May.

Then Lowdale, in a cabin, and the man in it was doing the same thing he has done for four months, which is not to answer.

"You've come for it again."

"I've not come for it," said Jonas Mercer. "I came for a Monday price."

"There's no Monday price, that's not a thing, there's what the book says." The man turned the light down on the desk, which is what he does. "But I'll tell you what there is, since you were honest with me in March when you were owed an honest answer. It's back. Not all of it. The plant went out Friday and came back Sunday and the van came back with it, and the week before that it came back Thursday and the week before that it came back when it did. The same crews, my vans, the same round, and the only difference is that they came back in the borough, and I could not tell you what I would do differently if they hadn't."

"You said there was no blood in it."

"There's no blood in it," said the man in the cabin. "If there was blood in it I'd charge more. I have wanted blood in it since March. I would like to be able to say that a week like that one in May cost me something, because a man who can say that has a thing, and I have not got a thing, I've got a diary."

**AND A VAN WENT OUT ON A FRIDAY AND CAME BACK ON A SUNDAY AND THE WOMAN IN MARSH LANE PAID WHAT SHE PAID IN FEBRUARY AND THE DRAIN MAN GOT BACK ABOUT HALF OF WHAT HE LOST AND WILL NOT GET THE REST, AND NOBODY ON THAT PARADE SAID ANYTHING ABOUT ANY OF IT, AND HANNA WRAY WENT BACK TO THE ROLL.**
