# Chapter 0602 — She Made an Arrangement for Somebody to Be Told

The Monday of the second of November 2020, from about one in the afternoon until about half past five in the afternoon, in a house in this borough.

**SHE ARRANGED IT, AND THE THING SHE WAS ARRANGING HAD BEEN TOLD TO HER ON A WEDNESDAY IN THE MIDDLE OF OCTOBER, AND SHE HAS SPENT THE WEEK SINCE MAKING IT GOOD ENOUGH TO DO, AND THIS AFTERNOON THE THIRD PERSON SHE PUT IN THAT ROOM ON PURPOSE HAS BEEN TOLD SOMETHING THAT WAS ALSO TOLD IN THAT CAR PARK ON THE WEDNESDAY OF THE FOURTEENTH OF OCTOBER BY A MAN IN HIS MID TWENTIES, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO WHO TOLD HER KNOWS ABOUT THE OTHER.**

She is a woman in her late forties. The man who is being told is a man in his early sixties. The man who told her first is a man in his mid twenties. The woman she put in the room is a woman in her early sixties and is the man's daughter and knows nothing about any part of it.

The thing she is arranging to have him told is that his landlord has put the rent up again in the new year.

---

Here is what happened on the Wednesday of the fourteenth of October, and it is worth setting out in sequence because the whole of the trouble is in the sequence.

The man in his mid twenties is the son of the woman in her early sixties. He is in a car park behind a supermarket on that Wednesday evening at about half past seven and the man in his early sixties is in the passenger seat of his car and they have been in that car park for about ten minutes with the engine off, waiting for the third man who is not coming.

And the man in his mid twenties says, without any preamble and out of the ordinary way people talk in a car, "He's put it up again."

And the man in his early sixties says, "Who?"

And the man in his mid twenties says, "Mr Adeyemi. It's in the letter. Another hundred and twenty from the January."

And the man in his early sixties says nothing for about a minute.

And then he says, "Right."

And then he says, "What will it be, then."

And the man in his mid twenties says, "About nine hundred."

And the man in his early sixties says, "Right."

And then he laughs at nothing, in the way a man does, and the other one does not look at him.

That is the whole of what was said in that car park and it took about four minutes and neither man thought anything of it.

Here is what the woman in her late forties did about it, and she was not in that car park and does not know that it happened.

She found out on Friday last, at about ten past one in the afternoon, on the telephone, from the man in his early sixties himself, and the reason she found out is that she rang him about something entirely different, which was the guttering.

He said, "Can I ring you back, I'm just going in."

And she said, "Go on, then."

And he rang back at about twenty past two and she said what she had rung about and he said, "It's not been done, I've rung them twice," and she said, "You want to go round and see them," and he said, "I know."

And she said, "Will you?"

And then she said, "You're all right?"

And he said, "Yes."

And she said, "You don't sound it."

And he said, "I've had a letter off the landlord."

And she said, "About what?"

And he said, "The rent."

And she said, "The rent's gone up?"

And he said, "About a hundred and twenty from the January. It's in the letter."

And she said, "Right."

And there was a pause of about four seconds.

And he said, "I'll ring you back."

And he hung up on it, and she sat in her own kitchen with the phone on the arm of the chair for about two minutes.

Here is the whole of the arrangement, and it took her the best part of a week to make, and she made it carefully and competently and out of genuine regard for him.

What she decided was that he must not be told on his own.

That is not a strange decision and it is not a dramatic one. It is what people do. He is a man in his early sixties who has lived in the same house since about 1998 and has a daughter in the next borough and no wife, and a hundred and twenty pounds is not a large sum of money to a person and it is a great deal to a man who is in that sort of situation, and she is not going to be the one who puts a letter on a table in front of him by herself on a Friday afternoon.

So she did three things over the weekend and last night.

She rang him on Sunday afternoon and asked him what he was doing. He said he was not doing anything. She said, "Right, I'm coming round," and he said, "Right," and did not say why.

She rang his daughter on Friday and asked her to come round this afternoon. She did not say why. She said, "There's a bit of paperwork I want your dad to look at with me." And the daughter in her early sixties said, "Right, I'll come about two."

And on Friday night she wrote three things on a sheet of paper in her own hand. The first was the date the letter came. The second was what the new figure would be, which she worked out herself off the rent book, and it was just under nine hundred, and she got that from a receipt and not from him. The third was the date it starts, which was the first of January. She put the three things down in that sequence because she had decided, over ten days of thinking about it, that the sequence was the whole of it, and that a man in his early sixties can take a date and a figure and a date and cannot take a conversation.

She put the sheet in her bag on the Friday night. She has not shown it to anybody and she has not read it since.

Here is what happened this afternoon.

His daughter came at about a quarter past two. She is a woman in her early sixties and she came because she was asked to and she did not know.

The woman in her late forties came at about half past two. He made tea. The three of them sat in the front room, which is where the whole of that house happens.

She said it at about ten past three and she had the sheet of paper in her hand and she did not read from it.

She said, "I've had a think about the letter."

He said, "Right."

She said, "You got it on the Friday. It came from Mr Adeyemi, and it's a hundred and twenty from the January."

He said, "A hundred and thirty, actually."

And that is the whole of the afternoon, and everything after that is detail.

She nodded and looked at the sheet, and she said, "My figure's just under nine hundred off the rent book, but that was last year, so it'll be more."

And he said, "It'll be a bit over nine."

She wrote it on the sheet while he watched. And she said, "It starts on the first of January."

And he said, "It does."

And his daughter said, "Dad?"

And he said, "It's all right."

And his daughter said, "You're not getting rid of us over that."

And he said, "I'm not going to be all right, love, but I'm not going to be all right about a lot of things, and this is not one I'd have been all right about either."

And his daughter said, "Well."

And the woman in her late forties said, "There's a page. I wrote it down."

And she put the sheet on his knee and he picked it up and read it, and it was the three things in the sequence she had decided on.

And he said, "You've had a good go at that."

And she said, "I thought I would."

And he said, "You've had a very good go at that."

And his daughter said, "Is there a page for me?"

And the woman in her late forties said, "No."

And his daughter said, "Right," and sat back.

---

Here is what neither of the two who told him knows about the other.

What the man in his early sixties has not been told, and cannot be told, is that his son already gave him the whole of it in that car park on the Wednesday of the fourteenth of October. He was not going to tell her, because the man in his mid twenties is young and does not know what he did, and because a man in his early sixties does not tell a woman in her late forties that his landlord has put it up again by hearing it from his own son in a car park with the engine off.

And he did not know she was going to say it. He has known about the letter since the Friday and has decided, on his own, that he was not going to ring anybody about it, and had gone as far as working out what he would do, which was to move in the June, which he can afford and which he does not want.

She does not know that anything was said in that car park. She has not seen the man in his mid twenties since the August and does not know what he does on a Wednesday evening or who he is in a car park with.

And his daughter, who was in the room, has heard one version of it, and she has not heard the other, and she does not know that there is another, and she would not have recognised it if she had.

**SHE PUT HIS DAUGHTER IN THAT ROOM ON PURPOSE AND SAID IT PROPERLY OFF A SHEET OF PAPER IN THE SEQUENCE SHE HAD CHOSEN, AND HE HAD BEEN TOLD IT A FORTNIGHT EARLIER IN A CAR PARK BY A YOUNG MAN WHO THOUGHT HE WAS TALKING ABOUT NOTHING, AND HE SAID THE FIGURE WAS A HUNDRED AND THIRTY, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO WHO TOLD HIM KNEW ABOUT THE OTHER, AND HE IS NOT GOING TO RING ANYBODY.**
