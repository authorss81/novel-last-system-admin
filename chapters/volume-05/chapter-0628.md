# Chapter 0628 — The Joke He Told On Purpose

Monday the fifteenth of March 2021. It is in this borough, and it runs from about a quarter past eight in the morning in a yard off a road of houses to about ten at night in a front room in the same road, and all of it happens on one washing line.

**A MAN IN HIS EARLY THIRTIES HAS SAID A REASON OUT LOUD THREE TIMES WITHOUT BEING ABLE TO REMEMBER SAYING IT FIRST, AND ON THIS DAY HE DECIDES TO SAY IT A FOURTH TIME TO A WOMAN WHO DID NOT ASK HIM FOR IT, AND WHEN SHE ASKS HIM WHY HE IS TELLING HER HE SAYS IT IS A JOKE, AND IT IS NOT A JOKE, AND HE IS USING HER.**

He is a man in his early thirties. He has the top floor of a house in this borough that has a woman in her mid forties in the bottom of it, and she has been in the house longer than he has and there are two doors off the hall and the stair is the same stair for both of them.

The washing is hers and his on one line and it has been that way since the summer he came.

---

Here is what is wrong with him, and he has known it since before the middle of the week and has not said it to anybody.

There is a reason he has been giving. It is short and it is about a bin and he could not describe it to you if you asked him, but it has got a shape.

He does not know where he got it from.

That is the whole of the trouble and he has been round it four times.

One: it is not his. He has never had a bin and he has never asked anybody for anything about a bin, and he has not lived on a road with a row of houses at the end of it.

Two: it arrived as a whole thing. Nobody said it in pieces in front of him. It came out of somebody's mouth complete, with the shape already on it, the way a word like *anyway* comes out of a mouth, and he took it and used it without any part of him stopping.

Three: he has said it three times, and the three times are the reason he cannot put his hand on the first one, because the first one has been eaten by the other two.

He said it in a kitchen at work in the week before last, to a man who was complaining about a supplier, and the man said *fair enough* and carried on, and he did not think about it again until the Tuesday.

He said it in a queue at the shop on the Saturday, to a woman he does not know, who was complaining about the rain and about something in her foot, and she said *that's right* and got served, and he did not think about it again until the Monday.

And then on the Friday he heard himself say it for the third time inside a matter of weeks, to a man in a doorway, and he heard it come out of his own mouth in his own voice, and he stood outside a launderette and worked out that he had done it.

And he has a reason in his mouth that is not his and that he has handed out three times to people who did not ask for it.

He cannot find where the first one was. He has been through it and there is nothing there. There is a work kitchen, and a queue at a shop, and a street with a launderette, and no door.

Here is what he decided, and it is worth setting down in his own words because he has not been able to say it out loud and he wrote it on the back of a fuel receipt and then took the receipt out to the bin and put it in and then took it out again.

On the Monday morning he wrote: *I am going to say it to her.*

And under that: *She tells everybody.*

And under that: *So it will go out of here and I will find out.*

And under that, at about half past eight in the morning with the tap running: *And I know it is not true, so I am going to lie to a woman who does my washing for nothing.*

And then he did not write anything else, and he read the four lines about six times over the morning, and he went to work, and he came home at about seven in the evening.

The washing was on the line when he came in and she was in the yard taking hers off.

He stood in the doorway with his bag on his shoulder and watched her take four sheets off and fold them over her arm and then he said it.

He said: "There's a fella down the road can't get his bin out, and he never asks."

And she said, "Does he."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Well, that's about right."

And he said, "Aren't you."

That is how it goes.

Here is what she did with it, and he had worked out beforehand that she would do exactly this and had worked it out at about ten past ten in the morning while he was loading a van and had not told her a thing.

She folded another sheet and she said, "You've said that before."

And he said, "Have I."

And she said, "You said it about a year ago."

And he said, "Who to."

And she said, "Me."

And he said, "Did I."

And she said, "You were on about your laundry and you were complaining about a woman at the far end of the row and you said she never asks for anything."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "Well, you've been at it a while."

And he said, "About a year."

And she said, "Longer than that."

And he said, "Have I."

And she said, "I've a memory like a tap."

He stood in the doorway with his bag on his shoulder and had got nothing.

Not because she did not answer. She answered everything, promptly, and cheerfully, and did not once ask him a question back, and that was the arrangement and she keeps it well.

Because the answer he wanted was a room with a door in it. A woman at the shop, or a man in a kitchen at work, or somebody on a bus, and a point in the week where the sentence came from and a mouth it came out of.

And what he got was a woman folding sheets who told him he has been saying it about as long as they have lived here.

He put his bag down in the hall and she carried the washing up and he followed her, and that was a mistake and he has thought so since, because he would not have had to say anything at all.

She put it on the table in the front room and she said, "There's a bit of blue in yours."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "It's not come out."

And he said, "It's a dark blue."

And she said, "It's come out of mine."

And he said, "I'll get something for it."

And she said, "I haven't said anything about it."

And he said, "I know."

And she said, "Right," and went out, and shut the door behind her, which she does.

And he stood in the front room.

Here is what he worked out between about half past seven and about nine, and he has it in order and it is not complicated.

One: it worked.

She is on the telephone at about half past seven to a woman at number thirty and he can hear the whole of it through the floor and she says *there's a lad at the top who's been going on about a fella down the road that can't get his bin out* and the woman at number thirty says something he cannot hear.

And at about ten past eight she is on again to somebody else and she says *he never asks* and he can hear that as well.

And he stands in a front room listening to a woman he has never paid use a sentence he has made up, and it is going out of this house into this road, and by Wednesday there will be people on this road he has never spoken to who have got it.

Two: it worked, and there is nothing in this borough that will tell him whether it is his sentence coming round again or a different one, and he has known that since he wrote the four lines out this morning and wrote it anyway.

Three: and this is the one that has taken the evening and it arrived at about nine when he went to the door to see whether the light was still on in her back room.

She has washed his things for a consideration of nothing since the summer he came. She has never mentioned it. She has never once mentioned it, and that is not the same as never having thought about it, and he has not thought about it either until about nine o'clock on a Monday evening.

And he has just used her.

Not for a lift, not for a parcel, not for a letter that would not reach a postbox. He has taken a woman who does his washing because two machines is silly, and who has never asked him for anything, and who tells things because she is that sort and always has been and he has relied on it, and he has put a false reason into her mouth on purpose and written it on the back of a receipt and stood in a doorway and waited for it to go.

She does not know. She will never know. She will go on telling it on this road for as long as she tells anything, and it will arrive back at him one morning in a shop or at the bottom of this road in the mouth of a woman who has no idea it started in a doorway of this house with a man who owes her nothing and has just decided he does.

And he has got no way of stopping it. He could tell her. He could go down and say I made it up this evening and I did it on purpose to see if it came back, and she would believe him, and she would stop saying it, because she would want to know what she has been saying and why a man would want his bin story going.

And if he does that, then in a month or so she will have decided between the two of them which one he is.

And there is no version of the evening in which he gets to keep being a man who is all right to live on top of a woman who does his washing.

He stood in the hall with his hand on the stairs and he went up.

---

At about ten he went down to the kitchen for a glass of water and she was in there with the iron and the wireless on.

And she said, "You don't have to say sorry."

And he said, "I didn't say anything."

And she said, "You've been up there a good while."

And she said, "It's the blue."

And he said, "I'll get something for it."

And she said, "You said that an hour ago."

And he said, "I'll get something."

And she said, "I don't want anything for it."

And she said, "What are you sorry about."

And he said, "The washing."

And she said, "You'd be surprised what comes out."

And he said, "It wasn't about the washing."

And she said, "I know it wasn't."

And she said, "I did think about it, though, while you were up there."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "Go on."

And she said, "What have you been doing up there."

And he said, "Thinking."

And she said, "About the bin."

And he said, "About the bin."

And she said, "Why are you telling me about a bin on a Monday night."

And he said, "It was a joke."

And she said, "It wasn't a joke."

And he said, "It was."

And she said, "It was about something and it's not about a bin."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "What makes you think it wasn't."

And she said, "Because I did the same thing once."

And he said, "What."

And she said, "Years back. I had a thing I used to say about a fella who never asked anybody for anything and it wasn't about a fella."

And he said, "What was it about."

And she said, "I'd rather not say and I'd rather you didn't ask."

And he said, "I won't."

And she said, "That's the first thing you've said tonight that was true."

And he said, "I know."

And she said, "All right."

And he said, "What was the fella like."

And she said, "You're doing it now."

And he said, "I'll go up."

And she said, "Go up."

**HE PUT A REASON INTO A WOMAN'S MOUTH ON PURPOSE BECAUSE SHE TELLS EVERYBODY ON THAT ROAD, AND HE STOOD IN A DOORWAY AND LET HER TAKE IT AND DID NOT WARN HER, AND SHE HAS BEEN WASHING HIS THINGS FOR NOTHING SINCE THE SUMMER HE CAME, AND SHE TOLD HIM HE HAD SAID THE SAME THING ABOUT A YEAR AGO AND HE HAS NOT GOT A ROOM WITH A DOOR IN IT, AND AT TEN O'CLOCK HE SAID IT WAS A JOKE AND SHE SAID IT WAS NOT A JOKE.**
