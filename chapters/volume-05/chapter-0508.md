# Chapter 0508 — The Thing Is Still Where He Put It

The Sunday of the twenty-eighth of July 2019, from about a quarter to twelve in the morning until about six in the afternoon. A street of twenty-two houses and the front door of one of them, in Tideglass.

**SOMETHING SMALL CAME OFF A PORCH WALL IN THE FIRST QUARTER OF 1998 AND WENT ONTO A LEDGE IN THAT MAN'S OWN HOUSE AND IS STILL ON IT, AND HE HAS SPOKEN OF IT ONCE ONLY, TO A MAN WHO HAS BEEN DEAD SINCE 2011, AND IN THE LAST WEEK OF JULY A WOMAN CAME TO HIS DOOR ABOUT SOMETHING ELSE ENTIRELY, AND HE ANSWERED HER TRUTHFULLY AND NOT ABOUT THAT, AND THE BELL IS STILL ON THAT LEDGE.**

He has lived at number four in that street since 1989 and he was in the building trade until about 2005 and after that he did what he calls the jobs nobody wants.

He does not answer the door for anybody he does not know. He has not answered the door for anybody he does not know since about 2013, with one exception.


The bell is painted. It is about five inch tall and it is a hand bell with a handle and the paint on it is a green that has gone the colour of a swimming pool in about 1997.

He took it in the week of the second of March 1998.

What he was doing at the time was a fortnight on a job at the front of a building near the top of that street, and the bell was screwed to a plate in the porch of a building two doors off, and there was no bell in the building by then and there had not been one since about 1979, and it was screwed through a plate into brick.

It came off with a screwdriver and four minutes and it took the plate with it and he put the plate back and made good with a filler that is still visible.

He carried it home under his arm at about half past five on the Thursday and put it on the ledge in the back room at about nine foot above the floor, and that ledge is still there and there is nothing on it.

That is twenty-one years and a bit over four months.


He told one person.

He told the man he worked with, in 2003, in a parked car at the top of that street, because that man was the only person he knew who would not say anything.

He did not say why. It came out sideways, which is how it goes when a thing has been sitting in a man for years. There is a thing in his house that came off a wall in a porch in 1998. It is on a ledge. Nobody else in the world knows.

And that man said, "Right."

And then he said, "Is it worth anything?"

And he said, "No."

That man died on the fourth of May 2011, and had not been seen since about January of that year.

Since then he has not been looking for anybody to tell. That is not the same thing as not wanting to and he has been through the difference four or five times without getting anywhere.

What he has is the arithmetic of it. Twenty-one years, and a single conversation in a parked car that lasted about ninety seconds, and one person who could have asked him to do something about it and never asked him anything.


And on the Sunday of the twenty-eighth of July a woman knocked.

All of that took about nine minutes at his front door and there was not one other thing said in it.

She is at number nineteen and has lived there since 2014, and there is a wall between her garden and the boundary of number four that has been leaning a bit since about 2016.

What she wanted was to know whether he would mind if she put two courses on the top of it, because her man had been up a ladder about four times looking at it and had said something about it in the summer.

And he said, "I wouldn't mind."

And she said, "That's all right, then. I've said to the man next door as well and he said he'd rather I didn't."

And he said, "It's not mine."

And she said, "No, but you're on this side of it."

And he said, "That's about the size of it."

And she said, "Right. I'll leave you then."

And that was the whole of the conversation and it took about nine minutes, because she does not leave anybody's front step quickly.


And here is the part that is this chapter, and he has not been able to move it out of his head since.

She was on his step at about half past two and she said, "You'll be all right on that wall."

And he said, "It isn't mine."

And she said, "You've said that."

And then she looked at the side of the house while she was getting her bag in, the way people do.

And she said, "Is that a bell? Up there."

And he said, "Yes."

And he said it straight away, without a pause, without going in, without doing anything with his face, and it came out of him the way a thing comes out of you when you have answered the same question about a thing in your own head several thousand times and had planned what your voice would do.

She said, "Oh. Right."

And she said, "I thought that was a bit of a bell. I thought it was like a shop thing."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "My auntie had one of those. On a nail."

And she said, "Sorry. I was just looking at your house."

And he said, "It's all right."

And she said, "I'll leave you, then, before you shut me out."

And she went down the path, and he shut the door, and he stood where she had been standing for about two minutes.


What he has taken from that is not that she found anything out. She found out nothing. She thought it was a shop bell and thought it was like her auntie's and thought she had said something wrong.

What he has taken from it is the four seconds before he said yes.

Because in those four seconds he went through the whole of it — the porch, the plate, the filler that is still visible, the parked car, the man who has been dead since 2011, twenty-one years and four months of a ledge with nothing on it until about 2003 and something on it since then — and what he found at the end of it was that he did not tell her because he had already decided not to, in about the first half second, and had spent the rest of it being surprised that he had decided as quickly as that.

He has been thinking about that all afternoon. He has been thinking about the speed of it.

What he took out of that porch in 1998 was worth about four pence, and he did not know that when he did it, and nobody in this borough has ever been under the impression that he took anything.

And the speed of his own answer on a Sunday afternoon in July 2019 is about four seconds, and he has had all afternoon and most of the evening to work out why a man answers a stranger at his door that fast.

What he has got to, and he does not think it is right and has not decided that it is wrong, is that it is not the bell he answered quickly.

It is that if he had said no, he would have had to explain, and if he had explained, there would have been a stranger in his house in 1998 in his own mind, standing where that man stood, and he does not want that man to have been standing there.


And here is the thing about the ledge that has nothing to do with any of it.

Nobody in that street has ever seen it. It is nine foot up in a back room that he is in for about ten minutes a week, in a house on a street of twenty-two where everybody knows where everybody's laundry pole is and nobody knows what is in the back rooms.

It is not hidden. He has never once thought of hiding it. He wipes the dust off it at the turn of the year because he wipes that ledge, and there is nothing else on that ledge, and there is nothing on that ledge that has come off a wall in a porch.

There is a space on the ledge about five inches wide at the left-hand end of it, and the ledge is about two foot, and he knows how wide the space is and he could tell anybody to the inch where the bell stands on it.

He said in 2003, in that car, that there is a thing in his house that came off a wall in a porch in 1998. He did not say which wall. He did not say which porch. He did not say the street.

Nobody has ever asked him and nobody is going to.


At about half past three he went into the back room and looked at the ledge and did not touch it. Then he came out and shut that door, which he does not usually do, and sat in the front room with the radio on for about two hours.


**A WOMAN AT NUMBER NINTEEN STOOD ON THE FRONT STEP OF NUMBER FOUR ON THE SUNDAY OF THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF JULY 2019 AND LOOKED UP AT THE BACK OF A HOUSE AND SAID WAS THAT A BELL, AND HE SAID YES.**
