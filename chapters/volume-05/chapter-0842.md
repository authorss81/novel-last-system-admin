# Chapter 0842 — Pads Laid Flat

Thursday the seventh of September 2023, in the kitchen at number nine from morning until the fridge hums in a quiet house, and pads curled in a door is the whole of that Thursday.

**THERE ARE PADS IN THE FRIDGE DOOR AT NUMBER NINE CURLED AT THE EDGES AND SHE MEANS TO LAY THEM FLAT WITHOUT REPLACING THEM, AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING WAITS AT THE KERB WITH HER BAGS AND DOES NOT STEP OFF IT.**

Those pads are the rubber mats in the fridge door, three of them, put in to keep bottles from sliding, gone hard at the rims with age and curled up at the corners.

The curl lifts one corner of each pad by half a finger, enough to rock a bottle and let it walk with the hum of the fridge through the night.

She does not replace them. New pads want money and a trip, and old pads gone hard still do their job once they lie, and lying is done with warmth and weight and not with shops.

Laying flat is done with books for weight and the warmth of the door they go back into.

She takes the pads out first and lays them on the table. Dust sits under each one in a pale outline, with a sticky ring where milk stood once, and she wipes the shelf below them clean before anything else.

The shelf below is enamel, marked with rings, and under the dust it is sound. She wipes it over with a dry cloth and the dust comes off it grey, so the pads go back to a clean face.

The curl is in the corners and not in the middle. Rubber gone hard keeps the shape it dried in, and these dried with the corners up after a wash in spring when they were left to dry on the line.

Warmth takes the memory out of rubber without melting it, and there is one warm place in a kitchen and it is not the table. She works each corner down with the flat of her hand, lays the pad flat, and stands a paperback on it, and the corner stays where she put it.

The woman who does her shopping waits at the kerb through the middle of the morning with two bags, calling once that there is no hurry.

“Leave the bags there, I am coming down,” she tells her from the door.

“I am fine here till you are through,” the woman tells her, and stays on the kerb side with the bags at her feet, out of the way of the path.

The kerb is the one outside number nine, and the woman waits out by the road with the bags at her feet through the whole of it, and the fridge is never spoken of.

She thinks about the pads while they sit under books, how rubber keeps the shape of whatever it last dried on. Left on a line with corners up, they learned to stand. Pressed flat under paperbacks with the door shut on them, they will learn to lie. Things take the shape of their last rest, and a wise person chooses the rest with care.

The kitchen smells of the faint sour of old milk from the rings and of the dust off the shelf, and the fridge hums on through it all, patient as an animal asleep.

Weight does the rest. Each pad goes back in the door flat with a book stood against it to keep the corner down, and an hour with the door closed finishes what the hand started. The books are two paperbacks off the shelf, stood on end, doing nothing except being heavy.

She leaves them an hour and takes them away. The pads stay. Bottle in is the test, and a bottle set on each pad stands without walking when the door shuts.

She wipes the door and the shelf below once more, and puts the bottles back in a row.

---

The cloth goes over the rail, and the paperbacks stand back on the shelf, and the pads stay where they are.

She stands in the quiet kitchen when the fridge hums and looks at what is there. Pads lying flat inside the fridge door, curled still a hair at the edges where age keeps them, and below them the shelf, wiped clean with the rings showing through.
