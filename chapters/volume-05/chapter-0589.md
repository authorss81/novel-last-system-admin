# Chapter 0589 — It's Not Like That

The Thursday of the twenty-seventh of August 2020, from about half past six in the morning until about half past nine in the evening, at a terrace of houses in this borough.

**HE SAID A SENTENCE TO HER IN 2019 THAT MEANT ONE THING AND SHE SAID THE SAME SENTENCE TO HIM THIS MORNING AND IT MEANT SOMETHING ELSE, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS FOUND OUT THAT THE OTHER ONE HEARD THEIR OWN.**

The terrace is eight houses and about half a century old and it runs down off a road with a garage at the top. The man is in his early forties and the woman is in her late thirties. Together since 2009, a daughter of nine, and not one argument in front of her about anything that mattered.

He goes out at about half past six and does the recycling and the empties and the two doors he has to drag and comes in at about ten to seven. She is already up.

The whole of the rest of that day is ordinary and stays ordinary, and the two of them are in it together from seven in the morning, and neither of them puts anything on the table that is not on the table.

---

The sentence is: "It's not like that."

It is not an unusual sentence. It is what a person says in about nine situations out of ten where one of them has just said something that is not quite right, and the person saying it is not accusing anybody of anything. It is the sentence you use when you want a conversation to stop.

She said it. That is the thing. In 2019 it was her, and this morning it was her once more, and it went out of her mouth the same way both times, at the same speed, with the same slight fall on the second half, and he heard it, and he heard the 2019 one underneath it.

Here is the 2019 one.

It was the September of 2019 and they had been in the house about six weeks and she had come back from a week at her sister's and had been short with the child all evening, and he had said, in the kitchen, in a reasonable voice, "You've not been yourself this week."

And she had said: "It's not like that."

And he had heard, in 2019, exactly what she had meant. He had heard a woman telling him he was reading her, and that she was not the sort of person who could be read, and that he should go back to whatever he had thought he knew. He had heard a door.

And he had gone off upstairs and not come back into that kitchen that night, and the thing about it, the part he has never said out loud to anybody including her, is that she had been right. He had no idea what her week had been like. He had noticed a mood and decided she ought to be able to account for it and she had told him he could not and he had accepted it.

---

This morning she said the same words.

They were at the end of the kitchen with the child at the table eating, and he had said, "You've had that list on the side for about a month," meaning the list on the side by the telephone, which was a list of things to do ahead of the middle of September, which she had made in August and had not touched since, and which he had mentioned four times in as many weeks, and which was on the side by the telephone every single day.

And she said: "It's not like that."

And he heard the 2019 sentence come out of her mouth exactly as it had come out of it in the September of 2019.

And this time what he heard in it was different, and it took him the whole of the drive to work to work out what the difference was, and by that point he was on the ring road and had to do the ring road.

Here is the 2020 one, and this is the part of it that neither of them will ever know about.

What she meant this morning, and what she would have said if he had pushed her on it, is that she is not avoiding the list. She has been not-mentioning the list on purpose, because the list has four things on it and three of the four are his, and every time he mentions it she has to say the thing she actually wants to say, which is: *none of those three are mine and I have been sitting here for a month waiting for one of you to see that.*

What he heard this morning was the kitchen in 2019 and a door closing, and he was braced for it, and it is not what she said.

He got to work at about ten to seven and thought about it for about an hour, and he did the thing he is not in the habit of doing, which is he wrote it on the back of a hand so it would not go out of his head.

He came in at about half past six.

She was in the kitchen with the tea made and the radio on very low, and they had the evening the way the two of them have an evening, which is the child and the television and an early night for one of them and a late one for the other.

At about half past nine he said, on the way past her to the sink:

"It's not like that."

And she said, "No," and "I know," and nothing else about it, because there was nothing else about it to say at half past nine on a Thursday.

He took that as her telling him to stop. She had meant it as her telling him that he had understood her wrongly, and she has not corrected that and is not going to, and he has not asked, and that is where the week goes.

Here is what she thought he had said. She thought he had said: *I have noticed the list, and I have noticed the three things on it that are mine, and I am not going to touch them, and I am not going to mention them any more.*

Which was the sentence she had been waiting nine years for, and which he had said out of a kitchen in 2019, on a Thursday evening, about a list on a side by a telephone.

And here is what he thought she meant by her not saying anything else about it. He thought she was putting it back the way she had put it back in 2019, which is to say without a word, and he is right about that, and it is the only thing about the two days that he has got right.

**THEY BOTH SAID THE SAME SENTENCE AND BOTH HEARD THE OTHER ONE'S, AND ON THE THURSDAY EVENING HE SAID IT ONCE MORE AND SHE TOOK IT AS THE THING SHE HAS BEEN WAITING NINE YEARS FOR, AND IT IS STILL TRUE ON SATURDAY AND NEITHER OF THEM WILL MENTION IT.**
