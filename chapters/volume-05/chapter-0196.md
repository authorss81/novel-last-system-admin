# Chapter 0196 — The Permission

Wednesday the thirtieth of September 2015, from about eleven in the morning, a first-floor room over a shop in Tideglass.

**A DOCUMENT ARRIVED IN THIS CITY THIS MORNING WITH A PERSON'S NAME ON IT, AND IT IS THE FIRST ONE IN NINE MONTHS, AND IT IS CORRECT, AND IT IS SIGNED.**

It came in by hand. A woman of about fifty-two came down on the morning train from a city called Alderwick, on the other side of the water, with a document in a plastic wallet and a folded piece of A4 with a list on it, and she put them on a table in a room over a shop and asked whether anybody wanted to go through it with her, and about six people said yes, and one of the six was Jonas Mercer, who had come down because ten is when a man walks to a parade of shops and a room is where a man goes after that.

She said her name was Irene Sandiford and that she was a duty clerk, and that she was here until the twelve-fifteen train, and that she was not the person who agreed to it and would not be able to say anything about whether it was a good idea. Both of those sentences were correct. She read it out.

**THE PERMISSION, AS SHE READ IT, TWO PAGES, THE SECOND OF THEM MOSTLY SCHEDULE, AND EVERY SENTENCE OF IT IS RIGHT:**

> **MUTUAL AID — TEMPORARY SECONDMENT, SHARED USE AND RETURN**
> **Reference AM/15/221. Between the city of Alderwick and this city.**
>
> **1.** A person named in the Schedule may be asked to work at a place in the other city for a period not exceeding fourteen days.
> **2.** A person named in the Schedule who is asked to work takes the work or does not take it, at the hour the work begins.
> **3.** A person named in the Schedule is paid the going rate, at the rate printed in the Schedule, in money, in full, before the work begins.
> **4.** A person named in the Schedule keeps the right to return to the place the work is done from, on the day the work ends, without asking anybody for anything.
> **5.** Where a person is receiving care in one city and this permission is in force, the other city pays that care at the rate in the Schedule. The person does not move.
> **6.** A claim arising out of work done under this permission is made in the city where the work was done, and not in the city the person came from.
> **7.** Nothing in this permission requires a person to be asked, to be found, to be named, or to be told anything.
>
> **SCHEDULE.** Nine persons. Name, age, last address in this city, work, rate, and the date the entry was made.
> **AGREED for Alderwick: I. SANDIFORD, duty clerk, 30 September 2015.**
> **COUNTER-SIGNED for this city: a clerk at the water office, 29 September 2015.**

There is no line under either signature with nothing in it. That is the first thing Jonas Mercer looked at, and it was the only thing about the page that made him stop for more than a second, and then he went on reading, because there was a great deal else on it. Somebody in this city signed it on Monday at a counter in a building by the water, and there are people on that counter, and nobody in the room could have said which of them, and the water office does not have a list either.

Clause two is the whole of the bargain and it is two sentences long. A person who is asked may take the work or not take it, and the decision is made at the hour the work begins, which is a strange hour to make it at and is not a strange hour if the person is loading a lorry at seven in the morning and the person asking is standing there. Clause three pays before the work, in money, which is the opposite of this borough and is the best sentence in the document. Clause four is a right of return and it costs the receiving city nothing to give and cannot be sold. Clause five pays for care where a person is and does not move the person, and it is a good clause, and it means a patient in this city can be paid for by a council across an estuary and will never know it happened.

Clause six is the one he read three times.

She said, when she got to it, without being asked and in the tone of somebody reading out a price at a counter: "That is so the city that took the work does not get billed for it by the city that sent the man."

"Yes," said a man of about twenty-eight who had come in and had not said anything before that. "That is what it is for."

"Two sides of a river, one wage, one bill. It is a good clause. If it were the other way round there would be an argument every time and the argument would be about the money and not about the work." She turned the page. "I am not defending it. I am reading it."

Then the schedule.

**AND THE SCHEDULE IS WHERE THE DOCUMENT STOPS BEING A DOCUMENT ABOUT WORK.**

The first two entries were men in their fifties and a woman in her fifties, all three with addresses in this city written out in full, down to the street and the number, and all three of them are people who would answer a telephone. The fourth was a man in his twenties with an address in Bellow Market. The fifth, sixth and seventh were in a column of their own because they are not in this city: a man who is at Alderwick, a woman who is at Alderwick, and a man who has been at Alderwick since March.

Then the eighth.

> **ASHGROVE, EWEN. About sixty-two. Last address in this city: one room, second floor, a house in Kilburn Street. Left the room in the second week of April. Rent outstanding to the person who let it: fourteen days. Work: none recorded. Rate: the going rate for the work. Entry made 11 August 2015. Present at Alderwick since 11 August 2015, in a rented room, in a work contract of fourteen days at a time, renewed six times.**

Six people looked at it. The man of about twenty-eight said the name in the room out loud, twice, the way people do, and then did not say it a third time.

"He is not missing," said Irene Sandiford. "That is the thing I am here about. I was asked to send a schedule and I was told at Alderwick that one of the nine was a name this city had been carrying, and so I am here, and I am not permitted to say anything else about him, and I would not have said anything about him if I was, because I have never met him."

Jonas Mercer looked at the eighth entry for a while.

In the second week of April a man of about sixty-two walked out of a room on the second floor of a house in Kilburn Street with a bag. The room was let again in June to somebody else. Fourteen days' rent was outstanding and about ninety pounds came with it, and the person who let the room has had that money in a tin in a drawer since April and has been to three places in this city about it and has been told the correct thing at all three, which is that there is no list and there is no way of looking anybody up and nobody in this borough can require anybody to do anything, and that a person who does not want to be found in this city is not found in this city. That was in April. It is now the end of September.

Nobody in this room asked for that information. Nobody in this city asked for it. A duty clerk in a city on the other bank was sent a schedule to fill in, and one of the names on it went to a person who had been carrying it, and the person sent it on, because a schedule with a name on it is a schedule with a name on it and nobody had a reason to take one out.

**AND THE GOOD NEWS, WHICH IS THAT A PERSON THIS BOROUGH COULD NOT FIND HAS BEEN FOUND, AND WHICH NOBODY IN THIS BOROUGH ASKED FOR, AND WHICH NOBODY IS GOING TO BE ABLE TO SAY WHETHER IT IS A RESCUE.**

It is not a rescue. He is in a rented room in a city across the water and he has been there since the eleventh of August and he has a contract of fourteen days at a time that has been renewed six times, and nobody has been in danger and nobody has been brought back and nobody has been sent for, and if the whole of the arrangement were cancelled this afternoon the only person in either city who would be worse off would be a man in Kilburn Street who is owed about ninety pounds that he has never expected to see again and was never going to chase.

A man who cannot be found is a thing this city has been careful with for nine months, and the care has cost about four people and the hours they spend in this borough, and it has been right, and this morning it produced the opposite of itself for no reason at all.

"A man of about sixty-two," said Jonas Mercer, "is now in a document."

"He is," said Irene Sandiford. "And the rate is printed next to his name, and the room is printed next to his name, and the date he arrived is printed next to his name. I would not want him in one of ours if it were mine to decide, and I am not the person who decides it, and I have said that already."

Nobody said anything about the ninety pounds. There was a place to say it and somebody in Kilburn Street would have been glad to hear about it, and neither of those things was on the table, and nobody in the room was going to make a telephone call to a city across an estuary in front of a duty clerk about money.

She got the twelve-fifteen. On the way out she said one more thing to the room, and it was correct, and Jonas Mercer wrote nothing down afterwards and did not repeat it to anybody.

"Nine names," she said, "and the offer goes out on a Friday night, and a person takes it or does not take it, and about a third of them take it every time. We have stopped writing to anybody who has said no once. There is no list of the ones who said no."

Then the door, and the twelve-fifteen, and a room with a plastic wallet on the table and nine names in it and one of them a name this city had been carrying for five months.
