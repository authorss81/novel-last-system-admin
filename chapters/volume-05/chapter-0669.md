# Chapter 0669 — What Is Hers To Mend

Wednesday the twenty-second of September 2021, in this borough. She is in her own house at number ten from morning until dark with tools out on the table, and the whole of the day is a curtain rail she can mend without knocking anywhere else.

**A WOMAN IN HER EARLY THIRTIES MENDS A CURTAIN RAIL IN HER OWN HOUSE ON A WEDNESDAY WITHOUT KNOCKING UP THE ROAD, AND PUTS THE TOOLS AWAY.**

She is a woman in her early thirties. She is at home all day. Nobody comes.

---

The rail in the back bedroom has been loose since the summer, with one bracket pulling out of the plaster and the curtain hanging low on one side, and she has put off doing it because the drill is loud and the wall is thin.

---
This morning she gets the drill and the plugs and the screws out on the kitchen table, and she clears the sill so nothing falls behind the settee.

She wants to get it fixed before dark without borrowing anything and without asking anything up the road, because asking up the road would make it a thing she had carried there.

---

She moves the bed out from the wall a foot and covers it with a sheet, and she drills the new hole slow with her hand steady on the grip, and the dust falls down onto the sheet in a pale fan.

---
The plug taps in tight with the end of the screwdriver, and the bracket goes back true, and the rail sits level when she lifts it on.

She tightens both brackets and hangs the curtain and draws it across and back, and it runs free.

---

She puts the drill and the bits back in their case and wipes the dust off the sill with a damp cloth, and she shakes the sheet out of the back door into the yard.

---
Against that is not the rail. It is the memory of the twenty-ninth of July, when she used the only knock on that road, had about a minute with an open door, used all of it on a corner of glass, said the tape and not the chair, put a roll of tape into the older woman's hand and went back down three doors with nothing in her hands.

She was told no in three seconds about a gate in May and did not ask why, and she still has not asked.

---

She eats at midday at her own table with the tools put away, and she looks out of her back window across the gardens towards number twelve, where the grass on the walked-flat line shows a little in the afternoon light.

---
She cannot see the gate from here. She can see the roof and the chimney and the top of the coal shed wall.

She does not go to the front to look. Looking from the front would make it a thing she was watching.

---

In the afternoon she tapes the back room window of her own house where the edge has lifted, because she taped a back room window in May up the road and she knows brown tape wants pressing down twice to stay.

---
She presses it with her thumb along the whole length and then again, and it stays.

Let in once when she was nineteen and stayed too long, and has worked out since that the reason she was never sent away is that nobody in that house can send anybody away. She thinks about that while her thumb moves, and then she stops thinking about it.

---

Her sister comes round at dark for ten minutes on her way back from a late, and they drink tea standing up in the kitchen with the curtain rail mended above them in the back bedroom.

---
Her sister does not mention number twelve. She does not mention it. The talk is of the council form and of a back door that shuts true now.

Her sister is not given an age in this house tonight, because the house is hers and the evening is ordinary and there is no need.

---

After her sister goes she checks the rail once more and draws the curtain across for the night, and the room looks right with the curtain hanging straight.

She bolts the front door and the back door and turns the kitchen light off, leaving the landing light on the way up.

---

The gate up the road is standing open against a coal shed wall with both bolts in an apron pocket. The brush is on a passage floor in the middle of the runner with the handle across the walking line.

Neither fact needs her. That is the whole of what she has learned since July.

---

She goes to bed with the tools away and the house straight, and the tape on her own window pressed down twice.

---

**AND A WOMAN IN HER EARLY THIRTIES HAS GOT THROUGH A DAY WITHOUT CARRYING ANYTHING UP THE ROAD SHE ONCE KNOCKED ON, AND HAS MENDED WHAT IS HERS WITH WHAT IS HERS, AND HAS NOT ASKED WHY A GATE WAS REFUSED IN THREE SECONDS IN MAY.**

(End of chapter 0669)
