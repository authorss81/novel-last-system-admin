# Chapter 0685 — He Does Not Say When It Comes Back

Monday the twenty-ninth of November 2021, in this borough. He is in a house off the ring road from first light until dark, and the whole of the day is a step ladder that has been in his van since the summer and is not in it by the time he gets back to it.

**A MAN IN EARLY FORTIES LENDS A STEP LADDER OUT OF HIS VAN IN A STREET AGAINST A WEDNESDAY HE DID NOT EXPLAIN AND SHE SAYS SHE HAS NOT GOT A WEDNESDAY, SPENDS THE NEXT MORNING OFF A PACKING BENCH INSTEAD OF ON IT, FINDS THE SPACE BEHIND THE OFFCUTS EMPTY AND DOES NOT DO ANYTHING ABOUT THAT EITHER, AND HAS TWELVE ON A SCRAP OF CARD IN THE SAME POCKET AND DOES NOT PUT IT TO ANYTHING.**

He is a man in his early forties. He is on his own all day except for a woman in the street, a woman at a door, a man of about thirty-five who works shifts, and the man at the bench opposite.

---

The house is a terrace and the job is a run of kitchen units and a pelmet, and the units are going up at the wrong end of the wall from where a person would want them because the plumbing is in and the plumbing was in before he came.

He has the ladder out of the van by the time the tools are off the tailgate.

That is the first thing and it is the only thing about today that is ordinary. It is an aluminium folding one, four treads, a tray in the top for the tins, and it lives in the van on its side behind a stack of offcuts that go from the front bulkhead back to about the middle, and it has been on that side since the summer because the other side has the leads and the dust sheets and the dust sheets are the first thing you want when it starts.

It has a strip of dark tape round the second rail where a hinge has been and was not, and a chalk mark on the third tread that is not his, and the maker's name on the rail has gone down to about half of itself.

He does not think about any of that.

---

The first woman is at the door of number four with a cup of tea she does not offer him.

"You've come for the units?"

"I've come for the units."

"I told the man on the phone I'd be out." She stays in the doorway with her hand on the frame. "It's the tall one by the window, the light's been flickering on that since October."

"August."

"Since October, I said."

"Right."

"That'll want a new one. Not today. He'll tell you it's the switch."

"He'll tell me it's the switch," he says, and does not say the rest of it, and the rest of it is that a man in a fleece with a fibreglass step ladder off the back of a van could be on that ladder before the kettle boiled, and he is on a different ladder in a different house with a tape measure, and neither of those facts is going to travel between the two of them.

She goes in and shuts the door and he fits four units on a run of a wall with a bracket he has to make twice because the first one is for a wall that is not straight and he has not measured the wall, and that is his own fault and he knows it while he is doing it.

---

He comes out of the front door of number four at about the middle of the morning with the tape round his neck and a pencil behind his ear and a woman in a white van is stopped across the road with the tailgate up and a skip she is looking at the wrong way round.

He has seen the van. Everybody in this borough has seen the van; there is not one of these roads where a white van with a bench along the passenger side is not a thing that goes past.

He goes over to it because she is standing in the road.

"You all right?"

"I'm all right." She has a hand under the bonnet edge and she is not looking at the engine. "I've got a skip I'm not allowed to put on that drive. It's the wrong side. I know it's the wrong side. I want to be somewhere else for a minute."

"You want a minute."

"I've had a morning."

He leans on the wall next to her, because it is cold and there is nowhere else to go.

"What's the job?"

"Two houses. One of them's a woman of about sixty and there's a rule in that house about the arm of a chair." She says the word rule the way people say a thing that has been going on too long. "And the other's got nothing in it and there's a skip on a drive that's got a wall on three sides of it."

"Right."

"You wouldn't have a ladder, would you."

---

That is the whole of it. She says it the way you say a thing you have already decided you are not going to get.

"Have you not got one?"

"I've got a folding stool. It's a stool." She nods at the back of her van, and there is a thing back there she cannot get to without moving something, and she does not move anything. "I need to get a wardrobe down off a wall in there and the wall's got a chimney breast on it and the chimney breast goes out into the room about so far." She puts a hand out about a foot. "I'd want to be up and I'd want to be able to put both arms out and I'd want to know the floor's not going to go."

"I've got one in the van."

"You've got one."

"Four tread. It's a good one. It's got a tray in the top."

She looks at him for a bit.

"How long do you want it?"

---

And that is the moment, and he knows it is the moment while it is happening, and it is the first time in a long while that a sentence has been available to him that he does not have to work out the inside of.

He has a run of wall units starting in the morning and he has not got anything to stand on.

"Take it," he says. "I don't need it till Wednesday."

"Right."

"You'll bring it Wednesday."

"I haven't got a Wednesday," she says. "I've got Monday and I've got whatever you call it. I'll drop it in."

"Where."

"I'll leave it on the wall by the tip off the ring road where the merchant lets me in. That's where I am most days. You know the one."

"I know the one."

"Right."

---

What he did not say was why Wednesday. What she did not say was that a Wednesday is a thing you can do.

The day goes out of it in the space of a sentence and neither of them puts anything back in, and that is how it is done, and he has been in this trade long enough to know that a date in a conversation is not a date in an arrangement. A date in a conversation is a thing two people have said out loud so that neither of them has to be the one who wanted it back. A date in an arrangement is a thing one of them has to go and enforce.

He thinks about that at the lights on the way home, and not for long, and not in a way that gets him anywhere, because the arrangement they have made is a good one and works for everybody and has no date in it, and a thing with no date in it is a thing with a different name.

She watches him get into the van and shuts the tailgate and he hears her get in and the door shut and then the engine and then nothing.

The van goes out with the ladder in it. That is the fact. That is the whole fact, and it is the only fact, and it is not going to be a fact anybody in this borough has to be reminded of.

---

It is the Tuesday and it is the job. He gets to the house before the man who booked it and stands in the kitchen with his hands in his pockets.

The units are a run of three at six foot, wall to wall, with a pelmet over the top of them, and the booker is a man of about thirty-five who works shifts and has left a list on the worktop in his own handwriting with the tape measure glyph beside each line.

The list does not say anything about a man at the bottom of a ladder. It is not a list that knows anything about ladders. It says three and a pelmet and it says the light and it says the wall by the window.

"There you are."

"I'm here."

"You'll want that one up high."

"I will."

"Only thing is, we've had the man out about the window and he was on a ladder and he put a smear on the wall, so if you could put a sheet down. There's sheets in the hall cupboard, the green ones."

"I'll put sheets down."

He puts sheets down. He puts them on the worktop and on the floor and he tapes the top edge of the run to the wall with his own tape because the plaster is old and he does not trust it, and he tapes the second one, and there is no ladder in the building and there is a packing bench in the back room with a plank across it and a length of offcut on the plank and the offcut is the right height if you do not mind working off a thing that moves.

He is on and off that bench all morning. He has the little steplool he made himself out of two offcuts in the summer and he is on and off that as well, and the tape at the top edge of the run goes up three times because the third time is the one that sticks.

The booker comes in at the end of the morning and looks at it.

"That's tidy."

"It'll do."

"It'll do," the man says, and goes to work, and does not say how long it took him, because he was not here for the morning and could not know, and that is the arrangement men like that have, and it is the arrangement he has been happy with for eleven years.

The man did not ask him about the ladder and he did not ask him about the ladder.

That is the not-asking and it costs him the whole of a morning and nobody in that house will ever know it was spent.

---

The unit is a lock-up on an estate of units and he has the third one and the man at the bench opposite has the second, and the two of them have been in the same building since before the summer came.

He puts the tailgate up and looks into the van before he does anything else, which is a thing he has started doing since the end of November and has not examined.

The stack of offcuts is where the offcuts are. The leads are where the leads are. The dust sheets are on the leads.

The space behind the offcuts is the width of a folding step ladder and it is empty and it is the width of a folding step ladder and it has been that shape since the summer.

He stands there.

The man at the bench opposite has his radio on low with the talk on it that neither of them follows, and he is planing a length of oak for a gate that is going on a cottage somewhere and has been going on that cottage since the spring, and he does not look up, and there is nothing in the way he is sitting that is asking a question.

"You not wanting that stool today?"

"No."

"You can have it. It's in the way where it is."

"No, I'll shift it."

He shifts the stool out of the way and puts it against the wall by the door where it is not in the way of anything, and that takes no time at all, and it is the most he does about the empty space behind the offcuts.

Then he goes and gets the shoebox off the top of his own bench and does not open it.

---

There is a card in the shirt pocket of the coat on the back of the cab door and it has TWELVE on it in his own writing in scribing pencil, and it is not the only thing in that pocket.

The other thing in that pocket is a letting agent's card with a lad on it in a shirt, and it has been in that pocket since the sixth of October, and he has not rung it, and the reason he has not rung it is a thing he has told himself four times and has never once made sound in his own head.

The card with TWELVE on it is the same: he wrote it in his own hand on a corner of scribing card at a tip off the ring road on the sixteenth of November because there was a taped box on the top of that heap with a street on it he knows and a number on it that is a house at the end of a road, and he read a name off the other half of that label and put the box back down the way it was and folded a bit of bag over its corner with his own tape so that when it went over the fence it would not be just loose rubbish.

He has not been up that road since the seventeenth of June.

He has gone past the end of it once, in the last week of August, without slowing down, and once on the sixth of October, when he stopped just past the turning with the engine running, where you can see down the short road to the side of number twelve.

He has a number written in a pocket and a name in his head that he will not put on anything, and neither of them has got him out of the van.

And a woman he has spoken to four times since the summer has got a ladder out of him this morning for no length of time at all, and he did not ask her one question about it, and that is not a moral matter and it is not even a thought. It is that a ladder is a ladder.

Except that the space behind the offcuts is a shaped space, and a shape means a person meant it to be there, and there is a man in this borough who put that shape there in the summer and is now standing in a lock-up with a pencil behind his ear and nothing to do about it.

---

He shuts the tailgate.

The drawer under the bench is shut. It has been shut since the sixteenth of November and it is shut now and nothing has gone into it and nothing has come out of it, and the new brush is in a plastic bag flat along the back of it and the nine pounds he paid for it in the summer is the only money he has ever put into that van out of his own pocket for anybody, and the woman who clears houses put her hand over her mouth in a kitchen and said she would do the gate.

There is a shovel in the van and there is a gate to hang in the spring and there is a man who wants the money up front and has wanted it since the summer.

He has not rung her. He is not going to ring her about a ladder, and the not-asking there is a real one and it has a real cost, and the cost is a folded aluminium step ladder that is somewhere in this borough this afternoon in a room or a porch or the back of a van with a box in it that is not his.

He drives home with the drawer shut and the shoebox on the bench and the stranger's brush in the side pocket of the canvas bag in the footwell with the stone in the leather bottom of it, and the tape round the second rail of that step ladder is the only thing on it that could tell anybody whose it is, and it is a strip of dark tape on an aluminium rail, and it is not an address, and there is nobody in this borough who would know what to do with it.

---

**A MAN IN HIS EARLY FORTIES HAS SAID WEDNESDAY OUT LOUD SO THAT HE WOULD NOT BE THE ONE WHO ASKED FOR IT BACK, HAS SPENT A WHOLE MORNING OFF A PACKING BENCH BECAUSE A BOOKER WOULD NOT HAVE HIM SAY THAT HE HAD NO LADDER, HAS PUT THE STEPOOL AGAINST A WALL INSTEAD OF ASKING THE MAN AT THE BENCH OPPOSITE WHETHER HE HAD ONE, AND HAS A NUMBER IN HIS SHIRT POCKET AND A CARD WITH A BOY ON IT IN THE SAME POCKET AND HAS USED NEITHER, AND THE SPACE BEHIND THE OFFCUTS IN HIS VAN IS A FOLDED STEP LADDER'S WIDE AND EMPTY.**
