# Chapter 0488 — Half of It

Sunday the twenty-eighth of April 2019, from half past eight until about half past six. A lane off a row, and a field at the end of it, in Tideglass.

**TWO MEN PAID HALF EACH FOR A HORSE AND A CART IN THE SECOND WEEK OF MARCH 2016, AND ONE OF THEM HAS TAKEN THAT CART OUT ON AN ORDINARY WEEKDAY MOST WEEKS SINCE AND THE OTHER HAS NOT ASKED FOR ANY OF HIS MONEY BACK SINCE NOVEMBER, AND ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF APRIL 2019 THE MAN WHO USES THE CART STOOD AT THE TOP OF THAT LANE WITH THE HORSE'S HEAD AND A HAND ON THE CART AND SAID THE WORD NO TO A MAN WHO HAD ALREADY TURNED UP TO HELP HIM, AND THEN HE CAME BACK AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO AND DID NOT SAY WHY HE HAD SAID IT.**

They are both in their fifties and they have been in the same row since they were small and they have never been the sort that talks.

There is a lane at the back of that row, about four hundred yard long, and it comes out on a field that belongs to a man in the town and that the two of them have had the use of since about 2016 at a figure neither of them has written down and neither of them would say out loud in a public place.

The field is about four acres and it is not good ground. It is wet at the bottom in February and hard at the top in July and it is eight foot higher at one end than the other, and the horse lives in a corner at the bottom of it in a shelter that a man from the town put up in 2016 and that has been repaired twice.

There is no arrangement here to describe. There are two men, a horse, a lane and a field.


What they bought, in the second week of March 2016, was a horse called Luce and a cart that came with her.

The cart was second hand and it was sound and it cost more than the horse. They found her at a place about fourteen miles off, at a gate, on a Saturday morning, having been told to look by a man who has since died of nothing interesting.

The brother put half in and the other put half in and it was done at the gate with two cans of drink between them and it took about five minutes, and neither of them has ever written it down, and neither of them can now remember who put in what.

That is three years ago.

---

One of them uses it.

He is the one who is at home in the middle of the day, and he has been at home in the middle of the day since about 2011, and what he does with the cart is the ordinary work of a man with a horse and a good deal of sense: he takes sand off the top of that lane for anybody who wants it, and he takes a trailer's worth of manure to three or four people who have gardens, and in about July he takes six loads of something that has to be gone by the middle of August.

He has gone out with that cart about nine times a month for three years, and there are seven or eight households in this borough that have had a load off him and paid him for it.

The other one has not gone out with it once since the summer of 2016.

What he does instead is go up the lane in the evening, four times a week, and see to the horse. He does the feed and he does the water and he does the farrier when the farrier comes, and he has been doing that for three years, and he has never once been late at it.

So the position is this: the man who does not use the cart looks after the horse, and the man who uses the cart does not look after the horse, and neither of them has ever said that out loud, and they have both always known it.


The asking started in 2017.

He asked four times that first year, at about the same time each year, always at the same sort of moment, which was in October when the leaves were down and there was nothing to do about.

He asked what he calls *my half*.

And the other one said the same thing every time, which was that there was no half of it to have because it was not that kind of thing, and that the man who looks after her is welcome to it.

And in 2018 he asked again. And in 2019 he asked twice, once in the second week of April and once in the last week of October, and the answer was the same, and he asked it in the end because he had worked out that if he kept asking then at some point he would either be given something or he would have to stop.


What happened in the last week of October is why he stopped.

The man who uses the cart did not go up the lane for sixteen days. Not sick, not away, not anything anybody was told — he simply was not there on the two evenings the other man came and on the Sunday, and he was not at the gate on the Saturday either when the other man went to fetch feed, and the horse was stood in the shelter with about a day and a half of what she should have had.

The other man did everything himself in those sixteen days. He got a neighbour to take the feed up in his own car twice because a sack is not a thing you can carry four hundred yard two at a time, and he did the farrier's feet himself with a rasp out of the side of the tool box, badly, and he has never told anybody about the feet.

And on the Thursday the man came back.

And the other one said it there and then, standing in the field with the horse behind them: *Right. When do you want your half back. April, say. I'll get it sorted.*

And the man who uses the cart did not answer for about three seconds.

And then he said, "There's no half of this to have."

And then he said, "You're the one who's up here."

And then he said, "It's not a debt, it's a horse."

And he said it in a good voice. He said it the way a man says a thing when he has already worked out that the other man has been carrying something and would rather he put it down.

And that is why the asking stopped, and it stopped in the field, in about three seconds, and nothing has been said about it since.

---

Here is what each of them thinks, and neither has ever asked the other.

The man who uses the cart thinks the other one never cared. He thinks it was always a thing that had to be mentioned to be real, and that once it had been said out loud it was a thing being kept, and that a man who keeps a thing like that is a man who is waiting for a moment to say it, and that he has been giving him that moment every October since 2017 and the man has never taken it.

He is wrong about that and he has never had anything to make him check it.

The other one thinks something else and thinks it harder.

He thinks the other man has forgotten. Not forgotten the horse and not forgotten the field and not forgotten who goes up the lane. Forgotten *that he ever asked*.

He has worked out, in those sixteen days of October and the three seconds after them, that the reason he asked at all was that he had expected to be asked, and that a man who expects to be asked is a man who thinks somebody has been keeping a figure. And that if nobody was keeping a figure then there was nothing to ask about, and a man who has asked about a thing that does not exist four times since 2017 is a man who would rather not ask again.

So he thinks the other one never knew.

That is also wrong, and he has also had nothing to make him check it.


The horse is difficult at the top of that lane.

She has always been difficult at the top of that lane. It is a rise of about four foot in the last thirty yard and a hedge on the left and a wall on the right and there is nowhere at all for her to get her shoulder round, and she plants her feet, and she has never once hit anything or gone over.

On the twenty-eighth of April he took two loads up to a man at the top of the row who was doing a drive and who had asked for six loads.

She stuck at the same place on the first load, for about a minute and a half, and he got her round it. She stuck at the same place on the second load for about four minutes and he could not get her round it, and the right-hand wheel on the cart had begun to go at about half past ten and by the time he had her round it the second time he had about two inches of daylight in one tyre and a sound coming out of the axle that he has been listening to since.

That is what happened, and it happened in full view of a street.

The other man was out in his own front garden at about half past ten, which is what he does on a Sunday, and he came out of his own gate with his coat across his shoulder and stood at the end of the lane watching a man try to get a horse up a hill.

And then he walked up the lane, which takes about nine minutes from that gate.

He did not ask. He came up the side of it and stood at the horse's head and put his hand flat on her cheek and said nothing at all.

And the man who uses the cart said, "You don't have to."

And the other one said, "No."

And that is all of it. The whole of what was said on that Sunday morning at the top of that lane was those four words and about five minutes of the two of them standing on either side of a horse's head.

Then the other one took the cart's head and got her up it.


At about half past two he stopped coming and got into his car at the top of the lane and drove off, and the man who uses the cart took the horse back down on his own.

Nothing has been said about it since. On the Sunday after, they were both at the field at about the same time and they did not speak. A fortnight after that the man who uses the cart came down that lane and the other one was there, and they did the feed together, and they said about four words between them across about an hour, and every one of those words was about the horse.

That is where it stands. Two men, each of whom has decided that the other forgot something that happened in a field in about three seconds in the last week of October, and neither will raise it, and in six months not one word about the money has passed between them or gone to anybody else in this borough.

**A CART WENT UP THE LANE BEHIND THAT HORSE ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF APRIL 2019 WITH A MAN HOLDING THE HEAD WHO HAD WALKED THE LENGTH OF THAT LANE TO DO IT AFTER SAYING NO, AND THE WHEEL THAT HAD BEEN GOING CAME OFF ROUGHLY A FOOT AND A HALF FURTHER ON AND NOTHING WAS SAID ABOUT ANY OF IT.**
