# Chapter 0498 — Not One Word of It

The Wednesday of the twelfth of June 2019, from about ten past eight in the morning until about one in the afternoon. A parade, one bus stop on it, and a bus that did not come, in Tideglass.

**TWO WOMEN WHO HAVE BEEN AT THE SAME BUS STOP ON THAT PARADE FOR ABOUT FIVE YEARS WERE BOTH THERE ON THE MORNING OF THE TWELFTH OF JUNE 2019 AND SAW A MAN BUY TWO NEWSPAPERS OUT OF ONE AND WALK OFF DOWN THE PARADE CARRYING BOTH OF THEM, AND NEITHER HAS EVER PUT ANY OF IT TO THE OTHER AND NEITHER IS GOING TO, AND THEY HAVE NEVER BEEN INTRODUCED AND NEITHER OF THEM KNOWS WHAT THE OTHER ONE IS CALLED.**

The stop is on the north side of the parade, about two thirds of the way up it, outside a shop that sells papers and cigarettes and has a rack of papers on the pavement outside it.

There are two women who use that stop and have used it for years.

One of them is there most mornings of the week and goes into town. The other one is there on Wednesdays and goes to a college in a building by a canal two stops away. Neither has ever asked the other a question. Both have known for about five years that the other one stands at that stop.

They get on the same bus. The bus holds about fifty people, and on a wet Wednesday about thirty. They have sat in different rows for five years and neither has ever moved.

---

Here is the man, and the whole of the fact, and it is a very small thing.

He comes down that parade at about ten to nine every morning, six days in seven, and he goes into the paper shop and buys two papers and pays for two, and he folds one of them and puts it into his coat pocket, and he carries the other one in his hand, and he goes on down the parade towards the turning place and out of sight.

He has done that for about five years.

In the March of 2014 a woman who lived with him died, and he has never once been into that shop and said one paper, and the woman who fills the rack has never asked him about it, and the young man who serves him has not asked him either, and he has watched about nine hundred mornings of it go past his own hands without anybody asking him one thing, because neither of them has ever thought it was any of their business.

Both of the two women at that stop have worked that out. Neither of them got it from him. Neither of them has ever spoken to him about it and neither is going to.

Here is how each of them got there, because it was not the same thing and it was not the same year.

The woman who goes into town worked it out in about the March of 2014, and what gave it to her was the coat.

He is a careful man, and careful men have a pocket they use for one thing, and for five years the folded paper has gone into the pocket on the left of that coat and the keys have gone into the pocket on the right, and a man who is careful about that has not done it by accident, and it has been the same every morning of those five years.

The woman who goes to the college worked it out in about the September of that same year, and what gave it to her was the better part of a minute of nothing.

She was standing at that stop in the second week of September at about eight in the morning, and she watched him stop in the doorway of that shop and stand there for the better part of a minute before he went in, and a man buying a paper does not stand in a doorway for the better part of a minute.

Neither of them has ever said one word about it, to him or to anybody, and neither of them has said one word to the other about it in five years, and each of the two of them has a different account of why she is not going to.

The woman who goes into town has told herself that it is not hers.

The argument she uses on herself is about manners. A man buys two papers, and the second one is for a house, and the house may be occupied by a daughter, or a sister, or by somebody who is not well, and there is not one thing in that which a woman standing at a bus stop has any business having an opinion about. And if she were wrong, then she would have said to a man in a street that his wife was dead when she was not, and there is no version of that which any of the three of them comes out of.

The woman who goes to the college has told herself something different, and she has been far more careful with it, and it is the worse of the two.

She has told herself that if she ever says it, and she is wrong, then she will have done that, and there is no putting it back. She would rather be wrong for another five years. She has worked out that she can afford that, and she has worked out that the reason she can afford it is that somebody else in that parish will say it one day, and it will not be her.

Both of those arguments are about the woman making them. Neither of them has anything to do with him, and he does not know that either of the two of them knows, and neither of them would put it to the other under any circumstances whatever.

---

What happened on the Wednesday is this.

The bus came at about ten past eight and both of them got on it and they sat in different rows.

And then it came back down that parade at about a quarter to nine, empty, going the other way, and it stopped, and the driver put his head out of the window and said that the service was not going past the turning place because of something in the street above, and that he had been sent back twice and did not know what it was about, and would they want to walk up and take the one from the top.

There were four people at that stop. Three of them went up.

And here is the thing about the two women, which neither of them has ever said to anybody and which is the only part of this that anybody standing on that parade would have found worth noticing.

They did not want to go up together and they went up together, and for about four hundred yard of that street they walked on the same side, about a yard apart, and neither of them said anything at all, and neither of them changed her speed, and neither of them looked at the other one, and at the top they stood on opposite sides of the turning place and looked in different directions.

And on the way up, at about two thirds of the way and at about five to nine, he came past them going down, with the folded one in his pocket and the other one in his hand.

Here is what each of the two women did at that moment, and neither of them knows that the other one did it.

The woman who goes into town did not look at him at all. She has not looked at him since about 2015. She went on up that street with her eyes on the near kerb, and she counted nine kerbstones, and she arrived at the turning place having counted nine.

The woman who goes to the college looked at him, and stopped walking for about half a second, and looked at the pocket, and then looked at the hand, and then went on up. And she had not done that since about 2016, and she had told herself for three years that she would do it again if she ever got the chance, and she has not said that to anybody either.

And then, at the top of the parade at about five to nine, the woman who goes into town said the first thing she has ever said to the other one.

She said: "It's nice, this."

And the other one said: "It's about six weeks it's been like this."

And the first one said: "It's the heat, it's the same every year."

And the other one said: "I know. We had it in May last year."

And then the bus came round the corner from the top and they got on it, and they sat in different rows, and at the far end the woman who goes into town got off and went up a street with her hands in her pockets and did not turn round.

That was four sentences, and it was about the weather, and it is the first conversation those two women have ever had, and neither of them has mentioned it to anybody since.

**A BUS CAME ROUND THE CORNER AT THE TOP OF THAT PARADE AT FIVE TO NINE ON THE MORNING OF THE TWELFTH OF JUNE 2019 AND TWO WOMEN WHO HAD STOOD AT THE SAME STOP FOR FIVE YEARS GOT ON IT AND SAT IN DIFFERENT ROWS, AND ONE OF THEM COUNTED NINE KERBSTONES ON THE WAY UP AND THE OTHER ONE LOOKED AT A MAN'S POCKET.**
