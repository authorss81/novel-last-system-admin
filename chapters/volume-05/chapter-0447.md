# Chapter 0447 — Two Mornings at the Ramp

Saturday the thirteenth of October 2018, with the Thursday and the Friday of that week. The same hour on each of the three days, a yard behind a terrace, in Tideglass.

**HE SAW HER MAKE THE SAME MISTAKE ON TWO MORNINGS RUNNING AND WORKED OUT WHAT IT WAS, AND HE DECIDED NOT TO SAY ANYTHING, AND ON THE THIRD MORNING SHE DID NOT MAKE IT, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS ANY IDEA THAT HE WAS THERE AT ALL.**

Behind a terrace of about thirty houses there is a yard, and the way into it is an arch with a chain hung across it that anybody can lift. There is a ramp at the far side of it, about eight foot long and dropping about a foot and a half onto a lane, and at the bottom of the ramp there is a kerb about four inch high that has to be got over, and past that kerb the lane goes down to the water.

A woman had a van for three days to clear a flat about two streets away, and she was alone in it, and she worked at it in that yard because it was the only place in that part of the town where she could stand a van with the back open and have room to turn a thing round.

He was in that yard three times, and all three times for about four minutes, and all three times at about the same hour, and he was not there for her and did not know she was there. He has a gateway at the end of that terrace where he keeps a van of his own, and the only way out of that gateway into the lane is through that yard, and the only time he is in a position to move the van is when whatever he has been doing in the terrace is finished for the day.


On the first of the three mornings she brought a bundle down the ramp and it let go on it.

The bundle was cut branch and lengths of something green and a great deal of leaf, bound round twice with a length of rope, and she had it on a sack barrow, and she had done the rope the way anybody does it when nobody has shown them. She took the standing part, put it round the outside of the two loops, hauled on it, and it came up tight and looked finished.

It is the wrong way. That knot caps itself. It is made to be pulled in one direction and that direction is the direction the load is not going.

She came down the ramp, and the near end ran about a foot and a half, and about a third of the bundle came off the barrow and went down the ramp on top of itself and finished up across the bottom of it in the wet. It took her about a minute to gather and it took her about four minutes to re-tie it and she re-tied it the same way.

He was about ten foot away with his bag half turned and his hand on the strap of his own bag, and he saw all of it, and he kept his mouth shut, and he told himself at the time that he had not got a reason to.

---

He came through that arch at the same time the next morning, and she was on the ramp with the barrow loaded, and he stood at his own van with his back to it and his hand on the strap and he watched.

She tied it the same way. She came down the ramp and the near end ran again, and a smaller piece came off this time, and it went down the ramp and into the kerb, and she had to get down on one knee in the wet to get it.

This time he had about six seconds in which he could have said one word from where he was standing.

He is a man who has done that work for twenty years. He knows exactly what the wrong thing is and he knows what the right thing is and it takes no longer to say the first part than to say his own name. He stood there and he turned it over, and here is what he turned over, and it took him the best part of a minute and it is the whole of why he did not say it.

To say it, he would have had to have been watching her. He was ten foot away behind a van. She would not have been able to work out what he was talking about, and the only two things she could have made of it would be that he was watching her, or that he was watching what she was about to do. Either one of those puts her in a position where she owes him something for having said it, and he would have had to stand there and be told he was right, and he does not want to be told he is right by anybody and certainly not over a knot.

He put his bag in the van, shut the door, and went up the terrace.

She had no idea anybody was behind the van. She has never had any idea. She got the rest of the bundles down that day in three goes and made nothing of them.

---

On the third morning he came through the arch at the same time again and stood where he had stood.

She put the rope round twice. Then she brought the standing part back through, and round the outside again, and then through the loop and under itself and pulled on it, and it came up hard and it did not look any better than the other one had looked and it was a completely different thing.

It went down that ramp without moving. It went over the kerb without moving. She got the barrow out the other side and turned it and went back up for the next one.

He shut his van door and went up the terrace. That was the last time he was in that yard.

He has not been back to that end of the town since. He has not told anybody, and he is not going to, and if he were put in front of that woman tomorrow he would have nothing whatever to say to her, because to say it would be to tell her he stood behind a van for six minutes and let her break her back on a ramp twice, which is a different thing from being right about a knot.

**ON THE THIRD MORNING A BUNDLE WENT DOWN A RAMP IN THAT YARD WITHOUT MOVING, AND A MAN WHO HAD WATCHED IT TWICE FROM BEHIND A VAN WENT UP THE TERRACE AND DID NOT TELL HER.**
