# Chapter 0599 — The Page She Could Not Find

The Saturday of the seventeenth of October 2020, from about half past nine in the morning until about ten at night, in a first-floor flat in this borough.

**HE SAID SOMETHING TO HER IN A CAR PARK IN THIS BOROUGH A FORTNIGHT AGO AND SHE WROTE IT DOWN PROPERLY THAT NIGHT, IN HER OWN HAND, ON THE RIGHT PAGE, WITH THE TIME ON IT, AND ON THIS WEDNESDAY SHE HAS BEEN LOOKING FOR THAT PAGE SINCE HALF PAST NINE, AND IT IS NOT IN THE BOOK.**

She is a woman in her mid thirties. He is a man in his mid forties. They work in the same place. She went there first. The whole of what has happened between them in two years is a working day.

The book is a hardback notebook with a rubber band round it. It has about five hundred pages in it and about three hundred of them have got something on them, and it is not a diary and it is not a list of anything, and she has never shown it to anybody.

---

Here is what he said.

He said it on the Friday before last, at about twenty past five in the evening, in a car park behind a building about a mile from where she lives, and it was the two of them standing by her car because his was the one further away and they had both come out at the same time as they always do.

He said, "I've put my notice in."

That is all he said. He did not say where he was going and she did not ask.

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "I'll see you Monday."

And she said, "Right," and got in her car and sat in it for about four minutes with the engine off, and then drove home.

Here is what she wrote down that night, and it is worth setting out exactly, because the exactness is the whole of what makes the rest of this week what it is.

She wrote the date. She wrote the place, which is a car park behind a building off a road, and she wrote the time, which was twenty past five. She wrote it in one line, in a full sentence, with the words he used and not any other words.

She wrote: *He said, in the car park, "I've put my notice in." That was all of it.*

And underneath that she wrote the two words *my notice* again, on their own, with a line under them.

And then she wrote, underneath that: *I did not ask where to and he did not say.*

And that is all she wrote. It is four lines on one page and the page is about a third of the way into the book.

Here is the looking, and it is the whole of what this day was.

She got the book down at about half past nine in the morning and she found the page at once, because she knew roughly where it was and she could see the line under *my notice*.

And then she made a mistake, and the mistake was ordinary, and it is the kind of mistake that costs a day.

She had looked at the page and had not read it. She had looked at the four lines and seen her own writing and shut the book and put it on the table and gone to get her tea, and on the way back she thought, *what did he say*, and the sentence came to her as the word *notice* on its own, with nothing either side of it, which is what a sentence sounds like when you have not read it for six days and the only part of it that has held is the middle.

And so she went through the book.

She went through it from the front at about half past ten. She went through it faster than that by one o'clock, because by one o'clock she was not looking for the page, she was looking for the word, and there are two other pages in that book with the word *notice* on them, both of them hers, both of them about a car.

She went through it again from the back at about half past three, and this time she was looking for the four lines, and she knew what the four lines said, or she thought she did, and it turned out that what she thought it said and what she had written are two different things and she cannot get from one to the other.

By about six she was not reading the book at all. She was counting pages, which is a different thing, and she got to about three hundred and stopped because three hundred is the number she thinks it is and she cannot make it be a different number.

She had a tea at about six and she did not turn the light on.

---

Here is where it was.

It was on the page it was always on. It was the third of the way into the book, and it is the same page, and the four lines are on it, and the line is under *my notice*, and the writing is hers.

She found it at about ten past eight at night, by accident, in the way that these things are found, which is that she had given up on the page and had started going through the book looking for the page before the one before it, in case the book had been opened at the wrong place, which is a thing that happens to a hardback with a rubber band and it had happened to her before.

She read the four lines.

And here is the part that is the whole of this day, and it is that she had spent the whole day looking for something that was never lost, and the reason she could not find it is that she had been looking for a sentence she had half remembered, and the sentence she had half remembered was not the sentence she had written down.

What she had written down was: *He said, in the car park, "I've put my notice in." That was all of it.*

What she had been carrying about all day, in the four minutes in the car park that Friday and in the eight days since, was: *He's put his notice in.*

The two of them are not the same sentence and the difference between them is the difference between a man telling a woman what he has done and a woman telling herself what a man has done, and the second one has the man in it and the first one does not, and she cannot now remember which of the two she actually had in the car park.

He does not know any of this.

He wrote to her on the Sunday before last, a short thing, about a place and a date and what he would want to know about the money. She has not answered it and is not going to today, and there is no silence being kept.

She has not told him she wrote it down. She is not going to. There is no reason and she is not able to produce one, and the reason she would give if anybody asked her is that it was nothing and it did not need doing.

The book is on the table with the rubber band round it. The page is in it at the third of the way. The two words are underlined.

And she has not written anything else about him, and she is not going to, and the page is going to stay where it is, and on Monday she will get in at the usual time and stand in the same place and he will say something else that he has to say to somebody and she will find out what it was then, and the four lines will be in the book at the third of the way for as long as the book is in that flat.

**SHE WROTE IT DOWN PROPERLY ON THE FRIDAY NIGHT WITH THE TIME ON IT AND IT WAS ON THE SAME PAGE ALL ALONG, AND SHE SPENT THE WHOLE DAY LOOKING FOR IT BECAUSE THE SENTENCE SHE WAS LOOKING FOR WAS NOT THE SENTENCE SHE WROTE, AND HE WROTE TO HER A FORTNIGHT AGO AND SHE HAS NOT ANSWED AND IS NOT GOING TO TODAY, AND HE WILL BE GONE BY CHRISTMAS AND SHE WILL KNOW WHEN.**
