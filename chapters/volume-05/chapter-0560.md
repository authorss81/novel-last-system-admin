# Chapter 0560 — The Third Sunday in March

The Wednesday of the twenty-second of April 2020, and the six weeks before it, in a town about half an hour from this borough and in one street of it.

**THE THREE OF THEM HAVE GONE TO THE SAME PLACE ON THE SAME SUNDAY EVERY MARCH SINCE 2013 AND IN THE MARCH OF THIS YEAR THE MIDDLE ONE OF THEM DID NOT GO, AND THE OTHER TWO FOUND OUT OVER A MEAL THAT NIGHT, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS ASKED HER WHY, AND SHE CAME BACK ON THE NINETEENTH OF APRIL AND SAID ONE SENTENCE ABOUT SOMETHING ELSE.**

There is a place about half an hour off, in a town where the road changes its name twice, and it does food and about nine other things after that, and it has a window along one side that people sit at.

For seven years the three of them have gone there on the third Sunday in March. Same time, same window, the place on the left as you come in. Each of them has paid for his or her own. Nobody has ever written down that arrangement because there is nothing to write down.

The sister is a woman in her mid thirties. The brother who is eldest is a man in his late forties and the brother who is youngest is a man in his early forties, and there has not been anybody else in it since about 2011.

On the Sunday of the fifteenth of March the two brothers went.

They left at about ten and were there for half an hour, and they sat where they sit, and she was not there at half past the hour and she was not there at twenty to twelve and she was not there at twelve.

Neither of them said anything about it in that place. The youngest read the whole of a menu and put it down and picked it up again. The eldest looked out of the window at the road for most of it, at a woman loading a car, at the same road.

They ordered at about ten past twelve and they ate and they paid separately at the till because they have always paid separately, and they left at about twenty past twelve, and neither of them said one word in the car.

That night the two of them ate at the eldest one's, which is what happens about four times a year, and the meal took about an hour, and there was a point in the middle of it where the youngest put his fork down.

"You going to say anything?"

"No," the eldest said.

He picked his fork up again. The other one said the fish were no good and had never been any good, and the eldest agreed with him, and they finished it and washed up in the way they wash up, and the younger went home at about ten and left the bowl on the side for the other one to find in the morning, which is what has happened since about 2016.

She had decided in the February and she did not tell either of them, and neither of them has asked her since, and she has not said it either, and what she is carrying is not the window and not the town and not the money.

She has not written it down and she has not told a living soul, and there is no part of it that either of the two of them could get at by looking at her for an afternoon.

The sister heard about it the way anybody hears about a thing like that. The youngest sent her a photograph on the Monday of what they had eaten and nothing else, and she sent back one word about it, and that was the whole of the exchange.

She has not mentioned the Sunday to either of them. She has not said she is sorry. She has not said she is not coming again. She has not asked why the two of them went without saying, and she has not told either of them what she decided in the February, or on what day, or in what state of mind.

She has seen both of them since. She has been in the same shop as the eldest on a Thursday and in the same shop as the youngest on a Saturday and has said how are you to both of them and meant it both times.

On the Wednesday of the eighteenth of March she was outside a shop at the same time as the eldest and they said three things about a road that was being dug up and then went their own ways.

On the Sunday of the nineteenth of April she went to the youngest brother's house at about four in the afternoon and put a box of shopping down on his step.

Then she said, "There's a new crack in the path outside your door," and he said, "There is," and she said, "It's going to get under the door before the year is out," and he said, "It's been doing that since Christmas," and she said, "I know," and she stood on that path for a minute and a bit and then got in her car and went home.

That is all she said. It was about the path and it was not about the fifteenth of March, and he let her get in the car, and he did not ask her in, and he shut the door after the car had gone and stood in his own corridor for a bit.

He has not mentioned it to the eldest. He has not mentioned it to anybody, and the path outside that door is going to get worse and the door is going to stick, and neither of those is what either of them is thinking about.

**NEITHER OF THEM HAS ASKED HER WHY SHE DID NOT COME AND SHE HAS NOT SAID, AND THE ONE THING SHE SAID ON THE NINETEENTH WAS ABOUT A CRACK IN A PATH, AND SHE HAS NOT BEEN BACK SINCE, AND IT IS NOT SOMETHING THE THREE OF THEM TALK ABOUT.**
