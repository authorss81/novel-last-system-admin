# Chapter 0758 — The Joints In The Front Path

Friday the sixteenth of September 2022, in this borough. She is in her own house at number nine from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a front step and path picked out twice, the second time worse.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES PICKS THE MOSS OUT OF THE JOINTS OF HER FRONT STEP AND PATH, THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING PICKS THEM HER OWN WAY AND PACKS THE LOOSE ROOT BACK IN, AND THE TWO OF THEM STAND AND LOOK AT THE JOINT AND NEITHER PUTS A HAND IN IT AGAIN.**

She is a woman in her late fifties at number nine on Friday. The woman who does her shopping comes to the front door with the bags, puts them down on the step, and does the second half of the picking.

---

She is in the kitchen from morning with the fire unlit and the back door shut true and its card at the hinge where it has been since March. The pads stand on the fridge in their stack with the first one at the bottom. The front room door is shut and the stools behind it are dusted and nobody is on them. She has sixty watts in the ceiling and the jug standing on the dresser with its lid down and the kettle on top of it.

Out of the front door the step goes down to a path of flags with a low rail along it, and the autumn has come into the joints of the flags and the risers of the step in the way it does when the summer is long and the rain comes in short heavy goes. There is moss in the joint at the top riser, and grass in the joint at the bottom one, and three flags along the path have a green edge down one side and a lifting corner where the root has got underneath.

She has a lollipop stick with the point broken off it and she is on her knees at the top riser from the middle of the morning, working along the joint with the stick, lifting the moss out at the root and dropping it in the flower bed at the side where it will not show. It is slow work and it comes out in a mat at a time, and the mat has white root in it like thread, and she puts the mat in the bed and moves along the joint.

She is halfway along the second flag when the woman who does her shopping comes up the path with the bags.

---

"You are down on your knees in the cold for that."

"That moss will be under the ice by the end of the week if I do not do it now." She does not get up. "The root is in the joint. That is the whole of it. You cannot cut it, you cannot drown it, you get it out at the root and the joint dries and it comes back next spring and not this year."

"I have a blade in my bag."

"A blade cuts the green off and leaves the root." She taps the joint with the stick. "That is worse than nothing. That is the grass in March and no green to see it coming."

The woman puts the bags on the top step and stands looking down at her.

"You have been on this joint for an hour."

"I have been on this joint for an hour and I am halfway along the second flag."

"Then give me the other end." The woman puts her handbag on the step beside the bags. "I will do the bottom of it while you are on the top and you will not have to turn round at every flag."

---

She gives her the stick without arguing, because there is a third flag with a lifting corner and she wants to get at the underside of it, and the woman goes down on her knees at the bottom of the path where the grass is worst.

It is not the same job.

"Where did that go." She looks round at the side of the bed. "You have taken the whole mat out."

"That is what I told you." The woman is three flags down and going fast. "You cannot take the moss out of a joint. It has not got a root you can get at. You get the whole thing up off the bed and you take it back and you put something else in the joint or you leave it open."

"That is not what I told you."

"That is not what I told you but it is what there is." The woman reaches into the bed at the side, not caring where the mats have gone. "Give me the blade out of my bag and I will cut the roots and the moss will not come back till next spring and that is a win either way."

"The root will be in the joint in the spring and it will go down the water and lift the flag."

"The flag is lifted already." The woman sits back on her heels and takes up the corner of the third flag so that she can see the daylight under it. "Look at that. That is not this week's moss. That is a flag that has been up since the winter and the moss has come in behind it and now it has gone green round the edge and the root has nowhere to go but down and out."

She looks at the underside of the flag and sees exactly what the woman is describing, a brown felt of root and old moss about the thickness of a thumb, and she cannot fault a word of it.

"Get it up," the woman says, and gets a blade out of her handbag and carries on. They do the whole path that way. The woman works with the blade and her fingers and comes up each mat whole with the root in it, and she drops them in the bed by the rail and does not look down, and by the middle of the day there is a heap along the bed the length of the path out of sight and the joints are bare to the sand.

Then the woman begins to put the loose back.

"Leave that."

"It will be as bad as a joint of mud and freeze in it and lift the flags in the spring."

"It will be a joint of mud and freeze in it." She puts a double handful of the loose down into the joint at the bottom riser and pushes it in with the end of the blade until it stands proud and knocks the sides of the flag. "That is what a joint is. It is sand with something in it. You do not leave a hole."

"You have put the green in."

"I have put what was in it back in it." The woman stands up off her knees and puts her hands on the rail and looks down the path. "It is not the same green. It is loose, it will go down in the first rain, and the whole of it will be in the gully in a fortnight and then you have got a joint and a good flag."

She stands on the step and looks down the length of it.

The path is done and it looks done. The joints are packed and level with the tops of the flags, the moss is off the risers, the lifting corner is down, and from the front door it is the best the path has looked since the spring. Along the bed under the rail there is a heap of mats and root and old green the length of the path, black underneath and drying on top.

The woman is at the step with her handbag in her hand and the bags of shopping still sitting where she put them.

"You are not going to put the other half in."

"I have put the other half in."

"You have put the top of it in." She comes down off the step and kneels at the bottom riser and pushes a thumbnail into the joint and brings up a pinch of it and lets it drop. "It is loose. It is in the top half only. The bottom half is still sand, and the root went back in with the first handful, and now it is packed over the top of the root."

"It is a joint. It is packed. It is done."

"It is done." The woman does not touch the joint. "And in the winter the water comes up out of the flags through that joint and stands in it, and it goes green again by Christmas and greener by February, and you will be on your knees in the same place with the same stick at the same speed."

She stands up and brushes her hands down her coat and looks at the joint without putting a hand in it.

The woman puts her handbag on her shoulder and looks down the path at the heap of mats along the bed.

"Do you want the shopping in or on the step."

---

"In." She picks up the bags and turns the key. "In through this door and straight into the kitchen, and you will not want to see the front room."

The woman stops on the step with her hand on the frame while she goes back for the rest of them, and neither of them looks down at the joint at the bottom riser again, where it stands packed level and a little proud the whole length of the flag.

Then she has the bags on the kitchen table in the dark and the front door shut behind her, and her thumb and the two fingers beside it are grey right down in the creases from the joint, and she stands and looks at them and then washes them at the cold tap, and the grit goes down the plug hole and the path outside keeps what it was packed with.
