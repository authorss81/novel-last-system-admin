# Chapter 0572 — The Hour She Is Not There

The Tuesday of the sixteenth of June 2020, and most of one evening of it, in a canteen about a mile from this borough.

**SHE HAS HAD ONE EVENING A WEEK FOR SEVEN YEARS WHEN SHE IS NOT IN THAT PLACE, AND A MAN WHO HAS SAT OPPOSITE HER AT THE SAME TWO SEATS SINCE BEFORE IT CHANGED HANDS HAS NEVER ASKED HER WHERE SHE GOES, ON THE SIXTEENTH OF JUNE HE WAS STILL IN HIS SEAT AT HALF PAST SEVEN WHEN SHE GOT UP, AND HE CAME IN TWELVE MINUTES LATE WITH NOTHING TO OFFER FOR IT, AND SHE DID NOT LOOK UP, AND IT HAS NOT HAPPENED SINCE.**

There are thirty seats in there and it is a canteen in a building that has grown three times. You pay at a hatch on the way in and you take what you are given and you sit where you can.

Two of those seats have been his and hers since before he came. They are at the end, against the far side, with a foot of gap between them and about four feet of clear floor behind.

He is a man in his mid forties. She is a woman in her early fifties. They work in different parts of the same building and they have eaten there on and off since before either of them had anything to say about it.

She goes on one evening a week and has done since 2013.

She gets up from those two seats at half past seven, puts her things over the back, goes out, and comes back at half past nine and sits down and eats whatever has been kept for her.

What he knows about that evening, without ever having asked, is four things and he has them the way a person has weather. She does not eat on it. Her hands are cold when she gets back. It is further than one ride up the road. And she has never once said a word about where she has been, and he has never once been the one to ask.

He worked the length of it out in about 2016, from the hour she gets back and from what she does with her sleeves. He worked the cold in her hands out between November and March, when there is a service she could have come back on that does not go the way she comes.

That is as far as it goes, and he has never in all that time gone a step past it.

On the Tuesday of the sixteenth he stayed on.

He had finished at half six, which is when he finishes most days, and he did not get up. He sat in that seat with his hands round an empty mug and there were nine people left in there and then about five and then about two.

At half past seven she got up and put her things over the back and said, "I'm going."

"Alright," he said.

She went out the hatch side and he watched the door for a second after it had shut and then he looked down at his hands.

There is a service from the front of that building at about twenty past nine. He knows that because he has used it twice.

He sat there from half past seven until about twenty to ten. Then he got up and went out the hatch side and up the path and round to the front and got on the one that was due, and it went at about ten to ten and it got him in at about twenty to ten.

That is twelve minutes. It is the same twelve minutes every day and he had never had any occasion to find out what it was.

She was at her seat when he got in. She had her tea in both hands and she did not look up and she did not move anything off the seat opposite, which she had done twice in all the years he has known her and not once since before he came.

He said nothing about the twelve minutes and she said nothing about them either, and neither of them has brought the twelve minutes up since, and he has not stayed on past his own time again.

**WHAT HE IS CARRYING IS THAT HE KNEW FOR YEARS THAT SHE WENT SOMEWHERE ON A TUESDAY EVENING AND NEVER FOUND OUT WHAT, AND THAT ON THE SIXTEENTH OF JUNE HE FOUND OUT IT COSTS HER ABOUT AN HOUR AND THREE QUARTERS EACH WAY WITH THE WAITING IN IT, AND THAT SHE WILL NOT BE TELLING HIM ANY OF IT, AND THAT SHE LOOKED AT HIM FOR ABOUT HALF A SECOND AND WENT BACK TO HER TEA.**
