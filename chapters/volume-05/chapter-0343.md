# Chapter 0343 — I'd Rather You Didn't

Friday the eleventh of August 2017, from about ten in the morning until about half past four in the afternoon, a street off the Cleeve road, a shop off a parade, and that street again, in Tideglass.

**A MAN SAID THE SAME SIX WORDS TWICE ON ONE DAY TO TWO PEOPLE WHO WERE BOTH ENTITLED TO HEAR THEM AND WHO BOTH DID SOMETHING WITH THEM, AND HE HAS NOT FOUND OUT WHICH OF THE TWO OF THEM HE MEANT, AND NEITHER OF THEM WILL EVER ASK HIM.**

The street runs off the Cleeve road and stops about a hundred yard in at a T against the back of a row, and there are nine houses on the left and four on the right and a man lives at the end of it on the left in the last house, and he has lived there nine years and he is not on anything and no road in this borough is his.

There are six sheets of corrugated iron leaning against the side wall of that house on the footway, about four foot by two foot each, corrugated both sides, with the rust on the underside of the top one, and they went up against that wall in the second week of July when he had the roof of an outbuilding at the bottom of that street taken off, and they have been leaning there since the second week of July and the grass has grown up about the bottom two of them.

They are not in anybody's way. That footway is about six foot wide and the sheets take up about two foot of it and there is four foot past them. What they are, said out loud on that street once, is a hazard to a woman with a bag, and that is all that has ever been said about them.

He put them up himself with a man from a place on the ring road, and he did the work over two days in the second week of July, and it took about three hours, and the man from that place said when he had finished that he would not want to be having them leaning like that, and the man at the end of the street said that they would be off by the end of the summer, and neither of those two sentences has been said to anybody since.

The bottom two of them have got grass up about them about a foot now, and the rust has come down the face of the top one in two wet weeks in July, and a woman who lives at number nine on that street has moved her bin out from under them and back under them twice and has not said anything about it to anybody, and the man at the end of that street knows about the bin because he watches from his step and has never mentioned it to her.

---

A man in a fleece was at the bottom of that street at about ten on the Friday morning with a paper in his hand, on his way to somewhere, and the man at the end of the street was on his own front step with a cup.

"You've got them across the footway," said the man in a fleece.

"They're off the road," said the man at the end of the street.

"They're across the footway, and there's a woman goes past that corner with a bag most mornings, and one day she is going to go past it and go over them." The man in a fleece took a drink of his coffee. "That's all. I'm not telling you what to do about it."

The man in his forties came up that street at about ten past ten and the man in a fleece said it to him and then went on down it, because the man in his forties is the one anybody in that street would ask.

"Who put them up."

"That one," said the man in a fleece, and nodded at the step, and went.

The man at the end of the street was still on the step with the cup and he said it to the man in his forties as he got level with him, and he said it the way a man says a thing he has already decided, and he said six words and did not explain them.

"I'd rather you didn't."

The man in his forties walked on about ten foot and then stopped and came back.

"Didn't what."

"Move them," said the man at the end of the street, and went inside and shut his front door, and he did not say one word about why, and the man in his forties did not ask him why.

The man in his forties has not touched those six sheets. He was in that street four times that day and he went past them on the road side every time, and there is nothing in this borough where a man like him is going to be told he has to move somebody's roofing.

He was asked by the man in a fleece and he was asked in the middle of a street by a man with a paper under his arm, and a man being asked in the middle of a street in this borough can either do the thing or say he is not doing it, and there is no third of those, and the second one is not a brush-off if you say it straight, and he said it straight.

That is all that happened at about ten past ten on the eleventh of August, and it took about nine seconds, and one of the nine was the man in a fleece going on down the street.

At about a quarter past one that afternoon the man at the end of the street was in a shop off a parade, and he had a length of batten in his hand that he had not been in there to buy, and the woman who works in a shop had the till open and there was nobody else in the shop.

"There's a fella told me to leave them," said the man at the end of the street. "Not him. A man at the bottom of it. He said there's a woman goes past with a bag."

"There's a woman goes past with a bag," said the woman who works in a shop. "There's four of them go past with a bag between the postbox and that corner. I have watched it from this window and it's about one in ten that has to go round."

"I know," said the man at the end of the street. "I have watched it too, from the step. I would rather you didn't."

She put the batten down on the counter and took her hand off it.

"Right," she said.

"I am not going to say what to," said the man at the end of the street. "That is the whole of it. There is a thing I would rather you didn't and you are the one I am saying it to, and I have already said the same six words to another man in that road this morning, and he is welcome to it as much as you are, and I am not going to tell you which of you I have said more to."

"You've said it to him right," said the woman who works in a shop.

"He can do as he likes with it," said the man at the end of the street.

He paid for the batten, which he did not need, and went out into the parade, and it is not written on anything at that shop and there is no card in that window and there is no tick anywhere, and the woman who works in a shop has not mentioned those six sheets to a living soul since and would not have mentioned them to the man in his forties if he had come in and stood at that counter for an hour.

What she took it to be, and she has never said this out loud to anybody including herself, is that a man was telling her not to put it in the window. That is the reading that came back to her first and it is the one that sat there, and it is not a wrong reading of six words, and it is not the reading he gave them.

There is a card that goes in that window about four times a year and it is the only thing in that shop that is ever written down, and on the last one in March a woman who walks with a stick said the thing in the window was a load of rubbish, and the woman who works in a shop wrote nothing and said nothing, and that has not come up again either.

---

There was a woman with a bag at that corner at about midday and again at about a quarter to two and both of those times she was in the road about two foot wide at the wall side of those sheets with a bag hooked over her wrist, and neither time she said anything to anybody, and both times the man at the end of that street was on his step watching her do it.

The man in his forties came to the bottom of that street again at about half past four, going the other way, and there was the man in a fleece at the corner with a paper under his arm.

"You not doing anything about them sheets," said the man in a fleece.

"I've not been asked to do anything about them sheets," said the man in his forties.

"That's the whole of what I wanted to know," said the man in a fleece, and went off up the street.

Six sheets of corrugated iron are leaning against the side wall of a house at the end of that street and the grass has grown about the bottom two of them and there is a hole in the road beside them where a man in this borough put one foot on going past on the eleventh of August, and it has rained on them since and the rust is down the face of the top one, and the man who put them up knew what he meant when he said the six words, or he did not, and he has not thought about it since the middle of the afternoon, and it will not come round again.

**A MAN BOUGHT A LENGTH OF BATTEN AT A QUARTER PAST ONE IN THE AFTERNOON THAT HE DID NOT NEED, AND SAID THE SAME SIX WORDS HE HAD SAID TO A STRANGER IN A ROAD THAT MORNING, AND WENT HOME.**
