# Chapter 0526 — No Need for You to Come

The Tuesday of the twenty-ninth of October 2019, and the rain was the sort that gets into a collar and then warms up, at the end of a row in Tideglass and in a street about five minutes' walk from it.

**THEY HAVE SAID GOOD MORNING TO EACH OTHER ON A TUESDAY MORNING SINCE 2015, AND HE HAS WALKED SLOWER PAST THE END OF THAT ROW SINCE MARCH AND HAS DONE IT ONCE THIS MORNING, AND THE OTHER MAN CAME OUT OF THE ENTRANCE AT TEN PAST EIGHT AND SAW HIM DOING IT.**

They are a man of about fifty-one and a man of about fifty-five and they have been in and out of the same trade since they were boys, and there is not much between them that either of them would put differently.

The younger one drives for a haulier on an estate four miles north. The older one drives for a different haulier, over the water. Both of them are out at about seven. For years the younger man's first job of the day took him along that row, and the older man has been finished at ten past eight since he took the rooms over a shop at the end of it, and from then on the younger one slowed down at the end of the row and said good morning, and the older man said good morning, and the younger one carried on.

The older man's part is standing on a pavement at ten past eight, which he would be doing anyway. The younger man's part is about two seconds of not walking at full speed, which nobody could see from a first-floor window and which neither of them has ever mentioned, and which is not the kind of thing a man remarks on.

The rooms over the shop are the only part of this that has a shape anybody could describe, and the shape is this: the older man comes out of an entrance at the end of a row of small shops at ten past eight most days and stands on the pavement, and the younger man comes along that row at ten past eight most days, and in all that time the two facts have stood next to each other without once touching.

---

The other thing is the March of 2019.

On the Friday before, which was the twenty-second of March, the older man had said in a yard that his lorry was playing up. He had said it sideways, in the manner of a man who has settled in advance that nobody is going to do anything about it, and the younger man heard it and thought about it for a day and then drove over on the Sunday, which was the twenty-fourth, with the intention of looking at it.

When he got there the lorry was outside, loaded, and signed off, and it had been signed off since the Friday afternoon, and the older man was at the back of it with his thumbs through his belt, pulling a car's catch shut behind him.

"There was no need for you to come," the older man said.

The younger man did not say anything. He stood in the yard for a moment and then he got back into his own lorry and drove home, and he did not put it to him on the way out, and the older man did not say anything else.

It was true. That is the thing about it that the younger man has been unable to get round in seven months.

There was no need for him to come. The lorry had been fixed the Friday afternoon, before he set off on the Sunday, by a man who was still there and who told the older man so in front of him. The older man had said on the Friday that the lorry was playing up, and the older man had said it in a yard with fourteen men in it, and it had been the sort of remark a man makes so that he does not have to say what he is going to do about it.

Everything the older man said was accurate, including the tone. There was nothing in that yard on that Sunday that the younger man could have done differently except not turn up, and not turning up was not in him, because there is a thing about him that has been true since he was about seventeen and that has cost him more than it has ever got him, and that thing is that if a man says a thing is playing up he will go and look at it.

The older man was not unkind about it. That is the other half and it is the half that has done most of the damage. He did not say it to put the younger man in his place. He said it in the tone of a man stating something that has simply turned out to be true and that he has no interest in arguing about, and then he shut his car and went in, and the younger man stood in the yard in the rain for about a minute and then got back into his own lorry and drove home.

He has not been at the end of that row since. Not once in seven months. His first job still takes him along it and there is a lay-by outside a place that sells tyres about two thirds of the way down, and he has slowed at the lay-by in the ordinary way, and then he has gone on at the ordinary speed, and the difference is about two seconds, and nobody has ever seen it happen and nobody ever will.

---

He decided at about half past seven, in his own kitchen, while he was making something to take with him. He lives on his own and has done since about 2013 and there is nobody in that house to tell.

That is the size of the decision. There was no speech and nothing went down on anything, and he was not certain when he had arrived at it, and he was not certain while the bread was in that he had arrived at it at all.

He got to the end of the row at about five to eight. You could see the far end of the row and the shape of the parked things along it, and the rain had not stopped and had not got any worse, and the older man was not out.

The younger one slowed down. He came to about the pace a man comes to when he is going to stop, and then he went on walking, and he got to the end of the row, and the older man was coming out of the entrance.

He had his hood down and his hand shut the way a man shuts it when there is something in it that is not worth putting down, and he saw the younger man, and he stopped.

Neither of them said good morning.

"There was no need for you to come," the older man said.

It is the same sentence, word for word, in the same voice, on a wet Tuesday morning at the end of a row of shops, and it took the younger man about two seconds to understand that he is not going to be told anything else.

There are two things it could mean and he worked out both of them inside the two seconds. It could mean that the older man remembers the yard in March and is standing there telling him not to come back to the end of that row. Or it could mean that the older man has not remembered March at all, and has got the sentence from somewhere, and that somewhere is a person, and that the sentence has been said to somebody else and is now being said to him.

He did not ask which.

What he had ready, in the two seconds, was the question, and it was ready because he had had it ready since about the sixth of April, and it was not a difficult question and it was not one that would take long. He did not ask it. He has not worked out since whether he did not ask it because he did not want to be told, or because he wanted to be told and could not stand the possibility of either answer, and he has no intention of finding out, and nobody is going to put it to him, because he has never let on to a living soul that he has got it.

Two men came out of the shop opposite with a long folded thing between them and had to turn sideways to get along the pavement, and the younger man moved back off the pavement to give them room, and when he looked up again the older man was still standing there.

"It'll hold till the weekend," the older man said, and nodded at the sky.

"Right," the younger man said.

The older man went in. The younger man stood where he was for about half a minute and then walked off up the row at his own speed, and he did not say anything as he went, and he did not look back, and he has not rung that flat since and there is no number in this borough that would get him to it.

He walked the five minutes home and he did not slow down once on the way, not at the lay-by and not at the bridge, and he was in his own kitchen at a quarter past eight with his lunch in his hand, and he stood at the end of his own front and ate it.

**HE STOOD AND ATE IT ALL, AND HE DID NOT SLOW DOWN ONCE ON THE WAY HOME, AND HE IS NOT GOING TO ASK, AND THE OTHER MAN HAS NOT SAID ONE WORD ABOUT MARCH, AND IT IS THE FIRST TUESDAY SINCE MARCH ON WHICH THE TWO OF THEM HAVE SAID NOTHING TO EACH OTHER.**
