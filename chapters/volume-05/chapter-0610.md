# Chapter 0610 — The Bag on the Wall

The Wednesday of the sixteenth of December 2020. It runs from about nine in the morning until about six in the evening. In this borough.

**SHE HAS BEEN MAKING A BAG UP EVERY WEDNESDAY MORNING SINCE BEFORE THE SUMMER AND SHE PUTS IT ON THE WALL BY THE DOOR, AND THE BAG IS FOR A WOMAN IN HER MID TWENTIES WHO LIVES UP THE ROAD AND CANNOT CARRY IT, AND TODAY THE WOMAN CAME OUT OF HER FRONT DOOR AT ABOUT A QUARTER TO TWELVE AND SAW HER STANDING THERE WITH IT.**

She is a woman in her early sixties. The woman she makes the bag for is in her mid twenties and lives about two hundred yards up a road of terraces and is at the front door of her own house about four times out of five.

---

The bag has been going on the wall since before the summer and it goes on at about nine in the morning and it is off the wall by about ten past.

Here is what it is, because the thing about it is that it is not a bag.

There is a loaf. There is a bag of shopping for about a person who eats. There is a wrapped thing from a bakery on the parade, which is the only part of it she does not decide herself, because she does not know what the girl wants and the girl has never once said.

And there is a piece of paper in a plastic wallet, and inside the paper there is a list, and the list has twelve things on it, and the list is weeks out of date every Wednesday, and always has been, and she does not date it.

The list is the whole of it. If the list is in the bag then somebody else has a copy of what this woman thinks a person in her mid twenties needs, and the list goes out of the house and is carried up a road.

Here is what she does and what she has never done about it.

She was a nurse. She did it in a hospital and then she did it in a place on the industrial estate and then she stopped about 2014 and has never worked since.

She is good at this. That is the thing that has made it hard.

She knows what a person with a bad hand needs and she knows what a person with money to spend needs and she knows the difference between a woman who cannot get to a shop and a woman who cannot carry anything back, and she knows which of the twelve things on the list are the ones that matter, and she has marked three of them with a ring, and the three are the things the girl would not buy for herself if she had money.

She has never asked the girl a single question about any of it. She has never been in the girl's house. She has never been past the front step and the girl has never come down the road to hers.

Here is today.

She was up before eight and she did what she does with the list, which is not to write it but to go over it in her head, in the sequence it goes in, and put the four or five that are not right on any given Wednesday in a pile at the side of the board and then put three or four of them back on.

That is the whole of how the list works and it has produced the same twelve things every Wednesday since before the summer and it is not a good list and it is not a bad list and it is a list.

The girl has been in hospital twice since the June and did not tell her, and she found out both times from the woman who does the shops across the road, and both times she put four extra things in the bag without saying anything about the hospital.

She was at the door with it at about ten past ten.

It is not a big bag. It is a carrier bag with the handles knotted over so it does not slide, and there is a strap on the shoulder of her coat that she uses for a folding seat, and she put the bag on the low wall by the door and stood next to it.

And then she did what she does, which is stand there.

That is the honest name for it and she would not use it herself.

She has worked out that if she goes back in then the girl has to come to the door, and a woman of twenty-five with one arm who has to come to a door is worse off than a woman of twenty-five with one arm who finds a bag on a wall. So she goes in, and then she comes out of the house again a bit later to fetch something she does not need, and the two of them get a couple of minutes.

The girl came out at about a quarter to twelve.

She was in a coat with the hood up and she came down the path and she saw the bag on the wall before she saw the woman, and she stopped at the gate.

And the woman said, "That's come."

And the girl said, "It's early."

And the woman said, "It's not early."

And the girl said, "It's nearly half past."

And the woman said, "I know what time it is."

And then the girl came across to the wall and put her hand on the bag to see what was in it, and the woman watched her do that.

She did it the way somebody looks for a thing they have decided is not there. Her hand went to the loaf first because that is where the loaf is, and then to the shopping bag inside, and then it stopped in the middle and stood still.

And the girl said, "There's no list in this one."

And the woman said, "There's no list in this one."

And the girl said, "Right."

And the woman said, "The bag's different."

And the girl said, "The bag's the same bag."

---

Here is the thing that the woman has been carrying and did not know she was carrying until about a quarter to twelve this morning, and it is this.

She has put a list into that bag about two hundred times since before the summer, and the list has been a document, and a document can be questioned, and she has understood all along that she does not want to be questioned, because the minute somebody asks her what is on it she has to have an answer that is not *because I know*.

And she put no list in the bag this morning.

She did not decide to. She made the bag at about half past eight and she went over the list at about ten to nine and she made the bag with the loaf and the shopping and the wrapped thing from the bakery, and there was no list in it, and she went and put it on the wall.

And she has not been able to work out since a quarter to twelve whether she left it out because she was in a hurry or because something in her knew.

The girl carried the bag back up the road with her good arm hooked under it and she did not say anything else and went in, and the woman went into her own house and shut the door.

She made a cup of tea and did not drink it.

Then she went and stood in the hall and did the thing she has never done, which is to open the plastic wallet and take the list out and look at the twelve items on it.

She looked at it for a while.

And she understood, standing in the hall of her own house at about half past twelve on a Wednesday in December, that the list is not for the girl.

The list is the reason she is still needed and the two of them have both known it and neither of them has ever said a word about it, and there was a document in a carrier bag two hundred times carrying the whole arrangement still.

And she put the list back in the wallet and put the wallet in the kitchen drawer where it lives, and she did not tear it up, and she did not put it in the bag on Wednesday, and she has not decided whether she will.

She went out again at about half past two with a cup she wanted emptied in a shop, and the shop was shut, and she stood at the gate with the cup in her hand.

The woman in her mid twenties came out at about ten past three carrying a bin bag, and she saw her at the gate, and she came down the path.

And the girl said, "There's a list in that cup."

And the woman said, "What?"

And the girl said, "You've put something in the cup. There's a sheet of paper with something written on it in it. I can see the corner of it."

And the woman looked down at the cup in her own hand and it was empty and she had not put anything in it and she stood there.

And the girl said, "Don't do that."

And the woman said, "I haven't."

And the girl said, "Right."

And the woman said, "I'll go and have a cup of tea instead."

She put the cup in the bin on the way back and went in and had the cup of tea she had not drunk at half past twelve.

**SHE PUT TWELVE THINGS IN A CARRIER BAG EVERY WEDNESDAY SINCE BEFORE THE SUMMER AND TODAY SHE LEFT THE LIST OUT OF IT FOR THE FIRST TIME AND THE WOMAN ASKED HER ABOUT IT AT THE GATE, AND THE LIST IS STILL IN THE DRAWER WHERE IT LIVES AND SHE HAS NOT DECIDED WHAT SHE WILL DO WITH IT.**
