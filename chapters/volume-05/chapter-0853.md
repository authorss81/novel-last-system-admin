# Chapter 0853 — Grit Into The Joints

Thursday the nineteenth of October 2023, along the path at number nine from the time the sun is onto the flags until the road has gone by, and the joints packed with grit is the whole of that Thursday.

**THE JOINTS OF THE PATH AT NUMBER NINE HAVE OPENED OUT WHERE THE RUN FALLS AND SHE MEANS TO BRUSH THEM OUT AND PACK THEM WITH GRIT WITHOUT LIFTING A FLAG, AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING STEPS OFF THE PAVEMENT ONTO THE GRASS TO COME ROUND RATHER THAN WALK ON THE JOINTS.**

That path runs from the gate at number nine to the front step, flags edge to edge, and it falls from the gate end to the step end by about the width of two fingers over the whole of it.

That fall is the whole of the trouble. On a level run a joint keeps what is put in it, and on a fall the joint is a drain and everything that goes in it goes on down and out at the low end, and what is left in the middle of a long joint by March is the grit that was too heavy to move.

What has gone out of these joints is the fine. Sand brushed in in the spring has gone down the fall and away, and what is left is the dish of the joint with a little coarse in the bottom of it and about half an inch of open air above that.

She does not lift a flag.

The bucket of grit is off the heap by the fence and it is dry and sharp and angular, the sort that comes out of a crusher and not the sort that comes out of a river, and it is the right thing for a joint on a fall because it is too heavy to wash out and too open to set like mortar.

She rakes a little of it into the bucket dry first and works it through her fingers, because grit that has been damped and dried in a bucket is grit with the fines back in it and it runs to the bottom of the joint and does nothing there.

The stiff brush goes into the joint first, on every one of them, and the dish comes clean and pale and you can see the edge of the flag on both sides of it with a finger's width of gap.

There is grass in two of them. A fine two-bladed grass, rooting in the joint, standing an inch and a half out of a dish full of open air with no soil under it at all.

She cuts those out with the hook first, right down into the joint and below the top of the flags, and takes the root with it and drops it on the path to go on the bed.

It is a different thing from the tuft she left standing in the kerb joint along the front. Grass rooting between two kerb stones is keeping the two stones to one another and there is nothing else in that joint doing it. Grass rooting in a flag joint is keeping nothing but its own bit of grit, and it keeps the top of the fill off the water and lets it in underneath, and a root in a flag joint is a root that will lift that flag in a wet winter.

The grit goes in dry and from the bottom.

She puts a shovelful into the dish and works it down with the back of the brush and the heel of the trowel, and it packs where she puts it and takes the shape of the gap, and she goes on until the dish is gone and there is grit standing a finger below the top of the two flags.

Not level with them. Below.

Grit standing level with the flags is grit standing proud of them, and grit proud of a flag is what a boot takes out, and what a boot takes out on a fall does not stay in the joint. A finger below is a joint that a heel crosses without finding anything to take.

The woman who does her shopping is on the pavement with her bags when she is half round the path, and instead of walking round on the flags she steps down off the kerb onto the grass and comes along the grass the whole length and up onto the path at the step end, and her feet never go near a joint.

She does it without any remark about it and she does not go slow.

The tenth joint is by the rail and it is the only one she does not fill.

The flag over it has been hollow under its near edge since the spring and has settled since, so it sits proud at the rail side and the joint under that edge is a pocket and not a dish, and the gap opens towards the rail instead of up towards the sky.

You cannot feed that one from the top. Grit pushed into it goes under the lip and along under the flag, and then it is under a flag in the dark with no way to see it and no way to put a hand on it, and the joint underneath that flag will go on exactly as it is whatever happens on top of it.

It wants the flag up and the ground under it packed, and that is a job for a day with a bar and two men and the rail off its brackets, and it is not a job for a Thursday with a bucket.

She sweeps that one out as far as the lip allows and leaves it open, and it is the only joint on the run that is empty at the end of the day.

She goes back along the path with a heel on each joint in turn. Nine of them take the heel and neither grit nor dust comes up and nothing moves. The tenth is as she left it and the flag above it gives a little under the heel and she stops there and does not go over it twice.

She thinks about the fall while she sweeps the grit off the tops of the flags. A path wants two things and only ever really gets one of them. It wants the joints kept full so that the feet of whatever walks on it go on to the stone and not down into the fill, and it wants the fill not to leave. On a level you can have both with anything at all. On a fall you can have them with something heavy enough to stay put and open enough to let the water through, and everything else has to be done by keeping the joints brushed out and full, which is a thing a person does and not a thing the path does for itself.

---

The bucket goes back on the heap by the fence, and the hook and the brush go by the door, and the two bits of grass root go out on the bed.

She stands at the step end when the road has gone by and looks along what is there. Nine joints full of grit with a finger of daylight still showing along both edges of each one, and the tenth open under the flag by the rail, where the lip is.
