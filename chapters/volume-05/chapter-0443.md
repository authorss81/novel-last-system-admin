# Chapter 0443 — A Stool in the Turning Head

Thursday the twenty-seventh of September 2018, about eight in the morning and about half past ten at night, a turning head at the top of Ashlay Street, in Tideglass.

**A STOOL STOOD IN THE MIDDLE OF A TURNING HEAD AT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND ONE MAN THOUGHT SOMEBODY HAD PUT IT OUT AND GONE BACK FOR SOMETHING, AND AT HALF PAST TEN AT NIGHT A SECOND MAN THOUGHT SOMEBODY HAD BEEN SITTING ON IT FOR A YEAR, AND BOTH OF THEM WERE RIGHT AND NEITHER HAS ASKED THE OTHER WHAT HE SAW.**

The turning head is where Ashlay Street ends in a hammerhead about twenty yard across, with a grass island in the middle of it that nobody mows between about June and about March, and the tarmac round the island is the widest bit of hard standing for about a mile.

The man in his forties came up Ashlay Street at about eight in the morning on foot, going to a van he had left in a side street, and he came into the head from the bottom, and there was a stool standing in the middle of it.

It is a wooden stool about eighteen inch high, four legs, no back, and it is standing on its legs in the middle of the tarmac about three yard from the kerb and not against anything, at an angle, with a bit of grit round the feet of it.

He stopped. He stood and looked at it for perhaps ten seconds and worked out the two obvious things, which are that somebody has carried a stool out of one of the houses along that head and put it down in the middle and gone back in to fetch something else, and that the man who did that is either inside one of the houses or standing at a window watching to see if somebody takes it.

He looked at the two or three front doors. There was no man at any of them and no curtain moved.

He decided he would give it a minute and then go on, and he did. It was still there when he came back off the side street with his van at about ten past eight, and it was still there when he came past it again at about a quarter to nine, and then he went to work.

He thought about it once more at about the middle of the morning, on a roof about a mile away, when the wind got under something. He thought, if it is still there at lunchtime somebody will move it, because by lunchtime on a Thursday everybody on that head is either at work or in a car. He did not think about it again until about half past ten that night, and by then it was a different thing entirely and he had no idea it was the same one.

---

The man in his thirties came up Ashlay Street at about half past ten at night, on foot, from the direction of the junction, and he had had something to drink earlier and not enough to matter.

He came into the head and stopped.

The stool was still in the middle of the tarmac at the same angle.

What he saw was this. The grit in front of it is flat. Not trodden all over, flat, in a patch about a foot across and a foot and a half deep, the shape and the size of two feet and the toe ends of a person sitting down facing the island with their knees out. The top of it is worn pale in two places, the front edge and the middle, and the pale is a different colour from the rest of the top and has been pale a long time, because the edges of the pale are soft and there is dust in the grain of the dark wood beside them.

Under the front edge there is a bottle top and there is a stub, and there have been a good many of them, and they have been trodden into the grit.

He stood there about two minutes.

His own reading, which he did not say to anybody and has not said since, was that somebody has been sitting on that stool facing the island most evenings for a year or more, and that nobody minds, and that it is the only place in that whole street a person can sit at that hour without somebody in a front room saying something about it.

He also thought, standing there, that the man who had put it out had never come back for it, and that whoever it belonged to had not asked after it, and that there is a difference between those two things and he could not have said which of them had happened.

There are sixteen houses looking onto that head and he does not know a soul in any of them. He has never been inside any of them. He has driven past that head in the dark more times than he could put a figure on.

The two men had not spoken to each other that day or the day before. They had not spoken to each other since a shop in June. He is not aware, standing on that island at half past ten, that anybody else in this town has looked at that stool in 2018, and if he had been asked he would have said it had been put out that morning by somebody in a hurry.

He went home at about a quarter to twelve.

---

The stool was gone on the Saturday morning.

There is no way of telling from that head whether it went back into a house, or was put out for something and collected, or was carried off on a barrow to somewhere else entirely. Neither man asked anybody, and neither man has mentioned it to the other or to anybody else.

They have seen each other twice since that Thursday, once in a shop in June and once on a pavement in the middle of August, and neither of them has ever stood on a corner with the other and gone through a morning with him, and there is no arrangement by which one of them would say to the other, out of the blue, on a pavement, that there was a stool in that turning head in September.


**A STOOL WENT OUT OF A TURNING HEAD IN SEPTEMBER AND TWO MEN GAVE IT TWO DIFFERENT EXPLANATIONS IN THEIR HEADS AND TOOK THEM HOME.**
