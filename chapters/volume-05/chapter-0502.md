# Chapter 0502 — The Other End of the Table

Sunday, the thirtieth of June 2019, from about half past two in the afternoon until about six in the evening. One street that comes off the top of a parade, and the whole length of it, in Tideglass.

**TWO MEN SOLD FROM THE SAME TABLE AT THE TOP OF THAT STREET AND IN JUNE 2013 THE YOUNGER OF THEM SAID ONE SENTENCE ABOUT WHAT WOULD HAPPEN IF EITHER OF THEM STOPPED, AND THE OLDER OF THEM SAID RIGHT, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS SAID ANOTHER WORD ABOUT IT SINCE 2013, AND IN MAY THE OLDER OF THEM FOUND OUT THAT HE HAD BEEN WRONG SINCE 2013 AND HAS TOLD NO PERSON, AND ON THE SUNDAY THE YOUNGER OF THEM CAME AT HALF PAST THREE AND WENT UP THAT STREET AND PUT UP HIS OWN TABLE WHERE THE OTHER ONE'S STOOD, AND THE OTHER ONE CAME BACK AT TEN PAST SIX AND STOPPED AT THE BOTTOM OF IT.**

The street is a shade over a hundred paces long. It comes off the top of a parade at the upper end and runs down to a row of low houses at the lower one, and it is straight, and there is a post at the top of it and a wall along the west side for most of the way and nothing at all along the other.

The older man is sixty-five or thereabouts and has had a table and a folding windbreak at the top of that street since about 1998. He is there every day but one, and the one day is a Sunday in December when the knee is bad.

The younger man is fifty or thereabouts. He stood at the other end of that table on the Fridays from 2011 until about March 2017 and then stopped coming, and has not been back since.

Nobody on that street has asked either of them why.

---

Here is the sentence. It is one sentence and it was said one Tuesday in the middle of June 2013 at about half past three, in the street, with about four people in it and the wind coming round the corner of the parade.

The younger man said: "If either of us is ever done for, the other one takes the table, and neither of us ever says the other one gave it up."

And the older man said: "Right."

And that is the entire negotiation. It took about as long as it takes to get a box of apples out of a car.

Here is what each of them did with it, and they are not the same thing.

The older man took it as a kindness, and he has been carrying it as one ever since.

What he heard in 2013 was a man saying *you can have this, and you do not have to ask, and I will not make a scene about it if you do.* And what he has done since is the exact thing that sentence was built to allow. He has got older and he has not once said to the younger man that his knee is going. He has got a woman who does his shopping on a Wednesday. He has been to a doctor about the knee twice and has not told either of them what was said, and the second time he came home and put it in the sideboard in the front room and shut the door of that and did not think about it again.

And what he has not done in all that time is get in touch. Not once. Not at Christmas, not in the March of 2016 when the man he works with died and the whole of that terrace turned out, not the January his own boiler went. Because a man who says *you can have it* and then rings a year later and says *have you thought about it* is a man who has misunderstood the sentence, and he is not going to be that man.

The younger man took it as an arrangement, and he has been carrying it as one since about half past three on a Friday in March 2017 when he stopped coming.

What he heard in 2013 was a practical arrangement between two men who are both aware of how it looks. He is the one who was going to have to stop first. He has a bad back and he was already doing the heavy end of it, and the older man was already the one who carried things and talked to people, and it had been arranged in one sentence by somebody who had noticed all of that before he had.

So he stopped coming one Friday and did not say he was stopping, on the grounds that saying it would start a conversation he had already had in his own head and settled. And then he did not go back, on the grounds that going back would put the other man in a position of having to say *come back*, which the sentence had specifically been worded to prevent.

Both of those readings are true. That is not the difficulty.

The difficulty is that they are answers to two different questions, and each of them is certain he asked the only question.

---

Here is what happened in May, and it took about four minutes in a doorway, and it is the reason the older man has said nothing to anybody since.

The man who lives at number nineteen, who is about seventy and who does not go out much, was in his own doorway on the Thursday of the sixteenth of May with his milk bottles in his hand, and the older man came up the street from the parade with his windbreak folded over his arm.

The man at number nineteen said, "You're all right for the season?"

And the older man said he was.

And then he said it, the way a man says something he has been keeping since the weather changed: "Your friend used to come down twice a week, didn't he. Before he stopped."

And the older man said that he had not seen him since about March 2017 and assumed he had gone further off.

He said: "I see him."

And the older man stood in the street.

He said, "He's not been up the top. He's been standing at the bottom. Near where the post is. He's been there Tuesdays and Fridays, I've seen him from the window, going on a year and a half, maybe more. Last time was about three weeks back and it was coming down."

And the older man said, "Coming down?"

He said, "Coming down."

And the older man said nothing for what the old man thought was a while and was in fact about a minute, and then he said he would not want to be keeping the man up and went back up to his table and put the windbreak up and stood behind it for the rest of that day and did not sell one thing.

Here is what he has been carrying since, and it is not the sentence, and it is not the man at number nineteen.

It is that a man stood at the bottom of that street in the rain on the wetter days for a year and a half, twice a week, with his back to the parade, and never came up it. And that the whole reason the other one had never come up it is that he had spent six years waiting to be told he could.

And that the sentence was about the table, and both of them have been using it to talk about something else entirely, and one of them has been doing it ever since on a street where the other one was standing at the bottom of in the wet.

He has not told anybody. He has not gone down to the parade to ask whether the man is in. He has not said one word of it to a living soul, and he has twice caught himself about to say it and did not.

Here is what happened on the Sunday.

The older man had gone out of that street at about half past one that afternoon in a car, about two miles, to his sister's, and he had not told anybody he was going and had not told anybody when he would be back, and that is not unusual and nobody would have thought about it.

The younger man came at about half past three.

He came on foot from the bus at the top of the parade and he had a table with him and he had carried it on a barrow from a car park two streets off, and he had it up the street in about nine minutes and it took him most of that.

He put it up at about ten to four at the same spot.

The windbreak was not there because the windbreak was with the older man, in a car, about two miles off, and what he put up instead was three boards on the street side of the table and it took him a while and it did not do any good and he left it.

There was nobody to speak to. He worked that street until about six and he sold about half of what he had brought.

At about ten past six the older man came back up that street on foot from the parade.

He got as far as the post.

He stood at the top of that street for about twenty minutes, on the same side, about thirty paces off, and he looked at the table and at the man behind it, and the man behind it looked up twice in twenty minutes and did not go over.

And then the older man turned round and went back down to the parade and got a bus.

**A MAN CAME UP A STREET AT TEN PAST SIX ON THE SUNDAY OF THE THIRTIETH OF JUNE 2019 AND FOUND ANOTHER MAN SELLING FROM A TABLE AT THE TOP OF IT, AND HE STOOD THIRTY PACES OFF FOR ABOUT TWENTY MINUTES AND THEN TURNED ROUND AND WENT BACK DOWN TO THE PARADE.**
