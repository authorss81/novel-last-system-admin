# Chapter 0302 — A Lamp in the Porch

Saturday the twenty-fifth of February 2017, from about half past six in the morning, a hall off a parade in Tideglass.

**A MAN CAME UP THAT PARADE IN THE DARK WITH A LADDER AND A BULB IN THE BACK OF A VAN AND PUT A LAMP IN A FITTING THAT HAD BEEN DEAD SINCE 2011, AND NOBODY ASKED HIM TO, AND HE ASKED NOBODY FOR ANYTHING, AND THE ONLY PERSON WHO FOUND OUT WAS A WOMAN WHO CAME IN AT EIGHT THAT NIGHT AND HAD NOT SEEN HIM DO IT.**

He was in that parade anyway. The shop next door to that hall has a landlord in a town sixty miles off who does not come, and on the Saturday of that week the man in the hi-vis was outside the front of it at about half past six in the morning with a set of keys from a man in a town four miles off who had a shop to open at eight and had been let into it by the person who holds the lease.

He had been up and down that parade four times since Christmas and the fitting over the porch of the hall had been dead every one of those times, and it had been dead since about 2011, and there is a switch inside the porch on the wall by the door that has never done anything because there was nothing on the other side of it.

The ladder was in the back of his van because it is always in the back of his van, and the bulb was in the back of his van because a box of them lives in the back of his van between the toolboxes and a coil of flex, and the old one in that fitting was not a bulb any more, it was a grey shape with a black ring round the neck of it.

He put the ladder on the top step of that porch and went up it.

He had the lamp in his hand for about four minutes and then the ladder came down and went in the van and the van came off the parade at about ten to seven.

---

The lamp is a bulkhead fitting about the size of a man's fist with a glass front and a chrome back and a ring on the bottom of it that comes out with two fingers and a bit of twisting. The ring is held by a grub screw and the grub screw had seized, and he got it off with a screwdriver and a drop of oil from the can that lives on his belt, and then the glass came off and the four little screws under it came out, and the old lamp came down on its flex and it was a lamp and not a bulb and it was about fourteen years old and it was wet inside.

He put the new one in and put the glass back and put the four screws in and put the ring on and did not need to put the flex back into anything.

Then he came down about three rungs and got a torch and put his head into the porch and looked at the switch on the wall by the door, which is an old brown bakelite switch on a round rose with a fuse in it behind the plate, and the fuse was a twenty-amp cartridge and it was fine, and there was nothing at all behind the switch, because whoever put that switch in had put a switch on a wall for a light that was never on that circuit, and the wiring behind the plate went up about a foot and stopped.

He cut the dead stub off with side cutters, came back up the ladder, opened the fitting, put the flex up into the rose and into the switch, and put the switch on the plate, and pressed the bottom of the switch with his thumb.

The lamp came on in that porch at about ten to seven in the morning and it came on warm and yellow and it put a circle of it on the boards about four foot across, and the meter is on the wall of that porch on the outside and he could see the reflection of the lamp in the glass of the meter from where he was on the ladder.

He put the ladder in the van, shut the back of it, and went up the parade towards the bank, and he was not going into that hall for about three hours.

---

Nobody was in that hall on the Saturday morning. It is not used on a Saturday until about half past seven in the evening and the woman of about forty-two with the key to that hall was not going in until half past five that afternoon, and the room on the first floor above the shop next door was empty because the man in it was at the works, and the strip fitting inside the porch came on with the street light outside it about a quarter past seven and the lamp outside went off again a few minutes after that, the way a photocell does.

The woman who works in a shop came in at about ten past eight that night and got as far as the door and stopped.

The porch was lit.

It is a small porch, about five foot by four, with a door on one side that shuts now and a meter on the outside wall of it and a boot scraper and a piece of matting and a dead plant in a pot on the step, and there had not been a light in it since about 2011, and the streetlight outside it came up over the top of the door from the corner and made a yellow smear on the glass and nothing at all inside.

"There's a light on," said the woman who works in a shop, to the hall.

"There is," said the man in his forties.

"Somebody's put a lamp in that porch."

"Aye," said the man in his forties, and did not put down what he had in his hand, which was a bit of paper with a figure on it that he has not passed to anybody and which was not about a lamp.

The woman of about forty-two, who holds the key to that hall, was at the counter with her hand in her pocket where her keys are, and she heard it, and she did not say anything about it, and the question of whether that lamp ought to be in that porch or ought not to be in that porch was not put to anybody in that room that night and was not put to the man who had put it there by anybody in that town.

The woman who works in a shop went in and sat down at the end of the table with her back to the window.

"It was off when I came at eight on Wednesday," she said. "So it's been done since Wednesday."

"Aye," said the man in his forties.

She sat with that for a moment.

"Right," she said, and swung her chair round the way she does, and said that she was not going to be the one to go and find out, and that whoever it was had better have had a ladder, and that there was a step on that porch you could go over.

The man in the hi-vis came up that parade again on the Tuesday with a different job about four doors along and he did not go past that hall, and nobody in that hall knew it was him, and nobody on that parade knew it was him, and the lamp in that porch is a lamp in a porch and is not on anything.

The hall has a meter on the outside wall of that porch and a pound and a bit in it most weeks and the woman with the key pays for that out of her own money, and a lamp on the same supply is a lamp on the same supply, and it does not put anybody on anything and does not take anybody off anything and does not make that porch a place anybody is entitled to stand in.

There is a bit of print under the switch on the wall of that porch now, about two inches by one, and it says the fitting's type and its wattage and an item number, and the item number is not a number anybody in this borough has ever had reason to write down.

**AND THE MAN WHO WORKS NIGHTS CAME UP THAT PARADE AT ABOUT TEN PAST SIX THE FOLLOWING WEDNESDAY MORNING AND SAW A LIT PORCH AND WENT PAST IT, AND HE HAS SAID NOTHING ABOUT IT TO ANYBODY AND WILL NOT, AND THE LAMP IS ON A DUSK SENSOR AND GOES OFF AT ABOUT TWENTY MINUTES PAST NINE.**
