# Chapter 0844 — Dust Out Of The Runner

Wednesday the thirteenth of September 2023, on the front step at number twelve from morning until the road quiets, and a worn runner full of dust is the whole of that Wednesday.

**THERE IS A BRUSH RUNNER BY THE FRONT AT NUMBER TWELVE WORN THIN AND SHE MEANS TO LIFT IT AND SHAKE THE DUST OUT WITHOUT WASHING IT, AND HER SISTER COMES DOWN THE PASSAGE WITH A BARROW AND TURNS IT AT THE GATE AND GOES BACK.**

That runner is the narrow brush mat inside the front, worn where feet turn on it, thin in the middle and thick at the ends, full of dust down to the backing.

The dust in it is a summer of boots, grey and soft, with grit at the bottom where the brush never reaches. A runner washed would take a day to dry and stink in the meantime, and shaking does for wool that washing ruins.

She lifts it first and lays it over the step. Light comes through the thin middle where the wear is, a pale patch the size of a hand, with the weave showing.

The step below is stone, marked with the outline of the runner in dust, dark round the edges where air never got. She brushes the outline clean and leaves the stone to air while the runner is up.

Shaking is done over the step, a fold at a time, snapping the runner downwind so the dust goes out onto the path and not back into the house.

She takes one end in both hands and snaps it. Dust comes out in a soft burst, hanging a moment before it settles, with grit pinging off the flags.

Her sister comes down the passage in the middle of the morning with the barrow empty, sets it down at the gate well clear of the flag packed in August.

“Mind the corner, it is fresh packed,” she tells her.

“I am round it,” her sister tells her, and wheels the barrow back up the passage without touching the flag, out of the dust of the shaking.

The wheels keep to the middle where the flags run true, and the shaking waits while they pass and goes on after.

She thinks about the runner while she snaps it, how wool wears where feet turn and nowhere else. The middle thin to the backing is a map of every coming and going through that door all summer, every visitor and every errand, written in worn pile. Shaking out the dust does not erase the map. It only makes it readable again.

Her arms ache with the snapping by the last fold, a clean ache across the shoulders, and dust has settled in her hair and on her lips, gritty and grey.

Fold follows fold. She shakes the runner end to end, then across, then end to end again, until a snap brings nothing but a faint puff.

The worn patch shows more once the dust is out. The middle where feet turn is thin to the backing, with the pattern gone and the warp showing through in lines. It is wear and not dirt, and no shaking nor washing brings pattern back to worn wool.

She lays the runner back inside, flat, with the worn patch to the light so it can be seen and stepped round. It lies without curling, which is the test of a shaking, and the ends sit square to the door.

She sweeps the dust off the step and the path where the shaking put it, into the pan and into the bucket, for the garden.

---

The brush goes back by the door, and the pan goes with it, and the bucket stands full of grey dust at the gate.

She stands on the road side of the step when the road quiets and looks at what is there. A runner lying flat again with the dust shaken out over the step, the bucket beside it, and in the middle the worn patch, pale and thin, showing where feet have gone all summer.
