# Chapter 0469 — The Armchair on the Kerb

Friday the twenty-fifth of January 2019, from about ten past seven until about ten to nine. A short stretch outside one building, in Tideglass.

**HE HAS BEEN CARRYING SOMETHING OUT OF HIS OWN HOUSE AND LEAVING IT ON THAT KERB EVERY WEEKDAY SINCE 2013, AND A WOMAN FOUR DOORS AWAY HAS BEEN BRINGING IT IN AT ONE, AND ON THE TWENTY-FIFTH OF JANUARY HE LEFT IT AND WALKED ON AND HAS NOT BEEN BACK FOR IT, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS ASKED THE OTHER ANYTHING.**

It is a wooden armchair with a plywood seat that has been webbed twice, and a cushion on it that has come loose at one corner and been tied down twice with a length of string, and it is his mother's and it is in the front room of that house and it has not been sat on in about six years.

He cannot have it in the room.

That is the whole of the reason and it is a reason he could say out loud in about four seconds and has never said. He is in that room every night. There is a bed in it and there is not space for the armchair. It was his mother's and it is not good and it is not worth anything, and he has known since about the summer of 2013 that he cannot keep it in the room and cannot have it in the passage, because the passage is four foot wide and it is where the meters are.

And he will not put it out on the road in the evening because it gets rained on.

So he carries it out in the morning.

He carries it out at about ten past seven and he puts it on the kerb at the point where that row of houses ends and the road bends, which is about ten yard in front of his own door, and he puts it facing the houses, and he walks on to the van.

He has done that about sixteen hundred times.

It goes out at ten past seven and it stays on that kerb until about one, and at about one a woman comes out of the last house of that row, lifts it off the kerb with one hand, carries it in, and puts it in her own front room by the door.

She has never asked him to. He has never asked her to. Neither of them has ever referred to it in six years, and there is no version of a conversation about it that either of them would have, and if you put them both in a room together the thing would take about nine seconds and end with one of them saying sorry and the other saying it was all right.

She has never been told where it goes after she has it. He has never asked. She takes it in and puts it by her door and on the Monday she puts it out again herself and he takes it back into his own passage at night and in the morning he carries it out again.

None of that has ever been said.

---

Here is what it is actually about and neither of them would give you this if you asked.

The armchair was in his mother's front room when she was living, which is a different house, and after, which is the same house he is in now, and it has been that armchair in that room for about ten years and the room has been his for six of them.

He does not want to throw it away. He has been within about a foot of throwing it away about nine times.

He cannot have it in the room. That is the whole of the physics of it, and no arrangement of his evenings is going to alter it.

And what that thing is, standing on that kerb from ten past seven until about one, is a thing in the world that is not in a house. That is what it has been for six years. It is out of the front room and it is not in a cupboard and it has not gone anywhere, and a man carrying it out of a door in the morning and a woman carrying it back in at one is a thing that two people are doing with a thing that neither of them owns and neither of them will discuss.

If he carried it out and put it on that kerb and it was not taken in, then it would be a thing that got left out. And he cannot do that to it. That is why he walks on and why he knows that somebody will come out at one.

On the Friday of the twenty-fifth of January he carried it out at ten past seven and put it on that kerb and turned round and walked back towards his own door.

He got about four yard.

And then he thought, not in words, in the way a body thinks at half past seven on a weekday morning, that he had a thing to do at about half past and that it was where it always was, and he carried on walking.

He got in the van.

---

He came back to that stretch at about ten to nine the same morning, because his van is on that road and he had to come back up it.

It was on the kerb. It had been rained on in between and the cushion on it, which is the colour of nothing much, is dark along the top about two inch deep where the rain has got into the seam, and there was a wet patch on the kerb underneath the front legs about the size of a tea tray.

It had not moved. Nobody in that row had moved it. The woman whose house it was comes out at about one, and nothing on that stretch would have brought her out at ten to nine.

He got back in the van.

He has not got out of that van on that side of the road since. He parks on the far side now. He has been parking on the far side since the twenty-fifth of January, which is a different street entirely and is about twenty seconds further away from his own front door, and he has done it every day, and he has not worked out a reason for it that he would say out loud.

And she came out at about one that day, the way she has come out at about one that day for six years, and she saw it on that kerb and she did not touch it.

She stood in her own doorway for about a minute.

She has been through it twice since and she has not got to a reason. The one she keeps coming back to is that picking it up now would be saying something about the twenty-fifth of January, and the twenty-fifth of January is a thing neither of them has mentioned, and it is not going to be her that turns the twenty-fifth of January into something that happened.

The other one, which she is not going to say to anybody, including herself, is that he has not come back for it, and that she has spent six years being the person who comes out at one, and that there is a version of this in which somebody else is the person who comes out at one, and she is not ready to have found out that she minded.

It was still on that kerb when it went dark. There is a wet patch under the front legs of it and nobody in that row has looked at it once today.

**ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF JANUARY A MAN CAME OUT OF A HOUSE IN THAT ROW AT TEN PAST SEVEN WITH A BAG AND WALKED PAST THE THING ON THE KERB WITHOUT LOOKING AT IT, AND AT ABOUT ONE A WOMAN CAME OUT OF THE LAST HOUSE OF THAT ROW AND LOOKED AT IT AND WENT BACK IN, AND THE WET PATCH UNDER THE FRONT LEGS HAD GROWN.**
