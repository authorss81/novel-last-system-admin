# Chapter 0436 — A Case, Laced Up

Saturday the twenty-fifth of August 2018, and the four working days that began on the Monday after it and stopped on the Thursday, one block of flats off a road off that parade, in Tideglass.

**A MAN IN HIS THIRTIES MOVED A CASE OFF A LOW TABLE IN A PASSAGE IN ORDER TO GET HIS OWN BAG OFF THE FLOOR BEHIND IT, READ A PIECE OF PAPER TIED TO THE HANDLE, PUT IT BACK, WORKED FOR TWO MORE DAYS UNDER IT, FOUND ON THE THURSDAY THAT IT HAD GONE, AND HAS NOT ASKED ANYBODY ANYTHING.**

The block is one of three that face each other across a turning head, and this is the one with the bin store and the drying green behind it. The job was a floor in a ground-floor back room that somebody had already taken up and found soft at the near corner, and it is four days, and he is there from about eight until about half past four.

The door is on the latch until nine, because the woman who lives in that flat is out by half past seven and does not want a key for a man she does not know. She came in on the Wednesday to get a coat and he was on his knees with a saw and she stood in the doorway for a moment and said, "You'll be all right, then," and that was the whole of what they said to each other in four days.

The case was on the table.

It is about two foot long, done up with two laces and a strap buckled over the top, and it is the kind a person takes to a hospital and brings back. It is packed. The laces are pulled tight, the strap is done, and the corners have gone square under whatever is in it, which means it was packed a while ago and has been sat on since.

It was on a low table in the passage with its handle up against the wall, and it has been on that table long enough to have a clean shape in the dust on the top of it, about two foot by nine inch, with the strap's edge across one end. He notices that on the first day and does not think about it again until the Thursday.

On the Monday he wanted his own bag. It was on the floor behind that table and it would not come out from under it without moving the case, so he put one hand on the case and swung the whole thing out about a foot on its hip and got the bag and set the case back where it had been, handle to the wall, strap at the top.

Tied through the handle, on a length of string, is a paper label the size of a postcard.

It is printed on one side and it is the sort of thing that comes off a rack in a booking office. There is a name on it. There is a place on it, and the place is not a name, it is a junction and then another place, and then a third in smaller type. And there is a date in October.

He read it in about a second and a half with the case swinging in his hand and his bag under his arm. He did not read it again that day. He did not turn it over.

He set the case back on the table, handle to the wall, at the same angle it had been at, or within an inch of it.

---

The floor went down to the joists at the near corner and up again with cement and sand and a new joist at that end, and that took the Monday, the Tuesday and most of the Wednesday, with the case in the passage the whole time and the door propped and the light on it. He walked past it about sixty times.

The woman came in on the Wednesday and went up and did not come down. On the Thursday she came in at about one and went up again. He heard her on that stair on the Wednesday and on the Thursday, and there are four flats on that stair and the other three were empty, and he worked that out on the Wednesday and not on the Thursday.

The case was not on the table and his bag was.

It had not gone out through the front door while he was there. He was out the back with the mixer running from about ten to twenty to twelve and in from about half past twelve, and there is one door to that flat and it is that front door, and it shuts on a hinge.

On the top of that table, where the case had been, there is dust, and the dust has a clean shape in it about two foot by nine inch, and the strap's edge is still marked across one end of it.

He stood and looked at it for a while. Then he went and finished the beading, which took about an hour and a quarter, and put his tools in his bag and shut the door and went.

There is no way of telling from that flat whether the case went on a bus, on a train or in a car, whether the date on that label is a day it was wanted by or a day it was coming back on, or whether it went at all. It may be in the car park at Brackness. It may be in a house in another town. Nobody in that building knows anything about it.

He has not been back to that block. There was nothing to go back for. The floor is up and the beading is on it, and the beading is the only thing in the world that anybody wanted out of those four days.

There is a mat by the front door of that flat that has not moved in the two years he has been in and out of that block, and it is the only thing in that passage he would have sworn to, and he would have sworn to the case as well, and on his way out that Thursday he stood in the doorway for a second with his bag at his feet and looked at the table and did not see anything on it at all, which is what a man does when there is nothing on it.

---

He has told nobody.

Not the man he works with, who would ask what he was doing at the end of that week and would be told, and would then have to sit there being told that nothing had happened. Not the woman at the counter where he buys his tea. Not the man whose van he shut the door of that morning.

He knows somebody's sister is going on a train in October. He knows her case had been packed and laced and standing on a table in a passage since before the middle of August. He knows the shape it has taken out of the dust of that table is still there and will not be wiped out now, because nobody in that house looks at a table.

He has not asked the name and could not have said it if he had been asked. He has not looked the junction up. He is not going to.

What he cannot put down is not the name. It is that a man can sit in a passage for three days and go out every evening not knowing what is in the room he has been in, and come in one morning and know one thing about it, and that the one thing is about somebody he will never meet and who does not owe him anything.

**THE CLEAN SHAPE IN THE DUST ON THAT TABLE IS STILL THERE AND HE KNOWS EXACTLY HOW BIG IT IS AND HE HAS TOLD NOBODY.**
