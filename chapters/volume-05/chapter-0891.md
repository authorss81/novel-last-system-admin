# Chapter 0891 — Under The Slab

Thursday the seventh of March 2024, at the foot of the ivy on the shed wall at number ten, in the middle of the afternoon. Under the arch of a stem, and what is underneath it.

**SHE HAS AN IVY GROWING UP THE WALL OF THE SHED AT THE END OF THE BED AT NUMBER TEN AND A SECOND STEM OF IT COMING UP THROUGH THE PAVING THREE FEET AWAY WITH NOTHING PLANTED THERE, AND SHE MEANS TO PUT ONE HAND DOWN INTO THE HOLLOW WHERE THE FIRST STEM LEAVES THE GROUND AND TURN IT PALM UP UNDER THE ARCH THAT STEM MAKES THERE, AND HER SISTER IS AT THE FRONT GATE WITH HER BACK TO THE ROAD.**

The ivy has been on that wall since before the shed was there.

It comes out of a bed of soil along the foot of the shed wall, about two feet in from the corner, and it goes up the wall in the way ivy goes up a wall, which is by gripping the mortar joints and putting a root into every one of them it can reach.

It goes up that wall about eight feet and carries on up it year after year and does not lose anything, and it is green from the foot to the top in every month of the year.

What she has been turning over since the autumn is the other stem.

There is a second stem of that ivy coming up through the paving about three feet along, in the middle of a strip of slabs where nothing has ever been planted, and she pulled at it in the summer and it came up with four inches of runner on it and went back, and it has come up again.

A plant does not come up in the middle of a path because a seed blew there. And it does not come up there if the root of it is in the bed at the foot of the wall, because a bed at the foot of a wall is two feet away from the middle of a path and a root does not jump.

So the two stems are joined somewhere under the paving, and the joining is the thing she wanted to find.

Her sister comes to the front gate from the path side with her back to the road, puts it to behind her with her heel, and comes down the path.

She went past the shed, and stopped at the end of the bed, and stood looking at the wall the ivy is on for a while, and then came back up the path and let herself out at the gate and shut it with her heel.

---

The strip of paving along the foot of that shed wall is three slabs wide and about six feet long, and it was laid flush.

The hollow the ivy has chosen is between the last two of those slabs and the wall, and it is about two inches deep and two wide, and it is full of leaf mould that has gone to a fine black powder over the years.

That hollow can only be emptied with fingers, which is a slow business and a dirty one.

So the fingers go in first, and work the leaf mould out of the way, and then the hand goes down palm down into the hollow and turns over, and the palm comes up under the stem.

Which is the only place a hand can get under an ivy stem, because everything else about an ivy is above the ground.

Under that stem the palm finds a hollow, and the hollow is going away.

The stem does not go into the soil at all. It goes along, under the paving, and the hand follows it the way a hand follows a drain, palm up, with the fingers pushing the leaf mould ahead of it.

Under the first slab. Under the second.

Three feet.

The joint it is running in is the one between the second and the third slabs, which is the widest of the four on that strip, and a root takes the widest joint it can find for the same reason a person takes the widest step.

And at three feet the hand comes up into the open again, in the gap between two slabs at the other end of the strip, and the fingers find the neck of the runner going down into the ground, and the stem comes up out of the gap and goes back over the soil.

The neck of it is thick enough to tell that it is well past being a first-year shoot, and the hand cannot follow it down, because below the neck there is the hardcore of that yard and a hand does not go into hardcore.

One stem up the wall and one stem along under the paving, joined at the far end of the run in a gap between two slabs where a bed of leaf mould has gathered over them.

The stem that goes up the wall is fed by the stem that goes along, and the root that actually carries both of them is down at the far end of that run, under the third slab, where a hand cannot follow it.

Because that is where the ground is.

The bed of soil along the foot of that wall is a bed of soil about a foot deep that was made up in the spring and has had a shed built against the outside of it. Under the paving is the old surface, and under the old surface is the hardcore the whole of that yard was built on.

So the ivy has one stem in good soil at the top of a foot of made ground, and its other stem in the made ground itself, running three feet in the dark with rubble round it and nothing else.

The reason the stem comes up in the middle of that path and the reason it keeps coming up is the same reason: it is under slabs in the dark on ground that never dries out, and a root that has found ground like that does not go back towards the light.

Ivy takes the widest joint it can find, runs along in it, and puts up a stem wherever it happens to come out.

---

What the hand felt under that stem is the part the wall gives no sign of.

The stem under the paving is not a stem at all for the first three feet. It is a woody runner about the thickness of a pencil, and it has no leaves on it at all, and it is not green. It is brown and fibrous and dry, and it has been lying on hardcore in the dark, and it is as sound as anything in that yard.

And there is more to that wall than the ivy, which is the part that costs something in the end.

An ivy on a wall is not a plant on a wall. It has put a root into the bed joint of every course it has reached, and those roots are in the mortar, and they take the mortar with them when they let go, and the bed joints on a north wall in this borough are the joints that want pointing.

So the wall behind that ivy is damp at the bottom two feet and green at the joints, and the wall will want the ivy off it before anybody can point a single joint of it, and the ivy will not come off that wall in any year that a person is willing to give it.

That wall will still be standing and still sound when the pointing is done on it, and the pointing cannot be done until the ivy is off it.

So the whole of that ivy is one plant with one root, the root is three feet from the wall under a slab in the made ground, and everything that grows on that wall comes out of a place that is of no use to anything else.

That root is the reason the stem keeps coming up in the path. Cut it off at the joint and it is back in a season. The wall away and the root is still under the slabs, sound, in the dark, and not one thing in that ivy has ever needed the wall.

The only way to see where that root sits is to lift that slab, which is two people and a bar and a morning in a wet week.

The ivy will go on growing out of a paving joint in the middle of that path, and it will not be doing anything different.
