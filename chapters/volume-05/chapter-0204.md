# Chapter 0204 — The Gully

Tuesday the tenth of November 2015, from about six in the evening, the low end of Peverell Street in Tideglass.

**TWO PEOPLE CLEARED A GULLY OUTSIDE A DOOR ON AN ORDINARY TUESDAY EVENING, AND NEITHER OF THEM IS ON ANYTHING, AND NOBODY IS GOING TO FIND OUT.**

The water of Monday the twelfth of October had gone out of this city by the Wednesday, and the river has been down about two feet since and it is down tonight, and the low end of Peverell Street has about an inch of silt on the pavement from the top of the yard to the fourth door back, and a gully outside the third door is packed solid with it, and the man who has that door is out, and there is a woman on the second floor of that house who comes down about once a day.

Jonas Mercer came down the street at about six because that is when he comes down it, and a man of about fifty-four was in the road with a handcart and a fridge on it and a shovel, and he had stopped at the third door.

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THAT STREET THAT EVENING, printed and fixed to a post at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A DOOR YOU CAN GET OUT OF IS A DOOR.**
> **NOBODY HAS TO HAVE DONE ANYTHING TO IT.**
> **NOTHING ON THIS NOTICE IS HELD AGAINST ANY PERSON.**

He had the gully open in about ten minutes and about four shovels of silt in the cart and the whole of it took him until about a quarter past seven, because a gully is not a hole and it is not the top of it that matters, and he went at it properly with a bar he had in the cart for exactly that.

A woman of about eighty-five came out of the house next door about ten minutes after he started and stood on her own step and watched him for a while, and then she went in and came out with a stiff broom and a bucket and swept what he had put on the pavement down to the kerb and into the bucket, and the two of them worked on the same hundred yards of pavement for about an hour and neither of them asked the other for anything.

"You've not got a brush for it," she said, at about half past six.

"I've a bar."

"You've a bar and you've got no brush. That's not a criticism, that's a survey."

He gave her the bar when the gully was open and she used it on the last of the silt and gave it back, and that is the whole of what was said between them, and he put the fridge on the cart and took it round the corner, and she put the bucket out for the dustman in the morning and went in, and neither of them said anything else, and neither of them knew the other's name and they were not asked and are not going to be.

There was a bus at about ten past seven and a shutter came down across the shop at the top of the street, the long slow kind, and the man inside it went on. A woman who lives in one of the flats over the parade had her window open and was listening to something she had not chosen, and the low end of the street got darker about a quarter sooner than the top of it does.

At about half past six a tipper went past the end of the street with a flatbed behind it and a man of about thirty-seven at the wheel, on his way to load at seven for the morning, and Jonas Mercer did not raise a hand and the man did not put his hand up, and neither of them slowed down, and a thing that arrived from a city across an estuary in an envelope had been in force for forty-one days.

**AND JONAS MERCER WAS THE ONLY PERSON ON THAT STREET WHO SAW ANY OF IT, AND HE DID NOT ASK EITHER OF THEM ANYTHING, AND THERE IS NOBODY IN THIS CITY HE COULD HAVE HANDLED IT TO.**

A man of about thirty-seven went across the water on the sixth of October for a fortnight and is out again on Thursday, and the money is in an envelope before the work, and nobody in this borough has asked him one question. A man of about sixty-two came across on Monday morning to find out why his name was on a piece of paper in a city he had left in April, and said out loud in a room over a shop on Tuesday that nobody had asked him to be found, and then said that a doctor in a town he could not name was going to be paid for out of a council that had never heard of him, and that he could not say no to it, and that he would like to.

Both of those are correct. Both of them are in force. Everything that has happened on this stretch of this city in seven weeks is correct and none of it is written down, and the drawing is on more walls than it was in September and nobody in this borough can say how many, and there is a square in the middle of every copy of it with nothing in it.

A permission is a thing a person may do. An obligation is a thing a person has to do. This borough has stood on the second of those for nine months and has never once been asked to show anybody what it did, and about six weeks ago a sheet of two pages came from a city on the other side of the water with the first of them on it in print, signed at both ends, correct in every sentence, and it is in force on this street tonight, and it is in force in a yard at the bottom of a street where a man with a pump and his own bags kept a doorway dry in September and nobody is ever going to know.

Nobody in this borough has worked out what either of them was handed. It is Tuesday the tenth of November, and it is open, and a man of about fifty-four and a woman of about eighty-five have gone in about a quarter past seven.
