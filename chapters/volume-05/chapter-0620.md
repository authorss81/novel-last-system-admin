# Chapter 0620 — The Terrace With the Gable

Saturday the sixth of February 2021. He is out of the house a little after eight and he is not back in it until it is dark, and all of it happens in this borough and one street outside it.

**A MAN HE HAS SPOKEN TO TWICE STOPPED HIM IN A YARD AND TOLD HIM THAT THE TERRACE HIS MOTHER USED TO LIVE IN WAS GOING TO BE KNOCKED DOWN, AND HE HAS SPENT THE DAY TRYING TO FIND OUT HOW THAT MAN CAME BY IT, AND HE HAS A MOTHER, AND HE HAS NOT ASKED HER.**

He is a man in his mid forties. Whatever a man who does drives will put in front of him, he takes, and has never once done as much of it as he should, and his mother lives in a terrace about a mile from the end of his road, and the terrace has a gable end on it that he can see from a dual carriageway if he stands at the crossing.

He has not stood in that terrace since he was a boy. His mother moved out of it before he left school and has been in a flat since, and he has been in the flat plenty.

---

Here is what was said.

He was in a yard off an industrial road getting a pallet off a man he works for, and there was another man in the yard who does not drive for the same firm and who he has spoken to twice, both times about a load.

And the man said, "You off the estate?"

And he said, "Which estate."

And the man said, "The old row. That gable end."

And he said, "You mean my mother's."

And the man said, "I mean the row."

And he said, "What about it."

And the man said, "They're going to pull the row down. There's a scheme on it."

And he said, "There's been a scheme on that row since I was at school."

And the man said, "There's a new one."

And he said, "How new."

And the man said, "I don't know. That's not mine, that's the fella that drives the van with the sign on."

And he said, "Right."

And the man said, "You'll know before I do, you'll be the one they ring."

And he said, "Will they."

And the man said, "They're not ringing me," and got back in the cab of the van.

He stood in that yard after the van went, long enough for the yard to empty out and go quiet again, which is a thing he has not mentioned to anybody.

What he has come away with is very little, and admitting that took him until the middle of the morning.

He has not got the man's name.

He has not got the firm. The man said a firm and the man does not work for the firm.

He has not got a date, and he has not got a scheme, and he has not got a letter, and he has not been asked to give anything.

What he has got is a man in a yard who is certain, and a gable end he can see from a crossing.

Here is the thing that he worked out before lunch and that has made the rest of the day what it is.

He has a mother.

That is the whole of it. That is the whole of the day in one sentence and he has not said it out loud anywhere and he would not say it to a person.

He could have asked her at any point since he was an adult. He has spoken to her most weeks. He has had her in a kitchen at Christmas and in a car park outside a hospital and in a queue at a bank. There has never once been a reason to ask about the terrace and she has never once offered it.

And if he asks her now, on a Saturday, in the middle of the morning, then he has got his mother's house from a man in a yard who drives a van with a sign on the side of it.

That is what he is carrying, and it is a small thing, and it is the first time in this account that a man has been stopped by the shape of the question rather than by the question.

Here is what he did with the rest of it.

He went and sat in the car at the top of the road and he wrote two lists on the back of a fuel receipt, which is a thing he does and which nobody knows about.

The first list was everything the man had said, in order, as near as he could get it, and it took him a while because the middle of it went back and forth about a scheme and he wanted the sequence of it.

The second list was every person he could think of who might have been in that man's ear. It is a short list. It is four long and three short and he has been in a yard off that industrial road perhaps a dozen times.

And then he sat there and worked out, at about half past twelve, that the second list was not a real exercise at all.

He does not want to know how the man knew. He had that before lunch and has done the two lists anyway.

---

He went and had his lunch at the place on the industrial road that does food, which he goes to, and the woman on the till asked him whether he was all right, which people do, and he said he was.

At about two he drove to the crossing and stood on the island and looked at the terrace from the outside of it, across the dual carriageway, which is about the closest he has been to that row since he was a boy.

You can see the gable from there. You can see the gable and five roofs and a bit of the road in front of them, and that is all of that row you can see from anywhere.

And he could not see anything on it that told him anything at all, and he stood there anyway, and a man in a van stopped and asked him if he was all right, which people do.

At about half past three he drove home and sat in the kitchen and did not ring her.

Here is why, and it took him most of the afternoon to get to a version he can write down.

It is not that she would be upset.

She has not mentioned that row once in the time he has been an adult and he has never once asked, and in between that pair of facts there is something neither of them is going to be first to touch, and he has got a good idea of what it is and it is to do with his grandmother and with his father going the other way when he was small and none of it is anybody's business in a car park.

So he does not ask because of that.

And he does not ask because a man in a yard said the row is going down and if he rings his mother and says so then he is a man passing on a rumour from a van, and if he rings his mother and says nothing then he has rung for nothing.

And he worked out towards five that there is a third version, and that he has been using the first two to stay out of it.

The third version is: he rings her and asks her a direct question about a house, on a Saturday, out of nowhere, and his mother finds out that he has been thinking about it.

And he would rather she did not know that.

She rang him at about ten past five.

That is the part of the day he had not planned and he has not got over it.

And she said, "You all right, you never call on a Saturday."

And he said, "I know."

And she said, "You were thinking of calling."

And he said, "I was."

And she said, "About what?"

And he said, "Nothing."

And she said, "Nothing."

And he said, "Where are you going on Saturday night."

And she said, "The pictures."

And he said, "Right."

And he did not say anything else for a bit, and then she said, "There's a woman here who wants the top flat."

And he said, "Who's she."

And she said, "I don't know her."

And he said, "How much."

And she said, "She isn't offering on it. She wants to know if it's got a balcony."

And he said, "It's got no balcony."

And she said, "That's what I told her."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "There's people been asking about that row, if that's what you were thinking about. The fella next door had a lad round about him. He's not selling. He's not asked anybody."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "Why?"

And he said, "No reason. I was just thinking about it."

And she said, "You've never thought about it in your life."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Well, then."

And he said, "Are you all right for the pictures."

And she said, "I'm going."

So that is where he is at about six o'clock on a Saturday evening, and here is what he has.

His mother is not selling the row and his mother never was going to and had no intention of anybody ringing her.

There was a man in a yard who was wrong.

And that man was wrong about the only thing he said, and he said it with a certainty that was worth something to this man, because it put a shape on a row of houses he has not stood in front of since he was a boy and had never once been in a position to have a question about.

And his mother telephoned him unprompted and settled it in about ninety seconds without either of them knowing that was what was happening.

And he knows now exactly where the man got it, which is from a fella with a ladder going round a street doing roofs, who went into the wrong end and asked the wrong person.

And it never happened, and he has had a Saturday about it.

**A MAN IN A YARD TOLD HIM A TERRACE WAS GOING TO BE KNOCKED DOWN AND HE SPENT THE DAY LOOKING FOR THE SOURCE AND HE HAD A MOTHER WHO WAS NEVER GOING TO SELL IT, AND HIS MOTHER RANG HIM AT ABOUT TEN PAST FIVE AND TOLD HIM HIMSELF WITHOUT HAVING BEEN ASKED, AND HE NOW KNOWS EXACTLY WHERE THE MAN GOT IT AND IT NEVER HAPPENED.**
