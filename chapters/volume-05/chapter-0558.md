# Chapter 0558 — Two Figures on One Slip

The Friday of the tenth of April 2020, and most of the day, in an office off a street of this borough.

**THEY HAVE SHARED ONE JOB SINCE 2018 AND HIS WEEKLY FIGURE AND HERS HAVE NOT BEEN THE SAME SINCE 2018, AND EACH OF THEM GOT THERE ALONE IN THE SAME WEEK OF MARCH, AND NOT ONE WORD HAS GONE BETWEEN THEM ABOUT IT, AND ON HIS OWN DATE HE WROTE A NUMBER DOWN AND WALKED OUT OF THAT BUILDING.**

The building is two floors and about twenty people work in it and the ground floor is the part that matters, because anybody who comes in off that street comes in for that.

She is a woman in her late twenties. He is a man in his mid thirties. They have both been there since 2018.

They take the appointments. That is the whole of what either of them does, and they have divided it the same way for about two years, and there is a list on the board in the corridor with the days down one side and the two of them down the other, and nobody has looked at that list in either direction in all that time.

---

She worked it out on the Thursday of the twelfth of March, a little before midday, standing in that corridor.

A man who had come in for half an hour asked her, in the ordinary way and pleasantly, what the figure was on the sheet that people sign, and she told him, and he said that it was more than he had been told, and went away.

She stood where she was. She looked at the list in the corridor and at the sheet and at nothing else, and then she went and did her appointments, and she did not go back into the office for about an hour.

She has not said anything to anybody since. Not to the woman who does the mornings, not to the two who come in on a Tuesday, not to him. She has not worked out what her figure should be, and she is not the sort of person who could, and she has not asked the two of them who set it.

He worked it out two days earlier.

On the Tuesday of the tenth of March he counted the week out twice on the surface at the end of that corridor, because the first count came up different from what he had, and the second count came up the same as the first, and then he had the two figures in front of him and the two figures were not the same.

That was a month ago and neither of them has opened it.

He has thought about it every day since, which he has not admitted to a living soul, and on the Monday he thought about asking her, and on the Wednesday he thought about saying nothing, and on the Thursday he did the appointments the same as he does them.

There is no rule anywhere in that building about what the two of them should be paid. There is a sheet with the two figures on it side by side, and there is a copy of that sheet in an office two floors up that neither of them has ever been in, and the figures have been different since 2018 and there is nothing on any sheet anywhere that says they should be the same.

He is not owed anything and nobody is short of anything and no one in that building has been cheated of a penny, and he knows that, and it is not why he wrote it on the slip.

On the Friday of the tenth of April he got a slip out of the pad on the surface where people sign things in, and he wrote one figure on it in his own handwriting, which is a small upright hand and everybody who works in that building knows it.

He wrote the number and nothing else. He did not write her number and he did not write the difference and he did not write a date.

He put it down at the end of that surface at about two o'clock, where the signing happens, so that anybody going out through that corridor would have had to see it if they were looking, which nobody was.

Then he went out at two and went home and did not come back until the Monday.

She found it at about four.

She was on her own and she went past that surface to get into the corridor and it was under her hand before she had read it, and she read it standing up, and then she folded it once and put it inside her jacket.

She has not shown it to anybody and she has not taken it out again. It went home in that jacket folded in half and it is still there.

She has not asked him whether he wrote it. She has not asked anybody else about the two figures. She has not gone up to that office two floors up to look at the sheet herself. She has kept the whole of it to herself, and there are about nine people who work in that building and she has spoken to all nine since, normally, about nothing.

She has also not changed anything. She goes at the times she went at. She takes the days off that list in the sequence they come.

He has not asked her whether she found it. He has not been in that building since he walked out of it at two, and the surface at the end of that corridor is the only place either of them could put it, and it is not mentioned between them, and they took the day between them on the Friday before this one and got through all of it in about four hours.

The one thing either of them would put a hand to is that not one syllable of it has ever been said aloud, in that building or anywhere else, and not one of the people who work in it has any idea that there is anything.

**HE WROTE ONE NUMBER IN A SMALL UPRIGHT HAND AND WALKED OUT AT TWO, AND SHE FOUND IT AT FOUR AND PUT IT INSIDE HER JACKET, AND IT IS STILL THERE AND NEITHER OF THEM IS GOING TO.**
