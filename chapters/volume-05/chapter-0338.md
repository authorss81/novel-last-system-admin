# Chapter 0338 — Shut for Two Hours

Friday the twenty-first of July 2017, from about a quarter past nine in the morning until about half past four in the afternoon, a parade of shops, a yard at the top of a street, and that parade again, in Tideglass.

**THE SHUTTER ON THE FISH SHOP AT THE FAR END OF THAT PARADE WAS DOWN AT A QUARTER PAST NINE AND WENT UP AGAIN AT ABOUT TEN TO TWELVE, AND A GATE AT THE TOP OF A STREET WAS FOUND HUNG ON THE WRONG SIDE BY A MAN WHO HAD NO REASON TO BE LOOKING AT IT, AND THE ONLY PERSON IN THIS BOROUGH WHO WAS TOLD BOTH OF THOSE THINGS WAS STANDING BEHIND A COUNTER THE WHOLE TIME, AND SHE DID NOT PUT THEM TOGETHER.**

The parade is nine shops and a shutter goes up on every one of them on a weekday, and the fish shop is the second from the top and it has a shutter of the old pressed steel kind with a fairground pattern in it, and it goes up at about eight every morning and it has gone up at about eight every morning for longer than the woman who works two doors down from it has been behind a counter.

On that Friday the man in a fleece came up that parade at about a quarter past nine going to the works with a paper in his hand and the fish shop was down.

Not half. Down. The bar across, the padlock on, and no light inside it, and no sound of a man in there, and the pavement outside it about four foot wide with a bus stop at the top end and a woman with a bag of shopping standing at the bus stop looking at that shutter as if she had been expecting it.

The man in a fleece did not stop. He went on up to the top of the parade and got a bus and did not think about it again until about ten to twelve.

The bus was four minutes late and there were six people on that stop and the woman with a bag of shopping was one of them, and she said to nobody in particular that the fish shop was shut, and the man in her forties said everybody knows, and the woman with the bag said no, that is what I am saying, and neither of those two women has ever spoken to the other one about anything.

He came down that parade at about five to twelve because his bus was late. The fish shop was open. The shutter was up, the bar was back on its brackets, there was a light on behind the counter and there was a man in there, and the man in the fish shop said good morning to him the way a man says good morning to somebody who comes past every morning of his life.

The man who puts shutters up went along that parade at about ten to twelve with his ladder over his shoulder and went past that shop without stopping and set his ladder up at the next one, and he has never been in that shop, and the man in the fish shop has never told him why it was shut, and a man at the far end of that parade would tell you he would not ask about it, and another would tell you it is nobody's business, and both of them are talking about a shutter they could have walked past in a quarter of a second.

Nobody on that parade found out anything about those two hours, then or afterwards, and nobody asked the man in the fish shop, and he has not said.

---

The yard is at the top of a street of about thirty houses and it is behind the second house on the left going up, and the man in his thirties went into it at about one in the afternoon because there is a cold run to a sink at the side of that house and he had been told in June it was slow.

He did not get to the sink for about four minutes.

The side gate of that yard is a field gate about four foot wide on a latch with a hasp, and it opens into the yard away from the wall. He put his hand on it and it came towards him about an inch and stopped dead against the brick of the house about a foot above the ground, because somebody has hung that gate on the other side.

It is not a difficult piece of work and it is not good work. The hangars are the wrong way round for the way the gate now swings, and the top one has been packed out with a piece of card and a screw through the strap, and the screw is bright, and the ground under the hinge post has been dug and filled in again inside about a fortnight, because the fill is a different colour to the ground round it and it has not settled.

Somebody has hung that gate the other way up and then pushed it in the wrong direction and packed the top to make it shut, and it shuts now, and that is what a person wanted, and it shuts against the wall of the house.

There is a woman at number four on that side of the street who has a car and who came into that yard in a car every night of the first week of July, and the man in his thirties knows that because he has been in that street about four times since March and has seen the lights of it go off at about ten, and there is nobody else in that street who parks in there, and he has not gone and asked the woman at number four whether she had a man in and has not said one word about a gate to anybody.

The man in his thirties stood in that yard and looked at it, and then he went and did the slow run with the tap turned a quarter of an inch further and it ran, and he shut it and went out through that gate, and it came away from the wall for about four inch and stopped and had to be pulled.

He has kept his mouth shut about that gate, and he has not been up that street since, and he does not know whose gate it is, and he would not have asked, because a gate that has been rehung is a gate somebody wanted rehung, and the man who rehung it did not do it for him.

---

The woman who works in a shop was at her counter at about ten that morning when the man who puts shutters up came past with his ladder over his shoulder on his way to the far end of the parade.

"That's the fish shop down at nine," he said. "I walked past at a quarter past and it was shut. I haven't been in there since March and I shan't be, but there's no sign on it."

"There isn't," said the woman who works in a shop.

"I'd not have said anything if you hadn't been stood there," said the man who puts shutters up, and went on up the parade with his ladder.

She thought about it twice between ten and midday and then she stopped thinking about it, because a shop is shut or a shop is not shut, and there is no third thing that a fishmonger is doing for two hours in the middle of a weekday.

The man in his thirties came by that shop a little before five that afternoon with his bag, stopped, and put the bag down on the step, which he does.

"I've got a gate to tell you about and it's a stupid one," he said. "Top of Marsh Lane side, back of the second house on the left going up. Somebody's hung that gate the other way so it shuts against the wall of the house. It's not right, and it's been done in a fortnight, and if somebody does that gate again they'll do it the same way and it'll be shut for a fortnight and then it won't shut at all."

"Somebody got paid for it," said the woman who works in a shop.

"That's what I thought," said the man in his thirties. "That's exactly what I thought, and it isn't my gate and there's nothing in it and the tap's the only reason I was in that yard."

"You're not going to say anything to whoever did it."

"There's nobody to say it to," said the man in his thirties. "I'd be standing in a street telling a stranger his hinge packout is a bit of card. If I was that fella I'd want the gate shut and I would not want a man with a bag telling me about a bit of card in front of the house."

She said that was fair, and she picked up her bag off the step for him, and she did not say one word to him about that fish shop being closed at a quarter past nine that morning, and she did not know that the fish shop had been shut at all until the man with the ladder told her, and she did not think about the two of them together for one second, and there is nothing in this borough that puts a gate at the top of Marsh Lane and a shutter at the far end of a parade in the same breath.

It is not a thing anybody has tried. She did not try it, and if she had tried it she would have got nowhere, because a fishmonger shut for two hours on a weekday morning is a thing that happens to a man in a fishmonger, and a gate hung against a wall is a thing that happens to a gate, and the only thing the two of them have in common is that two people in this borough know about both of them, and one of those two people is standing in a shop and the other one is in a road with a bag on his shoulder and there is no version of that which is a story.

**THE FISH SHOP OPENED AT ABOUT TEN TO TWELVE AND A GATE AT THE TOP OF MARSH LANE STAYED SHUT AGAINST A WALL OF BRICK, AND A WOMAN WHO WORKS IN A SHOP WENT HOME AT HALF PAST FOUR WITH A BAG ON HER ARM AND NOTHING ON HER MIND.**
