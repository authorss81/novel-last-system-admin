# Chapter 0728 — The Jug Lid

Saturday the twenty-eighth of May 2022, in this borough. She is in her own house at number nine from first light until dark, and the whole of it is a bulb that comes out and goes back in and a lid that stays as it is.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES TAKES THE SIXTY-WATT BULB DOWN IN HER OWN FRONT ROOM, WIPES THE SHADE AND PUTS THE SAME BULB BACK, LOOKS AT THE JUG LID WITH TWO LAYERS OF TAPE, SPEAKS TO HER SISTER AT THE GATE ABOUT THE BIN, AND PUTS THE JUG BACK ON THE SHELF WITHOUT A THIRD LAYER.**

She is a woman in her late fifties at number nine on Saturday. Her sister stops at the gate near dark and she speaks to her out there.

---

She starts in the front room opposite the window where the ash stool stands with three oak stools and one ash, and the space where nothing has been since the fifth of November.

The sixty-watt bulb has burned in there since the seventh of December. She stands on the ash stool and unscrews it with her right hand and brings it down and sets it on the window sill on a cloth.

The shade is grey with dust. She wipes it inside and out with a damp cloth and dries it with another, and the light that comes through it afterwards is the same light, only cleaner round the edges.

She puts the same bulb back, up the steps and in with two turns, and steps down and moves the stool back opposite the window where it was.

She has stood on that stool for a bulb and never once sat on it, and there is a difference, and she is not going to lose it at her age.

She does not put a new bulb in. A bulb that burns can stay, and a room that is cold with a clean shade is still a cold room, and she knows that.

---

In the kitchen she takes the jug down from the shelf and looks at the lid.

It has two layers of tape on it from April, laid edge to edge. A third layer would cover the rim right round, and she has the tape in the drawer and the scissors beside it, and she stands with the jug in her hands a long while.

Then she puts the tape back in the drawer without cutting any. A third layer would be saying something, and she does not want to say it. Two layers will do for a jug that only boils for one.

She sets the jug down on the table with the lid on, square, and shuts the drawer.

---

The six pads are on the fridge with the first one crossed twice. She looks at them while the kettle comes up and she does not touch them.

The back door shuts true on its card hinge. She tries it twice, in and out, and it shuts true both times. The fire has been unlit since Easter and she does not lay it.

She eats at the table with the jug steaming beside her, and the house is quiet round her in a way she likes on a Saturday, with the road empty and the front room clean behind her.

---

Her sister comes to the gate at dark with her bag on her arm and stands off on the pavement.

"You told a man to wait by a green bin," her sister says, not unkindly. "At number ten."

"I did," she says.

"There is no green bin at number ten. There never was."

"I know," she says. "I said it because my hands were full and the line was loud, and I wanted him gone from my hall."

Her sister looks at her a moment and then nods, and the hard part of the visit is over before it has begun.

"Did he go?" her sister says.

"He went," she says. "He went to wait where I told him, and that is on me."

"Will you put it right?"

"No," she says. "I will not go thirty foot and look at my own door now to prove what I said then. If he comes back I will tell him straight that I live at nine."

Her sister hitches the bag up and looks down the road toward number ten with its light off.

"You could walk round now," her sister says.

"I could," she says. "And I will not. Not tonight."

She lifts a hand in farewell and her sister lifts one back, and she goes in and shuts the back door true behind her, and puts the jug back on its shelf though it is already there, just to have something to do with her hands, and switches the front room light off at the doorway without going in.
