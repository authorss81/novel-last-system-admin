# Chapter 0811 — The Butt Out Of The Corner

Wednesday the third of May 2023, in this borough. She is in her own yard at number ten between the middle of the morning and the end of it, and a galvanised butt lying on its side in the corner is the whole of that day.

**A WOMAN IN HER EARLY THIRTIES HAS AN OLD GALVANISED BUTT LYING ON ITS SIDE IN THE CORNER OF THAT YARD THAT MEANS TO GO TO THE TIP AND MEANS TO HAVE THE TAP OFF IT FIRST; HER SISTER GETS IT UP ON A BASE THAT IS LEVEL AND HARD IN THE SAME YARD ON THE SAME MORNING, AND WHERE THE TWO HALVES OF THAT BUTT MEET IT STANDS ON SOMETHING THAT CAN CARRY THE WEIGHT OF A FULL BUTT, AND NEITHER OF THEM EVER FINDS OUT. NO WATER IS DRAWN, CARRIED, POURED OR EMPTIED BY A PERSON IN THAT CHAPTER AND NONE IS IN THE BUTT.**

Number ten, on that Wednesday. She is in her early thirties. The butt is a galvanised tank about four feet across and five feet tall, and it has stood in the corner of that yard on two courses of brick since the summer, and it came off its base in the frost before Christmas and has been on its side in that corner ever since.

She has been meaning to take it to the tip since January.

A butt is a thing that cannot go on a lorry with a hole in the bottom of it, and the tap is the reason for that.

The tap on that butt is a brass bib tap on a union, and the union has a nut on it, and if the nut comes off and the tap comes off and the hole is plugged from inside with a wooden bung then the butt will go on a lorry, and that is the whole of the journey and it has taken her four months.

She has a length of three-inch scaffold tube, a Stillson's wrench, a spanner and a hacksaw in the yard, and she starts on it about the middle of the morning.

Getting a tap off a galvanised butt is not a job about the tap. It is a job about the union nut behind it, which corrodes into a lump and then welds itself to the thread, and the tap itself comes out at the end like a plum.

You put the spanner on the nut first and you do not touch the tap, because if you turn the tap and not the nut you are turning the tap out of the tank and you will find out what that is.

She gets the spanner on the union nut and it does not want to turn at all. She gets the length of tube on the end of it for the leverage and puts the other end of the tube against the foot of the shed wall, because that is what the tube is for, and it turns about a quarter of an inch and stops with a bang that goes off round that yard.

She goes at it for about an hour with the tube and the Stillson and she gets the union nut about half a turn and no further, and the nut is not seized in one place. It is seized all round with rust that has swollen and then had the zinc on top of it that has swollen over the top of that.

She does not go at it harder. She goes at it slower, and she moves the spanner back a quarter at a time and takes it up again from a different angle, and she gets about three hundred degrees out of the union and then the round is complete and the nut will not come any further because the threaded part underneath it has not turned with it.

---

At about the middle of the morning the yard gate goes and her sister comes through it with a spade and a spirit level and a bag of chippings she has carried from the front.

She does not come down that yard to where the butt is. She goes straight down the other side, along the shed wall, because she has come to do the far end of the base that butt is going to stand on, and from where she is she cannot see the woman of the house at all, because the butt is lying on its side in the corner and is four feet across and it is exactly at the height of two crouching people.

The woman of the house is behind the butt with her face in the union nut and a length of tube braced against the shed wall, and she does not hear anything except the spade going into ground at the other end of that yard.

They are in the same yard for three hours and neither of them sees the other.

That is not a coincidence about the yard. It is the butt. A thing four feet across lying on its side makes a wall, and one of them is working at the tap end of it with her hands in a hole and the other is working at the opposite end of its footprint with a spade, and there is no line of sight over a galvanised tank between two people on the ground.

The sister's half is the base.

A butt standing on soft ground does not stand still. It goes out of round, and then the water in it comes down to the low side and it stays there, and it goes down further every winter until the bottom of it is in the ground.

So the base goes in: a square hole the size of the butt, dug to below the frost, four inches of the chippings she has carried in and the broken brick that comes out of the dig, put in in layers and rammed with the back of the spade and not packed solid, because a base packed solid is a base that traps water and a base that traps water is a base the butt stands in.

She rakes it flat and puts the spirit level on it and takes the high places off with the back of the spade, and she works from the far end of the square toward the butt, because the far end is the end she has dug first and the near end is where the ground has not been moved.

The woman of the house gets the tap off at about the middle of the afternoon.

She has been going at the union nut in three hundred degrees for three hours and she has got the wrench up to where she can see the top of the thread coming out of the tank, and she gets the Stillson on it and turns it one more time and the union nut comes off the thread all at once with a noise like a dropped spanner.

The thread on the butt is clean. The thread inside the nut is clean. They have not seized against each other at all. It was the nut, and it was the zinc over the rust over the nut, and it has let go all at once and it will not go back.

She takes the tap off the thread and out of the tank with her hands and puts it down on the flags inside the yard gate.

The hole where the tap was is about the size of a thumb and it is bright metal all round the edge of it and black down inside, and that is the only place on the whole of that butt where there is any light at all.

She plugs the hole herself, from the inside, which means getting herself under the butt on her knees in the corner with a torch.

The bung is a length of soft wood turned to a taper on a lathe she borrowed from a man in the village, and it goes into that hole about four inches and stays where it is. Then the washer goes on the outside and the union nut goes back on over it and comes down onto the bung, and the wood swells against the thread of the hole and the hole is shut and there is no more hole in that butt.

Her sister comes round the shed late in that afternoon with the spirit level and rakes the last of the chippings out level across the square.

She works to the middle of the square, where the dig she started at the far end meets the dig that had already been made at the near end by somebody who was not there, and there is no join in the base anywhere, because hardcore does not have a join. It has a place where it stops being one person's bucketful and starts being the other person's, and both of them rammed it, and it is one base from the near edge of it to the far edge of it.

She puts the spirit level on it in three places and takes the high place off with the back of the spade, and puts the spade against the shed wall out of the way, and goes out through the yard gate with her bag of empty chippings folded flat.

Then the butt goes up, and it goes up by itself and in stages, because that is the only way a thing like it goes up.

She has three lengths of softwood plank standing against the shed wall and she lays them along the line of the dig, and she puts a lever under the low side and gets it up onto its rim about six inches, and wedges it there.

A butt on its rim will roll if it is pushed and it will not roll if it is pushed badly. She gets it a quarter turn at a time with the end of the lever and a wedge behind it, and between each quarter turn she goes and shifts the planks under the rim so that the rim is always on timber and never on the chippings.

She is at that from the middle of the afternoon until the light has gone off the top of the shed, and it is not a job she could have done faster, and it is a job she can do alone because it is five jobs of ten minutes each and not one job of an hour.

The butt comes up onto the base after the light has gone and it is off by about three inches, and she walks the line of it and walks the line of it again and the light is not there any more.

She does not need the light to know that, because it is not level by eye, it is level because she has been carrying a spirit level round that yard all day and she can feel where the high place is with the heel of her hand on the chippings at the rim.

She shims it at the high side with three off-cuts of batten she had in her pocket, which is what she had them in her pocket for, and she drives them down with the lever and tests it again, and on the second test the bubble is not out anywhere along the whole of that rim she can get her hand round.

The tap is lying on the flags inside that yard gate where she put it down in the middle of the afternoon. Brass, about six inches long, with a union nut on the end of it and the washer still under the nut and the thread clean and bright and dry, and it is not going to the tip and it is not going back into the hole in that butt, and there is nothing wet in the butt and nothing wet to come.
