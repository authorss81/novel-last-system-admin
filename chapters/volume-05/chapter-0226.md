# Chapter 0226 — The Book With a Column In It

Sunday the sixth of March 2016, from about eleven in the morning, an office at Penhale, and the ten metres of concrete outside it.

**A SCHEME THAT COSTS NOTHING AND ASKS NOTHING IS PAID FOR OUT OF THE REGION IN PARTS AND FUEL AND HIRE AND SOMEBODY ELSE'S HARD STANDING, AND THE TWO PEOPLE WHO RUN IT WILL SHOW THE BOOK TO ANYBODY WHO ASKS, AND EVERY LINE IN IT IS TRUE, AND NOT ONE PERSON IN THAT YARD IS ABLE TO SAY NO TO ANYBODY.**

Jonas Mercer was back on the Monday bus at about eleven on the Sunday because the office is open on a Sunday and shut the rest of the week or near enough, and he had worked that out on the first visit and had told nobody.

The office is a hut on a concrete base with a window, a desk, two chairs, a kettle, a wall calendar with a picture of a river on it that has not been turned over since January, and a strip of light along the top of the door that is on all day and is not on at nine at night.

The man who keeps the book is in his forties and came in behind him with the kettle already going. He had a cardigan on and had clearly been in since ten.

"Sit down, sit down, no, stand, I don't care."

"I'd like to see the book," said Jonas Mercer.

"Then sit down and I'll show you the book." He put the kettle down and took a ring binder off the shelf above the desk, and did not open it, and put a hand flat on the cover. "Before I do, are you one of the yards?"

"No."

"Right." He opened it. "Doesn't matter, then. It's not secret. Never has been. If you're in the trade you can have it and if you're not you can have it and there's nobody here who cares either way, and I'll tell you the truth, the yard's got nothing to hide and there's nothing in here to hide."

The wall sheet behind the desk was the real one. A single sheet of A1 with a line ruled across it and column heads, and the heads were JOB, PLACE, DAYS, PARTS, FUEL, HIRE, and CLAIM, and under CLAIM there was one more line in smaller type, and above the rule at the top left, where a person would put a name, it said **LEVEL OF SERVICE** and the rest of that box was empty and had been empty since the sheet was printed.

"You can take that down and have it," the man said, following his eyes. "The boss says no, because the boss is at eighty-nine and thinks it's his only asset, and the boss is wrong, but he's the boss, and it's been up since before I came and I'll not be the one."

"How long have you done it?"

"Fourteen years. Before me it was a woman called Enid and she's dead." He turned the binder round. "It's a carbon book. You've got a top copy, a carbon, and then somebody's put it on a shelf, and the shelf is a shelf in this room, and the top copies go in a box under the bed at mine. That's it. That's the record. Nobody sends it anywhere."

He put a page in front of Jonas Mercer and turned it round so it was the right way up.

The page was half empty. What was on it was a job at a place they call the Folly, four miles north, in the middle of February, for five days, and against it a number for parts, a number for fuel, a number for hire, and then a number at the end, and against that number nothing at all.

"Explain the last one."

"That's the claim."

"Against what?"

"Against whoever asked for the job." The man said it quite flatly. "That's the bit people come in about. They think it's an invoice. It's not an invoice, and there's nobody to invoice, because the sheet on the wall says there's no standing, so there's no party to charge, so it isn't a bill and it never was. It's a claim form. You sign it at the bottom, it comes back here, and the sum on it goes into the pool, and the pool pays for the parts and the fuel and the hire, and if the pool's short in a month the rate goes up for everybody for that month, and if the pool's long then it carries over, and that's it, that's the whole financial arrangement in the region."

"And the sum on it. Where does that come from?"

"That's the rate."

"Which is?"

"The rate for that job." He closed the binder. "It's the same for every job of that sort, and it was the same in eighty-seven, and I'll tell you what I tell everybody who asks. You can have the book, you can look at the book, you can take the number off any page you like, and nobody in this region is going to tell you the rate has gone up, because it hasn't, and nobody is going to tell you it should have, because that takes a body with a department over it and there isn't one and hasn't been one, and that is not a scandal, that is just easy."

Somebody put his head round the door. It was a young man in a high-visibility jacket with a boot in his hand.

"Thursday's crew — can you do the Marlow job, the one with the—"

"Aye."

"He said he'd only know on the Wednesday."

"Then he'll know on the Wednesday and we'll have done it on the Thursday." The young man went. "That's how it is," the man said. "You can't say no. That's not because we're soft. That's because if you can say no once you've got a rule, and then there's a person in it, and then it's a business. There's nothing to say no *to* here. There's nine yards and about four hundred people and every one of them rings this office and asks for a crew, and every crew that's out came out of this office, and not one of them was charged, and not one of them was asked to sign anything that said what they owed, and that's not a favour we're doing them, that's the only way it works."

"Nobody's required to be one of the nine yards," he said, before Jonas Mercer had got that far. "That's the other half of the sheet on the wall and it's the half nobody prints. Anybody can ring this office and get a crew, and nobody's required to be a yard, and about four of the nine have never sent a crew anywhere and have never taken one, and their money goes into the pool exactly the same."

"Who decides the rate?"

"That's the thing you actually came for, isn't it." He said it without heat. "Nobody. It was set in 1987 when the thing was agreed, and it's been the rate since, and it is not going to be revised in fourteen years because revising it needs a body with a department over it and there isn't one and there hasn't been one, and that's not a scandal, that's a thing that's easy. And before you ask: the parts go up, and the fuel goes up, and the hire goes up, and the rate does not. That's not a man forgetting. That's what a rate is. A number that moves when the month is bad is a price, and if that were a price then anybody with a bad quarter could move it, and half of them would, and then you'd have nine rates in a region that agreed on one."

**AND THE WOMAN OF ABOUT EIGHTY-SIX CAME IN AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWELVE WITH A PIECE OF CHALK IN HER HAND AND PUT A TICK BESIDE THE LAST LENGTH OF PIPE ON THE PALLET, AND SAID THE ONE SENTENCE SHE HAD AND SHE SAID IT TO THE WINDOW AND NOT TO EITHER OF THEM, WHICH WAS ABOUT HER OWN FIELD AND WAS ON NOTHING:**

"I let that yard be used in eighty-seven. It was a field. I have never charged anybody one pound for it and I am not going to and there is nothing anybody can do to me about that and there is nothing anybody can do for me either."

Then she asked whether the kettle had boiled, and it had not, and she made it.

---

The sum on a job sheet is not what the job costs. That is the second half of it and Jonas Mercer did not have it on the Sunday, and he had it on the Thursday, in a car park four miles from the yard, off a haulier's invoice that a man in his forties had gone out and got without being asked to.

The part, the fuel, the hire and the labour came off that yard's own book and came to something four times the sum. The four times was not anybody's fault and it was not anybody's doing. The pool had been short most months since about 2011 and everybody in that office had known it and had said nothing, because saying it out loud in that room would be making an objection, and nobody in that room objects to anything, and the man in the car park said so himself with his hands open.

**AND A MAN STOOD BY A BOLLARD WITH A HAULIER'S INVOICE IN ONE HAND AND A JOB SHEET IN THE OTHER AND ADDED THE TWO COLUMNS UP IN PENCIL ON THE BACK OF A FUEL SLIP, TWICE, AND GOT THE SAME ANSWER BOTH TIMES, AND PUT BOTH PIECES OF PAPER IN HIS INSIDE POCKET, AND DID NOT WRITE IT DOWN ANYWHERE ELSE.**
