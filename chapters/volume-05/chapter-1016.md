# Chapter 1016 — Two Boards Along The Edge Of A Floor

Undated in this draft.

**THE ROOM OVER THE FRONT ROOM AT NUMBER NINE HAS ITS FLOOR DOWN TO THE BOARDS.**

There is a room at the top of that house, over the front room, and it is small and square with one window in the front and a light tube in the roof that is not a window and does not make a room bright. Nobody has had it open since before the summer, and the last time it was open the woman of that house had the carpet out of it in the spring and put it back wrong. The floor of it is the boards of the floor below with nothing on them, and a fortnight ago the edge of that floor began to sound hollow under the front door of the room below, so she took up four feet of the boards along the wall that has the light tube in it, and she put them back in the wrong order and a little too far in.

The room is the only one in that house with the afternoon in it. The kitchen has a range, and the range had been out for six weeks and she lit it the first night she was home and it has gone out twice since, and the front room below has a fire that is never in, and the back room is where the bed is. She comes up at the middle of the day and sits down on the boards because the boards are the warmest thing in that house and there is nothing to put on them.

She came off that book at the end of the season. Her name had been on it since she was seventeen and it came off because she asked for that, and she paid six weeks of her own work up the river for it, and she has not worked since. Nothing stands in front of her name and nothing stands behind it either, and no season is running, and that yard is doing a quarter of what it was doing.

He came up the frontage with the thing in him and got no further than the step, because she was at the bottom of that stair with her coat half on, and she looked at him for as long as it takes a person to make up their mind.

"You have come about her mother's range wall."

"You do not know that."

"I know your face on a wet morning and I have known it since before the war." She came up in front of him and not behind him, which is a thing she has worked out. "And I am going to be in this house for another hour and I would rather not spend it being polite about it. So put what you came about into words and then I will tell you what I make of it, because I have been on that book since I was seventeen and I have not been on one since the season shut, and I am the only person on this frontage who can tell you anything about it at all."

The two boards she has taken up are along the edge of the floor at the wall with the light tube in it, and they are two inches the wrong width and she has put them back two inches too far in from the skirting, so that they finish short of the wall by a finger's width the whole length of the room, and the edge of the floor stands open to the joists in a line from the door to the window. What is underneath is a joist every sixteen inches with about half an inch of dust on the top of each one, and she has looked at every one of them and got nothing out of it, and she has put two boards back in the wrong order over the top of that.

"I am going to ask you something and you are not going to like it."

"Ask it."

"What is it actually like. Off the book."

She stood in the middle of that room with her hands behind her and let him wait, and he had time to work out that she was not going to hand him a soft version of anything.

"You want it in a sentence and it is not one thing and I am not going to make it into one. It works like this. For five weeks there is a mark on that page, and everybody in that yard can put a finger on where it is, and the six weeks I did up the water are in it and are worth what I have been told they are worth, and I know exactly what I have got and what it is worth, and I could take it into any office in this borough. And the season has shut and the mark is off the page, and the six weeks went off the page with the mark."

"That is a good trade."

"That is not a trade. That is me getting one woman to ask another woman for me, and it is the only thing I have asked anybody for since before you first came up to this door." She brought her hands round from behind her. "And there is not one book left standing between your name and mine, and nothing in that yard has your name on anything, which is why you have come to ask a woman who is off it instead of asking the woman who keeps it. And I am giving you that now so that you need not spend an hour of my afternoon working it out."

That took her as long as it takes to read one column out, and not one part of it helped him and all of it was true.

He stood in that room and told her what he was going to do. He was going up to number twelve in the morning to ask the mother of that house for the wall behind her range, in nine words, with no figure in his hand, and if she turns him down he will come back down the frontage having done nothing. He told her the whole of it, including the part he had not planned to put to anybody, which is that he has not got a penny for a tray and a flue and a man's week, and that he has not asked a woman for money in his life.

She stood and listened to all of it with her hands at her sides, and at the end of it she picked up one of the two boards, looked at the underside of it, and put it down again a sixteenth of an inch out of line with its neighbour.

"You have told me all of that so that I cannot tell anybody you did not. That is the first sensible thing you have done up this frontage and I am not going to spoil it by telling you it was stupid." She crouched by the boards and got her fingers under the one she had just put down and brought it back to where it had been. "Go and ask her."

"You do not think it is stupid."

"I think you are going to be refused, and I think you ought to be refused, because a woman who has been on that book since she was seventeen does not want a man in her house on the strength of a wet fortnight." She got her hand flat on the boards where they run along the edge, and they are level with each other and not level with the boards they are laid against. "And I think that when you get there you will have that out of the mouth of somebody who has a right to give it you, and I am putting it in front of you now only so that you are not hearing it for the first time out of her mouth in her own pantry."

---

He went back down that stair past her and out into that passage with his hands empty and the whole of the thing in his mouth, and behind him the four feet of floor along that wall stood open to the joists the whole length of the room.

Coming off the book is not a reward. It is a different way of standing in an open yard with nothing on you at all, and nobody standing in it has been let off anything.

Going back up there another day to lift the other four feet would mean admitting out loud that there was something to find, and she is not ready for that, and the rest of that week's work stays not laid along the edge of that floor.
