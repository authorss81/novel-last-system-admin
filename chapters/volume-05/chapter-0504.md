# Chapter 0504 — Where It Had Been Standing

The Wednesday of the tenth of July 2019, from about half past one in the afternoon until about seven in the evening. A lane at the bottom end of a row, and a building on one side of it with a door in the end wall, in Tideglass.

**THE BOROUGH WENT ON BEING THE BOROUGH THROUGH A WEDNESDAY AFTERNOON, AND AT THE BOTTOM OF A LANE A LENGTH OF SHUTTERING CAME BACK TWICE IN THREE DAYS TO A PLACE NINE PEOPLE WALK ROUND, AND A MAN WHO COMES ROUND ON A WEDNESDAY TOOK IT AWAY IN A BARROW WITHOUT BEING ASKED BY ANYBODY, AND A DOOR IN AN END WALL STANDS OPEN ON ABOUT TWENTY FEET OF HARD FLOOR AND NOTHING ELSE, AND NOBODY IN THIS BOROUGH HAS EVER WORKED OUT WHAT IT IS FOR, AND DOWN A PASSAGE BY THE WATER THERE IS A ROOM WITH A WORKING BOOK OPEN IN IT, THE LAST LINE IN IT WAS WRITTEN IN SEPTEMBER 2015, THE RULE UNDER THAT ONE HAS BEEN BLANK SINCE THE END OF NOVEMBER 2014, AND ON THIS WEDNESDAY IT IS BLANK AT 1,687 DAYS.**

The lane is about seventy paces long. It comes down between the back of a row of eight and the side of a building that used to be something else, and at the bottom of it there is an open space about thirty foot across where three lanes meet and a brick store that is out of use and a wall with a door in it.

The door is a single wooden one, and it stands open about two days in three, and behind it there is a hard floor and about twenty feet of it and a wall at the end with a window in it that has been bricked up from the inside, and there is nothing in that space at all.

There is no lock on the door. There is nothing in there to lock. Nobody has ever sat in it, because there is nowhere to sit, and it is not a room anybody keeps anything in.

It is the third such place in that lane this year and there are two more above it, and all three doors are open on the same days, and no person who has ever lived in this borough has been able to tell anybody what they are for.

---

Here is what the whole of that Wednesday was, and it is an ordinary one.

At the top end of that lane there is a canopy on two poles and under the canopy a machine that stopped working before the youngest of that row moved in and has not been looked at since, and nobody in that lane has ever used it for anything. And on a Wednesday morning a man comes round and takes the week's rubbish away in a barrow, and he has done that on Wednesdays for longer than anybody there can remember, and he does the bottom of the lane last because it is the farthest from where he comes in.

At about half past one he was in the barrow with nine inches of yesterday on a newspaper and a length of flex and a wire coat hanger that somebody had put out in the wrong place, and there is no story about any of those three and he is not interested in any of those three, and he says the same three things to the same four people and none of those three things is about the flex.

There is one other thing on that Wednesday.

The shuttering is a length of a shop's outside shutter, about seven foot long, and it came off the front of a building on the parade in about 1994 and it has been a thing that moves around that part of the borough ever since. There are three people in this borough who could tell you where it was the month before and all three of them would be guessing.

On the Sunday before this Wednesday it was standing upright against the wall of the building with the door in it, leaning about a foot forward, in the middle of the twenty foot of open space where anybody coming down that lane with a barrow turns round it.

On the Monday a man coming out of the row with a hand trolley had to go round it, and he went round it, and he did not look at it.

On the Tuesday there was a woman from the parade in there with a barrow who stopped about ten foot short of it and stood, and stood, for about two minutes, and then went round it, and then stood again at the other side of it, and then went up the lane.

On the Wednesday morning the man with the barrow put the bins out and came back down the lane about twenty past two and stopped at the bottom, because it was in the way of the turning.

He stood there about a minute.

And then he said, out loud, to nobody, "That's been in the way all week."

And he did not ask anybody. There was nobody to ask. He turned the barrow round, put it against the shuttering, got his hands under it, and lifted it into the barrow, and it went in the way you would expect a length of shuttering to go, and he took it away with him up the lane and out onto the parade.

And that is the whole of the shuttering and nobody saw the end of it and nobody has said one word about it since.

And here is the thing that is not in anybody's mind.

Nobody on that lane, and nobody on the parade behind it, was under the impression that the shuttering had been put there by anybody. Two of the three who have an account of where it was have been asked in the last year, once each, by somebody who wanted a telephone number, and neither of them mentioned it, and neither of them was lying.

And the woman who stood at the bottom of that lane on the Tuesday for two minutes had come back to that place in the middle of the Wednesday at about half past four, on her way out, and had looked at where it had been, and had stood there for about the length of a cigarette, and had not gone to find anybody either.

**The space it had been standing in is about a foot square of concrete and there is nothing in it and it is now simply a foot of concrete.**

And the rest of the afternoon in that lane was the rest of the afternoon.

There is a second man who comes down that lane most days about half past three because there is a short cut through it and it saves him about six minutes, and he went down it on the Wednesday at half past three the way he goes down it, and the shuttering was not there, and he did not look at the concrete and did not look for anything.

There is a house at the top of that lane where somebody has been meaning to clear out a back room since about March, and on the Wednesday they got two boxes in and stopped, and the two boxes are still in the room.

And at about five the man who had taken the shuttering away came back down the lane with an empty barrow because the barrow goes back the same way, and he came to the bottom of it, and there was nothing in the way, and he turned the barrow round and went up and out onto the parade, and that is the last anybody in this borough has had to do with any of it.

---

And the rest of it, in the same hour and not in the same street, is this.

Four different accounts of a single thing are going round this borough and have been for about three years, and they do not agree with each other, and nobody has ever been in a room with two of them.

Nothing about that changed on this Wednesday. Nobody has laid two of them next to each other. Nobody put the question underneath them to a person. A fifth has not arrived.

And down a passage by that water there is a room with a working book in it that somebody left open to hold its own page. It stands at nineteen entries. The one at the end of them was written on the fourteenth of September 2015. The rule under that one has been blank since the Wednesday of the twenty-sixth of November 2014, and on this Wednesday it was blank at 1,687 days.

Nobody counted it. Nobody went down that passage. Nobody has ever been asked which of the four accounts is theirs.

And there is still one thing in this borough that a person could not give a name to if you asked them there in the middle of the day, and it is the same one it was in the middle of August last year, and it has not altered by a day, and what has happened to it since is that everybody who could have gone out and looked has done exactly that and come home again.

**A MAN CAME DOWN A LANE AT TWENTY PAST TWO ON THE WEDNESDAY OF THE TENTH OF JULY 2019 AND PICKED UP A LENGTH OF SHUTTERING THAT HAD BEEN STANDING IN THE SAME PLACE SINCE THE SUNDAY AND PUT IT IN A BARROW AND TOOK IT AWAY, AND NOBODY ASKED HIM ANYTHING, AND THE CONCRETE HE LIFTED IT OFF IS STILL THERE AND IS NOTHING.**
