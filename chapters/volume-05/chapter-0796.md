# Chapter 0796 — The Four Bites In The Post

Monday the twenty-seventh of February 2023, in this borough. Between the middle of the morning and the middle of the afternoon she is at the bottom of her own boundary at number ten, and four turns of wire round one post is the whole of what that day is about.

**A WOMAN IN HER EARLY THIRTIES HAS FOUR TURNS OF GALVANISED WIRE BIT INTO A POST ON A LEANING RUN OF CLOSE BOARDING AND MEANS TO CUT THEM OFF, HER SISTER CUTS THEM AND TAKES THE FAR POST OF THAT RUN OUT AND GETS THE BOTTOM RAIL BACK ON THE LINE OF THE REST OF THE RUN WITH THE WIRE'S OWN BITES SCARFED OVER AND A NEW TURN OF WIRE PUT BACK ON, AND THE RUN IS BETTER THAN IT HAS BEEN SINCE SHE HAD THE BOARDS OFF IT.**

It has been her yard and her boundary for as long as she has been at that house, and she has been a woman in her early thirties for all of it. Her sister is out at the bottom of that yard with her because the run is eleven feet long and there are four turns of wire round the post at the far end of it.

---

The run of close boarding along the bottom of that yard is eleven feet of a fence that was put up before she came and has been leaning ever since she could see it.

It leans toward the house. It has leaned toward the house for as long as she has been there and the two things that keep it up are the post at each end and a raking strut that somebody put against the far post out of a piece of scaffold pole. The strut goes up off the ground on the house side, crosses the far post about four feet up, and stands on past it. Where the two of them cross there are four turns of galvanised wire round the post and round the strut together, and the wire has bitten into the softwood of that post in four places and gone in about the thickness of a thumb, and it is bright where it has bitten.

Her plan is to cut them off.

She has had a pair of wire cutters in the shed since she came and she has not used them on that wire because cutting it is not free: the moment those four turns are gone the strut is loose against the post and the run has whatever the strut was giving it and no more.

---

The ground in that corner is the one thing in that yard she can never get anything into. It is the top of a bank that goes down to the ditch and it is as hard as a floor in a dry month and like a fist in a wet one, and everything she has ever tried to stand in it has gone in and leaned.

The four fence posts are stacked against the gable end of the house and have been since the summer. The old galvanised bath is on its side in the corner by the step and has been since she came and has no water in it and is not going to get any. The line goes across the yard from the gable to the shed with nothing on it since the autumn. Through the kitchen window the inside of that house looks as it did yesterday.

Her sister looks at the four bright bites for a while before she says anything.

---

"You are after that wire."

"That wire has been on my mind since the summer before last and it is coming off."

"And what happens to the run when you have."

"The run is not going to go over any further than it has."

"Nothing about that run is going to move until you take the weight off it." Her sister has gone along the run and is standing at the far end looking up the strut. "Look at this. That strut is standing on ground that is not going to take it, and it is leaning on the run and the run is leaning on the strut, and the two of them have been leaning on each other for twenty years and neither of them is doing anything about the other. Cut that wire and this strut comes down and takes the end of that fence with it."

"Then I will take the strut out."

"Then you will have the end of that fence off its post and a strut on the ground and nothing carrying anything, and you will be standing in the wet at the weekend doing this fence from the beginning with two people instead of one."

"Then what is to be done with it."

---

"It is done in the order it has to be done in, and the wire comes off last."

She says it flatly and she does not raise her voice, and she goes and gets a saw and a bar and the long bolster out of the shed.

"Nobody can stand a post in that ground as it is."

"Nobody can," her sister agrees. "That is half of why it is leaning. You set a post in ground like that and pack it and it has nothing to grip, and it goes over in the first winter and takes the fence with it."

"So it comes out."

"It comes out. And it does not come out by being dug, because you cannot dig in that and I am not having you on my knees in it. That post goes off at the ground with the saw and the stub comes out with the bar and we open the hole properly and put it back down to where a post wants to be, and then it stands up out of that bank like a post and not like a stick in a hole."

---

They cut the wire off first, because they cannot get the saw to it with the strut where it is.

Four turns, and each of them comes away with a bite of the wood in the crook of it, and the bites are exactly where they were. Her sister stands them in a row on the top of the post so that she can come back to them, and she does not throw them in the bin.

Then they lift the strut off. That is the first thing in that yard that has genuinely wanted two people, because the strut is pinned between the post and the ground it is standing on and it does not come off that post by itself. Her sister takes the weight of it at the top and the woman at number ten slides the bottom of it along the grass until the two of them come apart, and they lay it down above the bank where it will not roll into the ditch.

---

Then the post.

They saw it off at the ground, and it takes longer than either of them expects because the saw goes dull on it every twenty strokes and there is no stone in it to speak of and only the top of a bank to cut through. When the kerf goes right through, the stub in the ground gives about half an inch and stops.

Then the bar goes in beside it and one of them works it and the other puts a foot on the other end of it, and the stub comes up out of that ground with a sound like a boot coming out of a river and about nine inches of the bottom of it in pieces, and the hole underneath is a round hole going down further than they expected, and it has been a post hole all along with a post in the top of it.

Her sister stands and looks down into it.

"There. That is what is carrying that run up. It is a hole and not a hole, and that is the whole reason."

---

Then they bring the run back onto its line, and this is the part that has to be right.

The run is eleven feet of close boarding carried on two rails, and the far post comes out of it about a foot short of the bottom rail and comes up about four inches above the top one. With the post gone, the far end of the run comes down about five inches onto her sister's knee and the whole eleven feet of it begins to swing, and the woman at number ten gets both hands on the top rail at the near end and keeps it up with her own weight while her sister levers the far end up with the bar, an inch at a time, until the bottom rail at that end is running in the same plane as the bottom rail along the rest of that fence.

Then her sister looks along the bottom of the run from the near end to the far end without stopping.

"That is the line. Keep it there."

And they cannot let it go, and that is the shape of the next hour. The woman at number ten stands at the near end with both hands under the top rail and her own weight coming up through her arms, and her sister works the post about eight feet away with both hands full, and not one of them ever puts a hand on her hip.

---

Then she does the post, and she does it on the ground where it can be done properly, with the run off her back.

She does not put the old bites back under the new wire. She turns the post over on the grass and puts the flat of a chisel into each of the four in turn and takes the wire's own scar out of it, and then she takes a shallow bevel off the upper side of each of them so that a wire twisted tight over that spot bears on the whole width of the wood and not in a groove it has cut for itself.

"You have cut the bites out."

"I have taken them out and left the wood behind them true, and I have taken a bevel off the top of each one so that when you put a wire on this post it sits on the width of the wood and not in a hole it has made in it." She runs a thumbnail along the four places. "Those four bright marks are the only thing that tells you where the wire has been. In ten years there will not be one of them on this post."

---

Then the post goes back, and it goes down further than it was.

Down past the top of the bank and past the round hole, until the bottom of it is in the ground that has something in it, and it is packed in layers with broken stone on end at the bottom and hardcore over it and the earth trodden down on top, and her sister says the words while she does it, which is the only instruction she has ever given her about anything.

And the run comes down onto the top of it and the far end is carried again. The woman at number ten lets go of the top rail at the near end, and eleven feet of close boarding that has been in the air since the middle of the morning comes back onto its own post and stays on the line, and it is the first time since the summer she came that the bottom rail at that end has been in the same plane as the bottom rail of the rest of it.

---

Then the strut goes back against the post, and the new wire goes on.

A single turn this time, not four, and it goes round the post and round the strut and it is twisted off on itself and it is drawn up with a piece of flat bar round the near post and it is tight enough that if you get a bar under the strut it does not come out.

"That will keep that."

"That will keep that up for as long as that post stands in that ground, and that post stands in that ground because it is down past the round hole and not in the top of the bank. And one turn is right. Four turns on a post that is taking a strut is four chances for the wire to be the thing that lets go, and one turn on a post that is sound is the whole of the job."

She winds it on and it is one turn and it is tight and the four bright bites in the post are four small cuts and not four holes.

The run stands along the bottom of that yard in the same line as the rest of itself, and it is the best that fence has been since the boards came off the front of it.

---

Then her sister goes out of the gate at the bottom of the lane and she goes along the fence with her hand on the bottom rail, from the near end to the far end, once, and stops at the far post and puts her thumb in one of the four cuts.

She stands with her thumb in one of the four cuts in that post and thinks about the saw going dull on it every twenty strokes, and about the eight feet of air that eleven feet of fence was hanging over for an hour while somebody else did the bottom of a hole.

There are three of them and a saw in what that took, and she is one of the three, and there is nobody on that boundary but her.
