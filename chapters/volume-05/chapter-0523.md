# Chapter 0523 — A Page a Day

The Saturday of the twelfth of October 2019, from about two in the afternoon until about five. One house in this borough, in Tideglass.

**SHE HAS PUT SOMETHING DOWN EVERY SINGLE DAY SINCE THE FOURTEENTH OF JUNE AND NOBODY HAS SEEN IT, AND ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF SEPTEMBER A MAN WHO LIVES WITH HER SAID SHE HAD BEEN QUIET, AND SHE ANSWERED HIM, AND HE READ THE WHOLE OF IT IN ABOUT FOUR MINUTES, AND HE PUT NOT ONE QUESTION TO HER.**

She is a woman in her middle forties and she has a husband and there are no children and there have not been children for a long time and the subject does not come up.

What she writes on is the inside of the back cover of a hardback she has had since about 2011 and has not read since about 2013, and she writes on it with a pencil, and the cover is stiff and the pencil does not show through to the outside, and that was the reason she chose it and she has not told anybody that either.

---

She started on the fourteenth of June and she has not missed a day since, and on the twelfth of October it is one hundred and twenty days.

The first two weeks are not interesting and she knows they are not interesting and she kept going anyway, which was the point of the thing when she started it. She was not feeling worse. She was not in a state. Nothing in that house had changed and nothing was going to change.

What had happened is that she had worked out, in about the middle of May, that she was a person who had opinions, and that she had not said any of them out loud to anybody in a long time.

That is the whole of what happened. There was no event. There was not a row and there was not a death and there was not a word from anybody. A woman who is not seventy-five noticed that she had become somebody who agreed with whatever the last person said, and she could not remember when that had started, and she tried three evenings to work out when it had started, and she could not, and that was in the middle of May.

On the fourteenth of June she put the book on the kitchen side and wrote four lines on the inside of the back cover and put it away again.

Nobody knows. That is the whole of the first hundred and twenty days.

It is not a record and it is not hidden. It is on the side in the kitchen where the bills go, and her husband has a job and is out and is in his own end of the house on the evenings, and it is in a hardback he does not know is in the house.

It is not that she would not show anybody. She has wanted to show somebody almost every day of the hundred and twenty. She has wanted to show one particular person a particular number of times and she could not put a shape on that number and she has not tried.

On the second of July she wrote for about an hour instead of twenty minutes, and what she wrote was that she had been wrong about her sister in 2011 and that she had known she was wrong for about three years of those eight and had let it sit there.

On the nineteenth of August she wrote that she had paid for something in 2014 that she should not have paid for and that she had not minded paying for it and that not minding had been the actual problem.

On the second of September she wrote that she had a good life and that she had been quietly furious for a fortnight and that both of those were true at the same time and that she had never once said the second one out loud in this house.

On the twenty-second of September, at about half past eight in the evening, her husband came out of his own end and said, "Are you all right?"

And she said, "I'm all right."

And he said, "You've been very quiet."

And she said, "Have I."

And he said, "Since June."

And she sat there for about four seconds and then she said, "Come here a minute," and she went and got the hardback off the side and she put it on the low thing in front of the window and she opened it at the back and gave it to him.

And he said, "What is this."

And she said, "It's what I do instead of talking."

And he said, "How long."

And she said, "Since the fourteenth of June."

And he said, "Every day."

And she said, "Every day."

And he picked it up and read it standing up.

He read it in about four minutes. She knows it was about four minutes because she made a cup of tea in the four minutes and brought it over and put it down on the low thing and he had not got to the end.

He read all of it. There are a hundred and twenty days on it and it is not a great deal of writing and about a third of it is a single line.

And he read the last of it and shut it and did not give it back and did not put it down on the low thing, and he stood there with it in his hands for a while.

And she said, "Well."

And he said, "You didn't say."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "When did you find out you were going to say nothing about any of it?"

And she said, "In May."

And he said, "A hundred days."

And she said, "Yes."

And he said, "And you didn't say anything about that either."

And she said, "That was the first one."

And then he did the thing that she has not been able to put down since, and she has gone over it and she cannot make it anything other than what it was.

He said, "I'm not going to ask you about any of it."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "Not one question. Not tonight and not ever, and if you want to tell me some of it you will tell me, and if you don't then you don't, and I would rather not be anybody who sits down and goes through the whole of it with you."

And she said, "Why not."

And he said, "Because you'd tell me on account of my asking, and then the whole thing would turn into a conversation about the not-telling."

And she said, "Yes."

And he said, "So I'm not going to."

And she said, "All right."

And he said, "Can I keep it?"

And she said, "It's yours."

And he said, "Can I read it again on my own, on my own?"

And she said, "Yes."

And he said, "Not tonight."

And she said, "All right."

Here is what has happened since the twenty-second of September, and it is twenty days, and it is very little, and it is not nothing.

He has not asked her one question about any of it. Not one in twenty days, and she has been watching for it, and she has caught herself twice starting to set one up herself and then not doing it.

He has done three things.

The first is that he reads it. He has read the whole hundred and twenty days four times now, on his own, at his own end, with the door shut, and she knows because she can hear him turning pages and because he has said the number twice without being asked, once at about a week and once at about a fortnight.

The second is that he has started putting things in writing.

That is the one she has not got over and it is not a big thing. He has had a piece of paper on the side of the kitchen for about a fortnight now, and on it he writes the things that need saying to her, and mostly the things are ordinary, and once in a while one of them is not.

On the seventeenth of September there was one that said, I am not going to pretend I would have noticed, and that is fair.

She read it standing up in the kitchen at about half past six in the evening, and she stood there with it, and then she put it back on the side of the kitchen and went and did the things she does at half past six.

He came in at about seven and said, "Have you read it."

And she said, "Yes."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "It's fair."

And he said, "It is."

And that was the whole of the conversation the two of them have had about any of this, and it ran to about nineteen seconds.

The third is that some time in the first week of October she came in from the shops and found that the low thing in front of the window had been moved about a foot to the left, and that it was not in the way of the side any more, and that the hardback was on it, face down, at the back, and that he had left it there.

And this is the part that is hers and has nothing to do with him, and she has not told anybody about it, and there is nobody she could tell.

On the hundred and fifth day, which was the twenty-sixth of September, she wrote one line on the inside of the back cover and it is the only line in a hundred and twenty that is about another person in this house.

She wrote, He has started writing things down and putting them where I will find them, and he has not asked me one question, and I do not know what to do with a man who will not ask me a question.

And she did not say that to him, and she is not going to, and on the twelfth of October, at about half past four in the afternoon, she sat in the kitchen and looked at the open back of that hardback on the low thing in front of the window and she did not go and get the pencil.

**SHE HANDED HIM A HUNDRED AND TWENTY DAYS OF IT ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF SEPTEMBER AND HE GOT THROUGH THE WHOLE OF IT IN ABOUT FOUR MINUTES AND SAID HE WOULD NOT PUT ONE QUESTION TO HER ABOUT ANY PART OF IT, AND TWENTY DAYS LATER HE HAD SHIFTED THE THING IT LIES ON A FOOT TO THE LEFT SO THAT SHE COULD REACH IT, AND SHE SAT IN FRONT OF IT AT HALF PAST FOUR AND DID NOT GO FOR THE PENCIL.**
