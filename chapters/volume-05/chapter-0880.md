# Chapter 0880 — Wider At The Top Than At The Bottom

Saturday the twenty-seventh of January 2024, at the hole cut in the apron of the yard at the lock-up, from the middle of the day until the office glass has gone dark. How deep it is and what shape it is.

**THERE IS A SQUARE HOLE CUT INTO THE APRON OF THAT YARD FOUR CORNERS IN FROM THE OFFICE WALL WITH NOTHING IN IT, AND SHE MEANS TO PUT ONE HAND DOWN INTO THAT HOLE WITH THE PALM FLAT AGAINST THE SIDE OF IT AND PUSH HER FOREARM IN UNTIL IT WILL GO NO FURTHER, AND THE WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK STANDS ON THE FLAGS BY THE OFFICE STEPS.**

It has been there since the summer. Four corners in from the office wall, a foot square, cut with a breaker and a spade and finished off with a cold chisel, which is a day of work and is not a job anybody does for nothing.

There is nothing in it.

That is the part that has been in the way of everything else for four months. Whatever it was cut for was not finished, or was finished somewhere else, or was never the point.

Four months of nothing in it is not an oversight. An oversight gets a plank over it in the first week, because everybody who walks past a hole in a yard falls into it and everybody who has fallen into it once puts something over it. This one has had nothing over it since June, and it has stayed that way for one reason, which is that it is in the middle of the working part of that yard and putting a plank over it would put a plank where the barrow goes.

So the hole has stayed open because a hole is what everybody would rather have than a plank.

A hole like that has three things to know and two of them are free.

The first is how deep it is, which anybody can see into from the side of it with a stone dropped in. The second is what shape it is in plan, which anybody can see from above with a boot. The third is what shape it is in section, which cannot be had from outside it, because you have to be in it, and being in it means putting an arm in a foot square hole in a concrete apron in January.

She means to put an arm in it.

The arm goes in.

One hand first, palm flat against the side of the hole, the fingers down, and the hand goes in flat to the second knuckle with the wrist resting on the concrete, and that tells her nothing about the depth. It is the shape of the opening she already knows.

Then the wrist goes over the edge and the forearm follows, and the forearm goes down a foot and a bit, and then the forearm will not go any further and stops against the bottom.

Not the bottom of the hole. The bottom is at a spade's depth and the forearm is shorter than that, and what it stops against is the dish.

There is a dish in the bottom of that hole.

She turns the hand over in it, palm up, and feels the whole of the bottom, which is not flat at all.

A square hole cut in a hard standing does not give a flat bottom.

Whoever cut it stood on it and broke it out from the top with a spade, and a spade through four inches of concrete and into the compaction underneath comes up in loads and the loads do not fill the corners the way the last shovel full can. And then the rain and the wheel and the boot have been at it for four months and the bottom of a hole like that turns into a shallow bowl.

So the bottom is a bowl about a foot across and a couple of inches deep at the middle of it, and the bowl is filled with about an inch of soft brown stuff that has come down off the apron and out of the wind, and a bottom that gives that much is a bottom that has nothing under it at all.

What she was not ready for is that the hole is not square.

From above it is a foot square. Four corners, four straight edges, a cold chisel finish on all of them, and it is as square as a thing cut by eye can be.

From down inside it, with the palm against the far side and the hand going across to the near side, the hole is wider at the top than it is at the bottom, and it is wider at the top on one side and not on the other three.

The office side has gone. About three inches of it has gone over four months and the top of that edge is now a good foot above the bottom of it, and the top of the other three is about nine inches from the bottom of them.

Which is the whole of the finding and it takes about a minute to understand and an afternoon to sit with.

The apron falls to the office.

It falls about an inch in eight feet and it has fallen that way since it was laid, and that is what it is for. Everything that comes off that apron — the rain, the water off the gutter of the office, the water off the back of the lorry when it is washed — goes across it and off the low side, and the low side is the office side, and the office side is where four inches of that hole is.

So the hole is a drain. It has been a drain since it was cut, and nobody designed it as one, and the three inches that have gone on that side have gone because everything the yard has ever washed has gone into that edge of it every day since June.

The other three sides are nine inches deep and they are not moving, because nothing runs across them.

She puts her hand in a fourth time and runs it round the whole of the top of the hole, and it is as square as it ever was, and the near edge is crisp, and the far edge is a slope of loose grit and small stuff going down into the hole at about the angle loose material sits at.

And in the dish in the bottom there is no water standing in it, and there is about an inch of soft stuff in it, and it is the same stuff that has gone down the office edge.

So it drains. Whatever gets into the bottom of that hole goes through the flags and out, and the hole cannot be made to keep anything at the bottom of it, because the bottom of it is not a bottom.

Which is the whole of what that hole will ever be.

The one thing a person can be certain of about a hole like that is that it will be filled, and it will not be filled by anybody deciding to fill it. It will be filled on a wet afternoon by somebody who wants the ground back and has something to hand, and what will go into it is whatever is nearest to the yard door that day.

And there is nothing you could put into that hole which would stay where it was put, which is not an argument against putting something in. It is only the fact, and it is the fact about this hole and not about the next one.

---

The woman who books work comes in through the gate of the yard about two and stands on the flags by the office steps for a while.

She has a folder under her arm and she turns round twice looking at the frontage and then at the base of the office wall, and she reads the bottom of the wall without coming any further into the yard, and the hole is four corners behind her and out of that line.

Then she goes back out through the gate and the gate shuts, and the office glass goes dark about an hour later.

---

A foot square, a spade deep, square in plan and open at the top three inches more than it is at the bottom, and nothing put into that hole will stay where it is put.
