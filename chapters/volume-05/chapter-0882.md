# Chapter 0882 — Four Inches Behind The Stack

Tuesday the sixth of February 2024, at the wall end of the yard at the unit from first light until the yard has gone dark. The gap behind the stack of boards.

**THERE IS A STACK OF ELEVEN SPARE BOARDS ON THE FLAT AGAINST THE WALL AT THE END OF THAT YARD WITH THE OFFCUTS ON TOP OF IT, AND HE MEANS TO PUT A HAND FLAT INTO THE GAP BETWEEN THE SIDE OF THAT STACK AND THE WALL, EDGE FIRST, AND PUSH IT DOWN, AND THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN GOES OUT THROUGH THE SIDE GATE WITH A FOLDED SHEET UNDER HER ARM.**

The stack has been at the wall end of that yard since the summer and it has been rained on from the north side since the middle of September, and the top board of it has already been read and come out at a foot and a bit with a foot two and a quarter at the weathered end.

What is behind it has not been read at all.

A stack of boards against a wall is not a thing anybody looks behind. You cannot see behind it from anywhere except from above, and from above you see nothing, because the top board is on it.

But it matters, and it matters more than most things about a stack, because the whole question of a stack of boards against a wall is whether it can ever come down again.

If it is flush against the wall, it cannot. You cannot get your hands behind the ends of eleven boards to lift them. You have to take the whole thing out in one go, and eleven boards is more than one person can carry, and the only way out is to take them out from the top, which you can do, but it means lifting every board above the one you want.

If it is off the wall, it can be taken down from the front, one board at a time, and put back against the wall afterwards.

And there is a third reason to leave a gap, which is the weather.

A board stood on end against a wall takes what that wall gives it on the face that is against the wall, and gives it back out of the same face, and it never dries. Eleven boards stood on end against the same wall are eleven boards lying in the same wet, and the two outer faces of the stack are fine and everything between them is not.

Which is why nobody who has put a stack of boards up against a wall in this country has ever put it flush. There is always something under it, and the something is always two lengths of batten, and everybody does it and nobody says they have done it.

Which is why he goes round to the wall end of that stack in the first light with his hand open, and puts it in.

The hand goes in flat, edge first, palm to the stack and the fingers down along the gap, and it goes in about four inches before it is against the wall.

Four inches.

That is not a gap that has happened. A gap that has happened is not four inches at the gate end and four inches at the far end. A gap that has happened is four inches at one end and nothing at the other, because a stack pushed up against a wall in a hurry is pushed up against a wall in a hurry at the end nearest the person pushing.

He works the hand along the whole length of the stack.

Four inches at the gate end. Four along. Four at the far end, past the end of the bottom board.

And on the flags the whole way along that gap there are two lengths of batten lying flat.

Not stood on end. Lying, side by side, about a foot apart, running the whole length of the stack, and they are nothing to do with the boards and they have nothing on them and there is a month of grit on the top of both.

That is the whole of the finding and it explains everything above it.

Nobody built that stack four inches off a wall. Somebody laid two lengths of batten down along the wall end of that yard and built the stack off them, because a stack built off two battens is a stack with a gap behind it, and a stack with a gap behind it can be taken down from the front.

And then the battens were not wanted, or were not known about, or belonged to something else on that day, and they have stayed where they were laid.

They are both the same length as the stack. That is not luck either. Both are about the length of eleven-foot six and they came off the same piece of timber as each other and there are two ends of them and there were two ends of the stack, and a man setting out a stack of eleven boards does not measure anything and does not have to.

They are also, he can feel it with his fingers where they cross, not one piece of timber. One is softwood and one is hard, and the softwood one has gone grey along the top of it and the hardwood one has not, and they were not cut on the same day.

Which puts the bottom board of that stack on the battens and not on the flags.

About an inch. Two battens, an inch deep between them, a board on the top.

So the board at the bottom of that stack is a board lying on two battens with air under it the whole length of it, and it is the board that would come off last, and it is the board that has to come off before the battens can come out from under it.

And there are ten boards on top of it.

That is the whole of what is in that gap and it has been in that yard since the summer.

It also means the stack is better built than it looks. A board lying on two battens is a board lying flat, and eleven boards lying flat with their edges square to each other is a stack you can take a board off the top of and put back on without disturbing any of the others. A stack built directly on the flags in a yard that carries water gets a board with a bit of green under it, and one green board in a stack of eleven is a board that moves every time a board comes off above it.

He has never found green in that stack because he has never been able to get his hand to the side of the bottom board, and what a board gives up about how long it has lain on wet ground is written along that side of it.

---

The woman from two doors down goes out through the side gate at about eleven with a folded sheet under her arm.

She stops at the gate because he is at the wall end of the yard with his head in behind the stack and she cannot get through without walking past him.

"Is that a drawing for a window?"

"It's for the window. It's not the right size, that's the thing about it."

"Then somebody else wants to look at it."

"Window frames are all the same size. It's the opening that's different."

"The opening's what's different. That's why I'm standing at a gate talking about it."

Then she went out through the side gate and it shut behind her, and the wall end of the yard was the wall end of the yard.

---

So the battens stay under the bottom board and the bottom board stays on the battens and the ten stay on the bottom board, and nothing about that stack is going to be different until somebody wants a board out of the bottom of it rather than off the top.

Two lengths of batten that are not the same timber are lying on the flags in that gap, and the board they are carrying is the one at the bottom of the stack.
