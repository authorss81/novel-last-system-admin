# Chapter 1022 — A Name In A Shut Bay

Undated in this draft.

**AT THE GATE END OF THAT YARD ONE BAY HAS BEEN SHUT SINCE BEFORE THE SUMMER.**

That place has three bays off it. The third, at the gate end, has been standing with its face shut in front of the place since the spring, because there is nothing in it worth the bother of opening for. The shutter comes down over the whole of the opening on its runner and there is a foot and a half of daylight along the bottom of it and not one inch anywhere else, so that a man in that bay at the middle of a wet afternoon is standing in a dark with a line of light on the floor. The iron door runner is laid along the floor on the yard side of the opening, and it is about eight feet long and two and a half inches wide with a slot down the middle of it, and it has been off its bed and put back so that the left-hand end is level with the concrete and the right-hand end stands a quarter of an inch up on a strip of card somebody wedged under it in a hurry. The lintel over the opening has dropped a little over forty years and is not level with the lintel over the middle bay, and you can see the difference from the yard if you stand far enough back and look along the front.

Against the end wall of that bay there are nine or ten fire plates stood on edge on the concrete, and the two nearest the opening have gone rusty down one side where the wall runs wet, and they are all that bay has in it and they have stood there since before the summer.

He came up the yard in the middle of the afternoon and went into that bay and stood in it. He had not come to ask for anything. He had come up because a season is shut and nothing is going in that yard and there is nothing at the unit either and he had six hours in front of him and nowhere to put them, and a shut bay at the gate end of a builder's yard is the one place a man in his position can stand for an hour without anybody working out what he is up to.

She came along the wall about a quarter of an hour after him. She had seen the shutter up from the office window and she had come out of the office to find out who was in the bay, and she came as far as the mouth of it and stopped there and would not come one step further in, and he could not see her face at all and could hear where she was perfectly.

"You have not come about anything."

"No."

"Then you have come up here to stand in the dark."

"Yes."

She let about ten seconds go past before she put the next of it, and he understood that she was not going to put anything at all until he had put into words the thing she had come out of that office for.

"I am going to want your name."

---

She came out of that office for that and for nothing else, and he had known what it was going to be from the moment he came through that yard door, and he had not known until she put it into words that he was going to answer it.

"I have been asking you for work since the summer," he told her. "Four times, in that office, and you turned me down four times, and you were right four times, and this is not me coming back with a fifth of the same. My name is on no piece of paper anywhere in this borough. Not one sheet of paper in this borough carries my name. There is a sheet that a man signed at the foot of, and I ran a straight line through it in a cold room and set a woman's name under it in her own hand, because she asked me to and because I had nothing else to give her. So put it out now and I will tell you, and you can do what you like with it."

"That was a month ago."

"That was a month ago."

"Give it to me."

"My name is—"

"No." She stopped him where he was. "Not like that. Not in a shut bay in the dark at the end of a shut season with me standing in the mouth of it and you standing in the dark. I am not having it like that."

"Then how do you want it."

"I want it out loud from you in this room when I have asked you for it, and then I will want to know why I asked."

---

And he told her, in the dark, at the far end of that yard, and she did not put it down.

He could not see her at all and he could not tell whether she had moved, and after a while she put four sentences out into the dark, and the four of them were the same four she had put to him at that desk after the summer, and they were all about why she could not.

"A name comes on a page against work somebody asked for. That is the rule and the page carries one column and it always did." Her voice did not go up. "And I cannot put down whether a man came up here because somebody sent him or because he wanted something out of it for himself, and I have not been able to put that down about you since the spring and four goings in that office have not made it any clearer and a fifth will not either. And a man who gives me his name in the dark after a season has shut is not a man who asked me for it, and if I put it on that page in the morning it will be the one line on it that nobody asked for, and I have had one of those in my hand already this season and I could not put it down."

"That is fair."

"It is fair and it is no use to you, and both of those are true at once." She shifted where she was standing. "You have given me the only thing you own in a room where I could not repeat it if I tried, and you did it on purpose, and I want you to know that I know that."

He stood in that bay and understood what he had done and what it had not done. His name is in a room at that yard and it is on nothing. No sheet in this borough carries his name at the foot of it and none is going to, because the one woman who could put one there has told him in four sentences exactly what is wanted of a man before anything, which is that he ask her for it in front of somebody else and let her turn him down.


On the way out through the middle bay he stood and listened to the yard and worked out that not one man in this borough can be put down anywhere at all, and that he now has a name that two people know, and that one of the two keeps a book.

It cost him the one thing he had. He gave the only thing he owns to the one woman on that frontage who could have used it and who told him in four sentences why she could not, and it went on nothing at all, and there is one shut bay on that site where a man put his name out loud into the dark to somebody he could not see and it is the only place on this earth where it has ever been said.

He put his name into a shut bay at the end of a season with nothing at all behind it and nothing in front of it, so that one room in this borough now keeps a man by his name, and the yard light is coming in under that shutter in a slot an inch high and will be there in the morning.
