# Chapter 0544 — Three Things Nobody Corrected

The Friday of the thirty-first of January 2020, and most of the day, in five streets of this borough.

**THE TRAIN CAME IN BEHIND HER AND WENT OUT AGAIN IN UNDER A MINUTE, A SHOP GAVE A WOMAN SOMETHING ELSE BECAUSE SHE SAID THE USUAL TO A STRANGER, AND A MAN ON THE OTHER SIDE OF THAT BOROUGH HAS BEEN ORDERING THE SAME THING FOR FIVE YEARS UNDER A NAME THAT WENT OUT OF USE IN NOVEMBER, AND NONE OF THE THREE HAS ANYTHING TO DO WITH THE OTHER TWO.**

She is a woman past sixty who goes to a station four miles off most days of the week, because there are people there and because the house she comes out of has a view of the next building and nothing else.

On the Friday of the thirty-first of January she was at Lewisham station from about half past nine.

She had gone out along the far platform at about ten to ten to find out from the board what time the next one was, because the board on that platform is a different board and it says something different, and she does that most days.

She got to the end of it. She stood about a minute and a half looking at the board, which said what it said, and then she turned round.

The train was going out.

There is no way to say that that makes it anybody's doing. She had gone along that platform ninety times since about April and had never once been in a position where a train was going out from underneath her, because the timing of it is not possible to get wrong on purpose.

She stood at the end of the far platform and watched it go and then she came back along the platform she had started from and sat down on the seat at the wrong end, where she does not sit, and the next one came at about twenty past ten and she got on it.



She did not write to anybody about it. She did not go down to the office and tell them, and she did not ring the number in the fine line at the bottom of a ticket she did not keep. She sat on the seat at the wrong end of the platform for about twenty-five minutes and then she went down and got the next one and came home.

What she was not, at any point between ten to ten and twenty past ten, was somebody who had been badly treated. That is the part of it that will not leave her. Nobody made a mistake on her behalf. She went to the end of a platform to read a board, and a train did what trains do, and she was on the wrong side of a building when it did it, and she has been going to that end of the platform since about April.

She has done it once before, in July, and she took the one after that one as well, and she has never once put the two of them together in her own head, and if you were to describe it to somebody you would not be able to make it sound like anything at all.

---

The shop at the end of that street sells the things that this borough's shops sell, and it has sold the same bread behind the same glass for a long time.

The woman is in her late seventies and she has been going in on a Thursday for nineteen years and she has been going in on a Friday as well since about 2011, and she says the usual, and the usual has been whatever she says the usual is.

Since the middle of January the man at the front of that shop has not been the man who was there before. She worked out who he was on the first Thursday. On the second Thursday she said, "The usual, love," and the new man said, "Right," and gave her something else, which was in fact a different bread and not as good.

She looked at it on the way home and she thought about turning round and going back and she did not, because it was a Thursday and she had another three stops after that one.

On the Thursday after that she went back and she asked for the other one, the one she had been given the week before, and the new man gave her that one without any argument, and she has been going in every day since.

On the Friday of the thirty-first she went in a little before midday and she asked, of the new man, "Have you got what his dad used to do with the seeds?"

And the new man said, "Sorry?"

And she said, "The seeds. The dark ones."

And he said, "Right," and went and got them, and she took them home, and they are in the cupboard and they are not the same seeds as they were before the middle of January and they are not going to get any better being in the cupboard.

She has not mentioned it to anybody. She has not mentioned it to the new man either, and he has not mentioned it to anybody, and he has not asked her once who she means.



She had a fixed order and everybody in that shop since 1975 has known it, and the new man does not know it and did not ask, and she has not told him.

On the day of the thirty-first she went in and he had put out a different bread, and she looked at it and she took it and she said nothing, and she has been back six times since and every time she has said "The dark ones" and every time he has put out the dark ones, and neither of them has referred to it again.

The seeds are in the cupboard at the front of the house and she will use them and they will not be as good, and she has not said a word about that either, to anybody.

---

On the other side of this borough there is a place that has done a cooked breakfast on a Saturday morning since about 1990 and the man who has been going in for five years has been ordering the same thing for five years.

It is a breakfast. It has not changed. It has been the same price twice and it is not the same price now, and he does not know the price because he does not pay attention to it.

The name of it changed in November. That is all that happened. The name on the board went from one thing to another thing, and both are the same food, and the woman behind the front of that place said nothing about it when she changed it because it is a menu and menus get changed.

He has been going in and saying the old name since November.

She has been taking what he said and making it. She has asked him once, in about the fourth week, whether he was sure, and he said yes, and she made it.

There is no correcting anywhere in that exchange and neither of them has looked for any.



Down by the water there is a place that none of those five streets reaches, and in that place there is an old working book, about two inches deep. Somebody ruled a line under the final entry in a column in it in the September of 2015 and nobody has put anything below that line since. That gap measures one thousand eight hundred and ninety-two days.

No door was opened this week and nobody counted anything, and not one person in any of those five streets has ever been aware that the book is there.

There is one thing in this borough that nobody has ever managed to describe to anybody else, and there has been a man going about after it who said out loud, more than once, that he would find out what it was.

He has not found it. Nobody has, and nobody has taken it up after him, and there is nobody left here with any reason to go and look. That is not a change. It is the same as it was in the September and it will be the same in the March.

**THE TRAIN WENT OUT WITH HER STANDING AT THE WRONG END OF THE FAR PLATFORM, THE NEW MAN IN THE SHOP HAS SOLD HER SEEDS THAT ARE NOT THE SEEDS SHE ASKED FOR AND HAS NOT ASKED HER ONCE WHO SHE MEANS, THE WOMAN HAS TOLD NOBODY, AND THE MAN ON THE OTHER SIDE OF THIS BOROUGH IS STILL ORDERING A THING UNDER ITS OLD NAME AND IS STILL GETTING IT, AND NONE OF THAT IS GOING TO BE PUT RIGHT BY ANYBODY.**
