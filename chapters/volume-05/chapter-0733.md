# Chapter 0733 — The Front Room Light

Monday the thirteenth of June 2022, in this borough. She is in her own house at number nine from first light until dark, and the whole of it is a stool that gets dusted and a light that gets switched off early.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES DUSTS THE ASH STOOL IN HER OWN FRONT ROOM, SITS ON IT FOR THE FIRST TIME SINCE NOVEMBER, SPEAKS TO HER SISTER IN HER OWN KITCHEN ABOUT THE PADS, AND SWITCHES THE FRONT ROOM LIGHT OFF AT THE DOORWAY BEFORE SHE GOES TO BED.**

She is a woman in her late fifties at number nine on Monday. Her sister comes to her back door with no bag and she lets her in and speaks to her in the kitchen.

---

She starts in the kitchen with the six pads on the fridge, the first one crossed twice, and she does not touch them.

The jug is on the shelf with its two layers of tape. The back door shuts true. The fire is unlit and she leaves it unlit, though the evening is cool.

Then she takes the duster through to the front room opposite the window, where the three oak stools and one ash stand round nothing, and the space has been empty since the fifth of November.

---

She dusts the ash stool first, top and legs and rungs, and then the three oak ones, and then the window sill and the shelf with nothing on it.

The sixty-watt bulb burns above her with its clean shade from May, steady and yellow. She looks up at it and then back at the stool with its dust gone, and she does something she has not done since the autumn.

She sits down on it.

She has stood on it in May to reach the shade, and standing on a stool is a thing you do with your hands busy. Sitting is a thing you do with nothing to do, and that took a decision where the other one had not.

She sits with her hands in her lap and her feet flat on the boards, and the stool takes her without a sound. The room is cold and the light is on and the road is empty beyond the glass, and she sits there long enough for the cold to come through her skirt.

Then she gets up and sets the stool back opposite the window where it was, a finger to the left where she found it, and smooths the seat with her palm.

---

Her sister comes to the back door at dark with no bag, just herself, and she lets her in, and the door shuts true behind her the way the door has always shut.

"You sat in there," her sister says, seeing the dust on her skirt. "I can tell by your step coming down the passage."

"I did," she says. "A little while. It takes me the same as it did."

"What for?"

"To know," she says. "A stool you will not sit on turns into a thing you are afraid of, and I am not afraid of a stool."

Her sister takes her cardigan off over her arm and hangs it on the newel, which is a thing she does.

"Now sit down," her sister says, "and tell me what you ate."

"Nothing worth writing," she says. "Bread and an egg."

"That is not an answer."

"It is the truth with the crust off," she says. "You did not come ninety yards for an egg."

Her sister smiles, and the kitchen goes soft round them with the back door shut and the road gone out of it.

"And the pads?" her sister says.

"Six on the fridge," she says. "First crossed twice. I have not touched them today and I will touch them tomorrow."

"That is something," her sister says.

"It is enough," she says.

Her sister stays a while and looks at the four stools through the doorway and does not go into the front room, and neither of them asks her to.

"You will want somebody in there with you," her sister says at last, "when you do it again."

"No," she says. "I will want the door open and the road where it is, and that is what I have got."

---

She sees her sister out through the kitchen, and the back door shuts true behind her, and she stands a moment with her hand on it and hears the gate.

Then she goes through to the front room doorway.

The four stools stand round the empty space with the dust gone, and the ash one with the seat smoothed where she sat. She looks in a moment from the doorway without going in again.

Then she puts her hand to the switch by the frame and switches the front room light off, though it is not yet bedtime, and the room goes dark behind her with the stools in it, and she goes to the kitchen and sits at the table with her hands round a cup, and the house is dark at the front and lit at the back, the way it is when she has done what she meant to do.
