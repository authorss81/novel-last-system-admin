# Chapter 0733 — The Front Room Light

Monday the thirteenth of June 2022, in this borough. She is in her own house at number nine from first light until dark, and the whole of it is a stool that gets dusted and a light that gets switched off early.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES DUSTS THE ASH STOOL IN HER OWN FRONT ROOM, SITS ON IT FOR THE FIRST TIME SINCE NOVEMBER, SPEAKS TO HER SISTER AT THE GATE ABOUT THE PADS, AND SWITCHES THE FRONT ROOM LIGHT OFF BEFORE SHE GOES TO BED.**

She is a woman in her late fifties at number nine on Monday. Her sister leans on the gate with no bag and she speaks to her out there.

---

She starts in the kitchen with the six pads on the fridge, the first one crossed twice, and she does not touch them.

The jug is on the shelf with its two layers of tape. The back door shuts true. The fire is unlit and she leaves it unlit, though the evening is cool.

Then she takes the duster through to the front room opposite the window, where the three oak stools and one ash stand round nothing, and the space has been empty since the fifth of November.

---

She dusts the ash stool first, top and legs and rungs, and then the three oak ones, and then the window sill and the shelf with nothing on it.

The sixty-watt bulb burns above her with its clean shade from May, steady and yellow. She looks up at it and then back at the stool with its dust gone, and she does something she has not done since the autumn.

She sits down on it.

She sits with her hands in her lap and her feet flat on the boards, and the stool takes her without a sound. The room is cold and the light is on and the road is empty beyond the glass, and she sits there long enough for the cold to come through her skirt.

Then she gets up and sets the stool back opposite the window where it was, a finger to the left where she found it, and smooths the seat with her palm.

---

Her sister comes to the gate at dark with no bag, just herself, and leans on the post.

"You sat in there," her sister says, seeing the dust on her skirt.

"I did," she says. "A little while. It takes me the same as it did."

"What for?"

"To know," she says. "A stool you will not sit on turns into a thing you are afraid of, and I am not afraid of a stool."

Her sister smiles, and the evening goes soft round them with the lamps coming on down the road.

"And the pads?" her sister says.

"Six on the fridge," she says. "First crossed twice. I have not touched them today and I will touch them tomorrow."

"That is something," her sister says.

"It is enough," she says.

---

She sees her sister off down the pavement and goes back in through the kitchen to the front room doorway.

The four stools stand round the empty space with the dust gone, and the ash one with the seat smoothed where she sat. She looks in a moment from the doorway without going in again.

Then she reaches in and switches the front room light off, though it is not yet bedtime, and the room goes dark behind her with the stools in it, and she goes to the kitchen and sits at the table with her hands round a cup, and the house is dark at the front and lit at the back, the way it is when she has done what she meant to do.
