# Chapter 0756 — The Hole In The Yard

Wednesday the seventh of September 2022, in this borough. He is at his own unit and yard from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a hole filled with the wrong rubble by the wrong pair of hands.

**A MAN IN HIS EARLY FORTIES HAS A HOLE IN HIS OWN YARD TO FILL, A LAD WITH A VAN BRINGS RUBBLE THAT IS THE WRONG SHAPE, AND THE TWO OF THEM FIND THE FILL WILL ROCK AND NEITHER OF THEM PUT IT RIGHT.**

He is a man in his early forties at the unit on Wednesday. A lad with a van comes into the yard with rubble he did not choose, tips it in, and packs it his own way.

---

He is in the unit from early with the roller door up to its bracket and the bench along the wall with his pencil behind his ear and his hand going to the pocket every so often for the scrap of scribing card with a twelve on one side and room on the other. The oak gates lean in their stack against the far wall with nothing on them but the chalk that the weather has taken most of off, and the one carrying two marks and a clean patch stands at the end nearest the door. The two-tread stool is folded by the paint. The drawer under the bench is shut, and the bag with the brush in it is on the bottom of that drawer where it was put in July and has not been out.

Outside, the yard is hard and grey and the low wall along the front has gone the colour of a wet cigarette. At the mouth of the unit door the ground has opened out into a hollow the width of a car and about a foot deep in the middle, and it has been getting deeper since the summer because everything that comes in turns to go round it.

He has the barrow and a shovel and a bar he meant to break the lumps with, and he means to go down, tip the barrow in, break it, and roll it flat with the back of the shovel until the yard takes it back.

The van comes round off the ring road at the middle of the morning and stops at the kerb.

---

"You get a vanload of it free." The lad shuts his door and swings the tailgate. "The tip's on the corner where they are doing the pipe."

"That is pipe trench spoil and I have a hole, not a trench."

"It is brick." The lad looks back into the tray. "Half of it is brick. There is a bit of slab in there and a lump of blue and a bit of pipe, and I can pick the pipe out on my yard."

"You can pick the pipe out on your yard." He comes out to the tailgate and looks at what is in it. "What size."

"What size."

"How big are the lumps."

The lad turns round and looks at the tray for a while as though the answer might be written on the side of it.

"Big."

"That is not a size." He leans on the side of the tray. "There is a hole in my yard the width of a car and I am going to put this in it and roll it flat with the back of a shovel. If it goes in as fist-sized lumps I break them. If it goes in as head-sized lumps with rebar in it then I am putting my hole back for a winter and doing it twice."

"It is all under a metre." The lad swings his leg up into the tray. "Nobody digs that in one lump."

"Most people do." He looks at the mound. "Get it tipped and go on. I will pick out what will not do."

The lad tips it.

It comes out in a heap rather than a fall, and half of it is what he told the lad would be — broken brick, mostly, out of something that had been a wall once — and half of it is not. There is a piece of blue engineering brick as long as his forearm. There is a slab of paving about the width of a shovel blade with a mortar skin still stuck to the underside of it. There is a length of pipe with a socket on the end and a bar across it that could be a piece of reinforcement or could be the remains of a gate stay, and it is not going into a hole in a yard for any reason anybody could give him.

He puts the shovel down and picks the pipe out and puts it on the wall.

"Leave that." The lad is standing on the heap. "I told you I would pick it."

"You told me you would pick it on your yard."

"That is where I told you." The lad looks at his boots in the brick. "It is not the same as picking it here."

It is the same and they both know it, and the lad stays on the heap and takes his boots out of it slowly, one and then the other, and steps off onto the yard.

"Right." He goes for the bar. "Keep off it and I will break the blue and we will see what is under."

The bar goes through the blue brick twice and it comes apart into three pieces, one of which goes down into the hole and stands up on end. He breaks those too. The slab he turns over with the toe of his boot, looks at, and pushes into the hole flat with the shovel.

Then it is filling, and the lad takes the shovel off him.

"You will never pack that with a shovel." The lad gets a shovel out of his own van and comes back. "You want it in loose and then you want it walked. That is what a shovel does not do."

"I know what a shovel does not do." He watches. "And I know what a shovel does do, which is put it in small and get the corners."

"You cannot get the corners with a shovel on a fill like this." The lad goes to work. "You want it rolling."

It is better than his would have been. That is the trouble with it. The lad digs into the middle of the heap and throws the brick outward, and the outward brick goes down and the middle comes up, and every so often he gets both boots on the crown and rocks his weight and moves round it, and the heap goes down solid and level and comes up proud in the middle like a loaf that has proved.

"Do not stop there." He puts his hand on the lad's shoulder. "That is proud. That is proud by a finger and a half and it will rock the first time a barrow crosses it."

"It is level."

"It is level on the edges and up in the middle." He puts his boot on the crown of it and presses. "You have made a dish that goes down round the sides. Water will sit in that and freeze and lift the lot and the whole thing will come up in the spring as one piece and I will have to bar it out again."

"It is packed."

"It is packed proud." He looks at the lad. "Break the middle down, spread it to the sides, and take the crown off with the back of the shovel. Then come round the edge twice and leave it."

The lad looks at the loaf.

"It will not be level if I take the crown off."

"It will not be level." He picks up the shovel and puts it down in front of the lad with the handle towards him. "It will be a yard that has been filled instead of a mound somebody made on a yard. That is the whole of the trade and you know it as well as I do."

The lad takes the shovel.

---

He does it the way he was told, badly at first and then not so badly, breaking the crown down into the sides and going round the edge twice with the weight on his heels. It is nearer right when he stops than it was. It is not right. The pipe spoil is packed in a loose layer across the middle with no depth of cover over it, the blue brick has gone to the low side where the water will run, and the whole thing is now a shallow saucer with a thin place in the middle that will go soft first.

He stands and looks at it with his hand still on the shovel and does not say the last of it.

"You are going to say something."

"I am going to say nothing." He turns the shovel over and looks at the blade. "It is a yard. It is filled. It will do for the winter and it will not do for the second winter and by then you will not be here and neither will the van."

The lad takes his own shovel back to his van and shuts it. He goes to the gate and stands with his hand on it, and then turns round and comes back and steps into the middle of the new fill and knocks it flat with his heels, twice, hard, and comes off it.

"Nine foot of pipe over there is not mine either."

"It is not yours." He looks at the door. "Get on with your day."

The van goes out and the yard is quiet. He gets the bar and knocks the crown off the middle once more where the lad's heels have not been,  and then he stands with one heel on the top of the fill and puts his weight on it and lets it go.

It moves. It is not much and it is not nothing, and the cold comes up through the sole of his boot from the yard underneath, which is the sound a yard makes when it has not taken a fill back yet.

He takes his heel off it and looks at the boot, and then he picks up the bar and the shovel, puts them through the unit door under the bench, and shuts the roller door down on the last of the light with the fill standing proud in the middle of his yard where a lad with a van left it.
