# Chapter 0214 — Two Hands

Wednesday the thirtieth of December 2015, from about seven in the evening, a first-floor room over a shop in Ravensmere.

**ON THE LAST EVENING OF THE YEAR THERE ARE TWO PIECES OF PAPER IN THIS CITY AND BOTH OF THEM ARE IN FORCE, AND ONE OF THEM IS A PERMISSION AND ONE OF THEM IS AN OBLIGATION, AND THEY DESCRIBE THE SAME ACT, AND ONE OF THEM IS SHORTER THAN THE OTHER, AND NOBODY IN EITHER CITY CAN SAY WHICH OF THE TWO THE PEOPLE WERE HANDED.**

The room over the shop has a gas ring that does not work and an electric one that does, and a table, and three chairs, and it is let out to people on a Sunday for a class and to anybody who has nowhere warmer to sit between six and nine on a Wednesday in December, which on the thirtieth of December was three people.

Jonas Mercer had the sheet from the works office in a plastic wallet in his inside coat pocket, because Iselin Krogh had put it in his hand in a corridor on the fourth of December and said it was his and there was no charge for it and there was no record of who had taken one.

Nell Ardent had four sheets of her own on the table, and the top two of them were in her own writing, and the third one was the group's own printed agenda for the fourth of December with two lines pencilled beside item three, and the fourth was empty.

**AND THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THE WALL OF THAT ROOM THAT EVENING, printed at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A SHEET ON A WALL AND A SHEET IN AN OFFICE CAN BOTH BE IN FORCE.**
> **ONE OF THEM IS SHORTER.**
> **NEITHER OF THEM IS A LIE.**

"I've copied two things," said Nell Ardent, "and I want them on this table before I say anything, because I have been carrying them round in a bag since the fourth of December and I have not been able to put them anywhere, and that is the most irritating thing that has ever happened to me and I have had a fairly good run."

She put the first sheet down.

That one is off the wall in the corridor of the works office, and Jonas Mercer had read it on the fourth of December with the man of about thirty-one standing next to him, and it is a good sheet and it is short and it says this and only this:

*Nothing is required of any person in order to use any part of this system.*

"That is the whole of it," said Nell Ardent. "One line, six inches of type, four inches of white underneath it. And there is a woman of about seventy-three who comes to that standpipe at about half past six every morning, and she has been coming since before the filters were done, and she has never in her life been asked for anything, and she is right not to be, and that is the sentence on the wall."

She put the second sheet down beside it.

"And that is the sheet out of the office, the full one, which I have now read twice, because you read it out in front of me on the fourth and I copied both lines off it in a corridor afterwards, and I have compared the two with a ruler because that is what I do. The first line is the same first line, in the same type, and under it, at the same size, with the same space above it, there is this."

*A person has no standing in respect of anything recorded under this.*

"Same type," said Nell Ardent. "Same size. Same sheet. Same printing. Same day, near enough, because both of them carry the date the filters were done in a footnote. One of them has had a line taken off it, and it has not been left blank, it has been taken off, and the four inches underneath are white and they are not white because there was nothing there."

Nobody said anything for a bit. The gas ring ticked.

"Now I am going to say the thing I have been trying to say since the fourth of December and I am going to say it carefully because Tomas is not here and he would have done it better," said Nell Ardent, "and I am not going to say it to Jonas, because he has read more paper this month than the rest of us put together and I would like this to be a table. I am going to say it to the table, and Sanaa, you are the one I want to hear it against, because you are the only one of the three of us who spends her working days being handed a person that nobody can be made to answer for, and I have got nothing else to go on."

**AND THE QUESTION SHE HAD BEEN CARRYING CAME OUT OF HER MOUTH AS A FACT ABOUT TWO PIECES OF PAPER, AND SHE DID NOT ANSWER IT, AND IT IS NOT ANSWERED NOW.**

"A permission is a thing a person may do," said Nell Ardent. "An obligation is a thing a person has to do. And I have been carrying those two pieces of paper round this region since the fourth of December looking for a way of telling anybody which of them is on the wall and which of them is in the office, and there is not one, and I have got to the bottom of it, which is that they are the same act.

"Every one of those is a permission. You do not have to give anything to use the water. And on the same sheet, in the same type, it says that whatever the water made a note of is now not yours to have a say about. Those are not two different systems. That is one system, and it has a front door and no back, and the front door is the sentence on the wall and the back of it is the sentence underneath, and the sentence underneath is not hidden from anybody, and if you never ask for it then the thing on the wall is the whole of what that region has ever told a person about itself."

"Which of the two did they hand them," said Sanaa Iqbal.

"That," said Nell Ardent, "is exactly it, and I have not got an answer and I have looked, and I am not going to make one up in a room above a shop on the thirtieth of December, and if I did make one up then it would be the first thing anybody has said in nine months about any of this that was not true, and I would rather turn up in March with nothing."

"The answer is on the second sheet," said Jonas Mercer.

"The answer is on the second sheet," said Nell Ardent, "and the sheet with both lines on it is in an office in a building that owns a works, and it is not in any building in your borough, and the short one is on a wall that about two hundred people a day go past without reading, and the office hands the long one to anybody who walks in and asks for one, and you are the only person who has asked."

"Nobody is required to ask," said Sanaa Iqbal, "and everybody who comes to that standpipe is better off than they would be if somebody stood at the end of it with a clipboard, and I want that said in this room, because in about six months one of us is going to be tempted to make this into a fight about the sheet, and it is not a fight about the sheet. It is a fight about whether two sentences that are both true can be printed one above the other." She put her cup down. "And the answer to that is yes, and I do not like it, and there is nothing anybody can do about it before the end of the week, and we should go home."

**AND NOTHING WAS RENEWED ANYWHERE IN EITHER CITY THAT DAY, AND NOBODY NAMED ANYTHING, AND NOTHING WAS MINUTED, AND THE DOCUMENT MADE IN THIS CITY ON THE THIRTEENTH OF AUGUST IS NOT IN FORCE AND ITS LINE UNDER THE DATE ON PAGE TWO IS STILL EMPTY, AND THE TWO-PAGE ARRANGEMENT THAT CAME DOWN ON THE THIRTIETH OF SEPTEMBER IS IN FORCE, AND THE REGIONAL SYSTEM IS NOT GOING TO EXPLAIN ITSELF BEFORE THE END OF THE WEEK, AND NOTHING THAT HAD BEEN OPEN IN THIS BOROUGH SINCE THE SUMMER GOT CLOSED IN THAT ROOM, AND THE ONE THING THAT WENT IN WENT OUT EXACTLY AS IT WENT IN.**

Nell Ardent folded her four sheets in half and put them in her bag, in the order they had been in, which is a thing about her. Sanaa Iqbal washed her cup because there was a sink and because the person whose room it is is not there and does not mind. Jonas Mercer put the plastic wallet back in the inside pocket of his coat.

There was a bus at about ten past nine. Nell Ardent got on it and Jonas Mercer walked her to the stop and then walked back, and Sanaa Iqbal had gone out to the car park at about nine and had said she would see the other three on the Monday and had not said anything else about any of it. The group does not sit between Christmas and the sixth and Iselin Krogh is in on a Monday and had said so, and the works office is a two-storey building with a lamp in the car park that comes on at about half past four whether anybody is on it or not.

The early boat is at ten past seven on the Wednesday and all four of them will be on it, and it goes from the slip outside the works to the landing on the other side, which is the other way round from the twenty-ninth of November and the same seven minutes past ten in the morning, and there is nothing open in this city at six o'clock in the morning and there has not been since before any of them came.
