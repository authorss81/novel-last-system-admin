# Chapter 0243 — A Sheet

Sunday the twenty-ninth of May 2016, from about half past ten in the morning, a bus shelter at the end of a parade of shops in Tideglass, and a shop on that parade.

**ONE SHEET, NOT ADDRESSED TO ANYBODY, ONE LINE ACROSS THE TOP WITH NO BOX UNDER IT AND NO NAME, AND UNDER THE LINE A COLUMN OF PLACES, AND THE PLACES ARE TOWNS, AND IT ANSWERS NOTHING AND ASKS FOR NOTHING.**

There is a bus shelter at the end of the parade with a bench in it and a timetable that has been wrong since the winter, and the wind comes round the corner of the parade on a Sunday at about half ten in a way that takes paper out of a bag.

A woman got off the bus at about ten past eleven with a plastic wallet of papers under her arm and sat down on the bench and went through them, and she did not read them, she dealt with them: one pile on her knee, one pile on the seat, and the ones that were neither went on the ground, and then she picked them up again, which is what everybody does twice.

About half of them blew away on the third go round.

The woman who has been in the Methodist hall on Carrow Lane about two years was waiting for a bus that was late and stood at the shelter and watched her do it and then said, "Here, love, that's yours, that one on the bench."

It was on the bench, face up, at the end.

A4. One line of printing across the top in about six inches of type, and under it a column of places, and no box anywhere on it, and no name, and no address, and no date, and nothing at the bottom.

> *Any part of any system in this region may be used by any person.*

"That is the rule off the wall in the hut at Penhale," said the woman in the shelter. "That is word for word. I read it every time I go past and I have never once got tired of it."

The woman with the wallet looked at it and said, "It isn't mine," and got back on the bus, and did not want it explained, and was gone before anybody could have asked her anything at all.

There were eight places under the line. Netherby. Tolforth. Halsey Cross. Ansterwick. Kirkmoss. Ellersby. Marleby. Ockbrook.

"That's eight," said the woman in the shelter, counting them with a finger, which nobody had asked her to do. "I know one of them. Ockbrook's on the way to my sister's, I've driven past it, and that's the whole of what I know about it, and I'd not be able to point at it. The rest of them I couldn't tell you if you paid me."

"It's not signed," said the man of about eighteen, who had come along for no reason anybody could remember, and who washed up in a kitchen at the back of the parade and had been in a Methodist hall on Carrow Lane twice.

"It's not signed because there's nothing to sign. There's no name on it. There's nowhere to put one." The woman in the shelter turned it over and showed him the back, which is blank. "That's the whole thing. That's all of it. I went through it about four times on that bench and it's a rule and eight towns and it isn't asking us for anything, and I've had it in my hand for ten minutes and I still don't know what it's *for*."

---

The trade counter at the back of the shop on the parade has a lamp over it, and under the counter there is a drawer with a lock on it, and in the drawer there is an invoice spike, a roll of dockets and about six years of other people's money.

Hanna Wray read it standing up, twice, and did not sit down.

"That's the same six inches of type," she said. "That's off a machine that somebody else set. Look at the spacing. It's the same as the wall in the hut and it's the same as the thing on a corridor in the city on the far side of the water that the woman with the bag has got two of in her own handwriting, and that is a fact about printing and not about anything else."

"Is it the same?" said the man of about eighteen.

"It's the same sentence. It's not the same sheet. The one on the wall at Penhale has got a second line under it and nobody prints the second line." She squared it on the counter and put it down the side. "And this one's got eight towns under it and nobody printed a word about a single one of them, and that's what it is. That's the entire content of that piece of paper. A rule anybody can use, and eight places it can be used from. It doesn't say when it started. It doesn't say who set it and it isn't signed at the bottom. It doesn't say what it's for. And if you rang the number on the back of the timetable in that shelter you'd not get anybody who could tell you what to do with it, because there isn't anybody to ring. It is a thing that could not be acted on if a committee of this borough got hold of it tomorrow and tried for a fortnight."

The woman who has been in the Methodist hall about two years said, "Then why's it in my handbag?"

"Because it was on a bench," said Hanna Wray. "Hold on, though, before you put it anywhere. Can it be posted?"

"I don't know. There's no name on it."

"There's no name on it, no address, no reference, nothing to put on a stamp, and the post office round here will send anything without an address straight back, and I know that because I've done it with a bill." Hanna Wray turned it round on the counter without picking it up. "So it's not lost, and it's not mislaid, and there's nobody to send it to, and it's not been stolen, and there's nobody anybody can ring. That's four things a lost thing usually is and it's none of them."

She put it in the drawer under the trade counter, face up, on top of a docket from the week before, and shut the drawer, and the drawer has got a lock on it and she has got the only key and she has never once given it to anybody.

She did not open it again that day.

Jonas Mercer was in the doorway of the shop next door for about six minutes with his hands in his pockets, and the shop next door does not sell anything anybody wanted that morning, and he did not cross the road, and by the time he did look the woman with the wallet had gone, the man of about eighteen had gone to work, and the woman in the shelter had got on a bus and was not going to write the eight towns down for anybody.

**AND A SHEET WITH A RULE ON IT AND EIGHT TOWNS UNDER IT IS IN A DRAWER UNDER A TRADE COUNTER WITH AN INVOICE SPIKE IN IT, AND A SHEET IN THE SAME SIX INCHES OF TYPE IS ON A WALL IN A CORRIDOR IN A CITY ON THE OTHER SIDE OF THE WATER, AND A THIRD IS ON A WALL IN A HUT IN THE NEXT COUNTY, AND THE EIGHT TOWNS ARE NETHERBY, TOLFORT, HALSE CROSS, ANSTERWICK, KIRKMOSS, ELLERSBY, MARLEBY AND OCKBROOK, AND NOT ONE PERSON IN THIS BOROUGH COULD TELL YOU WHERE ANY OF THEM IS.**
