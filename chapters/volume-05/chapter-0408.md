# Chapter 0408 — A Dark Patch

Tuesday the first of May 2018, starting at about half past eight in the morning and running until about half past five in the afternoon, a first-floor flat in a block off a side street, in Tideglass.

**A MAN IN HIS SIXTIES OPENED A BAG THAT HAD BEEN ON THE TOP OF THAT CUPBOARD SINCE ABOUT 2014 AND SAW A HARD DARK PLACE IN THE BOTTOM OF IT AND DECIDED IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS THAT IT WAS NOTHING, AND ANOTHER MAN HAD ALREADY PUT HIS THUMB IN THAT SAME PLACE ON THAT MORNING AND SAID NOTHING ABOUT IT TO ANYBODY, AND NEITHER OF THEM WILL EVER MENTION IT AGAIN.**

The block is four flats and a passage and it is on a side street and it is about sixty years old and the stair goes up off that passage, and there is a bulb in a shade screwed to the wall at the turn, and that bulb has not been out since it went in.

The man in his sixties has the ground floor and has had it since about 2009. The first floor was let again in March and the people in it are in it now.

The room this happened in is the front room up there. It is about thirteen foot by ten and it has one window to the front and a fireplace that has been out of use since the nineteen-fifties, and against the left-hand wall there is a floor-to-ceiling cupboard with sliding doors, one of which does not run, and above the top of that cupboard on the wall there is a shelf about four foot long and about eight inch deep screwed to the wall.

On that shelf there is a canvas toolbag, and about two foot of a spirit level with the vial cover gone, and a coffee jar with about two inch of screws in it and the lid off, and a roll of lino with the end turned over on itself, and that has been the top of that cupboard for about four years.

---

He was in that flat on the morning of the first of May between about half past eight and about half past ten, doing nine foot of pipe and a new stopcock on the kitchen sink, and he was let in by a woman who lives in that flat, who went out at about nine and left him the key on the mat and took it back off the mat when she came in at about ten.

The man in his sixties was in the room with him for about twenty minutes of that, because that is where the man in his sixties spends the morning when he is in his own front room, and he sits by the fireplace with the window in front of him.

The toolbag was in the way of the lino roll and he had to move it to get at the roll, and he put it down on the boards with the bottom of it on the boards and the top of it against the skirting, and the bottom of that bag is the part that gets put down.

He put his thumb in it without meaning to, the way you do.

It is a canvas bag about eighteen inch long and about ten inch deep and about ten inch high, with a canvas flap and two buckles, one of which works. The bottom of it is a piece of hide, and the hide has gone stiff and dark and it is not stiff all over it.

There is a place in the bottom of that bag about the size of his two hands laid together, and it is darker than the hide around it, and it is not a colour, and the edge of it is crisp. It goes right through the hide to the canvas underneath and it has come up through the hide and dried there, and the hide is hard about a sixteenth of an inch thick in the middle of it and about nothing at the edges of it.

He put his thumb in the edge of it. It was soft at that edge, and where it was soft it went into a little way and came away and left the tip of his thumb grey.

That is about nine o'clock in the morning.

He did not say anything about it. The man in his sixties was about four foot off with his cup and did not look up, and there is no reason on earth why he would have, and a man does not open a bag in a flat he is working in and say to a man he has known for six years that there is a soft place in the bottom of it.

He put the bag back up on that shelf with the stiff place against the wall, which is the way it had been, and he got on with the sink.

He was out of that flat at about ten and he has not been back in it since and is not going to, and nobody in this borough knows that he ever looked at the bottom of a bag in it.

The man in his forties was in that flat at about two in the afternoon, for a different job, and he was let in by the same woman on the same key.

He had a length of two-inch oak in the van for a stair nose on the first flight of that stair, and about half an hour of work, and he had been in that block about four times since January and the man in his forties has a key to the passage door of that block and has had it for about six years.

The woman who lives in that flat said, when he got there, that he would want to be let in after, because the man downstairs is in and would let himself in through the passage anyway, and he said he would not be long.

He was in the front room of that first-floor flat with the door open for about twenty minutes. The man in his sixties was in his own front room at the bottom of that stair and came up at about ten past two because the man in his forties had a drill going and he wanted to know what it was.

He came in and stood in the doorway for about two minutes and they talked about the stair nose, and the man in his forties said he would take the bottom tread off on the Monday and get it back on the same day, and the man in his sixties said that was all right and went back down.

And the man in his forties was in that room and had his back to that shelf the whole time he was talking, and did not look at it until the man in his sixties had gone back down the stair.

He needed a screwdriver.

He has about nine of them in his own bag and every one of them is at the top of that block in his own van in a job in Bleam Street that runs to the Wednesday, and he had been given that job on the Monday and had brought the bag up and put it on the boards and there is nothing in it but the bits and a pair of snips and a pencil.

He said, "Have you got a screwdriver I can borrow for ten minutes."

The man in his sixties said, "There's one somewhere," and got up out of where he was sitting and went to the top of that cupboard and put the jar of screws and that roll on the landing and put his hand out for the toolbag.

He had not opened that bag since he gave the trade up. He came out of that trade at about sixty and it was given to him in about 2014 by a man who stopped working, and it has been on that shelf since, and there is nothing in it that he would want now and he has never in four years gone to it for anything.

He put it on the landing on its side and undid the buckle that works and turned the flap back.

The whole inside of that bag is stiff. There is a chisel in it with a handle that has come off and been put back, and there is a lump hammer in it, and there is about six inches of cold chisel, and the bottom of the bag is the hide.

He saw it with the flap open, from above, with the man in his forties three foot away looking at a piece of oak.

The dark place in the bottom of that bag is about the size of a hardback book. It goes right through the hide. The sides of that bag have been pressed in hard against it, and the line they have made runs straight for a good deal further than the place itself is wide, and the canvas along it is paler than the canvas either side of it, in the way canvas goes when it has been folded hard in one place a great many times.

He looked at it for about four seconds.

There is a wood stove in that room and there has been one in that room since about 1988, and that is what he decided, and he decided it in about four seconds and he is not a man who goes over things. Grease off thirty years of work, and a wet rag, and a stove close enough to touch, and that is all a man like him was ever going to come up with.

He never laid a finger on it, and that is still the position. He put his hand on the canvas at the near end of that bag, and he took the screwdriver out, and he shut the flap and did the buckle up.

Then he put the jar of screws and the roll back, and put that bag on that shelf with the stiff place against the wall, and went back down the stair and handed the screwdriver back through the door of his own front room and said, "There you are," and went and did the tread.

He has not mentioned that bag to anybody since and does not intend to, and the whole of what anybody would get out of him about it is that a bag is a bag.

---

**HE PUT IT BACK ON THAT SHELF WITH THE HARD PLACE AGAINST THE WALL AND TOOK THE SCREWDRIVER OUT OF IT, AND HE DID NOT TOUCH THE PLACE, AND THE OTHER MAN TOUCHED IT AT NINE IN THE MORNING AND SAID NOTHING, AND NEITHER OF THEM WILL RAISE IT AND ONE OF THEM HAS DECIDED IT IS NOTHING.**
