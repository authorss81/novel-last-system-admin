# Chapter 0592 — Sixty-One

The Tuesday of the eighth of September 2020, from about ten in the morning until about four in the afternoon, at a house on an estate in this borough.

**THEY HAVE BOTH HAD THE YEAR ON THAT HOUSE OUT SINCE THEY BOUGHT IT IN 2016 AND ONE OF THEM SAYS THE YEAR IT WAS ACTUALLY DONE OUT LOUD, AND IT IS THE ONLY THING EITHER OF THEM HAS SAID THAT COULD HAVE BEEN SAID AT ANY POINT SINCE 2016, AND NEITHER OF THEM DOES ONE SINGLE THING DIFFERENT AS A RESULT.**

They are a man in his early fifties and a woman in his early fifties and they have been married for twenty-six years and they bought the end house on that turning in 2016 and they are both tired of each other's company in the particular way of people who are tired of each other and are not going to say so.

There is a problem with that house that neither of them can name and has never been able to name and they have both had theories about and have compared the theories in the car and in the kitchen and have got nowhere.

---

The sentence is said at about ten in the morning, standing in the kitchen, because he has come in from the garage with a bit of paper in his hand.

It is the completion statement for the house. It came with the deeds and they have both looked at it and neither of them can now find it. It has the year on it in the middle of the second page, and the year on it is not the year the two of them have been telling people.

He says, "They built this in 1961, not 1974."

And then he waits, because he has been waiting since the Friday, and there is nothing else he wants, and she is the only other person in England who is allowed to have the list.

---

Here is what has been going on in that house since 2016, and it is one thing and it is small, and it is the only thing the two of them have ever argued about.

The house does not heat properly. That is all it is. It is not that nothing heats. It is that the heat in it goes somewhere, and the two of them have worked out four explanations for where it goes, and the four cannot all hold, and they have known that since about the November they bought it, and they have gone round the houses about it ever since.

He thinks the cavity has been filled. She thinks the boiler is at the far end. He thinks it is the doors. She thinks it is that nobody has ever turned the pump up.

They have argued about it gently, in a car, in a kitchen, in a shop, in the way of two people who are not really arguing. They have never once rung anybody. The reason they have never once rung anybody is a sentence that was said in 2016 by the man in his early fifties about that house, and this is it:

"You don't want to be told. You want it to be you and something in it."

Which was fair, and which she did not enjoy, and which is the reason the two of them have spent years going round a thing together instead of paying anybody to come and look at it.

And here is what happened this morning, and it is a very small thing.

The year is 1961.

Why the year matters is this, and it is the only reason the sentence has any weight at all, and neither of them notices it happen.

This house was built in 1961. That is before the rules about gas came in and about twenty-five years before anything anybody in this borough would recognise, and it means that whatever is going on with the heat in that house is not something anybody has done, and is nobody's doing, and is not going to be anybody's doing in a year's time.

The man in his early fifties stands at the kitchen table with the paper in his hand. He has had the year out since 2016. He has told people the year. There is a woman in his early fifties's sister who said "sixty-one?" about that house at a birthday and he said "seventy-four" and she wrote 1974 on a card, and he has never mentioned it since.

And here is what neither of them does with 1961, and this is the entire content of this chapter.

The man in his early fifties does not point out that she has had the year out too, because she has, and he does not leave it out of kindness. He leaves it alone because if he says it, she will ask him how long he has known that she had the year out, and that is a road he does not want to go down in a kitchen at ten in the morning on a Tuesday.

The woman in his early fifties does not point out that he has had the year out, for the same reason, from the other side.

They do not ring anybody. A year is the sort of thing that makes a person ring somebody. It is in her head within about an hour and it stays there and it does not come out.

They have lunch.

At about half past two the man in his early fifties puts the paper on the side by the telephone, under a list that is not his, and goes out to the garage and comes back in at about half past three with nothing he has done written down anywhere.

At about four the woman in his early fifties says she is going to the shop, and puts her coat on, and says, "It'll be dark in an hour."

And that is the whole of what 1961 did to that house on that day. Here is what it did not do. It did not stop the heat going somewhere. It did not put the cavity idea away. She is still going to say the pump is set where it should not be in about a fortnight and he is still going to say the doors. They have four explanations and they are going to keep all four of them as long as they live, and the year is not going to shift one of them.

It is the start of September and that paper is on the side by the telephone under somebody else's list, and nobody has looked at it since about half past two.

**THEY HAD BOTH HAD THE YEAR OUT SINCE 2016 AND ONE OF THEM SAID IT OUT LOUD AND THE OTHER ONE HAD IT OUT AS WELL AND SAID NOTHING, AND NEITHER OF THEM RANG ANYBODY, AND ALL FOUR EXPLANATIONS FOR THE HEAT IN THAT HOUSE ARE STILL ON THE TABLE, AND ONE OF THEM IS PROBABLY ALMOST HOLDING.**
