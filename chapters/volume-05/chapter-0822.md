# Chapter 0822 — A Length Set By A Fence

Thursday the twenty-second of June 2023, at the front of her own house at number nine, out on the kerb and in her garden from the middle of the morning until there is no light on the front of that house any more, and a limb off the plane tree that has lain across her fence since the winter is the whole of that Thursday.

**A LIMB OFF THE PLANE TREE ON THE FAR SIDE OF THE ROAD AT NUMBER NINE HAS LAIN ACROSS HER FRONT FENCE SINCE THE WINTER, HALF OF IT IN THE STREET AND HALF OF IT IN HER GARDEN, AND SHE MEANS TO CUT IT UP, AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING COMES ALONG THE FRONT IN THE MORNING, STOPS AT THE KERB TO LOOK AT IT, AND GOES ON.**

That limb came down in a storm before Christmas and it has been lying across the front fence of number nine ever since, and it is about seven feet long from the break to the end and five inches through where it broke off.

The fence is close boarding four feet high. The limb lies about two feet above the top of it, which is where it slid to and stopped, and about a foot of it hangs out over the kerb on the road side and the other six feet is over her garden.

Nothing has been done about it since it came down and there are two reasons, and only one of them is anybody's fault.

The first is that the tree is not hers.

It is on the far side of the road, in a garden that belongs to a house she has never seen the front of and never been inside and would not know the name of the person in. A plane of that size in that road is older than either of those houses and there are four of them on that stretch and she cannot tell from the pavement which of the four gardens it is standing in.

The second is that the limb is over her fence and not over her garden, and that makes it a different job entirely.

A limb that has come down into a garden is a limb somebody will come and take away, and there will be a man with a saw and a ladder and a truck and it will be gone in an hour.

A limb lying on top of a fence on a pavement is not anybody's job, and everybody who walks past it can see it and everybody who walks past it walks past it, and it will be there in June as it was in January.

---

She has a folding pruning saw with a blade about eighteen inches long, and she has a pair of gloves in the pocket of her coat, and that is what she has brought out to the front of that house on that Thursday.

The first thing she does is walk out into the road and look at it from the far side, which is the side everybody else sees it from.

From there it is about a foot above the pavement and it goes back over the fence and there is no way to see where it goes.

The second thing she does is measure it, without anything, by walking along it.

The rule for a length is not in the limb. That is the thing she works out over about twenty minutes on the pavement outside number nine with the saw folded in her hand.

A length has to go over that fence from where she is standing.

She is on the pavement. She cannot get under the limb, because it is a foot above her shoulder at the kerb and there is no way she is going to put two feet on the road in June. So the only thing she can do with a length is push it up and over, and for a thing to go over four feet of close boarding on the push it has to be light at the end and not much longer than about four feet, and it has to go up at an angle rather than straight up, and it has to be something she can control with both hands on the end of it.

Every one of those things comes from the fence.

Not from the limb. The limb is what it is and it does not care.

---

She starts at the break.

That is the hard end and it is where a foldingsaw is no use at all, because the break is a splinter of plane about two feet long where the limb came off the trunk, and it is a mess of torn wood and it is exactly where a foldingsaw cannot reach and where nobody should be putting a hand.

She has an ordinary bow saw in the shed for wood that is bigger than her hands, and she gets it, and she gets a pair of gloves out of the coat pocket because plane bark is grey and dusty and it comes off in your hands and stays in them.

The cut at the break is about three inches through and it is not across the limb. That is the thing about cutting a limb up on the ground.

A limb is strongest along the grain. Cut across the grain and the saw has to break the fibres in front of it and the kerf closes on the blade and the blade snaps. Cut on the side grain, a quarter turn round, and the saw has nothing to break in front of it and it goes through.

So the cut at the break is made from the side, with the saw at about ninety degrees to the length of the limb, and it takes a good few minutes, and the dust comes down off the bark and settles on the pavement, and she wipes the kerf out with the back of a glove when the saw begins to bind.

That first length comes off about four feet long, which is the length the fence has decided, and it is heavy enough that she nearly drops it on the way up.

Getting a length over that fence from the kerb is a thing that has to be worked out in the street in daylight with traffic.

It goes up on its end, because that is the only way four feet of plane will go over four feet of fence, and it stops with its top against the top board of the fence and its bottom end sticking out over the pavement at about the height of her chest.

That is the moment the whole of the job is in.

You cannot get a hand under it. You cannot put a shoulder to it. You have got the end of it in your hands and it is a foot above the fence top and the fence is in the way of your feet and the only thing left is to push.

She gets the top of it over.

It goes over the fence about three feet from where it was lying and the far end comes down inside her garden on the bed and she has to let go of it before she can get to it because she cannot get over the fence either and she is not going to try.

The first length goes down about a foot into her border and stays there.

Four more.

She does them the same way and it takes her about an hour, and about halfway through the second one a car comes past and the branch it brought with it touches her back, and she stands still with her foot on the cut-off end until it has gone.

The woman who does her shopping comes along the front of that house about the middle of that Thursday morning.

She stops at the kerb opposite, on the far side of the road, and looks across at the limb and the fence and the woman standing on the pavement in a coat with a saw.

"You want a hand?" she calls across.

"There is not much of it left to do," she tells her. "It is a job of about another half hour and I can do it standing up."

And the woman who does her shopping goes on up the road.

The last length is the end of the limb and it is the awkward one, because it is thin and it is nine feet from the break and she has not cut it yet and it is lying over the fence at the far end, past the point where the fence runs into the hedge side of the house.

Where it crosses the fence it is about two inches through, and everything about the length of it has to be decided by the same thing as every other length.

She measures it off with her arm. Four feet is as long as she can push over that fence from the pavement, and the end of the limb is out over the garden by about five feet, so there is one more length and it is going to be about four feet and it is going to be the one that decides how the last of that limb is got out of her garden.

She cuts it with the folding saw, sitting on the kerb with the branch propped against her knee and the saw going into the side grain about a foot from the end.

The end of a limb is the worst of it for cutting on the ground, because the end is where the branch was youngest and it is full of twigs, and a plane makes a litter of them and a foldingsaw goes round every one.

It takes about a quarter of an hour and she does it in three goes because the saw binds twice and she has to get a different grip on it.

The street side is finished at about half past four by her own reckoning and she looks at it from the far side of the road.

The limb is not in the road.

That is the only thing that had to happen to the road side and it has happened. There is a place on the pavement outside number nine where about a foot and a half of a limb used to be over the kerb, and there is nothing on the pavement and nothing over the fence and nothing hanging out over the road.

The rest of it is in her garden and there are six lengths of it lying about on the grass and the border.

The last one will not go over that fence from the pavement, because it is on the wrong side and it has to come out of the garden rather than go into it, and there is a way in at the front of that house between the open gate and the edge of the path about a foot wide, and four feet of plane goes through a foot of gap on its side.

She puts it through on its side and drags it along the path past the front step, where a spanner is still lying on the flags where a man dropped it, and along the front path and out into the grass.

Six lengths of a plane limb are lying in the front garden of number nine at the end of that Thursday, and they are not going anywhere, because privet does not rot and neither does this, and nobody collects.

The street is clear. That was the whole of what she went out there for, and it took her about four hours and one folding saw and a bow saw out of the shed and nobody was asked for any of it.
