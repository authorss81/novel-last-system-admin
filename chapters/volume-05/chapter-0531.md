# Chapter 0531 — The Third Man on the Green

The Sunday of the twenty-fourth of November 2019, and the whole of the afternoon, on a green and on the path at the end of it, in this borough.

**CROQUET ON A GREEN THAT BELONGS TO A CLUB WHICH IS SHUT FOR THE SEASON, EVERY SUNDAY SINCE THE MARCH OF 2013, AND ABOUT NINE SENTENCES BETWEEN THEM IN ALL OF THAT, AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF NOVEMBER A THIRD MAN CAME ROUND THE SIDE OF THE HUT AT A QUARTER PAST TWO, AND NEITHER OF THE OTHER TWO SAID ANYTHING ABOUT IT.**

The older of the two is a man of about sixty-one. The younger is a man of about fifty-one. They met in a queue in a shop in the town in 2011 and have played each other on a Sunday since the March of 2013, and neither of them has at any time had a reason you could put in a sentence.

The green belongs to a club that is shut for the season. That is the whole of why the arrangement exists and the reason it has lasted. There is a hut at the top end, and the two of them get in over the ditch along the side, where it has gone down to about the height of a man's waist in the last six years, and they have been getting in that way since 2013 and neither of them has ever mentioned it to the caretaker. The mallets are the club's. They are stacked on their sides along the inside of the hut and have been since the September, and the caretaker has not asked about them once.

They have a jug of water and a third hoop that does not stand true, and a peg that is bent, and a plastic marker that the younger man lost in 2016 and has never offered to replace. They have never once written a score down, and neither of them could tell you what the score is if you asked, and if you did ask, the answer would be that nobody had been keeping it, and that would be true.

The nine sentences are worth listing because they are the entire content of six years. Two of them were about the weather. One was about a horse. One was somebody's mother's leg. One was a question about whether a thing they had both seen was worth reporting. Four were the word right, said by one of them and received by the other.

What they have mostly done is play. Two games, then three if it was not raining, then the half-hour where they stand about and wait for it to get dark, and then they go up the path together to where their own cars are and say nothing.

---

The younger man got there at about ten past two and walked up the side of the green ahead of the other one, and at about a quarter past two, when the three of them were standing at the top end, a man the younger one works with came round the side of the hut.

He is a man of about forty-one. He works with the younger man and he has known him since the middle of 2017, and nobody in this borough knows a thing more about him than that. He had a pair of shoes on that were wrong for the grass and he said so himself before he had got his hands on a mallet.

He was at the fourth peg with his hands in his pockets, and he worked out inside about four seconds what had happened, and he worked out the rest of it over the following hour and a quarter without putting one word of it anywhere.

There were, he worked it out afterwards, a couple of things a man could do when he finds out. He could say so, and then he has to go on turning up, and he has to say so once a week for a year. Or he could say nothing, and then he has to work out what it means that the other one said nothing, and there is only one way to work that out, and the way it works out is the one that has happened.

The younger man said nothing. He handed the mallets across and said the hoop was still crooked and he said something about the shoes, and he did not say why there were three of them, and there was no moment in the whole afternoon when he nearly said it, and that is the part the older man could not get past.

Then they started.

Croquet for three is croquet for a pair with a rotation, and they settled it in about four minutes without discussing it, which in the first year took about six games to work out. Whoever was in had the game. The one who was out walked round the green with his hands behind his back and said nothing about it, in the manner of a man watching a match, and that is what the first half hour was.

The third man is not good. He is not bad enough to be laughed at and not good enough to be waited for, and he apologised twice for a stroke that was not his fault and hit his own ball on the wrong side of a hoop twice, and each time he said sorry, and both times the older man said nothing at all and the younger man said it was all right.

At about half past two the game finished and the third man won it, and the older of the two had not hit a good ball all afternoon.

**HE WON THE FIRST GAME IN TWENTY MINUTES AND SAID SORRY TWICE INSIDE IT, AND AFTERWARDS THE MAN WHO BROUGHT HIM RESET THE HOOPS AND WOULD NOT LET ANYBODY SAY WHO HAD HAD THE BEST OF IT, AND THE MAN WHO HAD BEEN COMING SINCE 2013 WALKED ROUND THE GREEN FOR THE WHOLE OF THE SECOND GAME WITH HIS HANDS BEHIND HIS BACK.**

They played a second and then, because it had not got dark, a third. In the second the older man was in with the younger one and the third man sat out, and he went round the edge twice and then stood at the top end for the rest of it, and the older man hit two of the hardest and worst shots of his life and made one of them, and the third man said "Oh" from the side, and then said "Oh, that's gone," and meant it kindly.

At about ten past three the younger man said he had a ring to make, did not make it, and left the mallets stacked against the outside of the hut rather than back in along the inside of it, which is what he always does, and the older man watched him do that and understood exactly what it was and said nothing about that either.

At about twenty-five to four the older man put his mallet down in the grass.

"I've got to be going," he said.

"You've not long to go," the third man said, and looked at the sky, and then said, "It's turned."

"I'll go up in a minute," the older man said.

He went at about twenty-five to four, which is an hour and a quarter from the time the three of them were standing at the top end, and it is not a round figure, and he has not said out loud to anybody, and did not say it to himself either, that he had counted.

The third man walked with him down as far as the second path and then stopped and waited, which is what you do. The younger man was at the side of the hut with the mallets on the grass at his feet.

"How long have you two been at this?" the third man said, at the second path, with his mallet under his arm.

"Since 2013," the older man said. "Six years, near."

"I'd have thought it was newer than that," the third man said. "You two don't say much."

"No," the older man said.

And that was all of it. The third man said nothing else about it, because he had no reason to, and he has not said anything about it since, and he does not know that he was brought here to be looked at, and nobody in this borough is going to tell him.

The third man went up the path at twenty to four and said good night to the man who had brought him and put a hand up to the other one as he passed, and the other one put his own hand up about half a second late and did not find out until the following day which of the pair it had been for.

The younger man pushed the mallets back in along the inside of the hut where they live and was still standing there at four o'clock when the older man came back up the path, and not one word about any part of the afternoon passed between the two of them, and the older man drove home the long way, and not one word about any part of the afternoon passed between the two of them, and the younger man stayed another twenty minutes on a green that belonged to a club that was shut.

**THE THIRD MAN PUT A HAND UP TO A MAN WHO DID NOT KNOW WHICH OF THE PAIR IT WAS FOR, AND THE MAN WHO BROUGHT HIM WENT UP THE PATH AFTERWARDS AND STOOD WITH HIS HANDS IN HIS POCKETS FOR TWENTY MINUTES ON A GREEN THAT BELONGS TO SOMEBODY ELSE, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO WHO HAVE BEEN COMING SINCE 2013 HAS ASKED THE OTHER WHY, AND NEITHER OF THEM EVER WILL.**
