# Chapter 0263 — A Statement

Monday the twelfth of September 2016, from about ten past nine in the morning, the first floor of a building by the water in Tideglass, and a records room on the same floor.

**A CORRECT STATEMENT CAME BACK FOR WORK THAT WAS DONE IN THIS BOROUGH BY A CREW FROM THIS BOROUGH AND WAS NEVER ORDERED BY ANYBODY IN THIS BOROUGH, AND IT WENT IN A BAG WITH THE OTHERS, AND THERE IS NOBODY IN THIS BOROUGH WHO CAN ANSWER IT, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAPPENED.**

It was on the sill in the corridor at about twenty to nine when she came in, and it had been on the sill since the Saturday, and the corridor is about twenty feet and has a window at one end and three doors and a fire door, and the sill is where post goes in that building when nobody is on that floor.

Nell Ardent, who is thirty-nine and copies things out in her own hand, picked it up and read it standing up, and then went and got her bag out of the office and put it on the table, and did not sit down.

It is one page. There is a heading, a reference of the kind they all have, a date in June, and one line of work, and a sum.

*Peverell Street — day gang, one gang, one day, gully and kerb, gang hours as booked.*

The sum was eighty-eight pounds.

On the same line, after the work, there is a booking reference. It is a job number off the yard at Penhale and it is four digits and it is not a number that anybody in this building has ever seen before, and it belongs to work that went out of this borough over the water in June and came back on the Friday.

"So it isn't ours," said Nell Ardent.

Jonas Mercer was in the corridor with a piece of paper in his coat and a biro and nowhere to put it, and he said, "There's a gang day on it," and then nothing else, and he is not on anything and has not been asked anything by anybody in this borough since a man behind a till asked him the price of a bend in July.

"It isn't ours," she said again, to the page. "That's the bit. There's a day gang of ours stood on our own street doing our own gully and kerb, and the hours went on the ticket of a job that is eight miles and an estuary away, and nobody in this borough asked them to do it and nobody in this borough put it there. The gang didn't put it there themselves either, because they'd have had to know a job number, and a gang doesn't know a job number."

Somebody had to say the other half of it, and it was said at about a quarter to ten by a woman with her hands in the pockets of a cardigan on a cold corridor, and it was said once and not elaborated on:

"And that is exactly the same as every other one of them."

---

Nina Calder, who is a records supervisor and has been one for nineteen years, read it at her desk with her reading glasses pushed up into her hair, and read it a second time, and put it down squared off.

"Right," she said. "First things, and they're all of them good news, and then there's one thing in it and I'll come to it. It is correct. Every field on it is correct and the arithmetic is correct and the rate on it is the rate that has been the rate since about 1987 and it is not going to be wrong. It is not a bill. It is not a demand. It is not a form — look at it, there is no box on it anywhere for anybody to sign, and nobody has to reply to it, and it will not chase. It is a statement, and a statement is a thing you put in a bag on a shelf."

"Then why have they sent it," said Nell Ardent.

"Because that's the process and it's the same process as the rest of them and it's a good process," said Nina Calder. "It goes into a pool and it gets paid out of the pool by people in a town over the water who will never know it existed, and the day it is paid it will stop existing, and it is a beautifully made piece of paper for a job that has actually been done and actually cost money. Whoever made that has done everything right."

"Then the one thing."

"Then the one thing is the middle of the page," said Nina Calder, "and it isn't a complaint, and I am not going to pretend to you that I can do anything about it. There is a line that says Peverell Street, and there is nothing anywhere on that page that says who asked for it. And I am the person in this building who would be able to tell you whether that street ordered a day gang this borough, and I can tell you that this borough's people do not order day gangs, because nobody in this borough has ever ordered anything of anybody, and that is on the record here as much as it is anywhere. So it wasn't ordered here, and the page says it was done here, and both of those are true, and there is nobody in this building to reconcile them and there is nobody in this town to reconcile them either, and I have been in this job for nineteen years and there is a great deal of paper on the desks in front of me and every bit of it is correct."

Nell Ardent stood there.

"Do you want to know the honest thing," said Nina Calder. "You're going to put it in the bag, and if you put it in the bag with the rest of them and go home, then you will have done the only available thing, and it is the correct thing, and there is no version of doing it better."

"There isn't," said Nell Ardent.

"I know there isn't," said Nina Calder, and put her glasses down on the desk, and pulled a book towards her, and did not say anything else about it, because there was a great deal else to do and because that had been the arrangement for nineteen years.

---

Nell Ardent went back out into the corridor and put the statement into the bag with the others, at the bottom, under the ones that had come out of a trade counter in a carrier bag in March.

She did not add it up. She stood in the corridor with the string pulled on the bag and she did not add it up, because eleven claims and one thousand eight hundred and twelve pounds since the spring is a thing a person can carry, and the reason a person can carry it is that it is small, and the moment somebody adds the twelfth one in they have started a sum, and a sum wants an answer, and there is nobody in this borough to give it.

She put the bag on the shelf behind the door where it had been since the August before last, and the string went round twice, and it was a bag on a shelf in a corridor in a building by the water, and it was not a file, and it was not a folder, and it had nobody's name on it and no date on it and nothing on the outside of it at all.

**AND A MAN CAME UP THE STAIRS AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN WITH A BUDGET FOR A GLAZIER IN IT AND WENT DOWN AGAIN WITHOUT KNOWING THAT ANYTHING HAD HAPPENED IN THAT CORRIDOR, AND THE FIRE DOOR WAS OPEN FOUR INCHES BECAUSE IT HAD BEEN OPEN SINCE HALF NINE, AND THE STRING WAS ROUND THE BAG TWICE AND THE BAG WAS ON THE SHELF BEHIND THE DOOR WHERE IT HAD BEEN SINCE THE AUGUST BEFORE LAST.**
