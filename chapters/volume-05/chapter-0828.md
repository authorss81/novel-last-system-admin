# Chapter 0828 — Eleven Boards And A Pair Of Bands

Saturday the fifteenth of July 2023, out on that frontage from the middle of the morning until the shadow of that office cabin is across the whole of it, and eleven boards that are going with the frontage is the whole of that Saturday.

**ELEVEN BOARDS ARE DOWN ALONG THE FRONTAGE AT THE UNIT AND HE MEANS TO TAKE THEM UP AND PUT THEM ON EDGE AGAINST A WALL, AND THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN IS AT THAT FRONTAGE FOR A QUARTER OF AN HOUR IN THE MIDDLE OF THE MORNING BECAUSE THE BOARDS ARE IN HER WAY.**

That frontage is scaffold boards laid down the front of the yard to walk on, eleven of them end to end, grey with use, with sand under them and weeds through the joints.

He put most of them down himself in June with another pair of hands in the middle of a run, two people to a board at the middle joint where the eight inches is over two steel bands underneath. The bands were there already, flat on the sand under the joint, and they went back under when the boards went down.

He starts at the near end with gloves and a bar, lifting each board at the end and walking it back to the wall.

The first three come up clean. Sand under them, dry, with the print of the board in it and nothing else. The fourth has a nail through it into a sleeper and he drives it out from below and lays it aside.

At the middle joint he finds the bands.

They are steel bands about two inches wide, dark with oil, banded at both ends round the eight inches where the two boards meet over them, tight, with the ends folded under. They were put there to tie the joint, and they have been under there since June, out of sight, with the boards on top of them.

He sits back on his heels and looks at them.

He does not know what they are. They are not part of the boards and they are not part of the sand, and they do not belong to the yard. They look like packing off something heavy, cut off and laid flat to keep the joint from rocking, and whoever put them there knew what he was doing and left no sign of why.

That is the stop, and it comes in the middle of the morning with his hands on the fourth board.

Whether he opens them. A banded joint asks to be cut to see what is under it, and one left shut asks to stay shut. He runs a thumb along the edge of one band and feels the fold under it, tight as a drum, with grit in the fold that has been there for weeks.

He does not open them. He slides the bar under the board above the bands and lifts it clear, and lays the bands back flat on the sand where they were, in the same place, with the folds under. What was put down by two people in the middle of a run stays as it was put down, and his work goes round it and not through it.

The woman from two doors down is at that frontage for about a quarter of an hour in the middle of the morning because the boards are in her way along the path. She stands clear by the cabin while he carries, and she does not touch a board and does not ask about them, and she does not look at them twice.

“I can get round by the wall,” she tells him.

“Give me a minute and the way will be clear,” he tells her. She waits until the way is clear and then she is gone up the path.

---

The rest of the day is lifting and carrying and laying on edge.

Eleven boards go up against the wall at the back of the yard, on edge, with battens under them out of the damp, in the order they came up, the short joint in the middle where the bands were kept together.

He sweeps the sand where they were into the hollows and leaves the ground level, and he coils the string he used to tie the stack and hangs it on a nail.

When the shadow of the office cabin is across the whole of the frontage he stands at the near end and looks down it to the wall. Eleven boards are on edge against the wall in a stack, and out of the middle of them one pair of steel bands is lying on top, uncut and unopened, with sand still in the folds.
