# Chapter 0462 — The Bottom of the Run

Monday the twenty-fourth of December 2018, from about half past eight in the morning until about half past ten. One building and the wall at the side of it, in Tideglass.

**A MAN TOLD A COMPANY THAT A LENGTH OF WALL WAS FINISHED AND LEFT EIGHTEEN INCH OF THE BOTTOM OF IT, AND HE HAS NOT GONE BACK, AND THE WOMAN WHO LIVES IN THAT BUILDING HAS SEEN IT AND HAS NOT ASKED HIM ANYTHING.**

A man in his forties does building work and odd jobs and he was on that building for three days in the middle of December, the last of them being the Friday of the twenty-first, at the end of a long job that a company had been in for about eight months, and the thing he was doing at the end of it was a length of wall about nine foot at the side of that building, where the render had come away and the brick behind it had started to go.

He took the loose render off. He cleaned the brick. He did a length of about nine foot of repointing in a proper lime mortar over three days, and it is the best piece of work anybody in that street has seen done on a building that is not being looked after.

And about the bottom eighteen inch of that nine foot, he did not do, and he told the man from the company it was done.

---

Here is the shape of the job and the reason nobody would have known.

The wall is at the side of a building where there is a passage between that building and the next one, and the passage is about four foot wide, and it goes from that street to a yard at the back. The render had come off nine foot of it and the brick behind had gone soft at the bottom about two courses in from the ground, in a line about eighteen inch long, where a pipe runs down inside the wall and has been wetting the bottom of that wall since long before the company was in that building.

To repoint the bottom of that run, you have to lift that pipe off the wall. It is the full height of the building inside that wall and there is no other way to get to the bottom eighteen inch without taking about four foot of it off a wall in a four foot passage, and putting it back, and being certain it is back where it was.

He did not do it. He is a man who has taken pipe off a wall about thirty times and he is not frightened of any of it.

He told the man from the company on the Friday.

He was on a ladder at the top of that run at about five to twelve and the man was on the ground with the sheets down, and he said, "That's away."

The man from the company wrote it on a docket. He was in that building for about four minutes that morning, and that four minutes is the whole of what that building is to him.

The company invoiced it. It got paid. Nobody came back.

On the Monday of the twenty-fourth she was on that passage at about half past nine in the morning with her own list, and she looked at that wall the way anybody looks at a wall, which is for about two seconds, and then she looked at it for about ten seconds.

The bottom eighteen inch is a different colour from the rest. The old mortar in it is grey and the new mortar above it is a pale buff, and where the two meet there is a straight line about eighteen inch long, and above that line the wall is sound.

It is not a bad piece of work. It is a piece of work with about eighteen inch of the bottom of it left, and it is the kind of left that you only see if you have been in that passage before, and everybody who goes down that passage is going through it in about a second and a half.

She has been down that passage about twice a week for about nine years. She has noticed it. She has not said anything.

She has not rung him.

That is not a decision she made. She went back up to the flat and she stood in her own front room for a while, and she worked out that she could telephone him in about a minute and that he would come and that it would take about a day and that it would cost about thirty pounds, and she found that she was not going to do that, and she has been through why twice since and got the same answer both times.

What she would have to ask is not "why did you not do the bottom of it." What she would have to ask is "does that pipe want doing," and that is a different question, and the answer to it might be yes, and if the answer is yes then the man in his forties is a man who looked at a length of wall and left a length of it, and she will not be the one who starts that in a passage between two buildings on a Monday morning in December.

And she is not going to raise it with a man she has had about four jobs done for over two years, because she cannot think of one way of raising it that leaves it as a fact about a wall and nothing else.

He has not gone back either.

He was there on the Friday morning and he said it was away and he was on that ladder and he looked at that bottom eighteen inch before he came down, and it is not a thing a man forgets. He put his hand on it with the trowel still in the other one.

He has had three days. Christmas is tomorrow and he has not spent any of it on that wall. He has thought about going back with the pipe off and the same mortar about nine times, and the reason he has not is not that the pipe is difficult.

---

The reason is at the top of that run, and he will not say it, and this is the chapter.

There is a length of about four foot of that wall, about five foot up, on the far side of the run from the brick he repointed, where the render is off and the brick behind it is soft along a joint, and it is above head height and it is not going to fall on anybody and it has been like that for years.

Every one of the men who have been on that building in eight months has looked at that four foot. He has watched three of them look at it. It is the thing that is wrong with that wall and it is the only thing on that wall that is actually wrong, and it is a job that wants doing properly, which means a scaffold, which means a fortnight, which means a price, which means somebody has to decide.

And there is nobody in that building to decide it. The company are in and out. The man from the company who wrote down that it was away is a man with a docket and about four minutes in that building.

If he had said anything on the Friday — anything at all about the bottom eighteen inch, or the pipe, or the four foot at the top — then somebody in that company would have had to write a second thing on a second docket, and that second thing would have become a job, and that job would have had to be priced, and the pricing of it is the point at which this stops being a length of wall that nobody has looked at and becomes a thing on a list belonging to people who are not in the borough.

He said it was away and the bottom of it stayed as it is, and there is a wall on that side of a building with nine foot of it done to a standard nobody is going to inspect and about eighteen inch of the bottom of it left, and a man standing in a four foot passage on a Monday morning in December can see those eighteen inch in ten seconds if he knows to look.

**HE CAME DOWN THE LADDER AT FIVE TO TWELVE ON THE FRIDAY OF THE TWENTY-FIRST AND SAID THE ONE SENTENCE, AND HE DID NOT GO BACK, AND ON THE MONDAY TWENTY-FOURTH SHE CAME DOWN THAT PASSAGE AND SAW IT AND SAID NOTHING.**
