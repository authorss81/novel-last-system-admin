# Chapter 0761 — The Length Of Oak

Thursday the twenty-ninth of September 2022, in this borough. He is at his own unit and yard from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a length of oak planed on two faces and left with a shoulder that is not square.

**A MAN IN HIS EARLY FORTIES PLANES A LENGTH OF OAK THAT IS GOING TO BE A GATE STILE AND THE MAN FROM THE COTTAGE CUTS THE SHOULDER OFF BY HAND, AND THE SHOULDER COMES OUT SHALLOW AT ONE END AND THE LENGTH IS LEFT ON THE BENCH.**

He is a man in his early forties at the unit on Thursday. The man from the cottage comes to the unit gate and waits on his hands, and cuts the shoulder off the oak with a chisel.

---

He is in the unit from early with the roller door up to its bracket and the bench along the wall under the window. The gates lean where they have leaned since the spring with the chalk gone pale on them, and the one that carries two marks and a clean patch stands at the end nearest the door. The folded two-tread stool is by the paint. The drawer under the bench is shut with the bag and the brush in it on the bottom where it was put in July.

The oak is on the bench. It came off a tree that came down in the gales three winters back and it has been on the rack ever since, squared on two faces and rough on the other two, and it has dried slowly and gone hard on the outside and softer in the middle, which is what happens to a thing that is stacked on end in a shed with one end open to the air.

He means to get it to width on the two rough faces, take a hair off the two already done, saw the shoulders to length, and have it ready to be put into a gate whenever a gate wants doing. He has a smoothing plane with a keen blade, a block plane, a saw, and a chisel he has not sharpened this year.

He gets as far as planing one face.

---

The man from the cottage is at the unit gate at the middle of the morning with his hands in front of him and waits there.

"You have not got time for me."

"I have not got time for you." He does not stop planing. "Come past the gate if you are coming past the gate."

The man comes past the gate and stands at the end of the bench and looks down the length of the oak without touching it.

"That is a good piece."

"That is a piece with a hard skin." He takes another pass and looks at the shaving. "That curl came off me a forearm long and the last one off it was no longer than my thumbnail. That is a tree that stood in the wind on one side. You cannot get a face out of that in an afternoon and you know it."

"You can get it in a week."

"You can get it in a week and it will be a different face at the far end than it is here."

The man looks down the oak again and then at the saw on the bench.

"You are going to saw the shoulders now."

"I am going to saw the shoulders when I have done the faces."

"You have done one face."

"I have done one face and a half."

---

He goes back to it and does the second face and does it better than the first, and by the middle of the day the oak is two good faces and a hand's width of the third, and the curls coming off the plane are as long as his forearm where the wood is soft and as short as a thumbnail where it is hard, and the middle of the piece is now thinner than the two ends and will be thinner again by the end of it because that is the middle of that tree.

He lays the saw down across the shoulder and marks it, and then the man from the cottage, who has been standing at the end of the bench with his hands in front of him for an hour, takes the chisel up off the bench.

"You will want that square to the face."

"I will want it square to the face."

"You will not get it with that saw." The man has the chisel on the waste side of the mark and looks at the face and then along the top edge of the oak.

"It is a saw. You cut in from the end and you are in the face by the time you are through."

"You are in the face by the time you are through and you are in it more at the far end than at the near end, because the saw wants to follow the saw." The man puts the flat of the chisel on the mark. "Give me that. I will take it off by hand and it will be square."

He gives him the chisel and goes to the roller door and stands with his shoulder against the jamb while the man works, because there is nothing else to do and because a man who takes a shoulder off by hand is not a man to be spoken to in the middle of it.

It takes a quarter of an hour and the sound of it is different from a saw. It is a knocking and a sliding, and then it is a scraping as the bevel comes up flat, and then a lot of knocking in short taps with the heel of the hand on the butt of the handle.

It stops.

"That is off."

He comes back along the bench and looks at it.

The shoulder is off. It is clean and it is out of the square. At the near end the new face is flat and true and at right angles to the planed side, and going away from him it comes up slowly, and at the far end the shoulder is standing out over the planed face by the thickness of the back of a knife, which on a stile that goes into a gate is a corner that will stand proud of the gate along one edge and be the first thing anybody sees from the path.

"You did not take it square."

"I took it square to the top of the piece." The man puts the chisel down. "Your top is not the same as your face. It has gone over. I told you it had a hard skin on it and you have taken the skin off one end and not the other, and the piece is not straight from end to end, so a square laid on the top will not be a square laid on the face."

"You could have put the square on the face."

"I could have put the square on the face." The man looks at the saw on the bench. "And then I would have been squaring the face and not the shoulder, and you would have got a square shoulder on a crooked piece, which is no better. That is the choice. You want it one or the other."

"Which of those am I getting."

"You are getting the second one." He puts his thumb on the corner where the shoulder stands proud. "And you can have it now if you want it, but it will be a hand's work with a chisel on a hard piece of oak and I will not come back and do it with a plane when you have taken it down to size anyway."

---

He does not have it done now. He looks at the piece for a while with his thumb on the corner, and then he clears the bench of the shavings with the back of his hand, which takes him longer than the planing did, and puts the smoothing plane and the block plane on the shelf above the bench where they live, iron down, with the irons that go with them, and puts the saw on the wall and the chisel in the block behind the door.

The length of oak stays on the bench.

The man from the cottage goes out through the unit and across the yard and out at the gate, and there is nobody else in the yard. He stands in the unit for a while with his hands on the bench on either side of the oak, one at the near end where the shoulder is true and one at the far end where it stands out, and he can feel the difference in the wood from six inches away without touching either.
