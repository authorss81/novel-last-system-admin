# Chapter 0487 — The Side Door Was on the Latch

Tuesday the twenty-third of April 2019 — a morning beginning at ten past six, and then the whole of the day after it. Two houses at the bottom of a street, in Tideglass.

**SOMEBODY WENT INTO THE HOUSE AT NUMBER TWENTY-TWO BETWEEN HALF PAST TWO AND TEN PAST FOUR ON THE MORNING OF THAT FRIDAY IN APRIL AND TOOK NOTHING AND BROKE NOTHING, AND THE ONLY DIFFERENCE ANYBODY FOUND WAS THAT THE SIDE DOOR AT THE BACK OF THAT HOUSE WAS ON THE LATCH, AND THE WOMAN WHO WAS TOLD ABOUT IT ON THE THIRTEENTH AND THE MAN WHO WAS TOLD ABOUT IT ON THE TWENTIETH HAVE NOT SAID A WORD TO EACH OTHER ABOUT EITHER HALF OF IT.**

Number twenty-two is the fourth from the bottom of a street that is about thirty houses long and has nothing at the top of it worth the walk.

She has been in that house about nine years and he has been in it since about 2008, and they went in together in 2018 and there is nobody else in it, and there has been no other person in that house since the people before them left in about 2010.

They are not people who check things. That is the description she would give and he would not disagree with it, and the reason they do not check things is that there has never been anything in that street worth checking.


What happened in the small hours of that Friday was this.

The front door was locked and bolted, as it is every night and as it has been every night since about 2007. The back door, which is a door off the kitchen into a yard about fourteen yard deep, was on the latch.

That is all.

Nothing was taken. She is sure of that and she has been through it twice. There is nothing in that house worth taking to anybody who has not got it already, and the two of them have not got anything worth having, and she went round the front room and the small room at the top on the Sunday and put her hand behind things, and there was nothing behind any of them that had been moved.

Nothing was broken either. No door forced, no glass, nothing pulled out of anything, no cupboard open. She has looked at all of it and there is not one mark anywhere that was not already there before.


She was told on the Saturday, at about half past ten in the morning, by the woman two doors down.

The woman two doors down is about the same age as she is and they are on speaking terms in the way of two women who have been on speaking terms for nine years without becoming the sort of women who ring each other.

The woman two doors down was up at about half past two that night because she had got up to go to the bathroom and found the floor cold where it has been cold every night for years, and she had gone through to the back room to put a blanket over her father's knees.

And from her own back room she looked out at number twenty-two's yard, because that is what a person looks at from a back room at half past two, and she saw a torch beam going along the side of that house.

Not in the yard. Along the side of the house, low down, the way a person walks who is being careful about the ground, and it went from the front of that house to about where the side door is and then it stopped and there was a shadow against the kitchen window, and then it was gone.

The woman two doors down did not think anything of it at the time. She went back to bed. It came back to her on the Thursday of the following week while she was pegging out, and she said it out loud to her own husband, and he said that was the man who took the washing machine away in 2016 or somebody much like him, and she said that at half past two it was nobody.

And then she put her washing out and went up number twenty-two's path with her basket on her hip and said it.

---

What she was told, and the exact words, were these.

She said, "I saw somebody in your side yard in the night. Going down the side of yours with a torch."

And she said, "I didn't think anything of it at the time."

And she said, "You never do."

That last one was a joke and it was meant well and it went about nine inches into the woman at number twenty-two.

And she said, "You never do" and then stood there, because she could hear herself, and then said, "Sorry. I meant — you know what I meant."

And she said, "It's all right."

And the woman two doors down said, "There's nothing in the yard. I only wanted you to know somebody was there."

And she said, "Right. That is all," which is more than she has said to that woman about anything since about 2016.

That is everything that was said, it ran to about four minutes, and neither woman got anything out of the other.


What she decided inside about four minutes of the gate being shut is this.

The man she lives with is going to be kept out of this.

The reason is that what she has been given is that somebody walked down the side of her own house at half past two in the morning, and that is a thing that happens in every street in this borough about once a year, and that if she tells him then he will be a man who has been told that somebody was in his yard, and he is not a man who is going to be left like that. She knows him. He will go out at night for a week. He will start putting the other chain on and then the other chain will not be enough and something will be bought.

And there is nothing at the other end of it. Whoever it was went down a side with a torch and went away again and there is not one thing in that house that has gone.

So she has decided that the other one does not need to know, and she has kept it, and she will go on keeping it, and it is the first thing in nine years she has held back from him and she has not told him that there is anything to hold back either.


He was told on the Saturday, the twentieth, at about four in the afternoon, by a man who lives at the other end of that street and who has been sitting in his own car at the far end of it since about half past one waiting for somebody who did not come.

He said it on his own doorstep and he said it as an account of where he had been.

He said, "I was sat in the car from about half past one."

And he said, "I was waiting on somebody and they never came and I was still there at about half past two when I saw your man go down the side of yours."

And he said, "It was too dark to say. Dark jacket, I think. About your height. He had a torch in his hand and he had the side door open with his hand and it went in behind him."

And he said, "I didn't want to go knocking."

And he said, "That's all. I've been sat there twenty minutes trying to work out whether to say anything and I thought I'd say."

And he said, "Sorry. I hope it's all right."

---

What he has got, and he has been with it for three days and he has not got out of it, is a description.

About his height. A dark jacket. That is all, and that is a great deal more than nothing, and how many men in this borough are about his height and own a dark jacket is not one and is not nine either, and there is no way of standing in that street and counting them.

He has been over his own jacket twice. It is a dark one and it hangs by the door, and he had worn it that same morning.

And he has done the thing that he knows he has done, which is to stand in his own front room at about half past ten at night working out how many men between that end of the street and his own front door could be seen from a car at half past two in the morning, and the answer he keeps arriving at is that it is more than one and less than he would like.


Here is what he is keeping from her, and it is staying kept.

If he tells her, then she will ask him whether it was him. Not out loud, because she is not that kind of woman and never has been, but she will stand in that kitchen and put her hand on the front of the fridge — which is a thing she does — and she will look at him.

And if she looks at him then he has either got to say no, and be lying to her, or say nothing, and be doing the other thing.

So he has decided that she does not need to be told, and that is a thing he decided on the Sunday morning after, at about nine, while she was out.

They have not compared notes. She has not mentioned the yard. He has not mentioned the car. He has not asked her whether anything happened that night and she has not asked him whether he was up.

They have had their tea and they have had the Saturday and they have had the Sunday and there is nothing in either of them that the other one has any way of knowing about.


On the Tuesday morning of the twenty-third of April he got up at about ten past six and put the chain on the other door as well, which takes about four seconds and which he did not tell her about either, and she came down at about twenty past six and found it and stood at that kitchen table and looked at it for a bit.

And then she went and got the post that had come on Saturday and put it on the table, and she made the tea, and she made no mention of the chain, and nothing at all was said by either of them.

**A MAN PUT THE SECOND CHAIN ON A DOOR AT THE BACK OF A HOUSE AT NUMBER TWENTY-TWO ON THE MORNING OF THE TWENTY-THIRD OF APRIL 2019 AND THE WOMAN WHO LIVES WITH HIM CAME DOWN AT TWENTY PAST SIX AND LOOKED AT IT AND SAID NOTHING AND WENT ON PUTTING THE LETTERS ON THE TABLE.**
