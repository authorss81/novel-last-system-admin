# Chapter 0878 — The Shelf In The Face Of The Bed

Saturday the twentieth of January 2024, along the face of the front bed at number twelve from the middle of the day until the light has gone off the wall, and the hollows that the stones came out of are the whole of that Saturday.

**THERE IS A FRONT BED WITH THE EDGING OUT OF IT AND FOUR FEET OF ITS FACE STANDING OPEN AGAINST THE LAWN, AND SHE MEANS TO SET A HAND FLAT AGAINST THAT FACE WITH THE FINGERS UP INTO ONE OF THE HOLLOWS AND THE PALM TURNED UP UNDER THE LIP OF IT, AND SO WORK ALONG IT, AND HER SISTER IS AT THE FRONT GATE WITH HER HAND ON THE LATCH.**

Six feet of edging came out of that bed in the spring and it is standing against the wall in the yard under a string, and the bed is a hole with three sides and a face, which is what a bed is once it has been opened.

The face is the part nobody looks at.

Everybody who has ever walked past that bed has seen the top of it, which is a strip of about two feet of bare ground going down to the lawn, and the grass round it, and the frame. From the path you cannot see the face at all, because the face is at the end of the bed and it goes down into the ground.

She has been in the bed twice this winter and both times she stood at the top of it and looked down.

The hollows are where the stones were.

There are sixteen of them along about four feet of that face, and they are not spread out evenly, because the stones were not laid in a line with their ends in a row. They were set one at a time as she came along the face and each one went in where it went, and the face is the record of the order she worked in.

Sixteen hollows. Eleven of them are the depth of a stone.

Five of them are about twice the depth of a stone.

That much is visible from above, with the light going across the face. What is not visible from above is what is behind the hollows, and that is what she goes down for.

The way to find out what is behind a hollow is to stand in front of it and put your hand in.

She sets the heel of her hand flat against the face, low down, so that the base of her palm is on solid ground and the hand itself goes up into the hollow. Then she turns the palm upward under the lip of it, so that the hollow is taken between the outside of the hand and the inside of the fingers, and the hand is taking the weight of the ground above it without any part of her arm going into the hole.

She works it along the face, one hollow at a time, and every one of them gives the same back at the same depth.

The hand tells her the depth before her eyes do, because a hand in a hollow knows the shape of the space behind it in a way an eye looking into a hole never does.

And the first one has a floor in it.

That is the finding, and it is in all of them, and it is the reason this afternoon takes until the light has gone off the wall.

There is a shelf.

Under every one of those sixteen hollows there is a firm flat surface about three inches down, and it does not go down with the hollow. It runs the whole four feet without a break, at the same level, the same thickness, and it is not sand and it is not stone. It is ground.

It is the bottom of a course that was there before the bed was made, and the bed was made on top of it, and when she took the edging out she took out the top nine inches of that bed and left the nine inches below, and what is left is a floor of old ground with a new face cut down the front of it.

A bed is never as deep as it looks. A bed is made to the depth that is wanted and it is made on whatever was there, and what was there was nine inches of an older bed, and that older bed had been made on something flat.

She does the five deep ones and they are the same as the others underneath.

Deep hollow, then a shelf three inches down, running straight through, same level all the way along.

Which means the five bad stones were not bedded deeper than the eleven good ones. They were bedded at the same depth, in the same ground, on the same shelf.

They came out loose because somebody went at them with a bar.

The five deep hollows are not five hollows.

They are five places where the shelf has been dug out.

She puts her hand down into the first of them and there is no floor at three inches, because the ground there gives the whole of itself away and then stops giving altogether. There is a hand's depth of loose ground on the face, and then the bar had gone in under it, and then the bar had been levered, and a bar going in under a shelf takes the shelf with it.

Four of the five are like that. The fifth one at the end is the same.

So there are five notches in that shelf, about a hand wide each, running the whole four feet at the same line, and they are cut from the outside in, and they are the shape of a bar going under a lip and coming up.

That is how the five bad stones came out. Not out of the ground. Out of the ground, and then off the shelf, by somebody who could not get the heel of a bar into a three-inch hollow with eleven stones already set along the front of it and did not want to come back and take the whole four feet up again.

She gets the bar out of the shed and goes along the face of that bed putting the heel of it into each of the five notches in turn, and it goes in as far as the bar goes, and the ground comes up against the side of the bar at about the same place every time, and there is a hard bottom under the notch about three inches below the level of the shelf.

Three inches. So the bar was not scraping along the top of the shelf. It was taken off the bottom of it and it left a ledge, and the ledge is still there, and it is the width of a bar.

Which is what puts the run in the spring out of reach.

The eleven good stones go back into a bed that has a firm shelf at three inches to lay them on, and they can be bedded on it and they will not move.

The five bad ones have to go back into five notches, and a notch in a shelf is not a bedding. A stone laid in a notch with the notch under one end of it is a stone with nothing under the other end of it, and it rocks, and a rocking stone in a March run of edging is a stone that is out of line by the summer.

To lay those five properly the shelf has to be made good first, all along that four feet, and made good is not a barrow of anything. It is the bar and a day and the shelf the same as the other eleven, and it is not a job for one woman with an arm that went on a different bed last March.

---

Her sister comes to the front gate towards the end of the afternoon and stands on the path side of it with her hand on the latch and does not come in.

She wants the coal and she is told the answer is that Tuesday was the day, which was what came back in October as well and will come back in about as many weeks as that. She is told the yard door sticks, and the yard door does stick, and it stuck in the summer and it has stuck since. She stands there with her hand on the latch the whole time, and the front bed is round the corner of the house out of sight from the gate, and she does not come up the path.

Then she goes, and the latch lifts off her hand, and the gate is a gate.

She puts the bar back against the side of the shed where the bar lives.

Three inches of shelf the whole four feet of that face, and five notches in it about a hand wide each where somebody took the floor off the bottom to get at five stones that did not want to come.
