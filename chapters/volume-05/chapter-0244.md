# Chapter 0244 — First

Saturday the fourth of June 2016, from about eleven in the morning, a building by the water in Tideglass, and a Methodist hall on Carrow Lane.

**A PIECE OF PAPER CAME OFF A BENCH IN THE WIND AND WENT INTO A DRAWER UNDER A TRADE COUNTER UNDER A LOCK AND CAME OUT AGAIN ONCE, AND IT IS ON A TABLE IN THAT ROOM THIS AFTERNOON, AND IT IS CORRECT, AND NOBODY HAS OPENED IT FURTHER THAN IT WAS OPENED, AND SOMEBODY IN THAT ROOM THIS AFTERNOON ASKED A QUESTION THAT NOBODY ANSWERED AND THAT WAS NOT WRITTEN DOWN.**

Nina Calder comes in about once a month when nobody is asking her for anything, and on the fourth of June she was in the records room with a cup of tea on the sill and a list of what was left in a store, and she said the door sticks.

Jonas Mercer stood at the table by the wall and did not open the register, because he never opens it.

The register had been at nineteen for longer than he had been coming in, and the nineteenth was the last line anybody had ever written in it, and under that line there was a ruled column with a heading at the top of it and nothing whatever under the heading, and the heading was a number of days. He had done that sum once a month for the whole of the time he had been coming in, and had never once been asked to do it for, and had never once put it on the page.

He had a piece of paper in his coat with a date on it and did the sum standing up in the margin with a biro, and it came to five hundred and fifty-six, and he wrote it on the piece of paper and put the piece of paper back in his coat.

"That's the same as last month," said Nina Calder, without being asked, because she was going down the list and it was on the list.

"It's the same as every month."

"It's the same as every month," she said, and went back to the list.

He was out of the building by twenty past eleven and down the parade by twelve, and he did not do anything with the rest of that day, and there was nothing waiting for him, and that had been the position since the eleventh of May.

---

The Methodist hall on Carrow Lane is on a meter and nobody rents it and somebody put a pound in on the Saturday afternoon the way somebody does.

Five people came, and then six. Hanna Wray came with a piece of paper in her hand, face up, and put it on the table in the middle, and did not unfold it, because it was not folded, and did not push it towards anybody.

"That was in a drawer on Sunday morning," she said, "under the trade counter, and I've brought it because somebody asked me to bring it and I'd like it back where it was by about five, and nobody's to take it home."

Netherby. Tolforth. Halsey Cross. Ansterwick. Kirkmoss. Ellersby. Marleby. Ockbrook.

The woman who has been in that hall about two years read them out twice, slowly, from where she was sitting, and looked up after the second one.

"That's it," she said. "I did that on a bench on Sunday and I'll do it now and it comes out the same. I know one of them and I know where it isn't."

"Do you," said the man of about eighteen.

"No. I know Ockbrook's on the way to my sister's. That's the whole of what I've got and I'd not point at it."

The man in his sixties, who is a building estimator and who is very good in that room, was at the table with his hands flat on it.

"Before anybody starts," he said, "I want to say one thing about that piece of paper and then I'll shut up about it for the rest of the day. There are five words for this and four of them you can point at. A permission. An obligation. A claim. A right of return. Every one of those four is a thing somebody could put a finger on and show you, and I've read all four of them, and I am not going to lay them out on a table this afternoon, because the last time anybody laid them out they made an argument out of them that rested on a document nobody can produce, and I have been the man who made that argument and I have stopped."

"What about the fifth?" said the woman of about forty-two with the key.

"The fifth is a compact," he said, "and a compact is the one you can't point at, and that's not a flaw in the compact, that's what a compact is. It's two people putting a thing down and neither of them keeping a copy. There's nothing in a drawer with our names on it, because if you kept a copy you'd have a form, and if you've got a form you've got a manager, and then the thing's stopped being two people and started being an office." He looked at the sheet in the middle of the table. "And there is nowhere in this borough and nowhere in any of those eight towns that anybody could go and lay a hand on that, and if you think that's a fault then you've never agreed to anything in your life, and if you don't think it's a fault then there's nothing to say about it either."

"So how would anybody know we did it," said the woman with the key.

Nobody answered her, and about four seconds went by, and the young woman of about twenty, who is at a college four days a week, said, "I don't think that's the question," and it was not the question.

The woman of about fifty-five came at about half past four, and she is the one who has the room at the end of the first-floor corridor in a building by the water, and she had not been in that hall before and came because somebody told her there was a piece of paper.

"Does that tell you where it came from," she said, standing at the end of the table and not touching anything.

"No," said Hanna Wray.

"Does it tell you when."

"No."

"Does it tell you who's running it."

"No," said Hanna Wray, "and I'd not have printed that if I'd had to, because that's the part somebody comes back for."

Tomas Renn asked whether the two sheets on the wall in the hut at Penhale came out of the same place, and the man of about eighteen said what everybody in the room was thinking, which was that nobody in this borough had ever asked Haldane Perry where his two sheets came from, and that the year printed under both of them is the year somebody agreed something, and that not one person in this room could tell you what the something was, and that nobody was going to go up the road on a Saturday and ask an eighty-nine-year-old man who keeps a book to explain a year to six people who have come about a sheet of paper.

"That came out wrong," said the man in his sixties. "Say it again the other way round. Nobody's too frightened to ask. There is simply nobody here with anything to offer him for an answer, and a man who keeps a book in a hut is not going to be asked a question by six people who have come about a sheet of paper, and he'd be right."

"And the one in the corridor on the far side of the water," said the woman of about fifty-five. "The one Nell has got two of in her own hand. Is that one of these?"

"I've no idea," said Hanna Wray, "and neither has she, and she'll tell you the same. She's got two pieces of A4 off a pen out of her own bag, compared with a ruler, and they're copies, and a copy is not a wall, and there's about two hundred people a day past the wall and not one of them in this room."

At about ten to five the man of about eighteen put his hands on the table, and he said it in the plainest words he had, and he said it to the room and not to anybody in it.

"Somebody had to be first," he said. "There's a rule on a piece of paper with eight towns under it and there's the same rule on a wall in a hut in the next county and there's one in a corridor in a city on the far side of the water, and there's a wall in a town up the estuary with a man's name under it. Somebody wrote the first one. Somebody set it up and put the towns under it. Where are they now? What became of the first lot?"

The woman of about forty-two with the key looked at him and said, "Are you on anything?"

"I'm on the dishes," said the man of about eighteen, "four nights and a Friday."

"Right," she said, and put her handbag up on the table in front of her and kept her hand off it, and nobody else in that room had a book out either.

Nobody answered him. Hanna Wray put the sheet back in the drawer under the trade counter at about ten past five with the lock on it, and turned the key twice, which is more than it takes.

**AND THE REGISTER IS AT NINETEEN AND THE LINE UNDER IT IS FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SIX DAYS AND IS STILL BLANK, AND THE SHEET WENT BACK IN THE DRAWER AT TEN PAST FIVE, AND IT WAS NOT WRITTEN DOWN, AND THE DOOR OF THE RECORDS ROOM STICKS.**
