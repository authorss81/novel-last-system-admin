# Chapter 0604 — Ten Things Done

The Friday of the thirteenth of November 2020, from about eight in the morning until about eight in the evening, in this borough, in three places that have nothing to do with one another.

**THE TEN PEOPLE WHO DID SOMETHING IN THIS BOROUGH IN THE LAST FORTNIGHT ALL DID IT AND FINISHED IT, AND A VAN IS STILL BY A GARAGE, A FORM IS STILL ON A TABLE UNDER A PENCIL, AND A MAN WHO DRIVES A BUS HAS READ A BOARD AND GOT BACK IN.**

Here is the first of the three small things, and it is going wrong in the ordinary way in the first place.

There is a row of nine garages behind a row of houses, and a man has had a pitch at the bottom of that row since before the row was built over. He repairs things other people have given up on. He has been in this borough since he was twenty-one, and he has a good eye, and he is slow, and he is expensive, and about half the people on that row will not use him and the other half will not go anywhere else.

Since the middle of the month he has had a job on him, and the job is a woman's electric chair. It came to him because the shop on the parade has stopped doing them. It is a good chair and a heavy one, and he got it up three steps into a house on the estate.

It is done. It goes. He tested it in her front room on Friday and she used it twice while he watched, and he said the price, and she said she would think about it, and he said right, and he carried it back down the three steps and put it in his van.

The van is still there. It goes on the Monday. He has not chased it and he will not.

And here is the second small thing, and it is going wrong in a different way, about a mile and a half from the first.

There is a woman who has a shop on a corner, and it has been hers since the June before last, and it is not doing what it did.

The thing that has gone wrong is the price of one line. She has sold a thing at the wrong price for six weeks and has known about it for five of them. The difference is about a penny on each one, and she has sold about two hundred.

She worked it out on a Sunday evening in her kitchen with a scrap of paper and a pencil, and she did it twice and got the same figure both times, and the figure is a bit over twenty pounds, and she sat looking at it for a while.

And she has gone on to the supplier's site four times since, and there is a form on there that says *report a pricing error*, and it takes nine fields, and one of the fields wants a number, and she has got to it, and she has not sent it.

That is not because she is frightened of it and it is not because she is busy. It is that the form wants the number of units, and if the number goes in, then all of it is on a record with her name on it, and about two hundred people get told that a shop on a corner in this borough sold a thing at the wrong price over the summer.

She is not going to do that to a shop with her name over the door that has four people in it, and they are her mother's two and a niece and a boy of nineteen on a Friday.

So she has done the other thing, which is the ordinary thing. On the Monday she went in and put the price up by a penny and told none of the four, and she is losing a penny a unit and has been since the Monday, and if the supplier has it right by the time she closes in the new year then it will have cost her nothing at all.

The form is still on the table under the pencil. She has moved the pencil twice to get at a bag.

And here is the third small thing, and it is going wrong in a third way, in a street that is not on the way to either of the other two.

There is a man who drives a bus on a route of about nine miles in this borough, and he has driven that route for nineteen years.

What has gone wrong is one junction. Since the middle of the month the works at the bottom of a road have meant that the buses on that route cannot turn left there and have to go straight on and come back, and the coming back is about two miles, and it adds about six minutes each way.

He has been doing it for a fortnight. He has done it exactly as instructed on every one of the runs he has driven since it changed, and he has not been late once, and he has not spoken to anybody about it except the man who sits behind him and who said, *you'd think they'd put a sign up.*

He has thought about the six minutes a great deal. If he goes in at the same time as ever and there is more traffic on the road, then the time is going somewhere, and the only place it can be going is into the two miles at the back, and everybody on that route knows it, and everybody on that route is doing the same thing at the same time.

He has not rung anybody. He has not written to anybody. He decided on the Thursday that he will not write, and he has not written.

On Friday he stopped at that junction on the way back, which he is not supposed to do, and got out and read the boards. They are boards about drainage, and one of them carries a month, and the month is the twelfth, and he got back in and did the loop and was four minutes early and did not tell anybody that either.

Here is the rest of the day in that borough, and it is worth setting out because ten people out of a hundred and thirty thousand is not a small number.

The woman in his early forties whose office is above a shop was in at about half past eight on the Friday with the door open, and the man in his early fifties who works with her came in at about nine, and the two of them have not mentioned the wallet, and he put it in the chest of drawers on the Friday of the twenty-fifth of September and it has been there seven weeks.

The woman in her early fifties who has a son who paints was in that kitchen on the Friday night at about half past eight and said nothing about the outside of a shop at the bottom of her road, which has been one colour since the end of September, and her son has not mentioned it either, and neither of them has mentioned it to the man in the mid fifties who keeps that shop, and he has not mentioned it to either of them.

The woman in her mid fifties was in town on the Saturday of the third of October and came home on a bus with a paper bag, and her husband has not asked where she was, and has not asked about the sideboard, and on the Wednesday of the sixth of October she asked him about the sideboard once and he said no.

The woman in her early thirties has been in every day this week and has been out of work since the March, and the man she spoke to on the Thursday of the eighth of October is not a man she is going to ring, and the man who told her to ring has not asked her how it went, and she has not said.

The woman in her mid thirties has not opened the book on her table, and the man she works with got the letter he wrote to her a fortnight ago, and it is in a drawer in that office and it is going to go into an envelope, and the envelope has his name on it and is going to be posted to a different address, and both of those are known to one person in this borough and are not known to either of the other two.

A man in his mid thirties was in a corridor of a building in another borough on the Tuesday of the thirteenth of October, and on the Thursday of the fifteenth, and again last week, and on Tuesday next week, and he has a piece of paper in his coat with his own handwriting on the back of it under somebody else's pencil, and he is going to a building opposite on the Tuesday.

A woman in her late forties has a sheet of three things in her bag and it has been in her bag since the Friday of the sixteenth of October, and the man she put it to is not going to ring anybody about a hundred and thirty pounds, and the young man who told him in a car park on the Wednesday of the fourteenth has not been told any of it.

A woman in her late thirties has a set of keys on a shelf by a door in another part of this borough that came off a fob in the June, and the woman in his mid forties who put them there has not looked at them since the Thursday of the twenty-second of October, and the man in his early twenties who gave them over in the queue has not been told where they went, and the man in his late thirties who carried them did what he was asked and has not mentioned it.

A man went back to a door in a row last Sunday and said a second thing in different words, and the woman he said it to said that she had understood it the first time, and neither of them has said anything else about it, and the other four people in that borough are not in this paragraph because they had nothing done and nothing to be given and have done nothing this fortnight at all.

That is ten, and at about eight o'clock this evening in a hundred and thirty thousand people there is a van that has not gone and a form that has not been sent and a man in his mid forties who has read a board about drainage and got back in and did his loop.

**TEN THINGS WERE DONE IN THIS BOROUGH IN A FORTNIGHT AND EVERY ONE OF THEM HAS BEEN FINISHED BY A PERSON WHO IS NOT WAITING FOR ANYTHING, AND THE GARAGE IS SHUT AND THE VAN IS ON THE PITCH AND THE PENCIL IS STILL ON THE FORM AND THE BUS WENT PAST THE JUNCTION TWENTY-THREE TIMES.**
