# Chapter 0793 — The Lid Off The Coal Hole

Wednesday the fifteenth of February 2023, in this borough. The coal hole is at the side of number nine with the lid off it and she is out there with it for a day that is a lid off and a lid on and nothing else.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES HAS A COAL HOLE WITH A GOOD LID ON IT AND A LOAD STANDING IN IT THAT HAS HAD NO AIR SINCE NOVEMBER, AND MEANS TO TAKE THE LID OFF HERSELF AND PUT IT BACK ON AFTER, THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING IS ALREADY KNEELING AT IT WHEN SHE GOES ROUND, AND DOES IT PROPERLY, AND SHE IS NOT ASKED IN AGAIN, AND DOES IT HERSELF AFTERWARDS IN THE WET.**

She has been in her late fifties for some years and number nine is hers, and the whole of that day is at the side of the kitchen with a coal hole in it.

---

There has been a hard frost on since Sunday with a sun on it in the mornings, and that is the kind of frost that does not go out of a hole in the side of a house that has not been opened since before Christmas.

Nothing in that kitchen has been moved since Sunday. There is a cloth across the range and a cup on the drainer with a skin on it, and the skirting behind the cooker has lifted away from the wall at the corner, which it has done since the summer. In the dark, before the bulb over the door comes on, the only light in that house is the red pilot on the cooker and the red light on the cold box in the corner, and by that light she cannot see the top of the coal hole at all.

In the porch the mat is rolled up the way it was rolled up before she came. The apron on the hook behind the back door has the back piece off an envelope in the pocket, and the hinge of that door has had nothing in it since the summer. She has known for a long while what the bottom edge of that door does where it goes over the stone, and it drops about the width of a hand there, and she does not try to shut it any harder than it wants to be shut.

Down the front path the gate is standing open across the flags behind two wedges with the spanner still lying where he dropped it on the flags. The bay of path she had taken up is in line with the flags above it and the tub of iron is on the middle of it, because that is where the tub goes. The pads on the fridge are stacked with the first at the bottom. The front room stays shut.

The coal hole has been shut since the twenty-seventh of November.

That is the day the new lid went on, and it is the day the air went out of that side of the house. Nothing has gone through that hole since, and the load standing in it has had nothing going up through the middle of it since before Christmas, and the frost that came in the week before Christmas has not been out of it since, and it is the fifteenth of February.

She can see the top of the load from the doorway. The boards and the blocks are standing on end in it the way they have stood since the autumn, and the front of them is the pale colour they have been since the day she carried the first lot in, and when she puts her palm flat on the shut lid in the cold the stone takes the heat out of her hand.

Her plan is the lid.

She means to get a bar under the front of it, lift it off the ledge, stand it against the wall of the house on edge, leave it there while she goes and does what she has to do, and put it back at the end of the day with its back edge where it goes and the two screws that are in the ledge going back into the two holes they came out of, and the pair at the front left where they are, because there is nothing under the front of that board for them to do anything with. She has been lifting that lid off and on like this for years and she knows exactly how it comes off, because it is a piece of a step and it is the weight of one.

---

The woman who does her shopping is at the coal hole when she goes round the side of the house.

She is down on her knees in the grass at the side of the kitchen with one hand flat on the lid and no bag anywhere.

"How long have you been there."

"Not long." The woman gets up off her knees and stands out of the way. "You were going to do it yourself."

"I was going to do it myself."

"You were going to do it yourself with a bar, and you were going to do it with your back to the weather, and there is a straight wind on the side of that house this morning."

---

Then she does it.

It takes both of them and it takes five minutes.

The two screws at the back corners come out first, four or five turns of the handle each, out of the sound ledge because that is where they went in in November, and she puts them in the pocket of her apron and they stay in there for the rest of the day. Then there is nothing to grip on the front of that lid, because its back edge sits on the ledge and the whole of the front of it stands four inches proud of the face of the wall with a gap under it the thickness of a nail, and there is nothing at all to lift it by. Her visitor goes round to the yard side with a long piece of batten and puts the end of it in under the front edge from below and brings it up, and the stone comes up off the ledge on the end of the batten in one movement with a noise like a boot coming out of a river, and then the two of them carry it round to the side of the house and stand it up on edge against the brick with the front face out of the wind.

It is the weight of a stone step about a foot and a half across.

Then the woman stands at the hole and looks down into it for about as long as it takes to count a thing and does not count anything.

"The front of it is dry."

"The front of it is dry because it has been dry since the autumn and there has been a hole in this wall letting the top of that pile breathe ever since it went in." She straightens up. "And it is not going to stay that way. It has had no air at all since the last week of November and the frost is still in the middle of it, and this hole is the only place on this side of the house that any of it can come out of."

She stands and looks at it.

"Then leave it off."

"Then I will leave it off." She looks round the side of that house. "And you will leave it on. And that is the whole of it, and it is what was said to you in the autumn. There is nothing in that hole to dig out and nothing to be carried away. It is a stack of firewood stood on end in the dark at the side of her, and the hole it is in has to be open."

---

Then she puts it back on.

She lifts it on her own, with the batten, the same way it came off. She stands it up over the ledge and brings it down with the back edge onto the ledge first, the way it goes, so that the whole of the front edge stands out over the brickwork with the gap along it the thickness of a nail. Then she puts her own two screws back into the ledge at the back corners, because she has done that once already in November and she knows where every one of them goes, and the screwdriver goes back into the pocket of her apron along with them. She puts two fingers under the front and lifts: the whole front comes up about the thickness of a thumb, which is right, and nothing at either end, which is right.

She runs her hand along the joint at the back, then down the two ends and out along the front, and there is nowhere on any of it she can put the edge of a thumbnail.

Then she goes round the side of the house to the front and watches her go and does not follow her, and does not call after her, and stands in the grass beside a coal hole with the lid on it and no air in it.

---

She is not asked in again.

There is a second frost in March and the load wants airing and she thinks about the batten and about the long piece of wood and about standing a stone step on edge against the brick, and she does not do any of it, and she does the lid herself.

She does it on a Wednesday in March and it is wet.

She does it properly as far as she gets. She backs the two screws out of the ledge at the back corners and puts them in her apron, and then she gets the bar under the front edge and brings the stone up and she stands it against the wall of the house on the side of the house the wind is not on. She goes in for the post and comes out and she does not leave it. She puts it back within the hour, which is the thing she has been told to do and the only thing she has been told to do.

And the ledge is wet.

That is the whole of it. There has been rain on that wall since before she got up, and there is a film of water lying along the ledge where the stone goes. A stone that weighs what that stone weighs does not come down onto a wet ledge at the back corner and stay where it is put. It slides about the width of a thumbnail, and she cannot see the back corner from where she is kneeling at the front of it, and she puts two fingers under the front and lifts, and it comes up as it should, and the two screws at the back go into nothing at all, because the stone has moved a thumbnail's width off the holes they go into, and at the near corner there is a gap about the width of a thumbnail, because nothing is standing on that part of the ledge now.

She goes round the house and comes back and looks at it from the front.

You cannot see that gap from the front at all. From the front the lid is shut and true and the whole of the front edge of it stands out from the brickwork with a hair of daylight under it, exactly as it was made to be in the autumn, and it is a good lid.

She goes in and out of that kitchen for the rest of the day with the fire unlit and the pads on the fridge, and the wind gets up on the side of that house later on and finds the near corner, and before the light has gone off it there is a thread of water coming off the ledge at that corner and running down the face of the brick and standing in the grass at the foot of it.

She is on her knees at that coal hole with the lid off it again at the end of the day, in the wet, with a bar in her hand and the front of the load standing on end under her and no air coming through anything anywhere on that side of that house, and she does not call for anybody, because there is nobody to call, because she did not ask.
