# Chapter 0510 — A Sentence in June

The Tuesday of the sixth of August 2019, most of that day, and it began a little after ten past nine. Two streets, and the length of a piece of pavement between them, in Tideglass.

**SOMETHING ORDINARY WAS SAID ACROSS FOUR FEET OF AIR IN JUNE 2019 AND WAS GONE FROM THE MIND OF THE MAN WHO SAID IT BEFORE HE MOVED ON, AND SHE KEPT IT AND IS STILL CARRYING IT, AND HE HAS GONE BACK OVER IT EVERY SINGLE DAY SINCE BECAUSE HE BELIEVES SHE KEPT IT, AND SHE HAS NO IDEA AT ALL THAT HE BELIEVES SHE KEPT IT, AND ON THE SIXTH OF AUGUST THEY CAME WITHIN ABOUT FOUR YARD OF EACH OTHER ON THE SAME PIECE OF PAVEMENT AND NEITHER OF THEM SPOKE.**

He has spoken to her three times in his life.

The first was back in 2015, outside a place on the parade, and he said the price of something before he asked what it was. The second was in about 2016 and was two words about a door being in the way. The third is the fourth of June.

They are not neighbours. Neither of them knows where the other one lives, neither has ever found out, and that has been true on about three hundred separate occasions since 2015.


Here is what he said, and it is one sentence, and he could not have told you one word of it on any day since.

It was a little after ten in the morning and there were about ten people in that line outside that door and it was not raining and it was the fourth of June.

She was two places in front of him.

He said — and this is the sentence, and he has gone back to it about four hundred times since:

"You'll be all right with it."

That is all.

She turned round.

And he said, "Sorry. I was talking to you. You'll be all right with it."

And she said, "Right."

And then his turn came and he went in, and it took about six minutes inside, and he came out and went down the street, and he did not think about it again until about half past nine that night when he got into bed.

That is the whole of the event: seven words and about seven seconds, on a Tuesday.


And what she took from it is this, and she has never put it down and has not told a person.

She took from it that he had looked at her.

Not at the back of her head. She is quite clear about that. He had looked at her face before he said it, in the ordinary way a man looks along a line of people at somebody he is going to speak to, and then he had said the sentence, and then she had turned round and he had said it again properly.

And what she has built on top of that in nine weeks is that a man who looks at a stranger's face in a line of ten people and then says *you'll be all right with it* has done one of about four things, and she does not know which, and she has gone round all four.

The first is that he knew her. The second is that he has heard something about her that she does not know has been said. The third is that he is the kind of man who says it to everybody. The fourth is that she is the only person in that line he could say it to.

And the third one is the one she is least able to put down, because she has never once in nine weeks been in a line with him in a way that would let her find out whether he says it to everybody, and she has not gone looking, and she has not arranged to be in a line with him, and she has not asked anybody anything.


And what he took from it, on the night of the fourth of June, was that he had been rude.

That is the whole of it and it has cost him nine weeks.

He got into bed and it came to him that he had said it to the back of a stranger's head in a line of people without knowing who she was, and that the sentence he had said was the sort of sentence that requires a conversation to be in progress, and that there was no conversation, and that she had had to turn round.

And he has been through it every day since.

What he cannot get past is the second half. He said it again, properly, when she turned round. He said, "Sorry. I was talking to you. You'll be all right with it." And she said, "Right." And that was correct, and it was finished, and four seconds covered all of it, and nothing was wrong.

And what he has not been able to work out since is why a woman who says *right* has been in his head every morning for nine weeks.

He goes over it. He gets to about half past nine in the morning most days and thinks about it and then goes to work.

He thinks she remembers it. That is his position and it is not a humble one, and he would be the first to say he has no evidence for it.

And there is one other thing he has started doing, about three weeks ago, and he has not told anybody that either.

When he sees a woman alone on a pavement ahead of him, and she is the wrong sort of build, and she is walking at his pace, he says nothing at all.


And on the sixth of August, this Tuesday, at about ten past ten in the morning, they were on the same piece of pavement.

Here is how that happened and neither of them arranged it.

He goes up that street on a Tuesday because his work is at the top of it. She goes down that street on a Tuesday because she has been going down it since about 2015 and has never had a reason.

They came towards each other at about a quarter past ten and passed, and he was on her left and she was on his right, and there were about four yard between them at the closest point.

And he did the thing he has been doing for three weeks and he said nothing at all.

And she went past him and did not turn round.


And here is what each of them is carrying about the same morning, and it is two entirely different mornings.

She is carrying the fourth of June.

What she thinks about when she thinks about it — and she has decided how often she is going to think about it, which is not often — is that she was on her way back from an appointment she had made for somebody else on the fourth of June and that a man she had never spoken to in her life looked at her face in a line of people and told her she would be all right with it.

And what has never once entered it is that he thinks about it.

If it had, it would be a different thing. If he had been seen anywhere near it, in a street, with a face, it would have been a thing about two people.

It is not that. It is a thing she is carrying on her own, and the whole of the difference between her version and his is that his version is about an apology and hers is about being looked at.


And he is carrying the same morning and what he is carrying is a woman on a pavement in a line of about ten people turning round, and the fact that he has been wrong about which part of it she is keeping ever since the June.

He does not know that she is keeping it at all. If he knew that, it would be over.

That is what he has got to, and it took him about six weeks.

If she remembers it then he has insulted a woman he has never met and she has been carrying that for nine weeks, and there is nothing at all he can do about it now and there never will be.

And that is the version he has been carrying, and it is entirely a fact about him.


At about half past two on that Tuesday she was in a kitchen on a street two hundred yard off that pavement and she was putting a new washer into a thing that had been dripping, and she stopped halfway through it, with the washer in her fingers, for no reason she could put into words, and then carried on.

**A MAN WALKED UP A STREET ON THE TUESDAY OF THE SIXTH OF AUGUST 2019 AT A QUARTER PAST TEN IN THE MORNING AND A WOMAN WALKED DOWN THE SAME STREET AND THEY PASSED EACH OTHER WITH ABOUT FOUR YARD BETWEEN THEM AND NEITHER OF THEM SPOKE.**
