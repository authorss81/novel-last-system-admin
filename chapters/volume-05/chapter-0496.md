# Chapter 0496 — She Did Not Go Past

Tuesday, the fourth of June 2019, that morning only, from about twenty past six until about a quarter to ten. One parade, and the length of a row of shops on the north side of it, in Tideglass.

**A WOMAN WHO LIVES AT NUMBER THIRTY-FOUR ON THAT PARADE WALKED OUT OF HER OWN FRONT EVERY MORNING AT ABOUT HALF PAST SEVEN, WENT UP TO THE TOP OF IT AND CAME BACK DOWN, FOR NINE YEARS, AND SHE STOPPED ON THE MORNING OF THE SECOND OF NOVEMBER LAST YEAR AND HAS TOLD NO PERSON WHY, AND A MAN WHO PUTS THE SHUTTER UP AT HALF PAST SEVEN HAS STOOD IN THE DOORWAY OF HIS OWN SHOP EVERY MORNING SINCE AND HAS SAID NOTHING TO HER ABOUT IT AND IS NOT GOING TO.**

The parade is about two hundred yard long and it is on a slight hill. There are nine shops on the north side of it and about as many on the south, and at the top there is a turning place and a wall.

Number sixty-one is a hardware shop. It has been a hardware shop since about 1994 and before that it was something else with the same front. The shop is about sixteen foot wide, with a doorway about three foot in from the left and one window that is a single sheet of glass, and above the window there are four rows of wooden slats that are the only thing on that front anybody has ever put up about anything.

The man who owns it puts the shutter up at half past six and it is a roller and it takes him about a minute and a half.

Then he stands in the doorway.

---

He has stood in that doorway for about six years and he has always told anybody who has asked that it is because of the air, and it is a shop with a door at one side and a wall at the other and the air in it is not different from the air outside it in any way a person could put a name to.

There is one thing about it that he has never said to anybody, and it is that he could not tell you which morning he started standing there instead of going back inside, if he was asked, and he has gone over it, and it does not go to a morning at all. It goes to the first morning he noticed that she was not going past.

That was the morning of Monday the twelfth of November 2018, and it was about ten past eight in the morning, and it was the first morning of about seven months of them.

---

Here is the woman, and the whole of it.

She is at number thirty-four, which is nine doors down the parade and past two empty shop fronts. She is not on the north side. She is on the south side, and her front door is opposite a hairdresser's and about a third of the way down the hill.

For nine years she went out of her own front at about half past seven every morning, walked up to the top of the parade, and walked back down, and then she went to work, and she got the bus at the top of the parade at about ten to eight.

That was the whole of it. There was nothing at the top of the parade. She did not have a reason that anybody in that row could see, and if one of them had asked her she would not have had one ready, and she has been asked twice, once by a woman who had moved in next door in 2014 and once by a man from the water board who was doing something to a stopcock.

And what she has never told anybody, and what she worked out on a Wednesday afternoon in September of the year before, is that the walk was not for her.

It was for the fact that she went up the parade on the side where the shops are, and that for about four of those nine years there was a man standing in a doorway on that side at about the time she reached the top, and that she did not look at him and he did not look at her, and that this was the entire content of the walk.

She has worked out that if she were honest with herself about it, she has been getting dressed at twenty past six every morning for nine years so as to be walking past a man.

And she decided on that Wednesday afternoon that she was not going to do that any more, and she went on doing it for about seven weeks, and then on Friday the second of November she did not go, and she has not been told a single person that she has stopped.

Here is what he did about it, which is nothing, and here is why.

He noticed on the Monday. He came out of the shop at about half past seven and she was not going up the parade, and he went back inside and did the same four things he does, and he thought about it at about ten past eight and then again at about half past ten.

That night he put the lamps in his shop window on at about ten and stood at his own door and looked up the parade towards number thirty-four on the south side, because from where he stands you can see the front of that house, and he stood there for about a quarter of an hour, and she was on her own step with her coat on, doing nothing, not going up.

And the thing he worked out that night was not about her at all.

It was that if he asked her, then it would be on the record that he had been standing in that doorway every morning for years in order to be there when she went past, and he has never in his life wanted any woman in that parade to be able to say that sentence about him, and he is not going to start now, and there is no version of the question that is not the question.

So the arrangement is that neither of them will raise it, and the arrangement was made in about four minutes on a Monday in November by two people who have never said one word to each other about any of it, and both of them believe that they made it separately, and neither of them has ever found out whether it is one arrangement or two.

What happened on the Tuesday is this, and it took about a minute and a half.

She had been away since the Saturday. She has a sister at the far end of the line and she went for four days and told nobody she was going, and she came back on the Sunday night.

On the Tuesday morning it was already hot by twenty past six, which is early even for the beginning of June, and he had the shutter halfway up and the four rows of slats were catching the sun off the parade.

And the step ladder was in the road.

He had put it out on the Friday before that to reach the gutter over the shop next door, which is not a high gutter and which he could have reached with a broom, and he had left it standing against the front of number sixty-one, and he had not gone back for it, because he went home at one on the Friday and came back on the Monday morning and looked straight past it.

Somebody in the dark that night had come across the parade and set it over, and it was lying across the footway about two foot out from the kerb, and it had been raining for about four hours before that, and it was black with it.

She came up the parade at about half past seven on the other side, because she still goes up and down that parade twice a day and has done every day since she stopped, and nobody has ever asked her why and nobody has ever noticed.

And when she got to the top she crossed over to the north side, which she has not done in about seven months, because the bus from the top of that parade goes from the south side and there is nothing at all on the north side.

She stopped.

And she stood the ladder up against the front of number sixty-one on her own, in about ten seconds, without going near anybody or touching anything else, and she put her hand flat on the top step to be certain it was not going to go over again.

And he came out of his own doorway with the handle of his own shutter still in his hand at the same moment.

Their hands were on it at the same time for about half a second, and then hers came away, and she stepped back about a foot and looked at the top step.

Nothing was said.

He said nothing because he had the handle of his own shutter still in his hand, and because a woman standing on her own step at twenty to eight in the morning with her coat on is not a thing a man opens his mouth about.

She said nothing because she had done a thing a stranger does and there was nothing in it to say, and because she had come across the parade for the first time in seven months and she had a reason for that which was not about a ladder.

He went back inside and shut the door about a foot and stood in there.

She crossed back to the south side and went down to thirty-four and went in, and her own door was shut by the time he looked.

And on the Wednesday and the Thursday and the Friday, at about half past seven, he stood in his own doorway and looked up the parade at the south side, and nothing came past, and on the Saturday he left the ladder against the front of the shop and took it in at lunchtime and put it away where it goes.

**A WOMAN STOOD A STEP LADDER BACK UP AGAINST THE FRONT OF NUMBER SIXTY-ONE ON THAT PARADE ON THE MORNING OF THE FOURTH OF JUNE 2019 AND A MAN CAME OUT OF THE DOORWAY OF NUMBER SIXTY-ONE WITH THE HANDLE OF HIS OWN SHUTTER STILL IN HIS HAND AND THEIR HANDS WERE ON IT FOR ABOUT HALF A SECOND, AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID ONE WORD, AND ON THE SATURDAY HE PUT THE LADDER AWAY.**
