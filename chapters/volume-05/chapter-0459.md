# Chapter 0459 — What He Said About the Stair at Night

Monday the tenth of December 2018, from about twenty to nine until about ten past nine. One street and a passage off it, in Tideglass.

**A WOMAN IN A PASSAGE ON THE MORNING OF THE TENTH OF DECEMBER ASKED A MAN WHETHER ANYBODY ELSE WAS IN THAT BUILDING AFTER DARK AND HE SAID NOT THAT HE HAD SEEN, WHICH WAS TRUE, AND SHE HAS PUT NOT ONE FURTHER QUESTION TO HIM SINCE, AND HE CANNOT TAKE IT BACK.**

There is a man in this borough who does small building work, and who has done four or five jobs in the last year in one building off that street, and who is known on that street the way a man is known when everybody knows what he is for.

There is a woman who has the second floor of that building and has had it since the summer, and who is up and about at all hours and sleeps badly, and who is not a person who goes round asking her neighbours questions.

She asked him one. It is the only one she has ever asked him and she asked it on a Monday morning in that passage.

---

What she asked was whether there was anybody else in that building at night.

She did not put it the way it sounds. She had been about four days without more than about two hours and she was standing in that passage with her coat on at about twenty to nine in the morning, and she said, "You'd know if there was anybody about in that building at night, wouldn't you."

And he answered, because it is a thing he can answer in about two seconds and it is what he is for.

He said, "Not that I've seen."

Here is what he had actually seen, and what he said, and the whole distance between them.

He has been going into that building most days since about August. He has a van, and he has a key for the yard and a key he was given for the door at the bottom, and he has been up that common stair two or three times a week since the summer and he has been in and out of it at every hour of the day there is.

At night he has been in that building twice. Both times he was there until about eight in the evening and both times the stair was empty behind him.

That is the whole of his evidence. It is a stair he has gone up in the light about seventy times and gone down in the light about seventy times, and two evenings when he was the last one out.

He said "not that I've seen," and every word of it is true, and it is the most careful sentence available and he chose it without knowing he was choosing it.

What she was asking was not that.

He worked that out about four days later, in the middle of an afternoon, doing something else entirely, and it came into his head the way a thing does when you have been on your own with it for a bit.

She sleeps at the front. The common stair is at the back. There is a woman on the third floor of that building who is in and out at all hours and there is a man on the ground floor who comes in at about two in the morning twice a week, and between those two floors and hers there is a length of stair she cannot get out of her hearing.

She was not asking him what he had seen. She was asking him a thing that she could not ask anybody else because everybody else in that building is a person she has to keep being decent to in a corridor.

And he cannot put it back.

It is not that the answer was false. He would like that to be the problem, because a man who has said something false can go back and correct it and has a reason for doing it and the correction is a thing that can be done. This was not that.

"Not that I've seen" is true and it will be true for the rest of his life, and it was true at twenty to nine on the Monday, and there is no point at which it stops being true.

What has happened since is that she has not asked him anything at all.

Not that. Everything. There was one question in six months and now there is none, and none of the absence has anything to do with him, and the man who asked her the question has gone on doing jobs in that building and saying the same things he has always said and getting paid the same and being asked for the same.

He has worked out that she believes him. He has not worked out what she is going to do, and there are about four versions of it and he has not managed to make himself prefer any of them, and he has noticed that not managing to prefer any of them is the part that is going to stay with him.

He is not going to go and put it right.

That has been available to him every day since the Monday. He could go back to that passage and say he was sorry, she should not have asked him, that he was the wrong person, that he has no idea.

He has not done it and he is not going to, and if anybody asked him why he would have to say that it is because he does not know what the rightness is. He can put right the fact that she asked the wrong man. He cannot put right the fact that the man she asked has been on that stair two times in six months and knows nothing whatever about what happens on it after eight in the evening.

If he said that to her it would be a man telling a woman who cannot sleep that the man she asked is no use to her, and it would be the second time he had made that building smaller for her, and he has not been asked for it and he is not going to offer it.

---

So the answer stands. It is true, it was quick, and it is the entire of what he has ever said to her about that stair, and there is nothing anywhere in this borough that says she asked him a different question, and there is nothing anywhere in this borough that says anybody answered it.

**HE PUT HIS BAG IN THE VAN AT TEN PAST NINE ON THAT MONDAY AND WENT DOWN THE STREET, AND SHE STOOD IN THAT PASSAGE FOR A WHILE AFTERWARDS AND THEN WENT UP THE STAIRS, AND NOT ONE THING HAS BEEN SAID BETWEEN THE TWO OF THEM IN THE SIX WEEKS SINCE.**
