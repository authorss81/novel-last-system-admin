# Chapter 0551 — The Column Stops

The Thursday of the fifth of March 2020, from about ten past seven in the morning until about half past four in the afternoon, in one block of thirty-eight flats in one street of this borough.

**HE HAS PUT ONE LINE IN A COLUMN EVERY TUESDAY MORNING SINCE THE MARCH OF 2018, AND A MAN CAME TO HIS DOOR IN FEBRUARY AND LOOKED INSIDE SOMETHING AND LEFT AGAIN, AND HE HAS NOT PUT A LINE IN SINCE, AND HE HAS NOT TOLD ANYBODY IN THAT BLOCK THAT HE HAS STOPPED.**

The block is thirty-eight flats in three floors. It went up in 1963 for people who worked on the water and it was divided up about 1990, and the water in it has never been right.

The cold is on the ground floor and it is on a Tuesday, at about seven, and it lasts about an hour, and then it comes back on its own at about eight. It has done that long enough that a dozen people in that block could tell you about it and every one of them still can.

He is a man in his late fifties. He has been in the flat on the first since 2016 and he has been writing it down since the March of 2018, on the day after, in a hard book, in a column, with the day, and the hour, and what the tap did.

He has never shown it to anybody. Nobody in that block has ever asked to see it.

The woman in her early sixties on the second floor has seen him with a pen in his own door on a Tuesday morning, and has said nothing, and has said nothing on any of them.

Nobody in that block knows what is written in that book. It is a column of days and hours and a note of what the tap did, and it is about a third of the way down the first page and it stops there.

---

The twelfth of February was a Wednesday, and it is the only day in two years anything has come to that door about any of this.

A man came at about half past ten carrying more than he had any reason to be carrying, and the woman in her early sixties let him in because the lock on that door sticks and it sticks for everybody, and he was in for about twenty minutes.

He went to the space under the window at the end of the flat and opened the door of it and looked inside and shut it again.

He did not look at the tap. He did not turn it. He did not ask the man in his late fifties a single question, and he wrote nothing down, and when he went he said that he would be in touch, and he has not been in touch and nobody in that block has heard from him since.

The man in his late fifties did not write the line that night.

He has not written one since.

There have been three Tuesdays since then: the eighteenth of February, the twenty-fifth, and the third of March. On all three of them the cold came at about seven and went at about eight, and on all three of them he got up, made a hot drink, and sat in his own front part, and the book was where it always was.

What he has not done is gone and looked.

He did that once, in 2018, in the first month of writing them down, and he went round the whole of that block at about a quarter past eight on one of them and put his hand on the outside of every door on the way, and on six of them somebody answered, and on four of them nobody did, and on two of them the person who answered said the thing he would have said.

He never did it again. That was the whole of the exercise and it lasted one morning.

On the eighteenth he was up at ten to seven. On the twenty-five he was up at five to seven. On the third of March he was up at ten past eight, which is not a thing he does.

On the Thursday of the fifth he was up at ten past seven as well, and there was nothing in the pipes at all, and he stood in the kitchen for a minute and a half listening to a building that was behaving.

He has been getting up at that hour on three mornings now, and he did not decide to, and he noticed it the way a man notices that he has started carrying something in the other hand.

He did not open the book.

He made the hot drink and he took it to the front part and he sat down and looked at nothing in particular for about a quarter of an hour, and then he got up and went to work, and the book was still where it is and shut.

---

The woman in her early sixties noticed the first of the three, on the evening of the eighteenth, and thought about going up and knocking, and did not.

She has thought about it on every one since.

There are four ways of asking a man about a thing like that and all four of them are worse than not asking. She worked all four of them out on the evening of the eighteenth and put them away and has not taken them out again.

The block is thirty-eight flats and there is no way in it to say to a man: you have written that down for two years and a man came and looked inside something and now you have stopped. There is no sentence for it, and she is a woman who has never once in six years made a sentence she could not finish, and she has not made this one.

On the Thursday she came up at about half past four and knocked on his door and he opened it.

"You were up early," she said.

"I couldn't sleep."

She said, "It's been doing it three weeks now, that." And she said it as a thing she was telling him about the weather, and she was not asking him anything, and she has never once in six years asked him anything.

"I know," he said.

And that is the whole of it. She went back down and he shut the door, and the book is where it always was, and it is shut, and the last line in it is the twelfth of February, and there is a blank after it where there has never been a blank before.

**HE PUT ONE IN EVERY TUESDAY FOR TWO YEARS AND THE COLUMN HAS BEEN BLANK EVER SINCE A MAN OPENED SOMETHING UNDER A WINDOW AND WENT AWAY AGAIN, AND HE HAS NOT TOLD THE ONE PERSON WHO HAS ASKED HIM NOTHING AT ALL.**
