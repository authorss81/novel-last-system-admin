# Chapter 0221 — Out of the Tray

Sunday the seventh of February 2016, from about ten in the morning, a building by the water in Tideglass, and then a corridor on the first floor.

**IT IS NOT IN THE TRAY ANY MORE AND NOBODY PUT IT ANYWHERE, AND A TRAY UNDER A WINDOW IS A TRAY UNDER A WINDOW AND DOES NOT BECOME A REGISTER BY BEING EMPTIED, AND NOBODY IN THIS BOROUGH IS GOING TO BE TOLD WHAT IT IS.**

The building by the water has a porter on the ground floor on a Sunday because the building has a lift in it and somebody has to be in the building when the lift is in it, and the man on the ground floor on the seventh of February was about ninety-two and had been the porter, or one of the porters, since before the war that the notice board in the entrance still advertises, and he was doing a door that had come up in the cold.

He knew Jonas Mercer's face, in the way that a man in a doorway knows anybody who has ever come at him with a question he cannot answer.

"You're not going up for a form," he said.

"No," said Jonas Mercer. "I've come about the thing that was in the tray on the twenty-sixth of December."

The man of about ninety-two put his weight on the door and thought about it properly, which took longer than the question had deserved, and which Jonas Mercer has never been able to stop being glad of.

"That was the water office's," he said. "That came in on the Saturday, that did, and it went in the tray, and that's the tray that's under that window with nothing in it now. And the Monday they came down for the water office's post and they took the tray up with them, both trays, and they go up and they come down again on the Friday, and the water office puts them on the shelf by the filing cabinets."

"Is it still on the shelf?"

"It was still on the shelf on the Thursday before Christmas, because I emptied the tray myself on the Friday and the water office had not touched it, and the second one was in there and there was nothing on top of it, and I remember because the woman from the water office said she'd want it and then she didn't." He shifted the door. "It came off that shelf in the last week of January. Somebody took it off and put it in a drawer, because that's what you do with a thing you don't know, you put it in a drawer, and the drawer's in the room at the end of that corridor and I couldn't tell you the room's name and neither could you, and the woman who has that room is about fifty-five and she is not in on a Sunday, and she is not in on a Sunday for anybody."

**AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAPPENED TO IT, AND IT TOOK ABOUT A MINUTE, AND NONE OF IT WAS DONE BY ANYBODY ON PURPOSE.**

**AND THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE INSIDE THE FRONT DOOR OF THAT BUILDING THAT MORNING, printed and fixed at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A TRAY UNDER A WINDOW IS A TRAY UNDER A WINDOW.**
> **EMPTY IS A THING A TRAY CAN BE.**
> **NEITHER OF THOSE IS A FILE.**

He went up.

The first floor is a corridor with a window at one end and three doors and a fire door, and the room at the end was shut and had a light over it that was off, and there is a slot for post on the door and nothing in the slot, and the whole of what was on the other side of the door was a drawer in a desk and Jonas Mercer did not put his ear to it and did not try the handle, because a door in this city is not a thing to try, and because it would have told him nothing that was not already on the page in front of him.

The corridor was cold and had a smell of floor polish in it and there was about eleven steps of nothing to do.

He had come to that building on the Saturday before the eighteenth of December and had read it, and he had put it in the tray himself, and he had stood at a corner afterwards and worked out that it was a price and it was a receipt and it was a precedent, and that all three of those were true and were not the same shape, and that this borough had no instrument for choosing between them.

Nothing about that had changed. It was a price, because the first thing that comes back along a piece of paper to a city that handed something out for nothing is a bill. It was a receipt, because six people in this city took fourteen days across the water in October, were paid in an envelope before the work, all six came home on the dates on a sheet, one of the six has since had a claim made about him by somebody who did not have to look at him. It was a precedent, because if this borough answers it, then this borough has agreed that the permission runs backwards as well as forwards, and if this borough does not answer it, then a man of about twenty who worked a fortnight at a pumping station and came home has not got anybody.

The only thing that had changed between the eighteenth of December and the seventh of February was that somebody in another city had read it. Nothing else has come back along a document to this borough in nine months. There was a schedule with nine names on it and it went out, and two sheets of paper and they went out in a plastic wallet, and a folded card in a canteen hatch drawer that never left the building, and that is all of it, and none of those three came back.

---

He had had the letter since the Saturday morning, and it had been written the day before in a building eight miles and an estuary away, and he had read it four times, and it was a page and a half in the same typing as the one from the twentieth of November, and the second paragraph was the reason it had been sent at all.

*You have said it more than once, to a room of about nine people, that a claim had come back to your own city under the sixth clause of the arrangement, and I have not asked you about that and I am not going to. I have read it. I have now read it because it is the same reference as mine and I have had mine since the eighteenth of December and I put mine in a folder in a drawer, and I told you on a telephone in December what I would not say, and I am not saying it now.*

*I am telling you that a document has travelled from one city to another without anybody in either of them deciding that it should, and that I do not know how, and that I have decided not to find out, because the two ways I can think of to find out are both worse than not knowing.*

*The fourth paragraph is the one you will want. The water office in your city has had your claim since the twenty-sixth of December and has not answered it and is not going to, because it has no instruction and nobody in it with a department is going to give it one, and I have known that since about the middle of January, and I did not write and tell you, and I am telling you now because you will find out in about six weeks and I would rather you had it now than at the end of it.*

Jonas Mercer had read it four times and had not answered it and was not going to answer it, and he did not know what the fourth paragraph was supposed to change, and he had gone over it the way you go over a sentence in your own handwriting looking for the bit that is in a different ink.

The corridor went on being a corridor.

He stood at the window at the end of it for about twenty minutes.

The tray is under that window downstairs. It is a tray. It is about the width of a folded newspaper and it is a shade off white and it has a chipped corner and it is on a windowsill in a building by the water in this city, and it was empty at about eleven o'clock this morning and it will be empty at eleven o'clock tomorrow morning, and that is the whole of what it is for, and it was not a register on the twenty-sixth of December and it is not one now.

And there is nothing in this borough that could answer that sheet. There are two people in this city with a department over any of it, and one of them made a document in August that is not in force and one of them did not, and neither of them has been asked about a claim in a drawer and neither of them is going to be. There is a woman in another city with a folder in a drawer who told him on a telephone in December that she was not going to say what was in it, and who has since said the same thing at him in a page and a half that is in his inside pocket, and neither of those two is the same as saying it. And there is a man of about twenty somewhere in this city today, doing whatever he does on a Sunday in February, who worked fourteen days at a pumping station on the other side of the water in October and was hurt on the last of them by somebody who did not have to look at him, and who has not been asked anything by anybody in either city.

On the stairs he stopped, because he had come down to ask one more question and had not asked it, and the question was whether the room at the end of that corridor had a window in it, and it had, and he had known that because he had been stood at the other end of the same corridor for twenty minutes, and there was no reason to go back up and no reason not to.

**AND A MAN STOOD AT THE BOTTOM OF A COLD FLIGHT OF STAIRS WITH A PAGE AND A HALF IN HIS INSIDE POCKET THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO ANSWER, AND A DRAWER AT THE OTHER END OF A COLD CORRIDOR WITH SOMEBODY ELSE'S CLAIM IN IT, AND NEITHER OF THOSE THINGS IS GOING TO BE OPENED BY HIM, AND HE WENT DOWN AT HALF PAST ELEVEN AND WALKED OUT PAST THE PARADE.**
