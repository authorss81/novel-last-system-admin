# Chapter 0732 — The Van Doors

Friday the tenth of June 2022, in this borough. She is out with the van from first light until dark, and the whole of it is a back that gets tidied and stays tidy till dark.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FORTIES CLEARS THE BACK OF HER VAN AT THE LOCK-UP, LEAVES THE BOX BEHIND THE BENCH WHERE IT HAS BEEN SINCE OCTOBER, SPEAKS TO A WOMAN WHO BOOKS A CLEARANCE ABOUT A DATE, AND SHUTS THE VAN DOORS BEFORE SHE DRIVES AWAY.**

She is a woman in her late forties at the lock-up on Friday. A woman who books work meets her at the doors near midday and she speaks to her out there.

---

She starts by pulling everything out of the van back onto the ground in the yard, piece by piece.

Boards of her own, shut, with the bolster on top. A bucket. A broom. A sack of torn rags for the tip. She lays it all out in a row and sweeps the van floor clean with the broom and shakes the mat over the fence.

The box rests in the dark behind the bench under rags, where it was put in October. She does not pull it out. She works round it, lifting the rags over it and setting them down again, and it never comes into the light.

What is under rags can stay under rags. She has read its number twice and written it nowhere, and today is not the day she writes it.

---

In the middle of the day a woman who books clearances comes to the doors with a slip in her hand.

"I have a front room for you," the woman says. "Two chairs and a sideboard. Nothing much."

"When?" she says.

"Next week. I will send the address round."

"You always say next week," she says, smiling to take the edge off. "Give me a day and I will be there."

The woman looks at the slip and then at her, and then folds the slip in half.

"Thursday," the woman says. "First light."

"Thursday, then," she says. "I will bring the boards and the van. If there is more than you said, I will bring the lad with the keys as well."

The woman nods and goes, and the slip goes with her in her pocket, and there is now a Thursday with something on it that was empty this morning.

---

She spends the afternoon putting the van back together, choosing what goes back in and what goes to the tip.

The good rags go back in a tied bag. The torn ones go in the sack for the tip. The boards go back under the bench with the bolster on top, shut, the way they have been since the middle of November. The drawer under the bench stays shut with its leads and screws inside, and she does not open it.

She lifts the folded blanket once to get a rag from under it and sets it back without unfolding it, square and flat. Set aside and unopened since November, and still so at dark.

She stands back and looks at the tidy back with everything in its place, and it is the first time in a month the van has looked like her own again.

---

At dark she shuts the van doors one after the other and tries both with both hands.

They stay shut. She walks round the front and gets in with the pad on her knee, and rules a line for Thursday under the cupboard job, with the road still to come, and puts the pad back in the door pocket.

Then she starts up and drives away with the tied bag in the back and the box still behind the bench in the dark of the lock-up, and the Thursday ahead of her with a front room on it, and the hall line still empty on the page before, waiting for a scene she has not yet had.
