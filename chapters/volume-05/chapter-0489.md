# Chapter 0489 — Two Coats on That Hook

Wednesday the first of May 2019, between ten past eight in the morning and about nine that evening. One street, and four houses in it, in Tideglass.

**A MAN STOOD OUTSIDE NUMBER SIXTEEN IN THAT STREET ON THE WEDNESDAY OF THE FIRST OF MAY 2019 FOR ABOUT FOUR MINUTES AND THEN WENT ON UP THE STREET AND DID NOT SAY ANYTHING TO ANYBODY ABOUT WHAT HE HAD WORKED OUT, AND THE WOMAN WHO LIVES AT NUMBER SIXTEEN SAW THE WHOLE OF THOSE FOUR MINUTES FROM HER OWN KITCHEN AND SAID NOTHING, AND IT IS THE EIGHTH TIME IN TEN YEARS THAT SOMEBODY HAS GOT AS FAR AS KNOWING THAT SOMETHING AND NOT AS FAR AS KNOWING WHICH.**

The street is about thirty houses long, all of one age, all built in about 1871, and it has a bus stop at the top of it and nothing at the bottom of it except a turning place.

Number sixteen is the fifth from the bottom on the left.

What he came for was a thing about guttering on number eighteen, which is not his job and which he was asked about by the woman at eighteen in the second week of April, and which took him twenty minutes on the Wednesday morning and which was not the reason he stopped outside number sixteen.

He stopped because of the coats.


There are two coats on the hook inside the open part of number sixteen, and you cannot see the hook from the street, and he could not see the coats.

Here is what he saw, in the order he saw it, and he has been through it in the same order about a dozen times since.

A woman's coat, on the hook, the kind of coat that is worn not put on. He knows that from about twenty minutes of his own life a day.

A man's coat on the back of the seat at the table under the window of that ground-floor front room, which is a thing you put a coat on when you are not going out.

A man's stick in the porch against the mat, which is not a thing you leave if you are out and not a thing you leave if you do not expect to be asked about.

And a man's voice, at about half past eight on a morning in February, from inside that front room, saying something short to somebody who did not answer it, at a volume you only hear if you are standing at that gate.

That is four things over about four minutes and he stopped walking after the third.

He has been building work and odd jobs in this borough for about eighteen years and he knows about seven other houses on that street and he has never once seen anybody at number sixteen who was not the woman.


Here is what he arrived at, inside about four minutes, and it is correct.

There are two people living at number sixteen.

That is all. It is a true thing, and he has not got past it since that morning, and he has not tried to, and there is a reason for that which he has also worked out and which he did not enjoy.

If he worked out who, then he would be a man in a street who knows something about a house that he has no business knowing, and the only two ways of being that man are saying something and saying nothing, and he has spent his whole life learning which of those two he is.

So he has stopped at two.

He has not knocked. In April, when he was in her front room for twenty minutes, he put nothing about number sixteen to her, and nothing about it has passed between them since, and she has put nothing to him.

He has also not asked the woman at number sixteen, which is the one she will care about, and he has not asked her because he could not put the question in a way that was not the other sort of question.

There is no version of *is there somebody else living here* that is not *I have been counting your coats*.

---

What she watched, on the first of May, at about ten past eight, is this.

She was at her own sink. She has a job that starts at a quarter to nine and she was doing what she does before it, which is standing at that sink with the radio off for about twenty minutes.

And there was a man going up that street at a walking pace and he stopped outside her own house.

She knew him. She has known him to say good morning to for about six years and she does not know what he does and does not want to and he has never offered.

She watched him for four minutes without moving.

And here is what she could see from where she was standing, and this is the part she has not got past: he did not look up at her. There is a pane of glass between her and that street and he was outside it for four minutes and he did not look at it once, and she still cannot decide for herself whether that means he never knew she was there, or whether he knew and was keeping his face away from the glass.

And in those four minutes he looked at the porch, and then at the side of the house, and then up at the second storey, and then he took his hands out of his pockets and went on up the street at the same pace he had come at.


What she knows, and has known since 2010, is this.

There is a second person at that house and he is her brother.

He was born in that street and went away at nine and was not back in this borough for about fifty years, and she has not seen him since she was a girl, and he wrote to her in the second week of January 2010 and she drove ninety miles and collected him in about four hours and brought him back and he has been in her back room ever since.

He is not a lodger. He is not a relative to anybody in that street. He has no money of any kind and no work and no papers she has looked at and no reason to be in that borough at all.

And she has never told one person.

Not the woman at number eighteen. Not her own sister. Not the man who does her guttering. Not the woman at number twenty who brings her shopping in when it rains.

That has been ten years and there has not been one conversation about it, and it has not been held by arrangement with anybody, because there is nobody to hold it with.

---

What she has done instead, in nine years, is what she has not done, and it has taken a great deal of doing.

She has not made a space. There is no space at that house. There is a place at that table in the evening and there is a man in it and nobody in that street has said anything, and she has not said anything either.

She has not introduced him and she has not explained him and she has not answered the question nobody has asked.

She has made sure that if anybody ever does ask, it will not be a question that can be asked of her by a stranger, because a stranger can be put off and a neighbour cannot.


And here is the whole of it, and it is the thing she has been able to say to herself since the eighth of May last year without it once being a comfort.

What that man worked out on the first of May is true.

There are two people at number sixteen.

What she has known since 2010 is also true.

There are two people at number sixteen, and one of them is her brother, and she went ninety miles for him, and there is no way of telling anybody that without it becoming a different thing that anybody in that street would have a view about.

The first of those is about a house. The second of those is about her.

She watched him arrive at the first one in four minutes and kept her mouth shut, and the second one she will hand him never, and the first one he has not put to her and will not, and both of those are settled and she has settled nothing else in her life.

**A WOMAN STOOD AT A SINK AT NUMBER SIXTEEN ON THE FIRST OF MAY 2019 AND WATCHED A MAN STAND OUTSIDE HER OWN HOUSE FOR FOUR MINUTES AND THEN WALK ON UP THAT STREET, AND SHE DID NOT MOVE AND SHE DID NOT GO OUT AND SHE DID NOT SAY ANYTHING TO HIM, AND HE DID NOT KNOCK.**
