# Chapter 0277 — Five Doors

Friday the twenty-fifth of November 2016, from about five past twelve in the morning, a first-floor landing in a building by the water in Tideglass, and a shop that shuts early on a parade.

**THERE ARE FIVE DOORS ON THAT LANDING AND ONE OF THEM SHUTS AND ONE OF THEM DOES NOT, AND A MAN WENT ALONG IT WITH FOUR REASONS IN HIS HEAD AND COULD DO NOTHING WITH ANY OF THEM, AND NOBODY JOINED THE TWO UP.**

The building by the water has five doors on the first floor along the front and a fire door at the top of the stair, and the agent who came along that landing on the Friday morning had the company that owns the freehold of it behind him, which is over the water and has been in the same family since before he was born, and which he had started at the property in March.

He had four reasons and they were all in his head. The fire door had been closing too fast since about the summer and had come back on its own hard enough on the Friday to be heard on the landing from the top and the bottom. The stair light was on a fitting that flickered. The letterboxes at that end were the old grey pattern and had been promised new ones in June. And the agent was supposed to look at the fronts of the doors while he was on the landing, and there is no sheet for that and there never has been one, and he had done two of the five before half past ten.

The first one was open and a man stood in it with a newspaper and told him the light had been flickering since the summer and that he had a torch.

The second one was shut and nobody answered, and he knocked twice more and put a card through the letterbox that said the name of the company and a telephone number, and that card went into a flat in this borough that nobody in this borough has ever rung and it is not on anything.

---

The third door is the woman of about fifty-five's, and it is on a month, and she has been in the room since the middle of October, and nobody put her in it and nobody arranged it.

She opened it about four inches and did not come out.

"It's the agent," she said. "For the water."

"I am not anybody's agent," said the man. "I work for them. There's a difference and it's the whole of my week."

The draught off that landing is the worst on the first floor because the window at the end of it has never been painted shut and there is a gap at the top of it about the width of a coin. In the fortnight before this the weather had been cold and still and then on Wednesday night it had gone to a gale, and the wind came down that landing in one long push all afternoon and every Friday and every Saturday for a week after.

While he was standing there talking, the door came back and shut behind her, once, hard enough to move the latch, and she had to put her hand out and lift it and open it again to get her elbow round the frame.

He did not say anything about that. He looked at the door, and he looked at the hasp on the inside of it — an ordinary hasp, a staple, and a screw eye, and the screw eye in the jamb and the staple screwed into the frame and the hasp dropped into it — and he did not ask her about it, and she did not tell him about it, and neither of them said where the hasp came from or who put it on.

"You'll have draught on that," he said.

"I have."

"Can I get somebody to look at the frame?"

"You can," said the woman of about fifty-five. "It's not mine. You'll be asking the wrong people, because it isn't the frame they've had a problem with."

He wrote nothing down, because he had nothing in his hand to write it down on, and he said, "Right," and moved on to the fourth door.

The fourth door was the next one along from hers counting past the fire door, and it was standing open three inches.

It had been shut when he came along the landing. It had not been shut at five past twelve either, and it was not shut at half past, and the woman who was in that room had a chair against it and a draught coming under it that had made a rug on the landing move every time a door opened anywhere on that floor.

He knocked and said his name and the name of the company and asked whether he could look at the front of the door.

"I'd rather you didn't," she said.

He did not go in. He did not ask again and he did not stand in the doorway. He said he was sorry to bother her and that there was nothing in particular, only that he was on the landing and it was a thing you did, and she said, and it is the whole of what she said, "I know it is," and shut the door until it was a hand's width and left it there.

The fifth door was at the end of that landing with that window against it, and the draught came off that end hard enough to lift a corner of a mat, and he shut it before he knocked, and a man in a dressing gown came to it and told him that the company had that window on a list going back before the summer, and that the list had been round about a fortnight, and that he would not stand in his own doorway for the sake of a list. The man said the round was not his, and the man in the dressing gown said that it was nobody's, and shut the door on it, and the draught came the whole length of that landing behind him with the door shut.

He went down the stair with the fire door coming back on him twice on the way, and he had a wedge in his pocket because he had come prepared to put one under it, and he did not put it under it, because a wedge under a fire door is a thing that is somebody's job, and he did not know whose job it was, and there was no body in this borough that could tell him by asking.

The bag is in the back of the shop that shuts early, and it has been in the back of that shop since about the summer, and it has a hasp in it and a staple and a screw eye, and on the Friday before this one the young woman of about twenty went in for a box of tea bags and the woman who runs the shop said, without being asked, that it was still in the bag and still had never been put in anything.

"I've got the fix," said the young woman of about twenty. "I paid for it in October and I've had it in a drawer ever since."

"You've had it in a drawer," said the woman who runs the shop. "It's a bag of post-fix, it's not a boiler. You'll use it when there's a door that's yours. That's all I've got to say about it and I've said it twice now, so shut up about it."

The young woman of about twenty paid for the tea and put the bag of tea on the counter in her other arm and did not say whose door it was, and did not say whether she had a door, and the shutter came down behind her at about ten past one as it does.

And the young woman of about twenty went back up the parade on the far side from the water with the tea in a bag against her chest, and she stopped at the corner by the bank long enough for a bus to come and go the other way, and then she carried on up the bank, and when she got in she put the bag of post-fix in the drawer upstairs on top of a scarf and pushed it to with her hip, and then she had her tea out of the bag standing at the window with the back of her coat still damp.

**AND THE GALE WENT ON UNTIL THE MONDAY, AND THE FIRE DOOR CAME BACK ON ITS OWN ALL DAY AND WAS WEDGED ON THE THURSDAY BY A MAN WHO CAME BACK WITH A DOOR WEDGE OFF A VAN AND PUT IT DOWN WITHOUT ASKING ANYBODY ANYTHING, AND ON THE FRIDAY MORNING THE LANDING WAS QUIET, AND THE WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE CAME OUT OF HER ROOM AT HALF PAST NINE TO GET A BAG FROM THE CORRIDOR OUTSIDE IT AND HELD THE BAG AGAINST HER KNEE WITH ONE HAND WHILE SHE PUT HER KEY IN WITH THE OTHER, BECAUSE THE HANDLE ON HER SIDE STOPS A FOOT SHORT OF THE DOOR WHEN THE SIDE WITH THE HASP IS IN.**
