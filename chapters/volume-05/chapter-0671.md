# Chapter 0671 — Paper Of Her Own

Saturday the second of October 2021, in this borough. She is in her van and in a house with eight boxes to be put back, from morning until dark, and the whole of the day is paper she does not borrow.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FORTIES PRICES A CLEARANCE ON HER OWN PAD ON HER KNEE IN HER VAN ON A SATURDAY, AND THE PAD STAYS IN THE VAN, AND SHE DOES NOT GO BACK FOR ANYTHING.**

She is a woman in her late forties. She is on her own all day. She does not go up that road.

---

She drives past the end of that road on her way to the job without turning in, and the turning goes by on her left with the houses quiet behind it, and she keeps her eyes on the road ahead.

---
She wants to price a clearance in a house on the far side of the ring road without using paper that is not hers, because using what is not hers once cost her a corner taken by tape.

The drawer under the bench on the passenger side is shut, with a bolster off her own board on top of two extension leads and a tin of screws, and it does not rattle.

---

The house has eight boxes to be put back and a hall to be cleared to the loft, and she walks through with the woman who booked it and writes nothing down until she is back in the van.

---
She has learned since June, when she wrote a room-by-room job list on the back of somebody's handwriting without turning it over and taped it inside a cupboard door that is going in a skip, that a woman who does this for a living uses the client's paper or her own, and never the back of another's.

She stands in each room and looks and remembers, back room and hall and box room and bathroom and landing, and she nods and says she will send a figure.

---

In the van she sits sideways on the bench with her pad on her knee and writes the rooms in her own order, which is the order she would do them, and she puts figures against each in pencil.

---
The pad is hers and stays in the van. The pencil is hers and goes back in her coat.

The cuts inside both palms are still pink where the skin pulls, with a plaster on one and not the other, and writing presses the crease and she eases her grip to spare it.

---

Against that is the memory of the gas envelope, six things on the back in pencil with nothing crossed off, face up square with the edge on a kitchen table under a window at number twelve, and the front of it with her own writing and the top corner gone.

---
Neither woman can write on that paper again. She knows that without being told, because tape took the corner on the twenty-second of June and there is no surface left to take ink.

She closes her own pad and puts a band round it, and the band is tight and the pad is shut.

---

At midday she eats bread in the van with the side door open to the yard, and a man from the house next door asks if she wants tea and she says no thank you, she has her own, and he nods and goes back in.

---
She is glad to be a person with her own tea and her own paper and her own drawer shut under a bench.

Four hours of gate work in August is still unpaid with no invoice, and she still thinks it was the right thing, and saying nothing in a kitchen on the sixteenth of July is still the right thing in her head, though it was his sentence and not hers.

---

In the afternoon she puts the eight boxes back herself without a word of the tone in her voice, and she sweeps the hall and stacks the loft ladders by the door for the next visit.

---
She does not ring the man who built the stool about any price, because asking him which side he is on is a bench thing and the bench that agreement was made on no longer exists.

She sends the figure by telephone from the van with the engine running, and the woman who booked it says yes, and that is the whole of it.

---

She drives home the long way round so she does not pass the end of that road twice, and the light goes early now in October and the lamps are on along the ring road.

The shop with the galvanised bucket by the door goes by on her right, the shop with a rack by the window where a man bought nine pounds' worth of brush with his own money, and she does not stop.

---

She parks and carries her bag in and washes at the sink, and the plaster on one palm is soft at the edges and she leaves it, and the other palm is open to the air.

---

She puts the pad on her own table with the band round it, and she will take it back to the van in the morning.

---

**AND A WOMAN IN HER LATE FORTIES HAS GOT THROUGH A SATURDAY WITHOUT WRITING ON ANYTHING THAT IS NOT HERS, AND WITHOUT DRIVING UP A ROAD SHE HAS NO WORK ON, AND A SHUT DRAWER UNDER A BENCH IN HER VAN IS STILL SHUT.**

(End of chapter 0671)
