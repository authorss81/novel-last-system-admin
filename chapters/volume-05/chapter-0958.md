# Chapter 0958 — Beside The Projecting Stack

Thursday the twenty-eighth of November 2024, at the front stack of number nine with the wind off the road and the flags streaming. Beside a stack.

**THE STACK STANDS OFF THE FRONT WALL IT WAS BUILT UP AGAINST BY ABOUT THREE INCHES THE WHOLE WAY DOWN, AND THE HAND GOES UP INTO THE SLOT WITH FOUR FINGERS IN AGAINST THE PLASTER AND A THUMB LAID ACROSS THE OUTSIDE OF THE BRICK, AND THEN THE THUMB IS LIFTED CLEAR OF IT, TO FIND OUT HOW DEEP THAT OPENING IS AND WHAT STANDS AGAINST THE WALL INSIDE IT.**

The stack is the chimney of the front room and it was built long after the house, or the part of it above the first two courses of the existing stack was, and it was built up against the front wall rather than into it, which is what anybody does when the middle of a wall in a house that is already let is out of reach.

There is a slot between the side of that stack and the front wall of the house about three inches wide at the widest place, and it runs the whole height of the stack from the top of the porch roof to the cement fillet at the top of it, and nothing on the front of that house can see a hand's width into it. The stack is nine inches by eleven on plan and it is ordinary soft red brick laid in a Flemish bond with the headers showing, and the brick at the arris of it on the road side has gone pale and has lost its arris along the last three feet, because the road runs along the side of that house and a brick wall standing that close to a road takes everything that comes off it.

A stack is carried out to the face of a wall for one reason and one reason only, and the reason is the flue. The flue in this house has to come out at the front because there are two rooms upstairs and no chimney in either of them, and anyone will do anything to the outside of a house before cutting a hole in a floor that somebody sleeps over.

The mortar in the slot side of that stack is the mortar of the stack, and it is a paler colour than the mortar in the front wall behind it, because the two were mixed by different hands in different years and neither of them knew the other was there. The slot is not as wide as it looks. The plastering inside the front room was done before the stack was built and the plaster carries the footprint of it, so the stack stands off the plaster by about two and a half inches and off the brick behind that by another half.

Where the stack was built up against the wall there are wall ties in it, cast-in wall ties of the twisted sort, and they are in the mortar joints on a spacing of about eighteen inches, and they are what is keeping the two sides of that slot the width it is.

The three bottom courses of that stack are the courses of the chimney that came before it and they are a darker and softer brick than everything above them, because they were fired in a different kiln and they were laid eighty years before the rest of that stack.

---

Up goes the hand into that slot at the height of the porch roof, palm to the wall, thumb laid across the face of the stack, the other four fingers pushed in against the plaster.

Doing it that way the thumb is doing the work. It is the brace, and the hand hangs off it, and the fingers only have to find the plaster inside and can be as wrong as they like about the rest of it. Then the thumb is lifted away from the brick and stays where it is, out in the air, and the whole weight of the hand is on nothing at all, which means it is in the slot.

The hand goes about an inch further in than it went with the thumb on the brick, because with a hand braced you stop when the tip of the thumb says so, and without one you stop when the fingers have nowhere to go. The slot takes the hand as far as the second knuckle and then the fingers are against the back of the wall, and there they stop against plaster that has been there since before the stack was built and is now about half an inch thick over brick that is the same age as the house.

The fingers themselves are against old lime plaster, and plaster of that age is soft enough to take a thumbnail and hard enough to keep the mark for a while afterwards, and there is one in the corner of the slot about two inches down which is nothing to do with anybody and has been there a long time.

The stack has never been repointed on the side that faces the slot, and on the side that faces the road it has been pointed twice with a cement mortar that has gone harder than the lime round it, which is the ordinary way of doing the front of a house and leaving the back of it. Where the fingers are is a wall with a chimney breast behind it, and the brick on the other face of that chimney breast is the same red brick and the same bond and about a hundred and forty years older than the stack the hand is in.

There is a lump of old plaster about the size of a fist on the back of the wall inside that slot, low down where the stack's first course has run into it, and it is hard and it is not loose, and it is in the way of nothing because nothing has ever wanted to be in that slot. Both sides of the slot are dry. The render on the outside of the front wall is gone hard and pale along the arris where it was never taken off the old brick, and it has begun to fall off three joints on the lowest course, and the slot behind it has been shut against that for a hundred and forty years and has nothing in it but air.

A slot like that is stiff rather than tight. Three inches of it is a good deal for a hand that has only to know what is in it, and it stays three inches wide because the stack is stiff enough not to lean on the wall it stands against, and the reason no one has put a hand into one for a hundred and forty years is not that it is difficult and not that it is dangerous, and not anything anybody would put in a letter. The stack above the porch roof carries the weight of nothing but its own bricks and the flue, and the flue is a nine-inch flue and it is in the middle of the stack and about a foot off each side of it, so most of that stack is carrying the flue and not much else.

At the top of the stack there is a cement fillet about two inches thick cast round the four sides and gone hard, and it is the stiffest single object on the whole of that frontage, and it is newer than everything else that is standing on the face of it. The woman who does her shopping has stopped at the far end of the boundary wall where it turns on the strip, on the grass side of it, with her bag hanging against her leg, and she is not going up the path.

She stands there a while with the wind taking the bottom of her coat, and between them they get to the shop at the top of the road, and whether it has moved its hours, and whether it was open at four on a Friday. Every word of that is about opening hours, and the thumb was up in the air again and the hand at her side before she reached the end of the wall. The wall goes on turning at the end of the strip and the stack goes on standing off the front of the house above the porch, and the slot between them is three inches at the widest and a hundred and forty years old at the bottom of it.

A hand can go up into that slot to the second knuckle and stop there without anything at all to make it, and whether the far end of that boundary wall is being turned past at this moment is a different question altogether.
