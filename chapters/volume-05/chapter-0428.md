# Chapter 0428 — Never Been In It

Monday the sixteenth of July 2018, and on four Mondays after it in that month and the next, a counter at the back of a shop off that parade, in Tideglass.

**HE TOLD HER HE HAD NEVER BEEN INSIDE THE BUILDING AT THE TOP OF THAT STREET AND HE HAD, AND HE SAID IT IN THE SAME VOICE HE USES FOR PRICES, AND SHE BELIEVED HIM, AND HE WENT ON SAYING IT FOR FOUR MORE WEEKS AFTER THAT.**

There is a building at the top of a street off that parade, four doors from the end, and it has stood empty since the second week of March.

It is a two-storey building with a shop in the ground floor and two rooms above it, and the shop has a big window with three-quarters of the glass gone, and the rooms above have been cleared. There is a yard at the back of it about thirty foot by twenty, with a boundary along two sides and a wall of another building on the third, and a hardstanding about half of it.

In the second week of April, at about ten in the morning on a day that does not matter, the man in his forties drove his own van into that yard, backed it up to the boundary, put the tailgate down for about twenty minutes, and took something out of it and put it against the wall of that yard and went away.

Nobody saw him. There is no window on that yard from any other building and the shop in the ground floor has no glass in it. Nobody in this borough knows that he was ever in that yard, and he has not told anybody, and he did not tell anybody at the time, and there was no reason that day that made him.

---

On the sixteenth of July, at about half past ten, he came through the front of that shop with a box of screws in his hand, which is a thing he buys most weeks of the year and always from her, because hers are the only ones in this borough that are not too soft.

She bagged them and wrote the figure on a pad and he put the money down, and while she was giving him the change she said, "That thing at the top of that street, do you know anything about it?"

He said, "Which one."

"The one with the glass out of the front," she said. "It's been standing empty since the end of March."

"Never been in it," he said.

"You never been in it?"

"Never been in it."

She said, "Right," and gave him the change and that was the end of it, and she asked him no second question about it, and she has not mentioned that building to him since.

Here is what the sentence was and what it was not.

It was not true. He had been inside that building in the last week of March to look at a thing that was not there any more, and he had been in that yard in the second week of April with his own van and the tailgate down. He knew both of those as well as he knew his own van.

It was also not said to protect anybody. Nobody in that building is at risk from anything and the only person with anything to do with that yard is a woman who owns the freehold and is not speaking to him and has not spoken to him since 2014. He did not say it for the owner, or for a neighbour, or for the man in his thirties, or for himself, or out of any loyalty to anything.

He said it because two men in the trade had asked him about it inside three weeks and he had said no both times, and a man cannot say no three times to a thing that keeps coming back, and what he decided on the Monday, standing at that counter with a bag of screws, was to have it out of his own mouth first, in his own time, in a voice that had nothing behind it.

It is the only sensible way round it and it took him about two seconds and it cost him nothing at all.

The two men who had asked him were not the same man and neither of them was anybody in this borough. One of them was on a roof in the town eight miles off in the second week of July and wanted to know whether that yard had a water supply on it, because it had not got one and he was about to put a scaffold over it. The other was at a kitchen table in a house at the end of a different street with a list of jobs in front of him and a telephone, and he asked it the way people ask a thing they have already decided the answer to. Neither of them had ever seen that yard. Neither of them had any interest in it. They had both asked it because it is a thing a man asks when he is talking.

That is the whole of the danger in that sentence and there is not much of it, and the reason he did not go and stand in that yard again to make the answer true is that he is not a man who goes and makes answers true. He made it true in June 2016, once, about a different thing, and nobody in this borough knows that either, and he has not checked since whether it still is.

---

He said it four more times in the four weeks after that, and he said it the same way every time, at the same speed, with the same face.

On the twenty-third of July, in that shop, when she said that a man had been in the window of that building and looked at the sign, and he said, "That one's been empty since the end of March," and she said, "It must be a bit of a state inside," and he said, "I've never been in it."

On the thirtieth of July, at that same counter, when she asked him whether it would be worth doing anything with a building like that and he said, "There's nothing up there worth the walk," and she said, "You never been up it?" and he said, "Never."

On the sixth of August, in a passage off that parade, to the man in his sixties, who was on his way somewhere and stopped for about two minutes, and who said that a man had been seen in that yard at the top of that street in the last week of July with a white van that had no signs on it, which is true, and who then said, "You'd know that yard, wouldn't you," which was not a question so much as the way that man opens a thing, and the man in his forties said, "No. Never been up it."

And on the thirteenth of August, at that counter again, when she said nothing at all about that building and he said it himself, unprompted, in the middle of buying something else, and then went on and paid for it.

She has never had any reason to doubt him. She has seen him in that shop since 2013. She has seen him go in and out of a dozen buildings a week for five years and has never once had cause to think about where he had been. If somebody in this borough sat her down and said, that man has said to you five times that he has never been inside that building, she would say, well, he knows his own business, and she would be right to say it, and she would be wrong about one thing, and she will never find out which.

The thing about a sentence that is not true is that it does not stay in one place. He said it once and it was a thing he had said. He has said it five times and it is now a fact about him, in the way that a thing a man has said about himself four times is a fact about him, and it is going to be there at the end of this year and next year, and the only person who could take it back is him, and he is not going to, and there is no mechanism anywhere in this borough by which taking it back would occur to anybody.

Nothing in this borough has corrected him. Nobody has corrected him because nobody has anything to correct. A man in his sixties has asked that question of four people in three weeks and got the same answer from three of them, and one of those three is a man who was in that yard in April with his own back to a building with no glass in it, and the man in his sixties counted them as three, which is what everybody would count.

He has not said one word of this to any person in this borough, and no person in this borough has asked him about it, and the sentence is not a thing anybody in this borough is owed the truth about, because a thing that is not true and has not caused one single thing to any single person is not a lie that anybody has to be told about.

**HE SAID IT AT THAT COUNTER ON THE THIRTEENTH OF AUGUST WHEN NOBODY HAD ASKED HIM ANYTHING, AND SHE PUT HIS CHANGE DOWN AND SAID NOTHING, AND THAT IS THE FIFTH TIME.**
