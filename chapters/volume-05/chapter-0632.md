# Chapter 0632 — The Woman Who Went Up The Hill

Saturday the third of April 2021. She is on a bus at about a quarter past nine in the morning in this borough and she is on the same bus back at about half past two in the afternoon, and the whole of it is one old man and one question she did not think she was going to have to ask twice.

**A WOMAN HAS HEARD THE FIVE WORDS FROM TWO PEOPLE WHO EACH SAID THEY GOT THEM OFF A MAN AT NUMBER FOUR ON A ROAD AT THE TOP OF THE HILL, AND SHE HAS DECIDED TO GO UP THERE AND ASK HIM WHERE HE GOT THEM, AND HE GIVES HER A REASON FOR HAVING SAID THEM AT ALL AND SHE COULD NOT MAKE IT SMALLER THAN IT WAS ON THE WAY DOWN, AND THE ONLY THING HE WILL TELL HER IS THAT ANOTHER MAN SAID THEM FIRST, AND THAT MAN WILL NOT SAY WHERE HE GOT THEM.**

She is a woman in her mid fifties. He is a man in his early seventies. She has known him to nod at for as long as she has been on that road and has never been inside his house and has never wanted to and has wanted to since the Friday.

She decided on Friday night late and she got on this bus because it was the only one and because going at a quarter past nine on a Saturday morning is the most you can do about a thing like that.

---

Here is what she had before she got on the bus, and it is short.

One: a man in a van went round the estate on the Tuesday last week and told everybody who would open a door that five words came off him.

Two: she heard that from two women at the depot on the Friday over a pallet of nothing, and both of them said the same thing, and both of them said *we had it off your number four*, and both of them said it in the voice of a woman reporting something she is sure of.

Three: she asked one of them who at number four, and the woman said *the old one*, and she said *what's his name*, and the woman said she didn't know, and it is a road of houses and everybody knows which house that is.

Four: and this is the part she had not got before Friday and got on Friday, which is that the man in the van said he could not say where he got them, and a man who goes round four streets saying a thing came off him and then will not say where from is not a man trying to protect anybody's name.

He is a man trying to keep it going.

That is what she thought at about ten past nine on this bus and she has not been able to put it down since, and it is what made her get on.

She got to number four at about ten past ten and she knocked, and it took a while, and when he opened there was a radio on behind him and he said, "It's not the gas."

And she said, "I know it's not the gas. I'm at number nineteen."

And he said, "You're at nineteen?"

And she said, "I'm at nineteen and I've come up here to ask you something and it's daft."

And he said, "Ask."

And she said, "Are you the man everybody's talking about?"

And he said, "Which man."

And she said, "The five words."

And he said, "Oh, them."

And he said, "Come in."

She did not go in and she has written that down twice since, on the back of a bus ticket, on the bus going down.

He stood in the doorway with the radio going and he asked her about three things and she answered two of them wrong.

And he said, "You've come up here to ask me where I got them."

And she said, "Yes."

And he said, "Why."

And she said, "Because the man in the van said they came off him and won't say where he got them, and two women at work said they came off you, and there's a thing at number thirty-one that's got the same five words said about her and nobody can say who's saying it."

And he said, "Number thirty-one."

And she said, "Yes."

And he said, "Girl?"

And she said, "Yes."

And he said, "Ay."

And she said, "You know her?"

And he said, "I don't know her. She's the one with the light on in the back at about that hour and I have looked at it more times than he would care to count and never once seen her."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "Are you one of them?"

And she said, "Am I one of what?"

And he said, "One that says it."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "Are you sure?"

And she said, "I've said it about three people since February and I gave up almost straight away. It's the reason I came up here, actually."

And he said, "Why's it the reason?"

And she said, "Because I wanted to know where I got it, and I've carried it about since the middle of February and it's the only five words in this borough that everybody has got."

And he said, "Come in."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "Why not."

And she said, "Because if I come in you'll give me a cup of tea and we'll be here till dark."

And he said, "That's about right."

And she said, "I'll stand."

Here is what he said about why, and she had it on the bus going down and she has been over it since and she has not managed to make it smaller than it is.

And she said, "Why do you say it."

And he said, "Because it gives a shape to somebody."

And she said, "That isn't an answer."

And he said, "It is where I'm from."

And she said, "Where are you from."

And he said, "A village with a pub in it and a bus that came twice a day."

And she said, "Was it there."

And he said, "It was everywhere. It's a thing people say when they've got nothing. You get on a bus with no money and you've got to say something."

And she said, "And you've brought it here."

And he said, "I haven't brought it. It came in my mouth on a bus and it got out on a bus and it did not have an address on it."

And she said, "You know where you got it from. The man in the van says he won't say and that's true. But the two women said they got it off you and that could be a mistake, and if it's a mistake then it started with him, and if it's not a mistake then it started with somebody before him, and you've been on this road as long as I have."

And he said, "About that."

And she said, "Is it true?"

And he said, "I've never said it."

And she said, "You say it about nine people a week. I've counted from the bus stop. You said it to the postman and to a woman with a pram and to the man who runs the shop."

And he said, "That's about right."

And she said, "You've never once said where you got it."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Do you know?"

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Then why won't you say?"

And he said, "It's not that I won't."

And she said, "What is it then?"

And he said, "There was a woman on number twenty-six who had my curtains for years and I have not seen her since about Christmas, and she never asked me for anything the whole time, and when she went she didn't say why, and I've been saying it about people ever since and I'd like you to tell me what else I've got."

And she said, "Oh."

And he said, "That's a true answer and it's got nothing to do with your question."

And she said, "It's got nothing to do with it at all."

And he said, "I know."

And she said, "Then don't tell me."

And he said, "You asked me why I say it."

And she said, "I did."

And he said, "That'll do."

And then she asked him where he got it, which is what she came for, and she had it ready and it was the only thing she came for.

And she said, "Somebody said it first."

And he said, "Ay."

And she said, "The man in the van said they came off him."

And he said, "They do."

And she said, "How long have you said them?"

And he said, "Since before Christmas."

And she said, "And he said them since the middle of February."

And he said, "Then it can't have come off him unless I got it from somewhere in between, and I haven't, and I know he won't believe that."

And she said, "So you got them off him."

And he said, "Yes."

And she said, "And he'll not say where he got them."

And he said, "He told you that?"

And she said, "Everybody has told me that."

And he said, "Well, that is a thing about him and not a thing about me, and if you want the end of this then you will have to go and ask him, and I can tell you now that he will not tell you, and he told me the same thing on the Tuesday and I did not ask him where he got them, and now I've told you and it's still not any further along."

And she said, "So where does it start?"

And he said, "It doesn't start. That's what I'm trying to tell you. There's no start. It came off a fella in a doorway and he got it off somebody and the somebody is not going to say, and it is not a thing with a beginning, it is a thing that's gone round four streets and nobody put it there on purpose."

And she said, "One of them did."

And he said, "Did who?"

And she said, "Put it there on purpose. I don't know who."

And he said, "You don't know that."

And she said, "I've got a feeling."

And he said, "That's the worst thing you can have about a thing like this and I'll tell you why. If somebody put it there on purpose then there's a man in this borough who did it to somebody, and it might be a man on a doorstep or a man in a kitchen and you'll never get him, and you'll spend the rest of your life on it and it will not get better. If nobody put it there on purpose then it is a thing people say, and the only thing to be done with it is to stop saying it and not to say it about anybody with a house on it."

And she said, "That's what I've done."

And he said, "Then you're the only one."

She got the bus down at about half past two.

And on it she wrote out four things and she has them now and she has read them over more than a dozen times.

One: a man in a van, mid fifties, who went round four streets on the Tuesday and would not say where he got them.

Two: an old man at number four who has had them since before Christmas and who has never once said where he got them either, and who gave her a woman and a set of curtains and years of it, and told her it was nothing to do with her question.

Three: whoever put them there, if anybody did, and she cannot put a street on it and she cannot put a floor in a house on it, and she cannot put a Monday on it.

Four: and this is the one she is not going to put in writing and is putting in writing: she is going to go up there this afternoon and tell the girl at number thirty-one that the man in the van did not start it and that an old man at number four said it first.

And she decided that on the bus and she has been carrying it since.

---

She did not do it.

She went down the hill and she got off at the parade and she had a cup of tea standing up and she went home and she put her bag down and she stood in her own kitchen for a bit.

And here is what she found, and she has found it properly and she has not gone round the houses to look.

The girl at number thirty-one does not need her to say it. That is the finding and it took her in her own kitchen without sitting down and she could have had it on Friday night on this bus and did not.

Because the man in the van said them to the girl on her own step, out loud, as an example. He said *I've said it about six people* and *once about me*, and the girl heard them the same way she heard them from the woman at the depot.

So she has been carrying a fact since Friday that is worth nothing at the other end, because the girl at number thirty-one has got both halves of it already and got them first, and she got them from the man in the van.

And there is not one thing she can take up there that the girl does not have.

And the man in the van has told a girl that they came off him, and the old man has told a woman on a doorstep that they came off him, and neither of them will go any further, and the only person who knows that both of them have said that is a woman in her mid fifties at number nineteen who is going to carry it on her own.

She is not going to say she does not know either. She has thought about that as well, and she has decided that she is not going to go round the estate saying there's no way of finding out where it came from, because she has found out a bit of it, and if she gives that up then she is doing the same thing the man in the van did, which is keeping a thing back so that it keeps its shape.

**SHE WENT UP THE HILL TO ASK AN OLD MAN WHERE FIVE WORDS CAME FROM AND HE GAVE HER A SET OF CURTAINS AND A WOMAN AT NUMBER TWENTY-SIX AND THEN TOLD HER THEY CAME OFF A MAN WHO WILL NOT SAY WHERE HE GOT THEM, AND SHE HAS SPENT THE AFTERNOON CARRYING TWO HANDS THAT WILL NOT JOIN UP, AND SHE HAS NOT GONE BACK UP THERE TO TELL A GIRL WHAT SHE KNOWS, BECAUSE THE GIRL WAS TOLD IT FIRST.**
