# Chapter 0519 — The Hat on the Hook

The Tuesday of the seventeenth of September 2019, the hours between about six in the evening and about nine. One lane in this borough, in Tideglass.

**EVERY WEDNESDAY EVENING SINCE 2012 A WOMAN HAS CARRIED A FELT HAT OFF A WALL IN THAT LANE AND HUNG IT ON A HOOK IN HER OWN PORCH, AND IN MAY A MAN AT NUMBER NINETEEN PUT IT FOUR YARDS FURTHER UP THE SAME LANE AND LEFT IT THERE. FOR FIFTEEN WEEKS SHE TOOK IT BACK DOWN ON WEDNESDAYS AND SAID NOTHING. ON THE FOURTH OF SEPTEMBER SHE DECIDED BY HERSELF THAT SHE WAS NOT GOING TO KEEP WAITING, AND SHE HAS TOLD NOBODY, AND SHE IS NOT GOING TO.**

The lane is about ninety yards long and there are a dozen houses in it and the far end is where the houses stop and the rest is a bank and a field and a place where three vehicles come in and turn about.

Number twelve is on the north side. Number sixteen is on the south side, opposite, and four yards up the lane from number twelve on the same north side is number fourteen, and there is a low wall outside number fourteen with nothing on it, and there has been nothing on it since 2012.

---

Here is the thing about the hat.

It is a felt hat. It is grey and it is older than it should be and it has a dent in the crown that has been in it since before the woman at number twelve ever picked it up.

The man who wears it is a man of about seventy and he lives at number thirty-eight, which is not in the lane, and he has a dog, and he walks the dog on a Wednesday at about half past five, and before he had the dog he walked the same way at the same hour with a stick.

The hat comes off his head at number thirty, which is where he gets the bus, and it comes off his head again about thirty yards further up the lane, and it does not go back on, and it has not gone back on since 2012.

He has never mentioned it. He does not know it is done. He puts his hands in his pockets and walks the rest of the way up the lane with his head bare and gets the bus at the end and has got the bus for seven years and the driver has never once said anything about it.

The woman at number twelve found it the first time in the September of 2012 and she picked it up because it was going to be down the runnel and she took it inside and put it on the hook in her porch and thought nothing further about it.

On the Wednesday it was on the wall again by about eight in the evening. She took it in again.

On the Wednesday after that it was on the wall, and she took it in, and the wall was outside her own house, which is where she picked it up, so the putting it on the hook cost her a walk out of her own front and nothing else.

And that is the whole of what she has done for seven years: pick it up off the wall outside her own house on a Wednesday evening and hang it on a hook in her own porch.

She has never spoken to the man about it. He has never noticed. On the two or three occasions when the weather has been bad enough that he has not gone up the lane at all, she has gone out on the Friday and taken the wall and put the hat in her own porch, and on the Wednesday after that she has found it on the wall again with no explanation.

Here is the part of it that other people in that lane know about, and it is one man.

The man at number nineteen is a man in his middle years and has lived there since 2014 and he is not a person who is liked and is not a person who is disliked. He has a job that takes him out most evenings and he is in that lane at about seven on a Wednesday most weeks.

On the twenty-second of May this year he came into the lane at about ten past seven and saw the hat on the wall outside number twelve, and he stopped, and he picked it up, and he carried it four yards up the lane and put it on the wall outside number fourteen.

He has not touched it since. Nobody has seen him touch it since.

And the reason he moved it, as far as anybody in that lane is concerned, is no reason anybody has ever established. He does not know the woman at number twelve. He has never spoken to her. She has never spoken to him.

She found it on the Wednesday evening of the twenty-second of May at about half past seven and she saw that it had been moved and she stood at her own front with it in her hand for a moment, and then she put it on the hook in her porch, and she shut her own front, and she said nothing to anybody.

And then she did the same thing for fourteen more Wednesdays.

That is the part that is hers and not anybody's. Every Wednesday evening for fifteen weeks she picked that hat up off a wall four yards from her own front and carried it to her own porch and hung it on a hook, and she did it knowing that it was there because a man at number nineteen had put it there, and she did it in order to see what he would do.

And what he did was nothing, for fifteen weeks, and he is not a man who was planning anything, and there was no test being run. She knows that. She worked that out somewhere around the fourth week and it did not stop her going out on the Wednesday.

On the fourth of September, at about half past seven in the evening, she picked the hat up off the wall outside number fourteen and carried it four yards and hung it on the hook in her own porch.

And she has not said one word to anybody, and she is not going to, and the man at number nineteen has walked past that hook twice since, on the Friday and on the Saturday, and stopped neither time.

Here is the whole of the seventeenth of September.

It rained from about four in the afternoon. It rained hard enough that the woman at number twelve brought her washing in and did not go out, and she had a meal and did the things she does on a wet Tuesday evening, and at about ten past six she looked out and it was still coming and the wall outside number fourteen was black with it.

And she went out and got the hat off the wall and hung it on the hook inside the porch, and shut the door, and that was that.

That is what she has done for seven years, and the one thing that is different about it on the seventeenth of September is the day of the week.

For seven years that hook has had that hat on it on a Wednesday night and on no other night at all. By seven o'clock on a Thursday morning it is empty, because the man at number thirty-eight comes up that lane at half past five and takes it off the hook and puts it on his head and goes on to the bus, and that has been the arrangement between a hook and a man since 2012.

She has never once seen him do it. She has only ever seen the result of it, which is that the hook is empty and the hat is on the wall outside number fourteen the next Wednesday evening.

On the seventeenth of September the day was wrong for it, and she put that hat on that hook at ten past six in the evening, and there was nothing in the whole of the arrangement of seven years that told her it was the wrong evening, and she did it anyway because it was raining and because she did not want it going into the runnel.

The man at number nineteen came up the lane at about seven.

He had his hand in his pocket for his own and he was going in at number nineteen, and he got as far as number twelve and stopped, and looked at the hat on that hook, and stood there in the rain for the best part of a minute, and then went on four yards and went in at his own front and shut it.

He did not go and knock on number twelve. He did not go and move the hat. He did not put it back on the wall outside number fourteen where it had been since the twenty-second of May, and he did not carry it down to the wall by number twelve, and he did not do the one obvious thing a man does when he finds out that a woman in a lane has been quietly undoing his work for sixteen weeks.

He went in and shut his own front and stood just inside it and did not put his bag down for a minute.

And the woman at number twelve was at her own front with her own lights on and she saw him stop.

She saw him stop and she saw him not come across and she saw him go in, and she stood at her own front for a while after that, and then she went and had a look at the hat on the hook.

It was where she had put it, and it was dry, and the fitting over the hook was burning away, and it had been burning since the eighteenth of March when the timer in that porch was changed by a man from a firm in the town, and nobody has ever been able to find out why that porch is on a separate supply.

She stood in her own front and listened to the rain on the runnel and she thought: it will be on that wall again on Wednesday.

**THE HOOK AT NUMBER TWELVE HAS HAD THAT HAT ON IT ON A WEDNESDAY NIGHT AND ON NO OTHER NIGHT SINCE 2012, AND ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF SEPTEMBER SHE PUT IT THERE ON THE WRONG DAY OF THE WEEK BECAUSE IT WAS RAINING, AND A MAN WALKING PAST AT SEVEN STOPPED AND LOOKED AT IT FOR THE BEST PART OF A MINUTE AND WENT ON AND IN, AND SHE STOOD AT HER OWN FRONT AND THOUGHT IT WOULD BE BACK ON THAT WALL ON WEDNESDAY.**
