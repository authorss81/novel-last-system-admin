# Chapter 0213 — The Claim

Saturday the twenty-sixth of December 2015, from about half past ten in the morning, a building by the water in Tideglass.

**A CLAIM CAME BACK TO THE CITY THAT GRANTED THE PERMISSION, IT IS CORRECT, IT ARRIVED IN AN ORDINARY WAY, IT IS ABOUT MONEY, AND THE CLAUSE THAT SENT IT IS THE SIXTH, AND NOBODY IN THIS BOROUGH CAN TELL WHETHER IT IS A PRICE, A RECEIPT OR A PRECEDENT.**

He had come in on the Saturday for the second time since the eleventh of November, and for the same reason and with the same result, which is that you cannot tell by looking at a book twice.

The book is on the table by the wall. It is the same book. The column is the same column and the heading is the same heading and there is nothing added to it, because there is nothing in this city that would add anything to it, and the last entry in it is the fourteenth of September, which is the last time anybody was called on, and it is the same handwriting it was on the eleventh of November, and it is the same length, and there is nothing at the end of it.

Nina Calder had a cup of tea and a newspaper and had told him on the phone on the Thursday that there was nothing to see, and he had come anyway, and she had not said anything about that when he came in except to say that the door sticks.

At about eleven a woman from the sorting office came in with a bundle and put it on the desk and went out again, and Nina Calder signed for the bundle and put the whole of it in the tray under the window, which is a tray, and which is not a register, and which nobody in this building has ever put anything in that mattered.

Then she read the top one, which took her about two seconds, and then she read it again, which took longer, and then she put it down on the desk face up and did not put it in the tray.

"Have you got a minute," she said, which is a thing Nina Calder has never said to Jonas Mercer in this building.

"That's not mine," he said.

"It's got the water office at the bottom of it and it's got the reference on it that a woman from Alderwick read out in this city on the thirtieth of September." She pushed it about six inches towards him and put her hands in her lap. "It's for the water office and the water office is two floors up and they've gone, and whoever signs for the water office comes down on a Monday, and I'm not going up two floors with it on a Saturday. Read it and then tell me what you'd like me to do with it, and whatever you say I'll do, and if you say nothing I'll put it in the tray, and I want that on the record, except that there is no record, which is the other half of what I am saying."

He read it.

It is one sheet. It is dated the eleventh of December and it carries a reference that is the same six characters as the reference on the permission, and a heading, and the heading says it is a claim made under the mutual-aid permission of the thirtieth of September 2015 and that it is made in the city in which the work was done. It names one man, who is about twenty, and it says he was employed for fourteen days in October under that permission at a pumping station on the north bank at Alderwick, and that on the last day of that fortnight he was injured, and that the injury is described in two lines, and that a sum is claimed.

The sum is in the middle of the page. It is not a large sum. It is about what a man of that age costs to keep for about a month, which is to say it is not a number that is going to make anybody's hair stand up, and Jonas Mercer has been in this borough a year and seven months and knows what a number that small means when it arrives on a piece of paper from another city.

The last paragraph asks the authority that granted the permission to provide the sum.

The permission does the rest of it, and the permission is the sixth clause, and Jonas Mercer has read the sixth clause about nine times and it says that a claim arising out of work done under the permission is made in the city where the work was done and not in the city the person came from.

The claim is made in Alderwick. The claim is correctly made. The claim is, on its face, in the one city in the world where it can be dealt with, and it has come to Tideglass, to a records desk in a building by the water, and it is addressed to the water office, and the water office is two floors up and has gone for Christmas.

He read it a second time. Then he put it down on the desk, squared up with the edge, which he did not have to do and did.

"That's the second one," said Nina Calder.

"Is it."

"I don't know what a first one is. I've had two letters this week with that reference on them and one of them was for a supplier and one of them was for you." She picked her newspaper up again. "What would you like me to do."

"I would like you to put it in the tray."

"That's what I do with the ones that come in on a Saturday. It goes down for the Monday and whoever signs for the water office takes it up and it goes in a pile, and it will be in a pile, and the pile is not anywhere anybody looks, and I have not got a way of telling you that that is what will happen, because I could be wrong, and the woman from the water office could come down and take it upstairs herself on the Monday and do something perfectly good with it."

"Put it in the tray," said Jonas Mercer.

She put it in the tray.

**AND HE KNEW WHAT IT WAS, AND HE DID NOT SAY SO, AND HE DID NOT GO AND FIND THE MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY, AND HE WENT OUT AT ABOUT HALF PAST ELEVEN AND WALKED TO THE CORNER AND STOOD THERE.**

It is a price, in that the first thing to come back along a piece of paper to a city that handed something out in September is a bill, and everything this borough has been living on for nine months was handed out for nothing, and there is a sum of money on a piece of paper in a tray downstairs and it is a sum of money that exists because of the thing that was handed out.

It is a receipt, in that there are six people in this city who took fourteen days of work across an estuary in October and were paid in an envelope before the work, and all six of them came home on the dates on a sheet, and one of the six has since had a claim made about him, and there is nothing anywhere in this borough that could tell those two things apart.

It is a precedent, in that it is the first one, and it says the sum is asked of the authority that granted the permission, and if this borough answers it then it has agreed that the permission runs backwards as well as forwards, and if this borough does not answer it then a man of about twenty who worked a fortnight at a pumping station and came home has not got anybody.

All three of those are true and they are not the same shape and this borough has no instrument for choosing between them, because a body that cannot require anybody to do anything cannot be told what it will do, and because the two people in this city with a department over any of this are not going to write anything down this Christmas, and because the woman in another city has one of her own and is not going to be asked by anybody to put hers next to this one.

He stood at the corner for about ten minutes and then went and had a coffee and read the paper and thought about a man of about twenty who is in this city somewhere, doing whatever he does on a Saturday in December, with a hurt that was described in two lines by somebody who did not have to look at him.

---

On the Saturday afternoon he telephoned the works in Ravensmere because there was one thing he could do with a telephone and he did it.

A woman answered and put him through to Iselin Krogh, who said she was in on a Saturday about twice a month and this was one of them.

"I've had a letter," she said, before he had got to anything. "Same reference as the one about the arrangements. It came on the eighteenth. I have not done anything with it because I have not got a person to hand it to, and it is not a minute and it is not in a minute, and I have put it in a folder with the other papers about the arrangement, which is a folder in a drawer, and I know what a folder in a drawer is."

"What is it," said Jonas Mercer.

"I am not going to say that on a telephone," said Iselin Krogh. "You have not told me what yours is."

He had not. There was a tray downstairs with a sheet of paper in it and a records supervisor who had gone home at half past four, and there was a man of about twenty in this city who had been hurt on the last day of a fortnight in October and had not been asked anything then and had not been asked anything now, and there was a clause in a document signed at both ends that said where a claim is made, and Jonas Mercer had read it and had not written it down and was not going to.

"I've had a letter as well," he said.

"I did not think you had not," said Iselin Krogh.

Neither of them said what either of them had, and the call went on for about another minute with nothing in it, and then she said that there was a boat at four o'clock on the Monday and that she would be glad to know if anybody was on it, and he said there were three of them already over there and that he was coming across on the Sunday morning and would be the fourth, and she said right, and that was all.

**AND BY THE TIME SOMEBODY ELSE KNEW WHAT IT WAS THERE WERE TWO OF THEM IN TWO CITIES, AND THERE IS NO DOCUMENT IN EITHER OF THOSE TWO CITIES THAT SAYS THAT EITHER OF THEM KNEW, AND THE CLAUSE THAT DID IT IS THE SIXTH ONE, AND IT IS CORRECT, AND IT IS CORRECT THE WAY ALL SEVEN CLAUSES OF THAT PERMISSION ARE CORRECT.**

The man of about twenty was at a football match in this city on the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of December, and a good eight hundred other people were, and one of them was him.
