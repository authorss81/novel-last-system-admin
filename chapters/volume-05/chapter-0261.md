# Chapter 0261 — Who Said It

Thursday the first of September 2016, from about half past seven in the evening, the Methodist hall on Carrow Lane in Tideglass.

**HE MADE AN ARGUMENT IN APRIL THAT HE HAS NEVER BEEN ABLE TO UNMAKE, AND ON THE FIRST OF SEPTEMBER SOMEBODY SAID IT IN THAT ROOM AS IF IT HAD BEEN HIS, AND HE DID NOT SAY IT WAS HIS AND HE DID NOT SAY IT WAS NOT HIS, AND THE REASON IT DOES NOT WORK WAS SAID BY A THIRD PERSON AND NOT BY HIM.**

About nine people came, and the gas was on because the woman who has been in that hall about two years puts money in that meter on the Friday, and the man from the parade had not been in that hall since the fourteenth of July and had been in the other one twice since.

The man in his sixties was sitting down, which is unusual. The man of about eighteen was at the side with his cardigan over the back of a chair. The woman of about fifty-five was in, with her handbag, which is where the two pieces of A4 live now that the corridor is being emptied in a fortnight. The young woman of about twenty. Mrs Obi with the pad shut on her knee. Two others. And a man in his forties who came for the last hour and brought nothing.

They did not do the six. Somebody started to about half past eight and the man in his sixties put the one sentence he has got about that, which he said in July, and four people laughed, and that was the end of it.

What they did for the first half hour was the price of a roof, because the roof in Corrance Lane is still off, and a question about whether the woman of about fifty-five could keep the two sheets, which she can and which is the whole of it, and a man who wanted to know whether anybody in this room had ever had a firm quote for a job and got one, and had not.

Then the man from the parade put his hand up and asked the room a question of his own.

"There's three pieces of paper, isn't there," he said. "That's what I've been getting at all summer. A permission, which is a sheet on a wall saying a person may do it. An obligation, which is a sheet on a wall saying a person has to do it. And the middle one, which is the one somebody was actually handed. And that's the one nobody's got, and that's the one that makes any difference, because if the middle one turned up, the other two wouldn't be a puzzle, they'd be a pair of ends, and you'd know which end you were holding and when it changed, and three of the six things you people keep asking each other would fall over at the same time."

He said it in his own voice, quickly, as a thing he had worked out, and he looked round the room while he said it the way a man does when he thinks he has got something.

Nobody in that room had heard him say it in a room in April. He had heard it in a corridor on Carrow Lane in the middle of July and had not been told whose it was, and had assumed that a thing said in a corridor in July came out of somebody who had earned it.

---

Mrs Obi did not say anything for about ten seconds.

"That came out of a man in a room over the road on the fourth of April," she said. "I shut it that night and I shut it again in July and I said in July that if it were raised again the person who raised it would be asked to leave, and I am not going to take anything back from what I said in July, and I want the room to hear me not take it back. That sentence still stands. It stands against every mouth in this borough including mine."

"Right," said the man from the parade.

"I'm not finished. I am not asking you to leave, and here is why, and it is the reason and not a kindness." She put both hands on the table over the shut pad. "I have shut that argument once. I shut it off one man, on one night, in one room. If I shut it off everybody who says it, then I have built the thing I shut it to stop, and I would rather have a false thing said out loud in this hall twice than have a thing in this borough where nobody can say it and everybody can repeat it, and I would rather that than be right about a room. I am not going to be right about a room. It is not available."

"And you shut it because it was wrong," said the man in his forties.

"I shut it because it rests on a piece of paper that nobody in this borough could put on a table, and everybody in that room had heard him say so twice, and if it were raised a third time by anybody then the person raising it would be standing in a room making a claim on the strength of a thing they had not seen." She looked along the table. "That is still why."

---

The woman of about fifty-five put her handbag on the floor under the chair and did not open it.

"Can I say the part that's actually wrong with it," she said, "since nobody else is going to."

The room let her.

"It doesn't need a third paper in this borough. It needs a third paper anywhere, and there isn't one, and there has never been one, and it isn't lost. It was never anything to be lost." She had her hands flat on the table now too, and she was not looking at anybody. "There is a sheet on a wall in a corridor in a city on the other side of that water, and it is a sheet. And in this borough, in a bag under that chair, I have got two pieces of A4 that are copies of the other two, and they are copies, and I have said so in this room in July, and I am not going to have them described as anything else tonight. A copy is not a wall. That is not a small distinction and I would like it noticed that I have been making it since April and nobody has ever asked me what the difference is for."

"It is the difference between the argument and the proof," said the young woman of about twenty.

"It is," said the woman of about fifty-five. "And the man in his sixties said in May that he could not unmake it, and that is not a thing anybody can do with an argument that is his own either. It's a thing you can only do by producing the middle of it, and the middle of it does not exist, and it never has, and I have been the person going round this borough with two photocopies in a handbag believing for about five months that they were the middle of something."

Nobody said anything to that for a while, and the gas ticked, and it is the same gas.

The man in his sixties was the only one who moved. He had his forearms on the table and he turned his head towards the man from the parade, and he said:

"You want to know whether I said that tonight. I made an argument in April and I have not been able to take it back and I told this room in May that I couldn't. So it is mine in the sense that it came out of my head in a room in April. It is not mine in the sense that it came out of your mouth tonight, and I'm not going to stand here and take a mouth that isn't mine, and I'm not going to stand here and say it didn't come from my head either, because it did. You've got the only version of it anybody is ever going to get, and it is two true halves, and I'd rather you had both than either."

"You could have said that in July," said the man from the parade.

"I could have said it in July," said the man in his sixties, "and in July I said nothing, and I have not got a better reason for July than I have got for tonight, and I am not going to manufacture one now to be tidier than July was."

At about ten the man from the parade asked whether anybody was going to write any of it down and was told there was nothing on the table to write it on, and nobody had brought anything, and that has been true of every one of these rooms since the first of them.

At about twenty to ten the man in his sixties said the other thing that was in the room, and he said it to nobody in particular and to the man of about eighteen in particular, and it was the only thing he said all evening that was not about a mouth.

"He asked it in June," he said. "And nobody has answered it, and there is not going to be an answer in this hall tonight, and there was not going to be an answer in April. There is no such thing as a hall answering it. I have watched three of you try, in three different rooms, with the same result every time, and the result is a room that has been together on a Monday. He is the only person in this borough who asked it out loud and he is on nothing and he will be on nothing, and I say that to him and not to you, and he will not be able to do one single thing with it, and I do not know one single thing to suggest, and I have looked."

"I know," said the man of about eighteen.

**AND AT TEN PAST TEN THEY PUT THE CHAIRS UP, AND THE MAN FROM THE PARADE STAYED AND DID FOUR OF THEM, AND THE MAN IN HIS SIXTIES CARRIED HIS OWN OUT AND PUT IT ON THE STEP, WHICH IS WHERE HE PUTS IT, AND THE MAN FROM THE PARADE TOOK THE LAST CHAIR AND THE FRONT DOOR AND WENT OUT AND SAID GOODNIGHT TO A STREET THAT DID NOT ANSWER.**
