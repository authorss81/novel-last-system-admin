# Chapter 0773 — The Gate On The Other Side

Sunday the twentieth of November 2022, in this borough. She is in her own house at number nine from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a gate hung the other way so it pulls shut, and a gate that pulls shut has to open into the yard across her own path.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES HAS A FRONT GATE THAT WILL NOT SHUT AND HAS TO BE LIFTED, A MAN WHO DOES ODD WORK UP THE ROAD REHANGS IT SO THAT IT PULLS SHUT INSTEAD OF PUSHING, AND IT NOW STANDS OPEN ACROSS HER OWN PATH FROM THE GATE TO HER OWN STEP.**

She is a woman in her late fifties at number nine on Sunday. The man who does odd work up the road comes to her gate in the morning with a spanner in his hand and does the second half his own way.

---

The back door shuts true and the card is where it has been at that hinge since March, and there is nothing in the grate. On the fridge there is a stack of pads with the first one at the bottom of it. The door of the front room is shut and stays shut. There is sixty watts in the ceiling and nothing else lit, and the jug is on the dresser with its lid down on it.

The step and path are as she left them. The joints are packed and level and the loose root is packed over the top of it, and the heap of mats along the bed under the rail has been there since September and has gone into a grey mat with the grass coming up through it. The middle flag of the path is bedded high at the door end and tilts to the rail, and it has a lip on it the width of a thumbnail and she catches her toe on it in the dark.

The gate will not shut.

She has known it since the spring. It hangs on the two straps at the hinge end and it drops at that end, so the gate comes down on the hinge side and swings away from the keep at the other end, and there is a gap at the latch end you could get a hand through. She lifts it to shut it. She has been lifting it to shut it since the spring, and when she does shut it the latch sits against the outside of the keep and does not go in, and a gust from down the lane takes it off again.

She has a spanner, a screwdriver, a piece of flat bar, an adjustable spanner that slips, and she means to take the gate off its straps, ease the hinge stile about an eighth of an inch, put a longer keep on the post, and put a new drop bolt and drop socket on the back of the gate.

The man who does odd work up the road comes to the gate in the morning with a spanner in his hand and nothing else.

---

"You are going to put a longer keep on."

"That is what I am going to do."

"Then it will shut for you." He looks through the gap at the hinge end. "Until the timber moves in the wet and it is a longer keep you want and you are back where you started."

"You have a better way."

"I have a way that does not need doing again. Which way does that gate open."

"It comes towards me. You come up the path and it comes at you and you have to push it away from you to get in."

"Then it is hung the wrong way round and it has been hung the wrong way round since it was made." He puts the spanner in his back pocket. "A gate that closes towards you pushes its latch against the keep and the wind in your yard keeps it there. A gate that closes away from you pulls the latch into the keep and the wind cannot do anything about it. You want it the other way."

---

He takes it off and rehangs it, and he is right, and it works.

The straps come off, the screws come out, and he sets the gate down on the path with the hinge end toward the gate post on the other side. He plugs and re-bores the four strap holes in the gate stile — he does not move them far, and she does not ask why, and it is because the top strap on a gate that has dropped a quarter of an inch over thirty years is on a stile that is no longer the same thickness it was — and he turns the straps over onto the other face and marks new holes on the post, and works them out, and puts them in.

Then the keep comes off the other post and goes on to the first one, and the latch comes back through the gate the other way so it works from the front, and he puts a new screw in the end grain of the strap and packs washers under the nuts.

It takes most of the morning.

Then he shuts it. It comes across on its own weight, the latch goes into the keep with a noise she has not heard from that gate in twenty years, and it knocks once on the way in as the frame drops onto its stop, and it is shut.

She puts both hands on it and pushes. It does not go. She puts her shoulder on the hinge end and leans on it while he is getting his things together. It does not go.

"That is shut."

"That is shut."

---

She goes through it into the front path to try it, because you do not know a gate until you have walked through it, and that is when she finds what a gate that pulls shut costs.

It opens into the yard.

The two feet of yard on the inside of the gate is where everybody stands and waits, and where the bin stands on a Thursday, and where she stands in the rain. The gate comes in towards her now and swings out at about a right angle to the path and stands across the path, and there is nothing between her and her own step but a length of open gate on the right-hand side with a bolt on the wrong face of it that you cannot reach from the outside because the strap is in the way.

She can walk round it. She can get the bin past it. She has done it twice.

"You have made a gate you cannot get through."

"You can get through it."

"I can get round it. That is not the same as getting through it, and I will be doing that every time a bin goes and every time anybody comes." She shuts it again and it goes in and latches, which does not make it any better. "And it was a gate I could walk through."

"You could walk through a gate that the wind had open." He has his spanner in his pocket again. "I have given you a gate that shuts. That is not the same thing as the gate you had and it is not going to be, and if you want one you can walk through without lifting, you want the frame off the post and a new frame hung, and that is a different morning and a different price and you have not asked me for it today."

---

She shuts it after he has gone and stands in the kitchen with the chain on, and it latches, and the sixty watts she has got is the same as it ever was, and the gap at the latch end is gone.

Then she goes out to the path in the dark to look at it and finds the other half of it.

The gate has been up on new holes twice in thirty years and this is the third time, and this time the holes are new and near the end of the post and not in the middle of it, because a strap that is turned over needs somewhere to go and the somewhere it has got is the outer four inches of the timber. She puts a thumb on the head of the new screw and pushes down on the post, and the screw turns in the timber about the width of a nail head before the post gives, because that is the end grain and the end grain has nothing behind it but air and a fence.

Then she goes in, and the gate stands open across her own path from the gate to her own step, and the path on the far side of it is packed level with loose root in every joint, and the bolt on the back of it is out of reach of anybody standing in the lane.
