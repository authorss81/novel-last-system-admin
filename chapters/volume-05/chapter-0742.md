# Chapter 0742 — Four Rooms

Friday the fifteenth of July 2022, in this borough. She is in her own lock-up from first light until dark, and the whole of it is a slip of paper with an address on it and a figure put into the air that nobody had written down.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FORTIES HAS A WOMAN BRING HER A SLIP AT THE LOCK-UP THAT NAMES FOUR ROOMS WHERE THE LIST ON THE OTHER SIDE OF IT NAMES THREE THINGS, ASKS TWICE WHETHER THE FOURTH IS A ROOM OR A LANDING AND IS TOLD IT IS A ROOM AND THAT IT IS EMPTY, ASKS FOR THE FIGURE TWICE AND IS GIVEN IT TWICE AND WRITES NEITHER, RULES ONE LINE ON THE INVOICE PAD AND PUTS THE DATE ON IT AND NOTHING ELSE, AND PUTS THE SLIP IN HER COAT.**

She is a woman in her late forties in her own lock-up on Friday. The woman who books work comes to the doors in the morning and she speaks to her out there.

---

The van is outside with the back open and the boards and the bolster in under the bench, shut, and the rag bag is tied on top of them, and the floor is swept.

Behind the bench the box is under the rags where it has been since October. Its corner is down and the rags are flat over it, because she had put them flat herself at the end of the last time and there was no reason to lift them.

The invoice pad is in the door pocket. It has four lines on it and one of them with a thing on it, and the cupboard job was done and paid for and the room is gone with it, and the hall line has never had anything on it and is not going to get anything on it from this woman.

---

The woman who books work comes at about the middle of the morning with a slip in her hand and the same expression she has.

"There is a job," the woman says. "Two streets back from the ring road. Wednesday."

"What is it."

"A clear," the woman says. "Two chairs, a sideboard and a bed. The old woman is in a home and the son wants the ground floor done before he sells."

She takes the slip and looks at it. The address is on the top of it in the woman's own writing and the list of the things is on the back in the same writing, and there are four rooms numbered on it.

"There are four rooms on here," she says.

"There are four rooms in the house."

"You have got three things on the back of it."

"There are three things in three of the rooms," the woman says. "You have written four rooms because I said four rooms."

---

She turned the slip over and back again.

"Which is the fourth."

She turned the slip over again as if the answer might be on the other side of it.

"The one at the top."

"Which is the top of the stairs."

"Yes."

"That is not an answer."

"That is a room," the woman says. "It has a door and it has a window and it is on your list as a room, and it is the top room and it is behind the other three, and that is all I know about it and all the woman in the home knew about it when she left."

"Is there anything in it."

"It is empty."

"You have not been up there."

"No," the woman says. "And neither has the woman in the home, and she lived there for nine years."

---

She put the slip down on the boards with the boards shut and stood with her arms folded looking at it.

"Who told you it was empty." She stopped with her bag half up on her hip.

"The last woman I cleared it for. Two years ago." The woman turned her bag over in her hands. "She told me the top room was empty and had been since the son moved out."

"And the last woman cleared it and left a room empty and neither of those is the same as there being nothing in it."

"No." The woman did not move her feet. "It is not."

She went on standing there.

"I will need to know what is in it before I can put a figure on it." She took her arms down and let them hang.

"I am not doing four rooms off a list with three things on it. The fourth room is either empty or it is not and those are two entirely different mornings."

"It is empty." The woman put her chin up a little and waited for it to be taken.

"You have said that twice and both times you have said it in the voice you use for things you have been told."

---

The woman shifted her weight on the boards of the step and folded her arms as well, so that for a moment they were both standing in the same shape.

"Right." The woman shifted her weight on the boards of the step.

"Then I will tell you what it is. It is four rooms and three things and you do it for what the three things are worth plus the fourth room at the same rate as a room, because a room is a room whatever is in it and a rate for a room is not an estimate of the work. That is how it is priced and it is how it has always been priced."

"That is a figure."

"That is a figure."

"Say the figure."

The woman gave her the figure.

She gave it to her once, and the woman did not write it on the slip, because there was nothing on a slip about it and there is no paper between the two of them but the address and the three things.

"Again." She did not move.

"I have given it."

"Say it again and I shall know I have not heard it right."

The woman gave it to her again, and the second time was the same as the first, and then the woman wrote it on the board of the wall behind the bench with a stub of blue crayon she keeps in her apron, and stood back from it and looked at it, and then rubbed it out with her thumb because a figure in crayon on a lock-up wall is a figure anybody can come in and read.

The figure is on the slip nowhere, and it is on the pad nowhere, and it is not in her own book at home, and the wall is clean, and she has it the way she has most things in here, which is by having been given it once by somebody who was not writing anything down.

---

She took the slip off the boards and turned it over and looked at the four rooms one more time.

"Wednesday. First light." She pulled her sleeve back over her wrist.

"I will bring the boards and the lad, because there is a stair in it and I am not doing a stair on my own."

"There is a stair in it."

"There is a stair in it and a room at the top of it that you have told me twice is empty." She put her thumb on the slip and left it there.

"And when I get up there on Wednesday and it is not empty, I am going to want somebody to have written the word empty down somewhere, and there is nobody in this transaction who will."

"I shall say it again."

"Do not bother," she says. "You will not need to."

---

At the end of the day she went to the van and took the pad out of the door pocket and ruled one more line at the bottom of the four, making five.

She put the date on it and nothing else, and she did not put the figure on it and she did not put four on it and she did not rule a line for the fourth room by itself.

The pad went back in the pocket and the slip went in the inside pocket of her coat with the address on the top and the three things on the back, and she shut the lock-up with the ring on the man with keys and drove out with the box in the dark behind the bench and its corner down and its lid not nailed.
