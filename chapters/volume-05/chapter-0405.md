# Chapter 0405 — The End Wall

Thursday the nineteenth of April 2018, from first light on that stair at about a quarter past eight until about one in the afternoon, a street off that parade, in Tideglass.

**TWO MEN WERE IN THE ROOM OVER A ROOM IN THE MIDDLE OF THAT STREET ON THE SAME MORNING TWENTY MINUTES APART, AND EACH OF THEM PUT A CEMENT SHOVEL DOWN SOMEWHERE, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS ANY IDEA THAT THE OTHER ONE WAS IN THAT ROOM, AND THE SHOVEL IS ABOUT TEN FOOT FROM WHERE EITHER OF THEM PUT IT.**

Ketley Street comes off that parade about two hundred yard back on the side away from the bank and runs about a hundred and fifty yard and stops at the back of a yard. There are nine houses in it and a lock-up garage at the end of it. Four of the houses on the parade side are shopfronted and the five behind them are houses.

The building this happened in is the third from the parade end. It has a shop at the front that shut in the second week of January, and behind that shop one room on the ground floor with a door on the yard, and a room over that room, and an outside stair up the side of it going up to a landing about four foot square.

Both those rooms are empty. They have been empty since the second week of January and everything that was in them went out in four goes in the second week of January, except what was in the cellar underneath, which did not go until the last Thursday of November because nobody had a key to the cellar until the twenty-third of November.

---

The man in his thirties had that room from a quarter past eight.

He was putting a window in. It is a shop conversion that was started in about 2011 and abandoned, and this room is over the room that was the stockroom, and the old window was a steel frame about two foot six by three foot with a hopper at the top of it that had seized shut years ago, and the new one is a hardwood casement he made himself in his own workshop at the top of that town, four lights, putty lines, and a sill that goes four inch past the reveal outside.

He had the old frame out and the opening bricked up and a lintel in by about nine, and he was on his knees on a board with the new frame in and wedges under it from about half past nine.

The room is about twelve foot by nine and it has a plaster ceiling and a fireplace on the end wall and a stack of eight sheets of plasterboard stood against the chimney breast from the January clear-out, which is a stack about five foot long and two foot high and about two foot off the floor.

There is a cellar under that room and there is a stair to it off the yard with a green door and a bar across it.

Out of that cellar, on the last Thursday of November, a man took a load of about nine things up those outside steps and left them in that room against the end wall, among them a cement shovel about five foot six long with an ash handle gone grey at the top and a blade with the top third of it worn bright.

That shovel stood against the end wall by the door until about February. Then it went forward, because the boards of that floor are not level and the end wall is the wrong way up for it, and by April it was up on its blade in the centre of that floor with the top of the shaft resting against the front of the plasterboard stack, about two foot clear of the end wall, with the stack holding it up and the stack leaning on it.

Nobody had been in that room since the last Thursday of November.

He found it at about ten past nine.

He was trying to get the last sheet of plasterboard out from behind the stack to make a good at the chimney breast, and the shovel was in the way of that, standing on the boards in the only place a man can stand in that room.

He got hold of it with one hand, put his foot on the blade so that it would not slide, and walked it back to the end wall and stood it there against the boarding, on the side of the door it had always stood on, with the blade flat and the shaft leaning over at the top so that it could not go over.

It took him about fifteen seconds.

He did not think anything about it. There is a shovel in half the cellars in this borough and that shovel had been in that room for five months and it was in the way and he moved it to where it had been.

He was in that room until about a quarter to ten. He had the window in and the internal bead on and the architrave on the inside by then, and he carried his tools down that outside stair, and shut the door of that room on the way past it, and went down Ketley Street to his van, and he has not been up that outside stair since and is not going to.

The man in his forties went up that outside stair at about ten past ten.

He is not doing the window. He is looking at a fireplace in that room, because a woman who lives in the last house in that street asked him in a shop off the parade on the Monday what it costs to open a chimney breast up, and he said he would come and look at it and measure it, and that is the whole of what he said and the whole of what he came for.

He has been in that room for about fifty minutes. He is on his knees in front of the chimney breast with a tape and a stub of pencil and he has taken the skirtings off the two sides of it and lifted the front corners of the concrete hearth and looked at the brick behind them, and he has written four figures on the back of his own hand, and he is not going to open it up this year and does not intend to.

The room was not empty when he got to it. There was a stack of eight sheets of plasterboard against that chimney breast and there was a shovel standing in the doorway on the end wall side of it, blade on the boards, shaft leaning over.

He came up that outside stair with a sheet of plasterboard about four foot by two under his arm and a bag of tools in his other hand, and the shovel was in the way of the door, because the door is two foot one and the sheet is four foot, and you cannot get a sheet through a door that has a shovel stood in the reveal of it.

He stopped. He had the sheet under his arm and the bag in his hand and his own weight on his back foot.

He put the bag down on the boards, went back down four steps, got hold of the shaft, and walked the shovel about ten foot to the far end of that room, and put it down flat on the top of the stack of plasterboard sheets, with the blade off the end so that it could not go over, and the shaft lying back along the length of the stack at a slight angle because that stack is not parallel with the wall it is against.

Then he came back up the outside stair, got his bag, brought the sheet in, and worked with it for about half an hour.

He did not look at that shovel again after he put it down. He was not on the same side of that room again for more than a few minutes at a time, and he had a hearth to get up, and he is not a man who goes back over a floor he has already been across.

He packed up and left that room at about ten to twelve and went down those steps, and he has not been in that building since.

The woman who works in a shop is in this chapter for about four minutes and for nothing else.

She went up Ketley Street at about a quarter to ten to a builder's merchant at the parade end of it for a length of something for a shop of her own, and the man behind that counter is the man who is behind that counter, and she was at the trade end of it with her back to the door for about three minutes.

He came in at about five to ten for a bag of nails and a roll of DPC, and she was still there, at the other end of that counter, about ten foot off him, with her hand on a bundle of something.

Neither of them said one word. He paid for what he came for and she went on with what she came for, and he went out and up Ketley Street at about five to ten and was up that outside stair and in that room at about ten past, and she went out of that shop at about ten past ten and down Ketley Street on the other side.

They were about ten foot apart at about five to ten in a shop with a floor between them and neither of them looked up.

She has not mentioned Ketley Street to anybody. He has not mentioned the woman who works in a shop since, and he did not know on the Thursday that he had stood ten foot off her across a counter in that street that morning, and he would not believe it if it were put to him, and that would be true.

---

**HE PUT IT ON THAT STACK BECAUSE HE HAD BOTH HANDS FULL AND WENT DOWN THOSE OUTSIDE STEPS WITH A SHEET UNDER HIS ARM, AND THE SHOVEL IS STILL ON THE TOP OF EIGHT SHEETS OF PLASTERBOARD IN A ROOM THAT HAS BEEN EMPTY SINCE THE SECOND WEEK OF JANUARY, AND NOTHING ELSE IN THAT ROOM IS.**
