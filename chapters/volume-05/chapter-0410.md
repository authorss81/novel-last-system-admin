# Chapter 0410 — Wired Glass

Thursday the tenth of May 2018, starting at about half past eight in the morning and running until about half past five in the afternoon, on two days in that order, a workshop at the bottom of a yard off that parade, in Tideglass.

**TWO MEN STRIPPED A RUN OF PIPE OUT OF A SHOP FLOOR FROM OPPOSITE ENDS ON TWO DAYS AND FINISHED IT AND MET IN THE MIDDLE OF IT, AND A WOMAN SAT AT A DESK BEHIND A SQUARE OF WIRED GLASS IN THE DOOR OF THE OFFICE AT THAT NEAR END FOR THE WHOLE OF BOTH DAYS, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO MEN KNEW SHE WAS WATCHING THEM, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED EITHER OF THEM HOW IT WENT.**

That building stands at the bottom of a yard with four workshops off it, and the yard is reached through an archway from that parade. It is one building of four and it was built as a coachworks about 1911 and it has a workshop at the front about seventy foot long and twenty-two wide and an office at the near end of it.

The workshop has a hard floor, a roof of corrugated iron on timber purlins about twelve foot off the floor, a high window each side with the panes out of about a third of them, and a roller door at the far end that goes up and down on a chain and a counterweight and has done since about 1968.

The office is brick, about eight foot by seven, built inside the near end of the workshop, and it has a door in it with a square of wired glass in the top half about eighteen inch by twenty-four, wired in a diamond mesh of about an eighth of an inch wire, and the glass is not painted and not frosted and you can see the whole length of that shop floor through it if the light is right.

---

The woman who works in a shop has the use of that office.

The shop at the front of that yard is not hers. The building is owned by a man who has owned it since about 1994 and lets the front part of it to her and lets the workshop behind it out by the week.

She is in that office in the mornings of that week because she is doing that shop's own figures for the quarter, and the telephone is in that office, and the day book is on that desk, and the light in there is on a pull switch by the door, and there is a seat behind the desk.

She was in that office from about half past nine on the Thursday until about one, and from about half past nine on the Friday until about one, and she was in there both of those days with the door shut and the square of glass clear, and she had the figures in front of her about half of the time.

The job in that workshop was for the man who owns that building. He came in on the Thursday at about half past eight and gave them both the key off his own ring and told them the run and where it stopped, and asked whether they wanted him to be there, and they said no, and he said that was all right, and went, and has not been back since.

The run is a length of cast iron heating pipe about five foot above the floor, running the length of that shop floor on the wall side, into a header tank in the corner at the far end, with six radiators on it.

They started at opposite ends on the Thursday morning.

The man in his forties started at the far end, under the header tank, and the man in his thirties started at the near end, twenty foot in from the office wall.

They worked separately for two days and there is no other way to do a run seventy foot long, and neither of them said at any point on either day that they would start at the same end.

On the Thursday the man in his forties took out about twenty-two foot of that pipe by about half past twelve, and the man in his thirties took out about eighteen foot by about the same time, and the middle eighteen foot of it was still in when the man who owns that building shut the roller door at about half past four and they went.

On the Friday the man in his forties was at the far end again at about half past eight and the man in his thirties was at the near end at about half past eight, and the first hour of the Friday is the two of them working towards each other with nothing between them.

They met at about ten past nine in the morning on the Friday, at a length of pipe on the wall about eight foot above the floor, in the middle of that shop floor, about thirty foot from either end of it.

There is a cast-iron column there. It goes from the floor to that roof and it is about five inch by five inch with a moulded cap, and it is in the middle of that run of pipe, and the pipe goes past it about four inch clear on the wall side of it.

She watched that from the desk.

The glass in that door is not square with the floor, it is a bit above the middle, and it looks down that shop floor, and on that Friday morning the light was coming in through the high windows on the other side, and for about ten minutes there was a great deal of light on that floor.

She could see both of them for most of that morning without turning her head. They were about thirty foot apart and both of them were on the other side of the middle. She could not hear a word of either of them from that desk with the door shut, and she did not try to, and she had her own work in front of her for about an hour of that Friday.

At about ten past nine they stopped.

She watched both of them come off what they were doing inside a couple of minutes of one another, and both of them stand and look at that column and at the pipe going past it, and she saw the man in his thirties go up on the top of the steps and put his eye along that pipe, and come down, and say something to the man in his forties that she could not hear, and the man in his forties got a piece of paper out of his pocket and came about ten foot along that run towards him and stood next to him and said something back.

That went on for about six minutes and then they stopped talking and the man in his forties went back to the far end and the man in his thirties came down off the steps.

They worked apart again for the rest of the morning, and neither of them went to the middle again until about half past one.

Here is what had happened and what each of them was right about.

The man in his forties had measured the day before. That column is at the third roof light from the far end, and that pipe is carried on clips screwed to the wall on the line of the pipe, and that column is directly in the way of one of those clips, and the only way to carry the pipe past it is to fix the clip to the far face of the column and take the round of it.

The man in his thirties wanted to take the pipe over the top of the column on a bend. There is about five foot between that column and that roof and the pipe could go up and over on two bends and a piece of stock, and he said that and he was right, and it will go up, and he was right that a man can put it there in an afternoon.

It is not going to stay there. The heating in that workshop runs off a tank on that wall and the flow pipe is about an inch and a half and it is at the high level of that run, and a length of it carried over the top of a column in a building like that is a length that will sit at the top of that pipe for ever, and the joint at that column will want replacing in about nine years, and there will be no room for a spanner on it when it does.

The man in his forties said that. She did not hear him say it.

And the man in his thirties said, and this is the part she could see because he was standing on the steps and gesturing with a length of copper, that he had carried an inch-and-a-half line over a column like that in a school two winters ago and it had been perfectly all right, and that was the facts.

And the man in his forties did not say he was not wrong and he did not say he was right, and the man in his thirties did not say it again.

The man in his forties did it his way. The pipe goes round the far side of that column, which is about nine foot further on, and it comes back onto the line afterwards with two bends and a short length, and the two clips on that column are on the back of it, on two of its four faces.

The man in his thirties took his own bit of it out again on the Friday afternoon.

That is the part she watched most of.

At about ten past one she saw the man in his forties shut his bag and go out through the roller door to his van, and he was out of that building for about eight minutes, and when he came back in she was at the desk and the door was shut.

At about twenty past one the man in his thirties was up on the steps at that column with a tube cutter on the length he had put there that morning. He took the two bends off it in about ten minutes and took the piece of stock out and took the two clips off the back of that column, and he put the whole of it in a bag and carried that bag to the far end of that shop floor and put it down there against the wall, and then he came back and did it the other way, with the man in his forties about five foot off him on the other side of the column, and they worked that last nine foot together in about an hour and a quarter without a word between them about it.

Neither of them said one word about that column again on that day. She was at the desk with the door shut and she heard a hammer about four foot off her for an hour and a quarter and she did not get up and open that door once.

At about half past four they cleared that floor. The old iron went out in sacks, the lagging came off in lengths, the new pipe was in and lagged and the clips were on it, and they swept that floor and shook the ladder out on the yard and put their bags in their vans.

The man in his thirties came in through that office door at about ten to five for the second half of his bag, which he had left in there on the Thursday, and the man in his forties was already out on that yard, and they shook hands on that yard at about five past five and got into their vans and went.

She was in that office for about another ten minutes after that, on the Friday, and then she shut the door of that office and went out and locked it and has not had that key.

She has not mentioned that day to either of those two men and she is not going to, and she has not mentioned it to anybody else either, and she does not know what either of them would say about a column if it were put to them and she is not going to find out.

---

**THEY CLEARED THAT FLOOR AND WENT OUT ON THAT YARD AND SHAKE HANDS AT FIVE PAST FIVE AND GOT INTO THEIR VANS, AND A WOMAN WHO SAT BEHIND A SQUARE OF WIRED GLASS IN A DOOR IN THAT NEAR END WENT OUT AND LOCKED THAT DOOR AND DID NOT SAY ONE WORD OF IT TO ANYBODY.**
