# Chapter 0738 — The Second Pad

Saturday the second of July 2022, in this borough. She is in her own house at number nine from first light until dark, and the whole of it is six pads on a fridge and a woman at the door she has not stood in before.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES READS THE SIX PADS ON HER OWN FRIDGE AND PUTS THEM IN THE ORDER THEY WERE PUT THERE IN, TAKES A WOMAN WHO HAS BEEN GOING TO THE SHOP FOR HER AT THE FRONT DOOR AND LETS HER READ ALL SIX ALOUD, PUTS THE FIRST ONE DOWN ON THE TABLE AND ADMITS SHE CANNOT ACCOUNT FOR IT BEING CROSSED TWICE, REFUSES HER THE FIFTH PAD OUTRIGHT, AND LETS THE SIX GO BACK ON THE FRIDGE IN A DIFFERENT ORDER WITHOUT PUTTING THE FIRST ONE BACK ON TOP.**

She is a woman in her late fifties at number nine on Saturday. A woman who has been going to the shop for her stands at her front door in the morning and she speaks to her in the kitchen.

---

The fridge has six pads on it and it has had six pads on it since March.

The first is crossed twice, which is two lines across the same words, one over the other, not quite parallel. The second is crossed once. The other four are not crossed at all.

She looks at them at about the middle of the morning with the kitchen light off and the fire out, as it has been out since Easter, and reads the six out loud to the fridge, which is the only company that has never once asked her anything about them.

Then she undoes it and puts them back in the order they were put there in, which is the order of March, and they are in that order already, and she has just found that out.

---

The knock comes in the morning at the front door, which is not the door anybody comes to.

It is the woman who has been going to the shop for her since about April and calling it going to the shop, because what she does is carry a bag to number nine and come back with a bag. She has a key to her own back door and no key to this one, so she comes to the front and stands on the step.

"I have got your milk and your bread and your six eggs," the woman says, "and I have not got the third thing."

"What third thing."

"You have had the same third thing written on since March," the woman says. "And I have gone round the shop for it twice and I cannot find it and I cannot find out what a thing called that is."

"Then it is not a thing you can get," she says. "That is the whole answer and I have had it since March."

She stands aside and the woman comes in and they go through to the kitchen, which is the way they have done it since April.

---

The woman puts the bag on the table and looks at the fridge.

"I know," she says. "I have known since the first time."

"You have not read them."

"I have read them four times off your kitchen table while you have been upstairs," the woman says. "You never told me not to."

"There was nothing to tell you," she says.

The woman takes the six off the fridge and squares them together in her hand the way you square a hand of cards, and reads the first one out loud.

"That one is crossed twice," the woman says.

"Yes."

"Crossed once and then crossed again over it. Why."

"I do not know," she says. "That is the honest answer and I have had it since the spring. I do not know whether I crossed it and then wrote it out again underneath, or whether I crossed it, and could not bring myself to wipe it out, and crossed it a second time. There is one of us and one pad and no anybody else in this house."

"You have had it crossed twice since March?"

"Since March," she says.

---

The woman reads the rest of them. She does not hurry and she does not put them down.

The second is crossed once. The third, fourth, fifth and sixth have not been touched at all, and the writing on them is thin at the top of each line where the pencil ran out and she went back over it twice without going over it hard.

"Give me that one," the woman says, and puts her thumb on the fifth.

"No."

"It has not been touched since March. Give it me and I will take it to the shop on the way and I will ask them, and I will ask him, and if it is a thing behind a glass I will ask for it by name and I will bring it round."

"You will ask a man in a shop about a thing he has never heard of, in a shop where everybody knows me," she says, "and I will be in this house when you come back, and I will not have told you no, and you will have told him."

The woman takes her thumb off the fifth pad.

"That is fair," she says.

"That is what happens."

---

She puts the second pad on the table in front of her, flat, and looks at the one line through it.

"You have done this one," she says.

"I did this one."

"And this is the first of them. It is the top pad and it has been on top since March whatever else has happened, so it is the first thing anybody looks at."

The woman does not look at the first one again.

"How many things are on that fridge," she says, "that you have done?"

"That is not a question with a number in it."

"It is a question with a number in it," the woman says. "You have six pads. You have crossed one twice and one once and four not at all. If you have only done one of them, why is one of them done twice."

She does not answer straight away, and she is aware of the length of the not answering, and the woman hears it too and waits, which she has never done before.

"Three," she says at last.

"Three?"

"Two finished and one that I gave up on," she says. "The first pad has the same thing on it twice because I did it, and did not think the first one was the one I had done, and wrote it again and did it again. That is the two. The one I gave up on is the only one in this house that has ever had a person start it and stop, and it has got no line on it at all, and there is nothing on that fridge that says I tried and gave up."

The woman looks at her for a second.

"You have had nobody to say that to," she says.

"No."

---

The woman puts the six back on the fridge.

She does not put them back in the order they were in. She puts them back with the first one at the bottom of the stack, which it has never been since March, and the second one on top of it, and the four that are not crossed after that, in the order they came off, so the first thing anybody looks at is now a pad with one line through it.

She steps back from the fridge and looks at what she has done.

She goes over to it and takes the top pad off and looks at the line through the second one and then at the first one underneath it with its two lines across it, and puts the top one back again, and the first one stays at the bottom.

Then she puts the bag of milk in the cold box of the fridge, which is on the other side and shut, and closes it, and washes her hands at the sink, and the woman takes her own cup down off the shelf where it lives and washes that too because she is in the kitchen.

At the door the woman says she will be out on the Thursday.

She puts the empty bottle in the bag by the back door, which is where the bag is and which is where she has never put one, and knots the bag over the top of it.

Then the front door is shut, which it has always shut, and the back door is shut true as it has been since she put a card at the hinge herself in March, and the fire is still out, and the cold box is shut on the milk, and the six pads are on the front of the fridge with the first one at the bottom of them, and the first pad is the only thing in this kitchen that has been put anywhere today.
