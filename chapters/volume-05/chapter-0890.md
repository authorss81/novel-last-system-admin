# Chapter 0890 — Two Hands Towards Each Other

Monday the fourth of March 2024, at the shed standing at the far end of that yard, from the time the wall has begun to take the sun until the light has gone off the bricks behind it. How far two hands get towards each other in a slot.

**SHE HAS A SHED STANDING AT THE FAR END OF THE YARD AT NUMBER TWELVE WITH THE SIDE OF IT A FOOT CLEAR OF THE BOUNDARY WALL AT THE GATE END, AND SHE MEANS TO PUT ONE HAND INTO THAT SLOT AT THE GATE END AND THE OTHER INTO IT AT THE FAR END AND WORK THEM TOWARDS EACH OTHER UNTIL THEY MEET, AND HER SISTER IS AT THE SHED DOOR.**

The shed was put up against the boundary at the far end of that yard, and it is not touching the wall.

That is not much of a thing to look at from the path, because the slot it left is about a foot wide at the gate end and three inches at the other, and it runs along the side of a shed in a corner where the ground is never walked.

What put it in her mind in February is that the shed has begun to lean.

Not much. Enough that she can stand at the gate end of that yard and see the shed's boards are not vertical, and enough that the wall it is standing away from is leaning out over the yard instead.

Two things leaning towards each other is a thing worth a number.

One hand goes into the slot at the gate end, flat, palm down.

It goes in about a foot, which is more than the gap is wide, so the hand is not flat across the gap but flat along the length of it, with the palm against the shed and the fingers pointing down the slot.

The gap is widest at the top of that run, because the shed's board leans out over the slot as it goes up, and by the ground it has closed to about the width of the palm.

The other hand goes in at the far end.

It goes in about three inches and stops on something.

Not on the wall. On brick.

So she works them towards each other, an inch at a time, both hands going down the slot from opposite ends, and the two hands meet at about two thirds along.

Where they meet there are three courses of brick laid across the slot, joining the side of the shed to the boundary wall.

Old brick, blue and hard, laid three courses deep and set flush with both faces in lime mortar that has gone soft, and they were never put there to carry the walls apart.

They were put there to stop the two of them coming together.

Blue brick is a hard brick, and a wall of blue brick is a wall that was built at the same time as the boundary rather than after it, and the three courses in that slot are the same age as the wall they are tying, which means the tie was put in when the shed was built and not later.

So the two walls have been trying to come together for the whole of that shed's life, and three courses of brick have been the whole of the argument against it, and the tie has not cracked.

She can check that without getting down on the ground. From the gate end of that yard, on a bright day, you can look down the slot and see a bar of daylight about a foot in, and the bar of daylight stops exactly where the brick starts.

Her sister comes to the shed door from outside about two in the afternoon and stands at it.

She wanted the short length of green wire off the shelf in the shed, and it was not on the shelf, and she asked whether it had been used for the frame at the front, and it had not. She wanted it understood that the roll behind the boards was for that frame, and that a person ought to know where a roll of wire is when they are standing in a shed asking for one.

Then she looked at the shed wall for a while, and then at the wall behind it, and told her that the whole thing had been standing long enough to have stopped straightening itself. She put it the way she puts things she has decided about and is not going to discuss, and she went away down the path without coming in.

---

A shed is put up against a wall by people who want a shed, not by people who want a gap.

So what happened at that end of that yard is that the boundary wall was older than the shed by a good many years, and the shed was built against it and stood off it because a wall built by hand in the ground moves, and a shed built by hand on the ground moves differently, and where two things meet like that they cannot both be in the same place.

And then somebody filled the difference with three courses of brick.

Which is ordinary work and takes half an hour, and it is the reason that gap is a dead end rather than a slot.

And a tie that carries nothing is the easiest kind there is to leave alone.

What the two hands found on the floor of that slot while they were down there is the better half of it.

The floor of the slot, the whole way from the gate end to the brick, is not ground.

It is a line of flat stones laid stone on stone along the length of the shed, with their long edges across the gap and their ends in the footings of both walls.

Which is what a gap like that needs.

The stones under the shed go in under the shed, and the stones under the boundary wall go in under the boundary wall, and where the shed stops and the boundary wall begins the same line of stones carries the two of them together. That is why there is a slot at all.

What she really wanted answered, underneath the number, is where the water off that shed roof goes.

It comes off the felt and off the battens above it and drops at the top of that slot, because the shed leans and the low side is the boundary side, and everything the roof sheds in a wet month comes down the gap between the two of them.

And it goes straight through. There is a line at the gate end of that slot where the stones of the floor are dark, and the dark runs about two feet from the gate end and stops, and the stones further along are the colour they were.

So the water goes in at the gate end, between the stones, into the ground under them, and out. Two fingers go between the stones at that dark line and there is ground behind them.

A gap between two walls filled from the bottom with loose material closes up in twenty years, because loose material settles and the walls come in on it. A gap between two walls with a floor of stones does not close, because the stones take the weight of both walls and pass it straight down into the ground.

So the shed and the boundary wall are both standing on a line of stones, and the stones are the reason they are still a foot apart at the gate end after however many years it has been.

The stones underneath are sound, the brick in the middle is sound, and the ground under the stones is sound, so everything in that corner is in the state it was put there in.

That corner will not be looked at again this year, and it does not need looking at.

That corner will be exactly as it is at the end of this year and at the end of the year after, and at the end of the year after that.

The three courses of brick in the middle of that slot are the whole of what can be done with it, and the only reason to put a hand in it at all is to find out how far in it goes.
