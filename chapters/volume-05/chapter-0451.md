# Chapter 0451 — The Tread

Sunday the fourth of November 2018 between two o'clock and about half past three, and then the Friday of that week for a minute. A building near that parade and some two hundred yard of it, in Tideglass.

**HE SAID ONE TRUE THING IN A DOORWAY AND WENT OUT, AND SOMEBODY DID IT PROPERLY AND PAID FOR IT OUT OF HER OWN MONEY, AND HE COULD NOT RAISE ONE SINGLE OBJECT AT ANY POINT, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS SAID ANYTHING ABOUT IT SINCE.**

She lives in two rooms on the top floor of that building. A man in his thirties has a long lease on a flat about two hundred yard off it with rooms on the first and the second and a stair that everybody on that parade uses as a way through, because there is no other way through.

On the Sunday of the fourth of November he came into that shop at about ten past two for something he had been intending to buy since August, and she was behind the front of that premises with the door open to the street because the heating in there is on, and they said what they say.

He got his change and stopped in the doorway with his back half out to the parade.

He said, "Your tread's gone through at the turn."

He said it the way a man says a thing he has been meaning to say for a fortnight and has run out of ways of saying it. Then he said, "On the way down."

Then he went out into the parade.

He did not say whose tread, and she did not ask. He did not say it was dangerous, and he did not say what to do about it, and he did not stay. She stood in that doorway for about a second after he had gone and said, "Right," to the street.


Here is exactly what he gave her, and it is everything, and it took him about two seconds.

That on the way down, at the turn where the stair comes round on itself with a window over it, one of the treads is worn through across the front two inch of it and has gone soft, and that anybody going down in the dark puts their heel in exactly that place, and that a person going down in the dark who thinks there is a tread there and there is not will go down four or five steps on their face.

That is all. There was no instruction in it. He told her a fact about her own stair, he told her where it was true, and he left the building.

He thought about it from about the middle of the parade and he could not think of one thing she would have to be able to say no to. There was no *you ought to*, there was no *I'll have a look*, there was nothing in it that put a job in front of her or a bill in front of her or an opinion in her mouth about somebody else's money.

---

She went down that stair at about twenty past two with a torch, on the off chance, and he was right.

The tread is the second one below the turn. It is a common beech tread with a nosing on it that has been painted over so many times it is more paint than wood, and across the front two inch of it the middle is gone, and the light comes through the tread where the light comes through the window over the turn, and you can see the glow of it at about half past two in the afternoon on a day like that one.

She stood on the fourth tread below it and looked at it for a while.

Then she went up and got a piece of paper and worked out that a man she used a couple of times last summer — a man who does stairs and does not ask questions and does not talk — would be about thirty pounds for a nosing, and a week to get to him, and about four days to do it.

She was not doing it because anybody told her to. She was doing it because a man who comes into that shop most weeks stood in her doorway, said nine words, and went out into the street, and because he was right, and because she is not a person who is going down four or five steps in the dark on a night like that because she did not want to spend thirty pounds.


He came in on that Friday at about ten past two, from the other end of that parade, and did not go down that stair.

He went across the forecourt to look at the window, which is a thing he does, and out of the corner of his eye at about the far end of the front of that shop he saw a strip of bright metal lying along the nosing of the second tread below the turn where the light comes through.

He stopped where he was for about a second.

Then he went back the way he had come and round by the arch and down that other stair and out onto the parade and went to his van.

It is a proper job. That is the first thing and it is the thing that took his legs out from under him on a pavement. The old tread has been lifted and the frame under it has been taken out and the frame is the same thickness as the one it is going into, so somebody thought about it, and there is a metal nosing let into the top of the tread with countersunk screws and the countersinks have been filled and sanded, and the paint on the new nosing is not the same paint as the paint on the rest of that stair and it is not the same as the paint on the stairs either side of it, because it was done in one morning by a man who does not care what colour a nosing is.

It cost about thirty pounds and she paid it herself out of her own money and told the man who did it what she wanted and did not take a price off him and did not have it done for nothing.

---

He could not have raised an object at any point, and he has gone back over it more than once since, which is the thing he cannot get past.

There was no request in it to refuse. He was not asked for his opinion and was not asked for anything at all. He did not know at the time whether anybody would act on it, or act on it that week, or act on it at all, and he found out on the Friday that somebody had acted on all of it inside four days, properly, at their own cost, with their own money, and had not mentioned it to a living soul.

The only thing he could have said is *you did not have to do that*, and if he had said it, it would have been a way of making it a thing between the two of them, and there is nothing between the two of them, and that is the correct state of it and he would not have wanted it any other way.

He did not say it. She did not tell him. He has not been up that stair since that Friday and she has not mentioned it and has not left a note and has not asked him to look, and on the seventeenth of November that nosing is still there with a slightly different colour on it and it is still the only new thing on that stair.

**HE STOOD ON A FORECOURT AND SAW A STRIP OF BRIGHT METAL AT THE FAR END OF A TREAD FROM TWO HUNDRED YARD AWAY, AND HE WENT ROUND BY THE ARCH AND DOWN THE OTHER STAIR AND OUT INTO THE PARADE.**
