# Chapter 0305 — The Angle by the Fence

Saturday the eleventh of March 2017, from about half past ten in the morning, the low end of Peverell Street in Tideglass.

**A MAN TOOK A DOOR OFF THREE HINGES AT A HOUSE AND LEFT IT STANDING ON ITS TOP EDGE IN THE ANGLE BETWEEN A BACK FENCE AND THE END OF A WALL, AND A WOMAN WENT ROUND IT WITH A PUSHCHAIR, AND A MAN PUT A LIGHT ON AT FOUR O'CLOCK FOR NOBODY, AND THE ONLY PERSON IN THAT LANE WHO FOUND ANYTHING OUT WAS A MAN CARRYING A BOILER WHO WAS NEVER IN THAT SHOP AT ALL.**

The house the door came off is the second one back in a terrace that runs off the Cleeve road, and a woman there is having a new one put in on the twenty-eighth of this month, and the fitter told her when he measured it that the old one was his to take away, which he said in a voice that did not leave much room for asking him anything about it.

The door is a pine door about two foot eight by six and four, painted brown, with a brass letter plate set high on it that has been painted over so many times that the shape of it is only a swelling under the paint, and the number three is under all of that somewhere and nobody has ever taken the paint off to look.

He had it off in about twenty minutes on the Friday and the three screws that came out of the jamb were in his pocket and the door was across the footwell of the van and it would not lie flat, because there is a length of scaffold in the back of that van and it is always in the back of that van, and he could not stand it on edge because the bulkhead was down and the front seat would not go forward.

So it went in the angle.

The angle is on the footpath that runs behind the low end of Peverell Street, between the back fence of one garden and the side wall of the next house, and it is the way through from one side of that end of the street to the other because the kerb is up along the front of those houses for about the length of two cars and everybody goes through.

He put the door on its top edge with the moulded side facing the concrete and the letter plate facing the fence, so the fence would take the weather off the plate and the road would not, and he tipped it back against the fence where it would lean, and it stood there.

Nobody was on the days, nobody was at the top of that lane at any hour of that day, and he wrote nothing down, and he did not tell the woman whose door it was where it had gone, and he did not tell the man who comes for the boiler that there was a door in that lane.

The woman with a pushchair came along that path at about ten to twelve, because the kerb is up at the front for about two cars, and the door was in the way, and what was left of the way was about a foot and a half.

She stopped. She got the child's weight up against her hip and held it there with one arm and put the other hand on the bar and came up with it, one side then the other, the near wheel coming off the concrete and the off wheel grinding along the base of the fence, and the fence went along about four inches of the paintwork on the top of that door as it did.

It took her about half a minute. There was nobody there to say anything to.

---

At about four o'clock the man in his sixties was in a front room on the first floor of the house on the corner at that end of the street, with the curtains open, with a cup he had not drunk from, and not doing anything at all, and he could see straight down that footpath because there is nothing between that window and that fence.

He watched the woman get the pushchair through. Then he watched the man in his thirties come back down that path at about two o'clock, look at the door from about six foot off, and get into his van and shut the door and go, without going over and looking at the front of it, which was the front of it because it was a door.

Then he watched the woman come back along the same path at about ten past two with the child asleep in it, and go through the gap again, and there was no door in the gap.

He went out of that room and came back about a minute later and put the light on over the door of that house, which has a switch inside by the door on a wall that has a brown bakelite switch on it, and then he went back and sat down and lifted the cup off the arm of the chair beside him and found it had gone cold while he was out of the room.

There is nobody in that house on the days. Nobody was under that light that day or the next day or the day after, except the woman with the pushchair, and she went along underneath it with a sleeping child in the chair and had no reason to look up at a window, because there was nothing to look up at.

---

He came back at about ten past two with a second van, one he had hired for the morning, and put the door on the boards flat and put four pieces of offcut under it and drove off, and nobody in that lane saw him do it because there was nobody in that lane.

On the Friday of that week a man came for the boiler at the shop that shuts early, and he had a sack barrow with the boiler on it, and he came down that lane instead of up the parade because the barrow is easier on the cobbles than the parade is and he had done that about a dozen times.

He stopped in the middle of that lane.

There was a door standing on its top edge in the angle with the moulded side facing the concrete and the letter plate facing the fence, and a scrape along the top four inches of the paintwork, and no chock of any kind under it and no rope and nothing tying it, and it had not fallen over in whatever weather had come in between.

He stood there and looked at it for about ten seconds. He had come in that lane with a barrow and a boiler and a Monday to get through, and he was not going to stand in a lane in front of a house asking a woman where a door came from, because the answer to a question like that is a story, and a story takes twenty minutes, and he has a shop to get a boiler into.

So he went back up the lane and knocked on the side door of that shop instead of going in the front, and the woman who runs that shop opened the side door and let him in that way, and she did not know that day that her boiler had come round the back, and the man with the barrow did not tell her, and nobody in that shop has ever found out that a door stood in that angle.

**AND THE DOOR WENT ON A HIRED VAN AT ABOUT TEN PAST TWO WITH FOUR PIECES OF OFFCUT UNDER IT, AND THE SCRAPE ALONG THE TOP FOUR INCHES OF THE PAINTWORK WAS NOT SCRUBBED OUT, AND ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF THIS MONTH A NEW DOOR WENT INTO THE SAME OPENING IN A HOUSE IN A TERRACE OFF THE CLEEVE ROAD, AND NOBODY IN THAT TERRACE HAS ASKED WHERE THE OLD ONE WENT.**
