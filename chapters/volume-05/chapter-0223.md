# Chapter 0223 — He Should Not Have Said It

Wednesday the seventeenth of February 2016, from about four in the afternoon, a building by the water in Marrowgate, and then the back room of the works.

**HE SAID THE TRUE THING ABOUT HIMSELF IN A BACK ROOM AT TWENTY PAST SIX IN THE EVENING, TO ONE PERSON, AND THEN SAID HE SHOULD NOT HAVE SAID IT, AND IT WAS NOT TAKEN BACK, AND NOTHING WAS ARRANGED FOR HIM.**

Jonas Mercer had crossed on the Tuesday and was going back on the Thursday, and there was nothing on the Wednesday that had to happen, and he went into the works at about four o'clock because he had walked past it twice and there was a light in the back room and he wanted to know whose it was.

It was a room about twelve feet by ten with a table, two chairs, a kettle, a shelf with ring binders on it that all said the same thing, a window that faces a yard nobody can see into, and a door that does not shut. There was a fire in a small stove that was off.

Walter Sallow was in it with his coat still on.

"Sit down," he said. "I've been in since ten. There's tea in the pot and it's the good tin, don't waste it."

**AND THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THE WALL OF THAT ROOM, printed and fixed at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A BACK ROOM IS A ROOM IN A BUILDING.**
> **A CUP OF TEA IS A CUP OF TEA.**
> **NEITHER OF THOSE IS A MINUTE.**

They drank the tea. Jonas Mercer told him the boat was at ten past seven on the Thursday morning and that he would be on it, and the old man said that was fine and that there was a bus from the landing but he would not be on it, and then neither of them said anything for about ten minutes, and the room did what a back room does with two people in it.

At about twenty past six Walter Sallow put his cup down on the table and squared it up to the edge, which Jonas Mercer had never once seen him do and understood immediately to be a thing he had got from somebody who worked in an office.

"Right," he said. "You've been here six weeks now and you've asked me about the rag, and about the woman who doesn't wash, and whether I'd be missed, and you haven't asked me the thing you came for. So I'm going to say it and then I'm going to be fed up with myself, and you can do what you like with it, and I have thought about saying it every day since the Wednesday you asked me about being all right with this."

He did not look up.

"It's not that I'm the only one who does it. That's what they say in there. That's the sentence, that's the arrangement, and it has got my name under it and it is up on the wall, and everybody in that room believes it and everybody in that room is wrong, and I have never said so, and I have no way of saying so that isn't the old man saying he's a bigger man than he is.

"He's the only one who can. That's a different sentence and it does exactly the same work.

"There's a man of ninety-one who opens that gate on a Sunday, and it's only been asked of him once in nine years and he did it and he went home. There's a woman of seventy-four sweeps that clinic and has swept it since we took the place over, and she could open that door perfectly well, and somebody in an office in a different town asked her to, once, and she said no, and she was entitled to, and nobody has thought to ask her again. There's a gardener rakes that yard and has raked it nine years and could not work the rack if you paid him and neither could I this morning, and there's two lads from the yard who carry the screens up off the bank and have never once been asked where the screens come from.

"Eleven years. I did not know I had said yes."

He stopped there and turned the cup about a quarter turn.

"I was asked once. In a room. In the spring, and I said yes, and I went outside, and I have not gone back into that room about it since, and I have not been asked since, and I have not asked, and there is nothing to hand it to. Everybody else who has ever been in that arrangement has got a door to hand it to. A person above me. A committee. A form with a name on it. I have got a gate, and a gate is a thing you come through, and it goes up at seven and it comes down at six, and there is no post in this arrangement and there never has been and there is not going to be, because a duty is an act and not a place, and a place cannot be given to a person, and all four of those sentences are correct and I have listened to them for eleven years and they are the reason nobody has ever asked me whether I would like to stop.

"And the bit I can't put anywhere, and the bit I have been carrying since the Wednesday you stood at the back of the shed and asked me if I was all right with this, is that there is nobody in this town I can be cross with. Not one person. The clerk's right. The man of ninety-one's right. The woman at the clinic's right. The woman who doesn't wash on a Tuesday because the water's off is right, and she ought to be, and I have never said one word to her about it and I'm not going to, and she does not know it's me. And the yard's right, and the filter's right, and the ninety pence a quarter is right, and the whole of it is better than the one I came from, and I have got no complaint, and I am the complaint, and there is nobody to make it to."

He picked the cup up and put it down again without drinking from it.

"I should not have said that," said Walter Sallow. "To you. On a Wednesday. In a back room. You are a man from a city three towns and an estuary away and you are not even staying to Thursday, and I have known you about six weeks, and whatever that was, it is not a thing to put on a man on a Wednesday."

---

Jonas Mercer said nothing at all.

There had been a corridor in December in a building eight miles and an estuary away, and a woman in it with a folder on a window ledge, and she had said a true thing and could not finish it and had said she should not have said it, and he had said nothing, and it had stayed in the corridor where she had put it, and he had told a lawyer on a Friday morning that nothing was wrong with the system and the water was good. He did not tell the old man any of that, and he did not say that he had done it before, and he did not say the sentence again, and he did not say a single word of it out loud for the rest of the evening.

The notebook was in the inside pocket of his coat on the table beside him, where it had been in December, and it was in the inside pocket of his coat at about ten past seven that evening and it had not been opened.

They did the only two things there are to do. He got the good tin refilled and they had a second cup of it. Walter Sallow told him about the third time the lorry had been asked for since the year the town bought it, and about a man in the second house on Church Street who has a dog and cannot get to the end of the road with it, and about the price of the dog food, and none of it went anywhere and all of it was true.

The notebook stayed shut. At about half past six the old man got up and put his coat on and said he had to go up and let the gate in at seven and would Jonas mind locking up, and Jonas Mercer said he would not mind, and he locked it, and Walter Sallow let him in at the gate and neither of them said anything else about the half hour before.

**AND A MAN SAID THE TRUTH ONCE, IN A ROOM, TO ONE PERSON, AND SAID HE SHOULD NOT HAVE SAID IT, AND IT WAS NOT TAKEN BACK, AND IT WAS NOT WRITTEN DOWN, AND JONAS MERCER GOT THE BOAT AT TEN PAST SEVEN ON THE THURSDAY, AND THE OTHER MAN WAS ON THE INTAKE AT HALF FIVE IN THE DARK, AND NOBODY IN THAT TOWN IS GOING TO KNOW THAT ANY OF IT WAS SAID.**
