# Chapter 0626 — The One He Sent

Friday the fifth of March 2021. He is out of the house at about ten past six and he is not back in until the light goes, and all of it happens on one row of houses in this borough.

**A MAN HAS DECIDED TO GO AND ASK THE WOMAN NEXT BUT ONE TO PUT HIS BIN OUT AGAIN, AND HE HAS NOT KNOCKED, AND THE WOMAN TWO DOORS DOWN STANDS IN HIS GATE ON FRIDAY MORNING AND ASKS HIM WHY HE HAS NOT ASKED, AND HE SAYS BECAUSE HE ASKED.**

He is a man in his early sixties. He lives at the end of a terrace of twelve and he has lived in this borough all his working life and he does not drive any more and he has not driven since before the summer.

The bin is a red wheelie with a catch on the lid that has never worked. It is not his catch and it is not his bin and it was the same before he was ever here.

The woman two doors down is a woman in her early forties and she has lived here longer than he has and she keeps two pots alive in what she calls the front and he has never been invited in.

---

Here is what happened on the Friday morning, at about a quarter past six, and he was in the road with his back against the wall and the bin lid open in front of him.

The woman two doors down came out with her own on a sack truck, which she does when her back is bad and her back is not bad, but there is a thing about the wheels and she has done it since before she lived here.

And she said, "Morning."

And he said, "Morning."

And she said, "You can have that one back."

And he said, "You can put it back."

And she said, "I'm not touching it."

And he said, "It's only a bin."

And she said, "It's only a bin," and she left it where it was, and went on up the row with the sack truck, and at the gate she stopped.

And she said, "You didn't get that one out Wednesday."

And he said, "I know I didn't."

And she said, "They went at about seven."

And he said, "I know what time they go."

And she said, "It was still on the kerb this morning when I came out."

And he said, "I did it Thursday."

And she said, "Thursday's no good."

And he said, "I know Thursday's no good."

And she said, "Have you asked her."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Have you asked her to do it again."

And he said, "I got as far as her gate."

And she said, "On which day."

And he said, "Wednesday evening."

And she said, "Did you knock."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Right."

And she said, "Why not."

And he said, "Because I asked."

That is the whole of what was said at the gate on Friday morning, and he has been over it since and he has not got past the third exchange.

---

Here is what is wrong, and he got to it at about half past eight in the kitchen with the tap running, which is a thing he does.

On Saturday before last he was in the road a bit before midday with the bin against his leg and the woman two doors down was watering whatever she grows in the two pots by her door.

And he said to her, "Could you ask her for me."

And she said, "Ask her for what."

And he said, "The bin. Wednesday."

And she said, "You want me to ask her to do your bin."

And he said, "You know her better than I do."

And she said, "I've said I'd have a word."

And he said, "Have a word."

And she said, "I'll see her about it."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "Will you be all right about it if I do."

And he said, "Yes."

And he went inside and shut the door and that was the end of it.

Here is the whole of what happened on the Saturday, and it took about a minute and a half, and the woman two doors down has not mentioned it since and that is fair, because he did not ask her to remember it.

And he has been carrying this since Saturday, and he has not examined it until about a quarter past eight on Friday morning.

One: he asked her to ask. He did not ask her to do it. The two are different and he knew they were different at the time because he said one of them and not the other.

Two: she said *I'll see her about it*, and that is not a promise, and he took it as a promise.

Three: and here is the part that has taken the morning. He went inside and he shut the door, and at some point between the Saturday and the Friday he stopped being a man who had asked a favour and became a man who had asked.

He did not see her. He did not see anybody. What he did was this. He told himself on the Saturday night that the other half had gone, and then on the Monday, because the bin was not out and he wanted it to be a thing that had happened rather than a thing he had failed at, and then on the Tuesday, and then on the Wednesday morning when he went to see his own gate and looked at it.

And the half that never went was the half that depended on somebody else going to a door.

That is the whole of the mistake and there is nobody else in it.

And then on the Wednesday morning, at about a quarter past seven, the woman next but one came out of her gate with her own bin and she said, "You said it on Saturday."

And he said, "Did I."

And she said, "Out on the footpath."

And he said, "I don't remember doing that."

And she said, "You don't have to."

And he said, "I think I was at the shops."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "What did I say."

And she said, "You'll never ask me."

And he said, "I'm asking you."

And she said, "Now."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "You'd better."

And she went off up the row and he stood in the road with his bin and he did not go and ask her.

He has been at the gate and has not knocked and has gone back in and has come out again, and he has done that four times, and it is not a decision any more. He made the decision on Saturday night. What he is doing now is something else and he does not have a name for it.


Here is what he did with the rest of it.

He put the bin out on the Friday night and it went down on the Friday, which is the other collection and which is the wrong one, and a woman on the row said something about it to her husband on the Saturday morning and he heard it through a window.

He did not go out. He had decided about that at about one in the afternoon and the decision was straightforward and he has not troubled it since.

He ate. He watched the afternoon go. He got his coat off the hook twice.

At about half past two he walked up to the parade and back, which he has done most Fridays for years and which takes him no time at all, and on the way back he went past her gate and she was out.

And she said, "You're still not knocking."

And he said, "I'll do it Monday."

And she said, "You'll not do it Monday."

And he said, "I'll do it Monday."

And she said, "You'll not," and she went in, and she was not being cruel and he has thought about that part since and she was not.

Here is the last of it and it is about half past six and she has come up his row with an envelope that she posts and he has opened the door before she can go.

And she said, "You asked me on Saturday."

And he said, "I did."

And she said, "You wanted me to ask her."

And he said, "I did."

And she said, "I didn't."

And he said, "You said you'd see her about it."

And she said, "I said I'd have a word."

And he said, "That's what I said."

And she said, "That's a different thing," and she did not sound as though she was accusing him of anything, which was the worst of it.

And he said, "I've told her I asked you."

And she said, "I know. She told me what you said."

And he said, "She didn't tell you I asked."

And she said, "She told me you'd told her you asked me."

And he said, "Did she tell you what I said."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "Right," and he shut the door a bit and then he opened it again, and she stayed on the step, which she has no reason to do and which she did anyway.

And she said, "You can go and ask her."

And he said, "I said I'd do it Monday."

And she said, "You said that this afternoon and you said it to me at this gate and I stood here and listened to it."

And he said, "I did."

And she said, "Then you'll not."

And he said, "No," and he looked at the road, and what he said next is the part he will be carrying at the end of the day and he has not got it tidy.

And he said, "I have told her I asked you. And I hadn't. And if I go up there now and say that, then she'll know I sent you, and then she'll want to know why I sent you, and then I'll have to say because I couldn't stand at her gate and ask her, and that's the whole of it and I have not said it out loud before and I'm saying it to you because you were in the road and it was you I said it to."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "So I'm not going to."

And she said, "That's your business."

And he said, "It is, isn't it."

And she said, "You'd better go and ask her for the bin."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Then it's not getting out Wednesday either."

And he said, "I know."

And she said, "It's her bin."

And he said, "I know it's her bin."

And she said, "She'll think it's her idea."

And he said, "She'll think I never asked."

And she said, "Well," and she went away down the row with the envelope that she still posts on a Friday, and he shut the door and stood in the hall with his coat on.

He is going to have to go up there. That is not in doubt and it was not in doubt on the Saturday, and the reason he has not gone is the one he said out loud at about half past six on a Friday evening to a woman standing on his step, and he said it to her because she was the person who could not be asked a follow-up question about it.

And it has cost him the bin on Wednesday and the Wednesday before that, and a fortnight of standing in his own road with the lid up.

And he would have gone on Friday if she had not asked him why, and she asked him why, and he gave her a reason that was the answer to a question nobody had put, because it was the true one and the true one was about Saturday and not about her.

**HIS BIN WENT OUT ON THE FRIDAY NIGHT WHEN THE COLLECTION ON THAT ROW IS A WEDNESDAY, AND HE HAS SAID THE WORDS I ASKED TO BOTH OF THE WOMEN HE SPOKE TO AND THE ONLY ONE HE ACTUALLY ASKED WAS ASKED TO PASS IT ON AND DID NOT, AND HE HAS STOOD IN HIS OWN ROAD WITH THE LID UP FOUR TIMES THIS WEEK WITHOUT KNOCKING, AND HE TOLD THE TRUTH AT HIS OWN DOOR IN THE ONE SENTENCE HE HAD NOT SAID OUT LOUD BEFORE, AND HE SAID IT TO THE WRONG PERSON FOR IT.**
