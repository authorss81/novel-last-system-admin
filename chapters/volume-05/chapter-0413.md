# Chapter 0413 — The Hot Cupboard

Tuesday the twenty-second of May 2018, starting at about half past eight in the morning and running until about half past six in the evening, a block of six flats on a side street, and a shop off that parade, in Tideglass.

**A MAN IN HIS FORTIES SPENT ABOUT EIGHT HOURS IN A BATHROOM ON THE THIRD FLOOR OF THAT BLOCK AND WENT INTO A HOT CUPBOARD OFF THE LANDING TWICE WITH HIS HEAD AND SHOULDERS IN IT, AND IT DID SOMETHING TO HIM BOTH TIMES, AND HE SAT DOWN ON A STEP FOR SIX MINUTES THE SECOND TIME AND CARRIED ON AND FINISHED THAT BATHROOM AND DID NOT RING ANYBODY, AND A MAN IN HIS THIRTIES SAW HIM SITTING THERE AND SAID NOTHING.**

There are six flats in that building, it is about seventy years old, and the door of it is on a side street, and the stair goes up off the passage in two flights with a half-landing between the second and third and between the fourth and the fifth, and the walls on the half-landings are solid and there is no window on either of them.

There is a window on the top landing and there is a window on the stair. Light comes down as far as the second half-landing and then it does not come any further, and a half-landing between the second and the third in that block is a dark place about four foot by six at all hours of the day.

In the wall of the lower half-landing there is a hot cupboard. It is about two foot four high and two foot wide and a foot and a half deep and it holds a cylinder that is four foot long and about two foot across, and the door of it is a plank door on two hinges, and one of the screws in the bottom hinge has worked its way out of the head and gone about two inches into the frame, and that door hangs about two inch proud and swings.

Nobody in that block has looked at that hinge since it went and nobody has said anything about it and the door is on a latch so it does not swing by itself.

---

He was let into that flat at about half past eight by a woman who lives in that third-floor flat, who has been in it since about February and who went out at about nine to a hospital appointment and came back at about ten and then went again.

The job is a bathroom on the third floor. The basin is a pedestal basin from about 1968 with a cracked pedestal and a hot tap that is seized, and the trap is a bottle trap with a tail that has gone brittle, and there is a length of copper on the hot side that has been remade twice by somebody else and is going to be done again.

He started at the pedestal. The old basin came off the floor at about ten past nine in two pieces and the cracked part went down the stairs in a bag.

He had the old trap off and the bottle out by about half past nine and that is when he found that the isolation valve on that hot side is not in the bathroom.

It is in the hot cupboard off that half-landing. It is a gate valve, about two and a half inch, brass, from about 1961, and it is under about four foot of lagging, and it is the only thing in that block that lets the third floor off the main, and it is up a dark half-landing in a cupboard the size of a man.

He had the lagging off that valve at about ten o'clock and turned it with a wrench and nothing happened, and he came out of that hot cupboard about half an hour later with the wrench and about nine foot of lagging in a sack.

It was about seventy-eight in that cupboard and it was about a body heat higher than the landing.

The heat in there comes off that cylinder, and the cylinder is lagged in about three inch, and it is lagged right down to within four inch of the boards, and the top eight inch of it is not lagged at all, and the head of a two hundred gallon cylinder in May in a cupboard the size of a man is a thing you do not put your head in twice.

He had been in there with his head and his right shoulder in to the second joint at about half past ten and he came out backwards on his knees and got his feet under him and stood up in the dark on that half-landing and put his hand flat on the wall.

He was wrong for about a minute.

Not falling, and not gone, and not anything that would have anybody's attention. His ears had gone and the inside of his head was doing something and there was a smell of hot brass and old lagging in the air, and the landing did not feel like a floor for about half a minute.

He stood on that half-landing with his hand on the wall for about a minute, and then he got his vice and his spanner out of his bag on the step, and put the vice on the landing floor, and took the whole of that run off with the spanner, which takes about twenty minutes in a cupboard the size of a man, and that was the first of the two times.

Then he went upstairs and got on with the basin for an hour and a half.

He went into that hot cupboard twice that day. The second of those was at about twenty past one in the afternoon, and it took him about ten minutes.

He was on the ladder that day and the basin and the tap are on the wall opposite the door of that hot cupboard and the tap has to be taken off the old isolation and the new one put on, and the isolation is inside that cupboard and the tail of the pipe is inside that cupboard and there is nowhere to stand and nowhere to put a knee.

He went in at about twenty past one and he came out at about ten to two.

He came out backwards, got about four steps down that stair, and stopped, and sat down in the dark on the lowest tread of the flight above him with his bag between his boots and his hand across his eyes, and stayed there for about six minutes.

For about four seconds he could not have told anybody whether he was all right, and then he could, and then he was angry with himself, and he said out loud, once, to himself, in about nine words, that he would not go back in there again that afternoon.

He kept his word. He finished the basin and the tap and the trap and made good round the pedestal and did the paint on the little bit of tile by going over the top of it, and he came out of that flat at about half past four.

He did not ring anybody. He did not tell anybody. He did not put a word of it in a phone or in a notebook, and there is no notebook, and he did not go back to that hot cupboard at the end of that day to have another look at the light in it.

He has never fixed the hinge on that door and he has never told anybody about that hinge and the door is still standing about two inch out from that frame.

The man in his thirties was in that block on the Tuesday morning, on the second floor, and he was back at about ten to two for something on the fourth.

He came up that stair at about ten to two with a bag of screws in his hand and went on past the lower half-landing on his way up.

The man in his forties was sat on the lowest tread of that flight, with his bag between his boots and his forearm across his eyes.

The light at the top of that stair comes down about four steps and stops. The man in his thirties was at the bottom of that flight when he saw him, and he carried on up, and said nothing, and came back down that same flight about twenty to three after his own business on the fourth floor, and the man in his forties was not on it and has not been on it since.

The man in his thirties has not mentioned it to the man in his forties, and has not mentioned it to anybody, and is not going to.

He was at the counter of a shop off that parade at about twenty to six that evening for a roll of tape and a bag of washers, and he had his money in his hand and put it on the glass and she gave him the change and the bag.

He put his left hand flat on that counter while she was giving him the change and he left it there for about two seconds after the coins were in his hand, and then he took it off and put it in his pocket.

The woman who works in a shop is on that counter. She noticed that, and she has not mentioned it to him, and he did not know she was on that counter and would not have thought about it if he had.

---

**HE CAME DOWN THAT STAIR, AND STOOD AT THE COUNTER FOR TWO SECONDS WITH HIS HAND ON IT, AND PUT THE MONEY IN HIS POCKET AND WENT OUT INTO THE PARADE, AND NOBODY HAS RUNG ANYBODY.**
