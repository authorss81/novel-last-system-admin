# Chapter 0345 — Four in the Morning

Thursday the seventeenth of August 2017, from about half past four in the morning until about half past four in the afternoon, a first-floor corridor in a block by the water, and a flat above three shops off a parade, in Tideglass.

**THERE HAS BEEN A SMELL OF GAS IN THAT CORRIDOR AT ABOUT FOUR IN THE MORNING SINCE THE MIDDLE OF AUGUST, AND THE WOMAN AT THE END OF IT HAS PUT HER SASH UP AND LOOKED AT HER COOKER TWICE AND CARRIED A PLASTIC CHAIR OUT ON TO THE CONCRETE FOR TWO NIGHTS, AND NOBODY HAS PUT HER IN THAT ROOM AND NOBODY HAS ARRANGED ONE WORD OF IT, AND IN ANOTHER PART OF THIS BOROUGH A WOMAN COUNTED THE MONEY IN HER SHOP ON HER OWN TABLE AND THE FIGURE SHE CAME OUT WITH IS A FIGURE AND MEANS NOTHING.**

The block is four storeys and it drops down to a street at the back, and every person who comes into it comes up the one stair at the back of it, and the landing at the top of that stair is about the length of a bath and has two doors off it and a window at the end that nobody has ever cleaned.

The smell came in on the morning of the tenth and it was there again on the twelfth and on the fourteenth and on the sixteenth, and gone by daylight and it is not there at ten at night and it is not there when the wind is on the land.

She gets it before she is properly awake. It is not on her and it is not out of the cooker and it is not out of the corridor, and she has had the sash up since the middle of the month anyway because it has not stopped being warm, and on those four mornings the air has been going out of that window and coming back in again off the water with the smell still in it, which is the part that has made her sit up.

There is no reason on earth for anybody in this borough to be on that landing at four in the morning and there has not been anybody.

---

Here is everything she has done about it, and not one person in that building has put a question to her about any part of it and not one of them has been told a thing.

She has put the sash up and left it up, which she was doing anyway.

She has looked at the cooker twice. She has a gas fire in a second room she has not lit since June, so that is not it either, and she has not gone over the gas in the front of that room where somebody once put a mat over the pipe and it is still matted, and there is a smell of it every time the wind comes off the land, and that is not this.

On the sixteenth she got up at half past four and sat on the edge of the bed for about an hour with the sash up and the door open, and the chair she keeps under that window was under the window, and at about twenty to five she carried it out on to the concrete and turned it round to face the stair.

She slept on it. She has had that room since February and she has slept in it every night since February and she slept on a plastic chair on a landing for two nights and got up at about half past six both times and put the chair back under the window and shut the sash when the sun was on it.

On the Thursday morning she was out in that corridor in the chair with a cardigan over her shoulders when the woman in the room next door along came out, at about twenty past five, and the woman in the room next door along had not been out of that door at that hour since June.

"That'll be the gas," said the woman in the room next door along.

"Aye," said the woman of about fifty-five.

"That's what it is, then." The woman in the room next door along stood in her own doorway with her hand on the frame and did not come out. "I'd got my window shut and I came out to see where it was coming from and it isn't coming from you, because you're sat in the corridor."

"That is where it's coming from," said the woman of about fifty-five, "or I would not be sat in it."

And the woman in the room next door along said right, and went back into her room, and left her own door where she always leaves and nothing else passed between them about it that morning.

The woman in the room next door along has a window that takes nothing off anything and she has shut it because the mornings have been cold since the middle of the month. She has not offered to do anything and she has not been asked to do anything, and nobody has put a note on her door, and there is no person in that building anybody could put a note to, because that block has not been anybody's job since before the middle of June and there is nobody in it who has a telephone.

The man in his forties went up that outside stair at about ten in the morning with a bag, because there was a small job in a flat on that landing and he had been asked for it in July by a man who has a telephone.

He had the smell on that landing. It was not there at ten and it had not been there when he went up, and the window at the end of that corridor was open and the woman of about fifty-five was standing in it with her cardigan on and the chair was back under the window where it goes.

"How long have you had that window open," he said.

"Since the middle of the month," said the woman of about fifty-five. "It's been warm."

"You'll have had a bit of everything through it in that heat."

"There's a smell off the gas off about four in the morning," said the woman of about fifty-five, "and it was on Monday, and it was on Wednesday night, and it is not out there now."

He stood in that corridor for about a minute.

"That's the river coming up under here when the wind's off the water," he said. "It's the same at the bottom of that stair and it's worse on the stairs than it is up here, and I've had it off that gully at the bottom of them for about as long as I can remember. There's a standpipe on that stair that nobody's looked at since the year before last and there's a vent on the back of that block that comes out under the second landing, and that vent is the last thing I'd be looking at if I were going to look at anything at all."

"That is what the woman next door along said."

"I did not know that," said the man in his forties, and then stopped, because there was nothing to do with that and he had heard himself take a woman's opinion into it.

He went and did the job he had been asked for, which was a length of laminate on a kitchen worktop, and it took him about an hour, and he came down that outside stair at about ten to twelve and did not say anything about the smell, and the woman of about fifty-five was in her room with the door shut and he did not knock, and he will not go up that stair again and nobody has asked him what he said about it.

He does not know whether it is the gully. He has said a thing that is true about a river coming up under a road in warm weather, and the woman next door along has said a thing that is also true, and he has put one of them over the other because she got to him first, and he has not been back, and there is no way anybody in that building is going to find out which of the two women was nearer the mark.

---

The woman who works in a shop came up the outside stair at the back of the three shops at about half past four that afternoon, with the day's money in a cloth bag in both hands, and she has gone up that stair with that bag at that time about four times a week since the middle of March and put it on her own table and counted it and taken it down again.

She counted it. Notes first, into one pile, in the order they came in the till, which is a thing she has done since she got the shop and which nobody has ever asked her about and which is not a system and is not a habit she would describe. Then the coin, which takes longer, and then the small silver, which she rolled in her hand and counted twice because two of the pounds are old and shine.

The figure was what the figure was. It was about the same as it had been on the three other Thursdays that month, to within a small amount that means with no cause anybody could give, and no reason on earth for it to be any different, and she wrote it on the back of a hand on her own pad, which is the only thing she writes numbers on, and folded the notes into the leather pouch and put the pouch in the small safe under the counter, and turned the key and took the key off the ring and put it in her pocket.

Nobody in that shop knows what that figure was. Nobody in that shop could say how much money goes through that till in a week, and she could not tell them, and she has never offered, and there is nobody in that borough who has asked her to.

The room at the end of that upstairs corridor is about a mile and a half from that flat, and the two of them have never been in the same room. The woman on the third floor of that block has never in her life climbed a stair that goes past a shop. The one behind that counter has never once been up an outside stair. Neither of them knows that the other one exists.

**A PLASTIC CHAIR WENT OUT ON TO A LANDING AT TWENTY TO FIVE IN THE MORNING AND CAME BACK UNDER A WINDOW AT HALF PAST SIX, AND A FIGURE WENT INTO A SAFE UNDER A COUNTER AT ABOUT HALF PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, AND NEITHER OF THOSE THINGS WAS GOING TO BE ANYBODY'S PROBLEM ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF AUGUST.**
