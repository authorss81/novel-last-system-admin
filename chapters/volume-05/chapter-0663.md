# Chapter 0663 — A Soft Place In A Ceiling

Friday the twenty-seventh of August 2021, in this borough. She is in her own bathroom and then on the stairs and the top landing of her own house from about ten at night until about ten past ten, and she is on the stairs for most of that, and the whole of it is about a quarter of an inch.

**A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-EIGHT GOES UP THE STAIRS OF HER OWN HOUSE FOR THE FIRST TIME SINCE THE SPRING, ALONE, WITH A TORCH, AND FINDS A SOFT PLACE IN THE PLASTER ON A LANDING SHE HAS NEVER SEEN IN THE DARK, AND STANDS UNDER A FIXED LADDER TO A HATCH IN THE CEILING WITH A HIGH LEVEL CISTERN AND A BALLCOCK ON IT THAT SHE HAS NEVER SEEN AND HAS NEVER BEEN NEAR, AND PUTS HER THUMB IN IT, AND GOES BACK DOWN AND PUTS THE CLOTH BACK IN THE BOWL UNDER THE TAP.**

She is a woman in her late seventies. She is on her own all night. Nobody knows she went up.

---

Here is how the night goes wrong, and it is about four minutes long, and it is not mysterious.

The tap is dripping.

It has been dripping since before Christmas and there is a folded cloth in the bowl under it, and the cloth is not a stopper and it is not a thing you leave a tap to do, it is a thing you empty out about twice a day so that a bathroom does not become a swimming pool.

And she has been emptying it twice a day since before Christmas, and at about ten at night on the Friday of the twenty-seventh of August 2021 she goes into the bathroom to do it, and she does it in the dark, because she has got a torch in her hand and has not turned it on, because the bowl is under the bath and she knows where the bowl is.

---

And she puts the cloth in the sink to wash it and she stands up, and the torch is in her hand, and she points it at the ceiling because that is what a torch is for and because her hand does it.

And there is a mark on that ceiling.

It is about a foot across and it is a soft brown in the middle of a white ceiling and it has got a hard edge on it, the way a puddle has a hard edge, and the middle of it is a shade darker than the ring round it, and the middle of it is not flat.

---

And she stands in a bathroom in the dark with a cloth in one hand and a torch in the other and does not move for about a minute.

And what she knows about that mark, standing there, is the following and she has known all of it since she was a girl in a house with a coal shed:

That is a cistern.

Not a pipe and not a roof and not a chimney. A high level cistern is up in the roof space on a board above the top of a house and it is fed by a ballcock, and when a ballcock is past its time it weeps, and a high level cistern that has been weeping for a long time wets the underside of the boarding and then the plaster between the joists, and then it comes down the wall behind the bathroom on the inside, and if nobody does anything about it, it comes through.

And she has a house with a coal shed and no path round the side and three storeys and the oldest of them is the thinnest, and it has had one of these since before the war, and nobody has been up that roof space but her.

---

And here is the part of the night that is the whole of it, and it happens in about a second and a half, in a bathroom, and it decides everything else.

She puts her thumb in it.

Not hard. She puts her thumb in the soft brown in the middle of the mark the way you would press a crust, and the plaster goes in about a quarter of an inch, and it feels exactly like a wet biscuit, and it comes back with a little grit on the pad of her thumb, which is grit, which is the top of the ceiling, which is the underside of the plaster, which is the boards over the cistern, which is the cistern.

And she is standing in her own bathroom at ten at night on a Friday and she has just proved something to herself that she cannot act on.

---

And she goes up.

She does not decide to. That is not how it works. She puts the cloth on the rail and goes out of the bathroom and up the passage and up the stairs, and the torch is in her hand, and the first thing she notices on the stairs is that she has not been up them since the spring and the stair carpet has not been down.

She goes up. That is all. It is not a decision and it does not take a decision.

---

And here is that house, and it is worth setting out plainly because she has not looked at it since the spring and neither has anybody else.

Three storeys. The front door and the front room and the kitchen at the back on the ground floor. The bathroom and the small back bedroom off the half landing. And the top floor, which is two rooms, and a landing.

And she has not been on that landing since the spring.

And the two doors on it are open. They have been open since before Christmas. There is nothing in either of them — a bed frame with no mattress, a wardrobe with no door, a chest of drawers with the drawers out — because the whole of that floor has been empty since the war, except for what a person goes up there for, and she has not gone up there for anything.

---

And she has never seen that landing in the dark.

That is the sentence and it is the reason the whole night is frightening in a way she will not say out loud, and it is this: it is a landing, it is four feet across, and she has walked over it a hundred times in daylight and it has a door on it and a window on it and a hatch in the ceiling of it, and at ten at night with a torch she cannot work out which of the three things in the room is the hatch.

And it takes her about a minute and a half to work it out, and she does it by going round the walls, and when she puts the torch up at the ceiling the hatch is directly above her.

---

It is a hatch about two feet square in a plain ceiling, and it is not a modern one. There is a frame round it and a hatch door on hinges and a hasp and a key, and the key is on a nail in the kitchen.

And next to it, set into the wall, there is the top of a ladder.

Not a folding ladder. A fixed one. Four rungs, screwed to the wall beside the hatch, going up into the dark, and the top of it goes out of sight about two feet above her head, and the rest of it is in the roof space where the torch will not reach.

---

And she stands under it.

And she does not go up it, and the reason she does not go up it takes her about four seconds and it is not fear of a dark roof space and it is not her age.

She has been up a ladder in the dark before, in a coal shed, in a garden, in a garage, in a house in Sale, and she can do it.

What stops her is the other end of the ladder.

A ballcock. A ballcock is a float on the end of a brass arm and it shuts a valve when the water comes up, and if it is weeping then the whole of the cistern is full and the whole of the cistern is about four feet above her head, and a woman of seventy-eight alone at ten at night cannot get two hands onto a ballcock in the dark at the top of a fixed ladder in a roof space that has a cistern in it.

And she knows that. She has known it since she was very small.

---

So she does not go up it.

And here is what she does instead, standing under it, and it is a thing she should not do and does, and it takes about a minute.

She gets the stool from the front room.

Not to climb it. To look up it.

She brings it out of the front room — and that is the first time she has been in that room since the Saturday, and the brush is on the sideboard where it is, and she goes round the sideboard and does not look at it — and she puts the stool under the hatch and stands on the middle rung of it, on the stool, with the torch up above her head, and she gets about a foot and a half higher than she was.

---

And she can see into the roof space.

That is all she gets. She can see the underside of the boards and a joist and about four feet of the black inside, and a bat, and nothing else, because the cistern is on the far side of the joist and the far side of the joist is the side with the light in it on a day like this and the cistern is not on that side.

And she can hear it.

And that is the thing she did not expect and will not forget and does not tell anybody.

She can hear water.

Not a drip. A sort of thin hiss, off to her right and up, and it is a noise she has not heard since she was a child in her father's house, and it means the ballcock is weeping at the rate the ballcock weeps at, and it has been doing it long enough to put a soft brown foot across a ceiling in a bathroom downstairs.

---

And she comes down off the stool and puts the stool back in the front room against the wall, and the brush is still on the sideboard with the handle towards the door, and she goes out of that room and shuts the door behind her, which is the first time she has shut that door since before Christmas.

---

And here is what she cannot do about it, and she works all of that out on the way down the stairs, and it takes about four minutes, and it is the whole of the rest of the night.

There is no way to stop it from downstairs.

That is the finding and she gets it on the landing and it is the reason she goes down those stairs slowly.

The pipe that feeds a high level cistern comes up out of the floor somewhere, and it comes up through a valve, and you turn that valve off and the cistern stops filling and the ballcock stops weeping and the ceiling stops. That is the whole of it. That is what a person would do.

And she does not know where that pipe comes up in that house.

She has lived in that house since before the war and she has never once looked for it, and it is not in the bathroom, because she would have seen it, and it is not under the stairs, and it is not in the kitchen, and the only place left in that house where a pipe comes up through a floor into a cistern is the roof space.

---

So the way to stop that ceiling is up a fixed ladder in the dark, at the top of four rungs, at the far end of a cistern, at ten at night, alone.

And she is seventy-eight.

And she cannot ask anybody to do it.

---

And that is the whole of what the night is. And here is the last of it, and it is on the half landing at about ten past ten, and it is very short.

The landing is over the front room. Not over the bathroom. Over the bathroom is a joist with nothing under it, and the front room is at the end of that run, and that is where the water is going to come through first when it comes through.

And the front room is the second thing on a piece of paper in pencil on a gas envelope on a kitchen table downstairs.

And the front room is the only room in that house anybody has ever sat in, and she has not sat in it since the Saturday of the seventh of August because there is a wire brush on a sideboard in it that she cannot ask anybody about.

---

And so there are two of the six things that are not jobs.

That is what she thinks about, on the stairs, and it is the only clear thinking she does that night.

The gate is a job. The window is a job. The hotplate was a job. The passage light was a job. The tap is a job and she has been doing it with a cloth since before Christmas and a cloth is not a job, it is a habit.

And the front room is not a job. The front room is a room that is going to get a stain on the ceiling in a room nobody sits in, and there is a thing on a sideboard in it that she cannot account for, and neither of those is a thing that can be put on a piece of paper and left on a kitchen table.

---

And she goes into the bathroom and puts the cloth back in the bowl under the tap, and it is wet through, and she pushes it down into the cold water with the side of her hand until it is under, and the tap goes on dripping onto it.

And she turns the torch off and goes to bed.

---

**A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-EIGHT WENT UP THE STAIRS OF HER OWN HOUSE ALONE WITH A TORCH AND FOUND A SOFT PLACE IN THE PLASTER THE WIDTH OF A THUMB AND STOOD ON A STOOL UNDER A HATCH IN THE CEILING AND HEARD A HIGH LEVEL CISTERN WEEPING AND COULD NOT GO UP FOUR RUNG LADDER TO IT AND DOES NOT KNOW WHERE THE PIPE COMES UP, AND THE FIXTURE IS DOWNSTAIRS AND THE BATHROOM IS DOWNSTAIRS AND NOTHING HAS BEEN ASKED FOR AND NOBODY HAS BEEN ASKED, AND THE ROOM IT IS GOING TO COME THROUGH IS THE ROOM SHE DOES NOT SIT IN, AND THAT IS THE SECOND THING ON THE LIST, AND THERE IS A THUMB PRINT IN THE PLASTER OVER THE BATHROOM AND NOBODY WILL EVER SEE IT.**

(End of chapter 0663)
