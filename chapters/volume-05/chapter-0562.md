# Chapter 0562 — The Board Coming Out

The Saturday of the second of May 2020, and most of one evening of it, in the top two floors of a block of flats off a street of this borough.

**SHE HAS PUT A BOARD OUT ONTO THAT CARPET AT ABOUT TEN EVERY EVENING SINCE 2014 AND HER HUSBAND HAS NEVER ASKED HER WHY, AND THE MAN OVER THE TOP OF HER HAS HEARD IT EVERY NIGHT FOR THREE YEARS AND HAS NEVER ASKED HER ANYTHING EITHER, AND ON THE SECOND OF MAY HE CAME DOWN.**

The block is three storeys and there are fifteen flats in it and it was built in about 1958 and the pipework in it is the pipework it came with.

She is in the flat on the second and has been in it since about 2010 and she is a woman in her mid twenties. He is directly over the top of her and has been there since about 2017 and he is a man in his late thirties.

There is very little between that flat and hers. There is about four inches of concrete and eight inches of a building that was not made for two people to be doing separate things at the same hour on purpose.

The board comes out at about ten.

Not at nine, and not at half past. At about ten, on the evenings she does it, which since about the March of 2014 has been four or five evenings a week and has not been anything else since.

She puts it down in front of the electric fire at the front of that flat because the one at the back of it is too cold for it. The feet of it go out onto the carpet, and the noise of that is a short scrape and then a sort of knock as the back leg finds the edge of the carpet and stops there.

The sound carries straight up. There is no carpet on the floor above hers and there is bare wood under that, and she has never once thought about that, because in six years it has never come back down to her.

She does not iron at the weekend while he is in. She does not iron anything of her own before about nine at night. There is one more shirt of hers at the bottom of the thing where the shirts live and it has been there since the February.

Her husband has never asked her about any of this. He is asleep by about half past nine and he has never once said what she is doing when the board comes out, and she has never once explained it, and neither of them has ever treated it as a thing that needed explaining.

---

He has heard it every night for three years.

He has not counted it and he would not be able to say how many nights out of how many. What he could say, if anybody had ever thought to ask him, and nobody ever has, is that he knows the sound of that board the way he knows the sound of the taps in that block at six in the morning.

He has been in that flat since about 2017, which is three years, and the sound was there when he came in. What he cannot get round is how it got to be a thing he knows, because a person does not decide to know a sound, and by the time it is worth knowing it is too late to be finding out what it was.

He has never gone down about it. He has not knocked. He has not put anything under her door. There has never been any conversation between those two flats at all and there would be nothing to open one about.

What he has done, on about four evenings in three weeks, is come down at about ten past ten and stand outside her door and then go back up.

On two of those evenings he did not knock at all.

On the Saturday of the second of May he came down at about ten past ten and knocked once and she opened it, and he stood on the step for barely a moment, said goodnight, went back up, and the two of them have not touched it since.

She is not a person who says things to people who have not asked her anything, and he is not a person who says things to people at all.

She had the board out and about four shirts done and two to go when she heard him on the doormat outside her own door.

She opened it and he was on the step with a jumper on over what he sleeps in, and he did not come forward, and neither of them moved for about a second and a half.

"Goodnight," he said.

"Goodnight," she said.

Then he said, "Sorry," and went up, and she stood inside her own door with it open until his shut at the top of that, and then she shut hers.

She has thought about that word since and she has not got round it. It was not asked for. Nobody had said anything to be sorry about that either of them knew of, and he said it on his own, on his way up, without stopping, and she has not worked out whether it was for the board or for the knocking or for nothing at all.

She has not gone up. She has not mentioned it to her husband. She has not said anything about it to either of them, and he has not come down, and she finished the other two shirts that night and put the board away at about a quarter past, which is what she has done since 2014.

He has not been down since the second of May. She knows what that means, because she is awake until well past midnight and she can hear anybody on that way up, and nothing has gone up or come down that way at all since then.

He said goodnight and went up and said sorry on the way, and she has not got round why. Since that night the subject has not been raised by either of them. Nobody did a favour and nobody agreed anything, and she has thought about that as well as she has thought about the word.
