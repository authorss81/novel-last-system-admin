# Chapter 0893 — The Arch Under The Pallet

Saturday the sixteenth of March 2024, with the two pallets lying one on the other in the far corner at the lock-up, after the office door has been shut. Beneath a pallet, and how far a hand goes along.

**SHE HAS TWO PALLETS LYING IN THE CORNER OF THAT YARD ONE ON THE OTHER AND NOT SQUARE TO EACH OTHER, AND SHE MEANS TO PUT ONE HAND IN FLAT UNDER THE FAR SIDE OF THE UPPER PALLET AND PUSH IT ALONG UNDER IT UNTIL THE ARM STOPS, AND THE WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK IS AT THE GATE OF THE YARD GOING OUT WITH HER COAT OVER HER ARM.**

The pallets come back from the jobs.

Every job that takes them out brings them back, and they go in the corner at the back of that yard because that is where they have always gone, and there are four of them there most weeks and there are two today.

Two pallets in a corner is not a mess. Four of them is. Two on top of each other at a slight angle is a thing that happens forty times a year in a yard like this, and it is finished when somebody needs the corner for something.

That corner has not been needed for anything since the summer.

The top one is not lying flat on the other.

From the office steps, through the gate, you can see that much, and you can see it from the office window as well, which is how it came to be looked at twice. The top pallet is tipped, its one edge up, sitting on the edge of the bottom one at the wall side, and the two of them are not square to each other by about the width of a plank.

She has been meaning to look at that for a fortnight, because the top pallet is the one the sheeting gets put on.

The way in is from the far side. The hand goes in flat, palm up, under the top pallet, with the fingers leading and the forearm after them, and it goes along under it.

The arm goes in about eight inches and stops on wood.

---

The woman who books work goes to the gate of that yard from inside about five with her coat over her arm, on her way out.

"You've got the small truck booked for the morning."

"It's booked for half eight."

"Half eight is the morning."

"I know what half eight is."

"Then I'll tell them half eight."

She stood at the gate with her hand flat on the bar across it and did not lift that either, and then told her that the Thursday gate post had been cancelled and that the man would ring back, and she went out through it and the gate came to behind her.

---

Eight inches in, the hand stops on wood.

Not on the underside of the top pallet, which is a foot above the concrete at that point, and not on the concrete.

On the stringer of the bottom pallet.

A pallet has blocks and it has boards, and it has three long pieces of timber running the length of it under the top boards, and those three long pieces are the stringers, and the stringer of a pallet sits a good deal lower down than the top of a block does.

Which means the stringer of the bottom pallet is higher than anything else on the bottom pallet except its blocks, and the bottom pallet's blocks are a foot away from where the hand is going in.

So the hand has come on the wrong timber. It has come on the stringer of the lower pallet while it was reaching for the floor.

She puts the hand in again on the same line and goes in past the near board of the top pallet, palm up, and the arm follows, and it goes in about eight inches and stops on the same stringer.

The top pallet is sitting on the stringer of the bottom one.

The hand comes out and goes in from the near edge, and on that side the air goes right in under the top pallet for its whole width, to the bottom pallet's block, and the block is on the concrete.

So the upper pallet is propped on one edge, on a stringer, with the other side of it hanging over the block of the pallet below.

Nobody does a thing like that on purpose, and nobody calls it a fault.

What happened is that the bottom pallet went down on the concrete with its blocks down, the top one went on at an angle, and the stringer of the lower pallet caught the edge of the upper one and propped it.

That is how it stays. A pallet propped like that does not move: it is propped on its own weight, and the only way to shift it would be to lift the high edge.

She gets her fingers under the high side and feels the gap, and then under the low side and feels nothing at all.

That is the end of it, and it costs nothing.

The pallets are sound softwood. The top one has a split in a board on the far side from where the stringer caught it, and the split runs the length of the board and is dry and has been there since the summer, and it does not go into the stringer, and the stringer that is doing the propping is solid along its whole length.

The pair of them is more stable than if they were squared up, and the split in the board does not open any further while the load stays on the high side.

A squared pair of pallets carries its load straight down through the blocks onto the ground. A crossed pair carries its load down at an angle onto the corner of a stringer, and a corner of a stringer on a concrete floor does not move.

The corner will be exactly as it is next month, and the arch of air under the far side of the top pallet will be there when the sheeting goes on it.

Two things about that corner explain why it stays as it is.

The first is the corner itself. Both pallets are square, and both are in the corner of a yard whose floor is concrete, and the concrete at that corner is the oldest and hardest concrete in the yard, because everything got tipped at that end of it while the yard was being made and then it was all trodden flat.

The second is that a pallet propped on a stringer cannot be walked into. Somebody can stand on the high edge of that top pallet and it will not go down. Somebody can put a hand on it and push, and it will not move, because the load is going down a corner onto a floor and not along anything.

So the corner is a corner that four people have walked round for two months rather than step over, and it will be that corner at the end of this month.

One more thing about that corner, and it is why they have been left unsquared.

Squaring them would mean lifting the top pallet off, which means standing over a corner of a yard and taking the weight of one pallet in both hands while the other pallet is walked out from underneath, and then putting it back down square on the low side in the dark, and then finding that it has not gone down square at all.

That is not a thing to be done in a yard in March, for a corner that is doing exactly what it was left to do.

The only thing to say about it is that anything put on that top pallet goes to the low end of it, which is how boards get found in the wrong place on a Monday morning, and which is a thing about pallets rather than about the yard.

The top pallet is a good thumb's width higher at its propped edge than along the other side of it, which is how a stack of two ends up propped and stays propped.
