# Chapter 0584 — Nothing Came of It

The Wednesday of the fifth of August 2020, from about half past nine in the morning until about six in the evening, in a shop, a square, an alley behind a parade and a pub in this borough, none of which has anything to do with another.

**SHE SAID FOUR WORDS TO A MAN IN A SHOP DOOR AT HALF PAST NINE AND THEY WERE STILL TRUE AT SIX IN THE EVENING, AND NOTHING CAME OF ANY OF IT THAT COULD BE PUT ANYWHERE, AND NONE OF THE THREE OTHER THINGS IN THAT BOROUGH TODAY HAS ANYTHING TO DO WITH HER, OR WITH HIM, OR WITH EACH OTHER.**

The fountain is in a square off a road with shops on it, and it comes out of a spout set into a pale surround about hip high, and about thirty people a day use it.

It runs brown for the first half minute. Then it runs clear.

It has done that since some time before the middle of July. Nobody has reported it. The two women who use it most have both learned to stand there and count, and the water comes out the same colour whatever the weather is doing and whatever is in the pipes that day.

Nobody in that square knows why and nobody has told anybody. It has been cleaned twice and the spout has been looked at by a man who did not know what he was looking for, and it goes on doing what it does.

---

She is a woman in her late seventies and she was at that shop at about half past nine on the Wednesday morning for a broadsheet and a jar of something.

The man is in his mid thirties and he has been coming on a Wednesday for a bit because she was not managing what she was carrying.

She was standing in the door with the jar in her hand when she said it. She said it to him, not to anybody else, and she said it flatly and without any weight on it:

"I won't say anything to him."

He said, "Right," and took the jar and the broadsheet and put them in the cloth she had come in with and held it out to her, and she paid for them, and she went home by the way she goes.

There was no third person. Nobody heard it but him.

He thought about it at about twenty to twelve, standing at the till, and he worked out that the somebody was the man two doors along, who has been digging something up at the front of her garden since about the June.

At about twenty past two he went and put a note through her door that said he was going to ring the council about the digging and to let her know either way. He did not expect to hear anything and he did not particularly want to.

She found it about half past three. She read it standing just inside her own front door. She did not put it down where anybody coming in would see it; she carried it through to the kitchen and put it face down in the box she keeps the things she means to deal with in, and there is a great deal in that box.

She has not said anything to the man two doors along. She was not going to, and she had said so out loud at about half past nine before anybody had asked her to, and having said it out loud she found she had one less thing to carry about on it, which was not what she had expected and was not nothing.

At six in the evening the four words were still true and he was still on that street and she was still in that house and neither of them had anything to do about it.

She had the tap on in the kitchen and stood at the open door of it for a bit, and there was a thing she has been trying to name since the March which she got no further with now than she did then. It is not one thing and it is not a big thing. It does not take a shape when she tries to give it one, and there is nobody in this borough she could put it in front of who would know what to do with it, and nobody in this borough who has any reason to go looking for it either, and she has decided again, as she decides most weeks, that it is very likely nothing at all and that it is probably about a timetable nobody has ever looked at.

She put the kettle on and it is a Wednesday and there is nothing after this.

The fourth space in that alley is the one the shop trolleys do not go back into. The catch on it has been broken since the June and one comes away from the bar perfectly well and then simply cannot be got back in. Nobody uses it. There is a geranium in it that somebody brought from home and nobody has ever asked about, and it is watered, and by now it is the best-kept thing in that alley.

The barometer above the bar of that pub has not moved since the June. There is a row of glasses on it and nobody has knocked it to set it, and the man behind that bar does not know what the weather is doing and works off how much daylight is coming in at the door and off the jukebox at the far end. It was half past two when the daylight went off that end and everybody in there agreed it was, without a word passing between any of them.

Three things, three places, one Wednesday, and not one of them has anything to do with another and none of them has anything to do with the four words. She said four words at half past nine and he said one at half past nine and both of those were still an accurate account of the situation at six in the evening, and the note went under her door and stayed under her door, and nothing came of any of it.
