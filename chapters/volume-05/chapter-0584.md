# Chapter 0584 — Nothing Came of It

The Wednesday of the fifth of August 2020, from about half past nine in the morning until about six in the evening, in a shop, a square, a car park and a pub in this borough that have nothing to do with one another.

**SHE SAID FOUR WORDS TO A MAN IN A SHOP DOORWAY AT HALF PAST NINE AND THEY WERE STILL TRUE AT SIX IN THE EVENING, AND NOTHING CAME OF ANY OF IT THAT COULD BE PUT ANYWHERE, AND NONE OF THE THREE OTHER THINGS IN THAT BOROUGH TODAY HAS ANYTHING TO DO WITH HER, OR WITH HIM, OR WITH EACH OTHER.**

The fountain is in a square off a road with shops on it and it has a bowl set into a low wall and a push thing on the side of it, and about thirty people a day use it.

It runs brown for the first half minute. Then it runs clear.

It has done that since some time before the middle of July. Nobody has reported it. The two women who use it most have both learned to stand there and count, and the water comes out the same colour whatever the weather is doing and whatever is in the pipes that day.

Nobody in that square knows why and nobody has told anybody. The bowl has been cleaned twice and the push thing has been looked at by a man who did not know what he was looking for, and it goes on doing what it does.

---

She is a woman in her late seventies and she was at that shop at about half past nine on the Wednesday morning for a paper and a jar of something.

The man is in his mid thirties and he has been coming on a Wednesday for a bit because she was not managing the bags.

She was standing in the doorway with the jar in her hand when she said it. She said it to him, not to anybody else, and she said it flatly and without any weight on it:

"I won't say anything to him."

He said, "Right," and took the jar and the paper and put them in a bag, and she paid for them, and she went home by the way she goes.

There was no third person. Nobody heard it but him.

---

He thought about it at about twenty to twelve, standing at the till, and he worked out that the somebody was the man two doors along, who has been doing something to the front of her hedge since about the June.

At about twenty past two he went and put a note through her letterbox that said he was going to ring the council about the hedge and to let her know either way. He did not expect to hear anything and he did not particularly want to.

She found it about half past three. She read it standing in her own front room. She did not put it on the hall table and she did not put it anywhere you would see it; she carried it through to the kitchen and put it face down in the drawer where she keeps the things she means to deal with, and there is a great deal in that drawer.

She has not said anything to the man two doors along. She was not going to, and she had said so out loud at about half past nine before anybody had asked her to, and having said it out loud she found she had one less thing to carry about on it, which was not what she had expected and was not nothing.

---

At six in the evening the four words were still true and he was still on that street and she was still in that house and neither of them had anything to do about it.

She had the tap on in the kitchen and she stood at the window for a bit, and there was a thing she has been trying to name since the March which she got no further with now than she did then. It is not one thing and it is not a big thing. It does not take a shape when she tries to give it one, and there is nobody in this borough she could put it in front of who would know what to do with it, and nobody in this borough who has any reason to go looking for it either, and she has decided again, as she decides most weeks, that it is very likely nothing at all and that it is probably about a bus timetable.

She put the kettle on and it is a Wednesday and there is nothing after this.

The bay at number four in that car park is the one the trolleys do not go back into. The catch on it has been broken since the June and a trolley comes away from the rail perfectly well and then simply cannot be got back into that one bay. Nobody uses it. There is a geranium in it that somebody brought from home and nobody has ever asked about, and it is watered, and by now it is the best-kept thing in that car park.

The clock in the back of that pub stopped in the June. There is a shelf of glasses on it and nobody has winded it, and the man behind that bar does not know what time it is and works off the light coming in the window and off the machine in the corner. It was half past two when the light went off the wall and everybody in that room agreed it was, without a word passing between any of them.

Three things, three places, one Wednesday, and not one of them has anything to do with another and none of them has anything to do with the four words, and the four words were said at half past nine in the morning and at six in the evening they were still true and nobody had to do a single thing about them.

**SHE SAID FOUR WORDS AT HALF PAST NINE AND HE SAID ONE AT HALF PAST NINE AND BOTH OF THOSE WERE STILL AN ACCURATE ACCOUNT OF THE SITUATION AT SIX IN THE EVENING, AND THE NOTE WENT UNDER HER MAT AND STAYED THERE, AND NOTHING CAME OF ANY OF IT.**
