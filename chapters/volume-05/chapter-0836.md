# Chapter 0836 — Chippings Round The Butt

Monday the fourteenth of August 2023, round the butt at number ten from the middle of the morning until the shade has crossed the yard, and chippings sunk from under a rim is the whole of that Monday.

**THERE IS A BUTT ON ITS BASE AT NUMBER TEN WITH THE CHIPPINGS SUNK ROUND IT AND SHE MEANS TO PACK THEM BACK WITHOUT MOVING IT, AND HER SISTER COMES IN AT THE FRONT AND GOES OUT THE SAME WAY AND LEAVES A BOTTLE ON THE FLAGS.**

That butt is the black one against the shed wall, on four flags with chippings round the foot of it to keep the base off the wet.

The chippings have gone down on one side since spring. Rain off the shed roof has washed the fines out from under the top layer, and the top layer has settled into the gaps, and the rim of the base now shows a thumb of dark plastic above the stones where it used to sit flush.

A butt out of level by a thumb does not sound like much. It is enough to put a lean on two hundred litres, and a lean on that weight wants to go on leaning, and the foot of a butt is the one part of it that cannot be patched once it splits.

She does not move it. A full butt is not to be shifted by one person, and an empty one wants a day to dry before it is dragged, and this one is half full and staying where it is.

Packing round a standing thing is done with the hands and a trowel, a handful at a time, working under the rim from the outside.

She starts with a bucket of clean chippings from the heap by the fence, the same size as what is there, washed by rain and pale.

The old chippings round the low side she pulls back first with the trowel, down to where the fines still sit firm. Dust rises off them, soft, and under the dust the stones are dark where the wet has sat longest.

The fines under are the bed. Where the roof water has run hardest the fines are gone altogether and the trowel meets the flags with a scrape, and where it has run less they sit in a thin skin, grey and close.

She does not dig into that skin. A shallow fix wants the firm part left as it is, and new stones laid up to it, and the tired ground under it not opened.

She works clean chippings under the rim a handful at a time, pushing them in with the flat of the trowel, feeling with her fingers where they go. The rim of the base is sharp plastic, and under it there is a hollow the depth of a finger where the old stones have dropped away.

The first handfuls vanish under the rim without raising anything. That is right. A hollow takes a lot before it shows, and the showing comes all at once at the end.

Her sister comes in at the front in the middle of the morning with shopping, puts a bottle of something on the flags by the butt, and goes out the same way.

“Leave that there till I am through with the stones,” she tells her.

“I am not stopping,” her sister tells her, and goes back through the gate with the bag still on her arm.

The bottle stands on the flags clear of the chippings, where it cannot be knocked, and the flags round it are dry. The tap above the butt on the wall is as it was in the morning, with a dry mouth and dust on the spout, and she does not touch it and does not reach for it through the whole job.

She thinks about the weight while she feeds stones under the rim. Two hundred litres leaning half a finger off true wants to keep leaning, slow as seasons, and the mending of it is a morning with a trowel and not a rescue. That suits her. Big jobs ask for crews and money, and small ones ask only for attention before the lean grows.

Her fingers learn the rim as she goes, the sharp edge of plastic, the cold of stones, the soft give of dust between. The hand knows before the eye when the line runs true, by the way the finger rides without dipping.

Round follows round. Chippings in, trowel flat under the rim, fingers after to feel the line, boot back to look at the rim against the shed wall.

The line to go by is the mould mark round the base, a faint ring put on in the making. Where the rim sits true that ring runs level with the top of the stones all round, and where it has sunk the ring dips.

She brings the low side up to the ring and then a grain more, because fresh packing settles under the first rain and a grain over is level after a week.

By the time the shade has crossed the yard the ring runs true all round. She runs a finger along it twice to be sure, and the finger meets the same height on the low side as on the high.

She pulls the surplus back from the face of the base with the brush, so the stones lie up to it and not over it, and sweeps the flags round the butt clean.

---

The clean bucket goes back half full, and the trowel is wiped on the grass, and the bottle is taken in off the flags.

She stands back by the shed and looks at what is there. A ring of chippings packed round the butt base, the tap above it untouched with dust on its spout, and on the flags where the bucket stood the drips of dust, pale rings that brush away.
