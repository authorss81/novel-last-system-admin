# Chapter 0734 — The Line Pegged Tighter

Thursday the sixteenth of June 2022, in this borough. She is in her own house at number ten from first light until dark, and the whole of it is a table that gets cleared and a yard she walks to the end of.

**A WOMAN IN HER EARLY THIRTIES PUTS THE HAMMER AND SCREWDRIVER AWAY IN THE DRAWER UNDER HER OWN WORKTOP, LEAVES THE THREE-LINE PAPER ON THE TABLE, SPEAKS TO HER SISTER AT THE GATE ABOUT THE NINETY YARDS, AND WALKS TO THE END OF HER OWN YARD AND BACK BEFORE DARK.**

She is a woman in her early thirties at number ten on Thursday. Her sister waits at the gate near dark and she speaks to her out there.

---

She starts at the table with the tools laid out on it since the end of May, ply and hammer and screwdriver and tape and pencil beside the three-line paper.

The shed felt is still gone off the front edge and the ply still will not do, and she knows now that no tool on this table will mend it. She picks up the hammer and the screwdriver and carries them to the drawer under the worktop and puts them away inside it, side by side.

The drawer is no longer empty. It has two tools in it and nothing else. The paper stays on the table where it has been since April, with the washing folded beside it and the three lines showing.

She shuts the drawer on the tools and leaves the paper out, and the table looks like a table again for the first time in weeks.

---

At the middle of the day she takes the tape roll and the pencil and puts them on the shelf above the worktop, out of the way.

The peg bag comes out of the drawer under the worktop, and she counts the pegs standing at the table because there is nothing else to do with her hands, and there are eleven of them where a line across that yard wants twelve.

She puts eleven back in the bag and the bag in the drawer, and does not go out to count the line.

The ply offcut she carries out to the yard and leans against the wall where it was, flat. It is not rubbish and it is not use, and the wall is where such things live till they are one or the other.

She pegs the washing out straight across the draught and brings it in dry at the middle of the afternoon, and folds it without covering the paper.

---

Her sister comes to the gate at dark and stands with her hands on the top bar.

"You cleared the table," her sister says.

"Half of it," she says. "The tools are away. The paper is still out."

"Will you bring it round?" her sister says. "The ninety yards. You could bring it to nine and we could look at it together."

She looks down the road toward number nine with its light on, and then at the paper behind her on the table through the open back door.

"I will not tonight," she says. "Not the whole way. I have not walked it since December and I will not start at dark."

Her sister nods, and neither argues, because both know the road by heart and both know what is on it.

"But I will walk to the end of my own yard," she says. "That much I will do."

---

She does it while her sister watches from the gate, not as a promise and not as a show, just as a walk.

She goes down the yard past the shed with its felt gone and the ply against the wall, past the line pegged straight, to the end wall where the yard meets the lane, and she lays her hand flat on the brick for a moment.

Then she turns and walks back, steady, with her sister watching, and the yard is only her own yard and not the road, but it is further than she has walked in an evening since April.

Her sister watches her in, and then goes off down the road with her bag on her arm and does not look back at the yard.

Then she goes out again as far as the line and puts her hand on the wire, and the peg at the shed end is the one she moved in May and pushed a finger further than it wanted, and it is still where she left it.

She does not loosen it. It is her own peg and her own hand that put it there, and there is nobody else who could shift it, and that is as much as the yard has to offer anybody tonight.

She goes in and puts the kettle on with the paper still on the table and the tools away in the drawer, and the yard behind her running the whole way to the end wall and no further, because that is where the wall is.
