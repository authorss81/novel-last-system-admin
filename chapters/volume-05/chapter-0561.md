# Chapter 0561 — Nine Stakes

The Monday of the twenty-seventh of April 2020, and the twenty-five days before it, at the edge of this borough.

**HE HAS KNOWN THAT GROUND SINCE HE WAS TEN AND THE PLACE HE KEEPS ON IT IS THE ONLY THING OUT THERE THAT IS HIS, AND IT IS GOING, AND HE HAS NOT BEEN TOLD A SINGLE DAY OF IT, AND ON THE SECOND OF APRIL A WOMAN HE HAS SPOKEN TO TWICE ASKED HIM ONE QUESTION THAT WAS NOT ABOUT ANY OF IT.**

There is a piece of ground off a road at the edge of this borough, with a track onto it and a gate at the top and about nine places on it, and each place is a building about eight foot by ten put up by whoever kept it, and there is a tap at the near end and a place to turn at the bottom.

There have been about nine of them all that time and there are nine now. Two of them have changed hands since 2011 and one of those was to a man who does not use it, and everybody who keeps something out there knows who the other seven are and nobody has ever asked any of them where their water comes from.

He is a man in his early sixties. He has had the third place from that gate since about 1971, which is when he was about ten and his father was still able to lift a thing.

He built the front of it himself when he was about nineteen, out of blocks that came off a building at the end of the road where they had taken something down. He is one of two people alive who could say what those blocks are and the other one is in a home about ten miles off and does not remember.

He has not been inside it since about 2011. He has not been on that ground at all since about 2011, not once, and there are about thirty people in this borough who know exactly where it is and not one of them has any idea what he has not been doing.

It is going. He has known that since about the September before last.

He was told nothing dated. Nobody has put anything in his hand and nobody has said a day. Somebody in an office asked him a fortnight ago whether he had anything on that ground and he said no, and that was the whole conversation, and he went on being asked by his own sister as if the conversation had not happened.

She is a woman in her mid forties and she works in a building in this borough that does not have anything to do with any of it. She was asked, in about the middle of March, to do one thing at that ground and one thing only, and the one thing was to take a man round it.

The gate has a padlock on it and the only spare key in the world was kept in the office she works in, which is how she came to be the one who was asked.

She asked him on the Friday before, and she said it plainly, and she did not give him any reason and he did not ask her for one.

They went round it on the second of April at about ten in the morning. She had the gate open before they got there and she waited with her hand on it until he was through.

He was not in a hurry and she was not, and it took about fifty minutes.

He showed her the nine places and the tap and the place to turn at the bottom, and he said one thing about each of them and nothing else, and she asked him one question and he answered her in one word.

She has not been back to that ground since the second of April and she was not asked to go back.

The question was at the near end, by the tap, at about ten past, and it had nothing to do with the place or with what was happening to it.

"Have you ever done anything else?"

"No," he said.

She said, "Right," and wrote nothing down, because she had not been asked to write anything down and there was nothing on any sheet anywhere with his name near it, and she closed the gate behind them and put the key back where it lives the same afternoon.

Neither of them has raised it since. Not the place, not the ground, not the fact of it going. She has sent him one message about the weather and he has not answered that one either.

---

On the Monday of the twenty-seventh of April he went, at about nine in the morning, on his own, and he had not been on that ground since 2011 and he went anyway.

The gate is not shut. The padlock is hanging in the catch and not in it, and she shut it and locked it and put the key back where it lives on the second of April and she has not been up that track since, and whoever came up here last did not shut it behind them either.

He did not shut it either. He went through it and left it where it was.

His place is the third one from the gate.

There is a new padlock on the front of it, and it is not his, and about four things are in there that are not his, and a plastic thing he has never seen. He did not go in. He stood at that door for about an hour in the end, in the sun, with the sun on his face, and he touched nothing at all.

Those nine stakes in the grass along the back of that place are his. He and his father strained about nine metres of wire between them in about 1979 to keep dogs off what they had, and the wire is still up, and eight of those stakes are still where they were, and the other one has gone over and is lying about two foot out from where it was in the grass.

He looked at that for a while and then he went home for his spade and brought it back in the car and put it away again, without touching the stake, and came away.

He has not told the woman and he has not told his sister and he has not told anybody. The wire is his and it is not going with the place if anybody ever comes to look, and he has not asked how anybody would know whose it was.

**HE HAS STOOD AT THE DOOR OF HIS OWN PLACE FOR AN HOUR AND HAS TOUCHED NOTHING AND HAS TOLD NOBODY, AND THOSE NINE STAKES ARE IN THE GRASS AND EIGHT OF THEM ARE WHERE THEY WERE AND ONE IS NOT, AND HE HAS NOT GONE BACK AND NOBODY HAS ASKED HIM.**
