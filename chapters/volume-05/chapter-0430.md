# Chapter 0430 — The Hook

Thursday the twenty-sixth of July 2018, from about a quarter to nine in the morning until about half past seven that evening, the back of a short terrace off a side street, in Tideglass.

**HE TOOK THIRTY FOOT OF FLEX OFF A HOOK IN A YARD AT TEN TO TEN IN THE MORNING WITHOUT KNOCKING AND PUT IT BACK ON THE SAME HOOK AT FIVE, AND THE MAN IT BELONGS TO WATCHED THE WHOLE OF THE PUTTING BACK FROM HIS OWN FRONT ROOM, AND HE HAS SAID NOTHING TO ANYBODY AND IS NOT GOING TO.**

The hook is a length of wrought iron about six inch long, screwed into the brick of a low outbuilding in the back of the middle house of that terrace, at about shoulder height, facing into the yard.

On it there is a four-way extension lead on a reel, with about thirty foot of flex on the drum and a drum that is not quite round any more and a cable tie round the flex about two foot from the socket end that has been there since 2013 and has been tightened twice. The flex has the print on it nearly rubbed off and the plug pins are bright where they go into a socket and black where they do not.

It is not shared and it is not communal and it has never been lent by anybody. It belongs to the man in his sixties, who bought it himself in a shop in the town eight miles off in the autumn of 2013 with money of his own, and it is on that hook every evening and every morning, and he has never once said to anybody in that terrace that it is his, because nobody in that terrace has ever asked.

---

The man in his forties went through that yard at ten to ten on the Thursday morning on his way to the back of the second house along, where he was in the middle of taking a range out and had put the isolator off and found the wall he needed to open up had an old spur on it that had been dead since about 2015.

He came through the yard from the passage between the houses, past the outbuilding, about nine foot from that hook, at a walking pace, on his way to where the work was, and he was carrying a breaker in one hand.

He stopped. He looked at the reel on the hook for about as long as it takes to decide to take your own gloves off, which is about two seconds, and he took it off the hook, and he uncoiled about ten foot of it in his left hand while walking, and he went and did the job.

He did not knock. He did not call out. He did not look at the front of the middle house, and there was nobody looking out of it, and nobody in that terrace knew that anything had happened until about half past three that afternoon.

A man takes things. He has taken things his whole life and there is no way of being a man of that age in this trade and not having taken things. It is not a rule he goes by and it is not a habit either. It is that a man looks at a thing in reach and his hand has already got it before the rest of him has asked anybody.

He did not put a wire round that reel and he did not unscrew it off the iron and take the whole hook off the brick, which a man with a bag in his van would have done and would have put back after dark. He took the reel off the hook and carried it out of that yard, which is the least version of it there is, and he carried it back to the same hook in the same state, and both of those things are the reason the man in his sixties decided what he decided at about a quarter to four, and the man in his forties has no idea that any of it is connected.

He used it between about ten and about four and he did the whole of that day on it. He found the old spur dead behind a faceplate that had been painted over twice, and he found a supply under the stairs of that house that was on its own circuit, and he cut the spur out and the plate off and a new plate in and a length of two-and-a-half out of the box in his own van, and the range is in the front of that house on its own cable with a fuse in a carrier that is not the right rating and has not been since about 2011, and that is a thing for another day and not this one.

He had the reel back on that hook at about five o'clock, and the thirty foot of it wound on the drum, and the cable tie where it had always been, and the plug in his hand and not in the wall, and he did not knock on that day either.

---

The man in his sixties came out of the front of his own house at about half past three that afternoon to go to the shop and stopped in his own back passage with his hand on the frame of the outside door.

There was nothing on that hook.

He looked at it. He looked at the brick round it where the iron was bedded in, and at the mark in the dust and the mortar under it where the reel had been sitting since the autumn of 2013, and at the bare hook, and then he looked at the yard, which was empty apart from the concrete and a wheelbarrow against the outbuilding and a man in his thirties at the far end of it with a sash on trestles in front of him.

He went back inside and put the kettle on and did not put his coat on.

He was not in any doubt about it. There are five things in this borough that would take a reel of flex off a hook like that and there are about two hundred men who could have done it, and he did not need to work out which, and he has never tried.

At about one the woman who lives at the end of that terrace came through that yard to get to her own bin, and she stopped by the trestles and looked at the sashes and then up at the outbuilding, and said, "Whose is that hook, then."

"That?" said the man in his thirties.

"That thing it was on a minute ago," she said. "There was a coil of something on it."

"He'll have taken it back," said the man in his thirties.

She said, "Right," and went and moved her bin and came back through and went into her own house.

He stood at the trestles afterwards for about a minute with the plane in his hand, and he knew exactly what he had just said, and he knew that it was not true, and he knew that she had been in that yard at about a quarter to ten because he had watched her come through it and go out again before the man in his forties came the other way.

He did not go and put it right. He has not gone and put it right. She has not thought about it again and has not asked anybody in that terrace about it, and there is nothing at the end of it for her.

He stood at his own front room window from about ten to four, which is the window that looks across that passage and into the front of the yard, and at about five he saw a van come up the side street and stop, and a man get out with a coil of flex over his shoulder, and walk to the outbuilding, and wind the reel off his shoulder, and put it up on that hook, and turn the drum on it once with his thumb, which is what a man does when he has wound a cable on with his arm instead of a handle.

The van went away up the side street.

The man in his sixties was at that window the whole of that and did not go out, and did not open the front of his house, and did not go across that yard, and has not mentioned to anybody in that terrace that he has got a reel of flex and that it was off his hook for about seven hours on a Thursday in the middle of summer.

He has decided something about it and the something is this: he is not going to be a man who makes a thing of thirty foot of flex.

There is nothing in what happened to him. Nothing was damaged. The socket end of that flex has the earth pin on it bent about five degrees and it was like that before, and he has not even noticed that it is like that. A thing went off a hook in a yard for seven hours and came back to it. A man in this borough is not going to spend a single evening of his life on that, and he decided that at about a quarter to four in the afternoon with his coat on, standing in his own back passage, and he has not changed his mind since and he will not be asked about it, because nobody in that terrace is going to ask him about it.



For most of that Thursday the other man in this borough had his van in the same yard and was at the far end of it, first from about half past eight until about one and then again from about two until about four, with a set of four sashes on trestles in front of him and a plane and a block of glass and a bottle of glue.

He saw all of it, from the first second to the last, and has never mentioned it to a living soul.

At about ten to ten on the Thursday morning he had a reason to say something. The man in his forties came past about nine foot from him with a reel over his shoulder and the man in his thirties had his plane in his hand and his mouth open, and he shut it.

The reason he shut it is his own and it is not a good one. He is not going to be the man in a yard who tells another man he has taken something off a hook. He has never been that man in his life. There is no circumstance he can think of in which he would be that man, and he had about half a second to find out whether he was still that sort of person and he was not, and he planed a piece of oak instead.

**HE STOOD BEHIND HIS OWN FRONT ROOM WINDOW WITH HIS COAT ON AND HIS HANDS IN HIS POCKETS AND LET THAT VAN GO UP THE SIDE STREET, AND HE HAS NOT RAISED IT WITH ANYBODY AND HE IS NOT GOING TO.**
