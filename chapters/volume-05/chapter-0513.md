# Chapter 0513 — What Brings You Here

The Wednesday of the twentieth of August 2019, from about half past six in the evening until about half past nine in the evening. The top of a sea wall, and about two hundred yard of it, in Tideglass.

**SHE HAS SAT NEAR THE MIDDLE OF THAT WALL, A GOOD THIRTY YARD OFF HIM, A DOZEN TIMES OVER FIVE MONTHS, AND TWICE HE HAS PUT A QUESTION TO HER ABOUT IT AND TWICE SHE HAS ANSWERED HIM TRUTHFULLY AND NEITHER ANSWER WAS THE ACTUAL ONE, AND HE HAS ACCEPTED THE SECOND, AND NOBODY ELSE ON THAT WALL WOULD HAVE ASKED HER THE RIGHT ONE, AND AT TEN TO SIX THAT WEDNESDAY HE TOLD HER THAT HE WOULD NOT BE THERE THE ONE AFTER.**

He has been going to that wall on a Wednesday evening since about 2014. He goes for a bit past six and is back at about half past seven.

She is a woman in her middle thirties, has been in this borough since about 2012, and has never fished in her life.


Here is what he has, and he has had it since about 2014 and he would put it in about four seconds if anybody ever asked him.

He goes because it is the only evening of the week that belongs to him. He does not expect to catch anything and there is a bait shop four streets off that he goes into about twice a year. He sits on that wall, which is about four foot wide and has a lip on it you can rest an arm on, and he puts a line out into about nine feet of water and he brings it in at about half past seven.

On a Wednesday in about March he noticed her at about twenty past six, sitting about thirty yard along the wall with a bag beside her, and he did not do anything about it.

He has not done anything about it since either. That is how it has been. She comes, he comes, and about thirty yard of wall is between them, and neither of them has ever moved along the wall towards the other.


And here is the two times he asked.

The first was in about April. She was there and he came along the wall and stopped at about ten yard because you cannot walk past a person on a thing four foot wide without stopping.

He said, "Everything all right?"

And she said, "Fine."

And he said, "Just passing."

And she said, "Aye."

And he went back to where he had been.

The second was in June, in the rain, when sitting thirty yard apart was not a thing either of them could do.

He came along and stopped and said, "You'll get soaked."

And she said, "I'll go in a minute."

And he said, "Right."

And he went back.

Both of those are true reasons and both of them are about the weather and neither of them is the reason either.


Here is the actual thing, and neither of them has said it.

She does not want anything off that wall and she has never once looked at the water.

She came the first time in the middle of March because she worked out that if she goes to that wall on a Wednesday evening, then for about an hour nobody in this borough knows where she is, and her evening stops being anybody else's business for an hour, and that there is not one person in her life who would think to look there.

She has been at work since she was twenty-two and she is in a house with a man and has no children and has a set of evenings she has never once been able to account for, and she has tried four other arrangements before this one and three of them were about a person and one was about something else entirely.

And the wall is the only one of the five that has cost her nothing and asked nothing of her.

And she is not a person who fishes. She did not know that a person needs a thing until about the second Wednesday, when she sat there with a bag and no line and understood that everybody on that wall except her had come from the same shop.


And on the twentieth of August, at about ten to six, he came along the wall and stopped.

He said, "You all right?"

And she said, "Aye."

And then he said the thing.

He said, "I'm not going next Wednesday."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "I've to go to my brother's. I've not been over there since about Easter and he rang at the weekend."

And she said, "That's all right."

And he said, "It's the first time in about five years I've missed one."

And she said, "Aye."

And he stood there for about four seconds.

And then he said, "You'd be all right up here on your own, would you."


And that is the chapter.

That is the actual question and it is the only one anybody in this borough would ever have asked her, and he did not ask it. He asked a question about her being all right on her own, which is about the wall and the evening and not about her, and there is a version of her where she answers the second one and lets the first one stand.

She has about four seconds and she takes about three of them.

And then she says: "I go up on the wall sometimes. I don't fish. That's all it is."

And he says, "Right."

And he says, "I know. I've seen you with no gear."

And he says, "It's a good spot, though. Whatever it is you're at."

And she says, "It is."

And he goes further along the wall and drops his line into nine feet of water.


And here is what she has done with those four seconds since about ten to six, and she has been doing it since.

The thing he actually asked was *would you like to come*.

He did not ask it and she did not get it. She got the whole of him turning up at ten to six on a Wednesday and telling her about a phone call from a brother, which is the one time in five years he has not gone.

And she has worked out that if she had said *no* then, he would have gone away with that, and if she had said *yes*, then the dozen times she has been there would have been a dozen times she was there because of him, and it would have to be one or the other and it could not be both.

So she gave him the true reason.

And it is the true reason. She does not fish. She comes up there because it is an hour of the week that belongs to her, and that is not a thing you can say to a man on a wall at ten to six on a Wednesday.


And here is his side of it, and it is not what she has assumed.

He believes her.

Not politely. He has gone over it. He went over it on the bus and again on the Thursday and once on the Saturday.

What he has is that she does not fish, and she has been coming for about five months, and she has never once asked him anything about it, and she has never once sat closer to him than about thirty yard, and on the two occasions he has spoken to her she has been entirely ordinary and has not tried to make anything of either of them.

And what he decided somewhere in about June is that she is a person who has decided that a wall at six o'clock on a Wednesday is worth having, and that he has no business at all in how she got to that conclusion, and that the correct thing to do about a person like that is to leave her to it.

He has a brother in a town four miles off and he has not been to see him since about Easter and there is nothing at all wrong with his brother.

He has said the thing about the brother because it is true and because a man says something true when a woman has told him she comes up a wall with no gear.


And here is what neither of them knows.

On the second of June, in the rain, when he said *you'll get soaked* and she said *I'll go in a minute* and then stood there for the better part of three quarters of an hour, she wrote something down on the back of her hand with a biro and rubbed it out at the bottom of the wall about twenty minutes later.

It is not about him.

That is all of it, and this borough is never going to hear one word of any of it.

**A MAN PUT SOMETHING TO A WOMAN ON A SEA WALL ON THE WEDNESDAY OF THE TWENTIETH OF AUGUST 2019 AND GOT A TRUE ANSWER OUT OF HER WHICH WAS NOT THE REASON FOR ANYTHING, AND TOOK IT AND WENT BACK TO WHAT HE CAME FOR.**
