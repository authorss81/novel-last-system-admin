# Chapter 0336 — The Set Off a Wall

Thursday the thirteenth of July 2017, from about half past nine in the morning until about half past four in the afternoon, a yard behind a shop off a parade, a lane two streets off it, and that shop, in Tideglass.

**A WIRELESS SET IN A BROWN CASE WAS LEANING AGAINST THE WALL OF A YARD IN JUNE AND IS ON THE BACK SHELF OF A SHOP OFF A PARADE BY THE MIDDLE OF JULY, AND A MAN CARRIED IT UNDER HIS ARM ABOUT TWO MILE, AND THE ONE PERSON WHO SAW IT IN THAT YARD IS THE PERSON WHO HAS IT BEHIND HER COUNTER, AND SHE HAS NOT PUT THOSE TWO THINGS TOGETHER.**

The yard is behind the third of nine shops on that parade and it belongs to the shop next door, and the shop next door has been shut on Mondays since the middle of March and the yard is used for nothing at all.

It is about twelve foot across and the wall down the left of it is the back of a store with no window in it, and there is a lean-to of corrugated iron along six foot of that wall with a wooden bench under it and about a dozen pots on the bench and the rest of the ground is bare brick and concrete with a gully grating in the corner.

The set is about the size of a tea tray. It is in a brown bakelite case with a handle across the top and a fabric speaker cloth on the front, and there is a crack running out of the top left corner of the case about four inch long with a piece of clear tape over it that has gone yellow, and the two knobs on the front are there and the dial glass is not scratched, and it works. There is a valve in it you can see through the cloth when the light is on it, and the tuning dial is marked in metres and not in watts.

It was against the wall of the lean-to on the Friday in the third week of May. It was there on the Monday after. It was there on the last Friday in June, and it was there on the Wednesday morning of the fifth of July, when the man in his thirties went into that yard for about six minutes over a bracket on a cold feed pipe that wanted nothing done to it.

He saw it on that Wednesday and he did not touch it and he did not think about it for four seconds, the way you do not think about a set of steps.

It was still working then. It was working off a battery that had been in it since before anybody in that street was born, and it came on at about the volume it had come on at in the twenties, which is a volume that fills a small room and does not fill a yard, and it went on for about four minutes on its own and then stopped.

Nobody in that parade has said one word to the woman who works in a shop about it and nobody in that yard has said one word to anybody, and the man who owns the shop next door has not been in that yard since the end of March.

---

He went back for it on the Thursday of the following week with his bag, at about half past ten in the morning, and he did not tell the woman who works in a shop that he was going to, and he did not tell anybody at all.

It came off the wall with a foot of it to lift. It is heavier than it looks and he had to turn it over twice to get his hand on the handle right, and he put it on the boards of his van with a folded blanket over it, which he has in the van for that, and shut the door and went on down the parade at about ten to twelve.

The parade is a little over half a mile. He has driven it about four times a day since the middle of March and he has walked it twice a day with a bag, and he came off the parade into the top of a lane, and he walked the lane, because a man with a wireless set in a van is a man who has stopped for something, and he did not want to be stopped for something.

He carried it about two mile. He went down past the greengrocer's and past the shop that shuts early and along the parade to the shop where the woman works, and he slowed down outside it at about five to twelve and stood on the step for about a minute with the case under his arm, and then he went in and put it on the counter and said what he had to say, which was about four sentences and none of which was about where it had been.

Three of those four sentences were about the case. There is a crack in it with tape over it and the tape has gone yellow, and he told her that, and he told her that a man in this town would have that shell off and made up and there was not a bit of it in this borough.

He has done a job in June in a kitchen in Corrance Lane and two days of that were in a house in March where a woman was going through everything her mother had left and was telling him what to put where. He has heard a great deal about what people do with things and none of it is anything to do with a wireless set in a yard, and he stood in the middle of that parade with a bakelite case under his arm and understood perfectly well that he was about to be a man in a shop with a thing he had got out of somebody's yard, and that there is one way of doing that and only one, and it is to say four sentences about a crack.

Then he took his change and went.

She had been in that shop two years and there is a back room behind the trade counter and the back room has a shelf along the top of the far wall with about nine inches between the top of the shelf and the ceiling, and there is a scale on the counter, a rack of small packets behind the till, and a space under the window where nobody can reach.

The set is on the back shelf now with a bit of newspaper under it and it is not plugged in and it is not on the account and it is not in the window and it is not for sale.

"I don't want it done," she said. "I know a man who would have that case off and made up and there is no way on this earth anybody is going to find a bit for that shell, and it is not going to the shop and it is not going to the man."

"Right," said the man in his thirties.

"He can leave it," she said. "If anybody comes for it they can have it. I am not going to be the woman who's got somebody's wireless in a shop."

That was on the Thursday. She did not ask him where it had been, and he did not say, and neither of them has mentioned it since.

She has had it on that shelf for a day now and it has not been on once, and there is no aerial on it, and the old set inside it is for a bin at the back of that shop along with a broken lamp and two trays and about a foot of somebody's Christmas, and nobody in that shop keeps a note of that either.

The shop that keeps a note of things is a shop eight miles off, and it has a counter, and it has a woman on it, and the woman on that counter did not know this borough and has never been asked about it.

---

Here is the whole of what happened to it, and there is not one person in this town who could tell it back in any other sequence.

The woman who works in a shop went out into that parade and into that yard nine times between the last week of May and the first week of July, and not always with any reason to be in there, and the set was against the wall of the lean-to every one of those nine times, and she saw it properly once, on the last Friday in June, and she never once said a word about it to a living soul, and there is nobody in that parade who knows that she went in there nine times.

The man in his thirties went into that yard for about six minutes on the fifth of July and came back for it on the Thursday and carried it about two mile and put it on a counter and said four sentences that had nothing in them about a yard.

She has not said to him where she first saw it. He has not asked. Neither of them knows that the other one of those two facts is true.

The set is on a shelf in a back room about eight inches back from a wall, with a bit of newspaper under it, and it is not plugged in and it is not working, and it is not dusted, and there is a hair on the case about two inches long that came off somebody.

And a man in his thirties drove past that shop at about five to twelve on the Thursday of that week and went on to three more jobs, and he has not been back into that yard and has not asked anybody anything about a set, and would not have said where it came from if he had been asked, because he had no answer that would have stood up.

**A WOMAN PUT A BIT OF NEWSPAPER UNDER A WIRELESS SET THAT A STRANGER PUT ON HER COUNTER AT FIVE TO TWELVE ON A THURSDAY IN JULY AND SHE HAS NEVER ASKED HIM ONE THING ABOUT IT.**
