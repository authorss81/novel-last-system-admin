# Chapter 0598 — The Woman Who Answered

The Monday of the twelfth of October 2020, from about half past one in the afternoon until about ten past six in the evening, in a first-floor flat in this borough.

**SHE WAS TOLD ON THE MONDAY BEFORE TO RING HIM, AND SHE RANG HIM AT HALF PAST TWO ON THE THURSDAY BEFORE LAST AND GOT THROUGH, AND SHE SPOKE TO A STRANGER FOR THE BEST PART OF AN HOUR ABOUT SOMETHING THAT WAS NOT WHAT SHE RANG ABOUT, AND SHE HAS RUNG BACK FOUR TIMES SINCE AND GOT HIM ONCE.**

She is a woman in her early thirties. The man she rang is a man in his early thirties, and he is not the man she was told to ring, and that has not come up yet, and by the time it comes up it will not matter.

The thing she was told to ring about is a job.

---

Here is what she was told.

The man who told her is in his late twenties and has worked with her for four years and he is not a friend of hers exactly. He said it on the Monday before last, at about ten past one, in a corridor where they eat, and he said it as a piece of news rather than a favour.

He said, "There's a lad down there, Lewis, he's doing the Wednesday and Thursday nights, he's asked after you."

And she said, "Has he?"

And he said, "He said you were the one to ask."

And she said, "Right," and he said, "Just ring him," and went back to his lunch.

That is the whole of what was said. It is not a sentence anybody said anything to her about. It is a name, a set of nights, and the words *just ring him*, and there was no number written down and no address, and she got both of them from the man with her on the Tuesday, who got them from somebody else, and by the time she had them she had been told three times that it was a formality and once that it was not a formality at all.

She has been out of work since the March.

Here is the call.

She rang at about half past two on the Thursday before last, from her own flat, at the table, with the number written on the back of a receipt, and it rang four times and a man answered and said the name of the company.

She said, "Is Lewis there?"

And the man said, "There's no Lewis here."

And she said, "Right. Sorry."

And the man said, "Who did you say?"

And she said, "Lewis. He does the Wednesday and Thursday nights."

And the man said, "Mate, there's four of us and we're all called something different. There's no Lewis here."

And she said, "Oh."

And there is the moment in the call, and it is at about three minutes, and she could have said *sorry, I've rung the wrong number* and hung up, and that is what a person does in the ordinary way.

Instead she said, "No, it's not the wrong number. It's just not the number I thought it was."

---

Here is what the call was about, which was not what she rang about.

The man on the other end of it is a man in his early thirties and he has been on the phone since a quarter to midday that morning and has had one break and this is the second time he has been put through to somebody who is not who they wanted, and he says so.

He said, "It's fine. Genuinely. I've been on since about quarter to twelve."

She said, "Sorry."

He said, "No, don't. If you hang up I'll just be sat here."

So she stayed on.

And then he told her about the thing, and the thing is this. He works in a place that does repairs, and a man came in on the Monday with a machine that had gone wrong, and the man was told it would be six weeks, and it is now well past that, and the man has rung four times and spoken to four different people and been told four different things by four people who are all doing their job, and none of the four is in a position to move it, and he is not angry, he is just a man who has a thing in his car that will not go.

And he said, "It's not even the money. I'd pay it. It's that I keep it in the car for my lad and he thinks it's in the car."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "Sorry, I'm not — you rang about something."

And she said, "It's all right. Go on."

And he went on.

They talked about that for about half an hour. She asked him two or three things and they were reasonable questions and he answered them. He told her about the four people. He told her about the machine and what it does and why it matters that it is in the car, and he did not once ask her anything about herself, and neither did she.

At about the thirty-five minute mark she noticed the time on the cooker and he noticed her noticing it, and he said, "You're all right, go on."

And she said, "I am. I just want to hear the end of it."

So he told her the end of it, which is that on the Thursday morning the machine had been done, and it had been done on the Wednesday night by a man who was not one of the four, and the man had not told anybody, and he has rung back twice this afternoon to be told that it is finished and that somebody will be in touch about the money.

And he said, "So it was done the whole time. I just didn't get told."

And she said, "That's the whole worst one, that."

And he said, "Isn't it."

After that there was a stretch of it where neither of them spoke, and she could hear a road going past outside.

Then she said, "I've got the wrong number, though. I was after a Lewis."

And he said, "Ah. Right."

And she said, "Sorry about all that."

And he said, "Don't be. Genuinely. I needed the first ten of them."

Here is what she did about the other thing.

She has rung back four times since, which is the Friday, the Monday, yesterday and this morning. Every one of them was the same number, because the man with her on the Tuesday said the number was the number, and the number is a good one, and there is a woman on the switchboard who puts her through to the maintenance office and the maintenance office has three people in it and none of them is called Lewis.

She got him once. That was on the Monday just gone, when the office was empty and she was told to try after midday, and at four minutes past twelve a man came on who said his name was Lewis and said he was the wrong Lewis and there was one on the Wednesday nights and they had gone away three weeks ago.

That was the whole of the job, and it took five weeks to find out, and nobody told her it was finished because nobody was ever going to.

The man in his late twenties, who is the one who told her, knows none of this. She has not told him and is not going to, because he said *just ring him*, and he did say *just ring him*, and the two words that would have saved her five weeks were in a corridor on the Monday before last at ten past one and she will not be putting *you never gave me a number* to him.

She rang the number again this morning and she was put through to the office and she asked whether there was a Lewis and the woman on the switchboard said there was not, and she said *I was told to ring him*, and the woman said, "Right, well, whoever told you, they didn't hear about it from us."

**SHE SPOKE TO A STRANGER FOR THE BEST PART OF AN HOUR ABOUT A MACHINE IN A CAR AND HE TOLD HER THE WHOLE OF IT AND SHE LET HIM, AND SHE HAS RUNG BACK FOUR TIMES AND GOT HIM ONCE, AND THE JOB SHE WAS RINGING ABOUT WENT AWAY THREE WEEKS BEFORE SHE EVER RANG, AND THE MAN WHO SENT HER IS NOT A MAN SHE IS GOING TO ASK ANYTHING.**
