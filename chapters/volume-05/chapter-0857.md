# Chapter 0857 — The Nut And The Quarter Turn

Wednesday the first of November 2023, in the shed at number ten from first light until the barrow will not turn any more. Twenty minutes on a nut, and nothing else.

**THE FRONT WHEEL NUT ON THE BARROW HAS WALKED ROUND THE AXLE ENOUGH FOR THE WHEEL TO PLAY, AND SHE MEANS TO BRING IT BACK WITH HER FINGERS AND PUT IT BACK, AND THE SPANNER IS NOT PICKED UP, AND HER SISTER PUTS A BOWL IN THE SINK AT THE KITCHEN WINDOW AND DOES NOT COME OUT.**

The barrow is a wheelbarrow, one wheel at the front and two legs behind it, and it lives in the shed at number ten between the palings and the bags of sand with the handles up against the wall.

She found the play on Sunday, on the flags by the shed, because the barrow has a shimmy in it now. It is not a bad one. It is a small turn of the wheel in the axle on every push, so that the barrow goes along beside you instead of straight, and on the flags it goes along beside you with a knock in it, and on the boards at the front of the shed the knock comes up through the frame and into her wrists.

A knock in a barrow that appears when nobody has touched the barrow is play in a wheel or play in its bearings. This one is play in the wheel, because the wheel can be turned with a finger on the tyre and the whole barrow turns with it and the axle does not.

So she goes at the nut.

It is an ordinary nut on the stub axle, with a flat washer under it, on the left of the wheel looking from the front, and there is nothing else on that end of the axle at all. No split pin, no tab washer, no locking plate. Whoever put it on last put it on with the flats of their fingers and left it.

---

She does not go for the spanner. It is on the shelf above the bench along with four other things, and it would do this in ten seconds instead of four minutes.

The nut is loose already. That is the whole of the finding and it took two seconds to arrive at. A nut that is going to take a spanner has to be a nut that is tight, and this one is not tight, because her fingers can turn it. And a nut that is loose on the end of an axle is not going to be made tight by a spanner and stay tight. It is only going to be made tight for as long as it takes for the wheel to play again and take the play out of the nut.

So the spanner is for a nut that has stopped going round by itself. This is not that, and turning it up and down four times with a spanner on a nut that is loose is a thing people do and it is the worst of the three ways of dealing with a wheel.

The other two are the split pin, which needs a hole drilled in the axle that is not there, and the locking nut, which is another part.

So: her fingers, and about twenty minutes, and that is the whole of what she has decided to do about it.

What she does before she does anything is put her thumb and forefinger on it and feel.

The threads on a nut run round it in a spiral and the grain of them is in the spiral, not across it, and that is the whole of what a nut is: a piece of hex steel with a spiral cut in it that goes one way round and one way only. Anything you put on the end of that spiral and turn it the other way is turning against a thing that has one direction in it.

The nut is off the flats. That is what she feels first and it is why it has not come off. A nut sitting square with the flats of the axle end can be turned by the wheel itself, because the flank of the thread catches the flank of the nut and turns it, and it will go round and round and be on the ground by the end of the week. A nut that has come round until it is bedded on a pair of corners is jammed, because a corner of a nut is an edge and an edge on a thread digs in and stops.

She can feel the corner with her fingers. She can feel the corner the other side of it too, so the nut is at about half a flat off square, which is where a nut stops, and it has been there a long time and it has been there for all the play.

Then she turns it.

She takes it between the first finger and the thumb of her right hand, the way you take a small screw out of a plate, and she turns it, and it comes.

She goes the way it wants to go, which is anticlockwise looking at that end of the axle, and it comes about a quarter of a turn and then it stops.

She stops with it. She does not force it. It is at the flats and the flats are square and the edges of the flats are against the edges of the flats on the end of the axle, and that is where it wants to be, and a very little more than that would carry it past the flats and set it down on a pair of corners again, which is where it started this morning.

A quarter of a turn on a nut like that is about a quarter of an inch of thread. It is not much. It is the whole distance between jammed and loose, and it takes about four seconds.

The barrow stands against the wall with the wheel on the floor and she grips the tyre between her hands and twists it both ways. The wheel turns and the axle does not, and the nut turns with the wheel because it is loose.

That is the stop. The wheel no longer plays on the axle, and the nut is on the flats where a nut belongs, and it will walk round again in due course because there is nothing on that axle to stop it walking round, and that is a job for a hole and a split pin and not for a Wednesday in November.

Her sister is at the kitchen window in the middle of the morning putting a bowl in the sink. The window is open about four inches and she is reaching down into it, and she does not come out and she does not look along the yard, and the bowl goes down into the sink and that is all that happens.

The barrow goes back against the wall with the handles up and the wheel under it, which is where it lives. The spanner stays on the shelf where it has been all along.

She does not stop there, because a wheel that has played has left one mark and she wants to see it while she is looking.

The washer under the nut is an ordinary flat one and it is not square.

It has walked. The nut came round by some amount while it was loose and the washer came round with it, and it is sitting now a quarter of a turn off square on the face of the hub, one of its edges a little under the lip of the hub and the edge opposite showing more of the face than it ought to. It has not been off. It has not been loose on the axle either. It turned with the nut and stopped when the nut stopped on its corners, and it is where it stopped.

A washer is a spring. It is the only spring in that wheel. What it has done is take up the run of the nut against the axle and give it back when the nut moves, and it cannot give back more than it has got.

She puts two fingers on the hub and two on the washer and does not touch the nut at all, and the washer turns back about an eighth of a turn under the fingers and stays there when she takes them off, because it is sprung and it is doing what it is for.

So there is a wheel on the barrow at number ten with its play taken out by a quarter of an inch, a nut sitting on its flats, and a washer under that nut a quarter of a turn out of square on the face of the hub, which is the only mark the play ever left on anything.
