# Chapter 0838 — Grit Under The Slab

Tuesday the twenty-second of August 2023, beside the rail at number nine from when the step cools until evening, and a slab tilted low at one corner is the whole of that Tuesday.

**THERE IS A SLAB TILTED TO THE RAIL AT NUMBER NINE STANDING LOW AT ONE CORNER AND SHE MEANS TO PACK GRIT UNDER IT WITHOUT LIFTING IT, AND THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING STOPS AT THE SIDE GATE STEP AND DOES NOT COME IN.**

That slab is the one by the back rail where the pail stands, tilted toward the rail since spring so the water runs off it away from the step.

The low corner has gone lower by half a finger with the summer. Boots turning on it have ground the grit out from under the edge, and the corner now sits on air over a hollow, with a soft tick under it when the pail goes down full.

She does not lift it. A slab by a rail wants the rail off first if it comes up, and the rail is set in the wall and staying, and a hollow under an edge on tired ground takes packing from the side without any of that.

She starts with a pot of sharp grit, a trowel, a brush, and a pail of dust swept from the path for filling.

The low corner is the one away from the wall, facing the grass. She kneels on the dry grass beside it and brushes the mouth of the joint clean, down to where the old bedding shows.

Dust comes out of the joint in a soft roll, with ants in it from the summer, and under the dust the bedding sits firm except at the very lip, where it has crumbled back half a finger.

She does not open that lip further. A shallow fix leaves the firm part alone and feeds the lip, and the tired ground under it is not to be cut into.

She spoons grit into the mouth a spoonful at a time, bedding it beneath with the trowel face down, pushing back until the trowel meets the firm part. The first lot drops straight into the hollow and the tick under the corner grows louder for a minute, which is the grit piling where the air was.

The woman who does her shopping stops at the side gate step in the middle of the afternoon with a bag, looks at the pail and the pot, and stays where she is.

“You are busy with that corner,” she tells her from the step.

“I am, and I will be a while yet,” she tells her, and the woman nods and goes on with the bag, out toward the road.

The step is the side gate step of number nine, three flags up from the path, and the woman keeps to the step the whole while with the bag on her arm, and the talk between them is two sentences and no more.

She thinks about the rail while she packs, how the slab leans to it on purpose so water runs away from the step. A tilt put there in spring is not a fault. The fault is only the extra half finger the summer added, and the mending keeps the purpose while taking the excess. That is the whole of shallow work. Leave the intent, correct the drift.

Her knees ache on the dry grass, and ants run over her shoes and off again, busy with their own summer under the slab she is feeding.

Pail on, pail off, that is the test. A slab that will take a pail full without sliding will take boots all winter, and a slab that ticks under a pail will tick under everything.

After three rounds the tick goes. After five the corner sits with the rest, and the pail goes down on it full and stays where it is put.

She brings the dust from the pail and brushes it into the joint over the grit, to blind it, so the sharp stuff below is not left open to boots. Dust over grit beds in with the first damp and makes a skin, and a skin keeps the grit where it was put.

She sweeps the face of the slab and the rail above it, where dust has settled in a pale film.

---

The pot goes back empty, and the pail stands on the slab full to the brim without moving, and the brush goes against the wall.

She steps back to the grass when the evening comes on and looks at what is there. A slab tilted to the rail with grit at its low corner, blinded with dust, and above it the rail, dry and true.
