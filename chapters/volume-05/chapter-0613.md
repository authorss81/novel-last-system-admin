# Chapter 0613 — The Bench He Took Off a Wall

The Thursday of the thirty-first of December 2020. It begins at about half past eight in the morning and it ends at about half past six in the evening. In this borough.

**HE TOOK A GATE OFF THE SIDE OF A GARAGE IN THE SEPTEMBER BECAUSE HE HAD BEEN GOING BACK UP TO LOOK AT IT AND HAD NEVER ONCE SAID TO ANYBODY THAT IT WAS NOT HIS, AND HE HAS CARRIED IT ROUND THIS BOROUGH EVER SINCE AND HAS PAID A MAN ONCE, AND TODAY HE HAS PUT IT DOWN AT THE BACK OF HIS OWN GARDEN WHERE IT STANDS ON THE SOIL.**

He is a man in his late fifties. He builds things. The gate is wrought iron and about five feet high and it weighs more than a man should put in a boot.

Here is how it came off.

There is a row of garages at the back of a row of houses and the man who rented the end one for years put the gate up on the side of it a long time ago, welded, with no feet, because whoever built the garage put a concrete post in the slab and the gate went on that.

And in about the September the man stopped coming, and left the key in the door of the office of the firm, and went, and the garages got a new tenant and a new man in the office.

And this man had gone up there with a van in about the September to look at a job and had come back with a photograph of a gate on a garage, and had been up there several times looking at it, and had never once asked the man it belonged to.

---

The day he took it off was a Thursday in the September and he has not had a version of it in his head where he asked anybody anything.

He went up at about seven in the morning because there was nobody about. He had a grinder and he cut the weld at the bottom of the right-hand stile, about half an inch above the slab, and it took longer than it should have because the concrete had got to the bottom of the steel and the disc kept going, and the frame came away with a lump of slab attached to one foot about the size of a dinner plate, which is still attached, and he has never thought about that.

He put it in the boot of his car. It went in with the seats down and the back seats up.

And he came home.

Here is what he has done since, in order, and he has kept it in this order since.

One: he put it in the boot and it stayed in the boot.

Two: he cleaned the concrete off the foot in the back driveway, about an afternoon of it, with a chisel and a grinder and a bucket, and it came off in the end and there is a bare patch of metal about the size of a dinner plate on the foot that has been cleaned and not painted.

Three: he took it to the scrapyard once. He got there, went round the back with it off the trailer ramp, and got as far as the office, and the man in the office was sorting and did not ask him anything and he put it down again and took it home and it went in the boot.

Four: he went up the road and paid a man he has used before to cut the slab away from the foot, because he had worked out that it would be worth something to somebody if it were clean. That was in the October and it was about ninety pounds. The foot is clean. It is not painted.

Five: he started looking at gates.

That is the one nobody would guess and it is the one that has eaten him. Since the October he has been looking at gates.

He has been to four of them. There is a man about nine miles off who does ironwork out of a unit behind a shop, and there is a place on the industrial estate, and there is a man in this borough who puts up rails and has a book of photographs, and there is a garden centre about four miles off with a yard at the back.

He went to all four and he took a tape measure to each one and he stood about four feet back from each one and looked at them, and he did not buy anything, and he did not ask the price of anything, and he did not ask for anybody's card.

He has worked out that he did not go to see gates. He went to see whether there was one for this gate. That is a different errand and it took him a while to work out that he had not been going to see gates at all.

Here is today.

The garage at the end of the row has a new tenant, which is a man in his early forties who keeps a white van and does electrical work, and he found the gate off the side of his garage on the first morning and had been asking about it at the office on and off since.

The office told him on about the Tuesday that there was a man who had taken it and there was a number.

He rang.

And this man said his name and the firm and said he had taken it off and had intended to put it up and had not got to it, and the man in the early forties said, "Well, that's where it was. Can you put it back?"

And he said he would.

And the man said, "It's a Saturday job."

And he said, "I'll come Saturday if that's no good for you, I'm not pushed."

And the man said, "Saturday's fine."

He was up there at about ten on the Saturday.

He took the gate with him in the back of the van and he had a bag of weld, a grinder, a sander, a hammer, a chisel, a paint tin he bought in the September and did not open, and a slab repair compound.

And he got it off the van and put it against the wall of the office, and the man in the early forties came out with a tape and looked at where it had been.

And he said, "It was a good bit of work."

And the man said, "I never saw it before you took it off."

And he said, "Nobody did. It was on there when I came."

And the man said, "Right."

And he said, "No, I meant it as a thing. It's been on years and it hasn't dropped. Look at the hinge side."

And the man said, "It's got a lump of concrete on it."

And he said, "That's about two inches of slab came off with it in the September."

And the man said, "Somebody's."

And he said, "Somebody's."

Here is the thing, and he told the man in the early forties none of it.

The wall has a post in it and the post is sound and the weld stubs are on it, and he could put this gate back in a morning and it would go up exactly as it came off.

Except that the wall is a different wall.

He went and looked at it. The garages were re-roofed in about the summer of 2019 and the whole row was stripped back and re-clad, and the old cladding was taken off the side that gate was on, and when it was put back on it went back on about an inch further out than it was, so the face of the wall that the post is on is now about an inch behind where the post ends.

So the gate goes on a post that no longer comes through the wall in the right place, and it would sit proud and it would look wrong and it would be the gate sitting proud by an inch, which is the kind of thing a man who owns a garage notices every morning for years.

He measured it. He got a tape and got the distance from the face of the wall to the face of the gate's flat bar and it is about an inch, and he wrote it on the back of his hand with a marker, and then he stopped and looked at it.

---

That is what happened at about ten past one.

He stood in that yard with a marker in his hand and about an inch written on the back of it and he knew, in about as long as it takes to look at a gate, that he was not going to cut the post off and extend it.

And he did not do it because it is not the gate's post any more, in the sense that matters, and because the man in the early forties did not ask him to put the gate back exactly and he was never going to say the word exactly, and because if he does this job he will do it right, and it will take the best part of two long mornings, and it will come out of the gate man in this borough who is not related to him.

So he said, "It's going to want the wall coming back."

And the man said, "What's the wall."

And he said, "That cladding's been on since the summer before last. It's proud of the post. It's an inch. You'd want to take a strip off and re-clad about eight foot and re-dressed the joint, and then the gate goes back on it as it was."

And the man said, "Who does cladding."

And he said, "There's a fella does it on the estate. He's slow and he's expensive but he's all right."

And the man said, "Can you give me his number?"

And he said, "I'll ring him for you."

And the man said, "Right."

And he said, "I'll not charge you for this, I'm not doing it, I want that clear. If he does it that's between you and him. I'll not put my name on it."

And the man said, "Right."

He went home at about half past three.

The gate is against the wall at the back of his own garden. He had it brought round when he got home and he set it down on the soil, at the back, against the wall, and it is standing there in the dark at about half past six now.

He has not put it up on the wall of his own garage.

He has a garage on the same road as the one it came off. That has been the plan since about the October and the wall at the side of it has a post in it that would take it, and the cladding on it is the old cladding and it would go up exactly.

And he has never cut the paint tin open.

Here is the thing that is underneath the whole of it, and he got to it this evening in the garage with his tea.

The first man put that gate up years ago for nothing. There was no invoice and there was no conversation about money and the man did it because he was in the yard one afternoon and the gate came off its hinges and he fixed it and put it back and welded it.

And then this man has taken it off a wall that is not his, welded to a post that is in somebody's slab, and has carried it round this borough in the back of a car and in the back of a van since the September, and has paid a man ninety pounds to make it worth something to somebody, and has looked at four gates, and has not put it up.

And if he puts it up on his own wall it is a gate a man in the early forties used to enjoy having on his garage, and he does not know that, and there is no way on earth he is ever going to find out.

And if he puts it back where it was, it is an inch proud, in a yard, on a wall that was clad over the post, to a man who does electrical work and will look at it every morning.

So it stands on the soil at the back of a garden in this borough with a clean patch of metal on the foot of it, and the paint tin is in the garage with the lid still on, and he has told a man in the early forties that it is not his to charge for.

**HE TOOK A GATE OFF A GARAGE HE HAD NO BUSINESS ON AND HE HAS CARRIED IT ROUND THIS BOROUGH SINCE THE SEPTEMBER AND HAS PAID A MAN ONCE TO CLEAN IT AND HAS BEEN TO FOUR OTHER GATES, AND IT IS STANDING ON THE SOIL AT THE BACK OF HIS GARDEN WITH A CLEAN PATCH ON THE FOOT OF IT.**
