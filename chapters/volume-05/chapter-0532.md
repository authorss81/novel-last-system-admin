# Chapter 0532 — Where She Puts Them Back

The Thursday of the twenty-eighth of November 2019, from before six in the morning until about ten at night, in one house in this borough.

A pair of spectacles belongs to nobody. She has been putting hers back where she finds them since 2016, there are three places she is certain of and four she could not swear to, and since the Friday before last there has been somebody in that house getting to them before her. On the twenty-eighth of November she came down early on purpose, and he had been down and gone back up.

The two of them are a woman of about sixty-two and a man of about sixty-five, and they have been in that house since 1989, and they are not loud with each other and have not been for a long time.

The spectacles are the only thing in that house either of them looks for.

She started needing them for reading in about 2011 and for anything else at all about 2013, and for about three years she kept them in one place, which was the top of the airing cupboard, and everything was fine, and she could put her hand on them in the dark. Then she started needing them in the evening as well as the morning, and once a thing is wanted twice in a day it is never going to be put back where it belongs, because it is never where it belongs in the first place, and by the end of 2015 she had stopped pretending and by the start of 2016 she had stopped meaning to.

So now they go where she has been. The sill of the bathroom window. The top of the cistern. The edge of the bath, which is the worst of the places and the commonest. The middle of the floor on the way out. The end of the long seat by the front window, on the side she has not sat on since about 2008. Once, in a way she could not account for, at the bottom of the cupboard where the outdoor things go, under two things of her own that were not in there in the summer. She never puts them anywhere on purpose and she has never once put them back where she first found them either, because by the time she has found them she has been somewhere else.

That is the rule and neither of them has ever discussed it. When she cannot find them she says one sentence, once, in a voice he can describe, and then she goes and looks again somewhere else, and the sentence is not repeated and has never once been the subject of anything.

What changed on the twentieth of November is that she could not find them for a good ten minutes and then they were on the sill of the bathroom window, folded.

She does not fold them.

Here is what the twentieth of November was, and she has it in the sequence it happened in, and she has told nobody and does not mean to.

She was up first, as she is most days, and she wanted them for the post and she could not find them anywhere, and for years now there had been nowhere in that house where a thing like that was put on purpose and meant. So she went along the three places. The cistern. The sill. The edge of the bath. She went back and did it in the same sequence, and that is the part she cannot account for, because in thirty years she has never gone back along a route the same way twice. And then she said the sentence, once, at about ten past six in the morning, standing in the kitchen with the post still in her hand.

He was upstairs and he heard it through the floor.

The sentence is not a question and it is not sharp and it is not loud. It is the same sentence she has said twice in thirty years and the whole of it is a number, and a man who has heard it three times can put a length on it, and the first of the two was in 1991, two years after they were in the house, and on both of them he was somewhere else in the borough and has not stopped regretting it. On the third he was in the house, two floors up, lying on top of the covers, at ten past six, and he came down at about half past and said he had come for the water and she said all right, and he took some water to the front of the house and drank it standing up and came back and filled the pan and said nothing about it, and that was that, and she put the post on the top of the letters.

So it is not a new thing between them. The new thing is what she found on the Friday.

On the Friday morning she went along the three places early, at about ten to six, because she had a reason, and the spectacles were on that sill, folded, the long way across, with the arms turned in. She told herself twice over the two days after that a person of her age would do that. Then on the Sunday she was in the kitchen at about half past six and he came in from the front of the house and went straight out again, and she heard him go, and that was the moment she stopped being able to put it down to her own hands.

They have been on that sill in that same place, folded, on every morning since the Friday, and it is the third of December that she has told herself she means to do something about it, and she has not told herself what the something is, and she is aware that not knowing is a decision, and that she made it on the Sunday and not this week.

She has not put a word of it to anybody, including him, and she has not done anything about it, and she has not once gone and looked for them in the places he uses, because that would be saying it.

---

Here is the part that is his and that he has not put anywhere.

He has been getting up at about half past five for a fortnight. He is not up because he cannot sleep. He is up because he is fifty-one in the mornings and sixty-five in the afternoons and there is a stretch of a good three quarters of an hour in which he is neither, and he has started spending it downstairs.

And while he is downstairs the house is quiet, and he goes into the back part and looks along the three places, and if they are not on the sill he puts them on the sill, folded, the long way across, with the arms turned in.

It is not for her. He would like it very much to be for her and it is not, and the reason is that he cannot bear to be in the house when the sentence comes. He has heard it twice in thirty years. Both times it was the same sentence and both times it took about as long as drinking something hot would and he has not been within sight of either, and he has decided since the twentieth that he would rather be in the house at the wrong time than not be in it at all, and he is aware that those are not the same thing and he is doing it anyway.

None of this has been said out loud and none of it is going to be, and there is no living soul in this borough he could tell it to.

He has also not asked himself, once in eight days, what she thinks about it. He has been not asking, deliberately, and with a great deal of care, in the way a man does not ask a question he has already worked out the answer to.

---

She got up at about ten to six on the twenty-eighth and did not put anything on over what she had slept in, and came down without switching on anything, which she has not done since about 2009.

The back part of the house is dark and it is colder than the front. She went along and put her hand along the top of the cistern, which she knew, and the sill of the bathroom window, which she knew, and the edge of the bath, which she did not, and then she came back and looked at the sill.

They were on the sill, folded, the long way across, with the arms turned in.

She stood in the cold for about a minute. She could not have said what she had expected to find. She had come down at twenty to six on purpose, on purpose is the whole of it, and she had expected to find him standing there with them in his hand, and she had a sentence ready for that, and she had been carrying it down the length of the front of the house and round into the kitchen, and she had not expected anything else and she had not expected this at all.

She went back up.

She was in the kitchen at about ten to seven with the water on and one of her own hands flat on the front of the thing she was leaning against when he came down, which is what he does at ten to seven every morning of the week and has done since the middle of November.

"Morning," he said.

"Morning," she said.

And he filled the pan and switched it on, because that is what a man does at ten to seven in a house like this, and she had her back to him and he came past her and went into the back part and she heard him not go down there, and then she heard him come back, and then she heard him stand at the far end of the kitchen for a moment with his hand on the side of the thing he leans on, on the other side of hers, and then he let go of it and picked up what he had come down for and said the water was hot and she said it was, and that was all of the twenty-eighth of November.
