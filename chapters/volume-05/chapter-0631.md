# Chapter 0631 — The Man Who Went Round With It

Tuesday the thirtieth of March 2021, in this borough. He starts at about seven in the morning with a list of nine doors on the back of an invoice and he finishes at about seven in the evening on a road at the top of the hill, and the whole of it is about five words.

**A MAN IN HIS MID FIFTIES HAS FOUND OUT THAT FIVE WORDS HE HAS BEEN SAYING HAVE GONE ROUND FOUR STREETS, AND HE HAS DECIDED TO GO ROUND ALL FOUR OF THEM AND SAY THEY CAME OFF HIM, AND A YOUNG WOMAN WHO LIVES ON ONE OF THEM ASKS HIM WHY HE IS GOING ROUND, AND HIS REASON IS FALSE AND HE KNOWS IT IS.**

He is a man in his mid fifties. He fits frames and hangs doors and has done since he was twenty-two and he is round four streets on this estate most weeks of the year in a van with a rack on it and a flask and a sandwich box.

He has said the five words since the middle of February and he did not get them anywhere and he has never once stopped and wondered where they came from, and that is the part of himself he is least proud of on this particular day.

---

Here is what he found out, and it took him the best part of the Monday, and it did not come from anybody telling him.

One: a woman at the chip shop on the estate asked him whether he had heard about the woman at number thirty-one.

Two: he said he hadn't.

Three: she said that the woman at number thirty-one never asks anybody for anything, and she is not the first one to say it and she had stopped at the shop and been told three times in a fortnight, and she wanted to know what it was all about because she has lived on that estate since she was a young woman and has never had a word with her and would not want to be somebody who goes round saying things.

And he said: don't.

And she said: I'm not, I'm asking.

And he said: leave it.

And she said: it's only the bins and the parcels.

And he said: leave it.

Here is what that gave him, and he had it by about the middle of the day and then drove about with it.

The five words had stopped being a thing people said and had become a thing people said about one person.

He has said it about a great many people. He has said it in the shop, at the school gates, in a doorway at the bottom of the hill, to a fella who was trying to sort out a leak, to the woman who runs the library, to a lad he fitted a door for and did not want to pay him.

And out of all that, on an estate of four streets, three people in a fortnight have handed him a house number.

And he knows why they have got a number, and here is the thing he has not said to anybody.

It is because he said it about her.

Not in the same five words, and he would not put it that way if anybody asked. But a fortnight ago he was in her street fitting a frame and a woman he did not know was at a gate on the other side of it, and he was not going anywhere and he said it, and he said it loud enough for her to hear, and he said it in the voice.

He does not know her name. He has been past number thirty-one a hundred times and she has never had the gate open when he has been there.

He said it loud enough for her to hear.

And the reason he said it loud enough for her to hear is not one he is going to write on a receipt.

Here is the decision, and he made it on Monday night late with the van outside and the list of nine doors on the inside of the windscreen in the rain.

He is going to go round the four streets and he is going to say, at every door where anybody will open it, that the five words came off him.

Not that they are true. Not that anybody ever asked for anything. Just that they came off him, and if you have heard them and you have got a house number attached to them, they came off him and not from anybody's gate.

And he is not going to say where he got them.

Here is why he is not, and he worked this out in about a minute and then spent the rest of the night on it.

Because he got them off a young man who has the top of a house off a road of terraces and who said them first, at a door, about a fella who could not get a bin out, and the young man said them in the voice, and he said them twice, and he looked like a man who had had them a fortnight and had not been told anything.

And if this man goes round four streets and says *it came off me*, then within the week somebody in this borough is going to work out that the young man said it first.

And then the young man is a man who said it first.

And he is a man in his early thirties who lives over a woman who does his washing for nothing and who went into her kitchen and put it into her mouth on purpose to see if it came back, and the whole of what he did was for that, and it has come back four streets, and it is attached to a woman who has never asked anybody for anything in her life.

So he is not going to say.

That is not a noble decision and he has not dressed it up as one since Monday night. He is doing one thing for a young man and one thing for a woman at number thirty-one and the second of them is the reason and the first of them is the excuse, and he knows which came first.

He came back at about seven on the Tuesday evening and it had been going since half past seven in the morning, and it took the best part of the day and it is not a round you do in a morning.

And that is the part nobody is going to believe, and he has already had two people not believe it.

You cannot go up to a door on a Tuesday morning and say *the five words came off me*. You cannot stand on a step with a folder under your arm and start there.

So you have to fit a door first.

He did nine doors. He did nine frames and a door and a set of downstairs windows and he was in and out of kitchens all day being fed, and at about half past two he was in a house on the third street and the woman doing the tea said, "Is it true about the woman at number thirty-one."

And he said, "What about her."

And she said, "What everybody's saying. That she never asks anybody for anything."

And he said, "It came off me."

And she said, "Off you?"

And he said, "Ay."

And she said, "Right."

And she said, "Well, I'd never have thought."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "It's not been said with your name on it."

And he said, "It has now."

And she said, "You'll get a bad name."

And he said, "I've had one."

And she said, "Have you."

And he said, "I'll not have it said about her, though."

And the woman did not say anything to that and she poured the tea and that was the end of it and he has thought about the tea since.

He did it at five more that evening, in about an hour, which is what he worked out is possible if you do not ask anybody to open a door.

A man at number twelve who he had fitted a handle for.

A lad at nineteen who he had been in the same street with.

The woman who keeps the papers at the parade.

A man on the second street who nodded and said *will you* and went in.

And a woman in her mid twenties at number thirty-one.

She opened before he had finished, because he was standing on the step with a folder and a clipboard and a tape measure round his neck and she thought he was somebody about the post.

And he said, "I don't want measuring you."

And she said, "Can I help you, then."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Right."

And he said, "I want to say something and it's about five words and you'll have had them off somebody."

And she said, "Go on."

And he said, "They came off me."

And she said, "What did."

And he said, "He never asks for anything."

And she said, "You've said that."

And he said, "I know I've said it."

And she said, "You've said it about six people to me."

And he said, "Have I."

And she said, "You've said it about the man who keeps the corner shop, and about the fella with the van, and about a woman on the parade who collects for the church, and once about me."

And he said, "Have I said it about you."

And she said, "Once. About me. In a queue at the parade and you did not know who I was."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "Go on."

And he said, "It's not about you."

And she said, "I didn't think it was."

And he said, "I've been round four streets telling anybody who'd open a door that the five words came off me, and if anybody has told you that they came off me, that's right, that's where they came from."

And she said, "Where did you get it."

And he said, "I can't tell you that."

And she said, "Why not."

And he said, "Because then it'll be off somebody else in about a week and we'll be back where we started."

And she said, "That makes sense."

And she said, "You don't want to hand it on."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Why not."

And he said, "Why are you asking me why I'm not."

And she said, "I'm asking because you said the woman at number thirty-one never asks anybody for anything and that's me, and I'm asking you why a man in a van is on my step saying they came off him."

And he stood on the step and he had the true reason right there and it was about nine inches away from his mouth.

Because she has lived here since she was a young woman and she is in her mid twenties and she has never asked anybody for anything and never been on a list anybody has made about her, and three people in a fortnight have said it about her, and two of them said it to him and he was one of the other two, and if he does not come up this path tonight then by the end of the month there will be a woman on this estate who has never spoken to her and does not know why she is angry.

That is the reason and it is the reason he will give his own wife when he gets in.

What he said was, "Because there's nothing at the end of it."

And she said, "What?"

And he said, "Nothing's at the end of it. That's the whole of it. Five words that came off nobody and meant nobody and it's gone round four streets in a fortnight and there's not one person in this estate it's true of, and I know that because I've said it about six people and it's not true of one of them."

And she said, "Including me."

And he said, "Including you."

And she said, "Right."

And she said, "That'll be interesting to put round."

And he said, "Don't put that round."

And she said, "Why not."

And he said, "Because then the five words have turned into six and I'm back where I started with a different thing to carry."

And she said, "All right."

And he said, "That's decent of you."

And she said, "I've not done anything."

And he said, "You were about to."

And she said, "I was, a bit."

And she said, "Why are you doing this at all."

And he said, "I've told you why."

And she said, "You said you can't say where you got it. That's a reason for not saying where you got it. It isn't a reason for coming round."

And he stood on the step.

And he said, "No."

And she said, "No?"

And he said, "No, it isn't."

And she said, "That's all right."

And she shut the door, and not on him, and he stood on the step and he has not gone down the path yet.

---

Here is what he has, and he will not be writing any of it on an invoice.

He has told a young woman on her own step that the five words came off him, and that is true, and it is the only true thing he has got.

He has given her a reason and it was *because there's nothing at the end of it*, and the reason is not true. There is something at the end of it. There is a girl at number thirty-one who has been charged with never asking for anything and there is a young man over a laundry who said it first to see if it would come back, and both of them are inside what he is doing and neither of them is going to hear about it from him.

He could have said that. She asked him twice and both times he had it ready and both times he gave her the true reason about the street and not the true reason about her.

And she knows he has not given her the true reason. She is a woman who works on a depot and is on her own four nights a week and she is not stupid, and she told him so in a way that was not unkind and was not going to let him off it, which is *that's all right*, and she shut her door on that and not on him.

He did not get it off a young man and say nothing about that. He got it off a young man and stood on a step at number thirty-one and declined to say so.

Both of them are the same decision and he made it in the space of a minute on Monday night in the rain with the van outside and the list of nine doors on the inside of the windscreen, and he has been carrying it since.

**HE WENT ROUND ALL FOUR STREETS IN A SINGLE RUN SAYING THAT FIVE WORDS HE HAS BEEN SAYING SINCE THE MIDDLE OF FEBRUARY CAME OFF HIM, AND HE DID NOT SAY THAT HE GOT THEM OFF A YOUNG MAN WHO PUT THEM INTO A WOMAN'S MOUTH ON PURPOSE, AND AT NUMBER THIRTY-ONE A GIRL WHO HAS LIVED THERE SINCE SHE WAS A YOUNG WOMAN ASKED HIM WHY HE WAS COMING ROUND AND HE TOLD HER THERE WAS NOTHING AT THE END OF IT, WHICH IS NOT TRUE, AND SHE SHUT THE DOOR ON THAT.**
