# Chapter 0622 — The Woman at the Corner of the Car Park

Wednesday the seventeenth of February 2021. He is in his car before half past seven and he is still on it at half past seven at night, and it is all in this borough.

**A WOMAN IN HER EARLY FIFTIES TOLD HIM IN A CAR PARK THAT A WOMAN HAS BEEN BACK TWICE TO A BUILDING HE USED TO WORK IN ASKING FOR A MAN, AND HE CANNOT ESTABLISH WHICH WOMAN, AND HE CANNOT ASK WITHOUT SAYING OUT LOUD THAT HE DID NOT KNOW HE WAS ASKED FOR.**

He is a man in his late thirties. He works somewhere with no lettering on the door and he goes in most days of the week and he has done for most of a decade, and he does not talk about the time before that.

The woman who used to work with him worked there with him. There were three of them and two have gone and one is still there.

He cannot say why that makes it impossible.

---

Here is what was said.

He was in the car park at about ten past eight in the morning doing what he does, which is sitting in the car with the engine off for a bit before he goes in.

And a woman came along the front of the row of cars and said, "I don't suppose you know."

And he said, "About what."

And she said, "Sorry."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "Is that the place."

And he said, "Which place."

And she said, "The big one. With the green doors."

And he said, "There's about nine of them with green doors on this road."

And she said, "There's a woman been asking for a man works there. He's never in when she calls and she's been twice."

And he said, "Has she said who."

And she said, "No. Just the name."

And she said, "And she said she was his lass."

Here is the position at about half past eight and it is not going to improve.

There were three women on that floor when he was there.

One is still there. He sees her every morning and she is at the same desk and she said good morning to him at about ten to eight this morning and he said good morning back.

One left in the summer for a place over the ring road and they had a drink after and she has his mobile and he has hers.

One left before that, quicker than anybody expected, and nobody where she went has heard a word since, and the only person in this borough who has said anything about any of it to anybody is the woman in her early fifties now standing in front of his car.

He cannot rule out either of the two who went.

And here is the part that has taken the rest of the day.

If it was the woman who left for the job over the ring road, then there is nothing in it. That woman tells everybody everything. She would have asked after him in a supermarket and it would have meant a thing about a birthday or a favour.

If it was the woman who left quicker than anybody expected, then a woman of about thirty has been to that building twice asking after a man who was in that place years ago and has never once been told that he left.

And he cannot rule that out, and he cannot ask, and he has not been able to stop since about nine o'clock.

He went in at about ten to nine and got through his day competently, and the competence of that day is the part of it that has frightened him ever since.

Later on the woman who is still there asked him whether the paperwork from the front office had come back and he said it had, and she said, "Are you all right, you look like you've been somewhere in your head all morning."

And he said, "Sorry."

And she said, "No, go on."

And she went back to her screen.

At about one he went out to his car to eat something and the woman in her early fifties was in the car park again, on the far side, on the phone, and she saw him and he got back in.

---

At about half past three he worked out the thing that is actually the difficulty, and it took him the whole morning to get to it and it took him an hour after that to be sure of it.

It is not the two women.

It is that he has to ask a question in order to find out, and the question is this: has anybody been asking for me.

And there is no version of asking that question in which he comes out of it the way he went in.

If he asks the woman at the desk, then he has told her he is the man somebody has been asking for, and from that moment the two of them know something about him, and she will be kind about it, and she will not mention it again, and it will be in the room.

If he asks the woman in the car park, then he has told a stranger he does not know that he is a man worth looking for, and he has said it in a car park on a Wednesday morning, and he will think about that for the rest of the day.

And there is a third thing and it is the real one and it did not arrive until about half past three.

If somebody has been asking for him and he has not known about it, then there is a person in this borough who has gone somewhere twice on his account and he has never once been told.

And he cannot make that be somebody else.

He went and found the woman in the car park at about four, which he has not written down anywhere and has not told anybody about.

She was by the pay machine with a bag of shopping.

And he said, "Sorry. Can I ask you something."

And she said, "Go on."

And he said, "Was there a name on the front."

And she said, "Just the first one."

And he said, "Was it a short one."

And she said, "It was short."

And he said, "Did she say a street."

And she said, "No. She said she was a lass and she'd be about so."

And he said, "So the whole of it is that somebody's been asking."

And she said, "Yes."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "You going to tell me you're not him?"

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Why not."

And he said, "Because you'd have believed me, and you'd have been in here twice telling somebody's daughter about it."

That is what he said and he has been over it in the car since and he cannot tell whether it was true or whether it was the true thing to say.

It was both. That is the honest answer and it took him until about six to get to it.

It was true that he is not the man, in the sense that no woman has been asking after him because he has not been in that place for some time.

And it was also the true thing to say, and the reason it came out so easily is that it was true, and the reason that frightens him is different.

If it is not him, then there is a man on that floor who has had a woman come twice and does not know, and that man is at a desk all day, and is not him.

And he is not going to walk into a building and tell a man at a desk that his daughter has been looking for him.

He got home at about half past six and he told nobody at all, and then he did something he has been thinking about since, which was to sit at the kitchen table and write down what he actually knows and what he has decided he does not want to find out.

What he knows is that a woman has been to that building twice and asked a person at the front for a man.

What he has decided is that he is not going to ask, and that the reason is not to be helpful and is not to be careful.

It is that he has not worked out what he would do if the answer was that it had been about him.

Because if it had, then he would have to decide whether to stand still and let somebody find him, which he has never once in his life done and does not know how to do.

And if he had to decide, he would have to do it in about a second, standing in a corridor with a woman in front of him, and he is a man who stands in car parks with the engine off for a bit before he goes in.

He wrote that down too. He has not shown it to anybody.

**SHE TOLD HIM AT TEN PAST EIGHT THAT A WOMAN HE USED TO WORK WITH HAS BEEN ASKING AFTER HIM, AND HE HAS SPENT THE DAY NOT ASKING WHETHER IT WAS HER, AND HE ASKED HER ONE QUESTION IN A CAR PARK AT FOUR O'CLOCK AND SHE WOULD HAVE BELIEVED HIM, AND HE WROTE DOWN AT HIS KITCHEN TABLE THAT HE COULD NOT SAY WHAT HE WOULD DO IF IT HAD.**
