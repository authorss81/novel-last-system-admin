# Chapter 0629 — The List She Did Not Take

Friday the nineteenth of March 2021. She is in this borough and so is he and neither of them is on anybody's row, and the day begins with a list on a clipboard and ends with her standing at a parade with a basket at her feet.

**A WOMAN HAS BEEN DOING A MAN'S SHOPPING FOR A GOOD WHILE AND HAS DECIDED TO STOP, AND HE COMES TO HER GATE AND ASKS HER WHY SHE HAS NOT BEEN, AND SHE GIVES HIM FIVE WORDS THAT DO NOT FIT HIM AT ALL, AND BEFORE LUNCH SHE HAS HEARD THE SAME FIVE WORDS FROM A STRANGER IN A QUEUE.**

She is a woman in her late sixties. He is a man in his late sixties. They have known each other since before either of them was living in this borough and they are not family and have never pretended to be.

She has done his shopping since before the summer. He writes the list out on a clipboard on a Sunday night and she takes it on a Tuesday, or she has, and she buys what is on it and not what is not on it, and he pays her back in a note and she has never once counted it.

---

Here is what was said at her gate, at about ten in the morning, and it took a good while because he would not go away and she would not open the gate all the way.

He had the clipboard in his hand and he had put it behind his back when he came up, and she saw him do it.

And she said, "You've not brought the list."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "I said I'd not come on Thursday."

And he said, "You said nothing to me."

And she said, "I said it to you on Wednesday."

And he said, "You said nothing."

And she said, "I said it to you on the telephone."

And he said, "You didn't."

And she said, "I said, I'm not doing the Tuesday any more."

And he said, "That isn't what you said."

And she said, "What did I say."

And he said, "You said, the weather's turned."

And she said, "And you said, is it."

And he said, "I did."

And she said, "Right," and she put her hand on the latch, and she did not lift it.

And he said, "You'll have missed the shop."

And she said, "I haven't missed it. I went on Thursday morning on my own and I got what I wanted."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "I haven't been to yours."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "Not once."

And he said, "Not once."

And she said, "I've had a think about it."

And he said, "About what."

And she said, "About the Tuesday."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "He never asks for anything."

And he said, "Who."

And she said, "That's my sentence and it's about your son and I'm sorry, it came out the same either way. He never asks for anything. Your son. He rings me at night and he asks me to look in and he has never once said would you mind and he has never once said I'd be grateful, and if he had said either of them I would have kept on."

And he said, "That's about him."

And she said, "It's about me and it's about him and I can't separate them, and it isn't about you, but you'll not want to hear that."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "You don't ask me for anything either. You write the list out and you bring it round and you say here's your list, and you have never once said would you, and if you had said it once I would have come every Tuesday of my life."

And he said, "I don't say that sort of thing."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "No."

And she said, "That's it. That's all of it."

And he said, "Right."

And he stood at the gate with the clipboard behind his back and did not say anything else for a while, and she waited for him to.

And he said, "That's a bit strong."

And she said, "It's not strong. It's a list of shopping."

And he said, "I've never once asked you."

And she said, "Never. Not once."

And he said, "Not once in all that?"

And she said, "You did say it once."

And he said, "When."

And she said, "Years back. You said, would you mind if I left the money."

And he said, "That was about money."

And she said, "It was the closest you've come."

And he said, "What money."

And she said, "Never mind."

That is the whole of what was said. She has been over it since and the only bit that has moved anything is the bit about the money, and she has not been able to put down why that is the bit.

Here is what she has got, and she did not have it while she was saying it, which is the finding of the day.

One: he is the only person she has ever done this for. She has done it for him since the summer and for nobody else since her mother died, and she has never mentioned that to him and she is not going to.

Two: the true reason is not the list and it is not the asking. The true reason is that his son telephoned her late at night in the middle of the month and asked her to keep an eye on his dad, and she said she would, and she has not been able to get past the fact that she said it at that hour to a woman she has known since they were both at the same place of work.

Three: the true reason is that she has decided she is not going to be the one who is told things at that hour about a man in his late sixties who cannot get a list to a supermarket, and if she keeps doing the Tuesday then she will be, and she will be that for years, and there will be a day somebody telephones her about that as well.

Four: and this is the part she has not admitted to herself until about half past three in the afternoon, and it is that she is sixty-eight and the walk there and back with bags is a thing she can do and is not going to be able to do for much longer, and if she stops now then she stops while she can still get up the stairs to her own front door, and if she waits then the stopping will be done to her.

None of that is about the asking. All of it is a decision, and she made it on the Wednesday night just before midnight, and she is not sorry about it and she did not expect to be doing this on a Friday morning with the gate half open.

And what she said at the gate was the asking.

Here is what she did with the rest of the morning.

He went away at about ten past ten with the clipboard still behind his back, and he did not put it round and he did not leave it, and she watched him go up the road and turn.

She did the washing. She went out to the parade because she wanted milk.

And that is the whole of what happened and she has had it over since and it is the only part of the day she would call a coincidence, and she does not believe in coincidence and she has never said so out loud in her life.

She was in the queue at the shop on the parade at about a quarter to twelve with her basket at her feet and there was a man in front of her she has never spoken to and a woman in front of him she has never spoken to, and they were talking about a third person that neither of them had ever spoken to and that she was never going to know one thing about.

And the man said, "I just don't think it's on."

And the woman said, "What isn't on."

And the man said, "Asking. He never asks for anything, and then he's surprised when nobody does it for him."

And the woman said, "Well, that's about right."

And the man said, "Aren't you."

And the woman said, "Aren't I what."

And the man said, "Aren't you. About right."

The man and the woman in that queue have never in their lives seen the woman at the end of the till queue with a basket at her feet. She is certain of that. She has lived in this borough since before she was married and she knows what a Tuesday looks like and she has never seen either of them on the parade.

And he said the five words in the voice of a man who has heard a thing a good many times, and the woman in front of him agreed with him straight away, and that is how it goes, and that is exactly how it goes, and she has heard a version of it three or four times since Christmas and has never once said it.

She stood there with her basket and she thought: that is a thing people say.

And then, before she had got a yard up the parade, she thought: that is my sentence.

And she has been carrying it since before the weekend without knowing it was a shape, and she put it into the middle of a sentence about a man who has asked her for the Tuesday every week since before the summer, and he did not ask her to have it out of him.

And she gave it to him anyway.

She put the basket down on the kerb outside the shop for a moment and then picked it up again, and a woman going past said, "You all right, love?"

And she said, "I've left the bag."

And the woman said, "Right."

She got home at about ten past twelve and she put the milk in and she sat down and she did not do anything else for about an hour, and that is not like her.

At about half past two the telephone went.

And he said, "Have you had a look at the list?"

And she said, "I told you Friday I'd not come."

And he said, "You told me nothing."

And she said, "I told you at your gate."

And he said, "At your gate."

And she said, "At my gate, at ten past ten, with you standing outside it."

And he said, "You've told me that twice now and I'd remember it."

And she said, "You'd remember the shopping."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "I'm not doing the Tuesday."

And he said, "Not at all?"

And she said, "Not at all."

And he said, "Well, that's that, then."

And she said, "That's that."

And he said, "I'll get the woman at number fifty-one to do it."

And she said, "You will."

And he said, "What?"

And she said, "Nothing."

And he said, "Say it."

And she said, "I said nothing."

And he said, "You've said something. You've said it twice now and both times you've stopped. Go on."

And she said, "You're going to ask a woman at number fifty-one and she is going to do it and she is not going to have known me from before, and you'll never know whether she asked you or not."

And he said, "Is that an objection?"

And she said, "That's an observation."

And he said, "You made it at my gate this morning with a gate open and now you're making it on the telephone, and I'd rather you made it at the gate."

And she said, "You'd rather I came to your gate."

And he said, "I'd rather we didn't have this on the telephone."

And she said, "It's easier on the telephone."

And he said, "Yes."

And she said, "That's what I thought."

And he said, "Is that all?"

And she said, "No."

And he said, "Go on."

And she said, "And I want to say one thing and then I'm not going to say anything else about it, and you're not to bring it up again."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "The reason I gave you at the gate this morning was the wrong reason."

And he said, "What."

And she said, "You never asked me and that is not why I stopped. I stopped because I have decided I am not going to be the one anybody telephones at that hour about."

And he said, "Has somebody telephoned you."

And she said, "Your son did."

And he said, "When."

And she said, "Middle of the month."

And he said, "About me."

And she said, "About keeping an eye on you."

And he said, "You said yes."

And she said, "I said yes."

And he said, "And you didn't tell me."

And she said, "No."

And he said, "Why."

And she said, "Because you'd have thought it was you."

And he said, "Did he say it was me."

And she said, "He didn't say anything. He said it was late and would I mind looking in."

And he said, "Right."

And she said, "And I said yes, and I sat here afterwards and I knew exactly what I had agreed to, and I have been doing his shopping since before the summer and I am not doing it for him and I am not doing it for you either, not any more, and it isn't the list and it isn't the asking."

And he said, "What is it."

And she said, "I'm tired of it."

And he said, "Right."

And he said, "Well."

And she said, "Well."

She sat at her own kitchen table after that with nothing switched on.

Here is what she has, and she is not going to be able to hand it to anybody, and she has been over it four times since he put the telephone down.

He asked her why she had not come and she gave him a reason that was about manners.

And then a man she has never spoken to said the same five words in a queue on the parade in the voice of a man who has heard a thing a good many times, and a woman agreed with him at once, and she stood there and worked out that it is round. Not round the town. Round her. It has got itself a shape and a voice and two people who agree with each other about a man who never asks anybody for anything, and she put a sentence into it this morning at her own gate, and she had had it in her mouth before that and did not know where she got it.

And the man she gave it to is the last person on earth it fits.

And he has just telephoned and said he will ask the woman at number fifty-one, and he will not say please, and she has told him he will not say please, and neither of them knows whether that is true.
