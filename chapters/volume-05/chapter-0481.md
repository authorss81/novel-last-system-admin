# Chapter 0481 — The Hour They Talked About Him

Thursday, 28 March 2019, from about half past three in the afternoon until about half past five. An hour, and everything two men said in it, in Tideglass.

**TWO MEN STOOD AT THE END OF A FOOTWAY FOR AN HOUR ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF MARCH AND SAID ABOUT A THOUSAND WORDS AND THREE QUARTERS OF THEM WERE ABOUT A THIRD MAN WHO WAS WALKING ABOUT A MILE AWAY AT THE TIME, AND NOT ONE OF THE WORDS WAS THE ONE BOTH OF THEM HAD COME TO SAY.**

The three of them are within about five years of one another and they have been in this borough all of their lives in the way that people who leave and come back are, and they did not make each other friends. They were put at the same table at a school neither of them can remember and that has since been sold.

There is a bit of a wall at the end of that footway, about six foot of it, waist high, with nothing behind it, and it is the only piece of that street anybody ever stands at, and the three of them have stood at it since about 1975.

There is a rule.

It was made in 2008, at about ten at night, outside a place on the upper side of the town, and the reason for it was a thing that happened that year to the third one of them, and the rule is one sentence, and the sentence is: *none of us tells the other two anything the other two do not already know.*

It was made once, in one minute, by one of them, and the other two agreed to it, and none of the three has ever explained it to anybody.

It has been kept for ten years and neither of the men on that footway has any idea whether the other one has kept it. That is the difficulty with a rule like that. You cannot ask. Asking is telling.

---

What each of them knows, and neither has said to the other, and the third one does not know that either of them knows:

The first man has seen him at about four in the morning twice in the last fortnight, coming out of the place at the top end, and has seen him in a pub on the twelfth of February with his coat still on.

The second man has seen him on the sixth of March at about ten in the morning in a shop in the town, and the other man was not there, and has not been told that he was not there, and has not asked.

Both of them have told themselves that they are not carrying anybody else's affairs. Both of them have also noticed in about the last month that neither of them has said one word to the other about any of it, and neither of them has done anything about that either.

Both of them have the same reason, and the same reason is not loyalty and it is not care.

The same reason is that in 2008 the three of them agreed, and a man who has agreed something is not a man who reminds anybody that he has agreed it. Reminding is telling. And the whole point of that rule in 2008 was that the three of them were going to be in a position where saying one word out loud would finish something for one of them, and none of the three was going to be the one who finished it.


They talk about him for an hour.

That is what the hour is. He is the subject of the whole of it and neither of them says the word that is the subject of the whole of it.

They go through about three weeks of him in about the order it happened. A thing he said to the first one of them outside a shop. A thing the second one of them heard him tell somebody else and is still going. The way he has been spending Saturdays. A run of trouble in about 2011 that neither of them has ever mentioned to anybody and that comes up in about the second ten minutes and goes again. His garden, which is the best of the three of them and which he has not done a thing in since about 2016. His wife, who is well, and who was well in 2008 as well, and who does not know about any of this and has never been told any of it and is not going to be.

Neither of them says anything about what he has been doing in the evenings.

It is not that they avoid it. It is that every road in that conversation goes to it and neither of them will take it, and they both know the other one will not, and they go round the same three corners about nine times between them in an hour.

The closest it comes is at about twenty past four.

The first man says that the second man should have told him if anything was going on.

And the second man says, "There isn't anything going on."

And the first man says, "Right," and they go on.

And the second man, standing there about a foot away from a man he has known since he was nine, hears himself say *there isn't anything going on* out loud to a man who has seen what he has seen, and goes on standing there, and neither of them ever refers to that sentence again.


A woman comes out of the house behind that bit of a wall at about half past four.

She is in her forties and she has lived in that house about twenty years and she has seen the third man come in at about four in the morning four times in six weeks, and she has not told anybody, and she is not going to, and she does not know that the two men standing at the wall are talking about him.

She comes out into about six foot of space because it has been bright and she wants the air. She sees two men standing at a wall doing nothing.

And she says, "You'll be stood there all afternoon."

And the first man says, "We will, likely," and she says, "Well," and goes back in and shut the door.

That is the whole of it. It took about nine seconds and it is the only time in that hour that either of the two men said a complete sentence to anybody who was not the other one.

---

What each of them took away from that footway at about half past five is the same thing and it is not the same.

The first one walked home up that street and thought: *he has seen all of it and he has not said one word, and he did not ask me a single question either, and he has been sitting there for an hour letting me talk about him, and there is no version of that in which he has not decided I am not somebody you talk to about this.*

The second one walked the other way and thought exactly that, about the first one.

Neither of them has been wrong about what the other one has done. Each of them has it exactly right. The rule is what they are both inside of, and it was made in 2008 by the third man, and there is nobody alive who could now tell either of them that what the other one did was the other one keeping to it.

**A MAN WALKED INTO A HOUSE ON THE UPPER SIDE OF THAT TOWN AT ABOUT TEN AT NIGHT ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF MARCH AND SAT DOWN IN A KITCHEN AND DID NOT SWITCH ANYTHING ON, AND NOBODY ELSE IN THAT HOUSE KNEW HE WAS NOT SLEEPING.**
