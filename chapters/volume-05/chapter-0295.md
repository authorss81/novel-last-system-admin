# Chapter 0295 — Four Steps

Tuesday the thirty-first of January 2017, from about half past three in the afternoon, four outside steps off a street off Marsh Lane in Tideglass.

**A HANDRAIL HAD BEEN LYING ACROSS THE TOP STEP OF THOSE FOUR STEPS SINCE BEFORE CHRISTMAS AND A MAN CARRYING A BAG OF TOOLS PICKED IT UP AND PUT IT BACK ON THE WALL IN ABOUT A QUARTER OF AN HOUR, AND A MAN WHO GOES TO WORK AT HALF PAST SIX CAME DOWN THOSE STEPS AT HALF PAST FOUR AND FOUND IT ON THE WALL, AND THE TWO OF THEM STOOD IN THE DOORWAY OF THAT FLAT, AND WHAT PASSED BETWEEN THEM AMOUNTED TO NOTHING THAT EITHER OF THEM HAD ASKED THE OTHER FOR.**

The four steps are outside a door in a street off Marsh Lane, up one flight from the pavement, with a rail on the brick beside them that is meant to be a handrail and is a handrail in the way a length of pipe is a handrail. There is one flat above that door and one flat below it and the ground floor has a shop in it that has been shut since before the summer, and the four steps are concrete with a nosing on each that has gone chalky.

The rail came off its brackets some time in December. It was lying on the top step on its side with a stone under the middle of it to keep the wind off it, and about a foot of it went out over the pavement, and anybody going up those steps had to go round it.

The man in his thirties came out of that shop's doorway at about half past three with a bag of tools, a length of flat bar in it and a cordless drill, having been in a kitchen at the top of that lane all afternoon and having come out of that shop's door because it is the same door you come out of whether you have business in there or not.

He saw the rail and he stopped on the second step and looked at it for a bit.

He put his bag down on the third step, and took the rail off the stone, and carried it up the four steps and stood it against the wall, and went down again and got the drill.

The brackets had come off the wall with the fixings still in them, and the holes in the brick were there behind them, and the fixings had turned in the brick a half-turn and then sheared, which is what fixings do in a wall that is old and wet and gets frozen.

He did not put new holes in. He put a new plug in each of the old ones with the drill and a bit the size of the hole, and drove them, and put the rail back over the brackets, and put the two long screws in that had come out with the brackets, and drew them up until the rail came up square with the wall, and tried it with his whole weight, and it did not move.

The whole of it took him about a quarter of an hour and it did not cost the person on the top step anything, and there was nobody to charge.

He put the two brackets that had come off in the side pocket of his jacket and picked his bag up and went back down the four steps.

A woman went past at about ten to four with a dog and looked up at what he was doing and said to nobody in particular that he had been at that an hour.

---

The woman of about forty-two with the key to the hall off the parade came up that street at about ten past four on her way to open that hall, and she had come up that lane because it is two streets shorter than Marsh Lane in this weather, and she went up those four steps out of the wind and stopped.

The rail was on the wall.

"That was on the step," said the woman of about forty-two.

"It was," said the man in his thirties.

"Since about December, that was," said the woman of about forty-two. "You go round it. You go round it and you look at it going round it."

"Aye," said the man in his thirties.

She looked at the rail for a moment longer, and then looked at the four steps, and then she went up them and let herself in, and that was the whole of what she said about it.

The man in his thirties went back down to the pavement and put his bag in the back of the van.

---

The man who works nights finished at half past four that day, and the tea was in him, and he came down those four steps with his coat open to get a paper off the low window sill inside the door, because that paper comes at about half past four and it is not worth going down a stair for.

The rail was on the wall and the top step was clear.

He stood on the top step with the paper in his hand and looked at it, and then he said, out loud, to nobody, "Somebody's had that up."

The man in his thirties was at the bottom of those steps with his bag in his hand, about to get into the van, and he said, "Aye."

"You done that?"

"Aye," said the man in his thirties. "There's two brackets off it. The fixings had sheared. I put plugs in the same holes."

"Which holes."

"The ones it came off," said the man in his thirties. "There's no point putting new ones in that wall."

The man who works nights went up the four steps again and put his hand on the rail and moved it, the way a man moves a thing he has not touched before, and it did not move.

"That'll hold, then," he said.

"It's on two plugs and a bracket each end," said the man in his thirties. "It's not going anywhere."

They stood there. The man in his thirties did not come up the steps, and the man who works nights did not go down them, and the doorway of that flat was four foot behind the man who works nights and neither of them went into it.

"I'd not thought about a rail," said the man who works nights. "I want to say that. I've come up that step about a hundred times since Christmas and I've not once thought, that's off its wall. You come up it in the dark and you go round it and you come down it in the dark and you go round it, and that's the thing about it. You don't register a thing you go round every day."

"Aye," said the man in his thirties.

"So who am I telling."

"You could ring the woman whose kitchen that is and ask her if she's had a word with the man who did it," said the man in his thirties. "That's what I'd do, if I was going to do anything with it. But I did it on my own time and I did it with my own plugs so there is nothing to ring anybody about."

"You could have knocked."

"I could have knocked," said the man in his thirties, "and then you'd have had a man on your step at half past four in the afternoon asking you was you all right, and you'd have had to make him a cup of tea, and then you'd have had a man on your step at half past four in the afternoon for nothing, and you'd have had it in your head that a man on your step at half past four in the afternoon is a thing that can happen to you."

The man who works nights had the paper folded in his hand.

"It's not a bad rail," he said.

"It's the same one that was on it," said the man in his thirties.

The man who works nights was at the works at about ten to five and on the days and at about two in the morning, and nothing about that rail was ever in his wages and nothing about it was ever going to be, and he was not on anything and nothing in that street was ever going to put him on anything, and the woman who let that flat to him had not been told that anything had happened to her steps and would not have been interested if she had.

The man in his thirties wrote nothing down. The job sheet in the van was for a kitchen at the top of that lane, and a kitchen is not a rail, and the rail is not on that job sheet, and no line on that sheet is ever going to have a rail on it.

**AND ON THE FRIDAY AND THE SATURDAY AND THE SUNDAY THE RAIL WAS ON THE WALL ABOVE THOSE FOUR STEPS, AND THE STONE WAS STILL UNDER THE DOOR SILL INSIDE THE FLAT BECAUSE NOBODY USED IT FOR ANYTHING, AND THE TWO BRACKETS THAT HAD COME OFF WERE IN THE SIDE POCKET OF A JACKET IN A VAN ON A STREET OFF MARSH LANE, AND THE MAN WHO PUT THAT RAIL ON THAT WALL NEVER WENT BACK UP THOSE STEPS AGAIN.**
