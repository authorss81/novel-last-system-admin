# Chapter 0743 — The Mirror On The Wall

Wednesday the twentieth of July 2022, in this borough. He is in a house two streets back from the ring road from first light until the middle of the afternoon, and the whole of it is a sideboard that will not go round a stair and a mirror that is not on anybody's list.

**A MAN IN HIS LATE TWENTIES GOES UP A TURNING STAIR IN A HOUSE WHERE THE CLEARANCE IS FOUR ROOMS AND THREE THINGS, COUNTS FOUR DOOR FRAMES WHERE THERE ARE THREE DOORS AND TELLS THE WOMAN HE IS WORKING FOR SO, GETS A SOLID SIDEBOARD DOWN IN TWO GOES ALONE, TAKES A MIRROR OFF THE LANDING WALL BECAUSE IT FOULS THE TURN, IS TOLD THAT THE MIRROR IS NOT THE WOMAN'S TO GIVE AND TAKES IT DOWN ANYWAY, PUTS IT IN THE BACK OF THE VAN FACING FORWARD, AND DOES NOT PUT ANYTHING ON THE BARE PATCH OF WALL IT CAME OFF.**

He is a man in his late twenties in a house two streets back from the ring road on Wednesday. The woman he is working for is in the house all day and he speaks to her in the hall and in the two rooms.

---

They came in at first light. The woman had found him two days before at a shop off the ring road where he does his own work and had asked him at the back of the van with the roller door up, and he had given her a yes and a no in the same breath.

The hall is straight and the stairs turn at the half landing with a wall on the inside of the turn, and a wide board on the wall of the turn, and the light from a front window coming up underneath the stair so that the bottom half of the stair is brighter than the top.

The sideboard is on the top floor in the room at the top of the stair. It is solid, it is old, it has two doors and four feet and it came out of a house somewhere else before this one.

"Can it go down." The woman stood at the bottom of the stair with her bag in her hand.

"It will not go round that." He nodded at the wall on the inside of the turn.

---

He went up first and counted, which is what he does before anything else.

Four door frames off the landing. Three doors on them. The fourth is a frame with a cupboard built into the back of it, with the frame still showing round the outside of the cupboard where the door used to swing, and no door on it.

"You have got four doors on this landing." He put his thumb on the frame nearest the turn.

"You have got four frames." The woman came up two steps. "I have been in this house four times and I have never counted a frame."

"Three doors." He knocked the frame with the back of his knuckles.

"The fourth one is a cupboard and somebody has framed the cupboard and left the frame showing round the outside of it, which is a thing somebody has done in a hurry and never come back to."

"That room is not on my list."

"The room it is in is not on your list. I am telling you about the landing." He put his hand flat on the newel and left it there a second.

---

The sideboard would go down as far as the half landing and no further.

He got it to the turn on his own with the boards under it, on his back going down and his feet on the board edge, which is a way of doing it that works if the stair is the right width and it is the right width and he is the right size.

At the turn he stopped it with his shoulder and put his head round the side of it and looked at the landing above.

The mirror is on the wall of the landing at the level of the turn, set in a frame, and it is wide, and it is flat on that wall, and it comes out past the line of the stair by the depth of a man's two fingers.

"That is what has it." He got his fingers round the edge of the mirror frame and stopped.

---

The woman came up two steps and looked.

"That is not mine to give." She put her hand on his wrist and took it away again.

"It is on the wall."

"It is on the wall and it is the old woman's and it is in her own son's house and I have a list," the woman says, in the voice of somebody reading out a rule, "and the list does not have a mirror on it, and if I say yes to a mirror this week I will be saying yes to a piano in October and there will be a piano coming down this stair at whoever's expense."

"You are not saying yes to anything. I am taking it down because it is fouling the turn, and it will come down anyway when this house is emptied, and it will come down to somebody who has not got two stairs to get on with."

"You have not asked him."

"I have not got his name and I have not got any way of getting his name, and that is not the reason and you know it is not."

The woman looked at the mirror for a moment.

"Do not damage the frame." It is out of her before she has finished the sentence and from the step, and then she comes up the other two.

---

He took the picture hook out of the plaster with two fingers and it came away whole, and he turned the mirror round off the wall and got his arms round the back of it and brought it down the two steps to the bottom of the turn and stood it against the wall in the passage below, face to the wall, where it is out of the way of the stair and of the light.

He went back up for the sideboard.

It took two goes. The first one got it down to the turn and stopped it and he stood on the half landing above it and worked it round the corner inch by inch with the boards, and the second go got it the rest of the way with his shoulder and both hands and the sound of it going down two or three steps at a time.

He got it to the bottom of the stair and stood it on the boards in the hall the way it was standing in the room.

And the woman was on the step behind him.

"That is not four rooms." He put a hand flat on the lid of it.

"No. That is three things and a mirror."

---

They had the middle of the day on the two rooms downstairs and the two chairs and the bed, and he carried the bed out in pieces because the bed frame is one thing and the mattress is another and there is no way to get either of them out of a front door at the same time.

The woman worked through the rooms with a bag and did not open the two drawers that were taped shut, and he did not ask about the tape.

At about the middle of the afternoon she went up to look at the top room for herself, because there was nothing left downstairs to do, and because the woman who booked the job had told her the room was empty twice and she had not put her own eyes on it in a house she was standing in all day.

She was up there a good deal longer than a room with nothing in it takes.

He carried the last of the chairs out and put them on the boards and came back in and she was on the step with her hand on the newel, and she said nothing at all about it, and then she went down the hall and put her bag in the sack for the tip and tied it, and did not look at him while she did it.

Then she came back down the hall and stood in front of the mirror standing face to the wall.

"That is going to have to go back up there." She put her fingers on the edge of it without moving it.

"Yes."

"Not today."

"No. Today it goes out with me and I put it in the back of the van and it stands up in a house off the ring road against a wall until somebody wants it, and if the old woman's son wants it back it goes back up that stair and it will foul the turn again and he can have it hung round the other side of the landing like a sensible man."

She looked at the back of the mirror, which is the wall side, and the two plates of the picture hook still in it.

"Do not put anything on that wall to go over it. Not a sheet and not a board and not another mirror."

"I will not put anything on it." He took his hand off it and wiped the mark his hand had left on the dust on the back of the mirror.

He shut the roller door on the van with the mirror stood against the side of it facing forward so that it did not go over in the first corner, and the sideboard was across the back with the boards under it and the strap round it.

He got in and sat with the engine off with the list of three things in his head where it had been all day. He had been in the top room at first light with the sideboard, and it had been bare with a light through the window and nothing else in it, and she had gone up in the middle of the day to look at it herself and had been a good deal longer in there than the room needed, and had come down the stairs without giving anything away and had tied her bag without looking at him.
