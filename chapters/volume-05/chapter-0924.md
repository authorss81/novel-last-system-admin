# Chapter 0924 — Across The Warped Gap

Friday the twelfth of July 2024, at the warped cold frame at number ten when the afternoon light slants across the glass. The gap at a frame peg.

**SHE HAS A WARPED COLD FRAME AT NUMBER TEN WITH A GAP AT ITS PEG AND SHE MEANS TO SPAN THUMB AND MIDDLE FINGER ACROSS THAT GAP WITH FOREFINGER RESTING ON TOP AND HER SISTER IS AT THE SIDE PATH FROM THE BEDS WITH A TWINE LOOP ROUND HER WRIST.**

The cold frame went up with the spring sowing when the beds were dug, and the glass above it has been wiped twice while the boards have kept their seats.

It is a frame of pine boards about four feet long, with the sides pegged and one board bowed off its peg a finger where the timber warped, and soil banked at its toe where the fork leaves it. She wants the gap at that peg, where the board stands off the post it is pegged to and never quite closes again.

The pine of the board faces the garden; the gap at the peg faces the timber inside it, and the two keep different accounts. So the thumb to middle span the frame gap idle-forefinger. Cool timber greets the span from below, gap rims snagging where fibres lift.

The span sits a hand wide and meets soil banked at the toe, black and tight, flecked with grit and a sliced worm cast from the forking weeks back. She bears the span down, then up against the bank, and the soil settles a scant quarter inch beneath the span, knitting anew as the hand lifts.

Under the span lies packed grit. Sharp pale grains heaped where the frame sheds its wash at the bank foot, sand interlocked grain on grain. The opening proves stub-short at the peg, banked earth choking its toe, and the choke wedges the board fast when gusts tug.

She lifts the span clear of the gap and rubs the dirt off against the frame upright, and stands with glass beside her as light leaves the beds. Her sister comes to the side path from the beds with a twine loop round her wrist and does not wind it up.

Loop on wrist, she halted where the path meets the beds, studying frame, then rows, then frame anew. Presently the loop came off and on again, staying wrist-bound as she walked on, the walk vacant in her wake.

One morning's work by the joiner put those gap boards together, with the ends housed and the pegs driven round them, and the glass went on last with old putty gone hard.

Seasoned putty, hard years on, outlasts fresh bedding through rain, desiccated long ago into crumbs instead of yield.

---

Crumbling under the near light has sprung the rebate wide down the board; the pane above sags mid-span and kicks at the tips, inviting draughts through the opening. Once a light has slipped like that, every storm puts its water down the underside of the bar, and the run cuts a furrow in the dirt at the frame toe.

Toe rill water sheers away at the swollen bank, the raised earth sipping the flow into itself. Raised like that, the bank takes the water the warped board throws at it, and what lies beyond the bank never gets wet at all.

Weather combed the grain out of the frame pine and the palm feels every stroke of it, rough from end to end. The rims of the gap are coarse with saw scoring, and the grit banked at the toe is rough grain by grain. Dawn damp pearls on coarse pine and rolls away, each roller scouring the face a little cleaner.

Earliest dry after rain goes to the combed-grain face; the oil-rubbed fellow nurses wet at its skirt till afternoon. The frame offers its coarse south cheek to noon and dries there ahead of its shaded gap, keeping northern lichen and a bare crown. North-board lichen creeps far where damp clings to the pine and falters where noon sun hits.

Let two summers go and a film of lichen varnishes that board, patterned so a thumbnail can trace its lines and a finger rubs nothing off. Where the beds are rubbed every spring the crust comes back edge-first, the centre of the patch sticking out to the last, and nothing gives the age away so plainly.

Glass shade nurses the lichen after the beds parch, and July marks the turn along the whole frame.

Light-shed showers converge over the warped board and plunge jointly on the peg gap. The opening swallows the heart of the plunge while the board spills the remainder, the bank guzzling till it blackens.

---

The far boards have no gap under them at all, so whatever falls on them goes over the glass and off the end; the peg end stays damp below all winter whatever the sun does overhead. Glass last wiped seasons back, yet the frame footing stays put to a finger's width under the same drenching.

The glass has settled its rebate by a coin's thickness across three summers, and the paint fleck that stood flush with the peg since the frame painting rests a whisker lower now.

Wet winters blister paint, yet the light-sheltered fleck adheres, vouching the board rim a keeper of paint. Nowhere else on the frame sits such a mark; the solitary fleck implies the boards descend plumb. Toe soil lies dark and close, netted with pale rootlets that went quiet over winter and bestir ahead of August.

There is a tenant in that gap and it has kept the same footing through the winter, and the dust at the mouth of the bank powders loose under a fingertip. Nothing shows at that peg but the wood itself; the board has carried no silk across it since the hinges last had oil.

Light bars bed with stiles snug, pane glass beds with putty snug out of the wet, and bed bricks bed with soil snug.

Seed trays inside the frame terrace toward the light, pine boxes black with damp at the corners. The corner joints finger together, tiny dovetails the maker cut by eye, one tail fat where the saw wandered. Glass panes overlap fish-scale-wise at the ridge, upper lapping lower, rain ticking from pane to pane in showers. The ticking quickens as showers fatten and slows to single plinks after, each plink flinging a bead off the lap. Seedlings in the trays lean pane-ward by midday, pale necks bending sunward, straightening in dusk cool. The bending writes the day in green, a script the morning watering erases. Evening cool unkinks each neck by slow degrees, and dawn finds the row standing parade-straight till the sun returns, cotyledons unfolding like tiny green flags, each pair a semaphore for rain, and the frame answers, ticking as boards swell in damp and sighing as they shrink in sun, a dialogue no draught interrupts. Putty dust powders the rebate after hot days, pale pollen the finger smears, and the smear wipes the glass a season longer, finger-polished till it shines, and the shine draws the eye along the lights end to end.

Forks in the bed fork the soil to a hand deep each spring, flat tines that part worms and leave them whole. Coils of spare twine hang on the frame peg, jute loops that stiffen in rain and slacken in sun. The bed rims showcase the seasons, tidemarks of algae that climb in wet springs and bleach in dry Julys. The tidemarks band the boards in greens and greys, each band a year the frame stood.

Corner pegs occupy mallet-rounded bored holes; the near one rides a hair high where warping boarded round it. Such elevation weathers the crown dark ahead of its sunken fellows, pale still in timber. The rebate under the near light runs open a finger where the putty crumbled, and the glass sits on two shims of slate with dust packed round them.

The plane chattered the rebate and it is coarse under the thumb to this day. The wedges are coarse where the knife pared them, and the laps coarse where the putty was squeezed out of them. Linseed wiped on the lights takes two finger-wide runners out of all that, amber lanes the sun deepens month by month. Runner gloss shows where the lights slide, bright amid rough, tacky after rain.

She backs off along the row of beds and reads the frame from the end of it, glass shut above, the gap keeping its soil. Off that bowed crown the dawn rain comes away in one piece, sluicing the pine bright, and the loose meal lifts while the tight grain stays where it is.

The frame stands where the garden set it, so the glass keeps its lights and the soil stays at its toe.
