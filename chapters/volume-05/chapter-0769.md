# Chapter 0769 — The Wire On The Gate Post

Wednesday the second of November 2022, in this borough. He is at his own unit and yard from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a gate post dug out and put back with two turns of wire on it and nothing at all in the middle.

**A MAN IN HIS EARLY FORTIES HAS A GATE POST THAT MOVES UNDER HIS HAND AND THE LATCH THAT WON'T CATCH, THE MAN FROM THE COTTAGE DIGS THE POST OUT AND RAMS IT AND WIRES IT AT THE TOP AND THE BOTTOM, AND THE POST NOW LEANS IN THE MIDDLE BETWEEN THE TWO TURNS LIKE A HINGE.**

He is a man in his early forties at the unit on Wednesday. The man from the cottage comes through the unit gate with a spade over his shoulder and goes straight out to the post and does the second half his own way.

---

The roller door is up to its bracket. Both gates are leaning on the wall they lean on, and their chalk has faded to almost nothing, so that the one at the door end now looks like something that was written on it once and rubbed out and did not quite go. The stool that folds in two stands where it stands against the paint. The drawer is shut, and lying on the bottom of it, where it has lain since July, is the bag with the brush in it.

The length of oak has been on the bench since the end of September, with dust on the planed face and a thumbprint of hard black wood at the far end, where the shoulder stands out over that face by about as much as the back of a knife is thick.

The latch is hooked back over the gate and has been since the spring. He has pulled it and pushed it a dozen times this month and it goes over and catches on nothing, and the gate is a gate that has to be lifted to be shut, which is what it has been since before the wire went on.

The post is the reason. He can put both hands on the gate post and move it, not much and not easily, but it moves, and it moves at about the height of his hip, which is the worst place for it to move.

He tried it in the spring and it moved then too. Somebody packed it that year with wire, and there is still wire on it, and the wire is rusted into the post in about a dozen places and does not come off and cannot be got off without cutting into the wood.

He has a spade, a bolster, a bar, a lump of packing from an old window frame, a coil of wire, and the intention of cutting the old wire off, knocking the post out of the ground, packing the hole out with brick and rubble and concrete, and bolting a new plate on for the latch.

The man from the cottage comes through the unit gate in the morning with a spade over his shoulder.

---

"You are not cutting that wire off."

"I am cutting that wire off."

"Cut that wire off and you have a post that moves, a hole full of rust, and nothing to catch the latch on." He drops his spade. "Where does it move."

"It moves about here." He puts his hand flat on the post at hip height and pushes.

"It moves in the middle." The man puts his own hand on it at the same height and pushes. "The bottom of that post is in ground that has never moved and the top of it is carried by the hinge on the gate, and the middle is the only part of it that has ever been loose. That wire went round it from the top down and it did nothing for the middle then and it is not going to do anything for the middle now."

"Then I will pack it."

"Then you will pack it." The man picks his spade up again. "Give me two minutes to dig it out and tell me what you are packing it with, because that is the whole of it."

"The rubble from the hole."

"That is right. Not the rubble off the yard."

"It is off the yard as well. There is a barrowful by the wall from when I filled the other hole."

"Then that is right too."

---

They have it out in under half an hour, which surprises him. The post is round, about the thickness of his wrist above the ground and thicker at the bottom where it has been set in concrete, and it comes out whole, and the hole under it is about a foot and a half deep and full of the inside of the ground that has been in and out of it for years.

The wood of the post is black from the ground up to about a hand's width and it is black and soft right through on the side that was facing the wall. Above that it is grey, and above that it is sound.

"Look at that."

"I have looked at that a long while."

"You have looked at it. You have not done anything about it."

Then the man fills the hole back in properly, in three courses, and it is the best part of the morning. He throws the coarse stuff in to a hand's width over the bottom and rams it with the back of the bolster, then the middle stuff, then the fine stuff at the top, and rams every one of them until the handle of the bolster rings when he hits it. He waters nothing into it and puts nothing soft in it. He puts the post back and drives it down until the collar of old concrete round it is at the same height as the rest of the ground, and packs that in and tamps it.

He does not stop there. He puts two turns of wire round it, one a hand's width down from the top and one a hand's width up off the ground, and twists them tight with the bar through the loops, and chops the ends off, and packs the tamped earth round the foot of it again.

The old wire stays on. It is rusted into the wood and it is under the new wire and it does nothing and he leaves it.

---

He tries it.

He puts both hands on the post at knee height and pushes and the post does not move and it does not move at the top either. He stands in the gate and hangs his weight on the gate and the gate comes against the latch and the latch goes over and does not catch, because the latch plate is on the gate and the staple is on the frame and the frame is a frame he welded badly in 2019 and the two are not in the right place for each other.

"I told you it was never the latch."

"You told me the latch would go home."

"The latch will go home when there is a new staple on the frame, and the staple is what I am not doing today." The man picks up his spade. "And I am not doing it because the staple wants a plate welded on that side and the frame is galvanised and cold, and you will do it in April with the door open and the frame warm. Then it will be a good job and it will last. Today it would last until the first cold morning and then it would be worse than it is now."

So the latch stays hooked back over the gate.

---

He looks at the post again in the last hour of the light, because it is his own post and he has just paid a morning for it.

The bottom of it is solid. He can put a heel against it and lean on it and it is a wall. The top of it is solid, because the gate is hung off the top of it, and the two new turns of wire are tight enough that he can get a finger under them and not a pencil.

The middle moves.

It does not move much. It is not the half inch the whole post used to give at hip height. It is the width of a thumbnail, and it is at the height of his hip, which is neither the black part nor the sound part but the grey part in between, and the hole he filled was full of the ground that has been in and out of it, and no matter how well it was rammed the middle of it is not the same ground as the bottom of it.

A post with wire round it at the top and at the bottom is a post that cannot come up and cannot go down. It can lean, in the middle, in the one place nothing is touching it, and nothing will tell him it is doing it except his hands.

He stands in his own gate with both hands on the post at the height of his hip and pushes, and it goes about the width of a thumbnail, and the wood there is neither black nor sound, because that was the middle of the hole and the middle of the hole never dried at all. He takes his hands off and stands in the yard in the dark with his palms feeling the grit off the ground.
