# Chapter 0775 — The New Coal Hole Lid

Sunday the twenty-seventh of November 2022, in this borough. She is in her own house at number nine from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is a coal hole lid made new and fitted true, and the air gone out of that side of the house.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FIFTIES HAS A COAL HOLE LID WARPED AND STANDING OPEN AT ONE CORNER AND MEANS TO PLANE IT FLAT, THE WOMAN WHO DOES HER SHOPPING MAKES A NEW LID OUT OF A LENGTH OF BOARD AND SCREWS IT DOWN, AND IT SHUTS, AND THAT HOLE WAS THE ONLY THING LETTING AIR THROUGH THAT SIDE OF THE HOUSE.**

She is a woman in her late fifties at number nine on Sunday. The woman who does her shopping comes to the front door with the bags and takes them in.

---

The fire is unlit and the back door is shut true with its card wedged in at the hinge. The pads are on the fridge with the first at the bottom and the front room stays shut. Down the front path the gate stands open across the flags behind two wedges.

The coal hole is in the outside wall of the kitchen, opposite the back door. It is a hole with a ledge at the top of it and a board on the ledge, and the board has warped.

One end of it has lifted off the ledge and stands up about the thickness of a coin, and the rain off the kitchen roof comes down the face of the wall and under the high end and stands in the hole. She has been putting a bowl out under it when it rains hard.

The lid is not a lid. It is a length of board the width of the hole and a bit over, and it has been on and off that ledge so often that the middle of it has a dish in it where her hands go, and the two ends have gone up, and it rocks.

She has a block plane and a chisel, and she means to take the dish out of the middle of the top and bring the two ends down to match it, so it goes down flat on the ledge and stops standing open.

---

The woman who does her shopping is on the front step at the middle of the morning.

---

"You are not going to plane that."

"I am going to plane that."

"You are going to plane a board with two ends up and a dip in the middle, and when you get the dip out of the middle the two ends will be worse, because the dip is what is keeping them up." She stops on the step. "Take your bags in and get your sleeves up."

---

She says nothing more about it until the lid is off the ledge and they are both looking at it. The underside is grey with the green of a winter of weather, and where it has stood open at that end there is a black line on the ledge and on the wall below it, and the ledge has gone soft under one corner.

"That has been open since the summer. That corner is soft. You can put your thumb in it."

"I can put my thumb in it."

"You will not get a lid to sit on that." She turns the board about against the light. "And that is not soft only from the rain coming in under it. The board itself is soft under there, and there is not enough of it to plane a lid any thinner than it already is."

"Then it will want a new one."

"Then it will want a new one, and there is a lid on it that shuts."

---

She measures the ledge off a paper twice and takes the board off the pile standing against the yard wall behind the coal hole. It is a length of stuff about a yard and a half long, four inches thick at one end and a bit under that at the other. It came off a step, and its bed is dressed and flat where it has been the top of one.

"That will not go on there."

"That will go on there with four inches standing proud of the front of it, which is what you want, because then it is a ledge and not a lid." She runs her thumb along the dressed face. "And that one is not warped. It has been lying on its edge against a wall since the summer, and a board does not warp on its edge."

"How do you know it is not warped."

"Because it came off the front of a step with a chisel, and a step does not warp. And you can tell by the face. Look at yours. It has got a dip in it and the two ends have gone up to make it. Take the wood off either side of that dip until it is thin enough not to matter and you have a lid with no strength in it, and the first frost bends it back."

---

She cuts it on the sawhorse in the yard and gets it back round the side of the house with an armful, and they fit it. She marks the overhang off and takes the underside flat with a chisel, and thins the back of it with a drawknife until it is a straight slope and the bed of it comes down from a full thickness at the back to a hand's width at the front.

She gets it up on the ledge on her own, which is not easy, and she does not get it true by eye. She stands back, shifts it, looks again, moves it a finger's width to the left.

"There."

"What have you done."

"I have put the back of it on the ledge and left the front standing out over it, and made it slope so it does not lie on the ledge at all. It is on one high point at the back and the weather gets to the whole of the underside." She looks up at it. "And it will not shut down flat, and it is not meant to. It has a gap at the front the thickness of your nail. Do you want that closed."

"It is shut."

"It is shut and there is a hole along the front of it the thickness of a nail. That is what a lid is for. That is not a lid that is shut. That is a lid that has been closed up."

---

Then she screws it.

She has no screws in her bag, so the woman gets four out of the drawer under the worktop. Two go through the dressed face at the back corners into the sound ledge, and the board does not jump when she opens it and shuts it. The two at the front go through nothing at all, because there is no ledge under the front of the board.

"Will that not drop off in the wind."

"There is no room for it to drop." She shuts it and lifts it with one hand. "That is the last time you will have to do anything to that hole at all."

---

They look at it. It shuts on the ledge and it does not rock and it does not stand open. She goes right round it with her hand, along the joint at the back, down the two ends and out along the front of it, and there is nowhere along it she can put a thumbnail. The water off the kitchen roof goes across the front of the board and off the end of it into the yard, and none of it comes into the hole.

"It is better than the lid was."

"It is better than the lid was by about forty years." The woman is packing her saw away. "Take your bags in and shut the door behind you."

She goes, and the gate goes, and the front door shuts.

---

Then she goes round to the coal hole to shut it for the night, because she shuts it every night, and she puts her hand down flat on the board.

It is shut. She cannot get her hand under it anywhere. She runs her hand along the joint at the back and down the two ends and out along the front, and there is no draught coming off it anywhere and nothing at all coming out from under it.

She stands there in the dark with her palm on a shut lid, and works it out, and it takes a while and she gets it in the wrong order twice.

That hole is on the north side of the house with nothing to wind at it. The kitchen has a fire in it most days and a warm brick wall behind the range, and that wall is warmer than the outside of any wall of that house on a day in November. The hole has been open at one end since the summer and at neither end before that, and warm air goes out through a hole and cold air comes in through the same hole. The back door is shut true with a card in it and the front room has been shut since the summer. There is no other opening in that side of the house at all, and there has not been one since the summer.

The front rows of the load in that hole were dry and pale and rang when you knocked them. The fourth row down was warm and the fifth was wet, and her arm went in to the elbow and there was green on the inside of her forearm. That was in the autumn, and she has not had her arm in it since and is not going to now.

But the front rows were dry because of that hole. That was the only air going through that side of the house, and it went up through the middle of a load of wood standing on end and came back down the outside of it, and it is the whole of the reason the top of that pile was the colour it was.

She stands in the dark with her hand flat on a shut lid that she was given and cannot now undo, and there is not one draught coming through it anywhere, and the whole of the air has gone out of one side of her house.
