# Chapter 0872 — The Third Length Off The Rack

Tuesday the twenty-sixth of December 2023, at the rack in the yard at the lock-up from the time the dew has come off the steel until the shutter is down. The third length, and how short it is.

**THE RACK IN THE YARD AT THE LOCK-UP HAS TWO LENGTHS OF BAR IN IT AND A THIRD LYING AGAINST THE WALL BESIDE IT, AND SHE MEANS TO LAY THAT THIRD ONE DOWN BESIDE THE TWO AND WALK THE ENDS EVEN WITH THE TOE OF HER BOOT, AND THE WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK IS STANDING AT THE OFFICE STEPS ON THE FAR SIDE OF THE YARD.**

The rack is a length of angle iron on two uprights, made in this yard about nine years ago out of two offcuts, and it is the only thing in this yard that is put up on purpose to take one sort of object and nothing else. It is not a bin and it is not a pallet rack. A pallet rack takes anything of about that width and a bin takes whatever is put in it, and both of them end up carrying whatever arrives on a bad morning. This takes bar, and only bar, and it takes it standing up.

It takes bar. Six foot lengths, in two bays, standing on end in an angle with the legs of the angle against the wall of the bay so the bar leans back and cannot roll out of it.

Two lengths are in it. The third is against the wall, lying flat in the grit where it has been since the summer.

The two lengths in the rack are the same length as each other, and that is not luck.

They came out of the same bar. A six foot bar comes off a truck in lengths and the lengths are the lengths, and one man cutting them for a rack cuts them all on the same mark with the same set stop, so that every length in this yard that came off that delivery is the same length and can go into either bay.

Which means the two in the rack are not a measuring instrument because of any care taken with them. They are a measuring instrument by accident.

So she takes the third one off the wall and lays it down on the flags beside the front of the rack and puts the near ends of both against the toe of her boot, which is the only stop in this yard that is square to the wall and does not move while a boot is on it, and then she walks her hands along both bars to the far ends.

She does it twice with the third one and once with the other, because a boot is a stop and not a datum, and a boot turned round between one walk and the next will give a different answer by about the width of a sole.

The third one runs out a foot short.

Not an inch. A foot, and it is a foot the same way along the whole length, because a bar that is a foot short at one end is a foot short at both. And it is short by a foot and not by a foot and a bit, which she establishes by laying her own boot heel against the end of the short one and looking down the two of them, and a boot heel is a thing everybody knows the size of without having to be told.

So she puts it in the rack.

She has to turn it to get it in, because a bar goes into that rack with the legs of the angle to the wall and this one is lying with them the other way, and when it is in it stands there perfectly well, upright, in a bay of its own, with a foot of daylight between its top end and the wall of the bay.

A rack is not a place where a number is written down. There is nowhere in this yard that says two, and she has found no end that says three, and the man who made this thing nine years ago is not going to come back and be asked.

It is in the rack because a rack is a place where bar goes, and because there were three lengths and two bays and nobody wanted to be the one to say that the third did not fit, and it is off the rack now because the third upright of the front gate went in the summer and the man who took the bar out to work on it put it down against the wall when he had finished and has not been asked about it since.

That is not a mystery and nothing in this yard knows about anything else.

What she did not expect was where the wear is on it.

There is no wear on the top of this bar anywhere along its length, which means it has been in and out of that rack twice, at most, and both times the same way round.

There is wear on one end of it. On the end that goes to the back of the bay, on the two long edges, in a band about a hand's width deep, and it is not bright and it is not grooved. It is the dull rounded look of metal that has been gripped in a gloved hand and pulled on, a lot, in one place, and then put back.

That is the end she pulled on when the bar was in the rack and would not come.

A bar in that rack comes out forwards. You get both hands on the top end and you draw it towards you and it comes over the front of the angle and it is done. If a bar has gone in the other way round, with the angle of the section facing out instead of back, it cannot come out forwards at all, because the angle is in the way, and the only thing you can do is put your hand in behind it and pull.

Which is what somebody did, a number of times, before they worked out that the bar wanted turning over in the rack and not pulling at.

So the bar is off the rack because it went in the wrong way round on the first day it was put there, and because the right way round is not written anywhere, and because the end that pulls has a hand's width of wear on it that tells anybody who picks it up which way round to put it.

There is a thing about that wear which is worth stopping on, because it is the difference between a mark and a habit.

A thing that is gripped and pulled on hard, in one place, a few dozen times over a few years, goes round. It does not get thin, because iron does not wear thin at the rate a hand can pull on it, and it does not get bright, because there is nothing here bright enough to come up. It gets round. And the roundness is on both long edges of the end and not on the end face, which is the part that tells you it was the long edges that got the fingers and not the end face.

That is a hand's width of the end. Somebody had that bar in a fist at the right angle and could not do anything else with it, and the bar has been keeping that piece of information for about four years with nothing else in the yard knowing that it was being kept.

She takes it back out and puts it down against the wall in the grit where it was, with the worn end towards the back, because that is where it was and because that is the way round it wants to go back in.

The shutter of the near bay comes down about an hour after that, and goes down in its guides, and stays where it is.

The woman who books work has been standing at the office steps on the far side of the yard the whole of the afternoon and has not come across it, and the rack is against the wall on this side and has been in full view of anybody standing on those steps for four hours.

---

Nothing on that rack has ever been the same length as anything else on it, except the two that are in it, which are the same length as each other, and the third one lying against the wall is a foot shorter than both because it was cut for something else in the first place.
