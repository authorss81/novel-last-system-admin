# Chapter 0388 — The Room at the Top

Sunday the third of February 2018, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past nine at night, a block of flats, and a landing in it, in Tideglass.

**A MAN IN HIS FORTIES LAID ABOUT THIRTY SQUARE FOOT OF HARDBOARD AND A LENGTH OF SHEET VINYL ON THE FLOOR OF A ROOM AT THE TOP OF A LANDING IN A BLOCK HE DOES NOT LIVE IN ON THE MORNING OF THE THIRD OF FEBRUARY, AND HE HAS NO BUSINESS IN THAT BLOCK AND HE WAS NOT ASKED TO DO THAT FLOOR, AND HE GOT ON WITH IT AND FINISHED IT, AND A WOMAN WHO LIVES IN THAT BUILDING CAME UP THAT LANDING AT ABOUT TWENTY TO ONE FOR A PARCEL AND SAW HIM IN THERE, AND SHE SAID NOTHING TO HIM AND HAS SAID NOTHING TO ANYBODY.**

The block is six flats, built about sixty years ago out of one house, and it stands at the end of a short turning off a side street with a strip of grass in front of it about as deep as a car. There is a passage at the ground level with the meters in it and the way up is off the end of that passage, and it goes up in two flights to a landing with two flats on it and then on to the top of it, where there is one flat and one room.

The room is about nine foot by seven. It has a cold slab floor, a plaster ceiling with an old brown stain in the far corner of it, a high vent on the outside of that building, a radiator that has been drained and does not work, and a lamp on a flex in the middle of the ceiling. It is not a flat, it appears on no plan of that building, and there is no meter for it on the account for that block.

It had a man in it until about the middle of last year. He was seventy-nine and he went to live with his daughter in the country and he did not take the room with him and it stood empty and shut from the week he went.

The woman who lets that block is a woman who has let it for years and she asked the man in his forties about it in the last week of November, in a corridor, in about a minute, and he said he would keep it shut.

That is the whole of the arrangement and there is nothing in writing and there is no money in it and the rent is not charged and he is not on anything.

---

He has had a bench in that room since about the beginning of December.

It is a bench he made out of a piece of chipboard on two lengths of 2 by 1 and it is against the brick at the far end, and he has put a vice on it, and there are two drawers under it made out of a couple of offcuts of melamine carcass, and it is the only place in this borough where he has anything to himself that is dry, warm and flat.

He cannot have it at his own place. He lives in two rooms and the floor of the front one goes down about half an inch in the middle and there is a woman and a girl in the house and he is not having a bench in that.

That is the whole of why the room is used. Not a workshop, not a store, not a business. A bench.

And the man in his thirties has a use for it as well and a different one. He has a machine that will not go in his own front room and he has been cutting on that bench since about the middle of December, on and off, and neither of them has ever said the word about it to anybody, and neither of them pays anything.

The floor was the last thing on that room that was ever done, and it was done about 1961.

It is a bitumen tile floor in brown and buff, laid on a wood fillet, and there are about two inches of it over a solid floor, and in the corner under the radiator somebody's chair has been standing on it since the nineteen-sixties and the tiles under that chair are about a quarter of an inch lower than the tiles four foot away.

He had looked at it in December and had thought about it, and on the morning of the third of February he decided to do it, and he told the man in his thirties on the Friday night, in a message, about four words of it, and the man in his thirties said he would be there at nine.

---

They had it up by about ten past twelve.

He went round the skirting with a long thin bar and got a corner up and then it came up in sheets about three foot square, and the fillet came with it in places and did not in others, and they pulled the whole of it and got it out of there and carried it down that landing in armfuls and put it in the yard at the bottom, and it took about twenty minutes and made more mess than the job needed to.

The solid floor under it is a screed about an inch and a half thick and it is level to within about a quarter of an inch across the whole of it, which is better than he expected and better than he would have laid it.

He put down hardboard in four sheets, cut round the vent and notched round the soil pipe, and taped the joints on the underside, and worked from the far corner back towards the entrance so that he was never standing on a sheet he had just laid.

The man in his thirties held the far end of the second sheet and the third and got them down flat, and after that he went and sat on the landing with a cup and let the man in his forties do the rest.

The sheet vinyl is about twelve foot by eight foot and it is the kind that has to be cut round a room and not dropped in. He cut it on the concrete of that landing with a straight edge and a knife, took it in, got it into the corner, and worked it round the pipe and the vent and the corner where the floor dips.

It took longer than the hardboard and it is the only part of it he is pleased with. It has not lifted at the corner and there is no bubble anywhere and the seam has gone where the entrance is.

The man in his thirties came back in at about half past twelve and looked at it and said it was better than the old one had been, which it was, and the man in his forties said that was because the old one was older than either of them, and nothing else passed between the two of them about that floor.

She came up that landing at about twenty to one for a parcel.

She has the flat at the bottom of that top landing and a woman two floors down had left a parcel on the top step for her that morning, and she comes up that way four or five times a day and has been coming up it since she moved in, and on the third of February she came up at about twenty to one and that room was standing open.

There was a man in his forties on his knees in it at the far end with a knife in his hand and a piece of card in the other one, and a heap of old brown tile against the brick beside him, and most of the new floor laid and a roll of vinyl and a bag of offcuts.

The man in his thirties was standing at the near end of that room with his coat on and his hands in his pockets and a cup in one of them.

She was in the passage of that landing for about two seconds.

What she took in, in the order she took it in, was a man doing a floor, and a man standing there watching him, and a room that is not a flat being worked in. She did not take in anything about either of them, and she did not look at their faces, and there was nothing in her face for either of them to read because she had not gone in and neither of them was looking at her.

She went on to the top step and got the parcel and came back down past the room and it was still open and she went into her own flat and shut it behind her.

She has not said a word about it to him. She has not said a word about it to the man in his thirties either, whom she knows by sight from that landing and from the parade. She has not said a word to the woman who lets that block, and she has not said a word to the woman two floors down who left the parcel.

What she has done is notice. She has been up that landing dozens of times since the third of February, and that room has been standing open twice — once while the two of them were in it, and once after, when the man in his forties was sweeping the bits of it into a bag. And she has gone past both times.

And she has said nothing to anybody about it and does not mean to, and it is not that she is holding to a rule. It is that there is no form of words for putting it to him that is not a complaint, and a woman who goes up to a man on a landing and says what have you been doing in that room has said the sentence that puts that room in front of a person who has a say about it, and she does not want that room in front of anybody. She has lived in that block nine years and she likes it and she pays her rent and the man in his forties has never once been in her part of that building and she would rather that went on.

He is not going to be told.

He does not know that anybody was up that landing. He was on his knees in that room with his back to the passage and then he was in the passage with a bag of offcuts and she was in her own flat with the parcel.

He has been back in that block once since, on the Thursday of the week after, to do half a day's work in one of the flats, and he came in through that passage and went up and the woman who lives in that building was out and he was not spoken to differently by anybody, because nobody in that block knows anything about it.

He will do the other floor in that room in March if the weather holds. He has not said that to anybody and has not been asked, and it is not arranged, and there is no money in it, and the woman who lets that block would not know it had happened if it had not.

**SHE WENT PAST THAT ROOM ON THE THURSDAY OF THE WEEK AFTER AND IT WAS SHUT AND SHE WENT ON DOWN THAT LANDING, AND THE MAN IN HIS FORTIES CAME THROUGH THAT PASSAGE AT TEN O'CLOCK THAT MORNING FOR HALF A DAY'S WORK IN ONE OF THOSE FLATS AND WAS NOT SPOKEN TO DIFFERENTLY BY ANYBODY IN THAT BUILDING.**
