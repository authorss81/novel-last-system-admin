# Chapter 0839 — Marks Gone Pale

Friday the twenty-fifth of August 2023, at the near gate of the unit from morning until the yard empties, and chalk gone pale on a brace is the whole of that Friday.

**THERE IS A GATE BRACE AT THE UNIT WITH CHALK MARKS GONE PALE AND HE MEANS TO RE MARK THE MEASURE THE BRACE ALREADY CARRIES, AND THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN STANDS IN THE DOORWAY AND GOES BEFORE THE MARKS ARE READ A SECOND TIME.**

That brace is the diagonal across the near gate, oak, put in square in spring with the gate, and marked in chalk with the measure it was cut to, three marks for the three cuts.

Rain and sun have taken the marks since. The chalk sits in the grain as a pale ghost, enough to see where marks were and not enough to read.

He does not put a new measure on. A brace cut and fitted wants the marks it was cut to and nothing else, and a fresh measure on old wood is a second story that only confuses the next job.

Re-marking is done with chalk and a rule, mark for mark over the old, with the rule laid along the brace to find the ghosts.

He starts with white chalk, a rule, a brush, and a rag.

The brace faces into the yard, catching weather off the wall. He brushes the face clean first. Dust comes off it in a soft sheet, with spider silk under the lower edge where a web has been across from the brace to the post since June.

The web is under the brace and not on it, strung from the bottom edge to the post, with dust in it, and he leaves it. A web under a brace catches dust and keeps it off the chalk, and it was there before the marks went pale and will be there after.

The three ghosts show once the dust is off. One near the top for the shoulder, one in the middle for the length, one near the foot for the birdsmouth. Each sits where the rule sat in spring, with the chalk sunk into the grain around it.

He lays the rule along the brace and finds each one, chalking over the ghost a stroke at a time. The rule is the same one the cuts were marked with, kept in the store since, with nicks on the edge that fit the nicks on the brace.

The woman from two doors down stands in the doorway of the unit at midday with an empty bag, watches the rule go along the brace, and goes before he reads the marks a second time.

“Mind your wet paint,” he tells her, though there is no paint, and she laughs once and goes on out to the road with the bag.

She does not step into the yard past the doorway, and she does not look at the brace twice, and nothing passes between them about the gate.

He thinks about the ghosts while he chalks, how wood keeps what was put on it long after eyes give up reading. The marks went pale but never left, sitting in the grain through rain and sun, waiting for chalk to find them again. Work remembers longer than people, and re-marking is only listening to what the brace already knows.

The chalk dust coats his fingers white to the second joint, and the rule edge has warmed in his hand from handling, oak and steel at the same summer heat.

He reads the three marks again off the rule once she has gone, to be sure the re-mark sits where the old sat. Top, middle, foot, each over its ghost, each true to the nicks.

The chalk goes on soft and comes up bright against the oak, too bright for old wood. He rubs each mark once with the rag, lightly, so the bright takes dust off his fingers and settles back to the pale the brace had in spring, readable and not new-looking.

He brushes the surplus dust off the face and off the rule, and chalks the date off the edge of the brace onto a scrap of wood for the store, not onto the gate.

---

The rule goes back in the store, and the chalk goes in the drawer with the brush, and the rag goes over the rail to air.

He steps back to the middle of the yard when it empties and looks at what is there. Chalk marks fresh on the gate brace where the pale ones were, readable at three paces, and under the brace the web, with dust in it, strung to the post.
