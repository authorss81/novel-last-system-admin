# Chapter 0195 — Peverell Street

Thursday the twenty-fourth of September 2015, from about ten in the morning, a street in Tideglass.

**NOTHING HAS HAPPENED TO ANYBODY ON THIS STREET SINCE FRIDAY AND THERE ARE MORE PEOPLE ON IT THAN THERE WERE.**

Peverell Street goes down from the top of the district to the yard line and it is a working street: a lock-up with a roll shutter, a place that sells mattresses, a launderette that opens at six, and a row of properties and then a yard at the bottom of it with a surface-water run down the side. It is not a district. It is not one of the eight on the drawing and it is not the square with nothing in it. It has never had a term and it is not going to be given one.

On the Friday morning a woman of about forty-six put a sheet of paper out into four places, and by that night it was on forty walls, and it has gone onto more of them since, and nobody has said how many and nobody is going to. There is a copy of it on the shutter of the lock-up at the top of this street, gummed along the top edge, with the top-left corner a little loose because the top corner of that shutter is where the roller goes.

Jonas Mercer came down at about ten because ten is when a man walks down a street.

There were about nine people on it and he counted them without meaning to, which is what he does, and nine is not a number that means anything in this borough.

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THIS STREET THIS MORNING, printed and fixed to a railing at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A SHEET OF PAPER ABOUT A SHAPE SAYS NOTHING ABOUT WHAT ANYBODY DID ON THE STREET IT IS ABOUT.**
> **A THING DONE ON A STREET BEFORE ANYBODY ASKED IS NOT WRONG.**
> **NOTHING ON THIS NOTICE IS HELD AGAINST ANY PERSON.**

A woman of about thirty-one with a barrow was at the bottom by the run, looking at a grate, with two children and a man of about fifty-three who had a white van. He did not look at the barrow and she did not look at him and the two of them had come out for the same reason, which is that a man had come round on Tuesday night with a flatbed and a roll of tape and had put a sheet of the drawing on the shutter, and the shutter goes up at seven, and people stand in front of shutters.

Nobody could say why the street was busier. A man in a hired jacket who was not working said he had been down twice this week to look and had not worked out what it was, and that the second time had been better than the first, and that was the whole of what he had come down for. A woman of about twenty-five who was going to work said she had heard it was the eight and had come to see whether this was one of the eight, and it is not a street, it is a district, and there is no district for a bit of one, and she said *right* and went to the bus.

That is what a week of a drawing does to a street. It does not help anybody. It tells people a thing they already had a version of, and they come and look, and they find out they were right, and they go home, and they come back, and there is a rule about that anywhere: a place that has been discussed is a place that has been dismissed.

At the bottom, by the run, the water was about six inches down in the channel where it always is, and the concrete at the low corner was dark and had a line on it about a foot above where the water was standing now.

Jonas Mercer stood at the top of the yard and looked at it for a while.

A doorway on the left of the yard had sandbags across it. Not a row of them laid flat. A row of them stood up on end against the frame of the door, two deep, the way a person stands them when they are going to be there a while, and it was a make you could buy in four shops in this city. There was a stopcock on the wall to the left of the door at about the height of a person's knee, and it had a new ferrule on it, and a stopcock key was in the hole, and it was on the side that stops the water going into the yard. The three pallets of block and the bags of aggregate that had been standing in that doorway since the summer were up on the wall side of the yard, on two trestles, and had been moved by somebody who had known which end of the yard to move them to.

Inside, the floor was dry. There was dust on it and a broom leaning in the corner and the place had the smell of a yard that is swept.

There was no water in it and there had been water in it, and somebody had put it right, and it was not obvious when. The inside of the doorway was not marked. The sandbags were stood against the frame and the last person out would have pulled the door to, and the door closes, and the bags are against the frame, and that is what a doorway looks like every day of the year.

Jonas Mercer did not go down the yard. He stood at the top of it and looked at about nine bags of sand and a new ferrule and a man about fifty yards away who was helping a woman of about thirty-one get a barrow up a kerb.

A man of about fifty-seven. He had a van with a pump on the back of it and a ladder and about four lengths of hose, and he was taking the weight of the barrow without being asked and without saying anything, which is a thing people do on this side of the city in the morning and which means nothing at all.

Jonas Mercer did not ask him. He has not asked anybody anything since the twenty-fifth of February and he was not going to start on a Thursday, and there is nobody in this city he could hand it to if he had.

**AND THE FINDING OF THE MORNING IS NOT ABOUT THE STREET AND IT IS NOT ABOUT THE DRAWING. IT IS THAT SOMEBODY USED THE DRAWING TO DO A CORRECT THING, AND DID IT BEFORE ANYBODY ASKED, AND IT CANNOT BE CHECKED BY ANYTHING, INCLUDING BY HIM, AND IT WAS RIGHT.**

He could not establish that the man of about fifty-seven had read the sheet on the shutter. He could not establish it and he was never going to be able to, and neither could anybody else, and the reason is not that anybody is hiding anything. The reason is that the sheet has no address on it. It shows eight shapes with a term and a name in each of them, and lines between them, and a square with nothing in it, and there is not one word on it about a street, a yard, a gate, a kerb or a run of surface water, and a person standing in front of it at seven in the morning is looking at a map of where things stop and not at a list of things to be done.

If that doorway had flooded on the Tuesday night, there would be a man in there now sweeping water out with a yard brush, and it would have been on the sheet at the front of the building by the time anybody came. If it had not flooded, there is nothing at all. A correct thing done at two in the morning by a man with his own pump and his own bags and his own reason, on a street nobody has a term for, on the strength of a drawing with no address on it, produces exactly one thing: a dry floor with dust on it and a broom in the corner, and there is no instrument in this city, on this street or in any building behind it, that could turn that back into an act.

And the going the other way is the same. If the man had put the bags in the wrong order, or the ferrule on the wrong side, and the yard had flooded and the block and the aggregate had gone, then what would exist would be a loss, and a loss in a yard is a thing that happens to people, and it is not a thing anybody could be shown happened because of a decision, because there was no decision. There was a man and a key and four hours.

Two people know any of it. One of them is standing at the top of a yard, and he is on nothing and he is not going to be asked, and the other one is at a kerb with a barrow. And a van with a pump on the back of it is not a record of anything, and a plate is not a record of anything, and nobody in this borough can ask anybody to produce either.

Jonas Mercer came back up the street at about eleven and the people had gone down to two. The corner at the top of the sheet on the shutter was lifting in the wind and dropping back against the roller, and he put it up with the heel of his hand where it had been, and it stayed for about a minute and then began to lift again.

A person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a drawing on a shutter is not a count. A yard at the bottom of a street has a man in it this morning because of a piece of paper with no address on it, and if it had flooded it would have a man in it sweeping out with a yard brush, and the paper would be on the shutter either way. Nine months of this borough have gone on learning that being put in a field and being asked are not the same thing. This morning a man did a correct thing because of a map, and nobody counted him, and nobody is going to, and the yard is dry.

A man of about fifty-seven drove down there in the night with his own bags and his own key and put a yard right, and Jonas Mercer stood at the top of it for a quarter of an hour and could not find out who, and put a corner of a sheet of paper back against a shutter, and there is nothing in this city that joins those two acts together or ever will.
