# Chapter 0356 — Four Feet of Stud

Thursday the twenty-eighth of September 2017, from about ten past eight in the morning until about half past five in the afternoon, a first-floor room at the top of a street, and an outside stair, in Tideglass.

**A MAN WENT INTO A ROOM HE HAS NO BUSINESS IN AND SPENT THE BEST PART OF A QUARTER OF AN HOUR LOOKING FOR SOMETHING WRONG WITH A PIECE OF WORK HE HAS HAD NOTHING TO DO WITH, AND HE COULD NOT FIND ANYTHING, AND HE TOLD NOBODY THAT HE HAD LOOKED.**

The room is the second floor of a house in a terrace that was cut into four about the summer before last, and it is reached by an outside stair at the back of the house with a steel string and a rail on the open side, and it is used as a place where four men keep things.

There is a bench along one wall with a vice on the end of it and a rack of tools over the top of it, and there is a window with the middle of it painted out, and there is a doorway on the far side that goes out to a landing with three more doors off it.

There was nothing in the corner by that doorway in April. There are two lengths of board on brackets on that wall now, six foot above the floor, which the man in his thirties made in the second week of June out of two pieces of an old floor that came up in the house.

They are about nine inch wide and an inch and a half thick, and they are not new, and there is still a bit of the paper on the faces of them.

He cut them off a floor that had been up in another house about a mile off, and he planed the front edges of them because a board with a splinters on it is a board that catches a cloth, and he found the studs with a short length of wire and a battery and marked where they were with a pencil across the wall, and he put four pressed steel angle brackets up and screwed them into the studs with two inch screws, and at the back corner of the long one there is a piece of card folded in three packing the bracket out so that the board sits level on a wall that is not.

That is all of it and it took him about two hours in the evening of the second week of June and there is nobody in that house who was told about it and there is no book.

Nobody in that room has ever said one word about it. It is a shelf in a room where four men keep things and it is the sort of thing a room like that has, and the man who made it told nobody he had made it and nobody asked, and the two of the four who use that room most have walked under it about a thousand times since the June.

---

The man in his forties went up that outside stair at about ten past eight on the Thursday with his bag, because he had a job in that house that morning and the job was upstairs.

It was a length of pipe and a set of clips and about half an hour of work, and he had it done by about nine.

He came out of the room he had been in and turned round to go down and he stopped.

He stood under the bottom board for a while.

A man in this borough will look at another man's work. It is not a decision and it does not take any longer than the turn of the head, and it is the only thing most of them have instead of a way of saying a thing is good.

He looked at it for the best part of a quarter of an hour and he did not go down the stair.

He put his bag down on the bench.

What he was looking for was a fault, and there are about nine of them he knows how to find in a thing like that, and he went through them.

He looked at whether the boards themselves were straight. They are straight, because they came off a floor that was straight, and you can see the grain running the whole length of both of them with no kink in it.

He looked at the length of the screws. Two inch screws into a stud, and a bracket on a stud with two inch screws in it is a bracket that stays up. He got his thumb under the head of the near one and put his eye to it and the head of it is in a line with the stud he could feel with his knuckle across the room, and the one at the other end of the same bracket is in a line with a stud.

He looked at the kind of brackets. They are the pressed kind, not the slotted kind, and a pressed bracket that has been screwed into a stud is not a bracket that comes out, and he knows that because he has pulled enough of them out of nothing to know it.

He got a spirit level out of his bag and put it on the lower board and it read level end to end, and he picked it up and turned it round and put it on again and it read level end to end the other way, and he put it back in his bag.

He looked at whether the wall behind was hollow. He knocked it with the back of his knuckles in about six places along the length of both boards and it is solid in every one of them.

He looked at the packing. There is a piece of card folded in three at the back corner of the long board and it is the only part of that job that he would not have done himself, and it is why the board is level.

He looked at the front edges and they are eased and there is not a splinter on either of them, and you cannot ease two edges in an evening without a plane and about half an hour on the stone, and he has done it and he knows what it costs in time.

And at about ten past nine he came to the thing he could find.

The bracket at the far end of the long board is on its own, and the three other brackets have got two screws in each of them, and that one has got one.

He looked at that for a while.

It would come out of a plasterboard wall in about a year and a half, on the strength of the one screw, if the board were ever leaned on. On a stud it will hold a long time. The stud is there, he could feel it, and the board is a board and not a mirror.

He is not going to say that to anybody.

It is not that he wanted the man who made it to know. It is that a man who goes into a room where he has no standing and puts his bag down and tells the man who owns the shelf that there is one screw in it is a man who has decided that the fault in it is his to say, and a man with no standing in a room does not get to decide which faults get said. He has watched that happen twice and both times it cost somebody.

So he picked his bag up off the bench and went down the outside stair and did the rest of his day.

---

The man in his thirties came up that stair at about half past four the same afternoon with his bag and let himself in at the top with a key that four men have copies of.

He put his bag on the bench and stood in the middle of that room for a minute and looked about, the way a man does when he has come into a room and has not come in for anything in particular.

The two boards are on that wall. He looked at them for about a second the way you look at a thing in a room you are in, and he did not look under them, and there is nothing to see under them from where a man stands in the middle of a room.

He took a chisel out of the rack and put it in his bag and went down again.

He has not been told that anybody looked at that board, and nobody is going to tell him, and if he was told he would want to know what the man thought of it and would have to say something back, and there is no version of that in a room four men keep things in.

The man in his forties has not been back up that stair since the Thursday morning and has not mentioned it to anybody.

**THE MAN WHO MADE THOSE TWO BOARDS PUT A PLANE ON THE FRONT EDGES OF THEM ON A TUESDAY IN JUNE, AND A MAN WITH NO STANDING IN THAT ROOM FOUND THAT OUT AT TEN PAST NINE ON A THURSDAY IN SEPTEMBER AND WENT DOWN THE STAIR WITH HIS BAG AND SAID NOTHING TO ANYBODY ABOUT IT.**
