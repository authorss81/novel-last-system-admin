# Chapter 0690 — A Man With A Skip Asks Her About Her Own Gate

Tuesday the twenty-first of December 2021, in this borough. She is in her own house from first light until dark with a man at the gate in the middle of the morning, and the whole of the day is a step ladder in a room she looks at from a doorway and a truth she gives away about her own gate for nothing.

**A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-EIGHT HAS A STEP LADDER STANDING SQUARE AGAINST THE WALL UNDER THE WINDOW OF A FRONT ROOM SHE HAS NOT SAT IN SINCE THE SIXTEENTH OF JULY AND HAS LOOKED AT IT FROM THE DOORWAY EVERY DAY SINCE FRIDAY, TELLS A MAN WITH A SKIP IN ONE SENTENCE THAT HER GATE HAS STOOD OPEN SINCE THE FIRST OF SEPTEMBER, HE DOES NOT ASK HER WHY, HE DOES NOT GO DOWN THE PATH, AND SHE STANDS IN THE DOORWAY WITH THE WIRE BRUSH AT HER FEET AND NEITHER MOVES.**

She is a woman of seventy-eight. She is on her own all day. One man speaks to her at her own gate.

---

It has been since Friday.

That is how she has it, and she has not written it anywhere and there is nothing in the house that would let her, and she keeps it by the Sundays on a newspaper she gets and does not read, and by the position of the box on the table, and by the fact that the dark has come at about the same minute every day and it came earlier on Friday.

The step ladder is against that wall under the window.

It has not moved. It is a folding one and it was put down folded and it stays folded, and the tray end is up, and the strip of dark tape round the second rail is at the height of her hip, and the wall behind it has a crack in the plaster that goes up from the skirting and stops about a foot above the tray end of the ladder, and she has looked at the crack and the tape in the same line every morning since Friday.

She looks at it from the doorway because that is where you look at a room from, and because the room has no other light in the day and the passage lamp behind her puts a strip of it on the boards about the width of the door.

---

The thing about that room and the ladder in it is that it has made the room bigger.

Not warmer. Not a room anybody would want. Bigger.

Before Friday, standing in that doorway, she had a pale patch on the ceiling just inside the door and a rush-seated chair against the wall by the window and a sideboard with nothing on it, and that was all, and she had got quite good at it, and she had got so good at it that she could stand in that doorway in the dark and know exactly how far across the room she could put her hand out and still not touch anything.

There is now something in the far corner.

It is four foot of aluminium standing square on folded feet, and it takes up about a foot and a half of the width of that wall, and it means that the far corner of that room is no longer a corner, it is a place where there is a thing, and she cannot see the corner behind it any more, and that is a thing she has not got used to since Friday and is not going to get used to.

---

The coal has to come out of that shed.

The shed door has been shut since the twenty-second of November and she shut it herself that day for the first time since before Christmas and did not put the padlock on it, and the padlock is hanging on the hasp where it hangs, and the key is on a hook behind her own back door where it has been since before Christmas.

It is the only building on that road with a lock on it.

She goes out through the front door and round the side and puts her hand on it, and the door is stiff at the top corner the way it was in the summer, and it comes open about four inches and then the rest of the way, and the cold in it is the outside cold and it goes over her face.

There is most of a sack of coal in there against the back wall and a clean dark rectangle in the dust on top of the heap where something sat in October that is not there now, and a shovel.

She gets half a sack out into a bucket and shuts the door behind her, which is the second time since before Christmas, and then stands in her own yard with the bucket and does not go and shut the door properly on the latch because there is no latch on it.

---

The man with the skip comes up the path in the middle of the morning.

She hears the lorry come up the road and stop about four houses down and then she hears it reverse and then she hears him come up on foot, and there is a knock on the gate frame rather than the door, which is a thing a man does when he can see from where he is standing that there is a woman in a kitchen window.

She goes out.

---

"Can I ask you something?"

"You can ask me something."

"I'm putting a skip on next door and I can't get the lorry up past you, and I can get it past you, I just need to know whether that gate shuts."

"Which gate."

"That one." He points at the one standing back against the wall of the coal shed. "That one's off its hinges and stood back off the wall and the road's about nine foot at that pinch. I can get a skip up there on a low loader if the gate's out of the way and I can get it up there with it shut if it's on the latch, and either way I can get it up there, but I need to know which one I'm doing so I'm not doing it twice."

"What difference does it make?"

"It makes the difference between doing it once and doing it twice, and I'd rather know before I get the lorry up there."

---

"Right," she says.

"Which of the two is it?"

"That."

"Always open?"

"Since the first of September."

"You'd want to shut it, that."

"I know."

"You've a padlock on your shed."

"You've been looking at my shed."

"I have," he says. "It's the first thing you see when you come up that path. There's a lock on it and it's hanging open in the hasp and the hasp's down, and that's not a lock, that's a thing that looks like a lock."

"Yes."

"You ever lock it?"

"No."

"Right." He looks at the lorry and at the road and does not say one further word about the gate or the shed or the woman, and she stands in her own gateway and waits for the rest of it the way you wait, and there is no rest of it.

---

And he is not a man who is being careful with her. That is the thing she takes away from it. He is a man on a job with two versions of a job in his head and she told him which one, and he took the answer and went back to the lorry, and he never once looked at her house except at the gate and the shed and the nine foot of road.

He has been up her path, looked at her gate, her shed and the width of her road, and taken a piece of information out of her that nobody has ever taken out of her, which is that there is a woman in this house who has not shut a gate since the first of September and has not locked a shed that has a lock on it, and he will be back up this road with a low loader and he will put a skip on next door and he will not think about this gateway again in his life.

And she cannot take it back.

She stands in the gateway after the lorry has gone and does the thing she has been doing since the first of September, which is to look at that road.

There is nothing on the road. There has not been anything on that road since the summer except a dog in the evening, a man with a lorry, a lad in a hood who did not knock, and a woman with a bright voice and a pole with a camera on it.

And now a man with a skip.

She says out loud, in her own gateway, to nobody: "Once or twice. That's all it is. Once or twice."

Then she goes in.

---

She puts the bucket on the sack by the back door and brings it in and puts it by the range, and the hotplate comes up warm, which is all that hotplate does, and it does not get hot enough to boil a pan and she does not expect it to. The element is a new one and it went in in the summer for a price she was given and did not argue about, and the old one dropped out in the middle of cooking in a pan and did not get hotter and went off.

She empties the bowl under the bath tap with the folded cloth in it and wrings the cloth out into the bowl and puts it back under the drip, and the tap has not stopped since before Christmas and she has not expected it to.

The mark on the bathroom ceiling has a hard edge and the middle of it is darker than the outside, and she puts her thumb to the edge about a quarter of an inch in and it comes away with grit on it, and she wipes the grit on the cloth and the grit goes in the weave.

She does not look at the ceiling this time either.

---

The gas envelope is face up square with the edge of the table, pencil side up, six things on the back in pencil, nothing crossed off, and no pencil in that house and there has not been since before Christmas.

The second thing on the back of that envelope is the front room.

It has been second since March. It is not a painting job and one line on the back of that envelope cannot explain it and it is not on there for want of a paint tin, and there is a paint tin in the cupboard under the stairs that she has not had the top off.

There is now a step ladder in that room.

---

She goes and stands in the doorway of the front room in the middle of the afternoon and looks at it, the way she has most afternoons this week.

Here is what she is thinking about and it is not about the ladder, and that is what makes it hard, and it is this:

A woman of about thirty stood in that doorway on Friday with her coat on and her sleeves pushed up and did not go in either, and she put the ladder down and squared it, and she said *I'll not talk you into anything*, and she did not have a name, and the woman of seventy-eight could not ask her one.

And a man with a skip has just been up her path and taken a piece of her out of her house in one sentence, and she gave it him, and she would give it him again, and she has never given that to anybody.

---

The brush is in the middle of the passage runner with the handle across the walking line and it has been there since the first of September and it has not been washed and it is not a wire brush off a shelf, it is a stiff one with a wooden handle worn pale at the end, and she put it there on purpose, on the first of September, in the dark, late, on her own, because a thing in the middle of a passage is a thing that gets stepped over and a person who has stepped over a thing has not asked about it.

It is at her feet now.

The front room door is open and the doorway is the width of the door and she is standing in it, and if she was to go into that room and pick the ladder up she would have to step over that brush to get into the room, and she would have to carry a folding step ladder back out over it, and she would have to have somewhere to put it.

The two of them, a wire brush and a step ladder, are in adjacent rooms in this house and have been since Friday and neither of them is going anywhere, and if she were a different woman she would take the brush in there and scrub the grate and the surround and do a thing to that room, and she is not a different woman.

She stands there for a bit with her hand on the door frame.

Then she goes and closes the front room door, which she does not have to do, and stands in the passage in the light of a lamp that has been on since June, and steps over the brush to get to the kitchen.

---

**A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-EIGHT HAS GIVEN A STRANGER THE FACT THAT HER GATE HAS STOOD OPEN SINCE THE FIRST OF SEPTEMBER AND THAT HER SHED HAS A PADLOCK ON IT THAT SHE HAS NEVER USED, HAS OPENED HER OWN SHUT COAL SHED DOOR AND SHUT IT AGAIN AND HAS STILL NOT PUT THE PADLOCK ON, HAS STOOD IN THE DOORWAY OF A ROOM WITH A STEP LADDER IN IT WITH A WIRE BRUSH AT HER FEET AND HAS DONE NEITHER THING, AND THE SECOND THING ON THE BACK OF THE GAS ENVELOPE IS STILL THE FRONT ROOM AND NOTHING HAS BEEN CROSSED OFF.**
