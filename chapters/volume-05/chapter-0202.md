# Chapter 0202 — Nobody Asked Me

Tuesday the third of November 2015, from about half past three in the afternoon, a first-floor room over a shop in Tideglass.

**A MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-TWO CAME ACROSS THE ESTUARY ON MONDAY MORNING TO FIND OUT WHY HIS NAME WAS ON A PIECE OF PAPER IN A CITY HE HAD LEFT IN APRIL, AND NOBODY BROUGHT HIM AND NOBODY ASKED HIM TO COME, AND HE CAME, AND ON TUESDAY AFTERNOON HE SAID THE SENTENCE HIMSELF.**

Irene Sandiford was in the room again with a plastic wallet. She had come down on the earlier train and she was going back on the six, and she put the second sheet of the schedule on the table, which is the sheet that gets filled in when the fourteen days run out, and there were seven rows on it, and six of them had dates in them, and the seventh had Ashgrove's name on it in her own hand because she had written it on a train.

There were about six people in the room. Nobody had come to meet anybody. Two of them were the same two who had been in it on the thirtieth of September and one of those was Jonas Mercer, who had not been asked to come and could not have been told not to.

The man of about sixty-two was standing by the window with his coat still on.

"I got a letter on Friday," he said. "From a woman in Alderwick. It said my details had been passed to a city I used to live in, and it said if I wanted to object I should write to her, and it gave her a reference, and I have not written, and I came over on Monday on the early boat and I have been sitting in a café since Monday morning because I did not know what to do with it."

"You could have written," said Sandiford.

"I could have. I have not written, and I am not going to write, because if I write I am in it, and if I am in it then somebody over there is in it, and I have been in it since the eleventh of August without anybody asking me and I would rather just be in it quietly."

"Would you," said Sandiford, and wrote nothing down, and the room did not laugh and she had not said it to be funny.

She read the seventh row out because he asked her to, twice, and it is what it says on the last Friday of the month: Ewen Ashgrove, about sixty-two, at Alderwick, contract renewed, fourteen days, present since 11 August, no absence, no claim, no contact with the sending city.

Then somebody at the back of the room — a woman of about thirty-eight who had come with a folder and had not said anything up to that point — said the true thing.

"A permission finds people," she said, and then stopped.

The room waited. She was about thirty-eight and she had come a long way to say a thing and she had got to the front of it and found that the front of it is where it stops working, because a permission that finds people finds them the same way the August document finds them, and the August document in this city is a field with four things in it, and the only difference between the two is the handwriting and the fact that one of them was signed twice.

She said, "It's the same as the other one. It's just better printed."

And then she sat down.

Nobody answered her. And the reason nobody answered her is not that she was wrong, and it is not politeness, and it is certainly not that anybody agreed with Jonas Mercer, who has not asked a question since the twenty-fifth of February and did not ask one then. It is that a person in a room saying *that is how it finds people* is heard as an objection, and she was not objecting, and there was no version of the sentence that was going to come out as description, and about four people in that room thought the same sentence at the same moment and about four of them had worked out before now that this city has no instrument for saying a true thing without it also sounding like a complaint.

**AND HE SAID IT BETTER HIMSELF, AND HE SAID IT TWICE, AND BOTH TIMES IT WAS TRUE, AND NOBODY OBJECTED TO ANY PART OF IT.**

"Nobody asked me to be found," said Ewen Ashgrove.

He said it to the table and not to anybody in it, the way a person says a thing they have had ready since Friday and have been carrying across an estuary on a Monday morning in a coat.

"That is the whole of it. There is no part of this anybody asked me for. I did not write. I did not ring. I have not sent anything to anybody in this city since April and I was not going to, and I have not, and on Wednesday the thirtieth of September I was working on a gully in a city where I have never lived and a woman on a different side of the water wrote my name in a line on a piece of A4, and that is all it took, and I am not being dramatic about it, I am telling you what happened because you have all got a piece of paper in front of you and I think somebody ought to have said it in the room."

Nobody said anything.

"And I want to say the other half of it," he said, "because I have thought about it since Friday and it is not what anybody in this room is going to want to hear. It has not cost me anything. That is the part I did not expect. Nobody has come and found me. Nobody has sent anybody. I have got a room and a contract and the money comes in an envelope before the work and I have eaten every day and I have been dry since August and I would not go back to the room in Kilburn Street on a Tuesday, and if all of this stopped today I would still be exactly where I am, and I would be exactly where I am, because I was already exactly where I am."

"Somebody sent your details over," said Sandiford.

"Your clerk sent a row," he said. "That is what a clerk does. I have worked for a man who did that with a list in 2011 and it did not mean anybody was thinking about me then either."

"And you cannot ask to be taken off it," said Sandiford, and it was not a question.

"No," he said. "That is the part I cannot get round. I could object in writing to your schedule, and the objection would be correct, and you would be obliged to note it, and the note would say that a person asked, and I would be a man who asked, and I have not asked anybody anything since I was twenty-two, and I am not going to start in order to be tidy."

He turned round then and looked at Jonas Mercer, who was standing about eight feet away with his hands in his pockets and had said nothing for an hour.

"You did that," he said. "The Friday in September. On the road with the hole in it."

"That is right."

"I read about it. I am not asking you about it." He said it flatly and it was not a deferential and it was not an accusation, and it was a man of about sixty-two telling a man of about thirty-two that he knew something and was not going to spend it, and then he turned back to the table.

"What I have got instead," he said, "is a doctor over there that I have never seen, in a town I cannot name, and if I am ill I go to it and it is paid for from the other side of the water by a council that has never heard of me, and I have already looked at the letter, and I have got a card, and it is correct, and I cannot say no to it, and I would like to say no to it."

"You could write and ask," said the woman of about thirty-eight.

"I could," he said, "and that is the answer, isn't it. Everything in this thing can be got out of by writing. And I cannot write. That is the whole of what is wrong with me and it is not a thing your document has done."

Nobody objected to any part of it. There were six people in that room and not one of them said *but he is safe*, and not one of them said *but that is what the clause is for*, and one of them — a man of about twenty-eight who had been in the room on the thirtieth — wrote nothing, and a woman of about fifty-two wrote nothing either, and there is no record anywhere in this city that a man of about sixty-two said on the afternoon of the third of November that nobody had asked him to be found, and nobody in this city is going to write it down and nobody is going to be asked to.

Jonas Mercer said one thing and it was a question about nothing and it was the only thing he said in that room.

"You'll go back on the boat."

"I go back at six. Sandiford's on the six." He picked his coat up off the back of a chair. "I'm working Thursday to Sunday over there, on a pumping station, and I'm on the Monday after that, and I have said yes to all of it and I have not been asked once, and I would do it again, and I am telling you both of those things in the same breath because I have found out this week that you can only say one of them out loud and the other one goes in your pocket."

"He is on a schedule in two cities," said the man of about twenty-eight. "He is the most counted man in this building."

"He has never been asked one thing," said Irene Sandiford.

He went out at about ten to four to catch the half-past five boat. Sandiford went at half past five on the train, and the room emptied the way rooms empty, and the second sheet went back into a plastic wallet and across the water, and the row with his name on it went with it, and there is no mark against it anywhere.
