# Chapter 0473 — The Ten Seconds at the Corner

Monday the eighteenth of February 2019, from about twenty to ten in the morning until about half past ten. One hundred yard of a street and two doorways, in Tideglass.

**SHE SAID NOTHING TO HIM FOR TWO YEARS AND THEN IN JANUARY SHE HELD A CORNER WITH HIM FOR TEN SECONDS WITHOUT SPEAKING, AND HE HAS DECIDED WHAT THAT WAS AND CANNOT ASK HER, AND SHE IS NOT GOING TO LEARN WHETHER HE GOT IT RIGHT, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS SAID A WORD SINCE.**

He is in his thirties and he goes out at about ten past nine and she goes out at about ten past, and for about two years their paths have crossed at the corner of that street and the next one four times a week and the two of them have never said one word.

There is no reason on earth that they ever would. He goes to a place off that street. She has a bus. Neither of them has set foot in the other's doorway. Neither of them knows where the other one works. Neither of them has ever put a question to the other one about anything at all.

What there is, is a corner.

What he knows about her is a set of things that are about four hundred times a year and that add up to about nothing at all.

He knows she is out on a Wednesday at about ten past because he is in his own door on a Wednesday at ten past and he hears her door and then he hears her go down the path.

He knows she carries a bag with a strap on it and that she puts the strap over the same shoulder every time.

He knows that when it is raining she goes to the corner and waits under the edge of the awning of the shop, and that she does not go in, and that when it stops she comes out and goes on.

He knows all of that and he has never once thought about any of it, and he has never once known that he knew it.

---

On the Thursday of the fourth of January, at about ten past ten in the morning, she stopped.

That is the whole of what she did. She was at the kerb where she always is, at the point where the two pavements meet, and he came round the corner about ten yards off with his hands in his pockets and his head down about a foot further forward than usual.

And she did not go.

She had been going for two years. She has gone across that corner four times a week since she moved in, and she goes at about ten past, and she does not wait for anybody and she does not look to see whether anybody is coming.

And on the fourth of January she stood at that corner and she did not go, and she waited.

He came across. He came up onto the kerb about four inches in front of her and stopped, and he was about a foot too close and too far round for either of them, and neither of them said anything, and about ten seconds went by.

Then he said, "Sorry," and went on, and she said, "That's all right," and went on.

Four words apiece, about ten seconds, and nothing at all has passed between those two people in two years except that.

What happened in the ten seconds is the thing.

He has been five weeks on it and he has got a great deal further than she would expect and no further at all, and what he has is this.

He knows what a man does when he is being polite to a woman on a corner, and he has done it, and he knows exactly what his own face does. It is not what his face did. On the fourth of January he came up to that kerb and stopped and looked straight at her and waited, and there was nothing in it of any kind that he can now go back and find.

And he has run that morning about thirty times and it comes out the same way every time, and the part he cannot get past is the second four or five seconds, which were not politeness.

He stood there. He could have gone. The pavement is four foot wide and he could have gone round her in a stride and said sorry and carried on and it would have taken him about half of what it actually took.

So he has worked out what it meant, and here it is, in the shape he has it in.

The only meaning that fits is that she wanted to be on that corner before him.

He has been at that kerb about four hundred times a year for two years and she has been at it about four hundred times a year for two years and he has never once arrived first and he has never once thought about it until the fourth of January. And on the fourth of January he arrived at a corner that she was standing on, and she waited for him, and that is the only thing there is to have read in it.

He is fairly sure of it. He has been over the grounds and he has not been able to make a hole in them.

---

What he cannot do is ask.

Not because of her. He has got as far as being able to say the sentence to a man in a van on a Wednesday and have it come out the way he means it, and if it were only the asking he would have done it.

What stops him is that there are three things it could be, and he has settled on one, and he is aware that he has settled on it, and the other two are still standing there.

The first is that she wanted to be on the corner before him. That is the one he has.

The second is that she did not see him at all until he was about four inches in front of her, and that the ten seconds were the same ten seconds anybody takes, and that there was nothing in it.

The third is that she was waiting for somebody else and he happened to be the one who came round the corner.

He knows the weight of those three. He has got the first one and he cannot weigh it against the other two without asking her, and the whole of asking her is putting the first one in front of her, and if he puts the first one in front of her and it is the second one, he will have told a woman he has never spoken to before that he stood at a corner for five weeks working out what it meant when she was on her way to a bus.

So he will not ask. He has decided that he will not, and he has not gone near it since about the twentieth of January, and it is not going to come up again, because the only way it could come up again is if she did it a second time, and he has worked out that he does not want her to do it a second time either, and that is the part of this he is least able to defend and has stopped defending.

What she did on the fourth of January, and what she has been doing since, and what she is not going to find out.

She stopped because she saw him coming about ten yards off.

And there is no reason. She has been through it. It was a Wednesday morning in the first week of January and she was at that kerb at about ten past doing what she does, and she saw him come round that corner, and something in her stopped her, and she has not been able to name the something since and she has had five weeks.

What she has decided is that she is not going to find out what he made of it.

If he did not notice, then she has a thing she has carried for five weeks for nothing, and she can put it down, and she can go on crossing that corner four times a week for the rest of however long she is there.

If he did notice, and worked out what it was, and came back — then there is a thing she would have to do about it, and there is nothing on her side of it. There is a corner and a bus and a bag with a strap on it and five weeks of not mentioning it. There is nothing there that a man could be answered with.

And so she is not going to find out, and the price of that is that she has to go on being a woman who stopped, and she has to do it every Wednesday at about ten past for as long as it goes on.

**ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF FEBRUARY SHE GOT THERE AT TEN PAST TEN AND HE CAME ROUND THAT CORNER AT TEN PAST AND WALKED PAST HER CLOSE ENOUGH FOR HER TO SEE THE CROWN OF HER HEAD, AND NEITHER OF THEM SPOKE, AND THAT WAS THE NINETY-FIRST TIME SINCE THE FOURTH OF JANUARY.**
