# Chapter 0529 — The Word He Stopped Saying

The Wednesday of the thirteenth of November 2019, from about half past four in the afternoon until about ten at night, in the rain, in a row of small shops in this borough.

**THEY HAVE BEEN IN THOSE TWO ROOMS ON A WEDNESDAY SINCE 2005, WHICH IS FOURTEEN YEARS, AND SHE HAS ARRIVED AT HALF PAST FOUR EVERY ONE OF THOSE WEDNESDAYS, AND HE HAS STOOD AT THE ENTRANCE AND SAID YES, AND HE DID NOT SAY IT ON THE THIRTIETH OF OCTOBER AND HAS NOT SAID IT SINCE.**

The two rooms are at the end of a row and you reach them through an archway that is open when they are open and shut when they are shut, and from the street there is nothing about the place that says anybody is in there, which is one of the reasons for it.

He is a man of about sixty-eight and she is a woman of about seventy-one. They are not related. They were not friends before 2005 and they have not been friends since, and the two of them have never once in fourteen years said a word to each other about what the arrangement is, and there is no arrangement.

What a Wednesday is, and has been since about 2016, is this. She comes at half past four. He is already in. He says one word at the entrance, which is yes, and she goes in. One of them goes to the bar and the other one does not, and it is always the same one, and neither of them has ever commented on it. She has a small glass of something and he has a small glass of something else and they sit at opposite ends of the long room, and about half an hour later one of them says something about the weather or the price of something or a person who has been in and gone out, and the other one answers, and then they sit again.

She leaves at about half past six. He walks her as far as the archway and not one step further, and he has not walked her further since about 2011, and she has never asked him to and has never said anything about the fact that he does not.

That is all of it, and it has been all of it for the best part of ten years. Before that there was more. In 2005 and 2006 there was a great deal more, and each of them has said, separately and never to the other, that a Wednesday is the whole of the reason given, and neither has ever said the other reason, which is the word at the entrance.

The word at the entrance is yes. He has said it every Wednesday since 2005, and it is not a big word and it does not mean come in, exactly. It is the sound a man makes at an entrance when the person he is waiting for has arrived and neither of them wants to make a thing of it.

---

He stopped on Wednesday the thirtieth of October 2019 and he said nothing about it and he has not said anything since.

Here is what that Wednesday was. She came at half past four as she has come at half past four since 2005, and he opened the archway and stood to one side and she went in, and he did not say anything. Not yes, not anything. She went in anyway, because she has gone into those two rooms on about seven hundred Wednesdays and her body does the archway without consulting her.

They had their drinks. She was on the side nearest the street that day and he was at the far end, and he did not come over, and she did not go over. There was a man and a woman in the middle who left at about twenty past five. At about half past six she got up.

At the archway, which is where he has walked her to every Wednesday since about 2011, she said, "Same time next Wednesday?"

She has never said that. In fourteen years she has never once said that, and the reason she said it on that Wednesday is that he had not said the word, and the question was the same question with a different mouth on it.

He said, "Right."

That is the last of it. He said right, and she went out through the archway and up the row in the rain to the bus, and she has not rung since, and he has not rung, and neither of them has been in a room with the other one since.

On Wednesday the sixth of November she came at half past four and she knocked on the brickwork beside the archway, because there had been a habit of knocking once a year, and nothing happened for about twenty minutes, and then she went home and she has not told anybody that she went home.

---

On Wednesday the thirteenth of November she came at half past four and she did not knock.

She stood at the end of the row, on the outside, in the rain, about nine feet back from the archway, and she stood there for the best part of an hour, and the rain was the kind that soaks a wool jacket in twenty minutes and gets into the shoes after that, and she had a wool jacket on and a holdall of hers that she has carried about in this town since 1974 and has never once put down in a place where anybody was going to be.

She did not knock. She had decided that on the bus on the way in and she has not changed her mind about it since, and she has not been able to tell anybody that she decided it, because there was nobody in the bus she could have said it to.

At about five past five a man who shuts those two rooms came out through the archway with his jacket on and a ring of other people's things in his hand, and he saw her, and he stopped.

"There's nobody in," he said.

She said nothing.

"There's nobody in," he said again, because she had not moved and he had not been sure the first one had gone anywhere.

"I know," she said.

He looked at her for a second longer than he needed to and then he went in and shut the archway behind him, and she heard him go along the inside of it, and then nothing came out of the place at all.

She stayed where she was until about a quarter to six. There is a covered bit outside a shop about thirty paces up the row and she has not been able to stand under it since about 2013, which is not a reason anybody else has ever had, and standing thirty paces up the row is a different thing from standing at the end of the row, and she worked that out while she was standing there.

At a quarter to six the rain eased off to nothing and she went home.

She lives about twenty minutes away by bus and she went it on foot, and she got in at about a quarter past six, and she put her wet jacket over the radiator and made something to eat and did not eat most of it, and she has not said a word about any part of this to anybody, and nobody has asked her, and the two facts are the same fact and she is the only person alive who knows both of them.

He lives about ten minutes away in the other direction and he was at home on both of those Wednesdays. There is nobody in this town who knows that and he has not said it.

What he did on the sixth was sit in his own front with his jacket on until about half past five and then get up and put the heating on, and what he did on the thirteenth was the same, and on both evenings that was the whole of what either of them did, and on the sixth he had not put the heating on at any time before half past five for about a week, and on the thirteenth he had not put it on at all until that hour, and he has not said why and he is not going to.

**HE GOT UP AND PUT THE HEATING ON AT ABOUT HALF PAST FIVE. SHE GOT HOME AT A QUARTER PAST SIX AND DID NOT GO AGAIN THAT NIGHT.**
