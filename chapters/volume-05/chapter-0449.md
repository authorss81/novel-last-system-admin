# Chapter 0449 — Number Nine

Monday the twenty-second of October 2018, called twenty past ten in the morning, and then again at four in the afternoon. Two houses, one street, in Tideglass.

**IT CAME TO A HOUSE WHERE NOTHING HAD COME FOR THREE WEEKS AND IT WAS FOR THE ONE NEXT DOOR, AND THE PEOPLE WHO OPENED IT KNEW THAT IT WAS FOR THE NEXT ONE, AND THE PERSON IT WAS FOR HAS NOT BEEN TOLD THAT IT ARRIVED, AND IT IS STILL IN THAT HALL.**

The man in his forties has the middle of that terrace and has had it about five years. The house on his right has been shut for three weeks, which is not unusual on that street, and the man who lives there came back into it on the Thursday before, in a car with a roof box on the top of it, and has been in and out of the place since with a set of keys on a fob.

There is nothing between those two houses but a gate and about nine foot of brick.

---

The knock came at about twenty past ten on the Monday and there were two of them.

The woman was about thirty-five and she had a carrier's box in the back of her car across the road with the lid up, and she was apologising before she had said what she was at, which told the man in his forties most of it before she got there.

She said: this is a house at number nine, and he said, this is nine, and she said, no, it's the one with the porch, it's next to this one, and the carrier read her writing and has brought it here.

He went out and looked at it in the road. It is a big cardboard box, taped round twice and taped across the top once, and it is about the size of a chest of drawers, and it has been carried down a street in the rain and it has not been opened.

He said, "Right."

He said, "I'll take it round."

She said, "No, it's all right, I'll come back for it," and he said, "I've a key and he hasn't, has he," and she said the man at nine has not been given one to anybody and she did not want to be knocking on a door at noon, and he said he would not either, and she said she would come back after work.

Then she said, "It's only going to be in the way in there," meaning the hall, and he said, "It is going to be in the way in here," and she said, "I'll be about six."

She left the box on his step. That is the whole of how it happened, and it took about four minutes, and neither of them shouted and neither of them was rude.


He brought it in at about half past ten and put it against the wall of that hall where the coats go, and he had to shift three coats to make room for it.

He did not open it. He wants to be plain about that, because it is the thing that would have been in everybody's mind. It is taped and it has somebody's name on the side of it in marker, and he did not open it and he has not opened it, and he has not read the name off the side of it except to see that it was a name.

He has moved it once. On the Sunday he wanted a coat off that hook, and he had to shift the box about a foot to get at it, and he put it back against the wall in about the same place, and that is the only time it has moved.

Here is the arrangement as it stands, and there is no arrangement.

He will not put a stranger's belongings on a stranger's step. If it is on that step it is a man standing on a doorstep in daylight with a box that is not his, and the man at nine will have to open his door and take it off him, and neither of them will ever be able to be in that street again in quite the same way.

And he will not carry it round. Not because the man at nine is anything. He has lived on that street five years and has not had a conversation with the man at number nine longer than a nod, and would not do it now if it were a box of biscuits.

And the woman will not put it back in her own car and take it round herself, because the carrier had already gone by the time he said anything, and because she is not going to be carrying a stranger's chest of drawers round that terrace on her own at six in the evening, and he can see that and so can she.

So it is against the wall of that hall.


She knocked at about ten past six. He told her again, quite civilly, that he would take it round in the morning, and she said she would rather not, and he said he did not want it in his hall, and she said he had said that once already and did not blame him, and he said he did not blame her either.

Then she said, "It's only a box," and he said, "It's only a box," and neither of them said the next thing, which is that the next thing is that it would be in his hall until one of them gave in and neither of them was going to.

She went at about ten past six.

---

He went down that street at about four the same afternoon to put a bottle back on a shelf at the end of it and number nine had a light on in the front room.

The man was in. She has not been able to establish that for certain, and neither has he, and the curtains on that house are the same curtains as every other house on that street.

He did not knock. He has not knocked on that door in five years and he has no reason to begin on the twenty-second of October, and a light showing through a curtain is not a reason, and he is sensible enough to know that it is not one.

The man at number nine has not been told that anything came for him. Nobody has knocked on that door with anything. On the seventeenth of November the box was still against the wall of that hall with three coats pushed up beside it, and the hall of that house is about five foot wide, and the man in his forties has started going down that hall sideways.

**THE MAN IN HIS FORTIES PICKED UP HIS OWN COAT OFF THAT HOOK EVERY MORNING AND PUT IT DOWN AGAIN BY THE BOX, AND HE HAS NEVER KNOCKED ON THE DOOR NEXT ALONG, AND THE LIGHT IN THAT FRONT ROOM CAME ON AT HALF PAST FOUR AND HE WALKED PAST IT.**
