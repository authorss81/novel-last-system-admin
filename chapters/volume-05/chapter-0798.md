# Chapter 0798 — The Gate On The Wall

Tuesday the seventh of March 2023, in this borough. Out in that yard at number twelve from the light coming up until she cannot stand in it, and a gate propped against that wall since September is the whole of what she is out there for.

**A WOMAN OF SEVENTY-EIGHT HAS A GATE PROPPED BACK AGAINST A WALL SINCE SEPTEMBER BECAUSE IT IS A GATE ON A WALL IT DOES NOT CLOSE AGAINST AND MEANS TO HAVE A CATCH PUT ON IT, HER SISTER WILL NOT, AND TAKES THE GATE OFF THE WALL, LETS IN A NEW HANGING STILE AT THE WALL END, HUNG UPON TWO NEW JAMB HINGES AND SETS A STAY IN THE GROUND, AND THE GATE IS A GATE AND SHUTS AND THE BOLT DROPS INTO ITS KEEP.**

First thing, when the light is barely on the flags, her sister knocks at the front door of that house, and the two of them go down the passage and out at the back, because the gate is propped against the wall and there is no other way past the outside of it.

---

The gate in that yard is on the wall.

It is not a gate in a gap. It is a gate hung on a pier at one side of the yard and standing against the wall on the other side when it is open, so that it lies flat out of the way and takes up nothing, which is what a gate on a wall is for. It has been standing like that since September and nobody has shut it since September.

The old woman means to have a catch put on it.

She has a bolt on the free stile of it. She has had that bolt since she came. There is a keep for it in the pier on the far side, and there has been a keep in that pier since before her time, and the bolt does not go into the keep and has not gone into the keep since the gate went up, because the gate does not shut far enough.

She cannot shut it. That is the whole of it. She has tried it with her hip. She has tried it with a bar off the wall. It comes across about a hand's width and stands there in the air and the bolt goes past the side of the keep and there is nothing on that pier for the bolt to find.

---

The outside lav is on the corner of that yard with its own little path and a door that shuts on a bolt that reaches about two inches past the keep. The dustbin stands against the wall past it with the lid up and nothing in it. There are three pots by the back step with the loam gone hard in two of them and nothing growing in the third. The meter box is on the wall beside the back door with its glass in and the dial where it was when she came. The coal shed is down the far side and has not been opened and is not going to be. The door of the front room is shut inside that house and they go down the passage past it and do not open it.

The gate stands against the wall with its top edge against the render and its bottom edge about a foot off the ground.

That is the second fault. It is not standing on the ground because the bottom of the hanging stile has gone soft, and the leaf has hung on those hinges for twenty years and drooped as it did so, and everything about it has come away from the wall at the bottom and come back to it at the top.

---

Her sister looks at it for about a minute and does not touch it.

"You are having a catch put on it."

"I am having a catch put on it."

"Somebody has to put it on, then."

"Somebody has to put it on."

"Not me." She says it the way she says everything, without any weight on it. "There is nothing on that pier for a catch to catch on. A catch catches. You cannot put a catch on to nothing, and if you screw a catch on to that gate you have put a catch on a gate and it will do exactly what it does now, which is sit in the air a hand's width from a keep."

"Then what else."

"Then it is the gate that has to come back, and not the catch."

---

They get it off the wall, and that takes the two of them and it takes longer than either of them expects.

The old woman takes the weight at the top corner where you can get a hand round it, and her sister works the bands off the stiles with a bar and a screwdriver, and then the leaf comes off its hooks and swings out into the yard with nothing on it and has to be caught, because it weighs what a door weighs and it is four feet by three and a half feet.

They lay it down on the flags, face up, with the hanging stile nearest them.

The hanging stile is the one that was against the wall. It is soft for about two feet at the bottom and hard as iron above that, and you can put your thumbnail into the soft part of it and it goes in the whole way, and the rail has pulled away from it along a joint that is open the width of a knife from the bottom up to about three feet.

The bolt is on the free stile and it is sound, and the keep in the pier is sound, and the gap between the end of the bolt and the side of the keep is a hand's width and about a tenth of an inch of daylight that they could conceivably close.

---

Her sister does not put a catch on and does not touch the bolt and the keep.

She cuts the hanging stile out.

She takes the soft two feet and a bit off the bottom of it and works the whole stile out of the head rail and the bottom rail with a bolster and a bar, because a stile that is soft at the bottom is soft all the way up it behind the sound skin, and there is no point keeping the top of it. It comes away in three pieces and the inside of it is black.

And then she lets in a new one.

The new stile is a length of oak off the top of a stack that came in on a lorry in the autumn and has been behind the lav since. It is planed to the width of the old one and to the thickness of the sound part of it, so it is a bit thicker than the old one was because the old one had gone soft and lost a face, and it is let into the head rail and the bottom rail dry, the same joint the old one had, and it is wedged up tight at the head with two hardwood wedges driven from the outside so that they finish flush and cannot be prised out from the front.

The bottom rail is not let into the new stile; the new stile is let into the head and the bottom, and she says that out loud, because it is the difference between a piece of wood with another piece of wood hanging off it and a frame.

---

Then the brace.

She does it dry and she does it let in, and she says what a brace is while she is doing it.

"You are not bolting a bar of iron across the face of that gate."

"No I am not."

"A catch is not a brace and a brace is not a catch, and they do not do each other's work. That gate is hanging on one edge of itself with all its own weight coming down that edge, and that edge is about to be eighteen inches long instead of four, and the bottom of it has been a foot off the ground for twenty years. Something has to keep the corners of that frame square."

She lets a length of oak into the hanging stile and the free stile on the diagonal, the whole length of the gate from the bottom of the new stile to the top of the far one, housings cut into the thickness of both stiles and one bolt through each end into the rail behind, and the brace goes in the frame instead of on the front of it.

---

Then the two new jamb hinges, and they are the reason a gate on a wall can be taken off and put on.

Jamb hinges are a pin with a hook at the top of it, and the hook goes into a socket cut in the jamb and the gate hangs off the hook and can be lifted off the hook and put back on the hook without the pin ever coming out. There are a pair of new ones at each end of the hanging stile, four inches apart, so that the load is not on one hook at one moment.

The jamb at that end is an oak post and not masonry, which is why a hook can be in it at all. The old two are out — a chisel and a bar and about ten minutes — and the new sockets are opened out to take the new hooks and packed behind them with a hardwood offcut so that they cannot turn in the oak. And they are set plumb with a short level stood against the face of the post, and that is what the level is for, not for the gate; it is for the hooks, because a jamb hook that is not plumb is a gate that hangs crooked.

And she sets the stay in the ground.

It is a cranked stay of wrought iron, three feet of it, with an eye at the top end, and it is set into a block of stone in the ground at the far side with the eye standing a foot above the flags, and it is the thing the gate opens onto and it is the thing that stops the gate being blown over in a gale. She sets the block where the leaf will come down onto it and she uses the level across the eye of it so that the gate comes onto the stop square and not onto one corner of it.

---

Then it goes back on the hooks, and there are two of them for it.

The old woman does not touch it while it goes up. She stands clear and her sister takes the whole weight and lifts the leaf onto the top hook and drops the bottom of it down onto the second one and it is hanging there off the wall again with a week's work in it.

Then her sister puts the bolt home.

The bolt goes across. The free stile of that gate is a hand's width back where it belongs because the frame has come back into the square it was cut in, and the new hanging stile is eighteen inches long where the old one was four, which is enough to hang a whole leaf off for twenty years, and the bolt goes into the keep.

Her sister puts her thumb on the head of the bolt and pushes it and it goes in as far as it will go.

---

Then the old woman shuts it herself.

She goes to the free end of it and puts both hands on the top rail and pulls, and it comes across on its own the whole way, and it comes down onto the stay in the ground, and the bolt drops into its keep with a noise like a pen going into a cap. Her hands are on her own gate and the gate is shut.

It has not shut since the September it was propped. Nobody shut it. It has been standing against that wall with a rail up at the height of her chest for five months because a woman of seventy-eight could not get it across a hand's width.

She opens it again and it comes back against the wall on its own, and shuts it again and it comes across and drops and latches.

The old woman stands in her own yard with her hands still on the rail, and the gate is shut, and it stays shut while she stands there.

A gate shuts and latches by being square, and being square is not a thing a person can talk into it. She has had five months of that gate standing against the render with its top rail at the height of her chest, and there is no pull on that handle now and no hip against it and no bar off the wall, and there is a bolt going into a keep that has been in that pier since before she came.

It is shut.
