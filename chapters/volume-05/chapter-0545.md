# Chapter 0545 — Four Feet Away

The Wednesday of the fifth of February 2020. Most of the working day, in a building at the end of one street of this borough.

**HE HAS DONE THE SAME WORK OUT OF THE SAME FLOOR FOR NINE YEARS AND THERE IS NO SIGN ANYWHERE ON IT, AND A YOUNG MAN HAS BEEN STANDING BESIDE HIM FOR THREE WEEKS AND HAS NOT BEEN TOLD WHAT ANY PART OF IT IS FOR, AND NOT ONE MINUTE OF IT HAS GONE ON HOW OLD EITHER OF THEM IS.**

The building is at the end of the street and it was a shop for a good many years and then it was nothing for six, and the man in his mid fifties has had it since the second of March 2011 and has done every part of it himself, which is not a figure anybody in that street has ever needed.

People come because somebody has told somebody. The somebody is always a different one. None of them has ever asked what he does, because what he does is not a thing you ask a man about in a street like this.

He is in before half past seven. He has a heater going at the back and a radio on low and he works with his hands and he does not talk while he works, and he did not talk while he worked for nine years until about three weeks ago, when there was somebody else in the place and the silence needed something in it.

---

Today it is a dresser. A low one, four feet high, laid out sideways across two low stands in the middle of the floor with an old towel folded under the leg that was loose.

The leg is not broken. That is the thing people get wrong when they bring something to him. The wood is sound and the joint has come apart because the glue has gone, which happens after a while and which nobody can do anything about until it happens.

He has the top off in about ten minutes. There is no cutting in it at all. He does that with a block of wood and the heel of his hand, and he works the joint loose in four places and breaks nothing, and the young man watches all of that and says nothing, and he has been watching for three weeks and has not said nothing about anything else.

The young man is in his early twenties. He is better with a brush than the man in his mid fifties is, and that has been established twice this week without either of them mentioning it, and he has still not been given the part that matters.

"What am I holding?"

"You can let that go."

He is holding the top of the dresser with both hands, and he is holding it correctly, and the man in his mid fifties looks at him for a second and then goes back to the joint.

Glue. The man in his mid fifties mixes it and the young man stirs it, and he stirs it too fast and the man looks at the stick and then at him, and neither of them says anything about the stick.

The joint goes back together at about ten to twelve. He clamps it, and one of the two clamps he has has a screw that turns by hand, and he sits down on the floor with that one for about six minutes while the other one does the work.

The young man asks it from the floor, in the voice of a man asking the price of milk.

"They've got a place at the end of the road."

"I know."

"Did you go and see it?"

"I went in November."

That is the whole of it for a while. The young man waits, and the man in his mid fifties does not add anything, and about a minute later the young man asks the second thing.

"Would you go again?"

"No," the man says.

He does not say why and he never will, and nothing else in this morning is going to bring it out. He went in November. He looked at what they have, and he came back, and he has not mentioned it since, and the young man has now been told and will not tell anybody either, because he is in his early twenties and has not worked out yet that there is a difference between the two of them.

It is a flat answer and it is the whole of what he is going to give. The young man takes it, and they go back in.

---

**HE ANSWERED IN FOUR WORDS AND HE HAS TOLD NOBODY ELSE SINCE NOVEMBER, AND THE ONLY PERSON IN THIS BOROUGH WHO KNOWS HE WENT IS TWENTY-SOMETHING AND WAS TOLD ONLY THAT HE DID.**

The second half of the day is the leg.

The leg is at the back left and the joint is the same glue, and it is worse than the one they did at ten to twelve, and the man in his mid fifties hands the whole of it over because that is what three weeks of standing beside somebody is for, and he goes four feet away and lets it happen.

The young man gets the joint apart in about half the time the man in his mid fifties would have taken. He is pleased about it and it shows in his shoulders. Then he puts the glue in, and puts the leg back, and clamps it, and turns round.

"That it?"

"That it," the man says.

He goes over it. It takes him about ten minutes and the young man watches all of that too, and there is nothing at all on that face, and the young man will think about it for a long time afterwards and will not understand what he was looking at.

The joint is the wrong way round. The old glue came apart because damp got in at the top and ran down into it, and the young man has put it back so that it will do the same again in a wet month, and the man in his mid fifties does not say the words, and he takes the clamps off and does the joint again.

Then he does it once more in the other order, to be certain, which he has never had to do in nine years.

At about four they get the side on, and he runs his hand along the joint and stops about a third of the way along, and then carries on.

"There is one more," he says. "Tomorrow. Go and get your jacket."

The young man gets his jacket. At the door the man in his mid fifties says three things, and the first is about the cold coming up off the ground that week, and the second is about the time, and the third is that the hinge is not to be done until the glue has gone off overnight.

Then the door is shut, and the young man is out in the street, and the man in his mid fifties is inside with a dresser that will not be ready until Thursday, and neither of them has mentioned a place at the end of the road once in eight hours, and the young man will not mention it either, and there is nobody else in that street who would know to ask.

**HE CAME BACK FROM THERE IN NOVEMBER AND HAS SAID NOTHING SINCE, AND THE YOUNG MAN PUT ONE JOINT BACK THE WAY IT HAD FAILED AND WAS TOLD NOTHING AT ALL, AND THE OLDER MAN TOOK THE CLAMPS OFF AND DID IT AGAIN BY HAND, AND THERE IS A HINGE TOMORROW.**
