# Chapter 0545 — The Part He Does Himself

The Wednesday of the fifth of February 2020, and most of the working day, in one street of this borough and in a building at the end of it.

**HE IS A MAN IN HIS MID FIFTIES AND HE HAS DONE THE SAME WORK FOR NINE YEARS AND HE HAS NEVER WRITTEN A WORD OF IT DOWN, AND A YOUNG MAN HAS BEEN STANDING IN THAT PLACE WITH HIM FOR ABOUT THREE WEEKS AND HAS NOT BEEN TOLD WHAT ANY OF IT IS FOR.**

The building is at the end of a street and it was a shop for a good many years and then it was nothing for six, and the man in his mid fifties has had it since the second of March 2011 and has done every part of it himself, which is not a figure anybody in that street has ever needed.

There is no sign on it. There has never been a sign on it. People come because somebody has told somebody, and the somebody is always a different one, and none of them has ever asked him what he does, because what he does is not a thing you ask a man about in a street.

He is in before half past seven. He has a heater going and a radio on low and he works with his hands and he does not talk while he works, and he has not talked while he worked for nine years until about three weeks ago, when there was somebody else in the place and there had to be something in the silence.

---

The work this week is a dresser. A low one, four feet high, put down sideways across two trestles in the middle of the floor with an old towel folded under the leg that was loose.

The leg is not broken. That is the thing people get wrong. The wood is sound and the joint has come apart because the glue has gone, which happens after a while and which nobody can do anything about until it happens.

He has the top off in about ten minutes. There is no cutting involved. He does it with a block of wood and the heel of his hand, and he works the joint loose in four places and does not break anything, and the young man watches all of that and says nothing, and the young man has been watching for three weeks and has not said nothing about anything else.

The young man is in his early twenties. He is better with a brush than the man in his mid fifties is, which is a fact that has been established twice this week without either of them mentioning it, and he has not yet been given the job of the part that matters.

"What am I holding?"

"You can let that go."

He is holding the top of the dresser with both hands, and he is holding it correctly, and the man in his mid fifties looks at him for a second and then goes back to the joint.

They do the glue. The man in his mid fifties mixes it and the young man stirs it, and he stirs it too fast and the man looks at the stick and then at him, and neither of them says anything about the stick.

The joint goes back together at about ten to twelve. He clamps it, and the two clamps he has are old and one of them has a screw that turns by hand, and he sits on the floor with that one for about six minutes while the other one does the work.


They break for lunch at about half past twelve and they eat in the back part, sitting on an upturned thing, and the young man asks the question.

"They've got a place at the end of the road."

"I know."

"Did you go and see it?"

"I went in November."

That is the whole of it for a while. The young man waits, and the man in his mid fifties does not add anything, and about a minute later the young man asks the second thing.

"Would you go again?"

"No," the man says.

He does not say why, and he never will, and nothing else in this morning is going to bring it out. He went in November. He looked at what they have, and he came back, and he told nobody that he had been, and he has not mentioned it since, and the young man has now been told and will not tell anybody either, because he is in his early twenties and he has not worked out yet that there is a difference between the two of them.

It is a flat answer and it is the whole of what he is going to give. The young man takes it, and they go back in.

---

**HE GAVE THE WHOLE OF IT TO THE YOUNG MAN BECAUSE A FORTNIGHT OF WATCHING IS FOR THAT, AND HE STOOD FOUR FEET AWAY AND LET IT HAPPEN, AND HE HAS TOLD NOBODY IN THIS BOROUGH THAT HE WENT TO THE END OF THE ROAD IN NOVEMBER.**

The second half is the leg.

The leg is at the back left and the joint is the same glue, and it is worse, and the man in his mid fifties gives the whole of it to the young man because that is what a week of watching is for, and he stands four feet away and watches him do it.

The young man gets the joint apart in about half the time the man in his mid fifties would have taken, and he is pleased about that and it shows in his shoulders, and then he puts the glue in and puts the leg back and clamps it and turns round and says, "That it?"

"That it," the man says.

He goes over. It takes him about ten minutes and the young man watches all of that too, and there is nothing at all on that face, which the young man will think about for a long time afterwards and will not understand.

The joint is the wrong way round. The old glue came apart because damp got in at the top and ran down, and the young man has put it back so it will do the same again, and the man in his mid fifties does not explain that and does not say the words, and he takes the clamps off and does the joint again.

Then he does it once more, in the other order, to be certain, which he has never had to do in nine years.

At about four they get the dresser's side on, and the man in his mid fifties runs his hand along the joint and stops about a third of the way along, and then carries on.

"There is one more," he says. "Tomorrow. Go and get your jacket."

The young man gets his jacket. At the door the man in his mid fifties says four sentences, and the first one is about the cold coming off the ground that week, and the second one is about the time, and the third one is that the hinge is not to be done until the glue has gone off overnight, and the fourth one is the young man's own name, said in a way that is not about the young man.

Then the door is shut and the young man is outside in the street, and the man in his mid fifties is inside with a dresser that will not be ready until Thursday, and neither of them has mentioned a place at the end of the road once in four hours, and the young man will not mention it either, and there is nobody else in that street who would know to ask.

**HE LOOKED AT WHAT THEY HAVE AND HE CAME BACK AND HE HAS SAID NOTHING FOR TEN WEEKS, AND THE BOY GOT THE ONE PART WRONG TWICE IN ONE AFTERNOON AND WAS TOLD NOTHING EITHER TIME, AND ON THURSDAY THERE WILL BE A HINGE.**
