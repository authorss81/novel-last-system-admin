# Chapter 0563 — The Back, Then the Front

The Tuesday of the fifth of May 2020, and most of the day, in one street of this borough and at the end of one garden in it.

**THEY HAVE DONE THE BACK AND THEN THE FRONT EVERY SATURDAY MORNING SINCE 2016, AND IN THE JANUARY THE BOY SIMPLY WAS NOT THERE AND GAVE NO REASON, AND THE FATHER CUT BOTH ENDS ALONE FOR NINE OF THEM, AND WHEN THE BOY DID TURN UP IN MARCH THE ONLY THING THAT HAPPENED WAS THAT THE MOWER CHANGED HANDS.**

They are two doors apart in one street of this borough and there are about fourteen of the houses in it and it is not a street anybody gives directions by.

He is a man in his mid sixties. The boy is a man in his late twenties and has been the boy since about 2012 and has been a man in his own right since about 2016, and nothing about how the two of them speak has changed since the boy was sixteen.

The garden at the back of the father's house is about fifty foot deep and about thirty wide and it has never been anything but grass, and there is a garage at the far end of it that came with the house and that the father uses for things that do not go in the house.

Every Saturday morning since about April 2016, at about half past nine, the two of them have done that grass.

The son did the back. The father did the front, which is on the street side and is about thirty foot by fifteen and has a bit round the side of the house that nobody has ever cut since about 1998. Then the two of them did the front between them, because it takes one person about fifty minutes and the father was not going to do an hour and ten with it on his own.

Then the son put the mower away and the father went in and that was it.

The mower belongs to the father. It cost more than either of them expected and the son paid for about half of it in about 2016 out of money he had not got, and neither of them has mentioned that either since 2016.

The boy stopped in the January and he did not say why.

He did not say he was not coming and he did not say it was something else. He was not there on the first Saturday of January after New Year and he was not there on any of the eight after it, and the father cut the back and the front and the front again on each of those nine, and did the whole of it alone, and did not ask.

There are about thirty people in that street who would have seen him on his own in that garden in February. Nine of them have said to somebody that the father is out there on his own now. None of them has been asked by him and none of them has offered.

He has not said one word about the nine Saturdays. Not to the boy, not to the woman he lives with, not to the man next door who has a key to that garage for no reason that either of them has ever explained.

On the Saturday of the fourteenth of March the boy came.

---

He came at about half past nine, which is the hour, and he did not go through the house and he did not knock, and he came down the side of that house with his hands in his pockets and stood at the far end of the back where he always stood.

The father was about nine foot into the back when he heard the gate and he carried on for another two passes and then stopped the mower, and he did not turn round.

He pulled the deadman, and he did not let go of it, and he did not say anything, and after a moment the boy crossed about twenty foot of that grass and took the handles off him, and then the father let go.

He did not say one word while that was happening. He has not said one word about it since and he is not going to, and neither has the boy, and there has been nothing between them since the fourteenth of March except the grass.

The boy has been there every Saturday since the fourteenth of March, which is the eighth of them and he has not missed one. He does the back and the father does the front and then they do the front between them, and the boy puts the mower away after, and on the twenty-first of March the father made him a tea and handed it to him and did not say anything about that either, and they have drunk about nine of them since.

---

**ON THE TUESDAY OF THE FIFTH OF MAY THE BOY RANG AND SAID HE WOULD NOT BE THERE ON THE SATURDAY, AND GAVE NO REASON, AND THE FATHER SAID AYE, AND SAID NOTHING ELSE, AND ON THE SATURDAY HE CUT THE BACK AND THE FRONT AND THE FRONT AGAIN.**

There is one day in every week now where the two of them find out the evening before whether it is a Saturday or not, and neither of them has ever remarked on that, and it has happened four times since March and there has been nothing said on any of the four occasions.

The father asked nothing on the fifth of May. He was in the kitchen when it rang and he took it there and said aye and then said nothing for about ten seconds, and the boy said, "Right, I'll let you know," and that was the whole of the call.

The boy has let him know on two of the four since. The other two Mondays it was neither of them who said anything, and both of them mowed.

On the Saturday before this one the father cut the back and the front and the front again, from about half past nine until about one, and he was out there on his own for about an hour and ten minutes of it, which is about how long the front takes one person, and nobody came.

**THE FATHER CUT BOTH ENDS OF THAT GARDEN ALONE FOR NINE SATURDAYS AND THE BOY HAS BEEN BACK FOR SEVEN OF THEM SINCE MARCH, AND THE ONLY SOUND THE TWO OF THEM HAVE MADE SINCE IS AYE ON A TUESDAY MORNING, AND NEITHER OF THEM IS GOING TO SAY ANYTHING ELSE.**
