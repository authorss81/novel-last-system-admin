# Chapter 0218 — The Bill

Friday the twenty-second of January 2016, from about ten in the morning, a room in Marrowgate, and a yard.

**THE PAY IS GOOD, THE HOUSE IS GOOD, THE WORK IS ORDINARY, THE ARRANGEMENT IS IN WRITING AND THE WRITING IS CORRECT, AND THE COST OF THE FIVE THINGS DOES NOT FALL ON THE MAN WHO DOES THEM AND FALLS ON EVERYBODY ELSE, AND THE CORRECT SENTENCE ABOUT THAT IS A SENTENCE THAT SOUNDS LIKE AN OBJECTION.**

The room was the same room and about seven of the nine people were the same people, and the item on the twenty-second was the year.

Mavis Bray had a single sheet in front of her with a column of figures down it and she gave it out and let people read it, which is not what a clerk does, and the reason she did it was that the sheet had been in that room every January for eleven years and everybody in it already knew what was on it.

The water is ninety pence a quarter. The shelter is free and has not had anybody in it who could not pay since the year the town bought it, and has not had anybody at all in it since the summer before last. The clinic is free and the doctor comes on four days and the nurse comes on five and the dispensing comes from the works's own store. The gate is open seven to six. The yard is anybody's who has a reason to be in it.

The works line was a figure and the works line was a quarter of the sheet.

"Numbers," said Jonas Mercer.

"That's a local thing," said Mavis Bray. "Look at the base."

He did, and the base was fine, and the bill for water in that town, per person, was about a seventh of what the same water cost the people on the far side of the estuary, and the reason was written at the bottom of the sheet in a sentence that had not been argued with by anybody in eleven years.

"That's the argument," she said, "and everybody who has ever stood in this room has made it, and none of us is stupid. The day the town bought that works there was a bill in it that nobody could pay, and about thirty houses stopped having water, and the choice was not between owning it and not owning it. It was between a bill nobody could pay and a rate everybody can. That's the whole of it. It isn't better because we're clever. It's better because there is one bill and it is on a rate, and there is no company in this arrangement to buy the works off us in a bad year, because there is no company in it."

"You bought it for nothing."

"We bought it for a nominal sum and the works came with a liability," she said. "That's in the sheet too if you ever want it. And the filter that everybody in this region will have heard about was done three years before we had it, by the people who owned it, and it cost them money and cost us nothing, and I am not going to sit in a room and pretend that was generosity. It was their asset. It is a very good asset. It was built before I came here and it will outlast me."

**AND THE WORKS LINE, WHICH WAS A QUARTER OF THE SHEET, WAS THE ONLY PART OF IT THAT NOBODY IN THAT ROOM COULD EXPLAIN, BECAUSE THE WORKS LINE IS MAINTENANCE AND MATERIALS AND LABOUR, AND THE LABOUR IN IT IS ONE MAN, AND HE IS PAID, AND THE LINE IS CORRECT.**

"He's on the rate roll," said Mavis Bray, before he asked it. "He's on the payroll. That's the whole of it. He is not on a rota and he is not on a list and he does not have a job description, and if he asked me what his job was I could not tell him in a sentence, and I have tried, because I have had eleven Januarys of this and about six of them I have tried."

---

He asked the question at about half past eleven, in the yard, with the lorry behind them and the shed door open and the brown bag in the corner.

"What happens when he isn't here."

"He has not been ill for a week in eleven years."

"That is not what I asked and you know it is not what I asked."

Mavis Bray stood in the yard with her sheet rolled up in her hand and looked at a town of about three thousand people that she had been the clerk of for twenty years, and Jonas Mercer watched her get to it, and it took her about four seconds.

"Last February," she said. "Nine days. He had something in his chest and he was in bed at home and I went to see him on the second day and he was in bed and he was not having anybody in the house, and I left. We hired a man from Nether End for three days. He is a decent man and he has got a van and a machine, and he could not read the pump house and he did not do the rack, because he did not know it needed doing, because there is nothing on the machine that says so. The water went cloudy on the Thursday and the clinic shut for most of a day because the dispensing comes out of that store, and the door was shut and about sixty people came and went. The yard gate stayed shut because nobody had a key, and the lorry went out once, to somebody who had asked for it, and a man had to walk up and ask Walter Sallow at about seven in the evening whether he could have the gate open."

"And the shelter."

"The boiler went out about eleven on the Sunday night and a man of about ninety-one — you know the one, he's on the gate on a Sunday — went out and got it going at about half past one in the morning and it took him two goes, and I found out about it on the Monday morning from the cleaner." She said it without any particular heat. "That's what it costs. That's the whole of the list and there is nothing wrong with any of it and every part of it happened inside a town of three thousand people, about a mile end to end."

"And the bill for those nine days."

"The bill for those nine days was the three days of that man and a fortnight of his wages because we paid him, and a tank, and that is all that anybody could put on a piece of paper." She stopped. "That is the bit I have never once said out loud in this room and I have been the clerk of this town for twenty years and it has been eleven Januaries."

She said that to a man who was not going to be in the room in June, and then she said the rest of it anyway, which Jonas Mercer understood was the price of standing in a yard with a woman who had been doing a job for two decades.

"The figure for the works is the same this year as it was last year, and it will be the same next year, and I have never once in eleven Januaries been able to make it go up, because there is nothing to compare it with. There is no nine days line. There is no line at all for what it costs on a day when it is not happening, and the reason there is no line is that it has never not happened, and that is the cheapest figure this town has ever produced, and it is cheap because of a man of eighty-six, and not because of anything we did, and if I say that in there this afternoon four people will hear it as me asking somebody to stop, and I am not asking anybody to do anything at all, and I have already told him so about three times and I have got nowhere and I am not going to."

Nobody said it in the room.

Jonas Mercer was in the room. There were seven people in it and a woman of about seventy-five who had come to the room on the first of the month every month for nine years and had not once been asked to be there and was on no list, and a man of about ninety-one, and five others, and about half past two in the afternoon, when Mavis Bray came back in with the sheet rolled up and put it on the table and asked whether anybody had anything else, four of them thought the sentence at the same time.

Nobody put it in the room. The clerk had said it to a man in a yard and had said it about a man who was standing on the other side of the yard with a spanner in his hand and had not heard a word of it.

He was the only person in two countries who could have said it there.

---

At about three o'clock Jonas Mercer asked the clerk for a copy of the arrangement, and she said there was one of those and it was on the wall of that room and it went up in about 1994 and it had never been reprinted because there had been nothing to change.

"I could ring the printer," she said.

"No," he said. "I know exactly what happens to a piece of paper like that and so do you. It goes into a plastic wallet and it goes into an inside pocket and it comes out on a table in about five months in a room where somebody asks a question, and then it is a sheet somebody brought, and it is not the sheet on the wall, and the difference between those two things is the whole of what I have been doing since the fifteenth of August and I have stopped."

She looked at him for a second and then said, "Right," and put the sheet back in her bag, and nobody asked him what he had just given up.

**AND THE ARRANGEMENT IS ON THE WALL IN THAT ROOM, IN FOUR LINES, IN THE SAME TYPE, AND IT HAS NOT BEEN ALTERED, AND NOBODY IN THIS BOROUGH COULD PRODUCE IT EVEN IF THEY WERE ASKED, AND IT IS THIS, AND EVERY WORD OF IT IS CORRECT:**

> *The works, the clinic, the shelter, the gate and the yard are held by the town of Marrowgate.*
> *Walter Sallow is responsible for them.*
> *A duty is an act and not a place.*
> *Nothing in this can be given to a person.*

Walter Sallow came in out of the yard at about ten past four and put the kettle on and asked whether anybody wanted a cup, and Mavis Bray said she would, and he poured it and put the milk back in the fridge, and there were about nine seconds in which nobody said anything. Then the man of about ninety-one asked whether the March sheet was going to be the same as the January one, and the clerk said that it would, and the room went on.

**AND WALTER SALLOW WAS ASKED ONCE, IN A ROOM, A FEW YEARS AGO, WHETHER HE WOULD TAKE IT ON, AND HE SAID THAT HE WOULD, AND HE HAS NOT BEEN ASKED ANYTHING SINCE, AND NOBODY HAS EVER ASKED HIM WHETHER HE WOULD LIKE TO STOP, AND NOBODY IS GOING TO.**
