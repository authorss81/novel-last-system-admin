# Chapter 0921 — Between The Stacked Boards

Sunday the thirtieth of June 2024, at the stacked boards at the back of the store at the unit with roof water ticking off the gutter. Between two stacked lengths.

**HE HAS A STACK OF BOARDS AT THE BACK OF THE STORE AT THE UNIT WITH A GAP BETWEEN TWO LENGTHS AND HE MEANS TO PUSH THE MIDDLE TWO FINGERS PAIRED INTO THAT GAP WITH OUTER FINGERS FOLDED BACK AND THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN IS AT THE SIDE DOORWAY FROM THE LANE WITH A FOLDED SACK ON HER SHOULDER.**

The stack has leaned at the back of that store since the roof was felted, and the boards above it have been shifted once while the stack has kept its lean.

It is a stack of pine lengths about eight feet long, with stickers between and sawdust in the splits, and the third from the top standing a finger off its neighbour where the timber bowed. He wants the gap between those two lengths, where the air moves and the dust lies.

The daylight between stacked lengths counts beyond their faces, for the faces meet the light and the daylight meets what seasoned boards breathe out. So the middle pair ease together between stacked boards. Chill dead air brushes the fingers in there, and sawdust-velvet board flanks tug at the skin.

A hand's breadth back from the gap, the pair come up against the sticker, dry and square, with the grain raised where the saw left it and the ends dark where oil soaked them seasons past. He works the pair up then down the crossbar, meeting honest timber throughout, only a coarser patch greeting the nails lower down.

What makes it rough is the saw. Pine throws splinters in summer when dry air meets wood that was cut green, and each month raises them where the boards cannot move.

Between the two lengths there is air and nothing else, and it moves; a finger put in comes out cooler than it went in, which is how the pair has stayed pale between where the boards round them have gone dark.

He pulls the hand free of the slit and strokes dust off on the stack edge, doorway-framed, lane at his back, studying the boards. The woman from two doors down comes to the side doorway from the lane with a folded sack on her shoulder and does not lean it against the frame.

She paused in the doorway, sack riding her shoulder, eyes going stack-ward then lane-ward and back. The runner-bean news came her way, first pick half a basket, and presently she hitched the sack higher, keeping it clear of the frame, before continuing down the lane with it aboard and the doorway vacant behind her.

Two feet toward the tail his fingers meet a second crossbar behind the deals. Not pine. A strip of oak, set across under the stack to keep it off the damp floor, about two inches wide and laid flat where the boards meet it.

He angles the bent wrist in and strokes its face with finger flanks through powdery gloom and chill dead air. It is a stack sticker, put there when the boards were piled, to throw the pile off the stone and let air under it.

Crossbars lift the stack a finger off the flags down its whole length, and the slit of air beneath carries old dust and the tang of offcuts dropped years back.

---

A pile raised off the flagstones comes through winter with every board solid. Draughts pass beneath and the bottom lengths stay crisp, whereas boards bedded flat on rock sweat underneath and blacken inside two years. Raised like this, the bottom lengths have kept pale and rough-grained, while the crown of the heap has tanned dark from skylight and handling.

Every crossbar meets the boards cross-grained, edge up, and oak laid athwart pine like that wicks wet sideways out of the joint instead of nursing it in the butt ends. Planks ride rough where crossbars shade them and glassy between where palms slide.

Pine lasts under a roof because slates shed storms while open stacks drink, and every cold season the open ones sink a grade deeper. Roofing gone tender crumbles mealy at a fingernail and sheds in flakes, while seasoned roofing pings faintly under a knuckle rap.

Aged felt rips into streamers where wet creeps under and heaves it, while felt over dry deals clings fast for a decade. Streamers kink upward where wet shoves, each kink baring a wan scar that tans over by the next rain.

The rack uprights at the end of the stack stand plumb on the floor stones, with the feet in angle irons and the bolts through them, and the near upright leans a hair out where the stack pushes it. A lean like that grows a little every year as the boards season and settle, while the far upright stays plumb against the wall. Roof water ticks off the gutter behind the work in showers and runs down the back of the store to the floor, where flags sag at the rack feet and wet idles in a thin pool till draughts sip it. The floor stones under the rack lie dark with dust drifted in lines, each line marking where the broom stops and the draught begins, and the lines go deeper toward the back where the air moves least.

The end grain of the stacked lengths shows the winters in rings, pale where the saw cut last spring and dark where the weather got in, and the third board from the top carries a knot the size of a coin with the grain swept round it. A knot like that seasons harder than the boards round it and stands a hair proud when the rest shrink, and the polish on its crown shows hands have passed over it every time the stack is reached.

The heap's deals extend unbowed tip to tail, grain streaming lengthwise, tails squared at the rear. Deals dish when the nether cheek stays moist and the upper dries, the moist cheek bellies and the dry cheek hollows, dishing the plank sunward. This lot dishes faintly storeward, admitting extra daylight between and seasoning the rick quicker year on year.

Dished deals pump daylight through by day and the crossbars pump it below by night, so a dished rick breathes each time the door swings. Flat deals pump nothing, and the rick sits sealed and mists, which explains crossbars under dished lots, thin air under flat.

Our heap lies rough on its crossbars, lifted off the flags by them, the dished lengths fanning spare draughts between of their own accord. A weather-roughened face films with powder levelly by dusk, while a draught-washed one stripes. Powder films this heap levelly tip to tail, its rough pine deaf to wind, while bench lids stripe where palms travel.

Grit scours the rack shadow on the flags and leaves it coarse there. The knot crowns are coarse with the grain swirling round them, and the sticker rims coarse with the burred edge the saw left. Tallow smeared on the upright corners takes two hand-high slides out of all that, pale lanes the shoulders polish weekly. Slide polish shows where boards are steadied, and lengthens with the season.

Slab offcuts lean against the rack end, limestone flags saved from the yard dig, their sawn rims still pale. Battens stacked atop the heap sticker the next load, pine laths that bow upward as they season. A fan of dust on the back wall marks where the door draught eddies, scalloped arcs light at the crown and dark at the skirt. The arcs shift with the seasons, crowns paling in summer dust and darkening in winter damp.

Three coach bolts keep the rack feet down, and each has been spun once since it went in, a single turn and no more. A bolt turned once and left goes on easing, cold season and warm, and nobody has ever got that back. The oxide under those heads has run out across the upright in marmalade and stops dead at the first unstained inch of pine, which is as far as the damp ever got.

The stack dries where the yard airs it, and the boards keep the gap the stickers gave them, and the dust stays in the splits.
