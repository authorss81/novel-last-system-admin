# Chapter 0375 — Do It

Friday the eighth of December 2017, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past nine at night, a house in a row off the Cleeve road, and a street, in Tideglass.

**A JOINT UNDER A KITCHEN SINK WAS WEEPING IN THAT HOUSE ON THAT MORNING AND A MAN CAME AND DID IT IN ABOUT TWENTY MINUTES, AND WHILE THE SINK WAS OFF HE FOUND THAT THE FRONT EDGE OF THE WORKTOP HAD GONE SOFT WHERE IT RAN INTO THE RAIL, AND HE TOOK IT OFF AND CUT A NEW PIECE AND PUT IT BACK, AND NOBODY HAD ASKED HIM TO. THE WOMAN OF THAT HOUSE WAS AT WORK AND WAS NOT THERE. THE MAN WHO WAS IN THE HOUSE SAID DO IT AND WENT OUT TO HIS OWN WORK AND CAME BACK AT HALF PAST SIX AND SAID NOTHING ABOUT IT, AND THE MAN WHO DID IT SAID NOTHING ABOUT IT EITHER, AND NEITHER OF THEM HAS SAID ANYTHING SINCE.**

The row is nine houses off the Cleeve road with a footway about four foot wide and no back yards at all, and the last of those nine houses has a strip of ground behind it about as long as a car and as wide as a door, fenced in with three foot of boarding, and there is a man in that last house, a woman, and a girl who is at college four days a week.

The woman telephoned the man at about half past eight on the Wednesday night, from the number of a card she keeps with the bills on a shelf in the front room, and the man had been in that kitchen in the summer and had put the sink in himself, and she said there was water coming out of the pipe under it and not into the cupboard, and he said he would come Friday morning.

She did not mention the worktop. She had not looked at the worktop. She had looked at it every day for about six years without seeing it.

He came at about a quarter to eight on the Friday. He had a bag with a press in it and a spirit level in a case and about four foot of clear plastic pipe in his coat, and he was in that kitchen about eight minutes and had the trap off the waste and the joint out and a new length of pipe in and a new trap in, and he ran the tap into a bucket and it came out of the joint clean and he put the cupboard back together and shut it.

The worktop is about two foot deep and runs the length of that wall and it is a laminate on a chipboard base on a frame, and it was fitted by somebody who did not put a rail in under it and did not leave room for one, and it sits on the top of the frame and against the wall with nothing behind its front edge at all.

While he was standing there with his hands in the cupboard he put his thumb under the front edge of it and it moved, and it came up about an eighth of an inch, and under it the chipboard was dark and soft at the front and crumbly between his thumb and his finger.

He looked at it for a moment and then went and got the worktop off.

---

That took longer than anything else he did that day. There was a strip of tile along the wall behind it that had to come off, and the tile had been laid straight and had to come off straight, and it took him about a quarter of an hour to get it up in whole pieces. He broke two.

He took the front edge off with a knife and a chisel, working along it in four or five places, and the old edge came away in two lengths and the front of the cabinet was a line of chipboard and dust. He cut a new piece off a sheet he had in the van on a table saw and got the profile right on the second try and glued and pinned it on, and it took another half hour, and he filled the two broken tiles with the same filler he used on joints and pushed them back in and wiped the line with his thumb and did not go over it again.

Then he wiped the worktop down, and swept the floor, and put the tools in the van, and it was about ten past ten.

The man of that house was not there.

He had been in the front room with a cup and the paper when the man came, and he had come in at about half past seven and was going to be at the bottom of the town by nine, and he had told the man at the door on the Wednesday that he would be out and had not said to what.

The woman was at the hospital from half past seven and does a shift that ends about half past four, and the girl was at college.

There was nobody in that house at all between about half past eight that morning and about half past four that afternoon, except a cat.

He came in out of the yard with the bag and the man in the front room said, "You're done, are you."

"I found your front edge," the man said. "It's gone soft. You want me to leave it or do it."

"Do it," said the man in the forties, from the front room doorway, with his cup in his hand.

"Right," said the other one, and went and got the worktop off.

That is the whole of what either of them said about the front edge of that worktop, then or afterwards, and it has never been referred to by anybody as anything at all.

The man in the forties went out at ten past eight and drove to the bottom of the town. He came back into that street at about half past five, put his bag in the passage, and went into the kitchen and put the kettle on.

There is a new piece of beech-effect laminate along the front of that worktop, about two foot long and one and a half inch wide, and it is the same colour as the rest of it and it is the same thickness, and the grain does not run with the grain, and a man who did not know which piece was which would not be able to tell it in ordinary light.

There are two tiles in that splash-back with a fine pale line in the joint where there is not one either side of them.

He looked at the front edge and put his tea down and said nothing. The man who had cut it was in the yard at the time, loading a bag of sand into the back of the pickup, and the woman of that house came in at about half past six and the girl came in at about eight and the front edge of that worktop was not raised with any of them that evening or on any evening since.

It is a worktop. It does what a worktop does and the rail that should be behind it is not there and never was, and everything about that job is as it should be, and there is no version in which anybody in that house would have rung a man to say that a piece of wood had been cut for them.

The man who cut it has been in about four houses a week since the summer and he has not told a soul about that worktop and nobody who works alongside him has any idea it happened, and it is not on any sheet of paper, and it was not asked for, and it cost the household nothing at all, which is a thing that happened and not a thing that was arranged.

---

On the Friday morning the man in his forties was in that kitchen at about a quarter to eight and he was out of the house before nine, and the woman of that house was at work from half past seven until about half past four, and the girl was at college, and the whole of what was done in that kitchen that day was done in a house with nobody in it but a cat and a man who had said do it and gone out to his own work.

**ON THE MONDAY AFTER, HE ASKED HER ONE QUESTION ABOUT THAT KITCHEN, WHICH WAS WHETHER THE TILES BEHIND IT WERE THE SAME AS THE ONES ON THE OTHER WALL, AND SHE SAID THEY WERE NOT, AND HE WROTE THAT DOWN ON THE BACK OF A SHEET OF HIS OWN, AND NEITHER OF THEM ASKED ABOUT THE FRONT EDGE OR MENTIONED IT THEN OR ON ANY DAY SINCE.**
