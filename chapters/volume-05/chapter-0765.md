# Chapter 0765 — The Pallets In The Yard

Sunday the sixteenth of October 2022, in this borough. She is at her own lock-up and yard from morning until the middle of the day, and the whole of it is four pallets stood on edge against a wall with the wet still in the middle of them.

**A WOMAN IN HER LATE FORTIES TURNS FOUR DAMP PALLETS OVER ON THE CONCRETE, THE WOMAN WHO BOOKS WORK STANDS THEM ON EDGE LEANING BACK OFF THE WALL WITH A FINGER OF AIR BETWEEN THEM, AND THE DAMP IS STILL IN THE MIDDLE OF ALL FOUR.**

She is a woman in her late forties at her own lock-up on Sunday. The woman who comes to book work comes into her yard in the morning and does the second half her own way.

---

The van went before it was light and has not come back. The roller door is up and the light comes in over the concrete. In the corner at the leg of the rack there is a heap of grit from the bottom of the rail, half a bucketful of it left after one bucket went out with the bins, and the concrete round it swept into a crescent nobody has walked on since.

The rack at the back is shut, and the boards and the bolster are behind it. The drawer with the leads and the screws is shut in the bench under the window. Behind the bench there is a box that has not been opened all summer.

In the corner where the boundary wall comes back to the roller door there are four pallets lying flat on the concrete in a stack, and the stack has been leaning into the wall since the summer.

She brought them in herself in June, four at a time on the back of the truck, and they were dry when they came off it. They have lain in that corner since June under a sheet of polythene that blew off in the first wind, which she has twice folded and laid back over them and which is now folded on the stack doing nothing.

The bottom boards of all four of them have gone the colour of a wet floorboard. It is not mud. She has put her thumb on it and it does not come off, and it is not only on the top pallet; it is on the bottom one too, where the ground is dry and nothing has run.

She has a long bar, a screwdriver, a stiff brush, wire and a sack of polythene, and she means to turn them over one at a time, scrape the black off the bottom boards, wire them together so they cannot walk again, and lay the polythene over the top with the bin lid on it.

The woman who comes to book work comes out from behind the doors at the middle of the morning with her coat over her arm and looks down at the corner.

---

"You have got them flat on the ground."

"They are flat on the ground and they have been since June."

"And the bottom of that one is black."

"The bottom of all four of them is black." She does not stop with the screwdriver. "Give me a minute and I will show you the bottom of all four of them, which is worse than the top of that one."

"You have got a polythene on that for nothing."

"The polythene blew off in the wind in August." She puts the blade of the screwdriver under the top board of the top pallet and levers. "That is a flat thing on a stack of flat things. The wet that is in that comes out of the wood and not out of the ground, and the day it stops raining it comes out anyway."

"How long does it take."

"It takes until it is dry and it has not been dry since June."

The woman watches the top pallet come up off the one underneath.

"Then you are turning them over."

"I am turning them over."

"You are going to turn them over and scrape them and put them back flat and wire the top one to the bottom one and put your polythene back over them and put the bin lid on it." The woman crouches down by the corner and puts two fingers into the gap between the top pallet and the one under it and brings them up black. "And then you will do that again in three months."

---

She does not argue with that. She gets the top one over and puts it down the other way up, and the underside is black over the whole of it and the black is not on the surface, it is in the wood, and the screws that came in from the deck years ago stand out of it in little black rings.

The second is the same. So is the third.

"Then what are you going to do with them."

"Stand them on edge." The woman stands up and looks along the wall the whole length of the yard. "With the tops back against the brick and the bottoms out from the brick. A hand's width, more than that. And a finger between each one so it can go up between them and not round them."

"They will lean over."

"They will lean back against a wall that has been there since before you were born, and you will wire them where they stand so they cannot go, and they will not lean over anything because there is nothing there to lean over."

"And they will be leaning."

"They will be leaning on the wall and the air will come up between the boards and out at the top, and in a fortnight that black on the outsides will be a grey and then a buff, and you can put your hand in the gap at the bottom and it will be dry air in there and not a smell."

She looks at the wall. It runs the whole width of the yard and stops at the return of the roller door, and there is room along it for four of them with a yard to spare. The ground at the foot of it is coarse gravel laid by somebody else years ago, and it is not where the water goes when it rains, because the water goes across the yard to the corner at the front.

"That is a hand's width along the foot of my wall with something standing in front of it and I cannot get at the wall."

"You cannot get at that wall in June or July." The woman is already at the rack end looking for something. "Give me your long bar and I will lift the gravel at the foot of the wall and put it back at the front, and you will be able to sweep it, and neither of us will be back here in a month because of it."

---

It takes the best part of the morning and it is harder than turning them over.

She levers each one up on the long bar and walks it over to the wall on its edge, and the woman backs it in until its bottom stands a hand's width clear of the brick, and drops a lump of concrete behind that bottom so it cannot go over. When four of them are up she goes along them and puts her weight on the top of each in turn, and then she puts a finger in between each pair and works it in to about the second joint and leaves it there.

They are better than flat. She says so standing in the middle of the yard looking at the corner. You can see down between them, and the air goes up in there, and the black on the two faces of every one of them is already a different colour from the black on the ground side.

"I will leave them like that."

"Leave them like that." The woman takes her coat off the rail. "And you will not put the polythene back over them, and you will not weight them, and you will stack nothing against that wall."

"I will not put anything on the top of them."

"Then they are done."

They have done the outsides. She puts her hand into the first gap between two pallets, level with the ground, and the faces either side of it are wet where they were an hour ago.

---

The woman is gone before she gets to it. She works her hand up the gap, past the two bottom boards of each pallet and into the space between the blocks, and then she does the same in the second gap.

They are the same in all four gaps. The boards that make the underside of a pallet are drying and will go on drying, and so are the boards that make the top of it. The air is going up the outside of each pallet and down between the blocks in the middle, and it is the air in the middle that has been sat in the dark since June, and that is where the water is.

A pallet is not a board. It is a dozen boards and nine little blocks, and the blocks are sawn out of the middle of the log with the pith still in them, and the pith is the wettest thing there is in a pallet.

She stands in her own yard at midday with four pallets up against her own wall that are better than they were this morning, and every block in the middle of each of them is as wet as it was in June.

Then she puts her hands into the first gap and works them up it. The air in there is dry and cold, and the two faces either side of it have gone from black to grey as far as her waist. Above her waist they are still black.

She goes along the other three and it is the same in all of them, and at the top of every gap, up where a hand of hers will not reach, there is nothing she can do at all.

She leaves the four pallets standing on edge against the wall, and goes and gets the stiff brush and scrapes the wall behind them where the stack has been leaning since June, because there is a mark on that wall and it is hers and she can reach it.
