# Chapter 0378 — The Half-Landing

Friday the twenty-second of December 2017, from about half past seven in the morning until about half past nine at night, a block of flats off a side turning, and a landing in it, in Tideglass.

**A LIGHT ON A LANDING IN A BLOCK OF EIGHT FLATS WENT ON AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE ON THE AFTERNOON OF THE TWENTY-SECOND OF DECEMBER AND USED TO GO ON AT ABOUT HALF PAST FIVE, AND THREE PEOPLE IN THAT BUILDING NOTICED IT INSIDE A WEEK, AND NOT ONE OF THE THREE SAID A WORD ABOUT IT TO THE MAN WHO HAS THE KEYS TO THAT BUILDING, AND THAT MAN WALKED UP THAT STAIR ON THE MORNING OF THE TWENTY-SEVENTH WITH HIS OWN HAND ON THE WALL AND LOOKED UP AT IT, AND HE HAS NOT BEEN BACK, AND IT IS STILL GOING ON AT HALF PAST THREE.**

There are seven flats in that block, which was put together out of two of the old houses on that side, and it stands back off a side turning with a forecourt about as deep as a van, and there is a path round it and a strip of grass and a low wall along the front of that grass.

The stair is one flight and a half. It goes up from the passage at the end of the forecourt, turns once on a half-landing, and goes up again to a top landing with two flats on it.

There are three lights on that stair. They are batten fittings screwed up under the ceiling with a lamp in each, and the middle one is on the half-landing, and all three of them are on one clock in a cupboard on the ground floor, and that clock has run that stair since before anybody in that block came into it.

The woman who lives in the first flat has been in that block since March and comes in on the bus and comes in at about ten past six in the evening in December.

The man in his thirties was in that block for four days that week taking out a bath and putting a bath in the flat on the top landing, and he was up and down that stair all day every day of it.

The man in his sixties goes into that block about once a week to have a cup of tea in the second flat and has gone on doing so for years.

The man who has the keys to that building has had them since before the summer, does not live in it, and came into that block on the morning of the twenty-seventh to take away three pieces of an old bathroom suite that had been standing in the passage at the bottom of that stair since October under a sheet of hardboard with somebody's name on it in marker pen.

---

On the Friday the half-landing was in the dark at about half past four in the afternoon.

The woman who lives in the first flat had noticed it on the Wednesday. She had come in at about ten past six and had gone up that stair to put a plate in her own fridge, because there is a fridge in her kitchen and nothing else in that flat that wants a plate, and she had gone up into the dark and had felt along the wall for the switch and had put the plate in and had come back down in the dark.

She had come in on the Monday and it had been light. She had come in on the Tuesday and it had been light. She had come in on the Wednesday and it had been dark, and she did not go to the cupboard on the ground floor and did not look at anything and did not say anything to anybody about it.

On the Friday she was in at about ten past six and there was no light on that half-landing, and she said out loud, to herself, on the bottom step, that it was early.

The man in his thirties was up that stair on the Friday about six times between about eight in the morning and about four in the afternoon. The light was on every time he went up in the morning and off every time he came down in the middle of the day, and the fourth time he came down at about twenty past three in the afternoon it was still on, and the fifth time he came down at about a quarter to four it had gone.

He stood on the bottom step at about a quarter to four that afternoon and looked up at the middle fitting for a while and then went out to his van.

He had a thing in his mind about the lamp in it. That fitting has a lamp in it and the other two have lamps in them and the middle one is the one that was replaced in the summer, and a lamp like that, when it is going, does not go out clean; it goes out and then comes back on after a minute or two, and a man who has had two of them go does not put it past.

He had decided by the time he got in the van that it was the lamp.

The man in his sixties was on that half-landing at about half past three in the afternoon on the Friday coming up from the passage with a plate of biscuits in his hand, and he came up into the light and it came on while he was still on the bottom step.

He stood there. It is not a thing you can walk past once it has happened, and he is a man who has been up and down stairs in this borough for thirty years and who has watched a great many fittings come on.

That is the whole of what he took from it. He stood on that half-landing with his plate and looked at the fitting, and then he went up to the second flat and had his tea and did not mention it, and has not mentioned it to anybody.

What he took from it, if that is worth putting down, was the switch. There is a switch on the wall on that half-landing opposite the fitting and it is a rocker about two inch by one, and it is the switch for the middle light only, and he has pushed that switch about ten thousand times in his life and it has always come back.

Somebody has got a hand on that switch and held it down. That is what a man in his sixties thinks when a light on a landing comes on by itself, and he thought it, and he went up to the second flat.

---

Nobody has said which of those three things it is.

The woman who lives in the first flat thinks the clock has slipped. She has not been in that cupboard and has not looked at it, and she has not said so to anybody, and if she were asked she would say she thought the clock had slipped, and she would be guessing.

The man in his thirties thinks the lamp in that fitting is going. He came back into that block on the Monday morning, because there was something else to do up there and he was coming back anyway, and he was on that half-landing at about eight and the light came on, and he stood and looked at it, and it is not his fitting and not his building and not his lamp, and he has said nothing about it to anybody and has not thought about it since the Monday.

The man in his sixties thinks somebody has been holding that switch down. He has never seen anybody holding that switch down and there was nobody on that half-landing when the light came on but himself.

Three explanations, three people, and not one of them said their explanation out loud in that building, and not one of them said the other two were possible either.

The man who has the keys came in on the Monday morning of the twenty-seventh at about half past nine and went to the passage at the bottom of that stair and shifted that old suite apart and got hold of the cistern end and had it down through the passage and out across the forecourt to his van, and it took him about ten minutes.

Coming back in with the empty hardboard he went up that stair. He put his hand on the wall on the up side because the wall on that side is cold. He was on the half-landing with the hardboard under his arm and he looked up at the middle fitting as he went past it, and that is all he did, because he was not asked about it and it had not occurred to him and there are three or four other things in that building he would be told about if anybody said anything.

He put the hardboard back and shut the passage and went out. He has not been in that block since and is not going to before the weather turns.

The clock is in a cupboard on the ground floor and it has not been looked at by anybody in that block since before the summer, and the switch on that half-landing still comes back, and the light is on that landing at about half past three in the afternoon and is off again by a quarter to four, and the woman who lives in the first flat has been up that stair in the dark on four evenings since and has said early out loud on the bottom step and to nobody else.

**THE WOMAN WHO LIVES IN THE FIRST FLAT SAYS THAT EARLY IN THE DARK IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT SHE KNOWS ABOUT THAT LANDING, AND THE MAN IN HIS THIRTIES SAYS NOTHING AT ALL ABOUT IT BECAUSE IT IS NOT HIS, AND THE MAN IN HIS SIXTIES SAYS NOTHING ABOUT IT BECAUSE HE HAS ALREADY DECIDED WHAT A SWITCH LOOKS LIKE WHEN SOMEBODY HAS BEEN HOLDING IT DOWN, AND NONE OF THE THREE HAS TOLD THE MAN WITH THE KEYS, AND HE IS NOT GOING TO BE TOLD.**
