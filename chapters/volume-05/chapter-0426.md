# Chapter 0426 — Half an Hour

Tuesday the tenth of July 2018, from about half past one in the afternoon until the light had gone off the roofs, one length of that parade and a bit of a street off it, in Tideglass.

**HE PUT HIS HAND ON IT AT TEN PAST TWO ON THE TUESDAY AFTERNOON AND STOOD THERE, AND HE HAD WALKED PAST IT A HUNDRED TIMES, AND HE LEFT IT EXACTLY WHERE IT WAS AND TOLD NOBODY, AND NOTHING HAS HAPPENED EITHER WAY AND NOBODY IS GOING TO BE TOLD.**

There is a house on the west side of that parade with a gable end about eighteen foot high and no downpipe on it at all, going down to a low wall and a gate and about four foot of forecourt.

Against the foot of that gable, on the flags, there is a stack of cast iron about four foot long and two foot high and about a foot and a half deep. It is old rainwater goods and it came off the roof of the house next door in the first week of July 2017, when a man and a lad took a roof off that house for a reroof and put the old material out along that wall for collection.

Nothing ever came for it.

There is a sheet of polythene over the top of it, weighed at the four corners with lumps of the guttering itself, and the sheet has gone brown and brittle where the sun has been on it and one corner has blown away and is lying in the gutter across the road. Under the sheet the iron has gone to a grey bloom in patches and a couple of the joints have wept rust down onto the flags in two brown streaks about a foot long.

There are four sections of downpipe socketed into each other at the bottom, and about nine foot of half-round guttering on top of that, and a hopper head, and two elbows, and a length of the guttering about a foot from the near end is cracked clean through where somebody put a stack of scaffold boards on it in the autumn of 2017 and has not moved the battens since.

A man in his forties has walked past that stack twice a week since July 2017 without ever once stopping.

---

He came up that parade at about ten past two on the Tuesday with an empty van and no job booked into it, which happens to him about twice a month and which is the only time in the week that he is in a street with nothing to do in it.

He was going past and he slowed down and stopped, and he put his left hand flat on the top length of guttering on the stack, where it was under the sheet and in the shade.

The iron was cold at the near end and about four degrees warmer where his hand was, and it was a shape his hand already knew, because he has been fitting this iron by hand for twenty-two years and this particular half-round is the size that fits the rafter on every house on that side of the street.

He left the sheet where it was. He did not lift it, and he did not put it back, and he did not move the lumps that were holding it at the corners.

He stood there with his hand on it for about ten minutes.

In that ten minutes three things went past him on that parade. A woman came out of the house next door with a dog that did not want to go, stopped on her own step about eight foot away, looked at him and then at his hand on the stack, and said nothing at all, and went back in, and shut the front door behind her without shutting it hard. A man went by on a bicycle at about the speed of a man who is late. A lorry came through from the bank end faster than anything on that parade goes — only the air off it told him, not the sound — and the air off it moved the brittle corner of that polythene sheet about a foot and a half and dropped it, and he put his hand flat on it and pushed it back down under the lump of iron that had been sitting on it.

That is the whole of what happened in the ten minutes. Nobody spoke to him. Nobody asked him what he was doing. Nobody has ever seen a man stand with his hand on a stack of iron on that parade and thought anything about it, because there was nothing to see.

While he was standing there he worked out three things, and he was right about all three of them.

The first is that the man who took that roof off went to work on a bridge out past the town eight miles off in the March of this year and has not been in this street since, and that he would not be back, and that the house next door is a woman's and she has had two people in the house about once a month since the June and does not want the front of it full of iron.

The second is that the gutter at that stack is not his and never was his and never could be his, and that if he put that iron in his own van and drove it away this afternoon, then in about six weeks there would be a woman at a door saying a stack had gone, and there would be nothing to find and nothing to say to her, and he would be the man it went from.

The third is that it will be gone before the autumn. That is not a guess. The sheet has gone brittle and the corner is off it and there is a man who buys up iron for a yard in the town eight miles off who comes into town twice a week and who has been into that parade three times since the June, and that man saw that stack the first time and has not come back, and he will come back.

And being right about all three of those was worth exactly nothing at all.

---

He got into his own van at about twenty past two and shut the doors and sat in the driver's seat with them shut, which is a thing he does perhaps twice a month, and which is not a habit, because a habit needs somebody to have noticed it and not one person ever has.

He took his coat off. He took a paper out of the door pocket and looked at it, and it was a list of four things he has to buy that day, and he put it back in the pocket in the order it was in, which is the order it was in.

He sat for about half an hour with the engine off and the windows up.

Here is what went through in that half an hour, and none of it came out of his mouth, and none of it will come out of it now.

It is not a discovery. He had known that stack was there since the July of 2017. He had walked past it and seen it and had not stopped and had not thought about it, in the way that a man does not think about a wall, and the thing that had happened at ten past two was not that he found out something. It was that his hand went onto it.

In the first week of July 2017 a man called Briggs had said, "You want any of this gutter, there'll be a bit of money in it for you." And he had said no. And he had said, "I'll come back for it next week." And he had known at the moment he said it that he was not going to, and he had said it anyway, and the reason he had said it was that a man cannot say no twice in the same minute and expects to be asked again, and he did not want to be asked again, and he had got what he wanted out of the second sentence.

He has not been offered a thing of Briggs's in about a year and has not thought about Briggs once.

And nobody is going to be impressed. Nothing that happens on that parade this afternoon will ever be described to anybody as an impressive thing, or as a thing at all, and the one person standing at the foot of it with his hand on it will not be told a word about it by anybody, and there is no question here that anybody has to answer, because nothing has been found out and nothing has gone wrong and there is nobody in a position to want anything done about it.

He put the engine in at about ten to three and went and did a job in a house off that same street that took him two hours, and at about half past five he came back out of that street and went up the parade past the gable end at the speed he goes past everything, and the stack was there, and the polythene was where he had put it back.

He came back up that parade on the Thursday, which was the day he had said he would, and on the Sunday morning, which was not a day he had said anything about.

**HE DROVE AWAY FROM IT AT TWENTY TO SIX AND DID NOT TELL THE WOMAN BEHIND THAT COUNTER OR THE MAN IN THE THIRTIES OR ANYBODY ELSE IN THIS BOROUGH, AND THE STACK OF IRON IS STILL AGAINST THAT GABLE END, AND HE HAS NOT DECIDED ANYTHING.**
