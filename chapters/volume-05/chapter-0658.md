# Chapter 0658 — The Back Pocket Of A Jacket On A Hook

Tuesday the third of August 2021, in this borough. He is in a unit off the industrial estate from about half past seven in the morning until about ten, and in a shop on the ring road at about ten, and the whole of it is a wire brush that he puts in a jacket pocket and cannot account for, and a second wire brush that he pays for with his own money and puts in a drawer in his own van.

**A MAN GOES INTO A UNIT AT HALF PAST SEVEN IN THE MORNING BECAUSE HE IS DOING A GATE THAT AFTERNOON AND HE HAS NO BRUSH, AND HE FINDS THE ONE HE LEFT ON A STRANGER'S GATE IN THE BACK POCKET OF A JACKET ON A HOOK, AND HE CANNOT WORK OUT HOW IT GOT INTO A POCKET, AND THEN HE BUYS A NEW ONE WITH HIS OWN MONEY BECAUSE THE OTHER ONE IS SOMEBODY ELSE'S, AND HE PUTS THE NEW ONE IN A DRAWER UNDER THE BENCH IN HIS VAN AND DOES NOT SHUT THE DRAWER, AND HE DOES NOT GO UP THAT ROAD.**

He is a man in his early forties. He is on his own in the unit. There is a man behind a glass case in a shop for about ninety seconds of the morning.

---

Here is what he came in for, and it is worth putting first because it is small and because everything else in the morning comes out of it.

The gate.

The drops on the back of the frame at number twelve are one solid lump of old gloss. He said so in a kitchen on the Friday of the sixteenth of July and he put a figure on it, and the figure is wrong, and the woman in her late forties said *do it*, and he said not that day, and it is now the first proper Tuesday he has had since the end of July.

He has the chisel. He has had the chisel since June.

He has not got a brush.

---

And here is the thing about a gate and a brush, and it is the thing he would have to explain to anybody and cannot.

You take the lump off. That is a chisel and about nine minutes and it is the whole of the actual work. And then there are four inches of loose old gloss left on the back of the frame where the lump was, and if you hang the gate back on that, the gate binds in the cold, and the woman in her late seventies has to have somebody back, and she will not have anybody back.

And you do not blow it off. You brush it off. With a wire brush, and you brush it into the gravel and you put your boot in the gravel and turn it.

And there is no way to do that with a rag.

---

So he is in the unit at about half past seven on the Tuesday morning of the third of August 2021 with a chisel in his hand and nothing to brush with.

And the bag is in the footwell of the van outside.

And the first thing he does is go out to the van for the bag.

---

He is in and out of that van four times before about eight o'clock, and here is the whole of it, and it takes about a minute and a half in total.

The bag is in the footwell, where it has been since the Wednesday of the nineteenth of May, and it is a canvas bag with a leather bottom and there is a stone in it that he has never found, and everything in it is his and everything in it is where he put it.

The bag is not in the van.

The bag is on the bench in the unit, on the top of it, on the end nearest the wall, because he took it out of the van on the Friday and put the four treads and a handrail in it and did not put it back.

And there is no brush in the bag.

And then he does the van, which is a thing he has never had to do in nineteen years of having a tool bag: the footwell, under the seat, behind the seat, the door pockets, the sun visor, the parcel shelf, the box under the parcel shelf, and the gap down the side of the middle seat where a chisel has gone missing twice.

And there is no brush in the van either.

---

And then he stands in the middle of the unit at about ten past eight with a shoebox on the top of the bench in front of him, and the shoebox is a stove element in a box with one letter on the side of it in his own pencil, and he cannot come in here without looking at it, and he has not come in here without looking at it since June.

And he says it out loud to an empty unit, and this is the only sentence he says all morning that is not a figure of a price or a name of a part.

He says, "Where is it."

---

And then, because a man who has looked in a van and a bag and a bench eventually goes to the one place he has not looked, and because in nineteen years there have been two jackets on the back of that unit on two hooks and both of them are his, he goes to the back of the unit where they hang.

There is a hook with a work jacket on it. There is a hook with no jacket on it, because the other jacket is in the van.

He puts his hand in the back pocket of the one on the hook.

---

And it is there.

A wire brush. Ash handle, nine inches, a crimp about three-quarters of an inch deep, and the wires at the ends of it are splayed, and there is a curl of dried gloss in the bottom of the crimp the colour of a bit of old egg, and it has got a bit of grit in it from a gravel drive.

And he stands in the back of a unit with his hand in a pocket and does not take it out for about four seconds.

---

And here is the thing that takes him the rest of the morning, and it is not a mystery, and he has not got anywhere with it by the time he shuts the unit up, and it is the only thing in the business that has ever properly beaten him.

A thing cannot get into a pocket.

That is not a joke and it is not a thought. He knows what a jacket is. It hangs on a hook on the back wall of a unit and it has been on that hook since the Thursday of the first of July and the door of that unit is locked when he is not in it, and the window is fixed and the alarm is on and there is a key on his belt and nobody else has a key.

And a wire brush does not climb.

And a jacket on a hook does not reach into a pocket and take a brush out of a van and put it in.

---

And he gets there in the end, and it takes him about four minutes at the bench, and it is this.

He put it there.

Not somebody. Him. Him at about a quarter to one in the afternoon on the twentieth of July on a set of steps on the ring road, on his knees, with a tread in one hand and a chisel in the other and the bag over a wall rail out of his reach, and he stood up, and he went into the side pocket, and he put it in the back pocket of the jacket he had on because the pocket was there.

And then he did the treads, and he did the handrail, and he put the bag in the van, and he drove home, and he took the jacket off and hung it on the hook in the unit because it is where it goes, and he did not empty the pockets, because a man does not empty the pockets of a jacket he has been kneeling in all afternoon.

And it has been on that hook since the twentieth of July. And the van has been outside. And the tool bag has been on the bench. And the brush has been in the jacket the entire time, and it is the only object in this borough that has been in the right place the whole time and looked in the wrong one first.

---

And he does not find that funny, and he stands there long enough for it to occur to him that he could find it funny, and then he thinks about the gloss in the crimp, and that is the end of that.

---

And then here is the decision, and he makes it standing at the bench, and it takes him about a minute, and it is the whole of what the morning is for.

He is not going to carry that brush up that road.

He cannot work out a sentence for it. He has had since Friday to find one and he has not found one and the reason he cannot find one is not that he does not know what happened, because he knows exactly what happened and he worked it out in four minutes at a bench. The reason is that a wire brush with a stranger's gloss in it is not a tool he can turn up with.

It is not his. It belongs to a gate at the end of a gravel drive on a road off a bigger road and to an old man with a mug in his hand, and there is no version of that in which he puts it in a pocket and takes it up a path to a woman of seventy-eight and works on her gate with it.

That is the whole of it and it took him a minute and it is not a reason anybody could check.

---

So he puts it in the bag.

He puts it in the side pocket of the bag with the chisel, and the bag is on the bench with the shoebox on the top of it, and there is a stone in the bag that he has never found, and there is now a wire brush in it that belongs to a gate that is not his.

And he does not feel good about that and he does not feel bad about it either, and what he feels is that he has put a thing back where it came from, which is not the same as either of those.

---

And then he goes out to the van and gets a bit of paper to write a job on, because it is the Tuesday of the third of August and he has nothing on the diary for the afternoon, and he is standing at the tailgate with a pen in his hand about a quarter to ten, and this is where the morning turns over.

He has a chisel. He has a van. He has a road with a gate on it at the end of it about a mile and a half from where he is standing.

And he does not go.

---

And the reason takes him the whole of the standing there to get to the bottom of, and he gets there.

A woman in her late forties said *I'll do your gate* in a kitchen with him in it on the Friday of the sixteenth of July.

She has not been up that road since. He knows that because he has driven past the end of it four times since the Friday and the light in that kitchen has been on every time and there has been no white van on it and no blue roll-up and no woman on a step with a chisel in her hand.

And if he goes up there this afternoon with his own money's brush in his own hand, then that is a man who has taken the gate off a woman who put her hand over her mouth in a kitchen to say she would do it.

And nobody is going to say anything about that. She will not say anything about that. She cannot say no to him, and she cannot say no to anybody, and that is the whole of what is wrong with that house, and if he walks up that path this afternoon he takes away the only thing that woman has offered anybody since the spring and there is no way on earth to put it back.

---

And he cannot tell her. That is the other half. He cannot ring her and say *don't do it, I'll do it*, because she will say *right*, and she will not do it, and she will not have thanked anybody for anything, and she will have given up the thing she gave.

And he cannot ring her and say nothing, because there is nothing in that conversation that is not a lie.

And he cannot go up there and not do it, because he would be in the passage.

And he cannot write it on a card and leave it on the step, and he does not do that, and the reason he does not do that is that she would open it in a kitchen with the woman in and she would not be able to ask anybody what to do about a card on a step, and there is nobody in that house she can ask.

---

So he stands at the tailgate of his own van on the Tuesday morning of the third of August 2021 with a pen in his hand and he does not write a job down.

And then, because a man standing at a tailgate with nothing on has to do something with his hands, he goes and buys a brush.

---

The shop is on the ring road and it is one of those places with a glass case along the side and a yard out the back, and he has been in it maybe a dozen times and he has never once been in it for a brush.

And there is a galvanised bucket by the door with brushes standing up in it in sawdust and a card on a wire stuck in the top of the handles, and he looks in the bucket for about two seconds and does not touch it, because everything in it is a wall brush, and he can see the crimp on all of them from the door and every one of them is soft and short and stamped with the name of a town off the ring road.

And he goes along the rack instead, which is by the window, and on the rack there are the proper ones, and he picks one with a long ash handle and a crimp you could get a knuckle into, and it is about nine pounds, and he pays for it out of his own pocket with a fiver and gets change.

And the man behind the glass says, "That one'll do anything you want."

And he says, "Right."

---

And here is what he is not doing in that shop, and it takes him about two seconds, and if anybody had been watching him it would have taken longer.

He does not think about the woman in her late forties. He has not thought about her since the Friday and he is not going to think about her in a shop on a ring road, and there is a bucket by the door about a yard from where he is standing and she has stood where he is standing.

And that is the whole of what the two of them have in common, and it is a bucket by a door on a ring road, and neither of them will ever know it was there.

---

He is back at the van at about ten past ten with the brush in a plastic bag in his hand.

And then he does the thing that ends the morning, and he does it in about four seconds and he does not think about it while he is doing it.

The drawer is under the bench on the passenger side. It is the drawer with the extension leads and the old bits of trim and a tin of screws in it. He has never shut that drawer since the van was new because there is nothing in it that a person needs shut.

He opens it and puts the brush in it, in the plastic bag, flat, along the back of it, and he takes his hand out.

And he does not shut it.

---

And that is what a man has in his van on the Tuesday of the third of August 2021 and it is not a tool bag and it is not a chisel and it is not a gate.

A drawer that will not shut, with nine pounds' worth of wire brush in it, bought with his own money, at a quarter to ten in the morning, for a gate at the end of a path he is not going up.

And in the footwell, in a canvas bag with a leather bottom and a stone in it that he has never found, there is a wire brush that belongs to a stranger and has a curl of old egg-coloured gloss in the bottom of the crimp and will never touch a gate in this borough either.

And the gate at number twelve is the first thing on a piece of paper in pencil in a kitchen on a road off the parade, and it has a woman in her late forties who said she would do it and a man in his early forties with a brush in a drawer who has worked out that he cannot go up that path with it, and neither of them has said one word to the other one about any of it, and the gate is waiting for a Saturday, and a man has not booked a Wednesday and is not going to.

(End of chapter 0658)
