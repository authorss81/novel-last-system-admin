# Chapter 0409 — The Brass Sole

Monday the seventh of May 2018, beginning at about a quarter past seven in the morning and finishing at about half past five in the afternoon, a passage and a back room in a block off a side street, in Tideglass.

**A MAN IN HIS FORTIES WENT INTO THE BACK ROOM OF A MAN IN HIS SIXTIES ABOUT TWENTY PAST NINE IN THE MORNING TO FIND A STOP TAP AND CAME OUT WITH A THIRTEEN INCH DOVETAIL LEVEL OFF THE SHELF BY THAT WINDOW, AND AT HALF PAST FOUR THAT AFTERNOON THE MAN IN HIS SIXTIES WENT INTO HIS OWN ROOM AND LOOKED ROUND IT TWICE AND SAID NOTHING TO ANYBODY AND HAS NOT ASKED.**

That block is four flats, and you go in off the street through a passage the width of one person. The passage is the width of one person with the meter cupboard on the left and the stair going up off the end of it.

The man in his sixties has had that ground floor since about 2009. There is a front room off that passage with a bay, and behind it, at the back of that ground floor, there is a back room about ten foot by nine with a window on the yard.

That back room has not been used as a room since about 2014. It has a cold concrete floor and a fireplace in it that is bricked up and a press bed frame in it with a mattress on it that is a mattress from about 1992.

He is clearing it in May. He is doing it in his own time, on his own, in about four goes, and he is taking the lot to a tip in his own car, and he is seventy-one and it takes him a whole morning to do a go.

He has not asked anybody for money and has not been given any, and he has not asked anybody to help him lift anything, and nobody in that block has offered.

---

The man in his forties took a load out of that room on the Thursday of the twelfth of April, in his own van, and was given a tenner for the petrol, and that is the whole of the money in it and there is nothing else and nobody put a name on it.

The man in his thirties took two bags out of that room in the second week of March, on his own, on foot, down that passage and out into the street, because he had a bag of plugs in the van he was not using and because the man in his sixties asked him to and he was in that street anyway.

Neither of them has done one other thing in that room and neither of them is on anything.

On the shelf by that window in that room there is a level.

It is a dovetail level. It is about thirteen inch long and three and a quarter wide and an inch and a quarter deep, and the stock is boxwood and has gone the brown of an old cricket ball, and there are brass plates let into the top face and the bottom face with three small screws in each, and the brass has gone green where the two plates meet, and the plate over the vial is gone and the vial itself is there and it is a glass tube about three inch long in a brass block, and there is a thumbwheel on the side of the block that moves it.

It has been on that shelf since about 1990, and before that it stood on a shelf in a room in a different house four miles off.

Nobody in that block knows what it is or whose it was. It is not on the list of the room, because there is no list, and the man in his sixties has never mentioned it to anybody and has never thought about it.

The man in his sixties was out of that house from about half past seven that morning on the Monday.

The man in his forties was let in at about a quarter past eight by the man in his thirties, who was in that passage at about eight with a bag of screws for a job at the second house in that street, and who had found the passage door on the latch the way it is most mornings, and let him in, and went on up the street.

The man in his forties was there about two hours over the Monday. He had the bottom tread off that ground-floor flight and back on by about half past nine, and that took him the best part of an hour and a half, and he was on his own the whole of it.

At about twenty past nine he went into the back room to find a stop tap.

The cold water to the first-floor bathroom comes off a pipe that runs in the duct between that bathroom and that back room, and the stop tap is behind the plaster in the back room, and a woman who lives in that first-floor flat had said on the Monday morning in a shop off that parade that the cold had gone cold in the bath and then gone cold again in about four minutes, which is a stop tap at the end of a run and not a stopcock, and she had asked him whether he would look.

It is a quarter turn of a copper stopcock in a plaster box about ten inch off that floor, and the plaster box has been opened and made good twice and he got it open in about ten minutes and turned the tap off and took about a minute off the top of the pipe and put a new washer in it and put the plaster box back.

He was in that room for about half an hour in all, and about ten minutes of that he was stood up with his back to that window and his bag on the floor.

He did not go to that shelf. He turned round at some point because the window is behind him and it is a bright one and it was in his eyes, and it was on turning round that he saw that level on the shelf by that window.

It was the only thing on that shelf. He looked at it for about two seconds.

On the Monday morning he had a job at the first house in that street on the Tuesday morning, and it was a stair carriage that somebody else's man had fitted in about 2011 out of true by about a quarter of an inch, and a stair carriage that is out of true in a staircase that is walked on every day in that house will go on getting worse, and he had put his own level in his own bag and put that bag in his own van on the Monday night, and that van is standing in a yard in another town and does not come back until the Wednesday.

He picked it up off that shelf with one hand and put it in his bag, and he did not look at the wall behind it, and he did not touch anything else in that room, and he shut the door of that room and did the tap and went and finished that stair on the Tuesday morning with it.

It is in his own van now. It lives on the shelf above the wheelarch on the driver's side with four other things on it, and it is in his hand most days, and it is not the best level in this borough and it is better than the one he owns.

The man in his sixties came back into that house at about half past four that afternoon.

He had been out since half past seven. He had been to a hospital appointment in town and had waited about two hours and had got a bus back.

He came in the front door of that ground floor, and the man in his forties had gone and his tools were gone and the bottom tread was back on that flight and the flight was not the way he found it and he did not go and look at it.

He went into the front room and stood in it for about a minute. Then he went into that back room.

He stood in the doorway.

He looked round that room. There is not much in it. The press bed frame, the mattress, the bricked-up fireplace, the concrete floor, a small table with nothing on it but an old flex, and the shelf by the window with nothing on that either.

He was in that doorway for about a minute and a half.

Then he went out into the passage and stood there for a bit, and then he went back in and stood in the doorway again and looked round it for about the same length of time.

What he had looked at the second time, and what he looked at both times, was the wall behind where that shelf is.

There is a pale rectangle on that plaster about thirteen inch by three and a quarter, and it is the cleanest thing on that wall, and it is clean because for about twenty-five years a level was standing on its edge against that wall and nothing else ever was, and the dust on the rest of that wall is about the thickness of a sheet of paper and there is none of it inside that rectangle.

He looked at that rectangle for about ten seconds on the second pass.

Then he went out of that room and shut the door and went and made his tea.

He has not raised the level or the rectangle or the room with anybody. Nobody has asked him about it. Neither of the two men has been asked about it. He has not stood in that doorway since. He has been in that back room four times since, for a bulb and a bag of picture hooks and two more goes with the car, and he has not looked at that shelf and he is not going to.

Put to him, that room is one he is emptying and he has never once written down what is in a room, and he is not starting.

The man in his thirties has not been in that back room since the second week of March. He carried two bags down from it and stood in it for about ten minutes and that level was on that shelf the whole of that time and he did not look at it and he has not thought about it since. He was in that passage at about eight that Monday and he let the man in his forties into that house and has not mentioned it since and is not going to.

**HE CAME OUT OF THAT ROOM WITH A LEVEL IN HIS BAG AND THE DOOR SHUT BEHIND HIM, AND A PALE RECTANGLE ABOUT THIRTEEN INCH WIDE IS ON THE WALL OF THAT ROOM AND NOBODY HAS ASKED HIM ABOUT IT AND HE IS NOT GOING TO ASK ANYBODY ABOUT IT.**
