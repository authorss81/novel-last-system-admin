# Chapter 1012 — A Person In The Room

Undated in this draft.

**THE KITCHEN AT NUMBER NINE HAS ITS RANGE LIT AFTER SIX WEEKS COLD.**

The kitchen at number nine has been the same room for fifty years and has one range, one table with the edge worn through to the wood at two places, and a door that hangs on a hinge at the top so that when there is a fire in the range the door stands in about two inches on its own and bangs unless there is something under it. There is a stone under it now. The hearth of that range is set back from the front edge of it by about three inches on the left and is flush with it on the right, which is not square with its own front and never has been, and the difference is where the soot collects when the door bangs. She has had the range lit since she came back, which is the first time it has been lit since the spring, and the kitchen has been warm for four days, and she has been in it more in those four days than in the six weeks before them.

There is a thing about that kitchen nobody in that house would put into words and he put into words on his knees in front of the range with a handful of somebody else's fire in his hand. A woman of her age in that borough, with a range and a table and a door that stands in on its own when there is a fire in it, has about as much in the way of heat as a person in a shed, and she has been away six weeks in rooms where the heat was somebody else's, and she came home to this one and lit it on the first night and has not let it go out since. He has been in more houses on this frontage than there are doors on it, in ten years and he has never once come in to a fire that somebody had made for him.

He came in the yard door with his coat over his arm because it was still going, and she was standing at the table with her back to the fire and a cup in front of her that she had not been drinking out of. She did not offer him anything, which at number nine has never once meant she was not glad to see him. She had been back to the lock-up since he was there and she had stood in front of the open book on the table in the middle bay and had read the column, and the fire had been in the range behind her the whole time she was doing it, and he could see by her face that she had come to a figure.

"Do not tell me what it cost," and she put her cup down before he had got the last of it out.

"I did not think you were going to bring it up either." She put her cup down. "That is what I have been working at for two days, and you have walked in and done the first half of it before I had my coat off my back. So now you can have the other half of it, which is that I know the price and I am going to pay it."

"What price."

"Six weeks."" She turned round and put her hands behind her on the rail. "The six weeks I did up the water in the spring go on that book, and the only way my own name comes off it is when a season finishes, and this one finishes on Friday, and if I am still down on it when that happens then the six weeks they did this spring are down there as well, and a person who has been on a book for fifty years finds out nothing else whatever when she finally asks."

---

He put his hand into the ash pan under that range and took out a handful and let it go, and what stayed on his fingers was fine and grey and had no warmth in it at all. He had put six months of his own soot down that range in his mother's kitchen and had never once known what a person's fire looked like from underneath it, and there was a whole order of things in this borough that he has learned standing up and not once sitting down.

"You have not asked me why I have not brought it up," she went on.

"No."

"You have not asked me because you already worked it out, and because if you ask I have to put it into words, and because you have never once in ten years asked anybody for a thing you had worked out." She came the length of the table and moved the stone out from under the range door and put it back again, which she did not need to do. "So I will put it into words, because I have worked at this for four days and I would rather have put it out straight."

"And I am not going up there on my own two hands, and I am not going to ask you for one word out of you at all. I am asking you to be in the room on Friday morning when it shuts. That is all of it. I want a person in the room while it happens."

He stood in that warm kitchen and worked out that he had been asked for something that was not work. And he worked out a second thing while he was doing it, which is that this was the fourth woman in four weeks who had handed him something and told him what it was for, and that not one of the four had asked him to do anything a person could be asked about, and that the woman standing in front of the range had just described a room with three people in it and herself as the one going up the water and had done it while she was warming her hands at a stove that is not square to its own front edge and never has been. In ten years on that frontage nobody had ever got as far as that. They had hired him, tolerated him, paid him in coins out of a folded paper, given him a bed frame to take out and a page to put his name on and a wall he was never offered, and one woman had given him a list of six houses with a name underneath it, and not one of those was a thing he could not have refused. This was a thing he could not refuse either, and it was not a thing he could pay for, and there was no figure that could be set against it in any office in this borough. He has been entered against work five times in ten years and one of the five was his own fault, and nobody has ever yet found a way of putting yourself down for standing in a room.

"Then I will be in the room," he told her.

"You will be the only thing in that room that is not a woman who goes up the water." She put the stone back under the door and it took the weight of it and the door stood in on its hinge the way it stands in when there is a fire behind it. "And on Friday morning, when she has read the column out, you can put whatever you have come here to put into words, if there is anything of it, and if there is not, then I will have had a person in the room and that will have been mine, and you can go down that frontage in the afternoon and be a man who was there."

The range door at nine stands in on its hinge with the fire behind it and a stone under the corner of it, and it will go on standing in like that until the season shuts, because she has decided that this room is going to be warm every day of that week whatever it costs her to heat it.
