# Chapter 0847 — The Strip In The Rail

Monday the twenty-fifth of September 2023, in the store at the unit from first light until the place has gone too dim to see into, and the strip combed along the whole length of the rail is the whole of that Monday.

**THERE IS A BRUSH STRIP IN THE RAIL ON THE FRONT OF THE MIDDLE SHELF IN THAT STORE PACKED WITH DUST AND GRIT SINCE THE SPRING AND HE MEANS TO COMB IT CLEAN WHERE IT SITS WITHOUT LIFTING IT OUT, AND THE WOMAN FROM TWO DOORS DOWN PUSHES A BARROW WITH A FOLDED SHEET ON IT ACROSS THE YARD AND OUT THROUGH THE FAR GATE.**

That store is a shed with three shelves and a bench, and the middle shelf runs the width of the far wall with a rail screwed to its front edge, and set into a channel on the outer face of that rail is a strip of bristles about the length of the shelf.

The strip is there to catch. Dust that falls on a shelf goes to the front and should stop there, and the bristles do the stopping, pointing down in the channel with their ends an eighth of an inch below the top of it.

They have not stopped anything since about the middle of June. Grit off the yard has got down into the roots of them and packed there, and the strip now stops nothing and keeps what it has taken, and the whole length of it has the grey of a shelf that has been swept with the strip and not the brush.

He does not lift it out. Lifting means two screws and a screwdriver and it means the strip comes up with everything the channel has been collecting since the spring, and it goes back down into a bare channel with the grit loose on the shelf above it, and loose grit on a shelf is grit on the floor by the end of the week and grit on the floor is grit in the concrete by the end of the winter.

So he brushes it in place, and the order matters.

A soft brush first, on the shelf face and along the rail and down the outside of it, to take the loose film off and not to drive anything further into the roots. The film comes off in a grey sheet and floats rather than falls, and he wipes it off with a cloth as he goes because in a store that size it is everywhere within a minute if it is left.

Then the stiff one, and this is the slow part.

He works the bristles with the corner of a card, in short strokes from the root of each tuft out towards the mouth of the channel, so that what is in the roots comes forward to the edge instead of being pushed further in. A brush that works down into a strip drives grit into it, and grit that goes down into a strip comes out again only when the strip is off.

They come out forward. Each tuft stands up off the shelf face as the card goes through it, and grey grit rolls out of the roots and drops through the gap between the rail and the shelf below and lands on the shelf underneath, and he goes along the whole length twice.

At about the third foot from the left end it is different. There the bottom two rows of bristles have welded together over about a span of a hand, and what comes out is not grey dust but a cake, hard, the colour of the mortar off the wall, and it breaks off the roots in pieces the size of a thumbnail.

He works it out with the card all the same and does not force it, and where the tuft comes away with a piece of root in it he leaves the piece on the shelf rather than put it back where the bristle is gone, because a bristle with a hole in the tuft and grit packed in the hole catches less than it did, and pretending otherwise helps nothing.

The strip has done its work through a winter and a summer and it is not new. A strip that has been cleaned and combed and had a hand's width of the bottom row taken out of it is a strip that will do the winter.

The woman from two doors down comes into the yard at midday pushing a barrow with a folded sheet lying flat on it, goes straight across to the far gate and out of it, and the gate lets her through with about a foot to spare on one side.

She does not slow at the store door and the wheels are on the grass the whole way across, and he waits at the door until the gate has come to behind her.

He thinks about the rail while he works, how it is a strip of dead animal put across the front of a shelf to keep the dust on the shelf, and how the whole of a store is dust that has not yet found the floor. Everything in the store is fine on its own account and every one of them makes dust, and the rail is the one part of the shelving whose whole job is to catch it, and it cannot be seen doing anything at all while it is working.

Behind the rail, against the back board of the shelf, the wall is paler than the wall either side of it.

That band is the width of the rail and as tall as it, and it is paler because nothing has been in front of it since the rail went on, and there is dust on it a finger thick behind the rail where his hand cannot go.

He does not put a hand behind the rail. The shelf above carries sacks of sand and the rail is screwed to the shelf it is fixed to, and a hand behind a rail on a shelf that carries weight is how a shelf comes down on a person with no warning at all. It stays where it is and it is the one place in that store he will not reach this week.

The test is a finger along the bristles. Where they stand up off the shelf face and spring back against the nail, the strip is clean. Where they stay laid over along the channel there is still grit in the roots, and he finds three more places like that and works them again until the finger comes away springing.

He sweeps the shelf below and the shelf above, and puts the cloth away, and puts the card back in the tin where the card always is.

---

The soft brush goes on the shelf above, and the card goes back in the tin, and the cloth goes out on the line by the gate.

He stands in the door of the store when it has gone too dim to see into and looks at what is there. A rail with its strip combed and the bristles standing up the whole length of it, and behind the rail on the wall a band of paler plaster with a finger of dust on top of it.
