# Chapter 0162 — Nobody Asked Me

Wednesday the twenty-fourth of June 2015, from about eleven in the morning, a room off a corridor, second floor, a building in Tideglass.

**THE PAGE OF THE TWENTY-NINTH OF MAY IS IN A TRAY IN A ROOM IN THIS BOROUGH AND IT WAS READ ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF JUNE BY A PERSON WHO WAS NOT ASKED TO READ IT, AND THAT PERSON IS NOT JONAS MERCER AND HE WAS IN THE ROOM AND DID NOT READ IT. A page with a signature block at the bottom of it is an instrument, and the person who reads it is held to by it, and the first thing anybody does with a page that asks for something is find out who wanted it, and the second thing is find out who would lose. She did both, in about nine minutes, and she was right about both. It is not in force. Nothing on it has happened, and nobody who has read it is going to say what is on it, including the woman who wrote it.**

---

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE IN THAT CORRIDOR, at about eye height, beside a fire door, and it defines nothing and nothing on it defines *holder*, and it requires nothing of anybody:**

> **A PAGE LEFT IN A TRAY IN THIS BUILDING IS NOT CIRCULATED AND IS NOT ACTIONED.**
> **NO PERSON IS ASKED TO SIGN, RETURN, OR ANSWER ANY PAGE IN THIS BUILDING.**
> **ANY PERSON MAY TAKE A PAGE OUT OF A TRAY AND PUT IT BACK.**

---

**AMA BOATENG, 29.** She is a duty manager at a supermarket on the far side of the market and she does about forty-eight hours a week and the rest of the week she is at her mother's.

She came in at about five to eleven with a folded piece of paper in her hand with a bin rota on it that she had written herself, and she has a constituency of a dozen households on one street of terraces behind the market, and the reason she was in that building is that the collection on that street has not been for a fortnight, because two men are off and there is nobody to send, and the woman of about seventy-six at the top of that street has been putting her bin out at six in the morning anyway, every morning, for a fortnight, and the bin has not been emptied and she has stopped mentioning it.

**She is not on anything. She is not on a rota of a council and she is not on a list and she is not a member of a body that has not met since January. She wrote the bin rota herself, in her own hand, and she came in with it, and she has never in her life been asked to fill in a box.**

---

**She was let in by a person on a rota in that building and she was put in the room to wait, and the room is where the tray is.**

The councillor was at something else until about eleven. Nobody told Ama Boateng to sit down and nobody told her not to touch anything, and there is a desk and a chair and a jug of water and a tray on the desk with about four other things in it, one of which is a single sheet of paper with the twenty-ninth of May on it.

She was in that room for about twenty minutes with nothing to do. **And anybody who says a person should not have read it has not been in a room for twenty minutes with a tray on the desk.**

She picked it up. It is one page. It has a date on it. It has a signature block at the bottom and the signature block is empty, and it is not on anybody's desk for circulation and it is not on a clipboard and it is not in a folder with anything else in it.

She read it. She read it the way you read a thing that has been left in a tray, which is once, properly, and then she put it back in the tray, in the same place, at the same angle.

**What she then did is the whole of that morning and nobody had thought of it and she did it standing up.**

---

"**Who wanted it.**"

Councillor Iven Sore came in at about five past eleven with a folder under her arm and stopped in the doorway, and she has a constituency and a reason and a page in a tray, and there is not one thing she says about it that is wrong.

"**I am not going to tell you what is on it,**" she said. "**And I want to be very clear that that is not me being cagey. If I tell you then you are a person who knows, and a person who knows is a person somebody can ask. I have not got a way of protecting you from that and neither has anybody in this building.**"

"**I did not ask you what is on it.**"

"**No, you did not, and I have noticed, and I am going to assume for the next ten minutes that you did not ask me on purpose and I am going to answer the two questions you actually asked and then you are going to leave.**"

---

"**Somebody has asked for one thing to be decided once, for everybody, instead of nine times, street by street, by hand,**" Ama said. "**I can work that out from the shape of it. One page. A space at the bottom for a person to agree, and the space is empty. That is not a letter and it is not a complaint, because a complaint has a person on it, and this has got a space where the person who agrees goes. So somebody wants it done once, and wants it done by somebody, and the somebody is going to be one person and not nine.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**And they want it done once because doing it nine times is slow, and because nine people doing it themselves cannot be written down, and because a thing that is written down can be pointed at, and this building likes being able to point at things.**"

"**Yes, and I would rather you had worked that out than had me hand it to you, and I am not going to compliment you for it, because you did it in a room where you were not supposed to be sitting.**"

---

"**And who loses.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Not them. Whoever's on it doesn't lose anything, they've got somebody in a building now. The people who lose are the ones who have been doing it themselves.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Because here's what happens. If it gets decided once, then it gets done by somebody who works for somebody, and the people who live on my street can't ask for it any more, because asking for it is a thing you do to a person who is going to have to go and do it. And right now I can knock on the door of a woman of seventy-six at six in the morning and say *I'll take the bin out*, and that's it, and there's nothing in it, and nobody's asked me to. The day that stops happening on my street it stops because somebody has decided it happens for the whole city instead, and it will be better, and it will be faster, and it will not be something a person can be blamed for, and it will be something a person cannot ask for.**"

"**That is the whole of it, and you had it before I had put this bag down, and I did not tell you any of it.**"

---

**AND THEN SHE SAID THE ONE THING THAT NOBODY HAD THOUGHT OF, AND SHE SAID IT OUT LOUD, IN A ROOM, IN DAYLIGHT, AND IT IS NOT A FOURTH ANSWER AND IT IS NOT GOING ANYWHERE.**

"**Can I say the actual thing.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**The person who reads it is the person who has to do it.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**There is a space at the bottom of that for a person to agree to it, and it is empty, and nobody's going to be asked to sign it, and I have read it. That's it. That's all that's happened. From about ten to eleven this morning that page has got a reader in it, and the reader is me, and there is nowhere on that page to put that, and it is not going to go on it, and nobody is ever going to be able to look at that page and know that anybody in this borough has read it except the two of us.**"

She put her hands flat on the back of the chair in front of her.

"**And I would rather be a person who read it and said so than a person who read it and said nothing, because a person who read it and said nothing is a person who did something on their own in a room and kept it, and that is a much worse thing to be, and I've watched people be that, and I'm not doing it.**"

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT THE COUNCILLOR SAID, AND NOT ONE WORD OF IT WAS WRONG, AND SHE DID NOT IMPROVE IT.**

"**That is the correctest thing anybody has said in this office in about two years and I am not going to improve on it, because if I improve on it I turn it into a policy and it is not a policy, it is a woman with a bin rota in her hand.**"

"**I know.**"

"**And I cannot stop you having read it, and I would not if I could, and I am not going to pretend to you that there is a line in this building that says you shouldn't have. There is a tray and a piece of paper in it and no lock on the tray, and that is a design and not an accident, and I have been putting things in trays in this borough for a very long time and every single one of them has been read by somebody who was not asked to read it, and I have never once been in a position to do anything about that, and this morning I have still not been in a position to do anything about it.**"

"**So what do I do.**"

"**Nothing. There is nothing on that page for you to do and nobody is going to ask you to do any of it.**"

---

**AND ON THE THURSDAY MORNING AT ABOUT NINE O'CLOCK SHE TELEPHONED THAT BUILDING, AND SAID ONE SENTENCE, AND HUNG UP.**

"**I read the page in the tray on Wednesday morning at about ten to eleven. Nobody asked me to. I am telling you that so that it is not a thing that happened to a page.**"

And the person who took the call said thank you and wrote nothing down, because there is nowhere to write it, and because a person who rings a building and says *I read something and nobody asked me* is a person who has done the whole of it in one sentence and there is no form in this borough that could do it better, and the page did not move and is not in force and has not been in force at any point since the twenty-ninth of May.

---

Nobody refused anything and nothing was harmed, and a woman of about twenty-nine went back to her shift at about half past four.

**He was at the end of that room with nothing in his hands and he did not read the page and he was not asked about the page and he said nothing, and a page that asks for something in this borough has now been read by one person who was not asked to read it, she has said so out loud in a room in daylight, and he is not a reader of it.**

The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and ten days old. The card in the slot is a hundred and twenty-four days old and nobody has taken it out of it. The letter in the tray on the second floor of the Old Ward is a hundred and twenty-six days old and is under two other pieces of post.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a woman of twenty-nine read a page in a room she was not supposed to be sitting in and worked out who would lose from it and was right, and she has not been helped, and nobody is going to help her, and the page is not in force, and the only thing anybody did about it was say it out loud, and that is not a service and it is not going to become one.**
