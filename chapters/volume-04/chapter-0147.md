# Chapter 0147 — A Term Is Worth A Supply

Sunday the seventeenth of May 2015, from about twenty past six in the morning, four hundred yards from a corridor on level two at Saint Orra west; and from about seven o'clock, a waiting area off that corridor.

**The hour between six and seven is stood in once in the next three weeks and once is all it is getting. Nothing was found in it and nothing was discovered in it. Everything that is known about it was worked out in April, it is four months old, and it is not going to be improved on. He asked no question of anybody and nobody asked him anything.**

---

**AND FIRST THE REVERSAL, SAID ONCE, BECAUSE IT IS A THING HE HAS DONE BEFORE AND HE IS NOT GOING TO LET IT PASS.**

Two weeks before that he had said, at the end of a Saturday morning, at the end of a corridor, that he was not going to stand in that hour again, and he had said it the way he says the counting. Then he went out at twenty past six on the Sunday morning of the third of May and stood in it for about half an hour, and nobody saw him and nobody is going to be told, and he wrote nothing down about it, which is what he does.

He is in it again.

**He is not going to say why, because the reason is four months old and it is not a new one and it does not belong in a morning about water. A man who decides on a Wednesday that he will not be in a place on a Sunday is a man who is in that place on a Sunday. There is no instrument in this city for stopping him and there is nobody who could tell him to, and the only thing that has ever held the shape of this borough together is one man not being caught out.**

**And he has stopped counting them. He was at eight by the morning of the third of May and he is not going to write down the next one, because a count is a rota with one name in it, and a rota with one name in it is the thing he has spent nine months refusing to be, and the arithmetic was the last piece of him that was still a habit and it has stopped being a habit, and the counting has been right in every week of this year up to the week it stopped.**

---

At six o'clock the term stopped.

**A term is six days. It is read at ten o'clock on the Saturday night before the Sunday it starts on. The gate opens at seven on that Sunday. It expires at six the following Sunday. The Saturday nights are what are a week apart. It is six days long, and not seven, and not a week, and a person who has been in this borough a long time will get that right and a person who has not will get it wrong inside a month.**

The term that had been in force began at seven o'clock on a Sunday morning in May and it stopped at six o'clock on this one, and nobody in this borough has to be told that, and there is no date on the night it was read on, and there has not been a date on one of those nights in this city since the twenty-fourth of January and there is not going to be one.

At six o'clock on that Sunday morning there was nothing in force in this borough at all.

**And that is the hour, and it happens every Sunday, and it has no name here, and there is no date in this city for it, and it is the only hour of the week in which this borough requires nothing of anybody at all. It is not being found out again here. It is a floor that is not level, and there is no paragraph and no term, and the row on the board that is empty is not empty, and the register has no nineteenth entry with a bulb out on it, and all of that was true in April and is true in May and is not going to be said again by anybody.**

He stood in it for about half an hour. Nothing happened. A light came on in a car park he could not see into, the same as the last time, and it was nothing.

---

At seven o'clock the next one came in and the gate went up.

**Nobody in this borough was told that it had gone up. Nobody in this borough has ever been told that it goes up, and nobody in this borough has to be told, and there is nobody anywhere in this city who could go and find out whether it had.**

---

He went in at about ten past seven, which is not that hour and is not anything.

**There were about eleven people in the waiting area off that corridor, and none of them had come for a term, and none of them knew a term existed, and he counted them and put the number down, and it is a count of a room on a Sunday morning in a heat and it is not a count of anybody, and there is nowhere in this borough for a count like that to go, and there is not going to be anything.**

A woman of about seventy with a shopping bag on her knees. A man of about thirty with a boy of about seven, both of them holding a plastic folder. A delivery driver in a high-visibility jacket, asleep sitting up. Two women in market aprons, one of them about forty-five with a bag of ice in it that had gone soft and one of them about sixty. A man of about fifty in a dark suit, standing, who had been there since about six. A woman of about sixty with a folded blanket across her knees. A girl of about seventeen with a school bag. A woman of about twenty-eight asleep. A man of about sixty with a walking frame he was not going to use.

**The room is the coolest room in that building and the reason is a window at the end of a corridor that has been propped with a wedge of wood on a fire door since a man of sixty-one put it there in May two years ago, and the radiator under that window is off, and the door onto the landing is open, and the ground-floor waiting room at that clinic has no window at all and is about four degrees warmer and full to the door with people who got there earlier.**

The woman in the market apron about forty-five said, to nobody, that the floor of her unit had been wet on Saturday and that she had thrown stock away, and the other one said, "**I know. Mine went too. There was a man stood on a bucket.**"

"**Did he get anything for it.**"

"**No.**"

"**Right.**"

---

**AND THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE IN THAT BUILDING, at eye height on the wall of that corridor, and it defines nothing and requires nothing of anybody and nothing on it defines *holder*, and the two conditions on the Old Ward board are not in this building and were not reconciled:**

> **THE WEEKEND CLINIC AT THIS GATE IS OPEN WHILE A TERM IS IN FORCE.**
> **A TERM IS SIX DAYS AND IT IS NOT RENEWED BY ANY PERSON.**
> **NOBODY IS REQUIRED TO BE TOLD WHAT A TERM IS.**

---

**AND HERE IS THE FINDING OF THE CHAPTER, AND IT IS NOT A DISCOVERY, AND HE DID NOT GO LOOKING FOR IT.**

He had been thinking, without putting it in words, since the Wednesday, that a term was worth something to somebody, and he had been carrying it round as a question about doors, because that is what a gate is and that is what it has always been for in this borough.

**A term is not worth a door. A term is worth a supply.**

---

Behind that gate on a Sunday the supply is the health van. The van comes up the apron at about ten past seven, and it brings a doctor, and it brings a cold box, and it brings a day list on a clipboard, and it takes away a bag of samples at about half past two, and it cannot do any of that with a locked gate in front of it, and neither could the doctor have parked on the apron, and neither could the samples have come out, and none of that is anybody going through a door. Not one person in about eleven people in that room is the reason the van is on the apron. The supply is the reason and the supply is not a person.

**A term of six days is the cheapest thing anybody in this city has ever produced and it is worth exactly what it carries, and what it carried on the seventeenth of May was a van, a doctor, a cold box, a clipboard and a bag of samples, and it will be worth exactly that again on the twenty-fourth and the thirty-first and after that for as long as two tired men keep turning up at ten o'clock on a Saturday because nobody wrote anything down.**

**And a supply is worth nothing at all to a person who cannot name it.**

Not one of the people in that room knew that anything was in force anywhere. The van came at about ten past seven and it was a van and a doctor and they were glad to see it, and not one of them would have been able to tell you afterwards what a term was, or that there had been one, or that there was going to be another one on Wednesday night at ten o'clock in a room with four chairs in it, and none of them would have thought to ask.

**And that is not a fault and it is not a gap and it is not a thing to be fixed. It is what a supply is. The whole of what a six-day promise buys is that about eleven people do not have to know it exists, and the price of that is that not one of them has any idea what would happen to them if it stopped, and they will not be told that either, and there is nobody in this borough who is required to tell them.**

---

The van came at about ten past seven and the room emptied by about half past.

Nobody was harmed and nobody was helped. **A thing being brought into a room by a term in force is not the same as a person being helped, and it makes no difference to the people in the room whether it is or is not, and the ones who got what they came for will not be able to tell you afterwards which of the two it was, and the two women in the market aprons had lost stock on Saturday and lost it whether or not anything was in force anywhere, and the man with the walking frame did not use the walking frame, and the girl of seventeen with the school bag has an examination on Thursday and was in that room because of a text message, and none of the eleven people in that room is on the term and none of them is owed anything by it.**

The register is at nineteen entries. The blank line under the nineteenth is a hundred and seventy-two days old. The card in the slot in that counter has been in it eighty-six days. The letter in the tray on the floor above has been in it eighty-eight days under two other pieces of post. The rota has still not been rebuilt and cannot be, and the row from eleven o'clock to half past one is still empty and still does not say *vacant*, and Bram Osei's Thursday is still refused in his own handwriting with a second line under it in somebody else's hand that nobody has looked at and nobody is going to.

**And another of those terms was read at ten o'clock on the following Saturday night by two tired men who are not on anything, and there is no date on it, and there has not been a date on one of those nights in this city since the twenty-fourth of January.**
