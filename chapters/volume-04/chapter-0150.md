# Chapter 0150 — A Kindness

Wednesday the twentieth of May 2015, from about half past ten in the morning, a doorway in Tideglass; and about four o'clock, a footbridge; and about half past five, a kitchen.

**He asked no question of anybody on that day and nobody asked him anything and he was not in a room with a list and he did not get one and he did not ask for one. Nobody was helped and nothing was put in any document.**

---

**SHE IS NINETEEN. She is a courier and she does it on a bike and she does about four days a week and the rest of the week she is with her grandmother. She knows the route markers on that district better than anybody in this city and there is no list of them anywhere and the markers are not on any sheet and there are about nine of them and she has never written any of them down.**

He had not seen her since the eighteenth of April before last, standing by a door in a net-loft kitchen on the wrong side of the city, on a Saturday morning before seven, when a councillor read a piece of paper out loud in about fifteen seconds, standing up, and folded it up and put it in her coat. **He was at the door and he had no warning of it at all, and he has not thought about it in thirteen months, and nobody told him anything about it then and nobody has told him anything about it since.**

She came off a step at about half past ten with a rucksack and a jumper tied round her waist and stopped, and did not come in, and stood in the doorway with her hand on the frame.

"**You're the one who does the doors,**" she said. "**That's what he called you. I'm not saying it like that. I'm saying that's the phrase.**"

"**All right.**"

"**Right.**"

---

**And then she said the thing that is the first half of the afternoon, and she said it in a doorway at half past ten in the morning without being asked anything, and she is right and he has not been able to improve on it since.**

"**Nine households on one street have had no water since Wednesday. Nine. I've got nine because I know they're nine, not because anyone's told me. Four of them have got somebody in them who can't carry a bottle up a stair, and one of them has got somebody in it who's nine and who has been going to the standpipe at the bottom with a bag because nobody would give her a bucket she could lift, and she's doing it twice a day, and I've been doing it for her since Tuesday and I'm nineteen and I've got a bike.**"

"**Who pays for the water at the standpipe.**"

"**She does. That's the point. She pays for it, out of nothing, and she does not tell anybody, and there is nothing in this city that could tell her that she is allowed to stop.**"

"**How much.**"

"**I don't know. I have not asked her and I am not going to ask her, and you are not going to either, because if I ask her what she pays then you know what she pays, and then somebody in a building knows what a woman on that street pays for water, and that is a thing that can be used.**"

---

**And the rest of the day was about a street, and a bridge, and a kitchen, and none of it went into anything, and here is the middle of it, which is the part that is hard.**

At about four o'clock he was on a footbridge over the cut and she came across it on the wrong side of the rail, on foot, with the bike over her shoulder, and they stopped, and the wind off the water was the only cold thing in that borough.

"**You've been in here nine months,**" she said. "**You know what your sentence is for.**"

"**I know what it says.**"

"**You've had it since April. You had it out loud in a room in daylight and about nine people heard it and it went on a sheet with no box on it. It's the only thing anybody's said in nine months that anybody's printed.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**It's a kindness.**"

---

He did not say anything for a bit.

"**A kindness,**" she said. "**That's the word for it where I come from. A kindness is a thing somebody does to somebody from outside, and nobody's owed it, and it doesn't put anybody in a room, and you can't build a floor out of it, and if you stop doing it then you haven't broken a promise, because you never made one. That's all it is. It's the right word. I have been using it as long as I can remember and I have never had to look it up.**"

"**That is what it is,**" he said.

"**Yes. And you know what the trouble with it is.**"

"**Tell me.**"

"**The trouble with it is that it goes to a person. Every time. You hand it to somebody and now there's a woman in this borough who is the woman you handed the kindness to, and in four years that is the only sentence anybody can say about her. Kindness. And there's no box on it and there's no name on it and there never will be, and she can't get rid of it and she doesn't know it's there, and it is nicer than the drawer, and it is exactly the same as the drawer.**"

---

**And that is the whole of the argument, and it went on for about an hour and a half on a footbridge in a heat, and there was a queue of about nine people on the bridge at one point because a man had stopped in the middle of it to look at a duck, and she did not raise her voice once, and he did not have a single answer that was worth anything.**

**And he did not improve the sentence.**

**He has had a pad since the fourth of September and it is the only place any of it is, and he did not take it out on that bridge, and he did not write down the word *kindness*, and if he had written it down it would have gone in a book that goes in a shop at the end of a street, and a word he has found for a thing other people have been living with for ten years is not a finding. It is a collection, and he did not do it, and that is the whole of the reason.**

**And the sentence is still exactly what it was in April. It has not been improved on. It is not going in a minute. It is not going on a wall, and nobody in this borough is going to quote it, and in a heat wave somebody is going to want to quote it and nobody is going to be allowed to.**

---

At about half past five she let him into a kitchen on the first floor above a shop, and there were four people in it and a jug of water going round, and nobody looked up, and nobody was asked whether they minded a stranger.

**"You're not having the jug,"** she said. "**There's nine of us and it's a jug.**"

"**I wasn't going to ask.**"

"**I know. That's why I'm telling you before you stand there looking like a man who is not going to ask, and then somebody in here says *are you all right, love*, and then you have to say something.**"

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT SHE WOULD NOT DO, AND SHE DID IT BEFORE HE ASKED FOR IT, WHICH IS HER WHOLE FUNCTION IN THIS BOOK AND IT IS A REAL ONE AND IT IS NOT A POSTURE.**

"**You're not getting the list.**"

"**I have not asked for it.**"

"**You came to a street at half past ten in the morning in a heat, and you stood in a doorway, and you wanted the list. You didn't ask for it because you're not allowed to ask anybody for anything, that's your whole thing, and it's a good thing, and it doesn't stop you wanting.**"

"**I did not want the list.**"

"**Then what did you want.**"

"**I wanted to know whether anybody on that street had been told that the water is being taken from the lowlands first.**"

"**Nobody has been told that, because there's nothing to be told. Nobody knows it's happening. They know the standpipe's dry and they know the tap's dry and they know the low water pressure's the same as it's always been. Nobody's been told anything, and that's not because somebody kept it from them, and that's not a secret, it's just that there's no one to tell them, and if somebody did tell them, then it'd be in a letter, and a letter's got a box on it, and a box on it has a name in it, and a name is the end of a street.**"

---

"**So it's the list you won't give me.**"

"**It's the list I won't give anybody.**"

"**That isn't the same thing.**"

"**No. It's a different thing and it's the only reason the list works.**"

She took it out of her pocket. It is a piece of paper about the size of a receipt with nine lines on it, and the lines are not names, they are street numbers and they are in her handwriting and there is nothing at the top of it.

"**This is not for you. This is not for a building. This is so that when one of us says a street, the other eight know which street. That's it. That's the whole function of it. And if it went into a building, then the first thing anybody would do with it is find out who is on it, and the second thing anybody would do is take the ones who are on it off the street they're on, because they're the ones who are on it.**"

"**Nobody in this borough is going to do that.**"

"**I know. That's not why I'm not giving it to you.**"

---

**AND THEN SHE GAVE HIM SOMETHING SHE DID NOT HAVE TO GIVE, AND IT IS THE THING THAT MADE ONE OF HIS FINDINGS SMALLER, AND HE LET IT MAKE IT SMALLER.**

"**You're doing the thing with the numbers,**" she said. "**You've been in that building every Friday for months and you go and stand at a wall. You've got a thing you go and look at and it's about how many people something is for. And you've assumed it's the same shape as the houses.**"

"**I have not said anything to you about numbers.**"

"**You don't have to. You've got it in your face when you say the word street.**"

"**All right.**"

"**Nine houses on that street. Thirteen people in them, most weeks. Because there are two vans in that street that are not anybody's address, and the people in them are not nobody, they are my aunt and a man called Terry who has been in the second one nine years and whose post comes to a shop, and if you count the houses and you count the people and you put one of those numbers on a thing, one of the two is going to be the wrong one, and it will be the right one for about a fortnight.**"

---

**And there it was, and it was smaller, and he did not improve it.**

**He has spent nine months working out that a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and he has got it right, and it is still true this evening in that kitchen and it is not going to be refuted here, and nothing she said touched it. What she said touched the other half of the thing he was standing on: that the number on the wall and the number in the street are not the same shape, and the difference between them is a woman in a van and a man called Terry who has been in that street nine years, and the difference is not an error, it is the street.**

**And he wrote one line of it down, in the pad, in his own hand, in a shop at the end of a street where nobody is ever going to look. The line says that the number of people in a street is not the number of houses and that the difference is not an error. It does not have the street in it. It does not have the two vans in it, and it does not have either of the two people in the vans in it, and that is not modesty: a pad that goes in a shop is a piece of paper, and a piece of paper with a street in it and a name in it is the exact thing a man of forty-five is carrying in a cardboard wallet in a van, and there are three of them and nobody owns them, and the only reason he is not carrying a fourth is that he worked this bit out himself on a bridge, and a rule does not need his name in it.**

---

"**Can I say one thing back and you not do anything with it.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**You've spent nine months telling this borough what a floor is for. And you were right, and it was the right sentence, and I want to tell you what I do with it, and I want you to hear it and then not do anything with it, and that is the whole of what I'm asking.**"

"**All right.**"

"**A kindness. That's all I do with it. I have got nine households and I'm not going to put a sentence on them. If you turn up in four years with a sheet that says what a floor is for, and it's got my street on it in a way I can't get off, then it isn't a kindness any more, it's an appointment, and I have got a grandmother.**"

"**Nobody is going to put a street on anything.**"

"**I know. I'm telling you what it is called here, so that when it does, somebody in this borough has got a word for it.**"

---

**Nobody was helped. Nobody was harmed. The list is in a pocket. Nine households on one street had no water on the twentieth of May and had water on the twenty-second, in the ordinary way, in the way that water comes back, and nobody in this borough did that and nobody in this borough can be asked about it. A person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and it is a great deal heavier in the third week of May than it was in the third week of April, and it is not going to be refuted, and nothing she said on a bridge touched it.**

**AND HERE IS WHAT HE DID NOT DO IN THAT KITCHEN, AND IT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT THE AFTERNOON WAS, AND NOBODY KNEW HE HAD NOT DONE IT.**

There is a sentence he has said out loud in rooms, and a woman of nineteen on a street had said the same thing to him for eleven years without ever being in a room, and he could have said it back to her in a kitchen with a jug going round, and it would have cost him nothing, and it would have been the truest thing anybody could have said to her, and it would have made her a person who had been told the right thing by the man who said it first.

**He did not say it. A sentence said in a room is a thing a room knows and it does not last, and if he had said it to her in a kitchen then it would have become his, and it is not his, and there is a woman of fifty-four in this borough who has stood in a shopfront since the fourth of July doing the one thing in this city a person can be held to, unpaid, and he is not going to hand her a second sentence and call it an argument.**

And she watched him not say it, and she did not ask him why, and he has no idea what to do with that either, and he is not going to write that down either.
