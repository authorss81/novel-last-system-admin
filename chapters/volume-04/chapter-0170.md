# Chapter 0170 — Three Things In June

Friday the tenth of July 2015, from about ten in the morning until about half past eleven, the vestibule and the ground floor of the Old Ward.

**A counter in a vestibule eleven feet by nine that answers what was true on a day and gives a date, and nothing else, and it did it the way it does everything, and it did not move, and a cool week is not a reason. A man of forty-seven came in at about a quarter past ten who was not asking about anything and got something nobody offered him, and it was correct, and it cost the woman behind the counter the length of a sentence and him the walk back out, and that is the whole of what a service is, and the count of refusals in this borough did not move. He asked no question of anybody and he was asked none.**

---

It is open on a Saturday morning and has been for years and nobody has ever written down why. On a Friday morning in the third week of July it is a counter with a chair behind it and about eleven people a day, and in the worst heat in nineteen years it was the same eleven and not the same eleven, and this week on a cool morning it is a different eleven again, and there is no way of knowing which is which and nobody is required to try.

**Nobody is required to be at that counter. It is a counter and not a service, and the reason it works is that the person standing at it cannot be found from any record in this city, and the reason she is there at about ten and about three on a Friday is that she has been doing it for nineteen years and she comes down from the floor above.**

She is about fifty. She keeps the minutes of a body that has not met since the middle of January. Nobody in this borough has ever asked her for a number of anything and she has never given one to anybody, and the drawer under that counter has no key on it and no list in it, and the tray on top of the drawer is not a list and is not a drawer, and neither of them is going to be dealt with this morning because it is a cool Friday and that is not a reason.

---

**AND THE PEOPLE CAME AND WENT, AND IT ANSWERED ABOUT A THING FOUR TIMES, AND IT REFUSED NOTHING, BECAUSE NOTHING WAS ASKED THAT IT WAS REQUIRED TO ANSWER.**

A woman of about thirty-three asked what the position had been in April, and was told what the position had been in April, and was given the date, and said that was fine.

A man of about twenty-nine asked whether a collection on a road had been altered, and was told that it had not, and was given the date of the notice, and did not ask who had made the notice.

A man of about fifty-six came in at about half past ten and said:

"**Can you tell me whether a person's been on that list.**"

"**I can tell you what was true on a day, and I can give you the date of it. That is the whole of what this counter is.**"

"**Right.**"

"**It is not a list of people and I could not answer that if there was one, and I am not going to tell you that there is not one, because I do not know that.**"

"**No,**" the man said. "**That is fair. Thanks.**"

He went. **Nothing was refused. Nothing had been required of that counter, and a counter that answers what was true on a day has not refused a question about a person; it has told a man the shape of itself, and he is the only one in this borough who has ever needed to be told that, and nobody sent him.** The standing count of refusals in this borough is eleven and it did not move this morning and this was not a twelfth.

---

**AND AT ABOUT A QUARTER PAST TEN A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SEVEN CAME IN AND STOOD AT THE COUNTER AND DID NOT ASK FOR ANYTHING.**

He repairs washing machines and he does not work for this borough and he has never been on anything and there is nothing about him in this city that anybody could look up.

He had a plastic bag with a tap washer in it and he put it on the counter and then did not say anything about it, and stood there for about a minute and a quarter, and the woman behind the counter did not ask him what he wanted, because the thing about that counter is that it waits.

"**Sorry,**" he said. "**I have got nothing to ask you. I know that is a waste of your morning.**"

"**It is not a waste of anything. I am stood here until about eleven.**"

"**Right.**"

"**What I can do is the other thing. If you tell me a month, I can tell you what was true on a day in that month and I can give you the date of it, and if you want to know which day something was, that is the whole of what I am for.**"

"**June,**" he said.

"**June.**"

"**I have been trying to work out since June what there is in this building, and I have got nowhere, and I have been to two of the offices and one of them has got a board you can look at and the other one wanted a reference number, and I have not got a reference number because I have not got anything to give a reference number.**"

"**That is because there is nothing to give a reference number to. I can tell you three things in June and the date on each of them, and there is no name on any of them.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**The nineteenth of June, a sheet went round this building. Four streets, nine lines, and it said the same thing nine times, and there is no name on it and no box on it, and it is in nine other buildings.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**The twelfth of June, a man with a clipboard filled a sheet in a car park and left the name column empty and told about nine people out loud that he was leaving it empty, which I have never heard anybody do in my life.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**The twenty-fifth of June, somebody got a thing they had asked for, and it was written on the back of a page, and it is the only time in nine months that anybody in this borough has written a correct answer down.**"

"And the names."

"**There are not any. On any of the three.**"

---

**AND SHE SAID ALL OF THAT THE WAY THAT COUNTER SAYS EVERYTHING, WHICH IS QUICKLY, AND SHE DID NOT KNOW SHE WAS DOING HER JOB WHILE SHE WAS DOING IT, AND HE HAD NOT ASKED FOR ONE PART OF IT.**

He stood there afterwards for a moment.

"**That is not what I came in for.**"

"**No.**"

"**That is better than what I came in for.**"

"**It is a Friday and it is cool and I have been in here about an hour and you are the first person in an hour who has not wanted a piece of paper off me. About eleven people come through on a good morning and it is always a piece of paper, and the piece of paper is never the thing, and I give them the thing anyway because that is what is at the front of me.**"

"**Right.**"

"**I am not saying that to be interesting. I am saying it because you have got a tap washer in a bag and you are not taking it to anybody here.**"

"**No,**" he said. "**I have been carrying this bag since Saturday.**"

---

**AND HE PUT THE BAG BACK IN HIS HAND AND SAID GOOD MORNING AND WENT OUT INTO A STREET THAT WAS TWENTY-TWO DEGREES, AND HE DID NOT WRITE ANYTHING DOWN, AND NOBODY IN THAT BUILDING WILL BE ABLE TO SAY WHO HE WAS.**

He has a business. He has a van. He is not findable, and there is no reason he should be, and he will not remember a Friday morning in July, or that a woman told him three true things about her own month and put a date on each of them and no name on any of them, and that he heard the whole of it standing still.

**Nobody was helped in a way that lasts. That counter is the only thing in this borough that answers anything at all and it cannot be given to anybody, it does not grow, it is not obliged to be kept going, and it worked this morning because one person was standing at it, and it did not move, and the weather did not cause it, and a cool week is not a reason and neither was the heat.** **It is the only thing anybody can come to in this borough and be told something true, and the help costs about eleven seconds, and that is the measure of that counter's answers and it has never been written down anywhere, and a room remembers him, and a room is not a record, and there is no way in this borough to ask a room what it remembers.**

Nobody in that vestibule was thanked and nobody refused to be thanked and nobody was refused anything.

The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and twenty-six days old and the bulb on the nineteenth is out and a cool Friday is not a reason to write on a blank line and nobody is obliged. The card in the slot is a hundred and forty days old and nobody has taken it out of it and nobody is going to. The letter in the tray on the floor above is a hundred and forty-two days old and it has two pieces of post lying on it and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a man of forty-seven was told three correct things about a month in a building and did not ask for them, and he is not on anything, and he was not owed anything before he came in and he is not owed anything now, and the counter did not move and was not a reason.**
