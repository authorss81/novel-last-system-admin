# Chapter 0160 — The Man In The Stockroom

Monday the twenty-second of June 2015, from about two in the afternoon, a shopfront in Tideglass; and about four o'clock, the stockroom at the back of it.

**The heat and the spill had run for a month and the thing that is not water was a room. It was a stockroom behind a shop that is half shut, and the problem in it belonged to a person, and every instrument in this borough that touches a person stops at a box. Somebody was helped, or something was, and it is not possible for anybody to say which, including the man at the back of it. Jonas Mercer did not do it. The reason he did not do it is about him. Nothing went in a document.**

---

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE IN THAT SHOP, on the inside of the glass at eye height, over a notice about a card machine, and it defines nothing and nothing on it defines *holder*, and it requires nothing of anybody:**

> **DRINKING WATER IS GIVEN AT THIS COUNTER WHILE THE SHOP IS OPEN.**
> **NO PERSON IS ASKED FOR ANYTHING IN ORDER TO BE GIVEN IT.**
> **NO PERSON WHO IS GIVEN IT IS ASKED WHERE THEY LIVE.**

---

The shop is a convenience shop on a parade in Tideglass and it has been there a long time and it is half shut because the shutters come down at about half past one in a heat, which is not a decision anybody made, it is just what a shopkeeper does with a shutter in June.

There was a man of about twenty-four behind the counter with a fan pointed at the fridge and a bottle of water going along the top of it, and he was right about the shutter and wrong about the fridge, and the fridge is the only cold thing in the building and it is cold for about four cans.

Jonas Mercer came in at about two for a bottle of water and gave the man a pound and did not ask him anything, and then he stood at the end of the counter for about a minute, because the door at the back was open and there was a man asleep on a folded blanket on the floor behind it with a sleeping bag rolled at one end and a bag against the wall and a jug on its side.

---

**AND HE DID NOT ASK, AND THE NOT-ASKING IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT THE INSTRUMENTS IN THIS BOROUGH ARE, AND IT IS NOT A THING HE HAS DECIDED TO DO. IT IS A THING THERE IS NOWHERE TO PUT.**

There is no form in this borough on which a person can be recorded as sleeping in a stockroom. If the shopkeeper rings anybody about him there is nobody to ring, because he has no address that is anybody else's business, and the shopkeeper has known about him since about the spring and has not rung anybody and has not written anything down and has not asked him one question.

**Every instrument in this city that touches a person stops at a box. The register is a box. The rota is a box. The counter will tell you what was true on a day. The charter record has a box at the top of every sheet and the box is empty on four of the nine. And the moment this man is written down anywhere, he is a person who is known to be somewhere, and a person who is known to be somewhere is a person who can be moved on, and a person who can be moved on is a person somebody has handled.**

**So the shopkeeper of about twenty-four has a man in his stockroom, and the shutter is half down, and the stockroom has no air and no window that opens, and it is thirty-one degrees in there, and nobody in this borough is going to be able to help him or unhelp him, and that is not a failure of a system. That is what happens at the end of every system that has ever been built in this city.**

---

**THE LANDLORD ASKED THE SHOPKEEPER IN MAY TO STOP LETTING ANYBODY IN. THE SHOPKEEPER DID NOT STOP LETTING ANYBODY IN AND DID NOT TELL THE LANDLORD AND DID NOT TELL ANYBODY, AND IT IS THE ONLY THING IN THAT BUILDING THAT KEEPS THAT DOOR OPEN, AND IT WORKS.**

**And the shop is not a drop-off point and the shutter is not a solution, and the two facts have nothing to do with each other, and the man on the blanket has been there on and off since about April and has a bag and a jug and washes at a place that is not in this borough and comes in at about eleven at night and goes out at about half past six, and he is not a customer and he has not stolen anything and there is no instrument in this city that could establish any of that, and it does not need establishing, and that is the last sentence anybody can say about it.**

---

**AND THEN AT ABOUT FOUR O'CLOCK SOMEBODY CAME IN WITH A VAN AND DID A THING, AND NOTHING IN THIS BOROUGH IS EVER GOING TO BE ABLE TO TELL YOU WHAT THE THING WAS.**

The shopkeeper had asked him, in about ten minutes, on his second visit this year, whether he had a fan he could leave in the stockroom until the end of the month, and the man had said he would see, and a man who does plastering comes about twice a month and is not a man who says things he does not mean.

A man of about twenty-six came through the door with the back of a van open behind him and said:

"**Is the ladder still in your yard?**"

"**Course it's in my yard.**"

"**I'm after the landing for the Whitmores.**"

"**Go on then.**"

He came back through with a pedestal fan in one hand that was not new and not his colour, and an extension lead, and he put the fan on the floor at the end of the stockroom where there was a socket, and plugged it in, and turned it round about a foot, and looked at the blanket, and said:

"**That'll be about four degrees in there.**"

"**Right.**"

"**It's a borrowed one, so.**"

"**I heard you say it.**"

And he went out to the van and drove off, and the shopkeeper of about twenty-four watched him go and said, to nobody:

"**He's about twice a month. He does the plastering.**"

---

**AND AT ABOUT SEVEN THE SHOPKEEPER PUT A JUG OF WATER ON THE CRATE BESIDE THE BLANKET, AND HE DID NOT SAY ANYTHING, AND HE WENT BACK AND SHUT THE SHUTTER ALL THE WAY DOWN AT ABOUT HALF PAST SEVEN BECAUSE IT HAD BEEN ABOUT FIVE IN THE SUN.**

Four degrees.

That is the whole of what anybody did on the twenty-second of June, and it was the correct thing to do and it was the only thing anybody did, and four degrees in a stockroom with no window that opens in the fourth week of a heat is not a solution and it is not a failure.

**And it is not possible to say, on the evidence available, whether a man was helped or whether a man was given four more days of being in a room in thirty-one degrees, and those are the same sentence, and the difference between them is the whole of what this borough has to give and does not give.**

---

**AND HERE IS THE REASON HE DID NOT DO IT, AND IT IS ABOUT HIM, AND IT IS NOT A PRINCIPLE.**

He was standing at the end of that counter at about four o'clock with a bottle of water he had already paid for. There was a van in the road and a man about to come through that door with a ladder, and if Jonas Mercer had gone out to the road and said *that man on the floor is about thirty-five and he has no address and the room is thirty-one degrees*, then a man of about twenty-six would have known, inside a second, that a stranger had been in that stockroom and had looked at a man on a blanket and had made a speech about him.

**And there is no version in which a man who has been the one who found somebody does that twice and gets away with it, and the three times it has happened in nine months all happened in rooms of his own.**

He did not say the price out loud then and he is not going to say it now, because he said it once, to one person, on the thirteenth of April, and a sentence said once is a thing that room knows and it does not last, and it is not going to be said again.

What is left, and it is enough, and it is not noble: **the man in the van had already got the fan out of the van before anybody said a word, and a man who is on a job at four o'clock on a Monday in June is a man who was going to be in that doorway anyway, and there is no version of this in which a man who is on nothing makes a man's chances better by being visible in a shop.**

---

Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody refused anything. Nobody was harmed. Nobody died and nobody was lucky and nobody was rescued.

**Nothing went in a document.** The shopkeeper's book that day has a jug of milk, a packet of biscuits and a pound change, and there is no line in it about the blanket or the fan and there is not going to be one, and the extension lead is a shop extension lead and the socket is the shop's socket and the fan belongs to a man who does plastering who came about twice a month, and nobody is going to find him and he is not a thread and he is not owed anything and he is not going to be asked.

**On the Tuesday the shutter was down until about ten and the stockroom was empty and the blanket was folded on the crate and the fan was against the wall with the cord wrapped round the base, and the jug was on the shelf, and there is no way in this borough of finding out where that man was on the night of the twenty-second of June, and that is not a gap in a record. That is the record.**

---

The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is blank and did not move. The card in the slot is a hundred and twenty-two days old and the letter in the tray is a hundred and twenty-four days old with two other pieces of post on top of it, and neither of them is anybody's duty.

The rota has still not been rebuilt and cannot be. The board stands at seven of nine with two chairs empty and no chair of that body at all and has not met since the middle of January, and the ninth seat has not been told anything and nobody is going to tell her.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a man asleep on a blanket in a stockroom in Tideglass on the twenty-second of June 2015 was not counted and was not on a list and was not owed anything, and four degrees was given to him by a person he was never told the name of, and nobody in that shop is going to be asked who carried it in, and it is not going to be in any document in this city for as long as this city lasts.**
