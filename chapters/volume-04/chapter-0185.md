# Chapter 0185 — The Second Heat

Saturday the first of August 2015, from about ten in the morning, a pavement on the far side of a market.

**The weather came back and it was not the same weather. In May it was a spike. This is a degrade, and a degrade is a different thing, and nobody in this borough has a system for a different thing. A proposal was put on a table out loud in a first-floor room in the afternoon by a person with a constituency and a correct instinct, and every sentence she said was correct, and the borough could not refuse it, and refusing it is the thing nobody in this borough can do. Nothing was written down and nobody put a name to anything. The heat did not by itself turn anything into a rota.**

---

**AT TEN IN THE MORNING IT WAS TWENTY-NINE, AND AT FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON IT WAS THIRTY-ONE, AND AT FOUR IN THE MORNING IT HAD BEEN TWENTY-SIX.**

That is the whole of the difference between the two heats and it took nine days to establish it and nobody established it. In May there were three days over thirty and after the third it broke and everybody in this borough said afterwards that they had got through it. That is true and it is the wrong word.

**A spike has a shape and you can see where it started. A degrade has no top.** Nine days of it now, and it was twenty-six degrees at four in the morning, which is the warmest night anybody in this borough can remember for the first of August, and the sun came onto the front of the shopfronts at about eleven and did not go off the concrete until about half past seven, and there was nothing anybody could do about that in the first week because there is nothing anybody can do about it on any day.

Nobody has a system for a degrade because a system for a spike is a thing you do on the third day, and a thing you do on the third day is still available a week in, and a thing that is available a week in is not the same thing as a thing that was necessary on the third day. **Everything this borough did in May is still available today. None of it is necessary today. All of it is being done anyway, by the people who did it, and they are not doing it because anybody asked them and they are not being asked.**

---

**AND THE PROOF OF IT IS ON A PAVEMENT ON THE FAR SIDE OF THE MARKET.**

Two trestle tables and a box of water bottles have been on that pavement since about the third of July. They went up at about eleven and came in at about five. On nine of the last eleven days they went up at about eleven and came in at about five, and nobody noticed which nine and nobody is going to.

**The crate is still there. It has a lid on it.**

Nobody put the lid on it. That is the whole of what is known about it, and it is a great deal, because a crate with a lid is not a thing somebody brought. A crate with a lid is a container, and a container with a lid is a place a thing can be kept, and the difference between the two is about four pounds of plastic and one afternoon in a life nobody in this borough knows anything about.

The man from the cold store at the back of that shop brings the tables out and takes them in and he has done it on nine of the last eleven days and he has never been asked about it and he is not going to be, and his age has never been written down in anything and is not going to be.

**"That is the day it stopped being a table,"** said the man of about sixty-eight, who has sold fruit off that frontage on Saturdays for nine years, and he said it to the pavement, and nobody asked him, and he was right. **"A table you can carry. That has to be fetched, and somebody has to go and fetch a lid, and the next thing you know there is a man in this borough who is going round with lids."**

And there is not. That is what he means and it is the whole of what he means.

---

**AND THE PROOF OF IT IS ALSO ON FOUR STREETS, WHERE SOMEONE PUT A THING OUT AND NOBODY ASKED AND NOBODY IS OBLIGED.**

A bottle of water has been on a top step outside a door on one of those four streets since about the twentieth of July. It is there in the morning and it is not there in the evening. **Since about the twenty-ninth of July there have been four of them, on four steps, and the woman of about seventy-nine has been drinking out of the one on her own step on most mornings since the middle of July and has not asked anybody where it comes from and is not going to. Nobody is required to put a bottle out. Nobody has ever been required to take one in. Nobody has said whose they are and nobody has said whether it is one person or four and the four of them have never met.**

And nobody has asked, and the reason nobody has asked is not discretion and it is not anybody's kindness and it did not occur to anybody as a decision. **The person who put the first one out is a person nobody could find if they tried, and asking which of four people put out a bottle is a question with a list behind it, and a list behind that is a list of people.**

---

**AND THE MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-NINE, WHO HAS LIVED ON HARKER STREET FOR TWELVE YEARS, IS RIGHT ABOUT THAT STREET AGAIN, AND HE IS RIGHT ABOUT SOMETHING ELSE.**

In July he said there is nowhere on that street to sit in the shade after two o'clock, and four people in a room agreed with him, and no instrument in this city can stop a building remembering him for saying it. He is right about the shade, and he is also right about what has happened to that street in nine days, and that half is worse.

"There are seven cars on this street at night," he said. "**There were none in May. Every one of them belongs to somebody who is doing the right thing, because it is cooler outside than in and there is nowhere else to go, and not one of the seven is doing anything wrong.**"

A street that had no shade in July has no parking in August, and a man who has lived on it for twelve years can tell you which seven and cannot tell you where the eighth would park if it needed to. **He has not asked anybody for anything and is not going to start, and nobody asked him anything, and he has now been right about that street in two months running and a building still has him in it from the first time.**

---

**AND THEN AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWO A WOMAN WENT UPSTAIRS AND PUT SOMETHING ON A TABLE IN A FIRST-FLOOR ROOM, AND SAID IT OUT LOUD, AND EVERY WORD OF IT WAS CORRECT.**

Councillor Iven Sore. She has a constituency and a reason and she is not a villain and she has never lied to anybody in this borough and she was not going to start on a Saturday afternoon in a heat.

She put a folder on the table and she did not open it and she said:

"**In May a hospital, a school, a market and a pumping station were each right about what had to happen first on the same morning, and on the seventeenth of May four of them were right at the same time and not one of them knew about the other three. Nine traders threw stock away. A woman of sixty went to hospital in an ambulance for something that was not serious. Nobody died and it was the closest thing this city has had to a disaster since I have been in this room, and it was handled by about four people, and every one of the four was right, and none of the four had been told to be there.**"

"**I am not going to tell you that four people is not enough. Four people is what we have. Four people is also the only thing we have ever had that worked, and I have spent nine months watching people in this borough be frightened of the day somebody writes down who the four are.**"

Then she said the other thing, and it is the sentence this borough is going to remember or not remember, and every word of it is correct:

"**And I have read the thing that says a sheet with a heading and a date on it cannot be got out of, and I agree with it, and a sheet that cannot be got out of is the cheapest public thing this borough has ever produced, and it is also the only instrument in this city that anybody can be shown.**"

**THERE ARE EIGHT PEOPLE IN THAT ROOM AND NOBODY CHAIRED IT AND THERE IS NO CHAIR OF THAT BODY, AND NOTHING WAS MINUTED, AND THE PROPOSAL WAS NOT AGREED AND NOT REFUSED AND NOBODY WAS PUT TO ANYBODY.**

---

**THE ARGUMENT FOR BUILDING IT WAS THAT IT IS EASIER. THE WHOLE OF IT.**

Not more dramatic, not more generous, not more correct. Easier. **A thing that is easier to do is a thing that gets done a week into a degrade as well as on the third day of a spike, and on the day after somebody stops turning up.** Nobody in that room argued that an instrument would produce better decisions. They argued that a person who is not paid and not required cannot be sent to a street, and cannot be sent twice, and cannot be sent at all if the person is asleep, and that a week into a degrade with no top the number of people who happen to be awake and correct at the same time goes down and not up.

**That is true, and it is true of a laundry round and of nine kitchen doors and of a cool hall opened at eleven by a woman with a key, and every one of those was correct in June.**

---

**AND THE ARGUMENT AGAINST IT WAS ALSO TRUE, AND IT WAS ONE SENTENCE, AND THE PERSON WHO SAID IT SAID IT ABOUT SOMETHING THAT HAD HAPPENED IN JUNE.**

Everything that was correct in June was correct because there was no instrument.

**A page in a tray in a room in Tideglass, dated the twenty-ninth of May, with an empty signature block at the bottom of it, not in force, not in a minute, not going round anybody, and read by one person who was not asked to read it and who telephoned the building the next morning and said so out loud twice. And a telephone call in June from a person who was owed nothing, which the office answered, and which the office did not write down, because there is nowhere to write it down, and which is the only reason that person is not a person somebody can ring about.**

**Both of those worked. Neither of them has a name on it. A thing that works without a name on it and a thing that works with a name on it are opposites and not variants, and every argument made in that room after about half past three was an argument between those two sentences, and both of the sentences were said out loud, in daylight, by people who were right.**

---

**AND THE FINDING IS THE THING NOBODY IN THAT ROOM WANTED, AND IT IS THE ONLY THING THAT HAPPENED.**

The borough could not refuse it.

Not would not. **Could not.** Because there is no body in this borough that can require anybody to do anything, and a body that cannot require anybody cannot be asked what it will do, and a proposal that has been made to such a body in a room in daylight on a Saturday afternoon cannot be taken back by anybody, because taking it back would require somebody to be able to say no on behalf of a thing that has never once been able to say anything about anybody.

**Nobody said no. Two people said that they were not sure, and one of those two had said the same thing in June, and the not being sure was not a no and could not be turned into one, and eight people in that room understood that at about ten to four, and not one of them said it out loud then or afterwards.**

**And that is the mechanism, and it is the only one this borough has got. A thing that is not written down cannot be refused and cannot be agreed to and cannot be dated, and the not being sure stays where it is, and what happens next is that somebody in a different room says the same thing in a slightly different way and it is no longer not being sure, it is a position, and nobody in this borough can trace the moment that happened, and there is no instrument in this city that could show anybody the hour.**

The correct outcome of May was that it went on being about four people, and it went on being about four people, and that was correct, and the wrong outcome is somebody building a thing out of it so that it is not four people.

**And the thing that has happened in this borough in the four months since is that about four people has become a habit, and a habit is not a decision and has no date on it and cannot be got out of, and the man of sixty-eight can tell you which nine days the tables went up and cannot tell you when it stopped being a favour.**

---

**AND THE FINDING IS THE OTHER THING AS WELL, AND IT IS THE SAME FINDING.**

Nobody wrote it down. There is no minute, no agenda and no form, and there is a folder on a table in a first-floor room that nobody has signed and nobody is going to, and it is the only new object in this borough since the first of July and it weighs almost nothing.

**A proposal that is not written down cannot be refused and cannot be accepted and cannot be dated, and it will be said again in a room by somebody who has it in a folder, and the day somebody writes it down is the day this borough finds out whether it was ever possible not to.**

**And the folder went back into her bag at about half past four in front of everybody, and nobody asked her to leave it, and eight people went out into a day of thirty-one degrees and got on with the Saturday they had been in the middle of.**

The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth has been blank for two hundred and forty-eight days, and a heat is not a reason and a cool week is not a reason and a second heat that is not the first heat is not a reason either, and nobody is obliged. The card in the slot on the ground floor is a hundred and sixty-two days old and nobody has taken it out of it. The letter in the tray on the floor above is a hundred and sixty-four days old, it has two pieces of post lying on it, and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and four people saying yes to a correct question in a heat has not helped anybody, and four bottles of water on four steps have not helped anybody, and a crate with a lid that nobody put on is not a service and does not become one, and it is going to be the second week of August before anything in this borough is a rota, and it will not be the heat that does it.**
