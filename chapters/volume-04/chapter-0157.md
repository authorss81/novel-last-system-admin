# Chapter 0157 — The Coolest Room In The Parish

Wednesday the seventeenth of June 2015, from about eleven in the morning, a church hall off the lowland; and about ten to four in the afternoon, the same hall.

**There is no form for what happens in that hall and there is no room in this borough in which it could be minuted, and not one person in it is on anything. It is the first time in nine months he has stood in a room in this borough that is not pointed at a question about owing, and nothing that happens in it is correct, and nothing that happens in it will ever be evidence of anything. He worked something out in it. He did not write it down. It is not a fourth answer.**

---

The heat had been on for four weeks and had not broken, and on the seventeenth of June it was thirty-three degrees in the shade of a bus shelter and there was nowhere in the eastern part of this borough you could sit down out of the sun.

He was on that side of the city because he had no reason to be on that side of the city. That is the honest whole of it. He has a pad in his coat and he has been unable to walk into a room for nine months without looking for what in the room points at a person who is owed something, and on that Wednesday he went into a church hall because it was on the way to nothing and because there was a sign outside it about a lunchtime opening.

---

**THE HALL HAS HIGH WINDOWS ON TWO SIDES AND NO AIR CONDITION AND A KITCHEN AT THE BACK WITH A TABLE IN IT, AND AT ABOUT ELEVEN O'CLOCK ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF JUNE IT HAD TWENTY-FIVE PEOPLE IN IT AND EVERY ONE OF THEM HAD COME IN FROM THE STREET.**

There was a fan on a chair that somebody had carried in from a house. There was a jug of water on a table with a spoon in it. There was a dog. There was a box of biscuits on its side with three biscuits in it and nobody was going to move it.

**Nobody was owed anything in that room. Nobody was waiting for anything in that room. There was no agenda on the wall and there was no notice and no reference and no box and no form, and nobody who came into that hall was asked to give a name, and nobody who came into that hall was on anything, and if a person had wanted to be written down in that hall there was nowhere in it to write them.**

**And he looked for the instrument for about ten minutes and it was not there and it was not going to be there, and that is the resistance, and it is the whole of it.**

Every instrument in this borough is an instrument for finding out who is owed something. The register, the counter, the rota, the board, the charter record, the delivery sheet, the form with the box on it — all of them point the same way, and in a heat everybody in a week is owed the same thing and almost nobody in this borough is owed anything at all, and every one of those instruments kept working perfectly through the fourth week of it and produced nothing.

**And this room had no instrument in it at all, and was the coolest room on that side of the city, and nobody in this city had asked anybody to open it, and nobody in this city was going to be told that it had been open.**

---

**THE PERSON WHO OPENED IT WAS SEVENTY-SEVEN AND HAD THE KEYS BECAUSE SHE IS THE CHURCH SECRETARY, AND SHE DID IT BECAUSE A MINISTER SAID SOMETHING ON A SUNDAY.**

**Margaret Oduya, 77.** She had opened the doors at about eleven and she had put the jug out and she had put the biscuits out and she had not counted anybody, and at about ten to four she said that her bus was at four and that the door would be locked and that the key would be in her bag.

"**Is it all right if I lock up at four,**" she said, to nobody, and about nine people said that it was all right.

"**It is not all right, it is my building, but you can be in it until I go and then it shuts, and that is not me being hard, that is me having a bus at four and no way of getting a key cut before Friday.**"

And she went at ten to four, and the door was locked, and the hall shut, and there was no form for the twenty-five people who had been in it.

---

**AND HE ASKED NOBODY ANYTHING FOR THREE HOURS, AND HE WAS SPOKEN TO THREE TIMES, AND NONE OF THE THREE WAS A QUESTION.**

The man on his right, who was about fifty-two, asked him whether the church roof was the responsibility of the parish or of the trust, and then told him, without waiting, that it was the trust, and that it had been the trust since about 1988, and that he had been coming to these meetings for nineteen years.

A woman of about thirty asked him whether she could put a chair by the wall because her hip, and he said of course, and she said thank you and meant it about the chair and nothing else.

And a boy of about nine asked him whether he was somebody's dad, and he said no, and the boy said right, and went back to the floor.

**Nobody in that hall knew who he was. Nobody in that hall was going to. There is no instrument in this city by which a man standing by a wall for three hours in a church hall becomes a thing anybody could be held to, and that is not a gap in the record. That is the record.**

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT HE WORKED OUT, AND IT IS NOT A DISCOVERY, AND HE DID NOT WRITE IT DOWN.**

He sat on a chair by the wall at about half past two with the pad in his coat and did not take it out.

The cheapest things anybody in this borough has produced in nine months, and there are only two, and he can name where each of them came from and he can name what is standing next to each of them.

**The first is a sentence a woman said out loud, in a room that had a tray on a table in it, on a Friday morning in May, to a woman with a shopping bag who had asked a question at a counter. It was four sentences long and it was correct and it cost the woman who said it nothing at all, and it was not written down, and there is nowhere in this city to write it down, and the woman who said it is not on anything and is not owed anything and is not going to be.**

**The second is a date on a slip of paper that was folded and put in the inside of a coat on a Saturday morning in April last year by a woman who read it out loud first. It is the only piece of paper in this city with nothing on it but a date, and it cost her a fold, and it has been in a coat for about fourteen months, and nobody in this city has asked anybody about it.**

**And both of those things came from people who were standing near a thing.**

Not from an instrument that anybody put there for the purpose. A counter is a thing in a building, and that woman was standing at one. A tray is a thing on a table, and that woman was standing beside one. A door is a thing in a wall, and that woman was standing against one. The rota, the register, the board and the box are not in that list, and the two useful sentences in nine months came out of none of them, and the two of them are a refusal given out loud and a date on a slip.

**And he has spent nine months building the instruments.**

---

**AND HE IS THIRTY-TWO, AND HE DID NOT WRITE IT DOWN, AND THE REASON IS NOT A BETTER REASON THAN ANY OTHER.**

A pad that goes in a shop at the end of a street is a piece of paper, and a piece of paper in his own hand is a thing that he is the only keeper of, and he has been the only keeper of that book since the fourth of September, and in a few years somebody is going to be able to describe exactly what is in it.

And the other thing, which is the real reason and which is not a reason he would say out loud: the two things he had just thought about were made by people who did not have a pad and did not need one, and the making of them took four sentences and a fold, and if he wrote down what he had worked out in a church hall on a Wednesday then he would be building the fourth instrument and there would be a shop at the end of a street with a man behind the counter who had a book about how a place is kept cool.

**The pad stayed in his coat. He has not got anything in the world that he is less able to defend than that decision, and he is not going to improve on it.**

---

Nobody was helped. Nobody was harmed. Nobody died, nobody was lucky, and nobody was thanked.

At ten to four the hall shut and about nine people stood about a minute in the car park and then went home, and the person who had brought the fan in from a house took the fan home with her, and the spoon went back in the jug, and the box of biscuits with three biscuits in it stayed on its side on the table for four more days and was found on the Monday.

**The register of narrowings is at nineteen entries and the blank line under the nineteenth is blank and has not moved since the middle of November and nobody is obliged to write on it. The card in the slot in the counter on the ground floor of the Old Ward is a hundred and seventeen days old. The letter in the tray on the second floor is a hundred and nineteen days old and has two other pieces of post on top of it and has not been opened in front of a second person, and what is under the post is not known to anybody in this borough and may not be said in a room.**

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and there is no instrument in that hall, and there were twenty-five people in it, and not one of them had been counted, and not one of them was owed anything, and the hall was four degrees the coolest place on that side of the city, and the reason it was open is that a woman of seventy-seven said so on a Sunday to a congregation and then came in on the Wednesday with a key.**

**And there is nothing in this borough that can tell anybody she did that, and there is nothing in this borough that can stop her doing it again on Thursday, and those two sentences have been true since the fourth of September and they are the only free thing anybody in this city has found.**
