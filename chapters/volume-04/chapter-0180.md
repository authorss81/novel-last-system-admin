# Chapter 0180 — Emptied On A Friday

Friday the twenty-fourth of July 2015, from about half past eight in the morning, the ground floor of the Old Ward, and the cupboard under the stairs.

**The cupboard is emptied on a Friday, and the tray is in it, and the tray is the whole of the cost of a decision made in about nine minutes by a woman of fifty on the twenty-ninth of June, and today the cost arrives. Nobody in that building learns anything. It is the first time in nine months that a piece of this borough's paperwork has actually been destroyed, and it is not said whether that is a loss or a correction. He asked no question of anybody and he said nothing.**

---

The cupboard under the stairs is the cleaning things and the spare bulbs and the fire blanket and a bag of string and a stepladder with a missing rung, and it gets emptied on a Friday by whoever is on.

**Nobody is on. There is no rota for the cupboard. There is a rota for the building on a Wednesday and it covers a different floor, and the Friday cupboard has been done, since anybody can remember, by whoever came through with a sack.**

The man who came through with a sack this morning is about thirty-six. He is the same man who was in that room on the twenty-third of June with a clipboard counting a shelf, and on the twenty-ninth of June with a paint tin, and who carried a tray of post down those stairs at about nine o'clock in the morning and put it in the back of a white van.

He has not been asked about the tray. He was not asked about it then and he is not being asked about it now and he is not going to be.

---

**AND THE CUPBOARD WAS EMPTIED IN THE ORDER THINGS COME OUT, AND EVERYBODY IN THIS BOROUGH HAS DONE IT THE SAME WAY SINCE FOREVER.**

The string. The bulbs — two boxes, both part full, and he put them back in the box they came out of and the box back in the cupboard. The fire blanket, in its bag, against the wall. The stepladder. A bucket.

And then the tray.

---

**AND THE TRAY HAS ABOUT SIXTY PIECES OF POST IN IT AND EVERY ONE OF THEM IS DEAD, AND THAT IS NOT A FIGURE OF SPEECH, IT IS WHAT THEY ARE.**

He picked it up off the bottom of the cupboard, which means he bent down and put his arm in to the elbow, and the tray came up with the weight of it and he put it on the step.

A man of thirty-two was standing in the vestibule at about ten past nine with nothing in his hands and he looked down into it, and this is the whole of what anybody in this borough will ever know about what was in that tray, and it is what he saw, and he is the only one who saw it, and he does not write things down.

**Three meeting notices for a group that has not met since March.** The same three, once each, and one of them is a second reminder with a different date on it, and nobody in this borough has ever been to that group and it is not anybody's group.

**A parking notice for a yard.** It has a yard on it and a date in June on it and a line about vehicles being left in an area. **There is a yard in this borough it could be and there are about nine, and nobody in that building can place it, and it has not been asked about and it is not going to be, and a notice about a yard is not about anybody.**

**And about fifty pages of something.**

That is what it was and that is all anybody will ever be able to say. About fifty pages of a thing sent round in April, in one building, to a list that somebody held, and it came back off the Friday round on the nineteenth of June and it did not go out again on the twenty-sixth because there was nothing in circulation, and there has been nothing in circulation since.

**Nobody looked at what it was. Nobody in that building, nobody on the floor above, nobody in this city. Fifty pages of a document that went round in April and came back and has now gone out of a building for the last time, and it is not a report, and it is not a record, and it is not anybody's paperwork in any sense that anybody can use, and it is dead paper, and it is the reason the tray was a tray.**

---

**AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX SAID NOTHING ABOUT IT, AND HE PUT IT IN A SACK, AND THE SACK IS NOT A LIST.**

"**Do you want that out?**"

He said that to the man of thirty-two, and he said it in the tone of a man clearing a cupboard, and he was not asking about the tray and he did not know that anything in that tray was the subject of a conversation in a building five weeks ago.

"**It goes with the rest,**" the man of thirty-two said.

"**Right.**"

He put the sixty pieces into the sack with the string and the bucket, and the sack went out through the back door and across a small yard and into a skip, and the skip is out at the back because it is Friday, and the skip goes on the next collection.

**And that is the first time in nine months that a piece of this borough's paperwork has actually been destroyed.**

Not filed. Not moved. Not put in a cupboard. Not scanned. **Destroyed**, in a skip behind a building, on a Friday morning, by a man of thirty-six who has a job that is not a job.

**And it is not said in that building, and it is not said here, whether that is a loss or a correction, and this is not a rhetorical way of putting it. Both of those are available and they do not exclude each other, and there is no instrument in this city that can tell which one it is, because telling would require somebody to have decided, and nobody decided, and the only person who ever came near deciding it said *say it's a tray* in a room on the twenty-ninth of June and then went back to a counter.**

---

**AND THE WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY CAME DOWN AT ABOUT HALF PAST EIGHT, AND STOOD IN THE DOORWAY, AND DID NOT HELP, AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR ANYTHING.**

She has been at the counter on the floor above for eleven years and she is not the woman who comes down to the ground floor at about ten and about three, and the two of them have never been the same person.

She came down because the man with the sack was making noise. She stood in the doorway for about a minute and a half and looked at the cupboard and at the man and at the sack, and she did not say a word about any of it, and then she went back up.

**She is the only instrument in this borough that could say what was in that tray, and she is not going to be asked, and the reason she is not going to be asked is not that she is difficult and it is not that she has been rude. It is that if she is asked, then it was a decision. And if it was a decision then it was made by a person, and the next time a tray comes back off a Friday in that building somebody is going to say *last time we made a decision about a tray*, and the second time a thing is a decision it has a precedent, and a precedent has a name on it, and the name on it would be hers.**

---

**AND THE TWO OF THEM ARE BOTH ENTIRELY RIGHT ABOUT IT, AND NEITHER OF THEM WILL EVER KNOW THAT THE OTHER ONE IS.**

**SHE** is right that it was post that came back off a Friday and was never post again, and that a list would have cost a person and a tray cost a Friday, and that on the day she made that choice it was the cheapest of the three things available to her and she was on her own time and she was not asked to decide anything.

**HE** is right that it was a tray of paper, and that paper is not a record, and that nothing in that cupboard had a shelf, or a place, or a person, and that a thing nobody can put a name on is a thing nobody can be asked about.

Both of those are true. They cannot both be tested, because testing them would mean asking one of them a question, and asking one of them a question is the thing that turns it into a decision. **So they stand there, one above and one below, being right, for as long as this building is here, and neither of them will remember by the end of the month, and that is the correct outcome.**

---

**AND HE STOOD IN THAT VESTIBULE FOR ABOUT TWENTY MINUTES AND SAID NOTHING AND ASKED NOTHING AND WROTE NOTHING DOWN.**

He looked at about fifty pages of a document he will never identify, on the twenty-fourth of July, in the ninth month, in a borough where nobody in nine months has been able to name the person who put a strip of paper in a drawer at about ten every morning, and he did not pick up a page, and he did not read one, and he did not ask the man of thirty-six what the fifty pages were, and he did not ask him to remember.

**There is no form in this borough on which a person could record that a tray went out of a cupboard into a sack on the twenty-fourth of July, and the tray is in a skip, and the skip is at the back of a building, and the building was painted on the twenty-ninth of June, and the woman who decided the whole thing was fifty and has eleven years in a room and will not remember, and the man who carried it is thirty-six and will not remember, and neither of them is a bad actor in this and neither of them is a good one, and they are two people doing a Friday.**

---

Nobody in that building was thanked and nobody refused to be thanked. Nothing was minuted, and **nothing at all has been described in anything that leaves that building this morning, because nothing was written.** The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and forty days old and a Friday is not a reason to write on it and nobody is obliged. The card in the slot is a hundred and fifty-four days old. The letter in the tray on the floor above is a hundred and fifty-six days old, it has two pieces of post lying on it, and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and about sixty pieces of dead paper have gone out of a building in a sack on a Friday morning and no person in this borough has been hurt by it and no person in this borough has been helped by it, and the only person in this city who could describe what was in that tray is standing in a vestibule with nothing in his hands and is not going to be asked, and is not going to ask.**
