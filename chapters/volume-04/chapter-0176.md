# Chapter 0176 — A Month Old

Sunday the nineteenth of July 2015, from about half past ten in the morning, a first-floor room over a shop in Tideglass.

**A correct piece of paper is a month old today and nobody has asked anybody about it, and a person who is competent and correct stands up in a room and proposes the cheapest thing anybody has done for four streets in nine months, and the proposal is not stupid and everybody in that room is right about something different, and nothing happens. He asked no question of anybody. It does not go in a minute, nobody is thanked, and it is not a fourth answer.**

---

The sheet went round this borough on Friday the nineteenth of June, folded in four, in a tray with the lid off and nine other things in it. It has a heading, the date, four streets down the left in a column headed *location*, and nine lines beside them, and every one of the nine lines reads *no requirement stated*.

**There is no name box anywhere on it.** It is in nine buildings. Nobody in any of them knows where it came from.

It went round a second time on Friday the tenth of July with one column added, headed *date seen again*, with three words printed under it in the same size type as the rest of it, and the space is empty.

**The June sheet was not withdrawn, not annotated and not reprinted, and it is still in nine buildings, and nobody in any of those nine buildings knows that there are two of them.**

The first one is a month old this morning. That is all. It is a piece of paper on a wall or in a folder in nine buildings and it has survived a month and nobody has asked about it and nobody is going to.

---

**THERE WERE FOUR PEOPLE IN THAT ROOM AND EVERY ONE OF THEM WAS RIGHT ABOUT SOMETHING.**

**A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-THREE** who was in that room in June because that is where she works on a Sunday, and who was not in the room in June at all.

**A MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-FOUR** who is a duty clerk at a works depot on the far side of the tracks, and who is the person that a printing run has to go through before it exists, and who is not on a committee and does not want anything out of it.

**A WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-SIX** who has the keys to that room and who uses it on a Sunday because everybody who uses it is off on a Sunday.

**And a man of thirty-two at the back with nothing in his hands who is not on the agenda of anything.**

---

"**It is a month today,**" the man of about sixty-four said. "**The nineteenth. It went round the nineteenth of June, and it has been a month.**"

"**Nobody has asked.**"

"**Nobody has asked, and that is the correct outcome, and I want to say that before anything else gets said in this room today. A piece of paper has been on a wall in nine buildings for a month and not one person has rung anybody about it. In this city that is not nothing. That is the best result a piece of paper has ever had.**"

"**Right.**"

"**So what are we doing.**"

"**We are not doing anything,**" the man of sixty-four said. "**That is the meeting. I would like it minuted.**"

"**Nothing is going to be minuted.**"

"**No,**" he said. "**It is not, is it.**"

---

**AND THE WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-THREE SAID THE CORRECTEST THING IN THAT ROOM, AND SHE SAID IT AS A PROPOSAL, AND IT WAS NOT STUPID.**

"**It is the cheapest thing anybody has done on those four streets in nine months.**"

"**It is nothing.**"

"**It is nothing that is a month old and still correct on every one of the nine lines. Everything else that happened in June was somebody standing somewhere. A laundry round, a school tank, a cool hall, a door wedged open with a bit of wood. All of those had a person in them and all of those cost somebody something and two of them are still going. That sheet cost a ream of paper and about nine minutes in a room, and it is still true this morning, and in November it will still be true, and it will not have gone anywhere, because there is nobody in it to go anywhere.**"

"**That is a fair point,**" the man of sixty-four said. "**And here is what I would do with it if it were mine to do, and I am not going to do it, and I am going to say it anyway because somebody in this room should hear the argument for it once.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**I would print it. I would print four hundred of them and I would put them in trays and I would send the trays round every building in the borough on a Friday, with the lid off, and I would not ask anybody to do anything with them, and I would not take a name off it, because there is not one on it. And then in six months you would not have a piece of paper from the heat. You would have a thing that everybody in this borough has been handed, on a schedule, by somebody, and that is a different thing and it is worth having.**"

---

**AND THE WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-SIX, WHO HAS THE KEYS TO THE ROOM, WAS AGAINST IT, AND SHE WAS AGAINST IT FOR THE REASON THAT MATTERS MOST.**

"**No.**"

"**You have not heard the argument.**"

"**I have heard the argument. I heard it in June, in a different room, from a woman of fifty-seven carrying a tray, and she said the thing that stops it dead, and she was not talking about this.**"

"**Which was.**"

"**That it went round in a tray with nine other things in it, on a Friday, with the lid off, and two of those things were a lunch menu and a leaflet about a closure, and nobody picked it up until somebody needed the leaflet.**"

"**So it got to nine buildings.**"

"**So it got to nine buildings and so nobody has ever asked about it. That is not a failure of the design. That is the design. You cannot ask about a thing that arrived in a tray with a lunch menu in it. If you print four hundred and you send the trays round on purpose, with a date on them that you chose, then in about three months somebody in this borough is going to walk into a room and say *we have had that*, and on the fourth month somebody is going to say *right, so when is the next one due*, and on the fifth month you have a schedule.**"

"**And a schedule is a person.**"

"**A schedule is a person. And not only that.**"

She put her hand flat on the table, which she does not usually do.

"**There are two of those sheets in nine buildings now and nobody knows it. One of them has a column on it with three words under it, and the words are *no name, no initials*, and the column is empty and it is going to stay empty, and that is the most dangerous piece of paper in this borough and it is correct. If you print a third one without the column, then the two of you will fight about which of the three is the real one, and the first thing anybody in a building does when there are three is ask which one is current, and the answer to that is a date.**"

---

**AND THE MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-FOUR SAID THE OTHER THING, AND HE WAS RIGHT ABOUT IT, AND IT IS THE SAME ARGUMENT COMING FROM THE OTHER END.**

"**I do not want it reprinted either, and I am the person who could do it, so I am going to be the one who says no,**" the man of sixty-four said. "**Not because of the date. Because of the column.**"

"**Because of the column.**"

"**You have got a space in that thing. You put a space in a document and the space is a question, and it is going to sit there unanswered in nine buildings until somebody fills it in, and the first person who fills it in decides what goes in it, and you do not even need to be the first person. You just need to be the person who was the first person. And on the tenth of July a man of forty-six said out loud, in a room, that he was not going to be the one in it, and I was not in that room and I heard about it afterwards, and he was right, and he is right now, and if I put four hundred more of them out I am putting four hundred more empty columns in nine buildings, and one of them is going to get filled in by somebody who is frightened and thinks they are helping.**"

"**So you would print it again and take the column off.**"

"**If I printed it again I would have to take the column off, because nobody is required to keep to three words printed under a heading, and I would be relying on a person reading a piece of paper and deciding to do the right thing, and that is the whole of what is wrong with this borough and it has been the whole of what is wrong with it since last September.**"

---

**AND A MAN OF THIRTY-TWO AT THE BACK SAID ONE THING, LATE, AND HE WAS ASKED NOTHING AND SAID IT ANYWAY, AND IT TOOK HIM TWO SENTENCES.**

"**The second one is the one to be careful of and none of you are talking about it.**"

"**Which one.**"

"**The one with the column in it. It is the most dangerous object in this borough and it is not dangerous because of the words under the column. It is dangerous because it is the only piece of paper in nine buildings that anybody could ever answer. The June one asks nothing. That one asks, and it does not know it asks, and the three words under it are the only defence anybody in this borough has ever been able to write on a piece of paper, and there is nothing behind them.**"

Nobody said anything for a moment. **He is not on anything and he may not chair anything and there is no chair of that body and there never was, and he was at the back of that room with nothing in his hands and what he said went into a room and stopped there.**

---

**AND THE THING WAS NOT STOPPED, AND IT WAS NOT HELPED, AND IT WAS NOT WRITTEN ABOUT.**

The proposal stayed in the room. It was not minuted. It was not refused, because nobody had put it to anybody and a man standing up in a room with an idea is not a request.

The man of sixty-four took two hundred of the June sheets out of a cupboard on the Friday of the tenth of July because that is what happens to the second hundred of anything in a building with a tray round, and nobody in that building knows to this day that two hundred sheets were taken out of a cupboard on the tenth of July, and nobody has ever asked him why.

**And one sentence went out of that room, and it was not said by the person who made the proposal.**

The woman of about fifty-six said it at about twenty to twelve, to the man of about sixty-four, at the door, on her way to lock up.

"**It is the cheapest thing anybody has done on those four streets this year and it has got no name on it, and that is the whole of why it works, and the day somebody asks me why it works I am going to have to say who told me, and I am not going to.**"

"**All right.**"

"**I am telling you now so that you can be the person I do not tell.**"

He looked at her for a second.

"**I will be that.**"

**And she went down and locked the door. Nobody in that room wrote anything down, and nobody said thank you, and in about a year there is going to be somebody in this borough who wants to know where a sentence came from, and there are three people in a room who could answer it, and one of them will, and which of them it is is not going to be written down either.**

The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and thirty-five days old. The card in the slot is a hundred and forty-nine days old. The letter in the tray on the floor above is a hundred and fifty-one days old, it has two pieces of post lying on it, and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a piece of paper with nine correct answers on it is a month old this morning and has been asked about by nobody, and a man in a room was right that it is the cheapest thing anybody has done on four streets this year, and a man in the same room was right that it must not be printed again, and both of those are correct, and there is no form in this borough on which either of them could be written, and the two of them are not going to be reconciled.**
