# Chapter 0153 — Who Goes Without

Saturday the twenty-third of May 2015, from about eleven in the morning, a car park, Tideglass; and Sunday the twenty-fourth, from about twenty past six in the morning, a first-floor corridor off the lowland.

**He asked no question of anybody on either of those days and nobody asked him anything. The eighteenth of those terms was read at ten o'clock on the Saturday night by two people who are not on anything and there is no date on it, and the gate went up at seven on the Sunday morning in a heat and nobody in this borough was told that it had. Nobody was harmed and nobody was helped.**

---

**And the hour between six and seven is not stood in this morning, and the difference between this morning and the one a week ago is on the page and it is this.**

A week ago there were eleven people in a room who could not get into a building, and the reason they could not get into it was an hour, and the hour is the only hour of the week in which this borough requires nothing of anybody at all, and that is true this morning as well and it is not going to be argued about.

**This morning there are nine people in a corridor and they are not waiting for anything to open. The hall they came to open at seven. The corridor has a window in it and the hall has two high windows and no fan and it was twenty-six degrees in there at a quarter past six. So they are in the corridor because it is marginally cooler, and six of them brought clipboards, and one of them was there at ten past six because he could not sit still, and every single one of them chose to be there, and not one of them is obliged to be.**

**An hour that requires nothing can be full. That is not a finding. It is what an hour is.**

---

**THE SATURDAY, IN A CAR PARK, IN DAYLIGHT, WHICH IS WHERE A CITY STARTS ARGUING ABOUT WHO GOES WITHOUT.**

It was thirty-six degrees in that car park at about eleven in the morning on the twenty-third of May and about fifty people were in it and there was one bowser coming on the Sunday and four streets that wanted it.

**That is the whole of the emergency. There is no order. There is no declaration and no notice and no form and no reference, and there is no committee, and nobody is chairing anything, and there is no board that has to sit, and the seven people who sit on a body that has not met since the middle of January are not here and are not required to be and would not be told.**

There were clipboards. There were about four of them and two of them belonged to men from the water office and two of them belonged to women who had brought them from home.

**The argument went on for two hours and everybody in it was right and none of it was settled by anybody who came to settle it.**

A man of about fifty said the street where the water had been off since Tuesday should go first, and he could not say how long it had been off and neither could anybody else, and he had four houses on it with a person in each who could not get a jug of water to the top of a stair.

A woman of about thirty said the school street should go first because there are about ninety children on it and the school was shut.

A man of about sixty said no, the school was shut, and a shut school does not drink, and that was correct and it stopped the argument for about a minute.

A woman of about seventy, who had come on a bus, said:

"**Then don't argue about the streets. Argue about the length. The one that's been off longest goes first, because the one that's only been off since this week has got the standpipe and the standpipe's the one we all know about, and the standpipe is not dry. It's dear. There's a difference and it's the whole difference and you can see it from here.**"

---

**And that ended it, at about half past one, in about four seconds, and nobody wrote it down.**

**And here is what the borough has got, and it is the only thing this borough has had in nine months that it can actually use, and it is nine people, and it is not going to be anybody's, and it is going to be gone in a fortnight.**

It is about nine people who can be asked. Not a record. A record would be a form, and a form would have a box on it, and a box would have a name in it, and the first thing anybody would do with nine names is find out whether the four streets on them are the four streets anybody is being helped on.

**Nobody wrote anything down. There is no minute of a car park. There is no agenda, no item, no reference, no crest, and nobody in that car park has been asked anything by anybody with a grade, and there is nobody in this city who could require one of those nine people to be there on the Sunday, and none of them could be produced afterwards as a person who was told to be.**

**And in a few years there is going to be a piece of paper in a room somewhere in this city about who decided what in this heat, and the four streets are going to be on it, and there are not going to be any names on it, and the reason there are not going to be any names on it is that there is nowhere for a name to go, and that is the correct outcome, and the price of it is that the nine people are findable, and they know they are findable, and one of them said so out loud in that car park and got laughed at, and the laughing was right.**

---

**AND THE SATURDAY NIGHT, WHICH IS ONE SENTENCE, BECAUSE IT IS NOT A ROOM.**

**At ten o'clock on the Saturday night the eighteenth of those terms was read aloud by two tired men who are not on anything, and it began at seven the next morning when the gate went up, and it is six days long and not a week, and there is no date on it.**

---

**AND THEN THE SUNDAY, AT TWENTY PAST SIX, IN THE CORRIDOR.**

Nine of them. Six clipboards. A jug of water going round that had been carried up a flight of stairs by somebody who had said nothing about it. A window at the end with a piece of card wedged in it. A fire door at the other end that is not locked and has not been since about the spring.

They were not locked out of anything. The hall was twenty-six degrees and the corridor was four degrees better than the hall and that was the whole of the arrangement.

---

**AND THIS IS WHAT HE DID, AND IT IS A CORRIDOR AT TWENTY PAST SIX ON A SUNDAY MORNING IN A HEAT, AND HE WAS NOT ASKED ANYTHING, AND HE DID NOT ASK ANYTHING, AND HE STAYED ABOUT HALF AN HOUR.**

He stood at the end by the window. He is not on anything. He is not a member and not a party and not a departmental officer and he may not chair anything and there is no chair of that body and there never was.

**The decision was about four streets and it had been made in a car park on the Saturday and it was being made again, differently, in a corridor, because that is what a decision does when it is not written down: it has to be made again, by hand, over and over, by whoever happens to be in the room, and none of the versions is the version.**

Nobody argued. That is what was different. They were not arguing, they were checking. One of them read a list out loud of four streets and nine households and the other one said that number was right and the other one said it was not and read it again and it was right.

**And the one who came in first, at about ten past six, did not have a clipboard and was not asked for one and did not get one, and was about sixty, and was there because a woman on the ground floor had said on the Saturday afternoon that the corridor would be open at seven and that anybody who wanted to be there when the list went out should be there by a quarter past six, and that was the whole of the instruction and it was given to nobody in particular in a doorway.**

---

**AND AT ABOUT HALF PAST SIX SOMEBODY ASKED THE MAN WHO TURNS THE BUCKET FOR SOMETHING, AND HE ANSWERED, AND IT IS CORRECT, AND NOBODY IN THIS BOROUGH HAS EVER TOLD HIM HE COULD BE FOUND.**

She came up the stairs with a barrow and four containers on it and a carrier bag, about thirty-one, from the street of ninety-one houses, and she had been carrying water for three days because somebody in that street who is nine could not lift a bucket, and she did not know the man and had never spoken to him and had seen him about four times in two years at the stand at the bus turning head.

She said, "**You're the bucket one, aren't you.**"

"**I am.**"

"**Right. Listen. I'm not asking you for anything free, I've got money, and I'll pay you the four pounds, I'll pay you Monday's as well and I don't want a receipt and I don't want to talk to anybody.**"

"**All right.**"

"**Right. Monday. Are you going to be there Monday.**"

"**I will be there at ten.**"

"**Ten. And if you're not.**"

"**I will be. But if I am not, and I am not saying that I will not be, I am saying what happens if I am not — the money in the slot does not go anywhere, and if the stand is not being worked then there is nothing to put money in, and if you put money in a slot at a stand that nobody is working that is money gone.**"

"**So what do I do Monday.**"

"**Come at ten. If I am not there at ten then come at eleven, and if I am not there at eleven then the money in the slot is not being taken by me, and I would want to know that, and I would want somebody to tell me, and nobody in this borough has ever asked me one single thing, and you have just asked me three and I have given you three, and that is the whole of what has happened here in twenty-six years.**"

---

**And here is the whole of it, and it is the finding of the second week of a heat in a borough where every instrument in it is pointed at a different question.**

**A woman with a barrow and a pencil asked a man at a stand for ordinary information, and he gave it to her, and it was the correctest thing anybody has said in ten days.**

**And that is not findability, and it is not the same shape as anything else in this borough, and it is not going to be put in the same sentence as anything else in this borough.**

A man who charges four pounds a bucket is the most findable man in the lowland, and has been for twenty-six years, and nobody did that to him and nobody is going to, and a paragraph in a minute cannot tell a stand from a bollard. A man a price makes findable and a man a shape in a room makes findable are two different men, and a woman of about sixty at the barrier end of a street has said so four times over nine months and is right, and they are not the same man, and there is no list in this city that could hold both without ending up holding a telephone number for every one of them.

**What happened on Sunday morning at half past six was neither of those things. It was a person asking a person, in a doorway, in a heat, and it lasted about two minutes, and it will be gone by Wednesday.**

**Nobody in this borough told him he was findable. He is not on a list and there is no list and there is nowhere in this city to put him. He has not been asked one thing by anybody in this borough in twenty-six years. A woman with a barrow asked him three on Sunday morning and he answered all three, and she is not going to ask him a fourth, and he said so himself.**

**And she wrote his face on the back of her hand in pencil because she had no paper. That is not a record. That is not a form, and there is no box on the back of a hand, and nobody in this borough is going to see it but the woman and the man, and she is going to rub it out on Monday when she does not need it.**

**And that is the whole of what a heat wave has done to this borough so far. It has not made anybody findable. It has made one woman find one man, and it cost her a pencil, and it lasted about two minutes, and there is no instrument in this city that could see it and there is not going to be one, and that is the correct outcome, and it is the only thing anybody got right this fortnight without anybody paying for it.**

---

**AND SHE WROTE HIS FACE DOWN ON THE BACK OF HER HAND, IN PENCIL, BECAUSE SHE HAD NO PAPER, AND HE SAW HER DO IT, AND HE SAID NOTHING, AND SHE ASKED HIM NOTHING, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT THE SECOND WEEK OF A HEAT PRODUCED IN THIS BOROUGH.**

Nobody was harmed. Nobody was helped. The hall opened at about ten past seven and about thirty people were in it and the argument was not in it.

**The register of narrowings is at nineteen entries and the blank line under the nineteenth is a hundred and seventy-nine days old and it did not move. The rota has still not been rebuilt and cannot be, and the board stands at seven of nine with two chairs empty and no chair of that body at all, and the lowland cannot be seated, and the reason has not been fixed and is not going to be, and a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and it is not going to be refuted in the third week of May, and none of the nine people in that corridor said a word about any of it.**

**And they went back down the stairs at about half past seven.**
