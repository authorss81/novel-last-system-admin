# Chapter 0168 — Two Nail Holes

Tuesday the seventh of July 2015, from about half past six in the morning, a shop in Tideglass; and from about eleven o'clock at night, the stockroom at the back of the same shop.

**A thing that was in that stockroom in June is not in it in July and nobody in that shop has been told anything about it, and nobody in that shop is going to be. The looking was done by a man of twenty-four and it took him about four minutes and it is the first time anybody in this borough has gone looking for anything in nine months. A man of thirty-two was in the same room at eleven at night with about four seconds of an idea available to him and did not use it and knows who did the looking. Nothing is written down, nobody is thanked, and he said nothing.**

---

The shop opens at half past six and has opened at half past six for about nine years, and the man who opens it is about twenty-four, and he is not on a rota, and he has not been on one since he was about eighteen, and he answers to nobody and is not findable from any record in this city.

**He has a stockroom at the back with no window that opens and a fan in it that a man of about twenty-six brought in on the twenty-second of June and said *that'll be about four degrees* and went back to his van. It was for a stockroom at thirty-one degrees in a heatwave. It is July.**

---

**AND AT ABOUT TEN PAST SEVEN ON TUESDAY MORNING HE WENT IN FOR A CRATE, AND HE LOOKED FOR THE FAN, AND HE LOOKED PROPERLY, AND IT TOOK HIM ABOUT FOUR MINUTES.**

Behind the door. On the shelf with the paper and the string and the two tins of screws. Under the bench. Behind the shelving unit that is not fastened to anything.

Nothing.

**Nobody in this borough has gone looking for anything in nine months. In nine months this borough has had a heat, a spill, a page, a row, a chair on a landing, a fan, a jug of water and a great many correct answers, and not one of those required anybody to look for anything, and on the seventh of July a man of twenty-four in a shop in Tideglass opened a door and moved a crate and spent about four minutes looking for a fan he had been given the use of for a fortnight.**

And then he put the crate under the window where he had wanted it, and came out, and shut the door, and did the shop.

---

**AND HE DID NOT RING ANYBODY, AND THE NUMBER FOR THE MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SIX IS IN A BOOK ON THE BACK OF THE COUNTER, AND HE LOOKED AT THE BOOK.**

He looked at the book for about four seconds. He did not pick it up. There is nothing in this borough he could have said on the telephone that would have produced the fan, and there is nothing he could have said that would not have produced a conversation about why the room was still thirty-one degrees in July, and he does not want that conversation, and he is twenty-four.

**So the fan is in a van somewhere in this borough and it is switched off and there is no instrument in this city, and no person in this city, that could find out where it went on the first of July, and nobody is going to ask, and that is correct, and it is the cheapest thing that has happened in that shop in years.**

**And here is the whole of what he did not do in the rest of that morning, and it is a list of things that were available to him and were not taken, and nobody in this borough is going to know that they were available.**

He did not buy another fan. There is a fan in a box in the back of that shop that he bought himself off a stall on the market some years ago for eight pounds and it is in the box and it has no blade guard and he does not put it up because he does not like the sound of it.

He did not telephone the man of about twenty-six. He did not telephone anybody else who might have been in the room.

He did not write it in the book on the back of the counter where he writes down the things he has ordered and the things he has been given, and he has never written a thing in that book that somebody gave him, and he did not start on the seventh of July.

He did not tell the man of about thirty-five that anything had changed, and there was nothing to tell him, and if he had told him he would have been a person who knew something.

**None of those is a decision. They are what happens when a thing goes that nobody in the room was going to be asked about, and a man of twenty-four made five small not-things before seven o'clock in the morning and has not thought about any of them since and is not going to.**

---

**AND THERE ARE FOUR PEOPLE IN THIS BOROUGH WHO KNOW THAT A FAN WAS IN THAT ROOM, AND THEY ARE THREE PEOPLE AND A WOMAN AT A COUNTER, AND THE THREE ARE THE MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SIX WHO BROUGHT IT, THE SHOPKEEPER, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-TWO WHO WAS IN THE SHOP AT HALF PAST FOUR ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF JUNE WITH A BOTTLE OF WATER HE HAD ALREADY PAID FOR.**

The fourth is the woman of about fifty at the counter on the second floor of the Old Ward, who has been there eleven years and who is not the woman who comes down to the ground floor at about ten and about three. She knows because the shopkeeper told her in about four words, in June, on a Friday, in a queue, on his way out, because he did not know who else in this city he was going to tell.

**The man of about thirty-five who sleeps in that room does not know. He has never known. He washes at a place outside this borough and comes in at about eleven at night and goes out at about half past six, and he is not a customer and he has not taken anything, and nobody has ever established where he sleeps and nobody is going to, and the cheapest privacy device in this city depends entirely on nobody being able to tell the difference between a thing that was never there and a thing that was taken away.**

---

**AND THE MAN OF THIRTY-SIX WITH THE CLIPBOARD AND THE PAINT TIN IS COUNTING SHELVES IN A DIFFERENT BUILDING ON A DIFFERENT MORNING AND HAS NOTHING TO DO WITH ANY OF IT.**

**AND AT ABOUT ELEVEN O'CLOCK AT NIGHT A MAN OF THIRTY-TWO WENT INTO THAT STOCKROOM AND STOOD IN IT FOR ABOUT TWENTY SECONDS AND DID NOT LOOK FOR THE FAN.**

He knew on the Friday that it had gone. Nobody told him. The man of twenty-four had a particular way of putting the crate down on the Friday afternoon and a particular way of not mentioning a thing, and a man who has spent a long time learning to read rooms that nobody has told him anything in can read that in about a second.

**He had about four seconds available to him in which he could have said *the fan's gone* to a man of twenty-four, and the four seconds would have bought about nine weeks of a man of twenty-four telling him things, and that is the whole of the value of it, and that is also the whole of the danger of it, and he has been at the end of rooms like that three times in nine months and it went wrong every time and he did not know it was going to go wrong any of the three times.**

So he said nothing, and he stood there, and he looked at the wall.

There are two nail holes in that wall, about two feet apart, a little above where the shelf line used to be, and the paint around them is not the same colour as the paint around them, and nothing else in that room has a mark on it at all.

---

**THE ROOM IS THIRTY-ONE DEGREES AT ELEVEN O'CLOCK AT NIGHT IN JULY, AND IT WAS THIRTY-ONE DEGREES AT ELEVEN O'CLOCK AT NIGHT IN APRIL, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FIVE CAME IN AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND WENT UP AND PUT HIS BAG AGAINST THE WALL ON THE SIDE WITH NO HOLES IN IT, AND HE DID NOT ASK ANYBODY ANYTHING, AND NOBODY ASKED HIM ANYTHING, AND HE IS NOT A CUSTOMER AND HE IS NOT FINDABLE AND HE IS NOT OWED ANYTHING BY ANYBODY INCLUDING HIMSELF.**

**Nobody in that stockroom was helped in July. It is not a rescue and it is not a failure and the two nail holes will still be two nail holes in a year and there is no way in this city to find out what was hanging on them, and that is not a design and it is not a decision, and it is the cheapest privacy device in the city and it is not going to be written down anywhere.**

The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and twenty-three days old. The card in the slot is a hundred and thirty-seven days old and nobody has taken it out of it. The letter in the tray is a hundred and thirty-nine days old, it is under two other pieces of post, and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And the four of them who know are not going to be a fifth, because there is no room in this borough for a fifth, and the reason there is no room for a fifth is that a fan is a thing and not a person, and a thing that is not there cannot be counted, and a thing that is not there cannot be missed by anybody who does not know that it was ever there, and there are four who know and all four are one of three people or a woman at a counter, and one of the three is a man of thirty-two who was not asked and is not going to be.**
