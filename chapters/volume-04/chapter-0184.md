# Chapter 0184 — A Bottle On A Step

Friday the thirty-first of July 2015, from about two in the afternoon, a first-floor room over a shop.

**The standing of this borough, said out loud in daylight, without answering anything and without improving anything. It is the fifth time anybody has said it and it is not better than the other four and it is not worse. It is the last ten days of July: two of the things in it have been going for seven weeks, one has been gone for a month, one has got a second copy, and one is new and is not a document and not a form and not a page and not a building. He is not on the agenda of anything. There were five people in the room and nobody was in charge and nobody took minutes.**

---

**THE END OF MAY.** A page in a tray with a space at the bottom of it for a person to agree, and the space empty, not in force, not gone round anybody, never going to be signed. Read by one person who was not asked to read it, who said so out loud in daylight, and who telephoned a building the next morning and said it a second time. The office wrote nothing down, because there is nowhere to write it.

**THE EIGHTEENTH OF FEBRUARY.** A letter in a tray on a second floor, sealed, under two pieces of post, not opened in front of a second person, not answered. **THE TWENTIETH OF FEBRUARY.** A card in a slot at a counter on the ground floor, folded once, no crest, no reference, no box, and the person at that counter has not read it and is not going to.

**THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF NOVEMBER.** A blank line under the nineteenth entry of a register, two hundred and forty-seven days, the longest it has ever been, the only fixed point in that building. Entry nineteen is a yard light. The maintenance is a bulb. The bulb is out. Nobody is obliged.

---

**THE SECOND WEEK OF JUNE AND THE WORST HEAT IN NINETEEN YEARS.** Nine doors and the same correct word at every one of them, and the word was nothing, and it was printed on the nineteenth with four streets in a column headed *location* and no name box on it.

**That sheet is in its seventh week, and so is a chair on a landing. There are two sheets.** The second one has a column headed *date seen again* with **no name, no initials** printed under it, and the space is empty and is going to stay empty, and the June sheet was not withdrawn. Nobody in any of the nine buildings knows there are two.

Also that week: a laundry round with the last four stops done first at a cost to a hospital bed, and none of it on a form, and a roster of the people who did it would be the only instrument in this city that could make them findable.

---

**THE SECOND HALF OF JUNE.** A grant in a sentence, and a man of thirty-eight wrote *agreed* and a date on the back of a woman's page, **and the bill for the paper landed on a man of sixty who fills a butt under his own downpipe, and nobody is going to tell him about a timer.** A report from 2011 on a shelf nobody opened. A room painted, and a tray of dead circulars into a cupboard, because a list costs a person and a tray costs a Friday.

**That tray is gone. It went into a sack on the twenty-fourth of this month and the sack went into a skip, and it is the first time in nine months that a piece of this borough's paperwork has been destroyed, and it is not said whether that is a loss or a correction.**

---

**THE FIRST WEEK OF JULY.** A form dated the twenty-third of June went down a lane and a man of sixty told the man carrying it to go away before he had been asked anything, and nothing is helped. A woman of twenty-nine put a shop's telephone number on a card in a shop window with nothing else on it.

**A fan went out of a stockroom in Tideglass on the first of July and it has been gone for a month.** There are two nail holes in a wall in that room and no way in this city to find out what was hanging on them.

---

**AND THE FIRST FORTNIGHT OF JULY, WHICH NOBODY ELSE WOULD LIST.**

Two trestle tables and a box of water bottles on a pavement on the far side of the market since about the third of July, with nothing written on them, **and a crate beside them that nobody brought, that nobody has claimed, and that is now a place.** A man of sixty-eight has sold fruit off that frontage for nine years and does not know who put it there.

A hut on a compound left unlatched on the twenty-first, and four miles away a market hall still without a cistern, and a woman of fifty-three there cleans it herself and does not know why. **Nobody is going to look at that, and not out of discretion. Because she is written down.**

A man of fifty-nine carried four bags up four floors on the twenty-second because he was going down anyway, and a woman of sixty-seven said thank you for a corridor in June, and the thanking did not attach to the bags.

On the twenty-third a man of sixty-nine said one true thing about a street he has lived on for twelve years, and four people agreed with him, **and that street now has a person in a building who is right about it.**

A cupboard was emptied on a Friday. A bench was not looked at, and a woman of seventy-one wiped a slat with a cloth from her own pocket, and nobody in that block will ever know. **That is the eleventh time this borough has been kept going by a person nobody is obliged to name, and it is not counted, because counting it is the thing that would stop it.**

A woman of forty-nine told a stranger on her doorstep that she would do it in August, and the count of standing refusals did not move, and it is eleven and not twelve.

And a corner shop behind a market has a second fridge in it that could not have been justified to anybody in April, and a woman of sixty-five has said the same sentence about a card in her window about thirty times in three weeks and is not going to stop.

---

**AND THE THING THAT IS NEW, AND IT IS THE THIRD SMALLEST THING ANYBODY IN THIS BOROUGH HAS PRODUCED. THE CHAIR ON A LANDING IS SMALLER AND SO ARE THE TABLES.**

**Since about the twentieth of July there has been a bottle of water on a top step outside a door on one of those four streets.** It is there in the morning and it is not there in the evening. Nobody has said whose it is and nobody has moved it, the people on that street have not mentioned it, and one of them has drunk out of it on most mornings since.

**It is not a document and not a form and not a page and not a building and not a rota and not a service, nobody is required to put it there or take it away, and a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped.**

---

**AND THE REST OF IT, WHICH DID NOT MOVE IN THE TEN DAYS.**

A drawer under a counter with no key on it and no list in it, a tray on top of it that is not a list, and nobody can say how many; the only figure ever written about it is nine and that was in March. Three pieces of paper in a van since about Christmas that nobody owns. A sentence about a catch tray that went into that drawer on the twentieth of May, which nobody in this borough knows is there, including him. A public report with a paragraph number on it, never answered, and a machine still running. Two conditions on one wall that do not agree, and two letters and one word in pencil on an enamel strip nobody has read. **Nothing in this borough defines *holder*.**

Seven of nine, two chairs empty, no chair of that body at all, not met since the middle of January. The ninth seat is fifty-four, unpaid, findable at a shopfront and at an address, and has not been told anything. The lowland is not on the layer and cannot be seated, because a node with no party is not a party and a place is not a person.

A gate that goes up at seven on a Sunday morning and stops at six on the Sunday morning after that, six days long and not a week, with no date on it anywhere in this city. **Nobody has handed one over since the fifteenth, and the hour between six and seven was not handed over either.** The row from eleven o'clock to half past one at a hospital is empty and does not say *vacant*, and a porter's Thursday is still refused in his own handwriting with a second line under it in somebody else's hand, and nobody has looked at the second line.

And a man of forty-four nobody can ask, a man of forty-nine nobody has told, a man of forty-eight nobody has asked, and a man of thirty-two who is four hundred yards away on purpose and has stopped counting.

**AND THE SENTENCE IS THE ONE THAT HAS BEEN TRUE IN THIS BOROUGH SINCE THE SECOND OF JUNE, AND WHICH A FAN, A PAGE, A ROW, A COLUMN, A CARD, A CRATE, A BAG, A BENCH, A BOTTLE AND A CLOTH DID NOT REFUTE: A PERSON WHO IS NOT COUNTED IS NOT A PERSON WHO IS HELPED.**

---

**AND THE LAST OF IT, WHICH IS A QUESTION, AND NOT ABOUT A PERSON, AND NOT ABOUT A FLOOD BARRIER, A SCHOOL CROSSING, A COUNTER-AMENDMENT, A SECOND SIGNATURE, A PROVISIONAL CIVIC STANDING, A NINETY-DAY RENEWAL OR AN ORDER.**

There are more things in this borough that have been done once, correctly, and then not done again and not written down, than there were in June. **All of them are still true and none of them is on anything, and the two done most often were done by people who are owed nothing, did not know they were doing them, and will not be asked. In nine months not one of them has ever been proved.**

A thing done once and not written down is a thing that four people remember differently, and if one of them talks it is a story, and if a building hears a story it is a record.

So which of them is the first one somebody in this borough will be able to prove, and is it going to be proved by a person who was standing there, or by a person who reads about it afterwards.
