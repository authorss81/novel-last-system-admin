# Chapter 0172 — The Landing

Wednesday the fifteenth of July 2015, from about ten past eight in the evening, a landing on the second floor of a block of flats in Tideglass.

**A chair goes out on that landing at about eight in the evening and comes in again at about six in the morning, and it has been doing that since about the middle of June, and it belongs to somebody in that block and it is not lost. It is an ordinary day and the chair is not dealt with, and the not-dealing is the correct outcome. Somebody who is not him did one ordinary small thing with it for no reason and he saw it and he did not write it down, and whether that was the right thing is not said. Nothing was resolved and nothing was minuted and nobody was thanked. He asked no question of anybody.**

---

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THAT LANDING, at about the height of a person's eye, screwed to the wall by the meter cupboard, and it defines nothing and nothing on it defines *holder*, and it requires nothing of anybody:**

> **A CHAIR ON A LANDING IS NOT A SHIFT AND NOT A ROTA.**
> **NO PERSON IS REQUIRED TO PUT A CHAIR OUT, BRING A CHAIR IN, WASH A CHAIR, OR ANSWER FOR A CHAIR.**
> **A CHAIR ON A LANDING IS AVAILABLE TO ANY PERSON WHO WANTS IT.**

---

It had been raining since about five and it stopped at about half past seven, and at about eight o'clock a chair came out of a door on that landing and went to the far end by the window and stood in the shade with its back to the brick.

**It is a wooden chair with a vinyl seat and it weighs about as much as a chair. It came out of a flat and it goes back into a flat and it belongs to somebody in that block and it is not lost and it is not a charity and it is not lent and it is not booked and it is not on anything.**

Nobody is required to put it out or to bring it in, or to wipe it, or to know that it has been out.

---

**HE HAS BEEN ON THAT LANDING FOUR TIMES SINCE THE MIDDLE OF JUNE AND HE HAS NEVER SEEN ANYBODY BRING THE CHAIR OUT OR TAKE IT IN, AND HE HAS NEVER ASKED, AND HE DOES NOT KNOW WHICH FLAT IT COMES FROM, AND NOBODY IN THAT BLOCK WOULD TELL HIM IF HE ASKED, AND HE IS NOT GOING TO ASK.**

It is the smallest thing anybody in this borough has produced since the fourth of September. It is smaller than a bin rota and smaller than a jug of water put on a crate for a stranger, and it is the only one of them that nobody in this borough can describe afterwards without sounding as though they are showing off.

He has thought about it three times in four weeks and each time he has got to the same place and stopped, and the place is this.

**A thing with a space at the bottom of it for a name can be refused.** That is not a flaw in it. That is what the space is for. A person can look at a thing with a name on it and say no, and the no holds, and nobody in this borough is going to come and ask the person why they said no, and a thing with a name on it is the safest thing there is.

**A thing with nothing on it cannot be refused and cannot be required either.** And that is very nearly the whole of what this borough has built in nine months, and he has believed it since the fourth of September, and it is nearly all of it and it is not all of it, and the two that are not are why he has got to the same place three times and stopped.

**And there are two of the first kind in this city. Both of them are in buildings.** He has been through both of them in his head about nine times since June and every time he has got to the same place, and the place is that they are the whole of the argument against him and he cannot answer either, and he has never seen either.

He knows one of them. He was in a room with it in the second week of June and he did not read it and he was not asked about it and he said nothing, and a woman of twenty-nine who was not asked to read it has said so out loud in daylight and again on the telephone.

**He has not got a note of the other one. Nobody in this borough has got a note of the other one. That is not because of him, and it is not because anybody is keeping it quiet, and if he asked the question in a room in daylight there would be about four people in the room who would each answer him differently and none of them would be able to say what it is for.**

And he is not going to ask it, on a landing, at ten past eight in the evening, in the rain, on his own, to a chair.

---

**AND AT ABOUT TEN PAST EIGHT A DOOR ON THE OTHER SIDE OF THAT LANDING OPENED AND A MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY-FIVE CAME OUT WITH A SHOPPING BAG AND PUT IT DOWN ON THE LANDING FLOOR.**

He had put the bag on the chair at about half past six when he came in from wherever he had been, which was the thing to sit on, and there is a landing and a bag and a chair and no table on that landing and everybody who has ever needed to put a bag down on a landing has put it on a chair.

The seat of the chair was wet from the rain and he had put his hand on the top of the back of it and looked at the seat and did not sit down.

Then he picked the bag up off the seat and put it on the floor on the other side of the landing, about two feet away from where the chair was standing, and straightened it so it was not going over, and went back inside and shut the door.

**That is the whole of what he did and it took him about four seconds and he did not do it for the man of thirty-two, who was standing at the other end of that landing with nothing in his hands, and he did not do it for the next person to come out, and he did not do it for anybody, and he said nothing to anybody and nobody said anything to him.**

---

**AND THE MAN OF THIRTY-TWO SAW IT, AND HE DID NOT WRITE IT DOWN, AND HE IS NOT GOING TO, AND IT IS NOT GOING ANYWHERE.**

A man of seventy-five put a shopping bag on a floor so that a stranger did not have to move a wet chair. That is a true sentence and it is a small one and it is a sentence about a man of seventy-five on a landing in July, and there is nowhere in this borough to put it, and if somebody were to say that it was the right thing then they would be making a claim about a stranger's wet chair that they have no standing to make, and if somebody were to say that it was a stupid thing to do then the man of seventy-five would not hear it and it would not matter.

**It is not a service. It is not a rota. It is not a rota that has not been written down. It cannot be pointed at and it cannot be repeated and it is not going to be a habit, and it is not a failure either, and there is no version of this evening in which anybody in this borough is helped and there is no version of it in which anybody is not.**

The rain had stopped. The chair is on that landing. It will be brought in at about six in the morning by somebody in that block who nobody in this borough can name and who is not on anything and who has never been asked and never will be.

Nobody in that block was thanked and nobody refused to be thanked. The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and thirty-one days old and the bulb on the nineteenth is out. The card in the slot is a hundred and forty-five days old and it is not going to be emptied. The letter in the tray on the floor above is a hundred and forty-seven days old and it has two pieces of post lying on it and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and the smallest thing anybody in this borough has produced since the fourth of September is a chair on a landing, and a bag on a floor, and neither of them is anybody's duty, and it is still true in July that a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped.**
