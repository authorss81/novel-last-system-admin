# Chapter 0175 — Nobody Put Them There

Saturday the eighteenth of July 2015, from about eleven in the morning, a pavement on the far side of the market; and from about five in the afternoon, the same pavement.

**A thing that was put out in the first week of July is still being put out in the last week of July, nobody has asked anybody about it once, and in three weeks it has picked up a container that nobody authorised. He asked no question of anybody and nobody asked him anything. Nothing was written down, it is not a rota, and it is not going to become one.**

---

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THAT PAVEMENT, printed and screwed to the pillar at the end of that shop's frontage at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing and nothing on it defines *holder*, and it requires nothing of anybody:**

> **A THING ON A PAVEMENT IS NOT A SERVICE.**
> **NO PERSON IS REQUIRED TO PUT ONE OUT, BRING ONE IN, COUNT ONE, OR ANSWER FOR ONE.**
> **A THING ON A PAVEMENT MAY BE LEFT ON A PAVEMENT.**

---

Two trestle tables and a cardboard box of water bottles have been on that pavement since about the third of July, put out at about eleven and brought in at about five, in the shade, with nothing written on them and nothing hanging over the front of them. A man who works in the cold store at the back of that shop puts them out, because he was already going out into the sun at about eleven with a sack truck and because there was a box of water going soft behind the counter that nobody was drinking.

Nobody is required to put them out or to bring them in. There is no list of them, no name on them and no sign.

**And on the Friday of the tenth of this month a crate appeared beside the box, and nobody brought it.**

---

**AND BY TODAY IT IS NOT A THING THAT HAPPENED ONCE. IT IS A PLACE.**

It is a plastic crate, the kind a shop gets fruit in, and it is about eighteen inches long, and it is standing on its short end against the leg of the near table with two full bottles in it and four empties standing up in the bottom.

**Nobody in this borough put that crate there.** The man from the cold store did not bring it out of the cold store, and there is nothing in this borough that says he did.

He did not decide to have it either. What happened is this. On the Friday of the tenth somebody put an empty in the box, because the box is for water and the box is what was there, and the man carrying the box in could not put a bottle back into it in front of people without also carrying the thing he had brought out, so he picked the empty out, turned it over, and stood it in the crate, and the crate was behind the shop already, for the onions, and it was in the right place on the way out.

**That is not a decision. That is a Tuesday, or a Friday, or whichever day it was, and the difference between a decision and a Tuesday is that a decision can be asked about and a Tuesday cannot.**

By the eighteenth the crate has a spot. It is against the leg of the near table and not beside the bin, and there are four empties standing up in it, and there is one that has been turned over so the label is facing the wall.

---

**AND THE THING THAT HAS HAPPENED, IN THREE WEEKS, TO A PAVEMENT, IS THAT SOMEBODY PUT A CONTAINER ON IT. THAT IS THE WHOLE OF THE HABIT. IT WAS NOT AUTHORISED BY ANYBODY.**

The tables cannot be pointed at. A table is furniture and a box is a box, and neither of them says a word to a stranger, and a stranger can stand at the front of that shop for as long as he likes and there is nothing in front of him that belongs to anybody.

**A crate is different. A crate is a container, and a container is a place a thing can be put, and a place a thing can be put is the first half of an arrangement.**

Nobody in this borough has ever written down that there are two trestle tables on that pavement. Nobody in this borough has ever written down the crate either, and there are four people in this borough who know about the crate, and three of them work at that shop and the fourth is a man of thirty-two standing at the end of the frontage at about eleven o'clock with nothing in his hands.

**And the crate is the only object on that pavement that a person could be asked about, because a crate has an end and an inside, and the man who fills it is a man.**

---

**AND HE DID NOT ASK. HE HAS NOT ASKED ANYBODY ANYTHING SINCE THE TWENTY-FIFTH OF FEBRUARY AND HE IS NOT GOING TO START ON A SATURDAY MORNING WITH A CRATE.**

He stood at the end of that frontage and looked at it for about a quarter of an hour, and the sun came round onto the pavement at about ten past eleven and the shade went off the tables, and the man from the cold store came out, saw that the sun had moved, and moved both tables about four feet along without being asked by anybody, the way you move a chair.

That is how a pavement is in the last week of July. The shade moves about four feet every two hours and everybody who puts a table in the shade knows that and nobody has ever written down how it works.

---

**AND AT ABOUT HALF PAST ELEVEN A MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-EIGHT WHO SELLS FRUIT OFF THAT SAME FRONTAGE ON SATURDAYS AND HAS DONE FOR NINE YEARS TOOK HIS TABLE DOWN THE PAVEMENT AND SAID THE THING OUT LOUD, TO THE PAVEMENT AND NOT TO ANYBODY.**

"**You have been looking at that crate.**"

"**I have.**"

"**Do not ask him.**"

"**I had not got that far.**"

"**You will, though. Everybody does. I have had that frontage nine years Saturdays and I will tell you the honest answer, which is that I do not know who put that crate there, and I am not going to pretend I have an idea. I have been here since about twenty past ten. It was there at about half past nine when I came with the van. That is what I know and that is all I know.**"

"**Right.**"

"**And here is the part you are actually asking, which is why nobody is going to tell you either.**"

He put the leg of his table down and looked up and down that frontage, which was busy, which is what a frontage is on a Saturday in July.

"**Nobody is going to tell you because nobody knows, and also because the second one of us says the name of the thing out loud to the other one, then it is a thing two people know about, and then the thing has got a route, and once it has got a route then in about a month there is a young man with a clipboard who comes up to you and says there is a scheme for water on the high street. I have seen it happen to a fella who put a table out with a box of water in 2011 and he was very pleased with himself, and by the following June there was a form. The form had a name box on it. The form was worse than the table. At least the table was free.**"

"**And the crate.**"

"**The crate is the part that worries me and the crate is not his. The crate is nobody's. If that crate is a bottle rack, then that is a thing on a pavement with a man's routine in it, and a man's routine in a thing is the whole difference between a table and a facility.**"

"**And if nobody asks, it just goes on.**"

"**If nobody asks, it goes on until somebody with a clipboard asks, and by then it will have been going on long enough that somebody can say it should not stop, and that is a hearing, and there is nobody in this borough who can hold a hearing.**"

---

**AND THAT IS THE FINDING, AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME IN NINE MONTHS THAT ANYBODY IN THIS BOROUGH HAS FOUND OUT THAT A THING HAS AN ORIGIN NOBODY CAN NAME, AND THE ORIGIN IS NOT A DOCUMENT AND NOT A PERSON ANYBODY CAN BE ASKED ABOUT.**

Two trestle tables and a box of water have an owner. Everybody on that frontage knows the man from the cold store puts them out and has never once asked him whether he is paid for it, and he is not paid for it, and everybody knows he is not paid for it, and that is the whole of the arrangement.

**A crate has nobody.** Nobody brought it. Nobody agreed to it. Nobody is on anything. It arrived the way the tables arrived, which is that nobody decided, and a man put it where it went and it stayed where he put it.

And that is cheaper than the tables, and it is the first thing anybody in this borough has produced that is cheaper than a man doing a thing for nothing.

---

**AND HERE IS THE PART THAT IS GOING TO COST SOMEBODY, AND IT IS A CRATE, AND IT IS ABOUT NUMBERS, AND NOBODY IS GOING TO COUNT ANYTHING.**

There were four empties standing up in that crate at about half past eleven. There will be more at five, because the crate is where a person puts an empty when the box is full and the man carrying the box in is standing right there.

**Four empties in a crate on a pavement is a number of people who drank some water on a Saturday in July in a sun, and it is a number of bottles, and it is not the same number.**

It is not the same number because a person can drink two, and a person can pick one up and not drink it and put it back, and a person can take one for somebody else. It will be more on Wednesday than it is today and fewer on Monday and there is no way of knowing why, and if the number of empties is a number of people then it is a list of people with nobody's name on it, which is a thing this borough has already got two of and has never once wanted.

**So nobody counts them. Not the man, not the man of thirty-two, not the man of about sixty-eight twenty yards away with a fruit table and a good view of a crate. Nobody counts them and nobody is required to and the crate is not a return and nothing in this borough will ever be able to say how many people drank water on that pavement on the eighteenth of July, and that is correct, and it is the same reason a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and the reason in this case is about a crate and is much smaller and it goes the same way.**

---

**AND AT ABOUT FIVE THE MAN FROM THE COLD STORE CAME OUT AND PUT THE TABLES IN AND TOOK THE BOX IN AND THEN WENT BACK FOR THE CRATE, WHICH HE HAD TO GO ROUND THE SIDE FOR, AND HE PUT FOUR EMPTIES AND TWO EMPTIES INTO A SACK ON THE WAY IN AND HE TOOK THE CRATE IN WITH THEM, AND THE PAVEMENT HAD NOTHING ON IT AT ALL BY ABOUT TEN PAST FIVE.**

He did not say anything. Nobody said anything to him. He has not been thanked and nobody has refused to be thanked and he will not be recognised and there is no form in this borough on which a person could write that a man carried a crate in on the eighteenth of July, and the reason there is not going to be one is that the crate would be on it and the crate is nobody's.

The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and thirty-four days old and a Saturday is not a reason to write on it and nobody is obliged. The card in the slot on the ground floor is a hundred and forty-eight days old and nobody has taken it out. The letter in the tray on the floor above is a hundred and fifty days old and it has two other pieces of post lying on it and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a crate came to a pavement that nobody agreed to, and nobody found out where it came from, and a man who works in a cold store is going to go round the side of that shop for a crate tomorrow at about five in the afternoon, and there is no document anywhere in this city that says so, and there is not going to be one.**
