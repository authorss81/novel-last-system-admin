# Chapter 0146 — Bay Two

Saturday the sixteenth of May 2015, from about half past nine in the morning, Bellow Market; and about twenty past ten, the yard behind it; and a bus turning head about four hundred yards from that yard, from about ten past ten.

**He asked no question of anybody in any of those three places and nobody asked him anything and he was introduced to nobody. The first person on the scene was a person with a bucket and a reason, and what that person did was correct, and it was not recognised, and it is not going to be, and nobody thanked him for it.**

---

The market was open and it was the third day of the heat and the whole of it had changed shape.

On Tuesday you could walk the length of it. On Friday you could walk it in about nine minutes and stop. On Saturday the traders had put trestle tables out into the middle of the aisle with cool boxes under them and jugs of water on them, and one man had a trailer of ice and was selling it by the bag, and the queue for the ice was longer than the queue for anything anybody had come to buy.

**Nobody had told them to do any of that. There is no instrument in this city that tells a market trader to put a table in an aisle in a heat, and there is no list of who has put one there, and the tables will be gone by Wednesday and nobody will ever have written any of it down.**

At the top of the market a man of fifty-eight was at his stand with a bucket and two urns and no drain, doing what he has done every morning since about 1989, and Jonas Mercer came down that aisle at about ten past ten and he did not stop and he was not spoken to and the man did not look at him, and that is the whole of what happened between them.

---

Behind the market there is a yard.

**The yard is not on any map anybody in this borough has, and it is on the borough's own sheet, and on the sheet it is a shape with a bay number in it. Bay two is the concrete apron between the market's back wall and a brick wall that belongs to a different building, and it is where a market-traders' association has a cleaning store in a rented unit, and it is where a van comes.**

**There is also a lock-up on that yard. A shutter, a padlock, and what the padlock is for.**

**There is no answer to that anywhere in this city. The man who used to answer for it is a market porter and has been one for thirty years and he is still at the market on Saturdays, and on Wednesday the twenty-sixth of November 2014, in a room in daylight, he was asked whether he would go on answering for the lock-up at Bellow Market, the shutter, the padlock and what the padlock is for, and he stated that he would not continue. No reason was recorded, at his request, and none was given. He was not asked a second question. The seat fell vacant that evening and it remains vacant, and no person may be required to take it, and there has been no answer since. Nobody in this borough has been asked a second time. The padlock is on. The shutter is down. And since the twenty-sixth of November nobody has written down what the padlock is for, and there is nowhere in this borough it could be written down.**

---

At about twenty past ten a white van came onto bay two and reversed up to the unit and stopped with its tailgate to the brick wall, and a man got out and put a board down and put a ramp under the tailgate, and there were four drums in the back of it and two on the board.

The van belonged to a cleaning contractor. It was a contractor's own van, the hose was the contractor's own hose, and the hose had a coupling on the end of it that had a date on the coupling because couplings have dates on them if anybody has ever looked at one, and nobody had ever looked at one.

**And here is the whole of the accident, and it takes about as long as it takes to turn your wrist, and it has happened as often a day in this city as anything else that is done properly, and no man in that yard did anything wrong in any way that anybody could put in a report.**

The pump was running. It had been running for about a minute and a half, which is what you do, because a pump running against a shut outlet is a pump you are about to find out something about. The assistant, who was twenty-two and on his third week, put his hand on the outlet valve and opened it, the way you open it, and the pressure in the hose went from nothing to something in the time it takes to turn your wrist, and the coupling on the end of a hose that is nineteen months old and has never been on an inspection list, because there is no inspection list and nobody has to keep one, came apart in two places.

About sixty litres went across the concrete.

**The gully is at the low corner of that apron, at the foot of the brick wall, and it is a cast grate the shape of a horseshoe set in the concrete, and it is the original one, and it has never been raised, and it is connected to a run that goes under the floor of the market.**

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT HE SAW FROM FOUR HUNDRED YARDS, AND HE DID NOT GO, AND BOTH OF THOSE THINGS ARE GOING TO STAY.**

It took him about a second and a half to work out what had happened and about another two seconds to work out that he was standing in the wrong place, and after that he did not move, and the reason he did not move is two reasons and the first one he has said out loud before and the second one he has not.

**The first one is the one that has been true since the twentieth of March: the finder is the handle. A man who is not on anything who walks into a chemical release becomes a man who was at a chemical release, and that is a thing that can be asked about, and the man who was at it cannot say no to anybody, ever, about any of it, and the only instrument in this borough that keeps a person from being askable is a person nobody points at.**

**The second one is that he would have been good at it.**

That is all. He would have been very good at it. He would have known what to do with the gully and the concrete and the downwind of the brick wall and the people who walk along that road to work in the morning, and he would have got somebody to do something about it inside four minutes, and there is not a person in that yard, on that road, or in this city who could have done it, and he was four hundred yards away and he knew that, and he stood there for about a quarter of an hour.

**A man standing off in the street is a man with a reason, and a reason is a thing that can be looked at, and the only amount of evidence you can get from that distance is whether a door is open or shut, and from where he was he could see the grate, and he could see that the grate was wet.**

---

**AND THE FIRST PERSON ON THE SCENE WAS NOT ANYBODY WHO HAD BEEN SENT.**

**He was a man with a bucket, and he had come up the road from a bus turning head with four buckets on a barrow because he is a man who has a barrow and buckets, and he was in that market at about ten past ten on a Saturday morning because he buys his food there, and he was on the yard because the yard is where the market's bins are and he is a man who has a reason to be near bins.**

**He has a stand at a bus turning head with a bucket on a chain and a board of forty slots, and a man who puts four pounds in a slot and takes a bucket and washes it and puts it back, and the man who does that has been the most findable man in the lowland for twenty-six years and nobody ever did that to him. He is not findable from any record in this city. He is on no list. He has no party, no reference, no holder field, no name box, no return, no rota, no minute and no form on which his name could be written, and the reason there is no record of him is not that he was missed. It is that nothing in this borough has ever had to write down a man who turns a bucket.**

**He got to that grate in about half a minute. He put down his barrow. He took the bucket he had come with, which was full of water he had paid four pounds for, and he put it on the split, and then he put his boot on the bucket, and he held it there.**

It is not much. It is a bucket and a boot and about two and a half minutes. The coupling was still going and the hose was still hissing and the grate was still taking everything it was given, and a full bucket under a split jet does very little except for about two and a half minutes, and those two and a half minutes meant that the water going into the gully under the floor of a food market was not a hundred per cent of the sixty litres, and it was not ninety, and it was not anything anybody will ever be able to state.

**Then the assistant, who was twenty-two, pulled the valve shut, and went for the isolation behind the unit, and the pressure came off, and the man with the boot took his boot off the bucket and put the bucket back on the barrow and went to wash it, because the bucket is a bucket and it is not a thing you leave.**

Nobody asked him anything. He was not asked where he had come from or what his name was or what he was doing. Nobody said thank you. Nobody said anything to him at all, and he did not wait to be spoken to, and he has been doing this for twenty-six years and nobody has ever waited to be spoken to.

---

**AND THIS IS THE FINDING, AND IT IS THE SAME FINDING AS THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF APRIL WITH A DIFFERENT THING ON THE END OF IT.**

**The first person on the scene will not be in the report.**

There will be a report. The contractor's own report will come in about a fortnight, and it will have a volume on it and a cause on it and a bay number on it and a recommendation in a box, and it will be a good report and every word of it will be correct, and in a few years somebody in a room will be able to put it on a table in front of a frightened person.

**And there is no field on it for a man with a bucket, because a man with a bucket is not a cause and not a volume and not a bay, and the day there is a field for him is the day he is on something.**

**And that is not a failure. There is no form in this borough on which anybody could record that a man in wet trousers stood on a bucket in that yard for two and a half minutes on the sixteenth of May, and there is not going to be one, and Jonas Mercer is not going to write one, and he wrote nothing down.**

---

What happened after that was ordinary and it happened to ordinary people.

The fire service came at about twenty-five to eleven. The water authority came before the fire service and had been called by somebody who was not a man with a bucket. The market opened at half past ten and closed at about eleven and opened again at about one and closed at about three, and traders who had water across the floor of their units threw stock away, and nine of them did, and one of them threw about six hundred pounds of it and stood in his own unit looking at it.

A woman of about sixty with asthma sat down on a crate at about ten to eleven because of the smell, and could not get up again for a while, and two men carried her out to the front and a paramedic came and she went home in an ambulance at about half past eleven.

**Nobody died. Nobody was rescued. Nothing was lucky and nothing was early. Nine units had water across the floor of them and nine traders threw stock away, and one of them threw about six hundred pounds of it. One person went to hospital in an ambulance for a thing that was not serious, and she had been in that yard for half an hour when it started, and nobody is going to be told about that in any document at all.**

---

**AND THEN IT WAS A SATURDAY, AND AT TEN O'CLOCK THAT NIGHT TWO TIRED MEN WHO ARE NOT ON ANYTHING READ A TERM OF SIX DAYS ALOUD TO EACH OTHER, AND IT BEGAN AT SEVEN THE NEXT MORNING WHEN THE GATE WENT UP, AND THERE IS NO DATE ON IT.**

**He was not in that building and he is not going to be in it, and nobody in this city has any way of knowing where he was that night, and nobody has ever been asked about any of those nights by anybody, and the reason there is no date on any of them is that a person is not required to be told, and there is nobody in this borough who has to write it down.**
