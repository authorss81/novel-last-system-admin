# Chapter 0165 — Under The Stairs

Monday the twenty-ninth of June 2015, from about eight in the morning, the second floor of the Old Ward.

**The room on that floor is being painted today, and so the shelves under the window are being cleared, and there is nowhere in this borough for a tray of post to go that is not a list, and a tray of post under a bundle of post is not a list. Nothing was opened. The card in the slot on the ground floor was not touched and the letter in the tray on the floor above was not touched and the post lying on the letter did not move. He asked no question of anybody and nobody asked him anything. A room in a building is being painted on a date that was set before any of this and that is a clock and not a threat and not a rescue and nobody in that building is going to be brought in because of it.**

---

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THAT FLOOR, at about shoulder height on the wall by the paint store, and it defines nothing and nothing on it defines *holder*, and it requires nothing of anybody:**

> **A TRAY ON A SHELF IN THIS BUILDING IS NOT AN ITEM.**
> **NO PERSON IS REQUIRED TO KEEP, MOVE, EMPTY, LIST OR ANSWER FOR A TRAY.**
> **A TRAY MAY BE PUT ANYWHERE IN THIS BUILDING.**

---

The man on the floor had been there since about ten to seven with a clipboard, a paint tin and a manila envelope, and he is about thirty-six and he is not on a rota and he is not on a board and he does not know why the room is being painted and has not asked, and when Jonas Mercer came up at about ten past eight the man had counted two of the four shelves and had not asked anybody anything either.

The room is one room. There is a desk, a chair, a window that does not open onto anything, a hardboard board on the wall that the paint is going on top of, and the shelves under the window, which are the reason the man is here.

**Nobody in this borough has ever decided what a shelf under a window in that room is for.** It takes trays. The trays come round on a Friday, they go out, and most of them come back. Some of them do not go out again, and when a tray does not go out again it sits on that shelf with a bundle of post on it, and it sits there until somebody needs the shelf, and the only two people who have ever needed that shelf are a man with a clipboard and a woman of about fifty with a key.

---

**AND THERE ARE FOUR TRAYS ON THE TOP SHELF, AND THREE OF THEM ARE NOT IN THE SAME POSITION, AND THE ARGUMENT ABOUT THEM TOOK ABOUT NINE MINUTES AND WAS DECIDED BY A PERSON WHO WAS ENTITLED TO DECIDE NOTHING.**

The first tray is not being moved. It has a bundle of post sitting on it and a buff card under the bundle, and the man of about thirty-six counted it last Tuesday, and it is going back on that shelf at the same angle when the paint is off, and nobody in that building has any business saying otherwise.

The second tray is the one the letter is in, and it is not on the shelf, and it is not in this room, and it did not move, and nobody lifted the post that is lying on it, and the woman of about fifty said four words about it when the man asked her which trays were going and then did not say anything else about it and he did not ask her a second time.

**The third tray is the one all of the rest of this is about.** It came back off the Friday round on the nineteenth of June with about sixty pieces of post in it, and it did not go out on the twenty-sixth because the twenty-sixth was a Friday in a cool week and there was nothing in circulation, and there has been nothing in circulation since, and the sixty pieces in it are not letters to anybody. They are internal circulars, three meeting notices for a group that has not met since March, a parking notice for a yard, and about fifty pages of something that was sent round in April and came back.

**And the fourth tray is empty and has been empty since the spring and nobody can say what it is waiting for.**

---

The woman of about fifty has been at the counter on that floor for eleven years and she came up at about half past eight with her coat off and she is not the woman who comes down to the ground floor at about ten and about three, and those two women are not the same person and have never been the same person.

She looked at the two trays for about half a minute and then she said:

"**There are three things you can do with that and I am not going to pretend there are four.**"

"**Right.**"

"**You can put it in a tray. And then it is a tray, and it goes on the Friday, and it comes back on the Friday, and it is on a shelf, and the shelf is what it is.**"

"**And the other two.**"

"**You can write a list of it. And then it is a thing somebody has made a list of, and a list has got a person on it who made it, because you cannot make a list without putting your hand on it, and then somebody can ask you what is on it, and you will have to remember.**"

"**And the third.**"

"**The cupboard under the stairs on the ground floor. Which is the cleaning things and the spare bulbs and the fire blanket, and which gets emptied on a Friday by whoever is on, and nobody looks in it, and nobody has ever looked in it.**"

The man of about thirty-six said nothing for a moment. **Nobody in that building is required to put anything anywhere, and the notice on the wall by the paint store says a tray may be put anywhere in the building, which is true, and which is the whole of the freedom anybody in that room has had all morning.**

"**The third one,**" he said.

"**You are going to have to tell the man with the van what it is,**" she said, "**because he is going to ask you, and he is right to ask you, and I am not going to stand in a doorway and say it for you.**"

"**It is post,**" he said.

"**It is post that came back off the Friday and did not go out,**" she said, "**and if you say it is post then in about a year somebody will put a date on it and call it a backlog, and then it will be a thing this building is behind on instead of a thing this building has got.**"

"**Right.**"

"**Say it's a tray.**"

"**It's a tray.**"

---

**AND THE FOURTH TRAY IS STILL ON THAT SHELF AND IT IS EMPTY AND IT HAS BEEN EMPTY SINCE THE SPRING, AND THE TWO OF THEM LOOKED AT IT AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID ANYTHING ABOUT IT, AND NOBODY HAS EVER ASKED IN THIS BOROUGH WHAT IT IS WAITING FOR.**

---

**AND HE CARRIED IT DOWN AT ABOUT NINE, AND IT IS HEAVIER THAN IT LOOKS, BECAUSE SIXTY PIECES OF POST WITH ELASTIC BANDS ON THEM IS NOT SIXTY PIECES OF POST.**

He went down the stairs with it against his chest and along the corridor and through the door into the vestibule and out into the street to put it in the back of a white van, and then he came back up and counted the third shelf, and Jonas Mercer went down with him and did not say anything and was not asked anything.

**And the cost of moving it is this, and it is not a large thing, and it is the whole of what happened on that floor today.**

The cupboard under the stairs on the ground floor is emptied on a Friday. The sixty pieces of internal circulars in that tray are dead paper. In about a year there will be a Friday and the tray will be in a skip or in a bin behind a shop, and nobody in this borough will be able to tell that it was ever on a shelf under a window in a room that has been painted, and the woman of about fifty will not remember either, and the man of about thirty-six will not remember, and the only two people who will ever have known that it was on that shelf are a man of thirty-two who was at the end of the corridor and a records supervisor who came off the bus on her own time once in May and put a piece of paper in a drawer with no key on it.

**And the reason it went in the cupboard and not anywhere else is not that the cupboard is better. It is that the other two places cost money.** A list costs a person. A tray costs a Friday.

---

**AND AT ABOUT TWENTY PAST TEN HE WENT BACK UP AND THE BUFF TRAY WAS ON THE SHELF AT THE SAME ANGLE, UNDER THE SAME BUNDLE OF POST, AND NOTHING WAS OPENED, AND NOBODY LOOKED AT IT AGAIN, AND NOBODY IS GOING TO ASK THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX WHETHER HE PICKED IT UP.**

A tray of post went out of a room in this borough on Monday the twenty-ninth of June and went into a cupboard on the ground floor of the same building, and there is no form in this borough on which a person could write down that it did, and there is not going to be one.

The register of narrowings is at nineteen entries and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and fifteen days old and it is still the only fixed point in that building, and entry nineteen is a yard light and the maintenance is a bulb and the bulb is out, and a repainted room is not a reason to write on a blank line and nobody is obliged. The card in the slot at the counter on the ground floor is a hundred and twenty-nine days old and it did not move this morning. The letter in the tray on the floor above is a hundred and thirty-one days old and it has two other pieces of post lying on it and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

Nobody in that building was helped and nobody in that building refused anything, and a woman of about fifty with eleven years in a room made a decision about a tray in about nine minutes that nobody will ever be able to check.
