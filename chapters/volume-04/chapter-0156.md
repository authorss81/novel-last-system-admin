# Chapter 0156 — Until Four O'Clock

Monday the fifteenth of June 2015, from about half past ten in the morning, a corridor, Tarn Street school; and about one o'clock in the afternoon, the same corridor.

**Two people were in that corridor who did not have to be in it, and nobody made either of them be there, and something was agreed in it in one sentence and the only thing written down was a number on a piece of paper and a name in a column on a sheet, and nobody in that corridor noticed either of those happening except one man who is not on the sheet and could not do a single thing about it. This is the second time since the twenty-first of May that a person in this borough who could have said no has said yes in a corridor, and nobody said so out loud, and the precedent is now two.**

---

The school had reopened last Wednesday on about three quarters of a tank, and it was about a sixth of a tank by half past ten this Monday, and the caretaker had worked that out on his own and the head teacher had worked it out on her own, and they had not told each other until the Friday, and by the Friday they had the same number and neither of them had got there by asking anybody.

**The caretaker is fifty-one. He has a ring of keys and he has been at that school thirty-one years and his key is a key and is not a holder, and he got a door open on the Friday of the fifth of June because getting a door open is what he has.**

The head teacher is about sixty. She has a folder and a mobile telephone she keeps checking. On the Friday of the fifth of June, at about ten to two, at the grain, she worked out that four thousand litres is three days in an open building and nine in a shut one, and she asked nobody, and the school shut until the Wednesday, and the school opened on the Wednesday.

**What neither of them had is a page that says what will be in that tank on Friday.**

---

**THE PAGE IS A PAGE OF WHAT IS IN A TANK THIS AFTERNOON. THAT IS ALL IT IS, AND THE WOMAN WHO WROTE IT IN A ROOM ON THE TWENTY-THIRD OF MAY WOULD SAY SO HERSELF IF ANYBODY ASKED HER, AND NOBODY IN THAT CORRIDOR HAS ASKED HER ANYTHING.**

The water office's sheet has Tarn Street marked full, in a box, in ink, with a tick beside it, and the tick is three weeks old and nobody has taken the tick off, and the tick is correct about the fifth of June.

"They're looking at that page and seeing a school with water in it," the caretaker said. "And there's a school with water in it. There's just not one on Monday."

---

**AND THEN A MAN CAME DOWN THAT CORRIDOR WHO HAD NOT BEEN ASKED TO, AND HE WAS NOT THERE FOR ANYBODY.**

Callum Nkemdirim, 38, from the water office, came at about half past ten with a folder under his arm and a stopcock key in his pocket, and he was going to look at the isolation valve under the stairs and then go to a different building, and the corridor is the way to the stairs.

Nobody had asked him to stop. He stopped because the head teacher came out of her room with a piece of paper in her hand and said four words to him in the passage, which were **can I ask you something**, and he said **yes**.

**And she asked for one thing, and it was one thing, and it was correct.**

The second bowser is booked for the Tuesday. The tank fills from about eleven until about two.

"**Four of the nine houses on that street are at the bottom of it. They cannot get up here before eight in the morning and they cannot carry a container up that hill in this heat, and if it shuts at two then they fill at two in the afternoon, in the sun, with children coming out of a school, and the ones who can do it are the ones who have somebody at home who can do it for them.**"

"**And you'd like it to stay till four.**"

"**I'd like it to stay till four.**"

"**It can stay till four.**"

---

**AND HE SAID IT IN ONE SENTENCE AND HE DID NOT HAVE TO SAY IT AT ALL, AND THE LORRY IS BOOKED FOR THE DAY EITHER WAY AND THERE IS NOWHERE ELSE IT HAS TO BE, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT IT COST ANYBODY, AND THAT IS WHY IT IS THE DANGEROUS ONE.**

"No," he said, more or less, in the way a man says no to a thing that is not a no. "**Yes, it can stay till four. I will put four on the sheet. I am telling you that is not a favour and it is not a special one either, because the lorry is out from eleven and it is paid for by the day, so if it stands there till four it is standing there for nothing. If I had said no to that I would be a person who turns a lorry round in this heat for a preference.**"

"**Thank you.**"

"**It is all right. I have written four on a piece of paper and that is all I have done.**"

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT NOBODY IN THAT CORRIDOR NOTICED, AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME IN NINE MONTHS THAT A ROTA IN THIS BOROUGH HAS WANTED TO INCLUDE ANYBODY, AND IT IS THE OLDEST THING THERE IS.**

Callum put the folder on the windowsill to get to his stopcock key, and the folder was open, and there was a delivery sheet in it with a column on the right-hand side headed **ON SITE**, and it was empty on the Tuesday line and empty on the Thursday line and empty on all five.

"**Put your name and I'll ring you when it's on its way,**" he said, to the caretaker, who was the one who would be there.

The caretaker said no. He said it in one syllable and he did not give a reason, and it was correct, and the head teacher said:

"**Put mine. I'll be here anyway.**"

And a man of thirty-eight wrote a woman's name in a column on a delivery sheet because she was in a corridor and it was the obvious thing to write, and he did it in one movement, and nobody thought about it, and nobody thanked anybody, and nobody in that corridor was ever going to be asked about it.

**And he noticed, at the end of that corridor, with nothing in his hands, and he could not do one single thing about it, and he is not on it, and he cannot be.**

---

**A ROTA IS NOT A PIECE OF PAPER. A ROTA IS A PERSON WHO CANNOT SAY NO.**

He has been at this nine months and he has watched the whole of it: a name in a column on a Tuesday is not a name in a column on a Tuesday, it is a person who is now the person somebody rings when a thing is late, and a person who has become the person somebody rings can never afterwards be the person nobody rings.

**And the head teacher put her own name in it, freely, in a corridor, in one movement, for a reason of her own, and nobody asked her to and nobody is going to make her.**

Nobody is required to be in a column on a delivery sheet. She is in it. That is a different sentence and it is the only interesting sentence in that building and it went past six people.

---

**AND THE PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT ON THAT SHEET ARE THE PEOPLE WHO WERE IN A CAR PARK.**

There are five lines on that delivery sheet with something in them now. Two of them are standpipes at the ends of streets and three of them are buildings. The four streets that were argued about in a car park in Tideglass on the twenty-third of May are on it as standpipes at the ends of streets, and the nine households on them are not on it, and the woman of about thirty-one with a barrow is not on it, and a man of fifty-two who lives at number fifty-eight and who went round nine doorways is not on it, and none of them has been asked and none of them is owed anything.

**The corridor does not know that there was a car park.**

There was a car park on the twenty-third of May at about thirty-six degrees with about fifty people in it and four streets, and a decision was made in it, and then it was made again in a corridor on a Sunday morning by nine people with six clipboards, and neither of those things is on the delivery sheet either, because a delivery sheet is a delivery sheet.

---

**AND THE PRECEDENT IS NOW TWO, AND NOBODY IN THAT CORRIDOR SAID SO OUT LOUD, INCLUDING HIM.**

On the twenty-first of May a woman of about forty-four who drives a round of twenty-two stops asked for four of them done first, and a person who could have said no said yes, and the cost of it landed on a woman of about ninety-one at stop eighteen and on a bed in a wing with no mechanical extract, and nobody wrote it down.

**This morning a woman of about sixty asked for two extra hours on a Tuesday and a person who could have said no said yes, and the cost of it landed nowhere at all, and nothing was written down, and the two of them are the same shape.**

A correct request granted once is a precedent, and a precedent does not need anybody to decide anything, and the whole of the danger of a precedent is that the next one is easier and the one after that costs less.

He could see it from the end of that corridor. He has seen it twice now and both times from about thirty feet away with nothing in his hands.

**He did not write it down. A pad that goes in a shop at the end of a street is a piece of paper, and a piece of paper with a delivery note in it is a piece of paper that somebody can produce.**

---

Nobody was harmed. Nobody was helped.

The register of narrowings is at nineteen entries and the blank line under the nineteenth was blank for a hundred and ninety-eight days on Friday and it is two hundred and one days old this Monday and it is still the only fixed point in that building, and entry nineteen is a yard light, and the maintenance is a bulb, and the bulb is out, and a hot June is not a reason to write a form.

The rota at Saint Orra west has still not been rebuilt and cannot be. The row from eleven o'clock to half past one is still empty and still does not say *vacant*. Bram Osei's Thursday is still refused in his own handwriting with a second line under it in somebody else's hand that nobody looked at in June and nobody is going to.

**And the fourth of those five lines on the delivery sheet has got a person's name against it, and the fifth has got a woman's, and the two people whose names they are are not required to be on it and would be the first to say so if anybody put them on it by hand.**
