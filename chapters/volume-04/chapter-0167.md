# Chapter 0167 — Nobody Asked Me Either

Monday the sixth of July 2015, from about five in the afternoon, a supermarket on the far side of the market; and from about ten to seven, a corner shop; and after that, a street of terraces behind the market.

**The woman who read the page of the twenty-ninth of May is twenty-nine and is on no committee of anything and is not going to be on one. She has a constituency of about a dozen households on one street of terraces and she has said one thing out loud in a building and it changed what she did on one day, and this is Monday and it is cool and it is the first week of July. She asked a man a question and got a correct answer and she is not going to ask him anything again. Then she worked out, on her own, in a room, that the cheapest thing she could do was stop being findable, and she did it badly, and it cost somebody a bin. He asked no question of anybody. Nothing was resolved and nobody was thanked.**

---

She had been on since about eight and she finished at about half past four on Mondays and Tuesdays, and after that there was an hour in the building in which the rota for the week got written out by hand, and on the Monday the sixth of July she was in the office off the corridor behind the tills at about ten to five with a pen and a pad and a bin rota she had started on the first of the month and had not given to anybody.

**A man of thirty-two was in the front seating area with a bottle of water he had bought and no reason to be there, and he came down the corridor at about five because the corridor goes past the office, and she said the only question anybody had put to anybody that afternoon before he had got to the door.**

"**Can I ask you something that is not a complaint.**"

"**You can ask me something.**"

"**What happens to a person who has read something they were not asked to read.**"

---

**AND HE ANSWERED HER, AND THE ANSWER WAS CORRECT, AND IT WAS WORTH NOTHING, AND HE DID NOT IMPROVE IT AND HE DID NOT MAKE IT SOUNDER THAN IT IS.**

"**Nothing happens to her.**"

"**Nothing.**"

"**Nothing. That is the whole of it. There is no process, because a process is a place a person has to be written into, and if she is written into it then she is a person who knows, and once she is a person who knows somebody can ask her what is on it, and then she has to remember. Nobody in this borough is required to tell you what happens to a person who reads something they were not asked to read, because nothing happens to her, and that is not a failure of the system. That is the system.**"

"**And you would know that, because**—"

"**Because I have said it before, in a corridor, to somebody else, and he agreed with me, and neither of us has any way of passing it on.**"

---

**AND SHE SAID THE PART THAT WAS ACTUALLY HAPPENING, WHICH WAS NOT THE QUESTION.**

"**I did not ask you because I do not know what happens. I asked you because I already know what happens and I wanted to hear a person who is not me say it out loud in a corridor.**"

"**All right.**"

"**Because here is what I have worked out and I cannot get anybody else to work it out. The building knows I read it. That is it. That is all it took. I telephoned them on the Thursday and I said one sentence and the woman who took the call said thank you, and now a building in this city knows that there is a person in it who reads what is in trays, and nobody asked her to write it down, and she wrote it down anyway, because that is what a building does with a thing that has been said to it in daylight.**"

"**She wrote it down.**"

"**Somewhere. Not necessarily on me. But the sentence is in the building. And the sentence is *a person called in and said she had read the thing off the tray*, and that sentence has a space in it where a name goes, and nobody has put a name in the space, and the space is there, and the space is the problem, and the space will be there in August.**"

"**So you are findable.**"

"**I am findable. And being findable is not the same as being owed. I have worked that out since the twenty-fourth of June and I have not been owed anything once.**"

---

**AND THEN SHE SAID THE THING THAT IS THE CORRECTEST THING SAID ANYWHERE IN THIS BOROUGH SINCE A MAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT LEFT THE NAME COLUMN EMPTY IN A CAR PARK, AND SHE SAID IT AS A PLAN AND NOT AS A FINDING, AND IT IS NOT GOING IN A MINUTE.**

"**The cheapest thing I can do is stop being findable. That is all. I do not need anybody to do anything. I do not need a page changed or a name taken off a thing. I just need there to be no route to me, because the second there is a route, somebody will use it, and they will use it well, and it will be for something, and I will end up doing a thing I did not choose because somebody had a piece of paper with my name in it and I could not remember putting it there.**"

"**That is correct.**"

"**I know it is correct. I have checked it against about four days of not being findable and nothing bad has happened to anybody.**"

"**So do it.**"

---

**AND SHE DID IT BADLY, AND IT WAS THE CORRECT INSTINCT AND THE WRONG EXECUTION, AND BOTH OF THOSE ARE THE SHAPE OF THIS BOROUGH.**

At about ten to seven on the Monday evening she walked up the street of terraces and turned left into the corner shop and asked the woman who keeps it, who is about sixty-five and has a card rail by the door and has never once been asked to look after anybody's number, for a blank card and a pen.

She wrote the shop's own telephone number on it. Nothing else. No name, no first name, no initial, no address, no hours, no apology and no explanation.

"**If anybody comes in and asks after the woman who left this, can you say that it is the shop's number and ring it, and not say anything else.**"

"**Say that you are not saying anything else, or say that there is nothing else.**"

"**Say that it is the shop's number.**"

"**I can do that.**"

"**Do not tell me who you said it to. I am not asking. Do not put my name on it. Do not write it down anywhere. I am going to be somebody who is not here.**"

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT IS WRONG WITH IT, AND SHE DID NOT KNOW IT WAS WRONG, AND HE DID NOT TELL HER.**

A shop is not a person. That is true and it is why she did it.

**A shop is a thing that is open, at a number, on a street, and a thing that is open at a number is a route.** She has not removed herself from the map. She has moved herself from one column of it to another column of the same map, and the column she has moved herself into has a woman of about sixty-five in it who did not ask, who cannot refuse, and who will be standing behind a counter being asked about a person she has never seen by somebody who has been told a sentence about a person she has never seen.

**And the sentence that will get said is *there is a woman who sorts the bins on that street, she does not want to be found*, and that sentence is a person, and a person is findable, and she has put herself in a document in a shop on a street, in her own handwriting, with a shop's telephone number on it, and nobody will ever be able to prove whose handwriting it is.**

She is the person who read a page off a tray because she was in a room for twenty minutes with nothing to do, and she has spent three weeks being right about everything, and the price of being right out loud in a building is that the building has got a person in it that it can go to, and there is no instrument in this city that can stop a building remembering a person, and she is the one who has just handed it a telephone number.

---

**AND AT ABOUT HALF PAST SEVEN SHE WALKED BACK DOWN THE STREET, AND THE BIN AT THE TOP OF IT WAS OUT ON THE KERB WHERE IT IS PUT OUT AT SIX EVERY MORNING BY A WOMAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY-SIX, AND SHE DID NOT TAKE IT IN.**

She has been taking that bin in since the middle of June, on and off, on the way back from her shift, for nothing, because the collection on that street has not been for a fortnight and because the woman at the top has stopped mentioning it. She did not take it in. She stood on the opposite pavement for about half a minute and then went home, and that is the whole of what she did about it, and nobody saw her not do it.

**A thing that only works while one person is standing in it is not a service and does not become one, and the price of it is not known by anybody, including the person paying it.**

---

**AND AT ABOUT NINE, ON THE SAME STREET, A LIGHT CAME ON AT THE TOP.**

A woman of about seventy-six came out of a door in a dressing gown and a cardigan and looked at the bin standing full on the kerb where she had put it out at six, and she stood there for a moment, and then she took hold of it and dragged it back up her own path to her own door, on her own, and put it inside, and shut the door.

**Nobody in this borough knows that that happened. She did not mention it, and she will not mention it, and that is not stoicism and it is not pride, it is a woman of about seventy-six who has stopped mentioning things in July, and who started in June, and who has a method, and the method has just been handed to her by a person of twenty-nine who did not decide to hand it to her.**

She had not carried a bin in since the middle of June.

---

**A man of thirty-two walked the length of that street at about ten past nine on his way nowhere and saw a light go off at the top of it and did not know what it was, and he did not knock, and he did not go up, and he was not asked anything by anybody, and he did not tell the woman of twenty-nine about it and is not going to.**

Nobody in this borough is required to do anything about any of this. A bin on a kerb is not a form. A woman of about sixty-three behind a counter has a shop's telephone number written on a card by somebody else and does not know who. And the cheapest thing a person in this borough can do is stop being findable, and it is the correct instinct, and this is what it costs, and the person it costs is not the person doing it.

The register of narrowings is at nineteen. The blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and twenty-two days old and nobody is obliged to write on it. The card in the slot in that building is a hundred and thirty-six days old and nobody has emptied it and nobody is going to. The letter in the tray on the floor above is a hundred and thirty-eight days old and it has two pieces of post lying on it and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a woman of twenty-nine read a page in a room she was not supposed to be sitting in, said so out loud twice, worked out who would lose from it, was right, and has now made herself into a sentence in a shop, and none of that has been a service and none of it is going to become one.**
