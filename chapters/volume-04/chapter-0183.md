# Chapter 0183 — Thirty Times

Thursday the thirtieth of July 2015, from about half past ten in the morning, a corner shop on a street of terraces behind the market.

**A month of being left alone has been done to one ordinary place in this borough, and what has been done to it is that it is stronger, and it is not said in a way that makes anything of that. The cost of it is a woman of about sixty-five who has been asked about a card in her own window about thirty times in three weeks by people she has never seen, and she has said the same sentence every time, and she is not going to stop saying it. He asked no question of anybody and nobody asked him anything, and the reason he did not ask is a thing about him and it is not about her. Nothing was helped and nothing was refused and no name went in a box.**

---

There is nothing wrong with it. That is the first thing and it is the whole difficulty.

The shop is a corner shop and it opens at eight and shuts at six, and it has a card rail by the door with about two hundred cards in it, and it has a counter, and it has a woman behind the counter who is about sixty-five and has been behind it for about nine years.

**The pavement outside it is four feet wide and the sun is on it from about eleven until about one. There is nothing wrong with the shop and nothing wrong with the street and nobody on that street has asked anybody for anything in four weeks.**

---

**AND THE THING THAT HAS HAPPENED TO IT SINCE THE FIRST WEEK OF JULY IS A PIECE OF CARD WITH A TELEPHONE NUMBER ON IT AND NOTHING ELSE ON IT.**

It is in the window, on the inside, at the bottom, at about the height of a person's hand. **It is the shop's own telephone number and there is no name on it and no initial and no address and no hours and no apology and no explanation.** It has been there for three weeks and it has not been moved and it has not been taken down and it is not a form and it does not refer to anything in this building.

The woman who keeps the shop was asked, in her own words, not to say who left it and not to write it down. **Nobody has told her anything since. She does not know anything. She is not a thread and she is not owed anything and she is not going to be told.**

---

**AND WHAT HAS HAPPENED, IN THREE WEEKS, IS THAT STRANGERS HAVE BEEN COMING INTO THAT SHOP AND ASKING HER ABOUT IT.**

They come in the way people come into a shop. They are not furtive about it and they are not unkind about it. They are ordinary and they are often young and they are nearly always polite, and they say something like:

"**Is this the number for the woman who sorts the bins on that street?**"

"**That's the shop's number.**"

"**Right, is it the same one?**"

"**It's the shop's number.**"

And they say thank you and they go.

**Nobody has ever said her name in that shop in connection with that card, and nobody has ever said anything that is on paper, and she has not written one word about it anywhere, and she is not going to.**

---

**AND IT HAS BEEN ABOUT THIRTY TIMES IN THREE WEEKS, AND NOBODY HAS COUNTED IT, AND IT IS NOT A COUNT OF ANYTHING EXCEPT A THING SHE DID.**

She is not certain of the number. She is certain of the shape of it. **It is most days. It is most days at about four in the afternoon when the people on that street have finished work, and it is most days at about half past ten in the morning, and it has not once been a bad interaction and it has not once been a row, and it is about thirty times.**

The reason it is about thirty times is that a shop is not a person and a shop is also a thing that is open, at a number, on a street, and a thing that is open at a number is a route. Somebody wanted to be a person that could not be asked after, and a person cannot be asked after if there is no route to her, and so a route was made, and the route is a shop, and a shop is staffed, and the woman who staffs it is about sixty-five and has a card rail by the door and cannot refuse.

**Nobody in this borough can be told that the woman behind that counter is the woman people mean. What they are told is that the number on the card is the shop's number, and that is true, and she is the one who says it, and she has said it about thirty times, and each of those thirty times somebody believed her, and each of those thirty times a sentence about a woman who sorts the bins on that street got a little more fixed in a place it was not supposed to be.**

---

**AND THE THING IS NOT GOING ANYWHERE, AND SHE IS NOT GOING TO STOP, AND THE FINDING IS THE OPPOSITE OF THE ONE ANYBODY WOULD EXPECT.**

Nobody has asked that shop for a form. Nobody has asked that street for a list. Nobody has printed a card, and nobody has asked the woman of sixty-five a single question in writing, and there is no page and no minute and no agenda item and no building has taken an interest in Harker Street or in any other street this month.

**The card has been left alone for three weeks, and being left alone is the only thing that has ever worked in this borough, and it has worked, and here is what it has done.**

The shop is busier than it was in June. People come in who have never been in it. There is more milk on the shelf than there was, and more of the cold things, and a second carrier bag roll, and there are two people in there at about eleven most days who are not neighbours. The card rail by the door is fuller than it was and it gets fuller by about one card a week from shops up the road, which is a thing that has nothing to do with the card in the window and is what a shop rail does.

**And the woman of sixty-five has bought a second fridge, which she could not have justified to anybody in April, and which is justified by nothing, and which is full.**

**A thing that has been left alone for four weeks is either stronger or finished and there is no instrument in this city that says which. This is the outside of one street of terraces behind a market on the thirtieth of July 2015, and it is stronger, and that is not an achievement and it is not a policy and it is not going to be in a minute.**

---

**AND IT IS NOT STRONGER FOR ANYBODY.**

Nobody in that shop has been helped by the card. **A person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and what has been counted in that shop in three weeks is the number of times a stranger has been told that a number is the shop's number, and that is not a number of anybody at all.**

**And the cost of it is the only cost of it, and it is a woman of about sixty-five who has done the same thing about thirty times, and she is not going to stop and she is not going to say anything about it, and there is no version of the next three months in which anybody finds out, and that is what the thirty is.**

---

**AND HE COULD HAVE ASKED HER ONE QUESTION, AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN, WITH THE SHOP EMPTY, AND SHE WOULD HAVE ANSWERED IT, AND HE DID NOT.**

The reason is a thing about him and it is not a thing about her, and it is not a principle and it is not the price of anything.

**He is the only man in this borough who could be believed.** That is not vanity and it is not a gift. It is the plain result of nine months in which almost every true thing anybody has heard in a room in this borough came out of his mouth, and a man who is believed about a street is a man somebody will come to about a street.

And if a man of thirty-two walks into that shop at half past ten on a Thursday with about a dozen people in it and asks the woman behind the counter one more question about that card, then **the shop becomes a place where a card is a subject.** And a shop is a thing that is open, at a number, on a street, and it is the one column of this borough's map that everybody thinks is safe because it is not a person, and he has said that out loud, in a corridor, to a woman of twenty-nine, in July, and he was right, and he is not going to be the man who puts her in it twice.

**So he bought a bag of washing-up liquid, which is what he came in for, and the woman of sixty-five charged him for it, and he did not look at the card on the way out, and he asked her nothing at all.**

---

Nobody in that shop was thanked and nobody refused to be thanked and nothing was helped and nothing was refused. **No name went in a box and the card in that window has a telephone number on it and nothing else, and the woman of about seventy-six at the top of that street is still carrying her own bin up her own path, and she is not going to mention it, and nobody has asked her.**

The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and forty-six days old and a Thursday is not a reason to write on it and nobody is obliged. The card in the slot on the ground floor is a hundred and sixty days old and nobody has taken it out of it. The letter in the tray on the floor above is a hundred and sixty-two days old, it has two pieces of post lying on it, and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a corner shop behind a market is busier on the thirtieth of July than it was on the thirtieth of June, and nobody in this borough could tell you why, and the reason is a piece of card with a shop's telephone number on it and nothing else, and a woman of sixty-five has been asked about it about thirty times and is not going to stop, and the man of thirty-two who would have been believed if he had asked stood in that shop and bought a bag of washing-up liquid and said nothing, and that is the only decision anybody made on that street this month.**
