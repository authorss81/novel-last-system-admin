# Chapter 0177 — Two Hundred Yards And A Gate

Tuesday the twenty-first of July 2015, from about ten in the morning, a first-floor room over a builder's merchant on an industrial road.

**A correct thing asked for and granted quickly, in a room, with no paper on it, and the cost of it lands about four miles away in a market hall on the other side of the boundary, on a person who was not in that room, who is not owed anything by anybody, and who is never going to be told. He asked no question of anybody. Nobody died, nobody was hurt, nothing was helped, and this is the cheapest thing that has happened in this borough in nine months and it is also the reason there is nothing here anybody can point at.**

---

The room has a desk, a chair, a window that faces a wall, a filing tray with nothing urgent in it and a wall calendar with a photograph of a footpath on it. There is a telephone on the desk and it works. There is no notice on the wall about anything.

The man in it is about fifty-five. He is a project coordinator on a small job and the compound outside the window belongs to the job, and the compound is fenced, and there is a welfare hut in the corner of it with a door and no light and a tap on the wall outside, and the hut has been locked since the last job finished in June.

**Nobody has to open that hut, or do anything with the compound at all. It is a compound and a hut and a tap on a wall, and there is a list of them somewhere, and none of it is a person.**

---

**THE WOMAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-ONE CAME IN AT ABOUT TEN AND SAT DOWN WITHOUT BEING ASKED TO, BECAUSE SHE HAD BEEN AT A MARKET SINCE FIVE THAT MORNING AND HER BACK WAS THE REASON.**

She has had a pitch at that market for about twenty-two years. She sells things that have to be kept cold or they are not worth selling, and the market's toilets are in a block on the far side of the site, and the block is locked at six and unlocked at seven, and she is on site at five.

"**I am not asking for a tap,**" she said. "**I have asked for a tap twice. I have asked a man about a tap twice this year and both times he was polite about it, and both times there was no tap, and I am not asking again and I have stopped mentioning it.**"

"**Right.**"

"**I am asking for a loo.**"

---

She said it in about that many words and she did not explain it and she was right not to.

"**Where.**"

"**Anywhere with a door on it and a tap.**"

"**That is most things.**"

"**Not on a market. The block is locked at six.**"

"**The block has a key.**"

"**The block has a key and the key is in a hut in an office in a car park and the office is shut at half four, and I am not going to stand in a car park at half four on a Friday to explain a market to anybody.**"

---

**AND THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE SAID YES IN ABOUT THREE SENTENCES, AND HE WAS RIGHT, AND THERE WAS NO PAPER ON IT, AND HE DID NOT GO OUT OF THE ROOM TO DO IT.**

"**There is a welfare hut in the yard on this compound. It has been locked since June. It is not ours, it is standing here until the next job, and there is a unit on the water inside it that is not plumbed to anything and a tap on the wall outside it that works. Put your people on the corner by the gate and not by the office, and be off by half six, because the gate is shut at half six and it is shut by a man who will not be nice about it.**"

"**Right.**"

"**I will have the hut open by twelve. There is a padlock on it and I am going to leave it unlatched, and that is not permission, that is a man who has not locked it.**"

"**That is all I wanted.**"

"**Then that is the whole of it.**"

---

**AND HE PUT THE TELEPHONE DOWN AND DID NOT WRITE IT DOWN, AND NOBODY IN THAT ROOM ASKED HIM TO WRITE ANYTHING DOWN, AND HE DID NOT GIVE HER A REFERENCE, AND SHE DID NOT ASK FOR ONE.**

Nobody in this borough has been given a reference for a loo this year. There is no form in this borough on which a woman of sixty-one can record that a compound's hut was open on the twenty-first of July, and there is not going to be one, and the hut is now unlatched, and an unlatched hut is the cheapest permission anybody has issued in nine months and it will not survive the next person who walks past it.

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT IT COST, AND IT IS ABOUT FOUR MILES AWAY, AND NOBODY IN THAT ROOM COULD HAVE SEEN IT.**

The unit in that hut is hired by the day from a yard over the boundary, in the next borough, and the yard is where the units live when they are not on a site.

**It was booked into that yard on Friday the seventeenth of July, at about ten past four in the afternoon, for a compound in this borough, by a man of fifty-five who has a job and a compound and a padlock and is correct about all three.**

Before it was booked into that yard, it was pencilled in for a market hall in that same next borough. Not booked. Penciled, on a board, on a Monday, by somebody who works there and who has the keys and who cleans it herself.

The hall has had no working toilet in its traders' block since the middle of June. The block was closed for works, the works overran, the block came back into use without the cistern, and nobody rang anybody about it, and about six traders a day have been going to the leisure centre at the other end of that town and coming back.

**The booking on that board was released on the Friday afternoon, at about half past three, by the woman who keeps that board, because the compound's booking was firm and the hall's was not, and one firm booking and one pencilled one is a very ordinary Friday.**

**So the unit went to a compound in this borough, and a market hall four miles away is still without a cistern, and it will be without one on the Tuesday and on the Wednesday and probably longer.**

---

**AND THE PERSON WHO IS PAYING FOR THAT IS A WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-THREE WHO HAS THE KEYS TO THAT HALL AND CLEANS IT HERSELF, AND SHE IS NOT IN THIS BOROUGH AND SHE IS NOT OWED ANYTHING BY ANYBODY.**

She opens the hall at about half six. She has a contract, which is a document, which has her name on it, which has an address on it, and she goes in at half six and she does the floors and the bins and the doors and she is there when the first six traders come in at about seven.

**She has been telling them for six weeks that there is a toilet at the leisure centre and that it is a five-minute walk, and she has been telling them the truth, and she does not know that a unit that would have gone into her traders' block on the Friday was put on a board and then taken off it.**

Nobody is going to tell her.

Not because it would upset her, and not because of the unit, and not because anybody in this borough is protecting anybody. **It is the plainest mechanism in this whole account and it is about a name on a piece of paper.** She is written down. There is a contract with her name on it and an address for it and a telephone number for it, and the day anybody in this borough rings that contract to ask whether a unit was released on the Friday evening, she stops being a woman who cleans a hall and becomes a person who has been asked about, and she will have to remember, and she will have to remember it in front of somebody who has come a long way to find out.

**A person who is written down to do a thing is a person somebody can ring about it. That is the whole of the reason the cost of this went where it went, and it is not discretion, and it is not kindness, and it did not occur to anybody as a decision.**

---

**AND A MAN OF THIRTY-TWO COULD HAVE FOUND OUT. HE HAD FOUR WAYS IN AN HOUR AND HE USED NONE OF THEM AND HE IS NOT GOING TO.**

He could have rung the yard. He could have driven four miles and stood in that market hall at about seven in the morning and asked six traders where the toilets were. He could have gone to the man of fifty-five and asked him, in daylight, in that room, in four words, whether the unit in his hut had been booked on the Friday.

He did none of that, and the reason has nothing to do with the man of fifty-five and nothing to do with the market, and it is this.

**And there is no duty here that anybody can be shown.** There is a compound, a hut, a tap and a unit, and the compound is not his, and the market is not his, and a man of thirty-two is not on anything in this borough and has no department and there is no building he could walk into on Tuesday and ask in a way that would go anywhere. The whole of his standing here is that he can say a true thing out loud to a person in a room, and a true thing said out loud in a room goes into the room, and that is all it does, and it has never once come back with an answer.

**So he is a man who can observe. He is not a man who can follow anything, and this borough has never once needed him to follow anything, and this is the first time in nine months that not following something has had a person in it.**

---

**AND IT IS ALSO THE CHEAPEST OUTCOME ANYWHERE IN THIS BOROUGH, AND IT IS ALSO THE REASON NOTHING IN THIS BOROUGH CAN BE POINTED AT.**

The request was correct. The grant was correct. The traders have a door and a tap for an hour and a half a day, about two hundred yards and a gate away from where they sell, and the gate is shut by a man who will not be nice about it at half six.

Nobody is hurt. Nobody dies. **Nothing is helped** — a thing has not been fixed, it has been moved, and a thing that has been moved from a place where nobody was looking to a place where nobody is looking has not been helped, and the traders will be doing their hands in a trough at the compound at half five and then standing at a pitch for another hour and a half.

**And a woman of fifty-three four miles away has a cistern that does not work and a bin to empty and six people a day to point at the leisure centre, and she does not know that the thing that would have fixed it went onto a compound in a different borough on a Friday evening, and nobody is ever going to tell her, and there is no instrument in this city that can make somebody tell her, and the reason is that no living person is under any obligation to.**

---

Nobody in that room was thanked. The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and thirty-seven days old and a Tuesday is not a reason to write on it and nobody is obliged. The card in the slot is a hundred and fifty-one days old. The letter in the tray on the floor above is a hundred and fifty-three days old, it has two pieces of post lying on it, and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a hut on a compound is unlatched, and four miles away a woman of fifty-three is telling six people where the toilets are, and the man who could have asked her is not going to, and the reason is on a contract with her name on it, and that is the whole of the mechanism, and it is not a moral position, and it is not going to change.**
