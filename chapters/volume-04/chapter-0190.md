# Chapter 0190 — Clause Five

Wednesday the ninth of September 2015, from about eleven in the morning, a corridor on the first floor of Saint Orra Civic Hospital.

**A correct instrument was applied correctly to one person and the result was bad. He read which clause outranks which and he was right and the transfer could not be confirmed and the delay did real harm and nobody died. A physician was in that corridor inside the hour and she was right, and he did not apologise for the rule, and he was not forgiven for the rule, and neither of those was available to anybody. This is the floor and it is not a lesson.**

---

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE IN THAT CORRIDOR, printed and fixed to the wall by the lift lobby at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing, and nothing on it defines *holder*:**

> **A MACHINE IS NOT A SERVICE UNTIL SOMEBODY CONFIRMS IT IN WRITING.**
> **NO PERSON IS REQUIRED TO CONFIRM ANYTHING.**
> **THE ORDER DOES NOT SAY WHERE ANYBODY GOES.**

---

**ROY ALBERY IS EIGHTY-ONE AND HE IS ON THE SECOND-FLOOR WARD AND HE GOES TO A MACHINE THREE TIMES A WEEK.**

He came in on the Friday before with what they found when they looked, and they have been trying since Saturday to get him to a machine at the other site, which is the Rossall building, which has two of them and has had two of them since March, and one of them has been out of order since the second of August and nobody has said whether it is going to be fixed.

**The sending site holds precedence over the receiving site until the receiving site has confirmed capacity in writing. That is clause five. It was drafted for a standpipe and a lorry in June and it was carried across to a bed without anybody deciding to carry it, and it is correct, and it is correct for what it was written for, and nobody in this borough has any instrument for saying that a thing written for water is wrong when it is applied to a person.**

---

**THE RECEIVING SITE COULD NOT CONFIRM IN WRITING, AND THE REASON IT COULD NOT CONFIRM IN WRITING IS THE REASON NOTHING IN THIS BOROUGH CAN DO ANYTHING AFTER HALF PAST FOUR.**

Confirming capacity in writing requires a person who can commit the machine. That person is on the other site, and that site is worked in the afternoon and is shut by about half past four, and the machine runs in the evening when there is nobody on it at all, and the confirmation has to be in writing, and writing it takes about two minutes, and there is no other person there who may do it, and there is no deputy, and there is no arrangement, and there is no rota, and the rota cannot be rebuilt, and the reason the rota cannot be rebuilt is that nobody in this borough is under any obligation to rebuild it.

**And there is a second thing about that site, and it is the actual reason, and it is the same shape as the first one. There are two machines there and one of them came off on the second of August and has not been back since and nobody has said whether it is going to be back, so half of what that building could take on is a machine of unknown status, and a person may not write down that she has capacity for an evening when half of the evening is a machine she has not been told anything about.**

**So the transfer is not refused. It is not pending. It is not in progress. There is no state of the system in which it is anything at all, and at about eleven on Wednesday morning a man of eighty-one was on the second floor of a building he has been in since Friday, and a machine was three miles away, and both of those things were correct.**

---

**AND HE SAID WHICH CLAUSE WAS IN FRONT, AND HE SAID IT IN A CORRIDOR, TO A PERSON WHO HAD NOT ASKED HIM.**

He was in the corridor because he is in corridors. He has asked no question of anybody since the twenty-fifth of February 2015.

A registrar from the receiving site said the thing that everybody in that corridor was thinking, which is that the transfer was clinically necessary and had been clinically necessary since about two o'clock on Saturday and that it could not be confirmed in writing, and Jonas said:

"**Clause five is in front. Clause one is about two services disagreeing about what happens first and this is not that, this is one site waiting for a pen. There is nothing in the whole of it that says what happens when the answer is correct and it does not arrive.**"

"That is right."

"**Then it stands until it arrives.**"

"**That is right as well. I am not saying anything else. I am saying I have a machine and a man who needs it and a piece of paper that has to be written by a person who is not here, and I am going to ring her at about twenty to six and ask her to write it, because that site shut at about half past four and the only thing left to ask her is the one machine she has not said a word about since the second of August, and if she cannot write it tonight then it is tomorrow afternoon and that is her decision and not mine, and either way it is my problem and not yours and not his, and I am saying it in a corridor because there is nowhere else to say it.**"

**And then he said the other thing, which he said in a corridor on that Wednesday and did not write down, and there was nowhere to put it and he did not look for somewhere:**

"**And I am not going to be able to say no to this.**"

---

**AND THE HOUR WAS NOT AN HOUR ANYBODY CHOSE, AND THE CAUSE IS ONE SENTENCE AND IT IS ABOUT A SHIFT AND NOT ABOUT A DOCUMENT.**

That site is worked in the afternoon and shut by about half past four. The registrar's own day ends at about six. Between those two points the only two people in the whole of this arrangement are still in it, and one of them is three miles away at home, and neither of them is on anything. **There was about an hour and a half in the whole of that Wednesday on which Roy Albery could have been confirmed in writing by anybody at all, and it was the same hour and a half on every day of that week, and it does not exist as a thing anybody can put on a page, and a fit happened at about four on that afternoon, which is half an hour before the hour opened.**

The two sentences on a card on a wall in a Methodist hall in this borough are about a body and about what it may be told, and neither of them is wrong, and neither of them has anything to say about a machine of unknown status on a site that shuts at half past four. **Nobody in that building on that afternoon was lazy and nobody in that building on that afternoon was wrong, and the whole of it fitted in a gap between two sets of hours that nobody had ever written down next to each other, and it fitted exactly, and that is the part that should not have fitted.**

---

**AND HE IS RIGHT, AND CORRECTLY IS THE DIFFICULTY, AND IT IS NOT A MORAL PROBLEM.**

He is not a member of anything and not a party to the order and not a departmental officer and has no grade and no department and no probation. He cannot be instructed, because there is nothing to instruct him through. He cannot be overruled, because there is no one above him. He cannot be removed, because there is nothing to remove him from.

**A person who is competent and correct and cannot say no is the shape of everything in this borough, and it is the shape of this man, and the order did not make him that and the order did not have to.**

**He is on the rota of it now without a rota. There is no list. Nobody in this borough is required to send him a precedence question, and none of them has, and about two a week are arriving at the places he happens to be, and he answers them, and he has answered every one, and he has never once not answered one, and there is no version of a day this borough can arrange in which he does not answer one.**

---

**THE CONFIRMATION ARRIVED AT ABOUT SIX IN THE AFTERNOON, AFTER SOMEBODY RANG THE OTHER SITE AT ABOUT TWENTY TO SIX AND THE WOMAN WHO CAN COMMIT THE MACHINE WROTE IT WHERE SHE WAS, AND IT CAME DOWN A CORRIDOR AS A PIECE OF PAPER, AND HE WAS MOVED AT ABOUT SIX FORTY.**

Nobody refused. **At about four o'clock in the afternoon Roy Albery had a fit on the second-floor ward of a building where he had been since Friday.**

He did not die. He was not rescued. **He had a bleed, and it was found, and he was moved at about six forty, and the machine was used at about seven, and he is alive this evening, and he is on the third floor of the Rossall building, and he is not going to walk unaided again, and that is what happened on that Wednesday and it is not a thing that will be described differently by anybody afterwards.**

**Nobody put a career in the way. Nobody in that building on that afternoon treated this as a lesson, and nobody in that building on that afternoon treated it as anything except a man who was not dialysed on the day he was going to be dialysed.**

---

**AND SHE WAS IN THAT CORRIDOR INSIDE THE HOUR, AND SHE WAS RIGHT.**

Sanaa Iqbal. Thirty-five. A physician at Saint Orra Civic Hospital, and she was on the second floor at about half past eleven because she had come up to see a man of about seventy-eight on the same ward and had been told on the way that a transfer was not going.

She came down the corridor, past the lift lobby, at about a quarter to twelve, and she did not know he was there and he was the only person in that corridor who was not there for a patient.

**They have not spoken since before September. That is a year and some weeks, and it is not a quarrel, and it is not a pause, and there is no word for what it is in this borough, and the word is not wanted.**

"**You read clause five.**"

"**I did.**"

"**And clause five is in front, and it is correct, and the machine is three miles away, and it could not be confirmed in writing.**"

"**That is all true.**"

"**I know it is all true. I have been in this building for twenty-two years and I have never once been able to make any of it untrue, and that is the whole of my qualification and I am not going to pretend to you that I have another one.**"

---

**AND THEN SHE SAID THE OTHER THING, AND SHE SAID IT ONCE, AND SHE SAID IT FLATLY, AND SHE DID NOT SAY IT TO MAKE ANYBODY FEEL ANYTHING.**

"**His name is Roy Albery. He is eighty-one. He has been on a machine three times a week since the February before last and his potassium this morning was the highest it has been since he came in and I have the number and I know the number and it is not an argument, it is a number about a person.**"

"**I know.**"

"**Then you know that this afternoon is the day. Not this week. This afternoon. And I am not going to say it to make you say something, and I am not going to say it to anybody else, and I am saying it to you because you are the only person in this corridor who is not a member of anything and cannot be disciplined about it and I have run out of the other kind.**"

He said nothing.

"**You are not going to apologise to me,**" she said. "**I want to say that now so that neither of us spends the afternoon waiting for it. You did not write it and you cannot unwrite it and there is nobody for you to apologise to, because the person it belongs to is a clause and a clause cannot hear you. And I am not going to forgive you for it, and you would not want me to, and it is not available.**"

"**I know.**"

"**Do you.**"

"**No.**"

"**No. That is the honest one. That is the first honest thing either of us has said in a year.**"

---

**AND THEY STOOD IN THAT CORRIDOR FOR ABOUT EIGHT MINUTES AND THEN SHE WENT BACK UP AND HE WENT DOWNSTAIRS AND NOTHING WAS ARRANGED.**

He did not apologise for the rule and he was not forgiven for the rule and neither of those was available to anybody in that corridor or in that building or anywhere else.

She had a ward and about thirty-one people on it and a number on it that she is answerable for. He has no department. **There is no instrument in this city that lets anybody stop a building remembering that the two of them were in a corridor at a quarter to twelve on that Wednesday, and there is not one that lets anybody stop a hospital remembering it either, and by Friday it will be a thing two people in that building know and by the end of the month it will be a thing about four people know, and none of them will say it, and none of them was asked.**

---

Nobody died and nobody was rescued. The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth has been blank for two hundred and eighty-seven days, and a transfer is not a narrowing. The card in the slot on the ground floor is two hundred and one days old and nobody has taken it out of it. The letter in the tray on the floor above is two hundred and three days old, with two pieces of post lying on it, and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And the row on the board in this hospital from eleven o'clock to half past one is still empty and does not say *vacant*, and a porter's Thursday on another board is still refused in his own handwriting with a second line under it in somebody else's hand, and he has asked twice that it not be looked at, and an order that can move a man across this city in a day did not rebuild a rota and did not look at a second line and is not going to.**

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and Roy Albery is counted, and being counted is how a machine three miles away and a piece of paper in the wrong hand and a corridor with one man in it who cannot be instructed came to be about a man of eighty-one on a Wednesday afternoon, and nobody in this borough is going to be told that counting him was the mistake.**
