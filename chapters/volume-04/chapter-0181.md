# Chapter 0181 — The Bench

Sunday the twenty-sixth of July 2015, from about two in the afternoon, the forecourt of a block of flats and the pavement outside it.

**A bench in a place in this borough that anybody in this borough is obliged to deal with, and the dealing is not done, and not doing it is correct, and somebody else does it, and nobody in that block will ever know. He asked no question of anybody. Nothing is minuted and nobody is thanked, and he may not chair anything and there is no chair of that body.**

---

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE IN THAT FORECOURT, printed and screwed to the wall of the block by the bin store at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing and nothing on it defines *holder*, and it requires nothing of anybody:**

> **A BENCH ON A PAVEMENT IS NOT AN INSPECTION.**
> **NO PERSON IS REQUIRED TO REPORT A BENCH, CLEAN A BENCH, OR ANSWER FOR A BENCH.**
> **A BENCH ON A PAVEMENT IS A BENCH ON A PAVEMENT.**

---

The bench is a two-slat bench on two cast-iron ends, and it is about nine years old, and it faces a wall with nothing on it. There is a plate on the iron end the size of a playing card and there is nothing written on it, because there has never been anything to write on it, and nobody in this borough has ever looked at it to establish that.

**Somebody in this borough is obliged to deal with that bench.** Not a person. A thing of this borough, which is a schedule, which is held by a works depot on the far side of the tracks, and which lists street furniture on estates, and which says in the ordinary way that such things are inspected and defects are reported.

Nobody has to do anything about it this afternoon, and the notice by the bin store says so in three lines and defines nothing at all.

---

**AND THE MAN WHO COMES IS ABOUT TWENTY-SEVEN, AND HE COMES WITH A FLATBED AND A CLIPBOARD, AND HE HAS A ROUND, AND THE ROUND IS NOT A ROTA.**

He is not in the building. He does not come every month and he does not come on a day of the week. He comes to that estate when he is on that estate, and the depot gives him about nine estates a week, and the nine are not the same nine every week, and there is a list of the nine in a building on the far side of the tracks that nobody in that block has ever seen.

**He was last at that bench in April. He came at about eleven, looked at the two slats, wrote something on a clipboard, and went. He has not been back. There is nothing that will bring him back.**

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT A DUTY ACTUALLY IS, AND IT IS NOT THE ONLY THING THAT MATTERS THIS AFTERNOON.**

**A duty is an act and not a place.** The duty is not the bench. The bench is a place, and a place can be pointed at, and a place that can be pointed at is a thing this borough has spent nine months refusing to produce. The duty is that somebody has to look at it. An act has to be done by a person. **The person who does that act is not on a rota, and the rota cannot be rebuilt, and the reason the rota cannot be rebuilt is that nobody in this borough is required to rebuild it.**

Everybody in this borough knows that. Nobody in this borough says it.

And that is not a failure of a system and it is not a scandal and it is not anybody's fault. **A rota is a list of people who cannot say no. Put a name against that act and it becomes a job, and a job can be moved, and a job can be asked why it did not happen, and the man who has to answer that is not the man of twenty-seven and is not the depot and is not anybody in particular. It would be a young man on a Friday afternoon being asked where April went.**

---

**AND AT ABOUT TEN PAST TWO A WOMAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY-ONE CAME OUT OF THE BLOCK WITH A CLOTH AND WIPED ONE OF THE TWO SLATS.**

She goes out at about two most days because the sun comes round onto that forecourt at about two and she has been doing this long enough that the wall is the only thing in that block worth sitting in front of.

There is grit on the left-hand slat. There has been grit on it since before the man of twenty-seven last came. She had a cloth in her coat pocket, which means she brought the cloth out with her, which means she knew before she came in that she was going to use it.

**She wiped the slat because she was going to sit on it. That is the whole of the act and it is not on a schedule and it is not in front of anybody and it was not done for anybody.**

Nobody in that block saw her do it. Nobody in that block knows that a duty exists that has not been done, because a duty that has not been done is a thing you can only know about from a list, and there is no list in that block, and there is a notice on the wall by the bin store that says in three lines that nobody has to do anything about a bench, and that notice is correct, and it is also the reason nobody in that block is going to be told.

**Nobody in that building is ever going to know. Not this year and not after the man of twenty-seven comes back in November with a clipboard, because when he comes back the bench will be clean and he will write nothing down about a clean bench, because there is nothing to write, and a man with a clipboard who has nothing to write is a man who has a clean day.**

---

**AND A MAN OF THIRTY-TWO STOOD ON THE PAVEMENT OUTSIDE THAT FORECOURT FOR ABOUT HALF AN HOUR AND ASKED NOBODY ANYTHING AND WAS ASKED NOTHING.**

**Four hundred yards up the road, past the parade, is the office that would know when the man of twenty-seven comes to that estate, and it is shut today because it is Sunday. That is a place, and a place a man goes to is the first half of a schedule, and he had no reason to be in it today, and he was not in it, and he is not going to be in it on a Tuesday either.**

He could have gone in on Monday and asked one question — *how often does that estate get looked at* — and he would have got a correct answer, and the answer would have been *as and when the round comes*, and he would have written nothing down and the round would have carried on and the bench would have been the same on the last day of July as it is on this one.

**And the whole of the reason he did not go is that there is no version of going in that improves anything, and the whole of the reason he is not going to go in September is that going in twice is how a thing becomes a thing somebody looks at.**

---

**AND THIS IS THE ELEVENTH TIME THIS BOROUGH HAS BEEN KEPT GOING BY A PERSON NOBODY IS OBLIGED TO NAME, AND IT IS NOT COUNTED HERE, BECAUSE COUNTING IT IS THE THING THAT WOULD STOP IT.**

It is the eleventh time. There have been a woman of about fifty with a tray, a woman of about fifty-seven with a tray on a Friday, a woman of about sixty-five with a card in her window, a woman of about seventy-six with a bin, a woman of about thirty-one with four containers, a man of sixty-three with a bucket, a man of twenty-four with a crate he moved before he opened, a man of about twenty-six with a fan he carried in, a man of about forty-two with a towel, a man of about forty-seven with a bag he was not asked about, and now a woman of about seventy-one with a cloth.

**And it is not the eleventh, and the reason it is not the eleventh is that the two of those lists are a list. And a list of people who did things is a list of people, and there is a drawer under a counter in this borough with a sentence in it about a tray that nobody knows is there, and the reason nobody knows is that a person who is written down to do a thing is a person somebody can ring about it, and a woman of seventy-one with a cloth in her coat pocket is the safest person in this city this afternoon and she will stay that way for exactly as long as nobody counts her.**

---

Nobody in that forecourt was thanked and nobody refused to be thanked. There is no form in this borough on which a person could write down that a bench was wiped on the twenty-sixth of July, and there is not going to be one, and a duty is an act and not a place, and the minute anybody turns that act into a place then somebody can be shown where the bench is and then somebody can be shown the block and then somebody can be shown the woman, and this borough has not done that in nine months and is not going to start on a Sunday.

The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and forty-two days old and a Sunday is not a reason to write on it and nobody is obliged. The card in the slot is a hundred and fifty-six days old. The letter in the tray is a hundred and fifty-eight days old, it has two pieces of post lying on it, and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a bench in a forecourt is the cheapest thing anybody in this borough is obliged to deal with and has not been dealt with, and a woman of about seventy-one wiped a slat with a cloth she brought out in her pocket, and nobody in that building is ever going to find out that anything needed doing.**
