# Chapter 0166 — Nobody Claims It

Thursday the second of July 2015, from about ten in the morning, a piece of hard ground four hundred yards from the end of a lane; and from about eleven o'clock, the mouth of the lane.

**A round that has been running for six years on nobody's asking came at about eleven, as it comes on a Thursday, and a man of about forty-one walked down that lane at about half past ten with a form in a plastic wallet and was told, correctly, by a man of sixty, to go away. Nothing was put to the man of sixty, so nothing was refused, and the count of refusals in this borough did not move. The round does not get funded and does not get done and nothing is helped. He asked no question of anybody and nobody asked him anything and he did not go up the lane.**

---

There is nothing at the end of that lane. That is what makes it a lane and not a street. It is about two hundred yards of concrete and brick and a row of garage doors with no house behind any of them, and then the end, where a standpipe comes out of a wall on a piece of hard ground with a chain and a padlock and a bollard and about two feet of grass, and the road that the lane comes off is a bus route.

**On the other side of the road from the end of that lane there is a bank and a bus shelter and a litter bin, and a man of thirty-two was sitting on the bank at about ten in the morning with a bottle of water he had already paid for.**

He has been in this city for a long time. He is not on the agenda of anything. He has no department and no grade and nobody can ring him about anything, and he has been on the far side of that road on purpose for about an hour, and four hundred yards is a place, and a place a man goes to is the first half of a schedule, and there is no way in this borough of knowing from here what a man does at the end of a lane with a hose in his hand.

---

**THE MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-ONE CAME DOWN THE LANE ON FOOT AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN, AND HE CAME FROM THE OTHER END, AND HE WAS CARRYING A PLASTIC WALLET WITH A FORM IN IT, AND THE FORM IS DATED TUESDAY THE TWENTY-THIRD OF JUNE.**

The twenty-third of June was in the heat. A form in this borough is a thing that gets written in a heat, and a form is almost never about the weather; the weather is only the reason there was time.

He was not going up to the standpipe to fill anything. He had come to find out whether the round was still going, and he had a piece of paper to find out with, and he had a building behind him that had asked him to.

**Nobody in this borough has ever written a form because it was a nice day, and nobody in this borough has ever stopped writing forms because a week turned over.**

---

**AND THE LORRY CAME AT ABOUT ELEVEN, AND IT CAME DOWN THE LANE THE WAY IT HAS COME EVERY THURSDAY FOR AS LONG AS ANYBODY DOWN THERE CAN REMEMBER, AND IT FILLED THE STANDPIPE, AND A MAN OF SIXTY PUT HIS HOSE ON.**

He is sixty. He is at number thirty-one and he has been there a long time and he pays for every drop that goes into the butt under his own downpipe out of his own pocket, and he does it because there is a woman at number twenty-nine who cannot get to the top of the lane, and that is the whole of the arrangement, and it is his own, and there is no form in this borough on which anybody could write either fact down next to the other.

The man of about forty-one was standing about nine feet from the standpipe with his wallet in his hand when the lorry turned in.

He did not ask the man of sixty anything. **He said, out loud, to a man of sixty who was coiling a hose, what he had come for, and he said all of it, and it took him about forty seconds, and none of it was a question.**

"**There is a service in the schedule that ends in this postcode and I have not been able to get anybody to say whether it is still running. It is on the sheet. I have got the sheet. It is a water service, it is a Thursday, it is a fill at the end of a lane, and it has got a reference on it and I have got the reference.**"

"**Aye.**"

"**So I have come down to see whether the standpipe at the end is being filled, and if it is, then the sheet is right, and if it is not, then the sheet is wrong, and either way there is a thing I can go back and write.**"

---

**AND THE MAN OF SIXTY TOLD HIM TO GO AWAY, IN ABOUT FOUR SENTENCES, AND HE WAS RIGHT, AND IT WAS NOT A REFUSAL BECAUSE NOBODY HAD ASKED HIM ANYTHING.**

"**You should go away.**"

"**Well, that is what I said, only slower.**"

"**I know what you said. You are going to write something on a piece of paper and put it in a wallet and take it back to a building, and I am telling you that you should not, and I am telling you before you have asked me, because if I wait for you to ask me then I have had to think about it.**"

"**That is fair.**"

"**Here is why, and then you can go. That round does not have a name on it. Not a man's name, not a crew's name, not a number with a word beside it. It comes on a Thursday because it has always come on a Thursday, and it comes because the driver is on that road anyway, and the day you put a name against it, the day it is a named job with a named reference, it is a job, and a job can be moved, and a job can be given away, and a job can be asked why it did not come, and the man who has to answer that question is not me and it will not be me, and it will be some lad on a Friday afternoon being asked where Thursday went.**"

"**So if I do not write it, it keeps coming.**"

"**If you do not write it, it keeps coming.**"

"**And if I do write it, it stops.**"

"**It might not stop. It might just go on a list somewhere and keep coming, and now there are two of everything, and the two of them argue.**"

---

**AND THE MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-ONE STOOD THERE FOR ANOTHER TEN SECONDS AND THEN PICKED UP HIS WALLET AND WENT BACK UP THE LANE ON FOOT, AND HE WAS RIGHT TO GO, AND HE DID NOT ASK A SECOND QUESTION, AND NOBODY IN THAT LANE HAS EVER SAID ANY OF THIS OUT LOUD BEFORE.**

The round is not funded. It has never been claimed by a person and it has never been claimed by a building and it is the last line on the allowance and it is the line that goes.

**A round is a rota with a vehicle on it, and a rota is a person who cannot say no, and that is the only reason anything works in this borough. This round has never had a rota. That is the whole of why it has run for six years, and putting one on it would end it, and everybody in that lane knows that, and none of them has ever said it, and the one time it was said out loud it was said this morning by a man of sixty to a stranger with a form, and he was right, and it cost him nothing, and it is the only reason anybody in that lane now knows it.**

The man of about forty-one went back to a building and put the wallet in a drawer and the form went in with it, and nothing was written, and there is no form in this borough on which a person could record that a man of forty-one was in the right place this morning.

---

**AND THE LORRY WENT AT ABOUT TWENTY PAST ELEVEN AND THE MAN OF SIXTY FILLED HIS BUTT UNDER HIS OWN DOWNPIPE AND PAID FOR IT AND WENT INSIDE, AND JONAS MERCER SAT ON THE BANK ACROSS THE ROAD AND DID NOT GO UP THE LANE AND SAID NOTHING AND WAS ASKED NOTHING.**

A thing that runs for six years on nobody's asking looks exactly like a thing that is not run by anybody. There is a van and a man and a hose and a butt under a downpipe and no document anywhere in this city that says any of it happens, and if the document were made this morning, correctly, by a correct man with a correct form dated the twenty-third of June, then something would happen to that round, and what would happen to it is either that it stops or that it ends up on a list somewhere next to another one of itself, and the two of them would argue.

Nobody in that lane was helped this morning. Nobody in that lane refused anything. The round came and it went and the man of sixty filled his butt and the woman at number twenty-nine was not mentioned and was not asked about and does not exist on any paper in this borough.

The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and eighteen days old and a Thursday is not a reason to write on it and nobody is obliged. The card in the slot is a hundred and thirty-two days old. The letter in the tray is a hundred and thirty-four days old and it has two other pieces of post on it and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.
