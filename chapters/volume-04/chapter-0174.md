# Chapter 0174 — Eight Days

Friday the seventeenth of July 2015, from about two in the afternoon, a first-floor room over a lock-up in Tideglass, lent by a man who had the keys and was not coming back until Monday.

**The standing of the city, said in a room in daylight, out loud, without answering anything and without improving anything, and said in the order of the weeks instead of the order of a room, and it is the third time anybody has said it and it is not better than the other two and it is not worse. One thing in it is new and it is not a document and it is not a form and it is not a page and it is not a building, and it is the second smallest thing anybody in this borough has produced. He is not on the agenda and he may not chair anything and there is no chair of that body and there never was and the board has not met since the middle of January, and the ninth seat has not been told anything, and the lowland cannot be seated, and a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and it is the middle of July and it is still true.**

---

There were four people in it. A man of fifty-two with a dog. A woman of about thirty-one with four containers and a carrier bag. A man of about forty-six who had asked for a column. A man of thirty-two at the back with nothing in his hands.

Nobody was in charge and nobody took minutes.

---

**THE TWENTY-NINTH OF MAY.** A page in a tray in a room in this borough, with a space at the bottom of it for a person to agree, and the space empty. Not in force, not gone round anybody, not in a minute, nobody going to be asked to sign it. Read by one person who was not asked to read it, who said so out loud in a room in daylight and telephoned the building the next morning and said it a second time, and the office wrote nothing down.

**THE EIGHTEENTH OF FEBRUARY.** A letter in a tray on the second floor, sealed, with two other pieces of post lying on it, not opened in front of a second person, not answered, a hundred and forty-nine days old, and what is under the post is not known to anybody and may not be said in a room.

**THE TWENTIETH OF FEBRUARY.** A card in a slot at a counter on the ground floor, four inches by two, folded once, not a form, no crest, no reference, no box, a hundred and forty-seven days old, nobody has taken it out, nobody has put it up, and the person at that counter has not read it and is not going to.

---

**THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF NOVEMBER.** A blank line under the nineteenth entry of a register. Two hundred and thirty-three days, and the longest it has ever been, and still the only fixed point in that building. Entry nineteen is a yard light. The maintenance is a bulb. The bulb is out. Nobody is obliged and a cool week is not a reason and a hot fortnight was not a reason.

---

**THE SECOND WEEK OF JUNE, AND THE WORST HEAT IN NINETEEN YEARS.** Nine doors and the same correct word at every one of them, and the word was nothing, and it was printed on the nineteenth with a heading and four streets in a column headed *location* and no name box anywhere on it, and it went round a building in a tray on a Friday and it is in nine other buildings and nobody in any of them knows where it came from. **Nine people said yes to a correct question in a heat and they have not been counted and nothing since the nineteenth of June has counted them.**

Also that week: a laundry round with the last four stops done first at a cost to a hospital bed and a woman of about ninety-one, granted in about four seconds by somebody who could have said no. Four thousand litres into a school tank from a corridor, by a telephone call, by a person who said out loud unasked that it was a third of the problem. A cool hall opened at eleven by a woman of seventy-seven with a key.

**Nobody had to do any of it. All of it was done by a person who was not required to, not one of it is on a form, and a roster of the people who did it would be the only instrument in this city that could make them findable, and there is not going to be one.**

---

**THE SECOND HALF OF JUNE.** A grant in a sentence, and a man of thirty-eight wrote *agreed* and a date on the back of a woman's page, which is the first time in nine months that a correct thing in this borough has come with a piece of paper on it. **A piece of paper has to be paid for out of something, and the only line on that list nobody had ever stood in front of and asked for was the round at the end of a lane, and the bill landed on a man of sixty who has been filling a butt under his own downpipe out of his own pocket for about six years, and there is no way in this borough to put his name next to that fact and there is not going to be one, and nobody has told him about a timer and nobody is going to.**

Also a report from 2011 on a shelf in a building it has nothing to do with that nobody opened, and a man of about thirty-six who picked it up on the twenty-third of June while he was counting that shelf and put it back at the same angle and will not remember and is never going to be asked.

**A room in that building was painted on the twenty-ninth, and a tray of post that had come back off a Friday and had not gone out again went into a cupboard on the ground floor, because a list costs a person and a tray costs a Friday, and a records supervisor made that decision in about nine minutes and nobody is ever going to be able to check it.**

---

**THE FIRST HALF OF JULY.** A form dated the twenty-third of June walked down a lane at half past ten in the morning in the hand of a man of about forty-one who was right, and a man of sixty told him he should go away before anybody had asked him anything, and he was right too, and the round did not get funded and does not get done and nothing is helped.

A column headed *date seen again* went into a sheet on the eighth of July, and under it three words, **no name, no initials**, and the space is empty and it is the emptiest and the most dangerous object in this borough and it is correct.

A woman of twenty-nine put a shop's telephone number on a card in a shop window on the sixth of July with nothing else on it, and asked the woman who keeps that shop not to say who left it, and left a number where nothing used to be. **And in a different shop, in a different part of the borough, a man of about twenty-four who keeps that one did not telephone the man of about twenty-six about a fan that was not brought back, and did not buy another one out of a box at the back, and did not write it in the book on the back of his own counter, and none of those three is a decision.**

A street of terraces where two men were working in the middle of a cool month, one with a price and a card and one with a blanket under his arm, and neither in a document, and a printed sheet in another building saying, correctly, *no requirement stated*.

A counter in a vestibule eleven feet by nine that told a man of forty-seven three true things about June, with the date on each and no name on any, and he had not asked, and the help was correct, and it lasted as long as it took to walk to the kerb.

A chair on a landing in Tideglass that goes out at about eight in the evening and comes in at about six, and a man of about seventy-five who put a shopping bag on the floor beside it so a stranger would not have to move a wet chair, and nobody in this borough has been told either of those things.

---

**AND THE PART OF JULY THAT IS THIS WEEK, AND IT IS THE ONLY THING IN THIS THAT IS NEW, AND IT IS NOT A DOCUMENT, NOT A FORM, NOT A PAGE AND NOT A BUILDING.**

**Since about the third of July there have been two trestle tables and a cardboard box of water bottles on the pavement outside a shop on the far side of the market, put out at about eleven and brought in at about five, in the shade, with nothing written on them and nothing hanging over the front of them.**

A man who works in the cold store at the back of that shop puts them out. He was already going out into the sun at about eleven with a sack truck and there was a box of water going soft behind the counter that nobody was drinking. The pavement outside that shop is in the sun from about eleven until about two. He is not on a rota, nobody is required to put it out or bring it in, and there is no list of it, no name on it and no sign.

**It is the second smallest thing anybody in this borough has produced. The chair on a landing in Tideglass is smaller, and the chair has been going out since about the middle of June, and nobody in this borough has said out loud that one of these is smaller than the other, and if they had it would have been a rota inside a fortnight and the tables would be inside by one.**

---

**AND THE REST OF IT, WHICH DID NOT MOVE IN JULY, AND IS NOT GOING TO.**

A drawer under a counter with no key on it and no list of what is in it, with a tray on top of it that is not a list, and nobody in this borough can say how many, and the only figure ever written about it is nine and that was in March and it is a figure about the past. Three pieces of paper in a cardboard wallet in a van, since about Christmas, that nobody owns. A sentence about a catch tray that went into that drawer on the twentieth of May, which nobody in this borough knows is there, including him, and which is not going to be found.

A public report with a paragraph number on it standing in front of four hundred and twenty women, never answered, never numbered, and a machine on level zero still running. Two conditions on one wall sixteen inches apart that do not agree. Two letters and one word in pencil on the back of an enamel strip that have not been read. Nothing in this borough defines *holder*.

Seven of nine, two chairs empty, no chair of that body at all, and it has not met since the middle of January and is not obliged to. The ninth seat is fifty-four, unpaid, findable at a shopfront and at an address, and has not been told anything. The lowland is not on the layer and cannot be seated, because a node with no party is not a party and a place is not a person, and neither the hot fortnight nor the cool fortnight fixed it. Six water points at Tier 1, £11,280 a year, received and not evidenced.

A gate that goes up at seven on a Sunday morning and stops at six on the Sunday morning after that, six days long and not a week, with no date on it anywhere in this city, read aloud by two tired men who are not on anything. **No handover has been staged since the fifteenth, and the hour between six and seven was staged once and once is all it is getting.**

The row from eleven o'clock to half past one at a hospital, empty, and not shaded, and it does not say *vacant*. A porter's Thursday still refused in his own handwriting with a second line in somebody else's hand under it, and nobody has looked at the second line and nobody is going to.

And a man of forty-four nobody can ask, a man of forty-nine nobody has told, a man of forty-eight nobody has asked, a woman of fifty-four at a shopfront, and a man of about thirty-three who worked out on his own in June that there is a person in this city who is not on anything.

---

**AND A SENTENCE THAT HAS BEEN TRUE IN THIS BOROUGH SINCE THE SECOND OF JUNE, AND WHICH THE HEAT DID NOT REFUTE AND THE COOL DID NOT REFUTE, AND WHICH A FAN, A PAGE, A ROW, A COLUMN, A CARD, A BAG ON A FLOOR AND TWO TRESTLE TABLES DID NOT REFUTE: A PERSON WHO IS NOT COUNTED IS NOT A PERSON WHO IS HELPED.**

A man of thirty-two is not on anything and is four hundred yards away on purpose and has stopped counting, and the count stops at eight.

---

**AND THE LAST OF IT, WHICH IS A QUESTION AND NOT A RESOLUTION, AND IT IS NOT ABOUT A PERSON.**

There are eight things in this borough that anybody can walk in and read, and every one of them has a day on it, and not one of them has a name on it.

The eighteenth of February. The twentieth of February. The twenty-sixth of November. The nineteenth of June. The twenty-fifth of June. The sixth of July. The eighth of July. The seventeenth, which is today.

**Seven of those eight are on paper somewhere and a person can walk in and look at them. The eighth is this room. There is nothing on the wall of it, there is no page and there is no minute, and the only account of the seventeenth of July that exists is four people saying it out loud to each other this afternoon with nobody taking it down, and if all four of them stopped walking into this room tomorrow the seventeenth of July would not be a day anybody in this borough could point at. It is the only one of the eight that is not a thing in a building, and it is the only one of the eight that could be lost by four people dying.**

**A day can be pointed at. A name cannot.** In nine months nobody in this city has found a person by a date, and nobody has been found by a name either, and that is the only privacy anybody here has ever had, and it was never designed and it is not defended and it is one table away from not existing. A room is not a record, and there is no way in this borough to ask a room what it remembers, and the eighth of these eight is a room.

So which of the eight is the first one somebody in this borough decides to keep, and is it going to be kept because it was good or because it was expensive.
