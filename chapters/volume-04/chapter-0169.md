# Chapter 0169 — Date Seen Again

Wednesday the eighth of July 2015, from about half past ten in the morning, a first-floor room at the back of a building in Tideglass.

**A correct document is wanted for a second time by a person who is right that a thing you cannot repeat is a feeling and who is wrong about the only instrument anybody in this borough has for repeating a thing. One column was added, and it is empty, and it is going to stay empty, and that is the correct outcome and it is also the emptiest object in this borough. He asked no question of anybody. It is not a fourth answer. It does not go in a minute and nobody was thanked.**

---

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE IN THAT ROOM, above the door, printed and screwed to the wall at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing and nothing on it defines *holder*, and it requires nothing of anybody:**

> **A SHEET IN A TRAY IN THIS BUILDING IS NOT A RETURN.**
> **NO PERSON IS REQUIRED TO RETURN, CIRCULATE, COMPLETE, EXPLAIN OR ANSWER FOR A SHEET IN A TRAY.**
> **ANY SHEET IN A TRAY IN THIS BUILDING MAY BE PUT BACK IN THE TRAY IT CAME FROM.**

---

The sheet went round on Friday the nineteenth of June, folded in four, in a tray with nine other things in it, and it is in nine buildings and nobody in any of them knows where it came from. It has a heading, the date, four streets down the left in a column headed *location*, nine lines beside them, and every one of the nine lines says *no requirement stated*.

**There is no name box anywhere on that sheet and there is no column for a name and that was the whole of what anybody in the room in June thought about, and the whole of what is correct about it.**

There were four people in that room on the eighth of July and every one of them was right about something.

---

**THE MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SIX** read it on the twenty-fourth of June in a tray in this building, and it is not his tray and it was not addressed to him, and he is not on a rota and he does not want anything out of it.

**DENISE HALLAM, 44**, who manages the contract the standpipes sit inside and who is the person a sheet goes out on.

**A WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-SEVEN** who takes a tray round the floors on a Friday and brings it back on the Friday, and has done for about six years, and does not read it.

**And a man of thirty-two at the back with nothing in his hands who is not on the agenda of anything.**

---

"**I have been round all four of them,**" the man of forty-six said. "**Twice. Once with the sheet and once without it, and I want to be clear that I went the second time as a person and not as a building.**"

"**Right.**"

"**And the second time I did not have anything in my hand and I got nine different sentences, and the nine different sentences were all *we have already said*. Not one of them was unkind. Not one of them was a complaint. And every single one of them was the truth, because they have already said it, and they said it correctly, and nobody has anything to add to it.**"

"**So where does that leave you.**"

"**It leaves me standing on a doorstep having been told the correct answer a second time and being unable to do a single thing with it.**"

---

**AND HERE IS WHY HE WANTED THE COLUMN, AND HE IS ENTIRELY RIGHT ABOUT IT, AND IT IS THE CORRECTEST THING SAID IN THAT ROOM.**

"**That sheet works for about a fortnight. It is a thing with a date on it, and I can carry it, and I can say *this is what we told you*, and for about a fortnight that is worth something. After that it is a piece of paper.**"

"**It is a piece of paper now.**"

"**It is worse than a piece of paper now, because a piece of paper can be thrown away and this one is going to still be on a wall somewhere, in nine buildings, by the autumn, saying the same nine words.**"

"**That is what it is for.**"

"**That is what it is for and it is correct and I am not arguing with it. I am saying that a thing you cannot repeat is a feeling. If I write on it that I went back on the second of July, then it is not one visit, it is two, and the two of them are a thing. If it is one visit then it is a feeling, and I have got it, and the nine of them have got it, and after that it is over.**"

---

**AND DENISE HALLAM SAID THE OTHER THING, AND SHE WAS RIGHT ABOUT THAT TOO, AND IT WAS NOT THE SAME THING.**

"**A column is a column.**"

"**I know what a column is.**"

"**You have asked for a column headed *date seen again*, and you are right that it will be empty in a fortnight, and you are right that it will be on a wall in nine buildings by the autumn, and you are right about all of it, and I am telling you what a column is, which is that the first person to write in it decides what goes in it.**"

"**Then I will put dates in it.**"

"**You will put dates in it. And then there is a date in it with a handwriting on it, and a handwriting is a person, and a person is somebody you can come and ask why they were on that street on the second of July.**"

"**I would not mind that.**"

"**You would not mind it because it is you. You have a reason to be on that street, and the reason is that somebody put a column on a piece of paper and you filled it in.**"

---

**AND THE WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-SEVEN SAID THE THIRD THING, FROM THE DOOR, CARRYING A TRAY, AND SHE WAS RIGHT AND SHE DID NOT PUT THE TRAY DOWN.**

"**It went round in this tray.**"

"**I know.**"

"**It went round in this tray with nine other things in it, on a Friday, with the lid off, and it was not handed to anybody. It was in a room. Nine other things were in that tray, and two of them were a lunch menu and a leaflet about a closure, and a sheet with a date and four streets on it was in there between them for about two days and nobody picked it up until somebody needed a leaflet.**"

"**That is not a route.**"

"**It is not a route. It is the reason it got to nine buildings and it is the reason nobody in nine buildings has ever asked about it. You cannot ask about a thing that arrived in a tray with a lunch menu in it. You have to have picked it up deliberately, and there is a difference between picking something up and needing it, and only one of those is a decision anybody makes on a Friday afternoon.**"

---

**AND THEN THE MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SIX WORKED IT OUT HIMSELF, IN THE ROOM, IN DAYLIGHT, IN ABOUT TWENTY SECONDS, AND HE SAID IT OUT LOUD BECAUSE HE HAD BEEN THINKING IT SINCE THE SECOND OF JULY.**

"**Then I will not fill it in.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**No. I have thought about it on two walks and I have got it now. If I fill that column in, then that column is about me, and I have put myself in a document in nine buildings, and in about a year somebody will want to know why a man of forty-six was going round those four streets on his own time, and there is no version of that answer that is any good. It will be *he was concerned*. It will be *he was being thorough*. And then I will do it again next year for nothing and get the same two sentences, and I will be a man in this borough who goes round four streets being thorough, forever, with no way to stop.**"

"**So you want the column.**"

"**I want the column. And I am not going to be the one in it.**"

---

**AND THE COLUMN WAS ADDED, AND IT IS EMPTY, AND IT IS GOING TO STAY EMPTY, AND THAT IS THE CORRECT OUTCOME.**

*location* · *no requirement stated* · **date seen again**

And under the column, printed in the same size type as the rest of it, three words: **no name, no initials.**

**A document that asked for nothing in the worst heat in nineteen years now has a space in it for asking, and the space is empty, and a space that somebody can ask in is a thing that can be answered in, and the answer goes in the column, and the column is a column, and a column wants a name in it, and the three words under it are the only defence anybody in this borough has ever been able to write against that, and they are three words on a piece of paper and they are not a rule and nobody is required to keep to them and there is nothing behind them.**

The man of forty-six put his hand flat on the table for a moment and then took it off it.

"**That is right,**" he said. "**And it will not work, and I asked for it anyway, and I would like it minuted that I said that.**"

"**Nothing is going to be minuted.**"

"**No,**" he said. "**It is not, is it.**"

---

**AND THE SHEET WENT ROUND AGAIN ON THE FRIDAY IN A TRAY, FOLDED IN FOUR, WITH THE LID OFF, AND THE JUNE SHEET IS STILL IN NINE BUILDINGS AND WAS NOT WITHDRAWN AND WAS NOT ANNOTATED AND WAS NOT REPRINTED, AND NOBODY IN ANY OF THOSE NINE BUILDINGS KNOWS THAT THERE ARE TWO OF THEM.**

He was at the back of that room with nothing in his hands and he asked no question of anybody and he said one thing out loud, late, after the other three had finished, and only to the man of forty-six, and it was this.

"**It is a level of service. That is what the June one is. It is a statement with a date on it of what this borough is going to do about nine households on four streets, and it is nothing, and nobody asked anybody to be nothing, and now it cannot be got out of. And that is what you are holding in your hand. Both of them. And neither one of them is a survey and neither one of them is a demand, and the only sentence anybody in this city is ever going to have about the second week of June is a piece of paper with a heading on it.**"

"**That is not an argument for the column.**"

"**No,**" he said. "**It is an argument for the column being empty.**"

---

Nobody in that room was thanked and nobody refused to be thanked and nobody in that room was helped. **There is no form in this borough on which anybody could record that a man of forty-six asked for a column on the eighth of July, and there is not going to be one, and the column is on a piece of paper going round a tray on a Friday, and the space for asking in it is empty this morning and will be empty in September.**

The register of narrowings is at nineteen. The blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and twenty-four days old and it is still the only fixed point in that building. The card in the slot is a hundred and thirty-eight days old. The letter in the tray is a hundred and forty days old, it is under two other pieces of post, and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a man of forty-six has been right about a feeling on four streets, and a piece of paper has been made that can be answered, and nothing has been answered, and it is the emptiest and the most dangerous object in this borough and it is correct.**
