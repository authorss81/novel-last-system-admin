# Chapter 0179 — Harker Street

Thursday the twenty-third of July 2015, from about two in the afternoon, a first-floor room over a shop on a parade of shops in Tideglass.

**A person with a constituency of his own is in a room saying one thing that is correct about it, and every other person in the room agrees with him, and the agreeing is the whole of what happens, and it is the most expensive thing this room has ever heard. It is the first day in about six weeks that anybody in this borough has had to think about the weather and nothing in that room moved because of it. He asked no question of anybody and nobody asked him anything. Nothing was resolved and nobody was thanked and nobody is going to tell them anything.**

---

The window in that room has been open since about one o'clock and there is no fan in it and there has not been a fan in it since the spring. It is twenty-nine degrees on a street of shopfronts with the sun on the front of them, and it is the first day since the heat broke on the twenty-first of June that anybody in this borough has had to think about weather at all, and a hot day is not a reason to look at a board or to ask anybody about a duty, and nobody has.

There were four people in it. A man of about sixty-nine. A woman of about fifty-eight who has the keys. A man of about seventy-four. And a man of thirty-two at the back with nothing in his hands.

---

**THE MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-NINE HAS LIVED ON HARKER STREET FOR TWELVE YEARS AND HE IS NOT ON ANYTHING AND HE IS NOT GOING TO BE, AND HE HAS A CONSTITUENCY OF ONE STREET AND IT IS THE ONLY CONSTITUENCY HE HAS EVER HAD.**

He came in at about two o'clock because he has been coming in on a Thursday since about March, and he came in and he sat down and he said it, and he said it once.

"**There is nowhere on Harker Street to sit in the shade after two o'clock.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**That is all. That is the whole thing I have come to say and I have been working out how to say it since March and it is fourteen words and it has taken me twelve years.**"

---

"**Is that all it is,**" the man of about seventy-four said. "**I drove that street for twenty-two years. Twice a day. In a bus. I never once thought about the shade on it.**"

"**You were driving.**"

"**I was driving.**"

"**There is a wall on the north side with about two feet of shadow on it at four o'clock in July and there is a wall on the south side with nothing on it at all, and there are thirty-one doors on that street and I have counted them about four times this year because I keep looking at them and there is nowhere.**"

---

"**Who is it for,**" the woman of about fifty-eight said. "**That is what I want to know before anybody agrees with him. Who is that for.**"

"**I do not know,**" the man of sixty-nine said. "**That is the honest answer and I would rather say it than a made-up one. It is not for old people. It is for whoever is out in the street at four o'clock in July, and that is a person with a pushchair, or a person who has walked to the shops, or a person who has come out of a hot flat to get some air, and there is nowhere to stand that is not in the sun, and I have watched three men try to do it in a fortnight and one of them do it in a hat.**"

---

**AND THE MAN OF ABOUT SEVENTY-FOUR SAID THE OTHER THING, AND HE WAS RIGHT, AND IT IS WHY THE ROOM IS GOING TO BE A PROBLEM.**

"**I will tell you what I have never been able to work out,**" he said. "**In twenty-two years of driving that street, the one thing I could have done about anything on it was stop and say something to somebody. There is no list. There is no form. There is nobody I could have rung. And that has been the most useful thing about that street and it is going to be the most dangerous thing about it, and I want the two things said in the same breath because they are the same thing.**"

"**Say the dangerous one.**"

"**If something ever did happen on that street — and I am not saying it is going to — then the person this borough would come to is him.**"

"**Me,**" the man of sixty-nine said. "**Yes.**"

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT HE SAID ABOUT HIMSELF, IN THE ROOM, IN DAYLIGHT, AND HE SAID IT ONCE, AND HE DID NOT ASK FOR IT NOT TO BE MINUED, AND NOTHING IS GOING TO BE MINUTED ANYWAY.**

"**I have never asked anybody for anything in twelve years and I am not going to start. If somebody built me a bench on that street I would be the person who asked for it and then I would be the person who can be asked why there is a bench on that street, and in about two years I would be a man in a magazine.**"

"**You would not.**"

"**I would,**" he said. "**Not this year. In about two years, and it would be one sentence in it, and there would be a photograph of the bench, and the photograph would be of a street with a man in it, and after that nobody on that street could say anything about the shade again without me being in it.**"

"**So you have come here to tell us and not to ask.**"

"**I have come here because I have been saying it out loud in my own kitchen since March and it is much better said to people who do not know me.**"

---

**AND EVERYBODY IN THAT ROOM AGREED WITH HIM, AND THE AGREEING IS THE COST, AND IT IS THE ONLY THING THAT HAPPENED IN THAT ROOM THIS AFTERNOON.**

The woman of about fifty-eight said it was right and said that it was not on anything.

The man of about seventy-four said it was right and said that he had driven past it two thousand times.

A man of thirty-two said nothing for about a minute and then said one sentence, to the room, unasked, which was: **that a street with a person in a building who is right about it is not a service, and does not become one, and is not a document, and is now going to exist whether anybody in this borough likes it or not.**

"**That is not a comfort,**" the woman of about fifty-eight said.

"**No,**" he said. "**It is not a comfort and it is not a threat and it is not anybody's fault. It is what a room is. A room hears a person say a true thing about a street, and from that moment the room has got a person in it who it can go to about that street. There is no instrument in this city that can stop a room remembering a person, and there is not one that can tell him it has not remembered him either, and he is the one who came.**"

---

**AND THE HEAT DID NOTHING. IT DID NOT CHANGE ONE THING IN THAT ROOM AND IT IS NOT GOING TO.**

At about half past three the woman of fifty-eight said that it was the first hot day since June, and the man of seventy-four said that in 1998 it had been thirty-four, and nobody connected that to anything, and nobody in that room suggested a tap, or a bottle of water, or a rota, or a form, or a list, or a shade.

**A hot day is not a reason to rebuild a rota and the rota has not been rebuilt. A hot day is not a reason to look at a board. A hot day is not a reason to look at a second line under anybody's handwriting, and nobody looked, and nobody is going to.**

---

**AND THE MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-NINE WAS ASKED NOTHING FOR THE WHOLE TIME HE WAS IN THAT ROOM.**

A man of thirty-two has asked no question of anybody since the twenty-fifth of February and he did not ask one this afternoon, and it is the one afternoon in this borough where he could have asked two good ones and got good answers and it would have cost a man of sixty-nine about ninety seconds of his life.

Nobody asked him what his name was. Nobody asked him which number he lived at. Nobody wrote Harker Street down on anything, and there is no form in this borough on which a street with a person in a building who is right about it could be recorded, and there is not going to be one, because recording it would mean a person, and a person is a thing you can come and ask.

**He said his street out loud in a room in daylight and that is the whole of what he did, and it was correct, and it was the cheapest thing a person can do with a summer, and it has put a person into a building, and he knew that when he said it and he said it anyway.**

---

He left at about four. He walked to the parade and turned right and got on the bus and went nine stops to a shop he has been going to for eleven years, and the man of seventy-four went out at the same time and said good afternoon to him on the stairs, which is a thing he has done to him before and will do again, and neither of them said anything else.

**Nobody in that room was thanked and nobody refused to be thanked. Nothing was resolved, nothing is going to be minuted, and nobody is going to tell him anything.**

The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and thirty-nine days old and twenty-nine degrees is not a reason to write on it and nobody is obliged. The card in the slot is a hundred and fifty-three days old. The letter in the tray on the floor above is a hundred and fifty-five days old, it has two pieces of post lying on it, and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a man of sixty-nine has been one person on one street for twelve years and this afternoon four people in a building agreed with him about it, and from today that street has a name in a building that is right about it, and that is not a service and does not become one, and he is not going to be asked again, and nobody is going to tell him anything.**
