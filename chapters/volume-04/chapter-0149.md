# Chapter 0149 — The Word On The Cover

Tuesday the nineteenth of May 2015, from about ten in the morning, a corridor, the records office, second floor, the Old Ward; and about two in the afternoon, the yard behind Bellow Market; and about four o'clock, the yard again.

**He asked no question of anybody on that day. The file was not opened and nobody was asked to reopen it, and nobody may be compelled to be told anything, including a correction. The woman who could have dealt with it was asked about a fan four days ago and gave the right answer, and she is not on a rota and cannot be made to be on one. One sentence was written at that counter on that borough's ground floor last Wednesday and nobody knows it and he is not going to be told.**

---

Nobody in that office knew what was on the shelf under the window.

**That is the first fact. It is not a secret and it is not a concealment. Three people work on that floor and all three of them have come past that shelf every working day for years, and not one of them has ever lifted the bundle off the bundle, because there is no reason to, and a tray of post on a shelf in a hot corridor in a building is a tray of post.**

---

**And the second fact is the one he had in his head by about half past ten, standing at the end of that corridor with nothing in his hands, and it is the whole of the resistance and it is four sentences and it is not going to be improved on.**

**A file that was not dealt with cannot be reopened by anybody who was not asked to reopen it.**

Not by him. He is not a department. He is not a party. He is not on a rota, he is not on a board, he has no grade, no department and no probation, and there is no box anywhere in this city that would take a request from him for a file, and if there were one then in a few years somebody would be able to look one up, and the first thing anybody would do with a list of files somebody had asked for is find out which files people had asked about and the second thing they would do is ask those people why.

Not the woman at the counter on that floor, who has been at it for eleven years and who is a different person from the woman who comes down to the ground floor, and the two of them have never been the same person and there is nothing anywhere in that building which says so and there is not going to be anything.

**And not the woman who comes down to the ground floor at about ten and about three, either, and the reason she cannot be is four sentences long and it is the whole of why Tuesday afternoon went the way it went.**

---

She has a job. She has had it for nineteen years. She came down to that counter at about ten and at about three because nobody has ever told her to and nobody could.

**The word *rota* is doing an enormous amount of work in this borough and it is doing it wrongly, and the reason it is doing it wrongly is that everybody here thinks of a rota as a piece of paper, and a rota is not a piece of paper. A rota is a person who cannot say no.**

If she were on one, then in a few years there would be a version of her that anybody could ask whether she had gone and looked. And a person who has been asked whether she went and looked is a person who is responsible for having gone and looked. And a person who is responsible for having gone and looked is a person who has to go and look.

**So she cannot be put on one, and she is not going to put herself on one, and on Friday a woman of about forty came into that vestibule with a bag of shopping and asked the counter whether the borough had fans, and gave her a correct answer. The whole of the price of a correct answer given out loud in an open room in a heat is that the room remembers it, and a room is not a record, and there is no way in this borough to ask a room what it remembers, and nobody who was in that room that morning could be asked about it by anybody with a grade.**

---

He got to the yard behind Bellow Market at about two in the afternoon.

**Nobody in this borough can be compelled to be told anything. Not the state of a bay, not the state of a gully, not the state of a padlock, and not that a file exists on a shelf in a building it has nothing to do with.**

The bay was washed. It had been hosed down on the Sunday by somebody with a hose, and the concrete was a shade darker than the concrete round it, and the line of it stopped about two feet short of the brick wall, which is what a hose does.

**At the low corner, at the foot of the brick wall, there is a cast grate the shape of a horseshoe set into the concrete, and the grate is the original one, and it has never been raised, and about four feet in front of it there is a wheel stop: a length of concrete, half a wheel high, cast into the apron, chipped at one end, painted at some point and repainted at some other point.**

A van had been backed up against that wheel stop on the Saturday morning, because a wheel stop is exactly what a van's rear wheel goes against, and that is what it is for, and it is the only thing in that yard whose purpose anybody can see.

**And there is no catch tray in front of the grate. There is a rectangular stain in the concrete where one stood for a long time, and the stain is older than the stain round it, and the tray is not there, and there is no pipe off the grate, and there is no bracket on the wall, and there is no hole in the wall.**

---

And the lock-up.

**The shutter is steel and it is down and it is about seven feet high, and there is a hasp on the bottom of it, and on the hasp there is a padlock, and the padlock is not rusted, and that is the entire observation available to a person standing in that yard on the nineteenth of May, and it is correct, and it is all anybody has got.**

**Nobody in this city has ever written down what that padlock is for.**

The man who used to answer for it is a market porter and has been one for thirty years and he was at that market on the Saturday morning and he was at it again this morning, and on Wednesday the twenty-sixth of November 2014, in a room in daylight, he was asked whether he would go on answering for the lock-up at Bellow Market, the shutter, the padlock and what the padlock is for, and he stated that he would not continue. No reason was recorded, at his request, and none was given. He was not asked a second question. The seat fell vacant that evening and it remains vacant, and no person may be compelled to take it, and there has been no answer since, and nobody in this borough has been asked a second time, and there is nowhere in this borough any of it could be written down.

**A padlock is the whole of a room's purpose in this city and the padlock has been changed since November, and the reason a person can stand in a yard and say that and be entirely right is that a padlock is a thing and anybody may look at a thing, and a person may not.**

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT HAPPENED AT ABOUT FOUR O'CLOCK, AND HE WAS NOT IN IT, AND NOBODY KNOWS IT, AND THE REASON NOBODY KNOWS IT IS THE WHOLE OF THE CHAPTER.**

The woman who comes down from that floor came off a bus at about ten past three at the end of that road, on her own time, with a bag, in a heat, and walked up the lane and into the yard because a card on a buff cover in a tray under a bundle of post had said **REPORT OF A RELEASE — MARKET YARD, BAY 2 — 14 OCTOBER 2011** and had the word **CLOSED** stamped on it, and nothing else.

**She did not open the file. Nobody asked her to and she was not going to and she could not have, because the file is in a tray in a building two miles from that yard and she is standing in the yard, and a cover with a word stamped on it is all she had and all she needed.**

She walked out to the low corner. She looked at the grate for a while. She looked at the wheel stop. She looked along the wall at the height of a person, which is what everybody does, and there was no bracket and no hole and nothing.

**And then she went back to the shutter and stood about four feet off it and looked at the hasp.**

**And then she went and sat on the low wall by the bins for about ten minutes with her bag between her feet, in the sun, in a heat, at twenty past three in the afternoon, because the sun coming off that yard at that hour is not a thing anybody can think in, and she was not on her own time off. She has half an hour.**

---

**AND HERE IS THE SENTENCE, AND IT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT ANYBODY IN THIS BOROUGH HAS MANAGED TO GET RIGHT ON THE RECORD THIS MONTH, AND IT IS ONE SENTENCE, AND IT IS ABOUT A THING AND NOT ABOUT A PERSON.**

**On Tuesday the nineteenth of May, at about four in the afternoon, the catch tray at the low corner of bay two in the market yard behind Bellow Market was not there, and there is a report of a release at that bay dated the fourteenth of October 2011 in a tray on the second floor of the Old Ward, and it has not been opened.**

**It is in a hand. It is dated. There is no reference on it and no box on it and no name on it, and there is no name on it including hers, and it went into the drawer under that counter at about ten the next morning when she came down, under the tray and behind the post, because that is where a person puts a thing that is a person's.**

**It is not a receipt. It cannot be paid, cannot be returned, cannot be filed and cannot be thrown away, and the last of those is not anybody's doing, it is the absence of a procedure.**

**It is not a minute. It is not a return. It is not going in one. Nobody has been asked for a reason. Nobody is going to be asked for one. It is not going on a wall and it is not the fourth of anything anybody has said in a room in nine months, and it is not a sentence about a floor or about a cost or about a purpose, and it is the first thing anybody in this borough has got right since the beginning of the month.**

---

**AND HERE IS THE THING THAT HAPPENED TO HER, AND SHE DID NOT NOTICE IT HAPPENING, AND HE WOULD NOTICE IT IN A CORRIDOR IN TWO DAYS AND IS NOT GOING TO SAY ANYTHING.**

She has nineteen years of putting a person's own account of a day into a tray, and about eleven people a day come through that door and about one in a week stands in front of it and asks what was true on a day, and it has never once occurred to her that she might be one of them.

**She wrote one. It is in a drawer with no key on it and there is no list of what is in that drawer, and she has not told anybody that she wrote it and nobody has asked her, and she is not going to be told what that drawer is going to be used for.**

**And the price of it is not going to be paid by her. It is going to be paid in a few years, by a person in a kitchen, holding a piece of paper, being handed something that somebody thought was help. And the thing that will make it evidence is the hand on it, and the hand is hers, and there is no name on it, and the only person in this city who could ever say whose hand it is is her, and she is not going to be asked, and she would answer if she were asked, and she is not going to be told that she has to be.**

---

Nobody was helped.

The catch tray is not at that gully and nobody in this borough is required to put it there, and there is a cover with a word on it in a tray under a bundle of post in a corridor that four people walk past, and the word is CLOSED, and a word with a stamp on it is not an answer, and it is not a bar, and it was enough to put one woman on a bus at ten past three on a Tuesday afternoon with a bag.

**And the report is still in the tray. It was not opened, it is not going to be opened, and nobody in this city is going to ask anybody to open it, and if it stays shut for four more years then somebody will be able to say, correctly and from outside, that a file was carried into a building and put on a shelf and never dealt with, and it is going to be said by a person who has never seen the file and does not know it is there.**
