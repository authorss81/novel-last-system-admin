# Chapter 0161 — Twenty-Two Degrees

Tuesday the twenty-third of June 2015, from about ten past ten in the morning, the vestibule and the ground floor, the Old Ward; and about half past eleven, the second floor of the same building.

**The weather turned on the Sunday and it was twenty-two degrees on the Tuesday and about eleven people went through that counter as usual, and not the same ones as on the Friday. A cool day is the hardest kind of day in this borough to say anything true about and this borough is the worst at it, and a cool day does not forgive anything, and everything that happened in June is still there in July and nobody has to do anything about any of it. He asked no question of anybody and nobody asked him anything. One of the four things nobody in this borough has an answer to is one day nearer than it was, because a man of thirty-six picked it up and put it back down and did not know what he had been holding, and he said nothing, and nobody is ever going to know whether that was the right thing to do.**

---

**It turned on the Sunday morning about eleven and it was twenty-two degrees by the Monday and it has not been above twenty-four since.**

He has been in this city through three of these and he knows what the heat does to a place and he has watched it do it to a place in June, and the thing nobody tells you about the fourth week is that it is not the heat that is the finding. The heat is only the weather. The finding is always the same shape and it is always about a person, and the heat is the only thing in nineteen years that has made enough people stand still at the same time for anybody to be able to see it.

**And a cool Tuesday puts all of that away and gives you back the thing you had before, which is a place where things happen, and this borough does not know what to do with that, because it has spent nine months being a place where things happen to people.**

---

**AND THE COUNTER, WHICH DID NOT MOVE, AND WAS NOT A REASON, AND WAS UNDER LESS PRESSURE THAN IT HAS BEEN SINCE THE FIFTEENTH OF MAY.**

He came in off the street at about ten past ten and the door of that vestibule was propped with a wedge of wood, and the wedge is the one from the fire door, and somebody propped it there on purpose about a month ago and nobody has taken it out, because a door propped in June is a door somebody propped, and nobody in that building is going to be the one who undoes it.

The counter is about eight feet long and answers what was true on a day, and gives a date, and about eleven people a day.

**In the heat the same eleven came through, and not the same eleven, and there is no way of knowing which is which. On the Tuesday it was a different eleven again, and the differentness was not a person refusing something. It was a person who did not need anything on a cool Tuesday, which is a thing that has never been recorded anywhere and is not going to be.**

It answered about a thing twice. It refused nothing, because nothing was refused, because nobody asked it anything about a gate, or a rota, or a compliance paragraph, or a person, and it would not have answered any of those if they had been asked, and the pressure of a heat is not a reason and the absence of a heat is not a reason either.

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT A COOL DAY SHOWS YOU, AND IT IS THE FINDING, AND IT IS NOT A DISCOVERY.**

**Everything that got made in the heat got made by a person.**

The last four stops of a laundry round, done first, at a cost, in a room the size of a garage, on the back of a delivery note. Four thousand litres into a school tank, on a Friday afternoon, by a person who got a third of a problem and said so out loud unasked and said where the other two thirds had gone. Nine identical answers collected at nine kitchen doors by a man with a dog. A standpipe left on until four because a woman of about sixty said four words in a passage. A cool room in a church hall opened at eleven by a woman of seventy-seven with a key.

**Not one of those is on a form. Not one of those has a name on it. Not one of those is a rota row, a board seat, a register entry, a minute, or a column on a delivery sheet, and there is no mechanism in this city that would let any of them happen again except the people who did them doing them again.**

And they are not going to be stopped by the weather. The woman of about thirty-one with a barrow was not carrying anything on the Tuesday because there was nothing to carry, and that is not a resolution, and she will be carrying again in August, and there is nothing anybody in this borough can give her that would change it, and the one thing she has that works is nine lines on a piece of paper the size of a receipt with street numbers in her handwriting and nothing at the top of it.

**And this is the hardest thing in the borough to say out loud and it is the only thing that has to be said about the heat: a week of weather made a small number of people do things they are not required to do, and a cool day does not turn that into a system, and the correct outcome is that it goes on being about four people, and the wrong outcome is that somebody builds a thing out of it so that it is not four people.**

---

At about half past ten he went up.

The building is open on a Saturday morning for that counter and has been for years and nobody has ever written down why. He had been in that corridor in May and in June and nobody has asked him anything on any of those visits and nobody is going to.

**The room on the second floor is being repainted on the twenty-ninth of June, and so the shelves under the window are being counted.**

A man of about thirty-six with a clipboard and a paint tin and a manila envelope was standing at the shelf under the window at about half past eleven, working down it, counting files by the inch.

**Nobody has ever asked him anything either. He is not on a rota. He is not on a board. He does not know why the room is being painted and he has not asked, and he is a person in this borough who is in a corridor with his hands on a shelf, and there is no instrument in this city that could say what he is about to do.**

---

**AND HE PICKED IT UP BECAUSE HE WAS COUNTING, AND BECAUSE IT WAS IN THE TRAY HE WAS COUNTING, AND HE PUT IT BACK EXACTLY AS HE FOUND IT, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAPPENED.**

A buff card with a printed box on it and a date and the word CLOSED on it, and the date is the fourteenth of October 2011, and the box says REPORT OF A RELEASE — MARKET YARD, BAY 2, and there is a bundle of post sitting on top of the tray it was in and the bundle of post did not move.

He looked at it for about a second and a half. He did not open it. Nobody in that room asked him to open it and he is not going to and there is no instruction anywhere in this borough that anybody has to open it.

He put it back in the same tray, under the same bundle of post, in the same place, at the same angle, and he went back to counting, and he will not remember doing it, and nobody is ever going to ask him whether he did.

---

**AND HE WAS AT THE END OF THAT CORRIDOR AND HE SAID NOTHING, AND HE HAD A SENTENCE'S WORTH OF TIME IN WHICH HE COULD HAVE SAID SOMETHING, AND WHETHER HE SHOULD HAVE SAID IT IS A QUESTION HE IS NOT GOING TO ANSWER AND NOBODY IS GOING TO ANSWER IT FOR HIM.**

What he could have said was the bay number and the date, out loud, to a man counting a shelf. And the man would have said *is it* and the file would have had two people in it in about a minute, and two people is the beginning of a record, and a record of a shelf is a thing that can be asked about afterwards by somebody who was not in this corridor.

There are four things in this borough that nobody has an answer to and he can name all four of them in a sentence, and he has never been able to finish any of them, and one of them was in that man's hands at about half past eleven on the twenty-third of June and the man does not know it.

**A thing that has been looked at by two people is a day nearer being answered. A thing that has been looked at by one person is a thing in a tray under a bundle of post in a corridor on the second floor of a building it has nothing to do with, and that is where it has been since the spring, and that is correct, and it is not going to be disturbed, and no one is going to write it down.**

Whether saying the bay number and the date out loud to a man with a clipboard on a Tuesday morning would have been the cheapest thing anybody has done in nine months or the end of the cheapest privacy device in this city is a question he has not answered and is not going to answer, and he stood at the end of that corridor for about a quarter of an hour afterwards and did not answer it, and then he went down the stairs and out into a street that was twenty-two degrees.

---

Nobody was helped. Nobody was harmed, nobody died, and the bulb on the nineteenth is still out.

The register of narrowings is at nineteen entries and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and nine days old and it is still the only fixed point in that building and it is still blank, and entry nineteen is a yard light and the maintenance is a bulb and the bulb is out, and a cool Tuesday is not a reason to write on a blank line and nobody is obliged to. The card in the slot is a hundred and twenty-three days old and the letter in the tray is a hundred and twenty-five days old and has two other pieces of post on top of it and has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and a cool day does not put that right, and a cool day is not evidence, and this borough has produced nothing in nine months that anybody in this borough can hold up and say *this, here, and it is counted*.**
