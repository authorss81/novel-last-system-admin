# Chapter 0145 — A Hot Fortnight

Friday the fifteenth of May 2015, from about ten past ten in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward; and the second floor of the same building at about half past one in the afternoon; and a street in Tideglass at about four o'clock in the afternoon.

**He asked no question of anybody in any of those three places and nobody asked him anything. The counter answered about a thing and refused twice, and both refusals were correct, and the register did not move, and a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and that is not news this morning either.**

---

It had been warm since Tuesday and by Friday it was a different kind of warm.

That is the whole of what a heat is in a place like this. It is not a wall of hot air and a red notice. It is a Tuesday that turns into a Friday, and by Friday everybody in the borough has stopped saying *isn't it lovely* in the voice they use for it, and the water is the first thing to go, and then the laundry, and then a fan in a wing that nobody has had a budget for.

He came in off the street at about ten past ten and the door of that vestibule was propped with a wedge of wood, and the wedge was the one from the fire door, and somebody had propped it there on purpose.

---

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THAT WALL, taped over the top half of an older notice nobody took down, It defines nothing and nothing on it defines *holder*, and it requires nothing of anybody, and the 1 March line on the enamel strip was not reissued and was not restated and was not quoted, and the two conditions on the wall beside it are sixteen inches apart and were not reconciled:**

> **DURING HEAT, WATER IS TAKEN FROM THE LOWLANDS FIRST.**
> **THE ORDER IN WHICH IT IS TAKEN IS NOT WRITTEN DOWN.**
> **NO PERSON IS ASKED FOR ANYTHING IN ORDER TO HAVE ANY WATER.**

---

The board at the end of that counter read nineteen entries and a blank line under the nineteenth.

**It has been blank since Wednesday the twenty-sixth of November 2014, which is a hundred and seventy days today, and it is still the only fixed point in that building, and entry nineteen is a yard light, and the maintenance is a bulb, and the bulb is out.** It had not moved once in the two days since he had stood at the other end of that counter and said out loud, in daylight, to a room, what the borough has. It was not going to move in a heat, and nobody is obliged to write on it, and a heat wave is not a reason to write a form.

There was a domestic fan on a chair by the counter, plugged into a socket behind the trolley, running on a little orange box with a clock on it that had been bought in a shop on Friday morning by somebody who was not going to be found, and it moved the air about the room and did nothing at all to the temperature of the room.

The woman of about fifty came down at about ten with a tray and put the tray on the counter, and neither of them said anything to each other.

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT A HEAT WAVE IS, IN THE ONLY TERMS THIS BOROUGH HAS FOR ANYTHING, AND IT IS A FINDING AND IT IS HIS, AND IT TOOK HIM UNTIL ABOUT HALF PAST ELEVEN.**

**Every instrument in this place is an instrument for finding out who is owed something.**

That is what a counter is. That is what a register is. That is what a minute is, and a return is, and a form is, and a shelf of dated pieces of paper under a counter is. Nine months of his life and four months of everybody else's has gone into building them and keeping them, and every one of them points in one direction: at a thing somebody is owed.

**A heat wave does not point in that direction. A heat wave does not distinguish. Everybody in a hot week is owed the same thing and almost nobody in this borough is owed anything at all, and so every instrument in it is pointed the wrong way, and it will keep working perfectly, and it will produce nothing.**

---

He stood at the east end of the counter with his back to the wall and he watched it work all morning, and it worked.

A man of about sixty came in at about half past ten and asked what was true of a standpipe on a post at the bottom of a car park, and he was given a state and a date, and the state was that on Friday morning there was no water coming out of that post, and the date was Friday the fifteenth of May, and the man wrote nothing down and put the paper in his pocket and went out, and Jonas Mercer watched him go and did not ask him a single thing.

**Nobody was helped. The man wanted water and he was given a date. That is what this room is and it is not going to be anything else while there is a person standing at it.**

And then, at about ten to twelve, a woman of about forty came in with a bag of shopping and asked the counter whether the borough had fans.

The woman at the counter did not stop what she was doing.

"**I could not tell you. I do not know what we have and I am not required to know, and there is a depot over the road with a man in it and he is not mine and I am not allowed to send anybody to him and I am certainly not going to go myself.**"

The woman said, "**I am not asking you to send anybody. I am asking whether you have any.**"

"**I could not tell you that either. And here is the rest of it, because you have come to a counter and a counter answers, and then you can do what you like with it. If I found out for you — and I am not going to, and you cannot make me — then what I would have is a piece of paper with the day on it saying where a fan was on the fifteenth of May. And if I did that for nine people this week, then by the middle of June I would have a list. And a list of who has got a fan is a list of who has not got one, and a list of who has not got one is a list of people you can ring, and I am not building one, and there is no list of it in this borough, and that is not an oversight.**"

The woman stood there with her bag.

"**That is all right,**" she said. "**I did not think it was.**"

"**No. And I am not telling you off. I am telling you about the counter.**"

---

**And that was the first of the two refusals, and it is correct, and it is the same rule she has been applying since the beginning of December, and she is not going to be thanked for it by anybody, including him, including today.**

The second one came at about twenty past twelve and it was about a person, and she refused that in the right order, in about four seconds, and then said the half that took the rest of it.

A woman of about sixty with a shopping bag asked her, in six words, whether her landlord was on a list, and she said no, and then said the other half of it, which is that she is right and it does not help her, and that there is no list of landlords in this borough and no form on any counter in this building that would take the question, and that if there were one then in a few years a man could be looked up by what he charges and by what he is owed, and those are two different things, and a form only has room for one of them.

"**Is he on a list. That is all I asked.**"

"**You asked whether he is on a list. There is no list. That is the whole of the answer and it is the correct one.**"

She said, "**Right,**" and went out, and she went back to her tray.

**Nobody in this borough may let that counter answer anything about a gate, or a rota, or a compliance paragraph, or a person, and it did not, and its refusals were right, and a counter in a heat is under more pressure than a counter in April, and the pressure was not a reason for any of it.**

---

At about half past one in the afternoon he went up the stairs to the second floor.

**He was not on a rota. He was not on anything. Nobody told him to go up and nobody knew that he had, and there is no instrument in this building that could record that he had.**

The second floor was worse than the ground floor. It has one window at the end of it and the sun comes in off the yard at about half past one and sits on the carpet, and there are four desks and three of them have somebody at them, and all three of them were working.

The woman of about fifty was up there with a jug, because the jug lives in the kitchen on that floor and the kitchen is at the far end and the corridor is the hot part.

She was looking for a fan.

**The building's fan is not a thing anybody is required to keep track of. It lives on a shelf on that floor. It has lived on that shelf for years. It is a grey box with a cable and it moves about four inches of air in a room this size and about that is the whole of its character.**

It was not on the shelf.

---

**AND HERE IS THE THING THAT HAPPENED ON THAT SECOND FLOOR, AND IT IS WHY ANYTHING ELSE IN THIS BOROUGH IS ABOUT TO BE ABOUT A YARD BEHIND A MARKET, AND THE PERSON IT HAPPENED TO WAS NOT LOOKING FOR IT.**

There were three trays on the second floor on a shelf under the window. The nearest one had post in it and a bundle sitting on top of the post. The middle one had a bundle in it and nothing on top, and the paper in it had gone soft where the sun coming in off that yard had got at it. The third one had a bundle in it, a pen across it, and nothing on top of that either, and she did not touch it.

She picked the middle tray up to put it on the top shelf, out of the sun, because the paper had gone soft and she wanted it off the sill.

**The file came with it. It came up out of the tray flat against the bottom and it was the length of the tray and it had a card cover on it and the cover was a buff card with a printed box in the middle of it and a reference in the box and a date under the box.**

The date was the fourteenth of October 2011.

And the box said, in the correct typeface, and she read it standing up in a hot corridor holding a tray in one hand:

> **REPORT OF A RELEASE — MARKET YARD, BAY 2 — 14 OCTOBER 2011**

And under it, in a smaller line, the word **CLOSED** had been stamped on it and the stamp was not very recent.

**A FILE THAT WAS CARRIED AND NOT DEALT WITH. It came out of a room over at the depot when the depot was given up in the spring and it was put in a tray and the tray was put on a shelf on a second floor in a building it had nothing to do with, and nobody has ever asked for it, and there is no list of what is in the tray, and a shelf is not an index, and the shelf is in a corridor that four people walk past.**

She put the tray back on the shelf.

**She put the post back on top of the bundle, which is not a thing anybody asked her to do and is not a thing anybody has noticed her do, and the bundle went on top of the file, and the file went into the tray again, and the tray went back on the shelf in the same place it had been in, and she did not open the file and she is not going to open it in there, and she is not going to tell anybody that it is there.**

**She has never been going to lift the post off that one. She looked at it while she was doing the other tray, which is a thing she is not going to think about, and the reason she is not going to think about it is that there is nothing to think about: it is a letter under two pieces of post, and a piece of paper under two other pieces of paper is a piece of paper that has stopped being dealt with, and that is the oldest way in the world of saying so, and anybody who works in an office knows it, and nobody in this borough has to be told it, and there is no instrument anywhere that could go and find a thing like that on a tray and ask what it is.**

---

**AND HE WAS ON THE GROUND FLOOR AT THE TIME AND DID NOT KNOW ANY OF IT, AND NOBODY TOLD HIM, AND HE IS NOT GOING TO BE TOLD.**

He does not know there is a file on that shelf. Nobody will tell him. There is no reason for anybody to walk up a staircase and tell a man who is not on anything that there is a file.

**And that is the arrangement, and the arrangement has been in place for about four days, and it is the same arrangement as the drawer, and it is the same arrangement as the card in the slot, and it is the reason the whole of this borough works and it is the reason nothing in it can be checked.**

---

At about four o'clock he went out to Tideglass on foot.

**The standpipe is a green post with a tap on it at the bottom of a car park and it has been there since before any of this and it is on the lowland's own sheet of the borough and it is one of six and it is dry.**

A woman of about sixty was standing at it with two empty jerry cans and a carrier bag. She had been there since about half past two. She told him, without being asked, that the man who used to come on the Tuesdays had not come, and that there was another one now, on a different street, and that the other one was four pounds a bucket and he took the money in a slot with forty on the board, and that he was the same as the first one in the sense that he did not ask who you were.

He did not ask her anything.

**A street of ninety-one houses on the lowland had no water on it on the fifteenth of May. Nobody in this borough knows how many people sleep on that street and the number is not the number of houses, and the difference between the two numbers is the whole of what has happened on that street since the winter, and there is no instrument in this city that can put the two numbers in the same sentence, and nobody may be required to be told anything, including the size of the difference.**

---

**And here is what Friday the fifteenth of May was, and it is a list of a day and not a standing, and it is not going to be written down anywhere.**

Water out on a street of ninety-one houses on the lowland, and a standpipe at the bottom of a car park dry since Thursday, and a laundry that cannot cool, and eighty-one beds on a board in a corridor with no mechanical extract in it, and a fan on a chair in a vestibule that moves the air about a room and does nothing to the temperature of the room, and a school that will close at one o'clock in four days, and about eleven people a day through a door in the heat.

A register with nineteen entries and a blank line under the nineteenth, a hundred and seventy days, and a bulb out on the nineteenth.

A card in a slot, eighty-four days. A letter in a tray upstairs, eighty-six days, under two other pieces of post. A drawer with no key on it and no list of what is in it, and there is more in it than there was in February, and nobody can say how many, including the woman who keeps it and including him.

**And a brown file on a shelf under a window, with a date on the cover of the fourteenth of October 2011 and the word CLOSED on it, and a bay number, and nobody in that building knows it is there.**

Nobody was helped. Nobody was harmed. Nobody died. The register did not move. And a heat wave is not a reason to write a form, and there is not going to be one, and a form would not have helped anybody on that street today, and the only thing that would have helped them today is water, and there is no instrument in this borough that produces water.
