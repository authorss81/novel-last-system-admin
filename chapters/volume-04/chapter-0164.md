# Chapter 0164 — Nothing Else Has

Friday the twenty-sixth of June 2015, from about half past two in the afternoon, a first-floor room over a shopfront in Tideglass.

**The standing of the city, said in a room in daylight, out loud, without answering anything and without improving anything. Three things in it are new, two of them a month old and one of them the smallest thing anybody in this borough has produced in nine months, and none of the three is a document. He asked no question, he chaired nothing, there is no chair of that body and there never was, the board has not met since the middle of January, the ninth seat has not been told anything, the lowland cannot be seated, and a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and it is the end of June and it is still true.**

---

The room was lent by a woman who is a shopkeeper, and she is not on anything and nobody made a speech about that. Nobody was in charge. There was no agenda and nobody offered to take minutes and nobody who was there wanted to.

**Ronnie Peart, 52**, who went round nine doorways. A woman of about thirty-one with a barrow. A woman of about seventy who came on a bus. A woman of twenty-nine with a bin rota in her coat. A man of thirty-eight from the water office. A man of thirty-two at the back of the room with nothing in his hands who is not on the agenda of anything.

The heat broke on the Sunday. It has been twenty-two degrees since the Monday.

---

**WHAT THE BOROUGH HAS, AND IT IS NOT A LIST, AND IT MAY NOT BE MINUTED, INDEXED, NUMBERED OR PUT ON A WALL, AND IT NAMES NO PERSON. NOTHING IN IT WAS ARRANGED AND NOTHING IN IT IS GOING TO BE.**

**And it is the end of June, and the heat broke on the Sunday, and the two of those together are one less thing, and that is the whole of the difference between this and the standing of the city as it stood six weeks ago.**

**THE THINGS THAT ARE NEW IN IT, AND THERE ARE THREE, AND TWO OF THEM ARE A MONTH OLD AND ONE OF THEM IS A WEEK OLD.**

**A row.** One page, printed, a heading, a date, four streets down the left in a column headed *location*, nine lines beside them, and every one of the nine lines the same. No name box anywhere on it and no column for one. It went round a building folded in four, in a tray with nine other things in it, on the Friday, and it is in nine other buildings now and nobody in any of them knows where it came from, and it is correct, and nobody had to do anything wrong to make it.

**A page in a tray in a room in Tideglass** with the twenty-ninth of May on it and an empty signature block at the bottom. Not in force. Not gone round anybody. Not in a minute and not going in one and nobody going to be asked to sign it. It has now been read by one person who was not asked to read it, and that person said so out loud, in a room, in daylight, on the Wednesday, and telephoned the building on the Thursday morning to say it a second time, and the office wrote nothing down.

**A chair.** There is a chair on a landing on a block in Tideglass that belongs to somebody in that block and is not lost, and somebody in that block puts it out on the landing at about eight in the evening, in the shade, for anybody who wants to sit down, and brings it in again at about six.

It has been doing that since about the middle of June. **It is the smallest thing anybody in this borough has produced since the fourth of September and it is the only one of these three that is not a piece of paper.**

**It is not a rota and it is not a shift and it is not a rota that has not been written down. It is not a form and it is not on a form. Nobody is required to move it and nobody is going to be told who moves it, and no instrument in this city could go and find out, and there is a woman of twenty-nine in that room who would be the right person to design the thing that found out and she has not designed it.**

Nobody in that room knew it happened. It was not mentioned. If it had been mentioned it would have been a rota inside a fortnight, and a rota inside a fortnight would have had a name in it, and the chair would have stopped going out on the landing at about eight, and everybody in that block would have said that was the council's business and the council's business had killed it.

**And the rest of it, which was all of it in April, and is all of it now, in the order of a room and not the order of a filing system.**

A counter in a vestibule eleven feet by nine that answers in about eleven seconds, and is not a service, and cannot be handed to anybody, and does not grow, and works because one person is standing at it who cannot be found from any record in this city. About eleven people a day. In the heat the same eleven came; in a cool week a different eleven came; and there is no way of telling which is which and nobody is required to try.

**A register with nineteen entries and a blank line under the nineteenth, two hundred and twelve days, and the bulb on the nineteenth out. A card in a slot in that building, a hundred and twenty-six days, that nobody empties. A letter in a tray on the floor above, a hundred and twenty-eight days, with two other pieces of post lying on it, not opened in front of a second person, and what is under the post is not known to anybody and may not be said in a room. A drawer under that counter with no key on it and no list of what is in it, with a tray sitting on top of it that is not a list, and one hand putting things in it, and nobody in this borough can say how many, and the only figure that has ever been written down about it is nine and that was in March and it is a figure about the past.**

Two conditions on the same wall, sixteen inches apart, that do not agree with each other and have never been made to agree. Nothing in this borough defines *holder*. Two letters and one word in pencil on the back of an enamel strip have not been read by anybody and are not going to be.

A public report with a paragraph number on it, standing in front of four hundred and twenty women, never answered, never numbered and never stated in a room; and a machine on level zero still running; and its own strip going into a drawer in that room dozens of times a day by a hand that has not been found and was not looked for and is not going to be.

Seven of nine, two chairs empty, no chair of that body at all, and it has not met since the middle of January and is not obliged to. The ninth seat is fifty-four, unpaid, findable at a shopfront and at an address, doing it since the fourth of July, and she has not been told anything and nobody is going to tell her. **The lowland is not on the layer and cannot be seated, and the reason is still that a node with no party is not a party and a place is not a person, and neither the hot fortnight nor the cool weekend fixed it.**

Six water points at Tier 1, £11,280 a year, sitting in paragraph 6.1 of a minute as received and not as evidence, and nobody has been asked to pay any part of it, and the line at the end of a lane of terraced houses and garages with no houses on it is the one nobody has ever asked for either, and the man who fills it has been filling it out of his own pocket for six years and nobody in this borough knows his name.

A sentence about what a service floor is for, in the fourth item of a sheet that requires nothing, said once, in daylight, on the twenty-seventh of April, and not repeated, not improved, not restated, and not on a wall. **Nobody in this borough quoted it in June and somebody is going to want to.** A name in about eleven people and in no document, and a hole in a record, and the hole is still the only honest thing anybody in this borough has produced about a person in nine months.

A gate that goes up at seven on a Sunday morning and stops at six on the Sunday morning after that, six days long and not a week, with no date on it anywhere in this city, read aloud by two tired men who are not on anything.

A bay behind a market with a wheel stop four feet in front of a grate and no catch tray, and a report about that bay on a shelf in a building it has nothing to do with, and nobody has opened it, and a man of about thirty-six picked it up while he was counting that shelf, read the cover, and put it back exactly as he found it, on the Tuesday, and told nobody, and will not remember doing it, and nobody is ever going to ask him.

**AND THE PEOPLE, WHO ARE NOT IN THAT AND CANNOT BE IN IT.**

A man of forty-four who cannot be asked one question about any of it, ever. A man of forty-nine who could be asked one question tomorrow morning and has not been. A man of forty-eight whom nobody has asked. A woman of fifty-four at a shopfront. A man of about forty-five with three pieces of paper in a cardboard wallet that nobody owns. A man of thirty-three who read a sheet in a corridor in May and asked a question it was never written to answer and came back on his own in June and got a correct answer that was worth nothing, and is not going to be asked anything again.

**And a man of sixty at number thirty-one at the end of a lane with a butt under his own downpipe that did not fill on the Thursday, and nobody is going to tell him why, and there is nowhere in this borough that anybody could put his name next to that fact.**

---

**And a sentence that has been true in this borough since the second of June and has not been refuted, and was not refuted by four thousand litres in a school tank, and was not refuted by a blank line standing at the longest it has ever been, and was not refuted by a page, and was not refuted by a row, and was not refuted by a cool Tuesday: a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped.**

**A man of thirty-two is not on anything and is four hundred yards away on purpose, and he has stopped counting, and the count stops at eight.**

---

**And then the last of it, which is a question, and it is not a resolution, and it is not the question this borough asked itself in that car park in June, and it is not about a person.**

There is a page in a tray in a room in this borough that asks for one thing to be done for the whole city instead of for a street, and there is a chair on a landing in this borough that goes out at about eight in the evening and comes in at about six, and the chair is the smaller of the two, and neither of them has a person in it.

Neither of them can be asked about. One of them has a space at the bottom of it for a name, and the other one has nothing, and a thing with a space for a name can be refused, and a thing with nothing on it cannot be refused and cannot be required either, and this borough has spent nine months getting that exactly right and has not noticed that it has got two of them.

**And the heat broke on the Sunday, and every single thing that happened in June is still there in July, and nobody has to do anything about any of it.**

**So which of the two is going to be the first one that somebody in this borough asks a person to agree to.**
