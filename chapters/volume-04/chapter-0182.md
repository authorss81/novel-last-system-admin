# Chapter 0182 — She Would Do It In August

Tuesday the twenty-eighth of July 2015, from about half past two in the afternoon, a street of houses on the near side of the market; and the same street at about ten past three.

**One of the eleven standing refusals nearly happens on a doorstep in the afternoon, or does not, and it is not a twelfth, and the count is eleven and not twelve. He was walking past and was not watching and is not him and was not the person at the door. Nothing is argued out and nothing is softened and nothing is tidied and nothing is revisited, and nobody refuses to be thanked.**

---

**THE ONLY LATTICE NOTICE ON THAT STREET, printed and screwed to a gatepost at about the height of a person of average height, and it defines nothing and nothing on it defines *holder*, and it requires nothing of anybody:**

> **A REQUEST MADE AT A DOOR IS NOT A RETURN.**
> **NO PERSON IS REQUIRED TO ANSWER FOR A REQUEST.**
> **NO PERSON IS REQUIRED TO SAY WHY THEY HAVE NOT ANSWERED.**

---

**THE MAN IS ABOUT TWENTY-ONE. HE HAS A CLIPBOARD AND A PLASTIC WALLET AND A BUILDING BEHIND HIM, AND HE HAS BEEN ROUND THIS STREET ONCE BEFORE IN JUNE.**

He is not asking anybody to fill anything in. **There is no form.** That is deliberate and it is clever and it is the cheapest instrument anybody in this borough has ever put in front of a household, and it works because it cannot be refused properly without a piece of paper to refuse on.

What he needs is for a person to say a sentence out loud, in front of him, so that he can write on a line on a sheet which reads *said by resident*. The sentence is *I will be in touch in the second week of August.* That is all. He is not asking anybody to agree to anything. **He is asking somebody to be audible.**

---

He came to that door at about twenty past two and he did it properly, and everything about the way he did it was correct.

"**Good afternoon. I am not selling you anything and there is no form. I have got a sheet here and I have got about forty doors on this street and I am asking everybody the same thing and I will say it to you once, and you can say no, and if you say no I will write *declined* and I will not come back.**"

"**Right.**"

"**The sentence is this. In August somebody from that building is going to come round about the water, and they will want to know whether the pressure is all right at the top of the house and whether anybody has had anything strange. And what I am after is not an answer now. What I am after is for you to say out loud now that you will speak to them in August, and that is all, and I write it on a line and I go, and then in August somebody comes and you say whatever you like to them including nothing.**"

"**And if I say it now, what happens.**"

"**Nothing happens. It is a sentence on a sheet.**"

---

**AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-NINE SAID NOTHING AT ALL FOR ABOUT TWELVE SECONDS, WHICH IS A LONG TIME ON A DOORSTEP, AND THEN SHE SAID ONE SENTENCE.**

"**You'd want me to say it now.**"

"**I would want you to say it now, yes.**"

"**Why now.**"

"**Because I am here now.**"

"**You are here now,**" she said. "**And in August there will be a person at this door, and that person will have come here because of a line of writing, and they will not be asking me a small thing. They will be asking me whether the water is all right, and I will say it is, and then they will ask me a second thing, and the second thing is the one I am not expecting, and by then I will be a person who said she would be in touch.**"

---

**AND SHE SAID THE SECOND HALF OF IT, AND SHE SAID IT PLEASANTLY, AND IT IS THE CORRECT THING TO HAVE SAID.**

"**Here is what I will do. In August, if somebody comes, I will open the door and I will answer whatever they ask me, and if they ask me my name I will give it to them, and I will give it to them the day they ask, in my own front door, to their face. And not in July to a lad with a sheet.**"

"**That is fair.**"

"**It is not fair or unfair. It is that I am not going to be on a piece of paper in July for a thing that is happening in August, because then the paper will be right and it will be in a building and there will be a person in the building who is right about me, and that is how it starts.**"

---

**AND HE SAID *RIGHT* AND WENT ON DOWN THE STREET, AND HE DID NOT WRITE ANYTHING ON THE SHEET FOR THAT DOOR, AND HE DID NOT WRITE *DECLINED*, AND HE DID NOT WRITE ANYTHING AT ALL.**

He did not knock again. He did not call back through the door. He did not write a note and leave it. He put the sheet under the board, said good afternoon to the next house, and did the sentence out loud on that doorstep as well, in the same words, because he was doing his job.

**She shut the door. It was not a slam. It was a woman of forty-nine closing a door on a Tuesday afternoon, about four minutes after she had opened it, having said one true thing and been listened to.**

---

**AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF IT, AND THE COUNT OF STANDING REFUSALS IN THIS BOROUGH IS ELEVEN AND NOT TWELVE, AND THIS WAS NOT A TWELFTH, AND HERE IS WHY, AND THE REASON IS THE SAME REASON EVERY TIME.**

A request refused twice is one request and not two. She was not asked twice. **Nothing was put to her in a form and there was no form and she was not asked to sign anything and she did not refuse anything that had been required of her, because nothing in this borough can require anything of anybody.**

**And nobody wrote it down, and nobody is going to appeal it, and those two things are the only reason it works.**

Not discretion. Not kindness. Not anybody's care for her.

**It is that it is not anybody's duty to record a refusal, and it is not anybody's duty to appeal one, and take either of those away and the whole thing stops.** If a refusal has to be written down, then it is a document, and a document is a thing a building keeps, and then the person who refused is a person somebody can ask what she refused and why and when. If a refusal can be appealed, then the person who refuses has to be in a position, and a position is a place you can be found.

Neither of those is true here. She will never know that her sentence was the correct one. He will never know whether she refused him or negotiated with him, and he will go back to that building on Friday with thirty-nine lines and a blank where the fortieth should be, and there is no form in this borough on which anybody could record that a woman of forty-nine said *not in July* on a doorstep, and there is not going to be one.

---

**AND A MAN OF THIRTY-TWO WALKED UP THAT STREET AT ABOUT TEN PAST THREE AND HE WAS NOT WATCHING AND HE WAS NOT HIM.**

He was going to a shop. There is a queue at that shop at about ten past three on a Tuesday in July and he was in it for about six minutes, and he came out of it, and a young man of about twenty-one was going up the pavement on the other side with a clipboard under his arm doing his sentence on door after door, and he was on the fortieth.

**He did not know which door was the one. There are about sixty doors on that street and they look the same and he had been behind a man in a queue, and he would not have turned round for a young man with a clipboard on a Tuesday.**

He asked nobody anything. Nobody asked him anything. He is not the person at the door, he is not the person with the wallet, and he is not going to be told any of it, and there is no version of the next fortnight in which he finds out that a woman of forty-nine got a young man to leave a line blank by being correct at him on her own doorstep in about four minutes.

**He may not chair anything and there is no chair of that body and there never was, and he was on a pavement with a bag of shopping and a line of four people behind him, and none of it touched him.**

---

The young man of about twenty-one said his sentence on thirty-nine doorways that afternoon. He will be on that street again in the second week of August and he will say it on forty, and the fortieth door will open or it will not, and the line on his sheet for that door will be filled in or it will not, and he has a building behind him that will ask him for the sheet on the Friday.

**He does not know that the reason he has thirty-nine lines instead of forty is a woman who was kind to him, and he is not going to find out, and neither is anybody else.**

Nobody in that street was thanked and nobody refused to be thanked. The register of narrowings is at nineteen and the blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and forty-four days old and a Tuesday is not a reason to write on it and nobody is obliged. The card in the slot is a hundred and fifty-eight days old. The letter in the tray is a hundred and sixty days old, it has two pieces of post lying on it, and it has not been opened in front of a second person and is not going to be.

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and eleven refusals stand and the count did not move this afternoon, and a woman of forty-nine said the correct thing to a stranger on her own doorstep and it cost her nothing and it cost him a line, and neither of them will ever know which of the two things happened.**
