# Chapter 0159 — A Row

Friday the nineteenth of June 2015, from about ten in the morning, a meeting room on the ground floor of the water office; and about one o'clock in the afternoon, a corridor in the same building.

**On the twelfth of June a man of fifty-two went round nine doorways and got the same answer at every one of them, and on the nineteenth a sheet with those nine answers on it went into a building. It has a heading and a date on it and there is no box anywhere on it with a name in it, and it is correct, and nobody in that room can see what it has become, and the one person in that room who can see it is the one person there who is not responsible for it. Nobody thanked anybody and nothing was helped, and this is the first time in nine months that a correct thing in this borough has been produced by a machine rather than by a person.**

---

**THE SHEET IS ONE PAGE AND IT IS PRINTED AND IT HAS FOUR STREETS DOWN THE LEFT-HAND SIDE IN A COLUMN HEADED *LOCATION*, AND NINE LINES BESIDE THEM, AND EVERY ONE OF THE NINE LINES SAYS THE SAME THREE WORDS.**

> *No requirement stated.*

Nine of them. Two of those four streets have two households on them, one has three and one has two, which is where nine comes from, and the person who typed it did not collapse them into four and was right not to.
There is no name box. There is no column for a name, and there is no column for a day-time number, and there is no space for either, and the reason there is no space for either is that the man who filled the field sheet in a car park on the twelfth of June said out loud, in front of about nine people, that he was not putting a name on it, and the reason he said it is in that car park and is not written down anywhere.

**There is a heading at the top. There is a date, which is the nineteenth of June 2015. There is nothing on that page that asks anybody to do anything.**

---

**AND THE ROOM, AND WHO IS RESPONSIBLE FOR IT, AND THE ONE PERSON IN IT WHO IS NOT.**

**Callum Nkemdirim, 38**, water office, planning. He filled the field sheet in with a pen he had not uncapped and he has had it printed because a result you have got once is an anecdote.

**Denise Hallam, 44**, who manages the contract the standpipes sit inside, and who is the person the sheet is going to go out on, and who could not stop it and does not want to.

A man of about thirty with a laptop, who is not a member of anything, and who did not speak.

And a man of thirty-two at a chair on the end of the table with nothing in his hands, who is not on the agenda of anything, who was not invited and was not turned away, and about whom nobody in that room thought at all, which is the whole of what his standing has ever been in this city and he has stopped being able to improve on it.

---

She said it without being asked, which is what she does, and she was right.

"**It is going on a Friday because a sheet has to go round. If it sat in a tray it would be a thing somebody remembered, and a thing somebody remembered is a feeling, and in August when this comes round again somebody is going to open a folder and find a page that says what this borough thought on the nineteenth of June.**" She turned the sheet round on the table so it was the right way up for him, which nobody had asked her to do. "**And I want to say what it is not, because I have had this conversation three times this month with people who have got it wrong. It is not a demand. There is nothing in it. Nobody is being asked for anything. It says that on four streets in the eastern part of the borough, in the week of the eighth of June, nine households were asked what they needed and no requirement was stated, and that is the position, and a position has to have a date on it or it is an opinion.**"

"**And it is correct.**"

"**It is exactly correct. That is the problem. Nothing in this borough is more reliable than a thing that is correct and that nobody can dispute, and I could not write a word on that page that would be more accurate than what is on it.**"

---

**AND HERE IS WHAT HE SAW, AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME IN NINE MONTHS THAT HE HAS SEEN SOMETHING HE HAS SPENT THE WHOLE NINE MONTHS TRYING TO BUILD, COME OUT OF SOMEBODY ELSE'S MACHINE.**

The page was not a count.

He had spent nine months on counts. A number on a board, a number on a wall, a number of beds, a number of houses, the number of people in a street and the number of houses in a street, and the distance between those two numbers, which is a human being, and a human being does not go in a box.

**This was not a count. A count says how many there are. That page said what this borough is going to do, and what it said it was going to do was nothing, and it said it about nine households, on four named streets, in a fortnight in June, in the worst heat in nineteen years.**

That is a level of service. It is the only kind anybody has ever written in this city that nobody has had to argue for and nobody can get out of.

Because here is what it now is, and it is not what anybody in that room thinks it is, and every one of them is a competent person who has been doing this for years:

**In about four years there will be a sheet in a building in this city that says that in the second week of June, on four streets, nine households were asked what they needed, and that the answer was that they needed nothing. And that will be true. It will be true in the way a printed thing is true, which is the way that survives.**

**And there is no way in this borough to say to that sheet that the nine were not asked, because they were asked. They were asked on three evenings by a man with a dog and no standing, at nine kitchen doors, and they gave the correct answer, and the answer is on the page, and the page is right.**

---

**AND THE PART HE COULD NOT PUT DOWN, AND THE REASON IS NOT A BETTER REASON THAN ANY OTHER, IS THIS: NOBODY HAD DONE ANYTHING WRONG.**

The man who went round was right and he was not thanked. The woman who did not give him her nine lines was right. The man with the pen filled in what he was given and left the names out and said out loud why. The woman who owns the contract is right that a sheet has to go round. The man with the laptop typed nine lines and did not collapse them into four.

**And in nine months this borough has produced a small number of correct things that are worth anything, and every one of them was made by a person, by hand, on their own time, in about a minute, and none of them had anything written on it. The last four stops of a laundry round, moved forward, at somebody else's expense. Four thousand litres into a tank that had been empty since the Thursday, in about twenty minutes, on a Friday afternoon. Every one of those cost a person something to do and none of them cost this borough anything at all.**

**This cost nothing either. And it is the first one in nine months that was made without a single person having to be brave.**

He sat at the end of that table and looked at nine identical correct lines and worked out that the thing he has been building, for nine months, by hand, one refusal at a time, is cheaper than a form, and it is worse, and it is going to win, because it does not need anybody to be brave and it does not need anybody to be owed anything.

**There is no form in this borough on which a person could write that they are not a series. And that absence is the only reason that page was allowed to exist, and the absence is why the page is the right shape, and the right shape is the dangerous one.**

---

Nobody thanked anybody. Nobody refused anything. Nobody was harmed.

**Nine people said nothing to nine doors and were right, and not one of them has been helped, and not one of them is on anything, and nobody is going to be able to find out in August that there was a car park on the twelfth of June, and there is no form on which anybody could write that a man of fifty-two went round nine houses for three evenings, and the envelope is in a drawer in a kitchen in number fifty-eight and nobody has asked him for it and nobody is going to.**

The register of narrowings is at nineteen entries. The blank line under the nineteenth is two hundred and five days old this morning and it did not move, and a heat wave is not a reason to write on a blank line and nobody is obliged to.

**And the sheet went out at about half past eleven, folded in four, in a tray with nine other things in it, and it will be read that afternoon by about a dozen people, and not one of them will know where it came from, and the man with the laptop will not read it, and the woman who owns the contract will not read it, and every single person who reads it will read it as an absence of a problem, because that is what it is, because that is exactly what it is.**

**And a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and nine of them said so themselves, and a page has been printed that says it, and nobody has been helped by it, and it is going to be a true sentence about this borough for a very long time.**
