# Chapter 0139 — A Saturday Night, The Fourteenth

Friday the twenty-fourth of April 2015, from about ten past ten in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward. And about two in the afternoon, a shop at the end of a street. And Sunday the twenty-sixth, from about twenty past six in the morning, four hundred yards from a building.

**There was no question of his in the vestibule on the Friday morning, and there was no question of his in the shop, and there was no question of his on the Sunday morning, and there has not been one since the twenty-fifth of February, and the counting has been right in every week of this year. There was one question of a stranger's at that counter on the Friday morning, and it was not about anything, and the counter did not answer it, and the counter was right, and he was four feet away for all of it.**

---

He had come in on the Friday morning to look at a board and a blank line, and the blank line under the nineteenth had been blank for a hundred and forty-nine days, which was the longest it had ever been still since the fourth of August, and he had not expected that to still be true and it was, and he stood and looked at it until somebody came through the door behind him, and he thought about a bulb.

At about twenty past ten a woman of about thirty came in behind him.

She was not in a hurry. She was the sort of person who has come in to ask a thing and has already decided she will not like the answer, which is a different thing from being in a hurry and takes longer.

"**Is the man who stands at the wall on a list?**"

Jonas Mercer was four feet away with his back to the wall and did not move and did not say anything.

Nina Calder did not look up from the tray.

"**I am not going to answer that.**"

"**I am not asking who he is. I am asking whether there is a piece of paper anywhere with his name on it, because a man at work told me a person comes in there and stands at the wall and does not ask for anything, and I said that is not a person you send anybody to, and he said everybody is on a list somewhere.**"

"**He is right and it does not help you, and I am not going to say which piece of paper he means, and there is no form on any counter in this building that would take your question, and if there were one then in about four years there would be a list of people who stand at walls, and I could not hand it to you and neither could anybody else, and I would rather you went away knowing that than knowing it was not there.**"

"**That is not an answer.**"

"**It is the answer. It is about four seconds long and you did not like it and I did not enjoy it.**"

The woman stood there about four seconds longer and then said, "**That's a shame,**" and went out into the street, and she did not come back, and nobody in that room told her anything, and Jonas Mercer stood against that wall afterwards and said nothing at all, and there was no point at which he could have said anything, and there is no point at which anybody could.

---

**And he had come in on the Friday morning to look at a blank line, and he had been stood at by a stranger asking a records counter whether a man at a wall is written down somewhere, and the counter was correct, and the answer cost about four seconds, and that is the first half of what he said out loud in that room on the thirteenth of March coming back at him in his own person, and he cannot take it back and has not tried to improve it.**

**Including him. That is the first time it has come back at him. He has been standing at that wall since the twenty-eighth of January and a man in a canteen in March and a woman of thirty in April have both heard about it, and neither of them got it from him, and one of them got it from a man who does not talk about his Fridays.**

And the answer that came back was the correct one, and it cost her four seconds, and it was correct because a counter that answers about a person is a service, and a service is a rota with one name in it, and hers is the name it would have.

**So the price of that floor is being paid in a currency where the only thing anybody can do about it is refuse, and refusing does not make the person safer, it makes them unattacked.** He has been in a corridor at half past four in the morning and four hundred yards away at ten o'clock at night for four months on the strength of a difference between those two things, and it is the only instrument in this borough that works, and it does not protect anybody. It only means nobody is aiming.

---

At about two o'clock on the Friday afternoon he went into the shop at the end of the street with the pad in his hand, and he did not say the thing.

He had said it once, on the thirteenth of April, and it had taken him about nine minutes, and she had answered it with the truth, which was that nobody survives standing in a corridor and there is no version of it in which a man in his thirties has got a position at a bus turning head and a Sunday morning in it.

**And on the twenty-fourth of April he stood at the end of that counter with the same thing in his mouth and he did not say it, and that is the first time in nine months that he has come into that shop with a sentence in him and gone out without it, and the reason is not that he has changed his mind.**

**The reason is that she told him in December that a thing like that has to be said to somebody four times a day for a week before it holds, and he has said it to her once, and a thing that has been said once has not been tested, and a thing that has not been tested is not a position. It is a preference. And he has got a preference and it is the only thing in nine months that he has built that nobody else can be asked about, and it is going to have to be either said four times a day for a week or accepted as a preference, and he is not going to say it four times a day for a week to a woman who oils a bench and does not read his pad.**

He is not going to ask her again. He is not going to anybody else with it. **Nobody is going to be told.**

Beatrice Osunde put the ash down and looked at him for about four seconds and then went back to the bench, and neither of them said one word about it, and afterwards she said this:

"**The oil came in on Tuesday and the tin is by the door and I would like it moved before the weekend. And that is not the same conversation and I am telling you that it is not the same conversation so that you know I know it is not the same conversation.**"

"**I know it is not.**"

"**Good. Then move the tin.**"

---

**And the reason he has not gone, which he worked out on the Friday morning at the wall and wrote down that night, is not the reason he had on the thirteenth of April, and the change is the whole of it.**

On the thirteenth of April the reason was about them. *I must not become a rota with one name in it in a building full of tired people, because then I am a duty, and a duty is a thing that gets put in a column.* That was a reason about other people and it was good and it held.

**On the twenty-fourth of April the reason is about him, and it is this: he has been doing it for nothing for four months, and the entire product is that two tired men can say that he was there.**

**That is all. Two tired men, one Saturday, in a waiting area with four chairs in it, at about half past six in the morning, on their own time, reading a term that is a promise, and afterwards one of them could say, to nobody, to a colleague, to a wife over a cup of tea in about 2019, *he was there that night*.**

And he has worked out that he would rather be that than a name on a list.

**And that is not a principle. It is a price. And there is a difference and he knows exactly what the difference is, because he has been in a room in daylight on the twenty-eighth of January and has said out loud what a thing like that is worth and it is nothing that anybody can be held to.**

He wrote it and it is four lines.

> *Twenty-fourth of April.*
> *For four months the reason I stay away was that I must not become a duty in that building. That was a good reason and it was about other people.*
> *The reason now is that I do not want to be written down, and the thing I get instead is that two tired men could say that I was there, and that is worth nothing to this borough and it is the only thing I get.*
> *So I have stopped having a reason and started having a price, and a price is a thing you pay and a reason is a thing you hold, and I have had this one since the thirteenth of April and it has not been tested and it is going to be mine to carry and I am not going to hand it to anybody, and that is the fourth thing in nine months I have not handed to anybody, and it is the first one I wanted to.*

---

**And on the Saturday night there was a handover at ten o'clock at a building four hundred yards from a piece of wet pavement, and there is no document about it and there is not going to be one, and it was the fourteenth of them, and nobody wrote a date on it, and there has not been a date written on any of the fourteen, and there is not going to be one. It was not put in a room and it is not going to be, and the reason is that he was four hundred yards away on a wet pavement in the dark, and a man standing on wet pavement is not in a room, and there is no way of knowing from four hundred yards what a room sounds like.**

The two men who read them aloud have read every one of these since the third of January 2015. **Neither of them has ever been asked about a single one of them by anybody, and neither of them ever will be, and the reason is not discretion. The reason is that there is nothing to ask about.**

**And nobody in that building knows that a man was four hundred yards away on purpose, and nobody in that building has any way of finding out, and a porter there told a man in a corridor on the second of March that he would not say that he was there and he would not say that he was not there, and he has told nobody, and there has been nothing since that required him to.**

And the last occasion on which any person in that building came within a corridor of any of this was in the first week of April, when a man stood at a wall on a Friday morning and did not ask about a gate and did not ask about a rota and did not ask about a person, and nobody there knew why he had come and nobody there is going to ask him.

---

**And at about twenty past six on Sunday the twenty-sixth of April, Jonas Mercer was four hundred yards away.**

The term that had been in force was the thirteenth of them. It had begun at seven o'clock on Sunday the nineteenth and it ran six days and it stopped at six o'clock that morning.

**The hour between six and seven on the twenty-sixth of April was, therefore, the hour in which no term was in force at all, and it was the fourth of those in four weeks, and there is no date in this city for it, and there was one on the fifth of April and there was one on the twelfth and there was one on the nineteenth, and nobody wrote any of them down and nobody is going to.**

At seven o'clock the fourteenth term came in. It was read at ten o'clock the night before by two men who are not on anything, and it lasts six days, and it expires at six o'clock on Sunday the third of May.

**There is no crossover. There is no hour in which two of them are running and there is no hour in which one of them runs an hour past. There is an hour every week in which this city has nothing, and then there is a door.**

---

He stood in it for about half an hour and looked at the shape of a building he could not see into, and the finding was four months old and he had not managed to improve on it.

**From four hundred yards you cannot tell whether a door is open or shut, and that is the correct amount of evidence, and it is the only evidence anybody in this borough will ever have about that hour, and the two men who read the terms aloud are the only two people in this city who know when it happens, and they are not asked and they are not going to be asked and they do not know they are the only two.**

And then, at about seven o'clock, a light came on in a car park on the far side of a building, and he could not tell what it was about, and it was nothing, and it was the fourteenth of those since the twenty-fifth of January, which is thirteen weeks, and it was about seven o'clock, and a light came on in a car park he could not see into, and nobody in this borough is entitled to say he had noticed it.

---

**And in the four days after that nothing at all happened in that building and nothing at all happened in this one, and the standing of it all is this.**

**The rota has still not been rebuilt and cannot be. The eleven o'clock to half past one row is still empty and still does not say *vacant*. Bram Osei's Thursday is still refused in his own handwriting with *no reason I can give* under a *no reason given* in somebody else's hand, and nobody has looked at the second line and nobody is going to.**

**The fifteenth of them is on Saturday the second of May, at ten o'clock at night, and there is no date on it, and there has not been a date on one of these since the twenty-fourth of January, and there is not going to be one, and a person is not required to be told and there is nobody in this borough who is required to write it down.**

And a register of narrowings is at nineteen entries with a blank line under the nineteenth that has been blank for a hundred and fifty-one days, and a card has been in the slot in a counter since the twentieth of February, and there is a tray on a second floor with post on top of something in it that nobody has dealt with, and a drawer under a counter with no key on it and no list of what is in it, and about eleven people a day, and two conditions on a wall sixteen inches apart that have never been reconciled and are not going to be.

**And a man of forty-four has a fortnight somewhere in this city with a bucket under a tap and nobody in this borough is able to ask him whether he has got a coat. And a man of forty-nine can be asked one question tomorrow morning about anything, for ever, by anybody, and is not going to be. And a man of forty-eight is not going to be asked, and Jonas Mercer has said that out loud in a corridor and is not going to unsay it.**

---

**And there is a woman in this borough who is going to be asked something in April, or in May, or in June, and it is not going to be Jonas Mercer, and he is not going to stop it, and he is not going to be in the room.**

She is fifty-four and she has stood in a shopfront since the fourth of July without being paid a penny and she is the only door in a building of thirty-one rented rooms, and nobody has told her what that is and nobody is going to.

On the eleventh of December, in a kitchen, she said that she has not got an answer ready and is not going to get one ready in a room, and that it is the same refusal as the day after and the same reason and it stands, and she said it once and it has not been improved on by anybody including him.

**And nobody in this borough is entitled to make her get one ready, and there is no instrument in this city by which anybody could, and the only thing anybody in this borough can do about it is the thing he has just spent a fortnight deciding to do with a Sunday morning, which is to be somewhere he is not required to be and to let the correct thing happen without him.**
