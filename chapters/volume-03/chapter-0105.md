# Chapter 0105 — What The Audit Found

**Friday the seventh of November 2014, ten in the morning, a room on the first floor above a shop in Gantry Ward.**

Jonas Mercer was not in it. He was two streets away at a counter with a lever-arch file he had gone to collect and had not opened, and the room had nine people in it, and one of them was Alden Rook, and none of them knew he was two streets away because nobody in that room knew why he would be.

**CivicCore Limited had audited itself.** Not the borough, not the Schedule, not the determination of the twenty-eighth of August, and not the twenty years of anybody else's premises. It had audited the nodes it holds, which is the thing it is paid to hold, and it had started on the first of September and it was now the seventh of November and it was finished.

Four hundred and ten flats' worth of somebody's evening.

That was the phrase the audit itself used, in a paragraph on page two, and it was true, and Jonas Mercer read it on the Monday standing up at a counter, and it turned out to be the sentence that decided what kind of document he was holding. **It is not a phrase anybody writes about their own work.** Rook had put it there on purpose, in a plain sentence, in the second paragraph, where anybody would read it in the first thirty seconds, and what it said was: *this cost two of my people about four hundred evenings and nobody in this room has been told, and the number is in the document because a document that hides its own cost is a document that can be used against the people who paid for it.*

**It found real faults. That is the part nobody expected and it is the reason the thing is dangerous.**

It found hinges with no last verified essential use recorded — one hundred and six of them, across four hundred and ten flats, on a service floor that has forty-one hinges on a chalk board at a time and nine of them that are not CivicCore's staff, which is a sentence Rook has been saying in rooms since March and has now put in a document.

It found forty-one places where the answer to *who answers for this* was a company, and thirty-eight places where the answer was a person, and nine where the answer was a description of a person, and it gave the nine as a list of the node references and it did not give the descriptions, and the note said: *the descriptions are not reproduced because reproducing them would create a record of a person, and this audit does not create records of persons.*

It found four nodes on a schedule at a tier where the fee had been paid by a body that no longer existed, and it did not say what it did about them, and it did not disconnect them, and it paid them itself, and Rook said in the room that this was the most expensive line item in the document and that he had looked for somebody to bill and there was nobody to bill, **and that the audit had paid four fees for four nodes that nobody is going to pay for, and that it would go on paying them, and that nobody in that borough had ever written down the sentence *nobody is going to pay for it* before he had.**

And it found a gate.

It found a mortuary gate — node 6-44 — and a car park barrier, node 3, and a staff stair, node 2, and a service riser, node 1: four nodes, of which one was a gate. And the finding runs to nine pages, and it is the longest finding in the document, and what it says, in the audit's own words, on page two hundred and nine, is this:

> **The maintenance of this gate has been performed continuously since approximately 2005 by one individual who is not employed by this company, is not a party to any agreement, and whose name does not appear in any field of any document associated with this node. The record of the last verified essential use of this node was made on 23 April 2014, in nine seconds, by a different individual, and is a record of the maintenance rather than of a use. On the audit's reading the gate is therefore maintained by an act and the act is performed by a person, and the person is not findable from the record and the record does not require it to be. This audit does not name either individual and this audit's position is that the record is correct and should not be amended. It further records that if the second individual were to cease performance, the node would narrow to its maintenance, and its maintenance is the first individual, and the first individual is not a party and could not be required to continue.**
>
> **This audit finds no fault at this node. This audit records that it is the most reliable arrangement this audit has encountered in four hundred and ten flats' worth of premises and that it is unlawful, and that both of those are correct.**

Four hundred and ten evenings, and there is not one of them in that document that is anybody's argument about a price. That was the whole of what Rook said in the room, and he said it in about nine minutes, and he did not say it once for effect.

"And it is not a favour to anybody," he said, to nine people. "**I am not doing it because we lost the price. I would like that on whatever you write down. We lost the price in August and we lost nothing else, and a document that says we are here auditing four hundred and ten flats because we lost an argument reads in four years as an apology and it is not one.** I am doing it because of what the registrar said in a doorway in August, and everybody in this borough thinks that sentence was a complaint, and it was not a complaint, it was an instruction.

"**A thing that was going to happen anyway now has to be argued for, in public, repeatedly, by people who are not paid to argue.** So I have argued for it. Once. On paper. Four hundred evenings of two people's time and a print run and about nine thousand pounds of it, and it is mine, and I have paid it, and it does not have to be paid again by anybody in this city, and I would like that written down in whatever room you are going to write it down in, because in about three years somebody is going to ask me why I did it and I would like the answer to be *because I could afford to and I did not want them to have to.*"

Nobody in the room argued with him and about four of them were not going to be on his side in a year.

**And the cover note, on page one, which is where a reader starts and which is the reason this chapter exists, has a line in it that nobody wrote on purpose.**

> *This document describes only what we hold.*
>
> *The city has nodes we do not hold, and have never held, and do not know about, and are not aware of. They are not on our schedules, they are not on our layer, they are not in our drawings, and they have never been in any of them. We have no instrument for finding them and we are not going to pretend to this document that we have, and we have not estimated the number, and we have not been asked for an estimate, and if we were asked for an estimate we would not be able to give one that would mean anything.*
>
> *The number of them is not in this report. It is not zero. It is not one. It is not a number anybody should write in a document, and there are people in this city who have already declined, in public and in the right order, to turn the absence of a number into a number of people, and this company agrees with them and has not helped.*
>
> *Every finding in this report is a finding about a node somebody is paying for. That is not a limitation of the audit. That is the audit.*

---

**Monday the tenth of November 2014, twenty past one in the afternoon, the counter, the records office, second floor.**

It was a public document, because CivicCore had put it on its own floor at Gantry Ward in a plastic wallet and had sent it to the Committee, and the Committee's receipt made it obtainable at that counter, and the counter photocopied four pages for anybody free and would not photocopy a hundred and sixty more without a fee and a signature.

Jonas Mercer read the whole of the findings section standing up over about an hour and forty minutes, and the woman at the counter brought him a chair at about half past two without being asked and without saying anything about it, which is the single most useful thing that counter has ever done for anybody.

He read page two hundred and nine twice.

**And then he came to the end of the report, which is where a report says what it is not, and the last paragraph of the last page said this.**

> *It is our view, and it is stated without qualification because we can support it, that the largest single category of service risk in this city is not any fault recorded in this report. It is the set of nodes we cannot describe, which is outside the scope of this document, which we are not able to size, and which we have concluded it would be dishonest to estimate in a document that will be read in rooms.*
>
> *We are a contractor. We are not in a position to do anything about that and we are not asking anybody to think that we are. We are telling four hundred and ten addresses' worth of people, and a standing Committee, and whoever else reads this, that we have found the edges of what we do, and the edges are the whole of the problem, and we are the wrong people to say so and we are saying it anyway because nobody in this city who is in a position to say it has said it yet, and we would rather be an uncredentialed witness who is right than a credentialed one that is silent.*
>
> *If this document is used to suggest that the unheld nodes are a problem to be brought within a contract, we ask that it not be. They are not a problem to be brought in. There is nothing to bring them into. There is nobody to bring them to, by a rule that has been in force since the first of March, and by the plainest possible operation of it.*

He read that four times, and then he put the four photocopied pages in the pad, and asked the woman at the counter whether the Committee had received a covering letter.

"There was one," she said. "It was two lines and it said *please find enclosed our audit of the nodes we hold* and the word *our* was underlined once, by hand, with a biro, in the middle of the page."

---

**The same afternoon, at about four o'clock, and this is the other half of the day and it is the half that matters.**

He came out of the records office with four pages in the pad and a number in his head, and the number was not the number of anything anybody can be helped by, and the number was a hundred and six, and the hundred and six was hinges with no recorded use, and it was a number of hinges and not of people and could not be worked into one.

And then, at about five past four, a letter came through the internal post in the same building, from the borough's housing office to CivicCore Limited, and it had a reference on it, and it was not marked private, and the woman at the counter read the envelope over her glasses and then read the first line over her glasses and then put it down flat and did not say anything, and Jonas Mercer was still standing there with four pages in his pad.

He had already spent his one question that day, on a covering letter, and he was not going to spend it twice, and the woman at the counter looked at him for about four seconds and then said:

"**That is not a question, Mr Mercer, and I am going to answer it anyway, because a counter is required to answer and a counter is not required to make one, and because I have worked this counter for eleven years and I am tired.** It is a question about your audit, and it is about the Carrow Lane association, and it is asking whether the hundred and twenty-eight rooms of that association are within the scope of it. And it is addressed to CivicCore and it has got a borough reference on it and a due date, and it will go out tomorrow, and **it is a completely ordinary letter and there is nothing in it that is anybody's fault and it is going to be answered in a fortnight and the answer is going to be read out.**

"**In the stock review. Every written question in that borough goes in a book and every answer gets read.**" She turned the envelope over. "The stock review is on the Thursday. It is half past two. And it is the chief officer of that association who answers for her four blocks, and she is on the attendance, and she is in the room when the answers are read, and she will be sitting about nine feet from the person who reads it. The answer will be two lines. *The association is not a party to CivicCore's service agreements and its premises are not within the scope of the audit.* Something like that.

"**And that is the true answer and it is worse than a wrong one, and I can tell you why it is worse, and you did not ask me, and I am saying it anyway.**" She put her pen down. "Because in about four years a file in a commercial company is going to say that a hundred and twenty-eight rooms in four blocks in this borough have never been looked at by anybody, in any document, in nine years. And the only instrument this city has for turning that sentence into anything at all is a list. **And the woman it is about has spent a year refusing to produce a list, in public, in front of nineteen people, and won.**"

Jonas Mercer stood in a records office with four photocopied pages in a pad.

**He could ring her. A person may tell a person anything. She is not a party to his standing, he is not on her rota, he has no authority over her and he has never had any, and he has not telephoned her five times and both of them have said in public that this is now permanent, and there is no rule in this city that says he may not pick up a telephone.**

He thought about it for about forty seconds, on the pavement, in the cold, with the pad in his hand.

**And then he worked out what he would be doing, and he did it standing on a pavement, and it took him about eleven seconds and it is the fourth time this year that eleven seconds has cost somebody something.**

If he rang her on Monday afternoon and said *a question has gone to CivicCore about your hundred and twenty-eight rooms and the answer is no and it will be read out on Thursday at half past two and you are in the room*, then on Thursday afternoon a chief officer of forty-seven would know that her four blocks have been recorded, in a company document, in the word *scope*, as being outside everything anybody has looked at for nine years.

And a chief officer who knows that is a chief officer who has to do something about it.

**And the only thing she can do about it is produce a list.** Not because she is a villain. Because it is Tuesday's answer and Thursday's meeting and a file in a commercial company that does not close, and she has eleven years and no assistant and £4,361.60 on no form, and the only instrument anybody has ever offered that borough is *tell us who is there*, and she has been refusing it since March, and she is not going to stop refusing it in November because a man who is not a party to anything felt frightened on a pavement.

He put the pad away.

He did not ring her. **That is the correct decision and it is the third time he has made it in a year and it is the first one that has been made about a thing he has read in a document rather than a thing that has happened to somebody in front of him, and it is the one that is going to cost the most, and he wrote it down on the back of the fourth envelope on the pavement outside a records office at about five past five in the afternoon on the tenth of November 2014, in a hand that is not his best, and it is the shortest thing in the pad all year.**

> *Monday 10 November. CivicCore's audit of the nodes it holds is public and I have read the findings. It is better than anything this borough has and it is not neutral and its cover note says the whole of the problem in nine lines and it was written by a contractor who is right.*
> *The borough's housing office has written to CivicCore asking whether the Carrow Lane association's 128 rooms are in it. The answer is no. The answer will be read out on Thursday at half past two in a room she is sitting in.*
> *I did not ring her. I stood on the pavement for about forty seconds and I did not ring her, and the reason is that if I tell her, she has to do something about it, and the only thing she can do is produce a list, and she has been refusing to produce a list since March and she won a public case in August on the grounds that she would not.*
> *I have now twice in eight months protected a person from a fact by not saying it, and in both cases I was protecting her from a document and not from a person, and I would like somebody to explain to me the difference, because I have been using it as if it were a principle since April and it is not a principle, it is a preference of mine, and I am the only person in this city it is available to.*

He put the four envelopes in the pad and went down the hill and did not go anywhere near a road that leads to a lowland.
