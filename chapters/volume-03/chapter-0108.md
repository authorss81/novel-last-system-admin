# Chapter 0108 — The Man Who Classifies Streets As Plant

**Friday the twenty-first of November 2014, twenty-five minutes past five in the evening, the Methodist hall, Carrow Lane, Tideglass.**

There were ten people. Nine chairs in the horseshoe, and a stack of nine folding chairs against the wall that had been there since September and had never once been brought out, and the seventh chair in the horseshoe had been empty for forty-four days.

**It had been empty since Wednesday the eighth of October, when KATHLEEN FERRIS, 33, night bus controller, nine years, wrote four lines on a piece of paper and gave them to a woman who keeps the minutes, and nobody argued with her, and the four lines are in a file, and nobody has read them out.**

She had written them in about eleven minutes on the back of a bus timetable.

> *I asked what we are for on the fourth of September and we spent an hour and ten minutes on it and we were not allowed to answer it. I have thought about it every day since and I have not got better at it. I am not stopping because of a job. I am stopping because you have not got an answer and you have decided that not having an answer is the same as not needing one, and I have been on the last bus for nine years and I know exactly what a vehicle does when nobody knows where it is going. — K. FERRIS, in her own words. She asked for it to be recorded that it is not about money and it is not about a rota and it is about the hour and ten minutes.*

The board did not replace her. **It could not.** A person who is named without being asked may decline afterwards in her own words and nothing follows from it, and no member may require a person to do anything, and there is no instrument in this city by which nine people who cannot require anybody can require one of themselves to sit down.

So for six weeks the seventh chair has been a shape.

And on the wall behind the horseshoe, where a Methodist hall has hung a cross and a photograph of a conference in 1978 and a notice about a jumble sale, there was a small card in a plastic sleeve, in the flat register, with no crest and no form number and no signature on it, that nobody in the building had admitted to putting there.

> **A SEAT IS A DUTY. A DUTY IS AN ACT AND NOT A PLACE. NOBODY ON THIS BODY CAN BE REQUIRED TO BE TOLD THAT THEY ARE A SEAT, AND NOBODY ON THIS BODY CAN BE REQUIRED TO TAKE ONE.**

---

**Jonas Mercer was against the left-hand wall again, with nothing in his hands, and the woman who keeps the minutes said at the top of the evening that he was not on the agenda, not a party, not a member and not a departmental officer, and that seat two had asked him to be in the room and that he was going to be asked one question, and that nobody was entitled to the answer and that he could decline it.**

He had said yes to that on the Thursday afternoon on the telephone, and he had said yes because he could have said no, and because a man who is not on the agenda who has been asked to be asked one question has been offered the only thing anybody has offered him since March, which is the right to decline.

The question came at nine minutes past six and it came from Ibrahim Sesay, 51, caretaker, Tarn Street school, thirty-one years, who stood up to ask it, which nobody had asked him to do.

"**Mr Mercer. In August, a chief officer stood up in a room with nineteen people in it and described six words that were written in this city in March in eleven minutes in a records room, and said she had never found out who wrote them and was not going to. Would you tell this room whether you wrote them.**"

"No," said Jonas Mercer, before he had decided, and about nine of the ten people in the room made a noise.

"Sorry — would you tell this room whether you would *not* have wanted anybody to know you wrote them."

"Yes."

He waited. The room waited. **And then somebody said the thing that he had been carrying for eight months, out loud, in a Methodist hall, to about ten people, in eleven words, in his own words, and he had not planned it and had not decided to and it came out of him the way a thing comes out of a man at the fourth line of a page when the hand has gone.**

"They are mine. **6-L/14, non-civic plant. Written on the fourteenth of March at 12:41 in a records room on the second floor and initialled, and lodged into the series six file on the twenty-second under a reporting officer number that is not mine, by Royden Achebe, forty-nine, field services, who did not read it before he lodged it and has never said so.**"

He said it in about nineteen seconds and he said it to the floor and not to anybody.

**And the room did four different things with it inside about forty seconds, and all four of them were correct, and that is the part he has not been able to put down since.**

**The first was a man of about fifty who said: "Then you have put all of us in it."**

"No," said Yolanda Fofana, without turning round. "**That is not a confession and I would like it said in the room, because he has been waiting eight months for somebody to call it one and I have been waiting with him. A confession is a thing a person does about a thing they did.** This is a fact about a document. It has been a fact about that document since the twenty-second of March, and the only difference is that it has been in four documents and a radio programme instead of one, and a man stood up in a public room in August and said he had been able to find out for two months and did not, and that was true, and it was not anybody's bad faith. He is not confessing to anything. He has said a true sentence about a piece of paper. That is all that has happened."

"Then it is nothing," said the man.

"**It is a great deal, and it is not a confession, and you are about to say the next thing wrong and I am going to stop you because I have been in nineteen years of rooms where somebody was about to say the next thing wrong.**"

**The second was Ibrahim Sesay, who had asked the question, and who sat down again, and then said from the chair:**

"Then the line in the form that has been my fault since March has got a cause."

"It has."

"It has been in a form on a wall in my office since the second of April and I have looked at it every day for about eight months and I have thought *somebody typed this wrong*." He took his glasses off. "**It was not typed wrong. It was decided in a room in eleven minutes by a man who had never been to the lowland, and I have been angry at a form, and I would like to say that in a room and have it in a minute, and I am not going to because it is not a thing anybody can be angry at and I have wasted eight months.**"

"Then say it in the minute," said the woman at the back.

"**No,**" said Ibrahim Sesay. "I have got a minute already with four lines in it from October and it is mine and it is about me and it is the best thing anybody has done for this board, and I am not going to put eight months of being wrong in the same drawer as it."

**The third was Gloria Nye, 44, self-employed heating engineer, twenty-one years, three shifts a week, and she did not say anything for about eleven seconds and then she said the sentence that Jonas Mercer has thought about every day since.**

"**Then I am going to say the unkind one, and I am going to say it as the liability and not as the insult, and it is about me and not about him.**"

"Go on."

"**If he can call a street plant then he can call a plant room plant.**" She put her hands flat on her knees. "I am the eighth seat of this board. I have been a heating engineer for twenty-one years and I have a van and a book and about four hundred clients and I am the nearest person to a boiler when one goes at eleven at night, and that is why I am here, and it is on the sheet. **And there is now a man in this city who looked at a piece of ground and wrote two words about it in eleven minutes and a panel accepted them in four weeks, and there is no form, and there is no rota, and there is no name on anything, and he is not on this board and he cannot be made to be on this board, and I can go anywhere in this borough with a bag of parts and decide what a thing is.**"

Nobody said anything.

"And if somebody in this room had put him in that chair in September then I would not be on this board, and neither would Dermot be, and neither would Winifred, and it would not be a board of nine people who are each answerable for a thing." She looked at the wall card. "**That is a liability. It is not a fault. It is the only reason there is a board at all.**"

**And the fourth was the same woman, about eleven seconds later, and it was the only one of the four that was about a person who was not in the room, and she said it to him and not to the wall.**

"**Mr Mercer. You have said out loud, in a room, the name of a man who is not in this room, and what he did and did not read, and he cannot answer it, and he is not forty-four.**"

"He is not."

"He is forty-nine, and he holds a duty at 6-L/16, and he telephoned a shop in August about a Tuesday, and he was answered, and that is the whole of what I know about him and I know it because it is on a sheet." She did not raise her voice. "**In August you put your own name to six words and you did not put his. Tonight you have put his name to a sentence about his own conduct, and nobody in this room can tell him, and you did not do it because you were angry with him. You did it because you were being complete.**"

"Yes," said Jonas Mercer.

"**Then be complete about what it is. It is not a bigger version of what you did in August, it is a different thing, and the difference is that a man of forty-four cannot be asked one question about any of it by anybody, ever, and a man of forty-nine can be asked one tomorrow morning and will have to answer it, and neither of them has been asked, and one of them cannot be.**"

---

**And then they asked him for the chair.**

It was not a demand and it could not have been, and it was made by a person who had been in the room for an hour and a half and who had stopped speaking some time ago, and she stood up to do it, and the whole room turned round.

"Mister Mercer."

"Yolanda."

"**I am going to ask you for the seventh seat and I am going to do it as a person and not as a board, because I am the ninth seat and a board cannot require anybody to do anything and I have not got a vote and I have not got a chair and I have got nothing except that I am the only one of the nine anybody can write to, and I am writing.**" She had the four lines on a piece of paper in her hand and she did not read them. "The seventh seat has been empty for forty-four days. We cannot fill it. We are not allowed to ask anybody, and we have spent six weeks working out who to want instead, and that is a rota, and I have watched a rota happen in this room from inside it and I did not enjoy it."

"Nor did anybody."

"**No. And I am not going to be able to finish this in one go.** The seventh seat is not really a district, because Ferris answered for the last bus and the last bus is not a place, and there is no district called last bus, and there is a man in this city who looked at a piece of ground and decided in eleven minutes that it was not civic, and half of this room has been trying to get hold of him since the second of October for exactly that reason and I do not think one of us has ever once stopped to ask what we actually wanted him for."

The room was completely quiet and the boiler in the hall made a noise.

"**I am asking you to take the seventh seat. You may say no. If you say no, it is minuted that you said no, and nobody in this room will ask you a second question about anything for the rest of your life, and I will personally make sure of that.**"

And Jonas Mercer stood against the left-hand wall of a Methodist hall on Carrow Lane on the twenty-first of November 2014, and he did not have to think about it, because he had been thinking about it since the twenty-eighth of October.

"**No. A man who calls a street plant must not be findable.**"

Eleven words. He had said them before, in a corridor, to Bea Osunde's back, in about June, and they had not been eleven words then.

Yolanda Fofana wrote them down. She wrote them on the four lines, on the back of the piece of paper, in her own hand, and she did not put a note beside them and she did not soften them and she did not say *and the reason is*.

"Thank you," she said.

"You are welcome," said Yolanda Fofana, and put a line under the eleven words, and read the whole of the four lines and the eleven words back to herself without saying any of it out loud, and then folded the paper once and put it in her bag.

And then the ninth seat of a cooperative steward board in a Methodist hall said the thing that was the actual reason, out loud, in a room, about eleven seconds, and she said it to the room and not to him.

"**And I am not going to tell the rest of you why, and I am not going to say the sentence, and there is a sentence and it is mine and I am not saying it, and that is the whole of what I have ever wanted this body for.**"

---

**They finished at about ten past eight and the minute went out on the Monday the twenty-fourth.**

Nobody rang him about it. He had told them on the telephone on Thursday that nobody was entitled to the answer, and about four of them had believed him, and the other six had gone straight on talking, and both of those were the correct response to being told a thing like that by a person who had earned it.

**On the Monday morning he came in at twenty past nine and the woman at the counter gave him a photocopy of four pages without being asked, which is the second time in three weeks that counter has done something for him that it is not required to do.**

> **Item 4. Seat seven. A person present, not on the agenda, not a party, not a member and not a departmental officer, was asked in the room, in daylight, whether he had written the classification of 6-L/14 as non-civic plant on 14 March 2014. He stated that he had. He stated that it was initialled by him and lodged on 22 March 2014 by a third party who did not read it before lodging it. He was asked nothing further and made no further statement.
>
> Recorded: that the person who asked the question has asked for it to be recorded that the statement was not made as a confession, and that a statement about a document is not a confession, and that no person in the room treated it as one.
>
> Recorded: that the seventh seat was offered to that person by the ninth seat, as a person and not as this body, and was declined in the following words, which are minuted as spoken and are not to be amended: **A man who calls a street plant must not be findable.** No reason was given, none was asked for, and none is recorded.
>
> Recorded: that the seventh seat falls vacant and remains vacant, and that no person may be required to take it, and that this body has no power to fill it and will not seek one.
>
> Nothing in this item requires any person to do anything.**

He read it four times standing up.

**It is a public document and it went out on the Monday and it will be in the borough's records by the end of the week, and the six words and the fact that he initialled them are now in the same document in a public record for the first time in eight months and one day, and the minute does not contain his name, because there is no box for one, and he cannot take any of it out.**

**Nobody in that room offered to take them out for him. Not one of the ten people, not the minute-taker, not the ninth seat, not the man who had said *then you have put all of us in it*, and not the woman who had said it was a liability and had been right.**

**And he did not ask, because asking would be asking for a favour, and a favour is a rota, and a rota with one name on it is the exact thing he has spent seven months refusing to be and the exact thing a man of forty-eight put on a supplier's acceptance in August with three lines in his own hand and got right.**

He put the four pages in the pad and asked the woman at the counter one question, which was the only one he asked that day, and it was four words.

"Who has a copy."

"You have a copy," she said. "It is on the counter list under Tuesday, and anybody can ask for it, and it goes out with the minutes, and I have had nine requests in two days and eight of them are journalists, and I am not one of those eight, and I am not going to be the reason a man who is not on the agenda finds out that he is on the front of a paper on a Tuesday morning."

Then Jonas Mercer went down the hill.

**The names are the work.** He had said that sentence to himself in a shop on the fourth of September and written it down three times and had not started it. He had started it on the twenty-eighth of October, in a room, with one word, and it had taken eight months and it had produced one name, and it was his own, and it was in a public record, and the sentence Bea Osunde had given him in a shop in September had been a sentence about *his* hand and he had done the thing with it that he has done with every other instrument this year.

He went into the shop at about half past eleven and told Bea Osunde the whole of it in about nine minutes, standing up, without sitting down, which is not how he does it.

She listened to all of it and then she said one thing.

"**So you have started.**"

"I have started."

"**In eight months.**" She had a length of ash on her shoulder and she put it down on the bench. "And the first one you said out loud was yours, and it went into a public document on a Monday, and there is a woman of fifty-four in Salter's Yard who is the only one of that nine anybody can write to, and a woman of thirty-four at the bottom of a street with a folding trolley who has told you twice there is going to be a date on it and no person on it, and a woman of seventy-two in room 14 who shut a door on you a fortnight ago, and a man of forty-four in a building in Gantry Ward that you are not going to go near, and a night porter whose name was on a chalk board for six weeks in a room he sat in every day."

"Yes."

"**Then I am going to say the thing I have been not saying since the fourth of September, and I am only going to say it once, and then I am going to go and cut a piece of ash,**" said Bea Osunde, "**and it is that being findable is not the same as being useful, and you have found out the first half in eight months, and the second half is a different job, and the second half is the one I was talking about, and you have done the easy one and it took you eight months and there are three people in this borough who need the hard one and I have watched all three of them get older this year while you were getting round to yourself.**"
