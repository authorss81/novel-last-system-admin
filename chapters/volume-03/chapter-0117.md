# Chapter 0117 — Nobody Is Going To Go Down There On Monday

**Monday the nineteenth of January 2015, about eleven in the morning, a corridor, second floor, the civic offices.**

He had been trying to work it out since the third of December and he had got to eight items and had written them on the back of the fourth envelope in a corridor on the Friday morning, and they are the whole of what a name in a room is not, and there is no ninth item and there is not going to be one.

> *A name said out loud in a room on the third of December 2014 cannot be un-said. Nobody in this borough has an instrument for un-saying it and there is not going to be one.*
> *It cannot be produced.*
> *It cannot be checked, because there is nothing to check it against, because there is no box.*
> *It cannot be argued with, because to argue with a name you have to be able to say whose it is, in public, twice.*
> *It cannot be minuted. The minute of the third of December says that a name was stated and does not contain it, and there is no box on the sheet and there is no box in this borough that says who wrote a thing down.*
> *It cannot be published, because the publication is the minute.*
> *It cannot be attached to a claim, because a claim has a reference and a date and a party and this has none of the three.*
> *And it cannot be found by anybody who is not one of the eleven people who were in that room, including me.*

He stood in that corridor on the Monday and read it and did not like it, and it is correct.

**In about four years somebody is going to come with a piece of paper about that stand at the bottom of Quarry Street. And there is not going to be one thing anybody in this borough can put beside it.** There is a page in the third week of November with a number in it that anybody can check, and the stand is not in it, and cannot be, because the page is a record of nodes and she is a person, and a person is not a node. There is a register on a wall with nineteen entries and a blank line, and she is not on it and must never be. There is a letter in a handbag with her name at the bottom of it, in her own hand, on purpose, and there is a minute that records a piece of paper going onto a table in a hall and does not say whose it was.

**And if that person stands at the bottom of that street with a folder, there will be nothing to put on the table but the folder.** No name, no number, no refusal, no minute, no form, no entry, no party, no date — and the stand will have a date on it and no person on it, and that will be the only deal there has ever been, and it will be the only deal there is.

**That is either the best outcome available in this borough or it is the worst one, and this chapter is not going to say which, and neither is anybody in it, and it is not going to be said this year.**

---

**About eleven people have her name.**

That is the whole of it and it is the price and it is not a leak. On the evening of the third of December there were about eleven people in a Methodist hall and a man said four syllables to the floor, and eleven people heard them, and it went into no document, and there is no register of the eleven and there never will be, because a list of who has heard a name is a list of eleven people and a list of eleven people is exactly the instrument this borough has spent eight months refusing to build.

**Nobody has been asked. Nobody is going to be asked. The ninth seat said in a hall on the third of December that we did not go and get her and we could not have, and that nobody in that room asked her for anything and nobody in that room is going to, and that nobody is to go down there on Monday.**

It is the nineteenth of January. There has been no Monday since the twelfth on which anybody went down there, and Jonas Mercer has been to a shop, a counter, a records office and a Methodist hall in seven weeks and has not once gone to the bottom of a street, and he has not telephoned, **and there is no number in this borough for anybody to telephone, and that is not on the list of eight and it is not going on it, and it is four words long and it is in the margin of the fourth envelope, under the eight.**

---

**And then the post came on the Monday, and it came to the hall, because the hall is the only address there is.**

It was one side of A4, printed, on borough stock, with a crest in the corner, and it had a reference and an issue date and a form number, and it came in an envelope with COMMITTEE SERVICES in the corner of it, and it was addressed to *the person who submitted correspondence to the Cooperative Steward Board*, care of the Methodist hall, Carrow Lane, because the return address on a letter that went through a door is the door.

> **CORRESPONDENCE: ACKNOWLEDGEMENT OF RECEIPT**
>
> *Your letter of 28 November 2014 has been received and is on file. Correspondence is not admitted to a sitting unless the writer is present.*
>
> *If you are not present on the date below, your correspondence will be treated as withdrawn.*
>
> **Date of sitting: to be confirmed.**
>
> **☐ I will attend ☐ I withdraw it ☐ I have not been asked to do either**
>
> **Name: ——————————————————  Date: ——————————**

Nobody in that hall sent it.

**The body has one power. It may require a party holding continuity to answer, receive the answer, and be asked to consider a matter. It cannot require a person to do anything, it cannot require a person to be told anything, and it cannot require a person to attend a sitting it is not required to hold.** A person at the bottom of a street is not a party. A person who wrote a letter is not a party. There was no party in the letter and there is no party in the envelope and there is no sitting and the date says *to be confirmed* and has been to be confirmed since the twenty-eighth of November.

And it came anyway, out of a department that does not know, addressed to a hall, with three boxes and a line for a name and a line for a date on it, **and the third of those three boxes is the only correct answer on the sheet and there is nowhere on the sheet to put it, and a records supervisor in this borough has been saying out loud since March that a box is a decision and that the absence of one is a decision, and neither of those two people was asked about this form and neither of them ever will be.**

The woman who keeps the minutes put it in the tray under the trestle table and she did not throw it away and she did not send it anywhere and she did not put it in a minute.

Then she wrote something on a second piece of paper, in biro, in about four minutes, and she did not sign it, and she left that one blank at the top, and she put the two of them together on the trestle table on the Wednesday afternoon at about half past three, and she shut the hall and went out, and she did not tell anybody.

**There is no crest on the second one and no reference and no form number and no box and no line for a name, and there was nobody in this borough who sent it and nobody who is waiting for it back, and it is the only piece of paper in that hall that a person can write on without becoming a thing in a file.** That is an accident and it is not going to be repeated, and a records supervisor in this borough has said out loud since March that a box is a decision and that the absence of one is a decision, and the woman who keeps the minutes decided nothing at all and left a piece of paper on a table.

---

**Wednesday the twenty-first of January 2015, about ten past four in the afternoon, the Methodist hall, Carrow Lane, Tideglass. Daylight. In a hall with the heating off.**

Nobody was in the hall.

Jonas Mercer was about four hundred yards away at a counter on the second floor of the civic offices with a lever-arch file he had gone to collect and had not opened, and he had come out at about ten past four and had not gone down the hill.

**Nobody had been told that anybody was coming, because nobody was going to be told, because a person who is findable is a person who can be asked, and a person who can be asked has to have an answer ready, and the whole of the design is that nobody in this borough has to have an answer ready on a Wednesday afternoon.**

There is a woman of thirty-four who has been at a stand at the bottom of a street at twenty to seven every morning with a folding trolley for eleven years, and she came at about twenty past four on the Wednesday, in daylight, on a bus, and she was not found, and nobody went and got her, and nobody telephoned her, and there is no number in this borough for anybody to telephone, and she came because a hall is a hall and a hall has a door and a door is the only address anybody gave her in November.

She came in through the door she had put a letter through on the twenty-eighth of November.

The two pieces of paper were on the trestle table where the minute-taker had left them, and she read both of them standing up in about four minutes, and she read the printed one twice, and she did not touch any of its three boxes, and nobody was watching.

**She did not write on the printed one. There is no name on the printed one and her name is not going on it, and the reason is that a piece of paper with a crest on it and a form number and a reference is a document, and a name at the top of a document is the first thing anybody in about four years would want, and she was not asked for it by anybody and she is not going to hand it over on a Wednesday afternoon to a hall.**

**What she wrote on was the second one, which is a sheet of A4 with four lines of another woman's biro on it and nothing else, unsigned, with no heading and no box and no line for anything, and which nobody sent and nobody is expecting and which cannot be used to show anybody anything at all.**

And then she took out a pen and she wrote at the top of that one, in her own hand, in biro — her name, and the street, and the date, in a line across the top like somebody signing a form, which is the first time in eleven years she has put those four things together on one piece of paper and she has done it on purpose and in daylight.

> **Bernice Ofori, of the bottom of Quarry Street. The 21st of January.**
>
> **I have not withdrawn anything. I asked a question. There is no sitting to attend and nobody has asked me to attend one.**

Nine seconds, at a fair walk, if you do not hurry it. And it cost her nothing: no money, no time, no seat, no water, no journey she had not planned, and no favour from anybody, and there was nobody in that hall at all.

She read it back. She did not cross anything out and she did not improve it, and she put no mark in any of the three boxes, and a person does not tick a box to say that she has not been asked to tick it, and putting a fourth box on that sheet would have made it a form with a place to put a refusal in, and that would have been a different document, and there was nobody there to draw it.

**She left the printed one on the table and left her four lines on the table beside it, on the top of the other four, and took nothing with her, because there was nothing of hers on that table to take — her own letter of the twenty-eighth of November has been in a handbag in a kitchen four hundred yards away since the third of December and it was still in it — and she went out through the door at about ten to five.**

---

**And the woman who keeps the minutes came back into the hall at eleven minutes past five and found both pieces of paper on the trestle table and stood looking at them for a while, and her name is not in this account and she is not going to be named in it, and there is no box anywhere in this borough that says who wrote a thing down.**

She read the top of it. She could not help it. It is the first time anybody has ever read a name off a piece of paper in that hall that she did not have to, and it took her about four seconds and then she looked away, and then she read it again, because nobody was going to stop her and because in about four years somebody is going to come and ask whether that hall holds correspondence.

Then she picked both pieces up — the printed one and her own, and she put the printed one in the tray where it had been, which is where a printed thing goes, and she put the sheet with her four lines at the top of it in a plastic wallet with a rubber band round it, and she did not put either of them in the minute, and she did not put either of them in a file, and she put the wallet in the drawer of the trestle table, and she shut the drawer, and there is no key.

**She has told nobody. She is not going to tell anybody. And in about four years there is going to be a drawer in a Methodist hall with four lines of biro in it that are not hers, and her own four lines above them, and nothing else in the world that says any of it happened, and no list of what is in any drawer in that building, and that is the only record of it in the world and it is a drawer, and the one record of it in the world is four lines in biro on a sheet of A4 that anybody could have written and nobody did.**

---

**And Jonas Mercer was told about it on the Friday morning, in a shop, by the woman it is most dangerous for him to be told about, in about nine seconds, and he asked her nothing about any of it, and a shop is a public room, and he had not spent his question that day, and he spent it once about two minutes later and there is no second one in this account of a Friday in January.**

Yolanda Fofana put a card down on the counter, face up, the way he puts things, and said:

"**A woman came to the hall on Wednesday at about twenty past four and she was not asked anything and she was not given anything and nobody went and got her and nobody telephoned her and there is no number in this borough for anybody to telephone, and she wrote nine seconds on a piece of paper with her name at the top of it, and she did it in daylight, and she knew exactly what she was doing.**"

"I am not going to ask you what is on it."

"I know you are not. That is why I am telling you." She put her hand flat on the counter. "**Here is what I have, and I am not going to be told what to do with it, and I am not going to put it in a bag in my kitchen, because a bag in my kitchen is a thing in a building of thirty-one rented rooms and I am the only person anybody can write to, and I have not been a place for other people's paper since the fourth of July and I am not starting in January.**"

"It is in the hall."

"**It is in a drawer in a hall, and nobody has a key, and there is no list of what is in the drawers, and that is the correct arrangement and I would like it minuted that I said so and that there is nobody to minute it.**"

"And the piece of paper in your bag."

"**The letter she wrote in November is in my bag. It has been there since the third of December, and for seven weeks it was the only piece of paper in this borough that had her name on it, and as of Wednesday there are two, and I am not going to be told where the other one is and I have not been, and neither have you, and the one in my bag is not a document and it is not a form and it is not a minute and it is not going to be adopted, and I have not decided anything about it and I am not going to be told, and if you write anything about this I will find out, and if you write anything about this then there will be a second thing in a file with her name in it and the first thing will have been done by you on the third of December in a room and the second one will have been done by you in a corridor, and in about four years those will be the two worst things anybody in this borough knows about you, and neither of them will be about a service.**"

"I am not going to write anything about it."

"**Good.**"

---

**And here is the question, and it was asked on the Friday in a shop by a man, and it was answered, and the answer is not an answer to it, and this chapter is not going to pretend that it is.**

He asked it once, in twelve words, and it was this: **whether a person may hold a document about herself in this borough.**

And Yolanda Fofana said: "**That is not a question about this borough. A person may put whatever she likes in her own bag. That has never needed an instrument and it is not going to get one, and you have spent eight months building instruments that cost nothing and this one costs nothing at all because it does not exist, and that is the reason it is worth having.**"

"That is not an answer."

"**No. It is a thing I have said in a shop and it is going in a shop and nowhere else, and you have heard it and you may do as you like with it, and if you put it on a wall then I will know in a week and I will not be angry, I will just not have said it.**"

---

**And that is what happened, and nobody in this borough was helped, and no rescue happened, and there was no water given to anybody and no seat given to anybody and no favour asked for by anybody, because there is no instrument in this borough that can do any of that and there is not going to be one.**

**The price of the letter of the twenty-eighth of November is now four lines of biro and four more above them in a drawer in a Methodist hall in Tideglass, and a sheet of A4 in a handbag, or not, and nobody is going to open either of them.**

**There are two pieces of paper in this city with her name on them.** Neither of them is a document. Neither of them has a crest on it or a form number or a reference or a box, and one of them has a date in her own hand and one of them has a date in her own hand as well, and neither of them can be used for anything, and neither of them establishes that anybody is anybody. **And neither of them says one word about a stand at the bottom of a street. That is the third thing about them, after the two above, and it is the one that matters, and it is the reason that in about four years a person with a folder will be able to put nothing on the table at all.**

A document is a lever, and she has put her own name on two pieces of paper that are not documents, and she has done it in daylight, and she is thirty-four, and she was in a hall on a Wednesday afternoon with nobody in it and nobody watching and she was not asked for anything by anybody, and she came on a bus she had planned herself.
