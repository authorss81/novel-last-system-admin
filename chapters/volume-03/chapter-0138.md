# Chapter 0138 — What A Drift Looks Like From The Inside

Friday the seventeenth of April 2015, about two in the afternoon, a shop at the end of a street. And Monday the twentieth of April, from about ten past ten in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward.

**There was no question of his in the shop on the Friday afternoon and there was no question of his in the vestibule on the Monday morning, and there has not been one since the twenty-fifth of February, and the counting has been right in every week of this year. There was one question of somebody else's in that vestibule on the Monday morning and it was six words and it was not a question about anything.**

---

The pad was open on the bench end and the bench oiling was going on at the other end and there was a customer at the far counter and Beatrice Osunde has not read that pad since the fourth of September and is not going to.

He had been doing arithmetic since the Friday morning and it was the fourth Friday running.

> *Seventeenth of April.*
> *Everything I know about the drift is a thing I worked out from the outside. A state given on a Tuesday can be wrong on the Wednesday. A counter that is required to answer and is not required to make one goes on answering the same state for as long as nobody comes back to argue it. There is no obligation in this city to come back. A person turning up is not an instrument, it is a coincidence, and a coincidence every week is a service.*
> *Every word of that is the outside. I have never once seen what it looks like from the side that has the paper.*

He put the pen down and picked it up again.

> *And I cannot see it. That is the finding before the finding. A slip can only be produced by the person holding it. The person holding it is not findable from any record in this city, and there is no record, and there is not going to be one, and that is the design and not an accident, and I built half of it.*

---

**And the man who was holding one came back on the Monday morning at about a quarter past ten, and he had come back for a reason that had nothing to do with a trough.**

He was about sixty. He had a coat on that was not the coat he had worn on the sixteenth of March. He put a hand into the inside of it and took something out and put it on the counter, which is the first time anybody has put anything on that counter in nine months that was not a question.

It was about four inches by two. It was folded once. It had a date on it and a hand on it and nothing else — no reference, no number, no crest, no form, no box and no name on it, including hers.

He put it down flat and turned it round so that the writing was the right way up for her, which is a thing a person does with a thing they have been carrying for five weeks and have taken out about four times in that period and have not known what to do with.

"**Is that a receipt.**"

---

Nina Calder looked at it and did not touch it.

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**I have had it five weeks. I have not been back. I do not know what it is and I am not going to be told by anybody else, and my wife has asked me twice what it is for and I have said I would find out, and I have not found out, and I have not thrown it away, and I would like to know now whether it means I owe somebody something, because if it does I would like to pay it and be rid of it.**"

"**It is not a receipt.**"

"**Then what is it.**"

"**It is a state. It says what was true on a day, in a hand, and there is no name on it and there is no number on it and there is no form number and no box, and it was not given to you for any purpose that requires you to do anything, and you are not obliged to do anything with it, and you are not able to do anything with it.**"

"**Then why have I got it.**"

"**Because you stood in front of me and asked me what was true on a day, and I wrote it down, and I am not required to and there is nobody who can make me.**"

---

**And she gave him the rest of it, and it took about eleven seconds, and it is the whole of what a piece of paper from that counter actually is, and she has never said it in a room before.**

"**You cannot pay it, because there is nothing to pay. You cannot return it, because there is nobody to return it to who is required to receive it. You cannot file it, because it has no reference and there is no box anywhere in this building that would take it. And you cannot throw it away, and I want you to understand that that one is not me. Throwing a piece of paper away is a decision about a document, and a person is supposed to be able to make that decision in about four seconds, and you cannot make this one in four seconds, and so it will be in your coat.**"

He looked at it on the counter for a while.

"**So what do I do with it.**"

"**Nothing. Carry it.**"

"**That is not doing nothing with it. That is doing something with it for ever.**"

"**Yes,**" she said. "**And I am not going to improve that, and I have never written one of them down twice and I am not going to start, and there is a person in this room who is not on the agenda and has not asked me anything and I am speaking to you and not to him, and you are entitled to know why, and the why is that if I answer this on a Tuesday then I have made a service of it, and I would not know on Wednesday what I had done on Tuesday.**"

---

He did not argue. He stood there about four seconds, and then he said, "**Then I am sorry I asked.**"

"**You did not ask. You asked me what it is and I told you. Nobody in this borough has ever said sorry to me at that counter and I am not going to make a thing of it and I would rather you did not either.**"

He put the slip back in the inside of his coat, and it went in the inside of the coat where it had been, and that was the end of it, and he went out into the street with it, and nobody helped him and nobody was harmed.

---

**And Jonas Mercer was four feet away at the wall and had not asked a question and did not ask one then, and what he wrote in the pad that night is the only thing he has produced in nine months that he has not improved since, and it is the inside of the drift.**

> *Twentieth of April, the vestibule, ten past ten.*
> *A state given on a day stops being a state about a day and becomes an object in a coat, and the moment it becomes an object it needs a procedure, and there is no procedure on the sheet, because there is no name box and no number and no box at all, and a document with no procedure cannot be paid or returned or filed or destroyed.*
> *And so it goes in a pocket. And nobody comes back to argue it, because a man with a piece of paper in a coat has no way of knowing whether he has been told something true, and the only way to know is to go back and ask, and going back is a decision, and nobody is required to make a decision.*
> *THAT IS THE DRIFT, AND IT IS NOT A THING THE COUNTER DOES. It is a thing that happens to a person who is handed a correct answer and given nothing to do with it.*

---

**And here is the whole of the thing, and it took a fortnight to get to and it is four lines, and it is the finding of this month, and it is not going on a wall.**

**A register that grows when nobody is hurt is a register somebody can hold over you. Nina Calder said that on the first of December and did not say it to me and I did not write it down and I have been carrying it since anyway.**

**A drawer of slips grows exactly the same way. Out of correct things done by careful people. Nobody is hurt by it. Nobody is helped by it. It grows at the rate of a person walking through a door and standing at a counter and asking a question that somebody is not required to ask, and it was nine pieces of paper on the ninth of March, and it is more than nine now, and the figure is not in any book and is not on the back of anybody's hand, and I have not counted it, and I am not going to, and she is not going to tell me.**

**And the difference between the register and the drawer is one line long: nobody ever wrote a rule for the person.**

The register has nineteen entries and a blank line under the nineteenth, and the blank line has been blank since the twenty-sixth of November, which is a hundred and forty-five days today, and there is no rule on that board about who may write under the nineteenth, and no rule that the person standing in front of it is required to be told that they may.

The drawer has no line. Not a blank one. **A person has no line at all.**

---

**And the arithmetic of the two things, done properly, and this is the part he has not written anywhere but the pad.**

Nine slips is a count of documents. About eleven people a day is a count of people. **They are two numbers about two different things and they may never be added together, and the reason is hers and was given out loud on the second of February: a slip is only written when somebody stands in front of her and asks what was true on a day, and a form that added the two together would be a form that said *this counter sees eleven people and produces one document*, and that is a level of service, and there is no level of service in that room.**

**And the second thing in that arithmetic is a plastic wallet in the glovebox of a van he does not own, and nobody in this borough can say how many pieces of paper are in it, and that is correct, and the reason it is correct is that a man of thirty-eight is not findable from any record in this city and has not asked anybody anything since the sixth of February.**

Two drawers, in two boroughs, in two vehicles that belong to nobody who is keeping them. **And both of them are growing, and neither of them has a rule on it, and in about four years somebody in this city is going to be able to say how many.**

And how many is a capacity.

**A capacity is a promise. The day there is a number of what a counter does, there is a morning somebody can say *we can only do eight today* and be entirely correct, and the cutting will not be done by anybody cruel. It will be done by a number.** Nobody has to cut anything. The number does it by existing. And a number of that kind does not have to be anybody's fault, and that is what makes it worse than a fault.

**And nobody is going to cut it, and nobody is going to be cruel, and in about four years there is going to be a number.**

---

He read that back on the Monday night in the shop and did not tear it out, and Beatrice Osunde, who does not read it, said one thing while she shut a till.

"**You have got the face of a man who has found a thing he cannot put down.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**Then I am not going to be told what it is and I am not going to be told what you are going to do about it, and I am going to say one thing and then I am going to lock up.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**In about four years it is not going to be you that it matters to, is it. It is going to be some poor devil of a frightened person in a kitchen, and somebody is going to put something on that table, and it is not going to be the bulb.**"

---

**And here is the last thing about the drift, and it is the part that will not leave him, and he wrote it on the Tuesday and he has not improved it and it is four lines and it is the last paragraph of that page.**

**A slip in a drawer is going to be evidence one day.**

Not his evidence. Not a record of a service. **A piece of paper that somebody kept because they could not think what else to do with it, handed over by a person who thinks they are helping.**

Somebody will open that drawer, one day, for a reason that is entirely good — because a woman is frightened, or a man has been asked a question, or somebody has come with a piece of paper of their own and needs a piece of paper in return, and the most obvious thing in the world to hand them is the thing you have.

**And the thing you have will have a date on it and a hand on it and no name on it, and it will be true, and it will be about a trough and a tap on a yard wall and a standpipe at the bottom of a car park, and it will prove that somebody in this borough wrote something down, on a day, for no reason, and told nobody.**

And there is no version of that piece of paper in which the person it was written for is in it. **Because they were not. Because they are never in it. Because there is no name box and there is not going to be one, and that is the whole of what this borough has been for nine months and it is going to be a piece of paper on a table in a kitchen in about four years, in a hand nobody will be able to read, about a trough.**

**And nobody is going to be able to say afterwards whether it was a service or a choice, and neither is he, and that is the cost of it, and he has known the price of it since the twenty-sixth of January and has never written it down and is not going to.**
