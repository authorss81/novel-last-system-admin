# Chapter 0131 — A Thing That Is True Twice

Saturday the fourteenth of March 2015, at ten o'clock at night, a waiting area off a corridor on level two, Saint Orra west. And Monday the sixteenth of March, from about ten past ten in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward.

**There was no question of his in either room and there was no question of his in the corridor, and he has not asked one since the twenty-fifth of February, and the counting has been right in every week of this year. There was one question of somebody else's, in a public room, at about half past ten on the Monday morning, and it was not his, and it is the fourth person who has stood at that counter and asked about a thing and not about himself.**

---

At ten o'clock on the Saturday night, two men were in a waiting area with four chairs in it and a machine for hot water at the end of it that has not worked since about August.

**Nothing was written down. There is no minute, because that is not a body that minutes anything. There is no entry in any log, and the log is a clipboard with a hardboard back and a sheet of A4 clipped to it and eleven rows with the first four written on at some point in October by a man who then stopped, and it is on the fourth chair, and it was not touched.**

The two men were Ezra Mbeki, who has been in and out of that building since 1992, and Trevor Nunn, 58, a night porter, nineteen years, who is not on a roster because the roster does not exist. They read a term that runs six days. It began at seven o'clock the next morning. It expires at six o'clock on Sunday the twenty-second.

**That was the eighth of them in seven weeks, and there is no date written down anywhere in this city for it, and there is no date written down for the seven before it, and the reason is that a person is not required to be told and there is nobody in this borough who is required to write it down.**

Jonas Mercer was four hundred yards away at ten o'clock on the Saturday night and he had decided to be, and he had decided it on the Friday, and he did not telephone anybody and nobody telephoned him, and the four hundred yards is a piece of wet pavement by a wall at the end of a street with a bus turning head at one end of it, and he has stood on it twice, in the rain, in January, and once in the dark in December, and Bea Osunde does not know that he goes there at all.

Neither Mbeki nor Nunn knows that a man refused out loud, in a corridor, in daylight, on the second of March, to be in that room on the seventh of March, and neither of them is going to be told, and there is no way in this borough to tell them, and that is not a failure of a records system. There is no records system.

---

On the Monday morning Jonas Mercer came in off the street at about ten past ten and stood at the east end of a corridor that is forty-one feet long, and he did not go to the counter, and he did not ask anything, and the reason he did not go to the counter is that he had not come for a state and he had not come for a date.

**A counter is required to answer. A counter is not required to make one. Those are two different jobs and this borough has money for neither of them and a person for one of them, and he has understood that since the second of February and has been living on it.**

He stood there a while and then he went down a corridor he does not usually go down, off the main one, past a fire door that is on a hold-open wedge that a man of sixty-one put on the floor in May, and he came back past the counter about ten to eleven, and while he was gone a man came and went.

---

The man was about sixty. He had a galvanised can of about ten litres under his arm and he had not been in that vestibule in his life and did not know what a records counter was.

He was in the room about two minutes before he said it, and he said it to her, and Jonas Mercer was four feet away at the wall and heard all of it.

"**I want to know what was in the trough by the lock-ups.**"

Nina Calder put her pen down.

"**Say the day.**"

"**Say what you mean by the day.**"

"**A state is only worth anything with a day on it, and I am not writing one for a thing without a day, because a thing without a day is a thing somebody is about to be told, and the day is the whole of the part that is any use to him.**"

"**The second of February.**"

"**Then that is a fair question and it has a fair answer and it is about eleven seconds.**"

---

"**On the second of February, at about ten past ten in the morning, the trough by the lock-ups was not frozen, and there was about two inches of something green in the bottom of it, and it was not water.**"

The man did not write it down. He had a can under his arm and nowhere to put a pen.

"**That is what was in it.**"

"**That is what was in it on the second of February, and that is what the man who asked me put on the second of February, and those are two different sentences and the difference is the whole of what I am able to give anybody.**"

"**Are those different things.**"

"**They are not different things this morning. They will be, in about four years, and it will be the second sentence that does it.**"

---

He put the can down on the floor, which nobody does in that room, and he stood over it.

"**Then it is not much use to me, is it.**"

"**No,**" said Nina Calder. "**And I am not going to improve it, and I have got three halves to this and you are going to get all of them, and then you are going to go and do whatever it is you were going to go and do, and I am not going to ask you what that is either.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Half one: the trough by the lock-ups had not run since about the twenty-second of December, and it had been frozen for the whole of that time, and it came back on its own before the second of February and there was two inches of green in the bottom of it, and that green was not one thing. It was what a market has been putting in a trough for forty years, settling out of water that came off a roof. I have never looked at that trough. There is no record of what is in any trough in this borough and there never was and if I looked it up then I would be a records counter that has told a man whether to put water in a can, and that is a service.**"

"**Half two.**"

"**Half two: the man who asked me on the second of February was standing in front of me at ten past ten with a bowser to fill and a lunchtime to decide by, and he told me what he could see from where he was standing, which is a thing men do, and I wrote it down because he asked me what was true on a day, and I have not been asked to make a state and I am not required to.**"

"**And half three.**"

"**Half three: what you want to know is whether you could have taken water out of it in February, and that was never on the piece of paper and it never could be, and there is nobody in this city I could ask that, and if there were, I would be a service, and I would not know on Tuesday what I had done on Monday.**"

---

The man picked the can up off the floor.

"**Then I will not put the drum in.**"

"**That is the answer to the question you did not ask, and I have not written it down and there is nothing on the counter to write it on.**"

He said, "**What was in it was mine.**"

And she said, "**No it was not, and I have not said that to you as a fact, and if I had said it as a fact I would be a records counter with an opinion about a trough and I would rather you did not have that either, and there is no form in this borough on which I could tell you what was in a trough on a Tuesday in February and there is not going to be one.**"

Then he went out into the street with the can and the answer, and nobody helped him and nobody was harmed and he was not going to drink out of a trough in February and he was not going to in March either, and neither was anybody else.

---

**And the asking was recorded by nobody, and the not-answering was correct, and neither of those is a service, and a man of sixty walked out of a vestibule eleven feet by nine with a can and a sentence, and one of the four people in this borough who has asked that counter about a thing is not on any list and has never come again.**

And what happened in that room, and it happened in about eleven seconds, and the whole of it is the first time in six weeks that anybody in this borough has seen what a state does when it is not a state.

**A slip is a record of a person's account of a thing. It is not a record of the thing. There is nobody in this city who can require a records supervisor to say what a thing is, and so the only thing anybody can be given is what a man saw from where he was standing at ten past ten on a Tuesday, and that is a true sentence, and it is in a hand, and it has a date on it, and it is wrong.**

And he worked out the rest of it standing at a wall four feet from a counter he had not gone to, and it took him no longer than the walk to the end of that corridor and back, and he has written it out in a pad in a shop on the Monday night and it is four lines and there is no form on which he could put it.

> *Sixteenth of March.*
> *A state that has gone stale belongs to a thing. The trough is a thing. It is out there. Anybody may look at it. A thing can be wrong and a thing can be looked at again, and there is no person standing between the wrongness and the looking, and that is as close as this borough gets to a correction and it has no form in it.*
> *A state that has been contradicted belongs to a person. It cannot be looked at, because the only person who can contradict it is the man who said it, and he was standing in front of her, and he has not come back, and there is nobody who could ask him to, and a person may not be required to answer anything, including a correction.*
> *So: one has an owner and the other has a person, and this borough has no instrument for either, and the first kind rots and the second kind cannot be moved at all, and the second kind is the one that is in a drawer under that counter.*

---

**And the whole of the drift, in one sentence, and he did not say it out loud to anybody and is not going to: a slip gets written when a man asks what was true on a day, and a man is the only instrument there has ever been, and a man is a coincidence, and a coincidence that comes back every fortnight is a service, and the man with the bowser came on the twenty-sixth of January and on the second of February and on the thirteenth of February and has not come since, and it is a Friday, and it will be a Friday, and if he comes on a Friday for another four years then that is not a man, that is a rota with one name on it that nobody wrote down and that nobody could write down.**

He has not got a kitchen. He has got a bench at the end of a shop and a woman oiling it who has been in that street thirty-one years and who has refused to tell him he is brave four times.

"**Say the bit you are not going to say.**"

"**There is no bit I am not going to say.**"

"**Mr Mercer. There is always a bit you are not going to say, and I have known you since the fourth of September and I have watched you leave a sentence in a room nine times.**"

"**Then here it is. I worked out on the second of February that a counter drifts, and I wrote it down and I have been carrying it for six weeks as though it were a thing that had a date on it, and it turns out that is what I have been carrying. It is not a drift. It is a piece of paper with a man's account of a Tuesday on it, and the man is the instrument, and the instrument does not come back.**"

Beatrice Osunde put the ash down, which is the thing she does when she is about to say something she has said before.

"**And what are you going to do about it.**"

"**Nothing.**"

"**Say why not, and do not stand there.**"

"**Because the only thing available to me is to go and find the people who were there on a day and ask them whether they are sure, and that is a person standing in a corridor asking another person about a day, and in about four years there would be a list of the days people were sure about, and it would have my name on the top of it as the man who asked, and nobody could fill it in, and in about four years after that there would be a form.**"

He did not say the rest of it and she did not ask him for the rest of it, and she has never once asked him for the rest of it.

---

**And on the second floor of the records office there is a tray, and there has been a tray there since before anybody in this borough started arguing about floors, and a tray is where post goes and that is the whole of what a tray is for.**

There is a thing under the post in that tray. It came in on Wednesday the eighteenth of February at about half past nine, in an envelope that was not on borough stock, with a departmental reference on it in the correct typeface, and it is about four lines long, and it is not a reason column, and it is the smallest request anybody in this borough has made since the first of March.

It has been there twenty-six days.

**And in the last two or three days somebody has started to leave post on top of it, which is the oldest way in the world of saying that a thing has not been dealt with, and it is not a form, and it is not a minute, and nobody in that office has said one word about it, and nobody is going to, and Jonas Mercer did not go up and did not ask and does not know what is under it and is never going to.**

---

**And the register of narrowings is at nineteen entries, and the blank line under the nineteenth is blank, and it has been blank since Wednesday the twenty-sixth of November, which is a hundred and ten days today, and it did not move once this week and it did not move once in the last ten chapters and it is the only fixed point in the building.**

Entry nineteen is a yard light. The maintenance is a bulb. The bulb is out.

The card in the slot in the counter has been in it since the twentieth of February and nobody has taken it out and nobody has put it up. There is a drawer under that counter with no key on it and no list of what is in it and about eleven people a day through the door, and those are two numbers about two different things and they must never be added together, and a form that added them would be a level of service, and there is no level of service in that room.

**And a man of sixty walked out of it at about eleven with a can and a sentence, and a man of fifty-eight has a slip in an inside pocket that is seven weeks old and has not been looked at since the day he wrote it down, and neither of those two men is on any list, and neither of them was asked anything today, and the second one was in this borough in January and will probably be in it again in April, and if he is, then in about four years there will be a man who comes on a Friday, and nobody will have written any of it down, and that is not a failure. That is the whole of the arrangement.**
