# Chapter 0132 — The Man Who Turned The Bucket

Wednesday the eighteenth of March 2015, about ten past ten in the morning, a corridor, ground floor, the Old Ward. And Friday the twentieth of March, about ten past three in the afternoon, the same corridor, and the same afternoon in a shop at the end of it.

**There was no question of his in the corridor on the Friday afternoon and there was no question of his in the shop and there was no question of anybody's in either, and he has not asked one since the twenty-fifth of February, and the counting has been right in every week of this year.**

---

The whole of what anybody in this borough knows about the man is one sentence, and the sentence was said out loud in a room in daylight on the thirteenth of January by a man of fifty-eight who has had a stand at the top of the lowland market for twenty-six years and got there at five in the morning with a bucket, two urns and no drain.

He gave the state of five of the six water points in about two minutes without stopping, and it was the best testimony that hall has had in four months and nothing in it was wrong.

One of them was a stand with a bucket on a chain and a board of forty slots that works because a man puts money in the slot and takes the bucket and washes it and puts it back.

"And **Jonas Mercer could not tell you his name either.**"

That was said on the fourteenth of January. It is still true on the eighteenth of March, and it is the only thing anybody knows, and nobody has been asked anything about it since.

**He is not findable from any record in this city and has never been. There is no party, no reference, no holder field, no name box, no return, no rota, no minute and no form on which his name could be written, and he has been standing in a street every day of his life for a length of time that is not recorded anywhere either, and the reason there is no record of him is not that he was missed. It is that nothing in this borough has ever had to write down a man who turns a bucket.**

---

**A counter is the cheapest privacy device anybody in this city has ever built and it cost nothing and nobody designed it and there is no form for it anywhere.**

Think about what a counter does. There is a counter along the left of that vestibule, about three feet of wood between a person and a person, and the wood is the whole of it. A man can come in off the street with a question or a payment or a complaint and he can be answered by somebody he cannot see past, and he can leave, and nobody in that room has to be able to describe his face, because nobody in that room is required to remember it, and nothing about him is required to go anywhere, and that is a wall, and it is the cheapest wall in this borough and it is made of nothing.

The man at the board of forty slots has no wall.

**He has to take the money.** That is the whole of it. There is no counter between him and the street because there is no way to put one there that would work: a slot in a pillar takes coins, and coins do not have to be handed to anybody, and everybody who has ever used that stand has done exactly that, and the man whose business it is stands at that pillar anyway, in the open, at a bus turning head, at the hour the buses come, on the same ground, the way a man has stood at the same ground since before anybody in this borough was keeping records of anything.

He is the most findable man in the lowland and he has been for twenty-six years and nobody did it.

---

Jonas Mercer stood at a wall in a corridor a while on the Wednesday morning and worked out that the sentence he had said out loud in a room on the thirteenth of March had a second half he had not said, and the second half is worse.

> *I said in that room on the thirteenth of March what it costs, and I was right, and I have been carrying it round for a week as though I had built it.*
> *I did not build it. A counter is a wall. A price is a hole in a wall. The man who turns that bucket is findable because nobody made him buy anything at a table from behind a screen, and that has got nothing to do with me and it has got nothing to do with the twenty-sixth of January, and I have been minting findability since the third of April by saying sentences in rooms, and I thought I was the one doing it.*
> *The lowland has been findable for twenty-six years and the reason is that the water costs money.*

---

**And here is the part that stops his hand, and he worked it out at about ten past eleven on the Wednesday morning in a corridor forty-one feet long, standing still, with a fire door on a wedge behind him.**

The stand is one of the six. The six are in paragraph 6.1 of the minute of the nineteenth of May, recorded as received and not as evidence, eleven thousand two hundred and eighty pounds a year, and nobody has been asked to pay any part of it.

**A green pillar, a bucket on a chain, a board of forty slots. That is the shape. The shape is in a minute, in a paragraph, with a number in it, and it is signed by nobody and has no name box on it, and a man in a corridor in a haversack in nine seconds could go from paragraph 6.1 of the nineteenth of May to a bus turning head and stand there, and there would be a man in front of him, every day, for as long as the pillar stands.**

Nobody would have to spend eleven seconds. Nobody would have to invent anything. The handle is already cut, and it is not new, and it was cut by a price, and the price was not the borough's and it is not a service and it is not a form and it is not on anybody's instrument.

And if somebody went and looked, and stood there, and asked a man of sixty or sixty-five what his name was, then that man would be in a corridor with a man of thirty-two who has civic standing and no grade, and there is no form on which that would be written, and in about four years there would be a list of people that man had spoken to, and it would be the fourth list, and he has three already, and he is not going to make a fourth.

---

**And that is the finding, and it is not a scheme, and it is the first time in nine months that he has stood in a corridor with a thing he has built and refused to use it, and the method is his own and it is the only thing he has built this year.**

**The finder is the handle.**

For eight months he has made two men findable by saying things about them in rooms, and a man of forty-four is a wall and a man of forty-nine is a door, and neither of them was in a room. Beatrice Osunde told him in December that a handle is cheap and that a receipt is not an apology, and she was talking about a bill, and she was right, and he has been carrying the sentence and not the thing underneath it.

**The thing underneath it is that the cheapest handle in this city is not a date. It is a person walking up to a pillar and being handed a bucket. And the most expensive thing anybody can do to a man who already has one is to go and introduce yourself.**

So he did not go. He did not go on the Wednesday, and he did not go on the Thursday, and he did not go on the Friday, and nobody asked him to and there was nothing in any day of that week that said a man had chosen anything at all.

**And the stand went on working, and the water went on costing what it costs, and the man went on being findable, and nobody was helped, and nobody was hurt, and the sixth of the six is still not named in any document that leaves the street, and the reason a man of fifty-eight may not be asked where it is has not changed.**

---

On the Friday afternoon, at about ten past three, he was in the corridor at the east end with his back to the wall, and Nina Calder came down to the counter the way she comes down to the counter, which is twice a day and because nobody has ever told her to.

He did not go over. He did not ask her anything. **He counted the people through the door between about ten past three and about half past three, on the back of his left hand, and then he stopped, and the reason he stopped is the whole of the reason he was there.**

He counted five in about twenty minutes, which is not a daily figure and is not going to be turned into one. He did not write the figure down anywhere. He had it in his head and then he had it not in his head, deliberately, the way you put a thing down when you have just worked out that putting it down is the first half of picking it up.

**Because a count of people through a door is a level of service the moment anybody can be held to it, and the moment a man can be held to it there is a morning somebody can say *we can only do eight today* and be entirely correct, and the whole of what that counter is doing in eleven feet by nine is that nobody is holding anybody to anything, and he came within about nine seconds of being the man who started.**

He has known that number since about the sixth of March. It is on the back of her hand and it is on the back of his and it is in no book, and nobody in this borough can be asked to confirm it, and neither of them is going to be asked.

---

**And then there is the other half of it, and it is the only reason he came down to that corridor on the Friday, and it is a thing about the drift and not about a man.**

About eleven people a day come through that door. Between the sixth of March and the twentieth of March, four people have stood at that counter and asked about a thing, and three of them are men who have all come more than once, and the fourth came on the sixteenth and asked about a day and was told a thing about a day and will not come again.

**The people who come in are not the people who come back, and there is no way of telling the difference from inside the room, and that is not a defect. It is the reason the room works.**

Somebody who asks a question about a day has already decided that a date is a thing worth having, and that decision takes somebody about four months and a trough, and it is not something a counter can hand out, and it is not something anybody in this borough has ever handed anybody, and if the counter handed it out then it would be a service and it would scale and it would be a thing you could buy.

Somebody who does not ask is not refusing anything. He has a can, or a drum, or a bag, or a route, and he is in a hurry, and a man stands in that vestibule for about a minute and leaves with nothing, and that is most of what the room is, and it has never been written down, and it is not going to be.

**And the drift is invisible from in there, and it is invisible for a reason nobody wants to hear, and the reason is that the only people who could contradict a state are the people who have already asked once. The eleven do not come back. The three do. And a contradiction requires a second visit, and a second visit is a decision, and nobody in this borough is required to decide anything at all.**

So the state on the twenty-sixth of January has been in an inside pocket in a coat for seven weeks and nobody has come back to argue with it, and there is no obligation in this city to come back, and a person turning up is not an instrument. It is a coincidence. And a coincidence that comes back every fortnight is a service.

**And a man of fifty-eight has a bowser and a man on the bowser paid out of his own pocket on Fridays, and he is the only one of the three whose money somebody else depends on, and he has not been to that counter in five weeks, and nobody in this borough is entitled to ask him why, and Jonas Mercer is not going to ask him, and there is no form on which anybody could ask him.**

---

He wrote it in the shop on the Friday night, on the back page of a pad, and Beatrice Osunde was cutting a piece of ash and did not read it, which is the reason he writes there.

> *Twentieth of March.*
> *Findability is not a thing this borough has been minting. Findability is what a price does when there is no counter in front of it. I have spent eight months saying sentences in rooms and telling myself I was making people findable, and the man who turns the bucket at the turning head has been findable for twenty-six years and the reason is that the water costs four pounds and a man has to take the money with his own hand in the open.*
> *And I have worked out today that I could not go and stand there. Not because it is dangerous and not because he would not have spoken to me. Because I would have become the second thing that ever happened to him, and the first one is a price, and a price is a handle, and I would have been a handle with a date on it and my name on it.*

She read that last line twice, which she has never done about anything he has written, and then she said one thing and she said it once and she went back to the bench.

"**Then say the other half of it, because you have written the half that lets you off.**"

"**There is no other half.**"

"**There is a priced-access case and a recognition case and they are not the same thing and you know that and I am not going to explain it to you in a shop. One of those two men is visible because water costs money. The other one of those two men is visible because a woman in a hall said a shape out loud in a room and nine other people were listening. They are not the same shape and they are not the same man and if you ever write a sentence that puts them in the same list then the list is a list of people somebody can ring, and I have been in this street thirty-one years and I have watched that happen to about four people and I am not going to watch it happen to a man who turns a bucket for nothing.**"

"**I am not going to write the sentence.**"

"**Good. Now put the ash away, it is getting late, and you have not eaten and it is half past five.**"

---

**And nothing in that week cost anybody anything, and the man at the board of forty slots is still not findable from any record in this city, and is not on any list, and has not been asked anything, and will not be, and the water still costs what it costs, and a priced-access case is not a recognition case and that was not argued and is not going to be argued and has been the position since the second of June.**

**And the register of narrowings is at nineteen entries, and the blank line under the nineteenth is blank, and it has been blank for a hundred and fourteen days, and entry nineteen is a yard light and the maintenance is a bulb and the bulb is out, and a card has been in the slot in that counter since the twentieth of February and nobody has taken it out and nobody has put it up.**

**And a counter answers in about eleven seconds, and gives a state instead of a list, and a date, and there is no form on which anybody could write that these are not a series, and a box that said *not a series* would be a series, and the room is eleven feet by nine, and there is no level of service in it, and the day there is one somebody can cut it.**

**And the man is not the only one.**
