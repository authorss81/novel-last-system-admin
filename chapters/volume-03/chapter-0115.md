# Chapter 0115 — The Smallest Possible Room, Before Six O'Clock

**Saturday the third of January 2015, four minutes to ten at night, the corridor outside level two, Saint Orra west.**

**Jonas Mercer was four hundred yards away, on foot, and he was not going to come, and the reason is the reason and not a rule, and it took him about eleven seconds standing in the rain at the bottom of a service road where there is no pavement.**

If he is in that corridor at ten o'clock on the last night before a term expires, then for the rest of his life there is a version of that corridor with him in it. And the two people who do it — and they will do it, because they do it, because that is the thing they do — will be able to say *he was there*. And a thing that two tired people can say in about four years is a rota with one name on it, and that is the exact object he has spent eight months refusing to be, and it cost a bank four tonnes of stone to know it.

**So he does not go, and he does not telephone anybody to ask whether it went, and ringing is the same as going, and he stands in the rain for about four minutes and then goes home.**

---

**Sunday the fourth of January 2015, about half past four in the morning.**

He walked. It is about fifty minutes from the shop in the rain and it was about two degrees and he did it because there is no bus and because he has not got a car.

**The smallest possible room in this city is not eleven feet by nine.** Eleven feet by nine has a counter in it and a board and a slot for post. The smallest possible room is off a corridor on level two at Saint Orra west and it is a waiting area with four chairs in it and a machine for hot water that has not worked since about August, and it has no door, and it is not a room anybody has ever thought to call anything, and it is where the two people have to be at ten o'clock at night, and it has been that room since the first week of April.

He got there at about twenty-five past four.

**The term in force had one hour and thirty-five minutes left in it.** The one that had been read aloud at ten o'clock on Saturday the twenty-seventh of December at the last handover, which had begun at seven o'clock on Sunday the twenty-eighth of December and had run its six days, and a term is six days and it means six whole days and not the seventh morning: Monday to Saturday, and then six o'clock on the Sunday, when the next one takes over at seven. The rota has still not been rebuilt and there is no instrument in this borough by which the terms could be anything else.

The one that had been read at ten o'clock last night began at seven o'clock this morning.

**Between six o'clock and seven o'clock this morning there is no term at all.** That is not a fault anybody has been told about. It is arithmetic. A term is six days. Six days from seven o'clock on Sunday the fourth of January is six o'clock on Sunday the eleventh of January, and the gate opens at seven, and once a week, every Sunday morning, for an hour, there is a door in this borough that exists because a person decided to be there, and in about four years somebody is going to write a schedule for that hour and the schedule will be good.

---

There was a light on in the corridor and there were two people in it, and neither of them was on anything, because there is nothing to be on.

**EZRA MBEKI, who has been in and out of that building since 1992 and who is one of four people in this city who could read a handover aloud, and TREVOR NUNN, 61, a night porter, twenty-two years, who is not on a roster because the roster does not exist.**

Neither of them had seen Jonas Mercer and neither of them was looking for him. Trevor Nunn was sitting in one of the four chairs with his coat on over his uniform. Ezra Mbeki was standing at the wall where the board is.

He did not go into the room. He stood about eleven feet back in the corridor, at the other end, out of the light, with nothing in his hands, and he stayed there until about half past six, which is two hours and five minutes, and he did not ask one question, and the counting has been right in every chapter of this year and there was no question in the corridor and there was not going to be one.

**On the wall, on a board that has been up since the second of April and that nobody has rebuilt, in a plastic wallet, there is a printed sheet with the days down it and the hours down it and the boxes in it, and it is the rota, and the rota is the thing that is missing.**

The eleven o'clock to half past one row is empty. It has been empty since the second of April. It is not shaded and it does not say *vacant*, and it is the only row on that sheet with nothing in it at all, and it is in the same typeface as every other row, and anybody coming up that corridor reads it every morning and has read it every morning for nine months and has not been asked about it.

**On the Thursday row there is a name written and then nothing underneath it except two lines, one in the hand of the man and one in somebody else's:**

> **B. OSEI. NO.**
> *no reason I can give*
> *(no reason given)*

That is what a refusal looks like when it is put on a board by a body that cannot require anybody and that has decided — in a corridor, by a doctor, in June — that it is going to print a bad refusal exactly as it was given to it rather than tidy it. **The second row underneath it is not empty. It is a refusal that has not been asked for, and somebody has written in it that no reason was given, and that line was written by somebody else, and he did not ask them to write it, and he has asked twice that it not be looked at.**

**And on the Friday and Saturday rows there is a card in a plastic sleeve that has been there since June, and it is Priya Ranganathan's, and she put it there herself, and it stays there because it is hers and because she has a daughter of eleven and because the line is not funded from the first of June and has not been since, and the card says so and does not say why.**

---

**At about ten to six somebody put a clipboard on the fourth chair.**

It was a hardboard board with a sheet of A4 clipped to it and a printed heading and rows, and it was the kind of thing that gets left out at night for whoever is in at the start, and it had eleven rows and the first four had been written on at some point in October by a man who had then stopped.

Jonas Mercer did not touch it.

He looked at it for about four seconds and then he looked at the wall, and the reason he did not touch it is the reason he has not written a name on anything since March, and it is not a principle: **a log with one name in it is a rota with one name in it, and the man who fills in the log becomes the man who is required to fill in the log, and the day that happens the man who stopped in October is a man who let somebody down, and in about four years that clipboard is in a drawer and somebody is going to use it to find out who he was.**

He has been asked to explain that in a corridor twice this year and he has explained it twice, in the same words, and both times to somebody who did not want an explanation.

**At six o'clock the term expired.** Nothing happened at six o'clock. There was no alarm and there was no beep and there was no light and nobody came down a corridor with a piece of paper, and that is the sentence, and it is the whole of what nine months has been for.

**At about a quarter to seven a woman came up the ramp from the ambulance bay and the gate went, and it went because somebody was standing at it, and she had a key and a job and a department, and she had not been asked to be there either and there is no box anywhere that says she had to be.**

---

**And here is the only move anybody in this borough had on the morning of the fourth of January 2015, and it is not a move, and it cost nothing, and it is the only thing that worked.**

**The gate did not open at seven because of anything a man did in a corridor at half past four in the morning, and it is worth being exact about that, because a chapter like this one exists to be misunderstood.** It opened at seven because of ten o'clock the night before — because at ten o'clock on Saturday the third of January, in a waiting area with four chairs and a dead hot-water machine, two people who are not on any instrument in this city read five lines out loud to each other, and then sat down, and then went to bed.

**A man who cannot be required to do anything is the only person in that building who can be there without being a service.** That is what he has been for eight months, and it is worth nothing, and it is the only thing that works, and there is no instrument that can put him on the board that says the rota, because there is no board, and if there were a board he would be asked to sit on it, and if he sat on it he would be a member, and a member can be asked things, and a man who can be asked things is a door.

---

**Nobody in this borough is going to be told that it worked.**

There is no document about Saturday the third of January. There is no minute, because that is not a body that minutes anything. There is no entry in any log, because the log is on a clipboard and he did not touch it and nobody asked him to and the two men in the corridor went home at about half past six. There is no date anywhere in this city on a piece of paper that says the handover for the term beginning at seven o'clock on Sunday the fourth of January 2015 was read at ten o'clock on Saturday the third of January 2015 by two people, and **there is not going to be one, and that is not an oversight, and the reason it is not an oversight is that it is nobody's job.**

**And it is the first time in this account that a thing has been got right by nobody writing it down.**

---

**And the forty minutes of the fifteenth of April is not the time the door took.** The forty minutes is the time until the family knew, and it belongs to a woman of eighty-four in bay six and to a laundry bag and to a bier up a ramp, and it is not about a door and it is not in this chapter and no other figure of minutes is going to appear in this account, ever, because there is no instrument in this city that measured it and there never was.

---

**It is January. The second half of the volume opens here, and the volume's question is a service floor and not a corridor, and nobody has built one yet.**

**The terms are six days. They have been six days since the first week of April. The rota has still not been rebuilt. The gate still runs on its local term and it cannot be renewed by the department, by a contractor, by a manager or by a rule mender with civic standing and no grade, and it cannot be renewed at all except by two people standing in a waiting area with four chairs and a machine for hot water that has not worked since August, at ten o'clock at night, on a Saturday, because nobody wrote a date on anything and therefore nobody has to turn up, and they turn up.**

**The next one expires at six o'clock on Sunday the eleventh of January, and the handover for it is on the Saturday night before, at ten o'clock, and there is no date written down anywhere for that Saturday night either, because a person is not required to be told and there is nobody in this borough who is required to write it.**
