# Chapter 0110 — The Floor, Again

**Monday the first of December 2014, ten past nine in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward.**

It is eleven feet by nine. There is a counter on the left with a slot in it for post, and the slot has been empty since the eighteenth of July, and there is a board on the wall at the end of it, and the register on that board has **nineteen entries** on it.

Jonas Mercer stood in front of it with his hands behind his back and read the last two of them four times, and then he read the seventeenth one, because he has read the seventeenth one about nine times since October and it has not got any better.

> **18.** *14 Nov 14. Node, riser space, rear block, Carrow Lane, Tideglass. Constructed 1938. Placed on a continuity schedule 29 Oct 14 for a condition survey, Tier 1, £1,880 per year. The survey instruction lapsed 12 Nov 14 and no answer to a return has been received. No party. Narrowed to its maintenance on 14 Nov 14. Holder field: none. Class per determination of 28 Aug 14.*
>
> **19.** *26 Nov 14. Node, yard light, rear yard, Carrow Lane, Tideglass. Fitted 1998. No party. Last verified essential use: none recorded. Narrowed to its maintenance on 26 Nov 14. Holder field: none. Maintenance is a bulb. The bulb is out.*

**Three times since the first of October, and every one of the three is correct, and he was in the room for one of them.**

He had known about the riser space on the Wednesday of the nineteenth of November, standing ninety feet back across a service alley in the cold, reading a sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet that said the freeholder's intention was to carry out a condition survey and report on the feasibility of restored supply. He had not known about the light until this morning, and the light is the funniest thing on the board, and it is also the truest, and there is nobody in this borough he is entitled to tell.

**The roof void is the other half of the same instruction and it is still on a schedule at a thousand eight hundred and eighty pounds a year, and one of a pair went and the other did not, and there is nobody in this city he is entitled to ask which of them is correct.**

At the bottom of the register, under the nineteenth line, there was a blank line. No rule, no caption, no label. It was blank on Monday morning and it was blank on Monday afternoon and it will be blank on the fourth of January, and nobody in this borough has asked me to promise that.

Above it, where anybody coming up the ground-floor corridor has to walk underneath, the two conditions from August were still there. The one on the city's own paper, printed 14/8 and initialled N.C. And sixteen inches below it the strip with nothing on its face at all, undated, unattributed, with two initials and one word in pencil on the back of it, and the word is *not mine*, and nobody is going to be told whose they are, including him, and he has been asked once and did not take it.

---

Nina Calder came in at about half past ten with a tray of correspondence on her knee and put it on the counter, and she had read the audit and the second record and the fourth page of nothing, and she stood down at the right-hand end of the board with her back to the post slot and looked at the register the way she looks at a thing she designed.

"It has grown three times," she said.

"Yes."

"Since the first of October, which is a Wednesday, and it had been still since the fourth of August, and I put that in a sentence in a corridor in September and you can check it." She did not point at anything. "**Three times in nine weeks, and it is the first time in the history of that board that it has moved three times, and every one of the three is right, and I want you to say the thing out loud that I have been not saying since Monday.**"

"A register that only grows when somebody is hurt is a register with a motive in it."

"A register that grows when nobody is hurt is a register with a motive in it *and* a register somebody can hold over you." She turned round and looked at the light in the ceiling. "**Mr Mercer, that is a bulb. That is a bulb in a yard behind a block of thirty rented rooms and it has been out since about the summer and nobody has been able to replace it and nobody is going to be, and the reason nobody can replace it is that there is no party for the yard. And the reason it is on that board at all is that a delay of three days and twenty hours ended at six o'clock in the morning on the fourth of July. It is on the same board as a re-asserted claim at a meter room, and if anybody in this borough ever uses this board against anybody, they will not use the meter room. They will use the bulb.**"

"They will use the bulb," said Jonas Mercer.

"Yes." She picked the tray up again. "And I built the absence of the box in March on purpose and I have said so out loud three times, and a design is not a promise, and this is the week the design got used twice and neither time by anybody who meant to."

---

They did the inventory, because he could not do it alone and she was the only person in the city who had read all of it, and neither of them was going to write it down.

"Left standing," she said, going down it the way she goes down a list. "The determination. The quarterly, with the number of times in it and the source of the number in the same paragraph. The record of shapes. The reading rule, which is out of the room now and cannot be got back in, and which a managing agent has refused a copy of for a reason that is entirely correct. **And the number in November, which is the first document in this city anybody can be held to, and the chair refused to adopt it and asked for the refusing to be minuted, and that is in the public record and is the best thing that Committee has done since June.**"

"Not standing. The letter is answered and nobody in this city is entitled to know what the answer said, the four meters did not go into the cupboard, and the price of it is a sheet of A4 on a door and a woman in room 14 who is a great deal more frightened than she was in May and no more findable."

"Yes. And there is a hall on Carrow Lane with two chairs empty in it, and one of them has been empty for fifty-six days, and there is a man of forty-eight who said in August that a job in November has a department in it, and there is not going to be a third vacancy, because a job in December has a Christmas in it."

"**And the one you did.**"

"Yes."

"You said out loud in a room on the twenty-first of November that six words were yours, and you said it in front of about ten people, and one of the things you said with them was the name of a man of forty-nine in Gantry Ward who lodges classifications and did not read that one before he lodged it. **He was not in the room. He has not been told. He cannot answer it, and there is no instrument in this city that will make anybody tell him.**"

"That is not the same as the man of forty-four."

"**It is not the same and it is not smaller, and that is what I want said in a corridor and not in a minute.** In August a man of forty-four could be connected to six words by a man with a register. In November a man of forty-nine could be connected to a sentence about what he did and did not read, by a man who is not even his friend. Neither of them was in the room. Neither of them has been told. And you cannot un-say either of those, and you know that you cannot, because you have spent five months building instruments that cost nothing and you have just used up the last one."

"I will not write it down."

"I know you will not. That is why I said it in a corridor and not in a minute."

---

At about two o'clock he walked forty minutes to a Methodist hall on Carrow Lane, and the hall was cold, and the piano lid was shut, and there is a trestle table at the back of it with the registrar's one-page statement on it in a plastic wallet.

There was a second thing on the trestle table. It was one sheet of A4, folded once, and it was face up, and it was not in a tray and not in a wallet and there was nothing behind it.

"**That came on Friday,**" said the woman who keeps the minutes.

She was standing about four feet away with her coat still on, and Jonas Mercer had seen her five times since the fourth of September and had never once been told who she was, and he noticed, standing there, that he did not know whether she was fifty or seventy.

"**It came through the door.** It is addressed to the cooperative steward board, care of this hall, which is this hall's address, because the minute is published and the minute says where we sit. Somebody put it through the door of a Methodist hall on a Friday afternoon, which means they stood outside in the rain and worked out that a body of nine who have no money and no address have got a hall and a Saturday, and then they put a piece of paper through the door of it, and I found it on the Saturday morning at about nine with the key I have had since the fourth of September, and I have had it in my bag since the Saturday and I have not told anybody and I have not rung anybody."

"You opened it."

"I opened it on the Saturday and I have read it about nine times, and I have never in my life been handed one of these, and on Saturday I put a piece of paper in my bag that is not about a seat, and that is the whole of what has happened and I would like it noticed that nothing has happened."

"What is on it."

"**One question, and it is not a good question, and I am not going to improve it.** It asks whether the stand at the bottom of Quarry Street is one of the six. And then it says, and these are its words: *I am not asking you for anything. There is no form on this and I know there is no form on this, and I am writing it on Friday the twenty-eighth of November and I am not writing it at work. I am asking because a number went out in November and anybody can check it, and I would like to know whether my stand is in it, and if it is in it then in about four years somebody is going to come with a piece of paper about that stand, and I would like there to be one already on the table that says it is a service and does not say who is at it.*"

"And it is signed."

"It is signed." She took her hand off the bag. "**That is the part I have had in my bag for three days. She has put her name at the bottom of a piece of paper in her own hand, and there is no box on it and there is no form and it is not going in the minute, and she says in the letter that she has done it because a date has been on that stand for eleven years and no person ever has been, and that this is the once.**"

---

**He did not take the letter and she did not give it to him and neither of them suggested it.**

He read it standing up, on the trestle table, in about four minutes, and it was one side of A4 in biro with no crest and no form number and no reference and no date in a box, and the handwriting was not a clerk's.

He asked his one question, and it was five words, and it was the only question he asked in that room, and he asked it to the back of her coat.

"**What is your name.**"

"Nobody has asked me that." She did not turn round. "It is on the sheet on the wall at the top, in the box that says *minute taken by*, and it has been on every sheet since the fourth of September, and **there is no other box anywhere in this borough that says who wrote a thing down.** I have written thirteen of them and my name is on a piece of A4 and in no minute at all, and that is not a decision anybody made, and I have had this for about eleven weeks and I have never once had to think about it, and it is the first of December and I am thinking about it."

"It is not in the minute."

"The sheet is the minute until about four o'clock on the Monday, and after that it is only a sheet, and I have not given the sheet to anybody." She picked up her bag. "**And I am going to say the thing I have been not saying since Saturday, and then I am going to go and open this hall, and then you are going to do whatever it is you came here to do and I am not going to help you with it. A letter with no addressee and no form on it is not a document. I have been a clerk of one thing or another for twenty-six years and I have never in my life been handed one of these, and I have spent three days and about an afternoon of my own Saturday working out what a letter is, and I have got the following, and it is not mine and I am not going to put it in the minute: it is not a document, so it cannot be filed; it is not a form, so it cannot be refused; it is not a minute, so it cannot be minuted; and it is addressed to a room, so it cannot be delivered.** And that last one is the whole of it, because a letter is a thing that goes to a person, and this one goes to a Wednesday, and there is no version of this in which anybody in this borough can put it in a person's hands and be right."

"Then what did you do with it."

"I carried it for three days and I have brought it here on the Wednesday, and I am going to leave it on this table at the top of the evening with nothing written on it. If the ninth seat is in the room then she will know what to do with it, because she is a person and a person may be told a thing. And if she is not in the room then it sits on this table until the fourteenth of December, and on the fourteenth of December it goes in the bin, and neither of those things is a decision I have made and both of them are the only ones available."

---

**Wednesday the third of December 2014, twenty minutes to seven in the morning, the bottom of Quarry Street.**

He got there at twenty to seven because she was there at twenty to seven, every morning, with a folding trolley, and he had known that since the eighth of September and had not once been there at that hour, and the reason he had not was not a rule and had never been a rule.

She stopped with the trolley when she saw him, in the way that people stop when they have decided in advance what they are not going to say, and she did not say it.

"I have not come to ask you anything," said Jonas Mercer.

"You have come at twenty to seven in the morning to a stand at the bottom of a street where nobody can find it, so you have not come to ask me anything, and I am not going to help you with whatever it is, and it is cold and you have not got a hat on."

"There was a letter on Friday. It went through the door of a hall on Carrow Lane and it is signed at the bottom in your own hand and it has your name on it, and it is the only piece of paper in this city with your name on it, and I did not put it there and I did not ask you for it."

"Then that is the first useful thing anybody has done for me in eleven years and I do not want it," she said, "**and I want you to be very clear that I know exactly what it is. There is a date on that stand and no person on it. That is the only deal there is and I have kept to it since before the second of July and I have kept to it by not having a name on anything, and on Friday I put my name on a piece of paper, and I did it on purpose, and I did it because I have been a person at the end of a street for eleven years and there is one thing in this borough I can be and it is a person in a room, and a room is not a record, and I got that right before I wrote a word of it.**"

"Yes."

"**So where are you going to put it.**"

"That is exactly the question and I have been asking it since the eighth of September, and I have asked it in a records office and a hall and a corridor and I have not got one answer out of anybody, because the answer is that you cannot put it anywhere. There is no form for it. It is not a document and it cannot be filed and it cannot be minuted and it cannot be delivered, because it is addressed to a room and a room is not a person. **That letter cannot reach you. It has your name on it and it cannot reach you. And there is one instrument left in this city and it is a mouth.**"

She put both hands on the handles of the trolley.

"Say that again."

"I am not going to say it to you."

"That," she said, "is the first time anybody has been careful about me in eleven years and it is the worst thing that has ever been done to me, and I want you to understand that I would have said no and you have not asked me and you have gone off to a room to say my name in it without me, and that is the whole of what this city is and I have known it since the second of July."

He did not say anything.

"**When.**"

"About half past five this evening. In the hall. There will be about eleven people in it and you will not be one of them and I am not going to ask you for anything, and I am not going to write it down either, and I am telling you the day and the hour because you have kept a date on a stand for eleven years and a person who keeps a date is entitled to know when it stops being one."

"Right," said the woman at the bottom of Quarry Street. "Then there is going to be a person in this borough after tonight, and she is not going to be a person in a document, and I would like that written down somewhere, and I would like it written down by somebody who is not you."

---

**Wednesday the third of December 2014, half past five in the evening, the Methodist hall, Carrow Lane, Tideglass.**

There were about eleven people and nine chairs in a horseshoe and two of them were empty and one of the two had been empty for fifty-six days. There is a card in a plastic sleeve on the wall about nine feet from the door and nobody in the building has admitted to putting it there, and on the trestle table at the back there was one sheet of A4, face up, with nothing written on it and nothing behind it, and about nine folding chairs against the wall that nobody brought out.

Jonas Mercer came at half past five and stood against the left-hand wall with his hands behind his back, and the woman who keeps the minutes said at the top of the evening that he was not on the agenda, not a party, not a member and not a departmental officer, and that nobody was entitled to the answer to anything he might say and that he could decline to say it.

He had not asked a question all evening and he was not going to start.

**The sheet on the trestle table was read out at about six.**

"There is a piece of paper on that table," said the woman who keeps the minutes, "and it is not a document and it is not a form and it is not a minute, and it is not going in this minute, and I am going to read it out because it was addressed to a room and this is the room. **It is signed. It asks whether the stand at the bottom of Quarry Street is one of the six shapes in the number of November. And it says that the person who signed it is not asking for anything, and that she has put her name at the bottom of it on purpose, and that there is a date on that stand and no person on it, and that this is the once.**"

Nobody said anything. A bus went past outside, the way a bus goes past outside a Methodist hall.

"**And it does not go in the minute,**" she said. "**And that is not my decision and it is not anybody's in this room, and I would like it minuted that I said so.**"

"Minutes," said the woman at the back.

Then a man of fifty-six who was not a member and had come along said: "Mr Mercer, you are the only person in this room who is not on the agenda, and there is a piece of paper on that table with a name on it, and you are the only person in this borough who is good at this. **Would you chair this.**"

"No," said Jonas Mercer.

"Would you read it out, then. You could read it out and it would be the same words and it would be a document—"

"It would be a document," said Jonas Mercer, "and a document is a lever, and she has said in writing on that sheet that she has put her name on it because a room is not a record, and I am not going to be the person who turns that into a record, and I am not going to chair it, and I am not going to say anything else about it tonight."

The room waited.

**And then he said it, out loud, in a room, in about nine seconds, once, having been told in a shop in September that the names are the work and having written the sentence down three times and not started.**

"**Bernice Ofori.**"

He said it to the floor and not to anybody. It was four syllables and it was the first time in this city that anybody had said it in a room with more than one other person in it, and it had been in a handbag in a Methodist hall since Friday, and it had been in his own head since Monday afternoon, and he had been at it since the fourth of September, which was ninety days, and he could name the order he had asked in.

**Nobody asked him a second question.** Nobody said *are you sure* and nobody said *is that her* and nobody said *what is her name*, and the man of fifty-six who had asked him to chair it sat down.

Yolanda Fofana did not turn round, and said, in about eleven seconds, to the room and not to him:

"**Then that is a person. And it is the first time in this borough that a name has been said out loud in a room by somebody who was asked for nothing and gave it anyway, and I want three things said in this room while he is standing there. We did not go and get her and we could not have. Nobody in this room asked her for anything, and nobody in this room is going to. And nobody is to go down there on Monday.**"

"Nobody is going to go down there on Monday," said Jonas Mercer.

"You are not, either."

"No."

"**Good,**" said Yolanda Fofana. "**The ninth seat has done its afternoon and there is a thing on that table that I am going to take home and read, and that is the whole of what I am going to do with it, and I would like everybody to stop looking at me.**"

The woman who keeps the minutes wrote for about forty seconds and then read it back, in the room, standing up, and it was on the sheet and it went out on the Friday and it is in the borough's records now, and it is a public document, and this is all of what is in it.

> **Item 5. A person present, not on the agenda, not a party, not a member and not a departmental officer, stated a name in the room, once, at 18:41. He was not asked a second question and made no further statement. Nobody asked him a second question. No person was asked to repeat the name, no person was asked whether it was correct, and no person was asked anything about it.**
>
> **Recorded: that a piece of paper on the table at the top of the evening was not a document, not a form and not a minute, and was not entered in this minute, and that the person who took this minute said so in the room and asked for that to be recorded, and it is recorded.**
>
> **Recorded: that no name appears in this minute, and that this minute has no box for one, and that this is a decision and not an oversight.**
>
> **Nothing in this item requires any person to do anything.**

He read it standing up. It is a public document and it went out on the Friday and the name is not in it, and the name is in about eleven people in a Methodist hall in Tideglass, and there is no instrument in this borough that can un-say a thing in a room, and there is one that can un-write it, and it is the same instrument they have had since March.

**And nobody offered to take it out of him and he did not ask**, because asking would be asking for a favour, and a favour is a rota, and a rota with one name on it is the exact thing he has spent seven months refusing to be and the exact thing a man of forty-eight put on a supplier's acceptance in August with three lines in his own hand and got right.

---

**And then, because it was half past seven and there were about nine people in the room, and because nobody had asked him anything and he was not on the agenda, he did the thing he has never once done in a room, which is to stand there and count what was left standing, out loud, in the order he does it, and it took about four minutes and nobody stopped him.**

Two chairs empty. The seventh since the eighth of October, when a night bus controller of thirty-three wrote four lines on the back of a timetable and gave them to a woman who keeps minutes, and nobody has read them out. The eighth since the Wednesday before last, when a market porter of fifty-eight stood up in a room and said *right, I'm going* and did not use the chair, and the whole of the power this borough has been able to give anybody in a room since the third of April is that he was allowed to.

**Seven of nine. Nobody can fill either and nobody can unfill either and there is no power in this city that could.**

Four of the nine have said they will stop if it turns into a job. One of them said it on the eighth of October in eleven words and then apologised for saying it and has not withdrawn it. One of them said it in October, meant it, has thought about it every day since, has not stopped, asked to be asked why, and nobody was able to ask her, and she is still sitting in the fourth chair. The ninth seat is a woman of fifty-four who is findable at a shopfront in Salter's Yard, who has been doing it for nothing since the fourth of July, who said in a hall in September that she would be doing it for nothing until she is sixty-one, and who is the only door in a building of thirty-one rented rooms, and **nobody in this room has told her that she is a door and nobody in this room is going to, and the reason is the wall card on that wall and not anybody's kindness.**

The lowland is not on the layer and cannot be seated and will not be seated this year, and six water points at eleven thousand two hundred and eighty pounds a year are in a minute from the nineteenth of May, and there is no money in this body and no party to answer and no instrument by which the pot could be made. Six shapes were identified in October and are named in no document, and one of them is a street that nobody in this room is going to name.

There are four tonnes of stone and about eleven hundred and eighty pounds of it owed to nobody at all on a bank at the bottom of a chamber, and a sill beam, and a man of forty-four who signed a line in ninety seconds in August that he does not understand, and who has a fortnight somewhere in this city with a bucket under a tap, and whom nobody is able to ask. There is a second tray on a shelf in that building with a rubber band and a date on it and one question on it that has had the same answer since the twenty-ninth of May, and he went into that building in September and came out and wrote three lines in a margin because a man in an apron asked him for a piece of paper that does not exist.

There is a Sunday coming. **The Saint Orra west term expires at six o'clock in the morning on Sunday the fourth of January, and the terms are six days and the rota has still not been rebuilt and the gate still runs on its local term and nobody can renew it but the people who read it aloud at ten at night, and the handover is on the Saturday night before, and there is no date written down anywhere for that Saturday night, because a person is not required to be told and there is nobody in this borough who is required to write it.** Bram Osei's Thursday is still refused on the board in his own words. Priya Ranganathan's card is still on the board because she put it there. The eleven o'clock to half past one gap is still published and still unfilled.

There is a records supervisor who has six refusals in her in nineteen years and does not call them that, who said out loud that the day she initials a sentence about a document it becomes a rule about documents, who was right, and whose page went out unsigned, and about four hundred people have added their names to it, and it is still the only document in this city anybody can be held to.

And there is a man who has a seam mark that runs past the elbow, and a right hand that is numb to the elbow and does not close, and he has been meaning to mention it to somebody for a month, and he is thirty-two.

"That is the floor," he said. "**It is eleven feet by nine and it is the same eleven feet by nine it was in July, and everything in this borough that has happened this year has happened in a room about the size of a telephone box, and none of it has added a single hinge to a board in Gantry Ward, and I would like that minuted and I would like it minuted as an observation and not as an achievement.**"

"Minutes," said the woman at the back.

**And the ninth seat picked up her bag, and the ninth seat is the only seat in that body that cannot be emptied by anybody else, and everybody in that room knew the sentence about what she had said in a hall in September, and nobody in that room knew the other one, and there was a fourth page of a regulation from 2008 in three people's heads and in a handbag in Tideglass, and it was in neither of those, and it stays there.**

---

He walked down the hill in the dark and did not go anywhere near a road that leads to a lowland, and he did not write it down, and on Thursday morning at about nine he did four hours of something else, and on Friday the minute went out with no name on it.

And on the Friday afternoon a woman of thirty-four with a folding trolley was at the bottom of Quarry Street at twenty to seven in the morning, which is what she does, and there was nothing on the stand but the date that has been on it for eleven years, and she had not been asked anything by anybody, and she was not going to be, and there was now no deal at all.
