# Chapter 0137 — The Hour Between Six And Seven

Saturday the eleventh of April 2015, about ten past ten in the evening, four hundred yards from a building. And Sunday the twelfth, from about twenty past six in the morning, a ground-floor corridor, Saint Orra west. And Monday the thirteenth of April, about ten past ten in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward.

**There was no question of his in the corridor at twenty past six on the Sunday morning and there was no question of his anywhere else that day, and there has not been one since the twenty-fifth of February, and the counting has been right in every week of this year. There was another handover on the Saturday night and it is not going to be dated here and no name is going on it and nobody is going to be asked about it.**

---

He had worked it out on the Saturday afternoon, standing up, and it took no longer than that, and what he had worked out was about a place.

**Four hundred yards is not an answer. Four hundred yards is a place, and a place a man goes to is the first half of a schedule, and a schedule is a rota with one name on it, and he has stood on a piece of wet pavement at a bus turning head five times since December, two of them at ten o'clock on a Saturday night, and he is thirty-two.**

On the third of April, at about twenty past six in the morning, four hundred yards had been the correct amount of evidence and the only place a man could stand and not be a rota. On the eleventh of April he stood on the same pavement at about ten past ten at night for about half an hour and worked out that the reason four hundred yards was safe was not that it was far away.

**It was safe because on the third of April it was a decision he had made on the Friday, and it was the first time. And the reason it is not safe now is that a piece of wet pavement is a piece of wet pavement however far off it is.**

A thing a man does once is a decision. A thing a man does four times, on a night, in the same four hundred yards, is a man who is expected to four hundred yards away on a night, and the only person in this borough who could confirm that is a porter, and a porter who can confirm it is a person with a duty and no name on it.

**So he went and stood in the corridor instead, on the Sunday morning, at about twenty past six, and he has stood in a corridor on a Sunday morning exactly twice, and the first of them was at half past four on the fourth of January and the second is this one, and the first had not been planned for a week and this one had, and that is the whole of the difference, and four months has not taught him anything except how to see it.**

---

**A term is six days. It is read at ten o'clock on the Saturday night before the Sunday it starts on. The gate opens at seven on that Sunday. It expires at six on the following Sunday.**

**The Saturday nights are what are a week apart. That is the whole trick and there is nobody in this borough who built it and nobody who could. It is why there is an hour on every Sunday morning between six and seven when no term is in force at all.**

At twenty past six on Sunday the twelfth of April, no term was in force anywhere in this city.

The corridor was empty. There was a light on. There was a board at the end of it with an empty row on it that does not say *vacant* and a man's refused Thursday in his own handwriting on it. There was a fire door on a wedge. There was a window with the corridor's reflection in it. There was a door four feet to his left, and it was shut, and it was a door, and there was nothing behind it that was in force.

**He did not open it.**

He had a claim to a door in this volume and he had spent it on the twenty-eighth of January, in a room, in daylight, in about four seconds, and it is the only thing he has ever spent in nine months, and he is not going to go back to a thing he has already spent because it would make the next four days easier. There was nothing in that corridor he was allowed to open and he opened nothing and there was no moment in the whole of the hour when he wanted to.

---

**And here is what an hour is, when you stand in one and it is the only hour of the week in which this borough requires nothing of anybody.**

It is a floor that is not level, and there is nobody in it.

A door exists on a term. The term is read by two men at ten o'clock on a Saturday night in a waiting area with four chairs in it and a machine for hot water that has not worked since about August, and it lasts six days, and then at six o'clock on the Sunday morning it does not exist, and at seven o'clock the next one exists, and for one hour in between **there is no civic permission for anything at all in this borough, and there is no name on that hour, and nobody in this city would be able to tell you when it happens.**

He stood in it for about half an hour. Nothing happened. Nothing was supposed to happen. **In that hour, four hundred and twenty women are not on the far side of a paragraph number, because there is no paragraph and there is no term. In that hour the eleven o'clock to half past one row is not empty, because there is no rota and there is no row and there is no board being read by anybody. In that hour the board in the vestibule at the end of the Old Ward has no nineteenth entry with a bulb out on it, because there is no register in force and no board in force and no hand in force to move it.**

A term is a promise made by two tired people at ten o'clock on a Saturday night, and a promise that runs six days and then stops, made by two people who are not on anything, on their own time, with nothing written down, is the cheapest thing anybody in this city has ever produced.

**And in about four years somebody is going to write a schedule for that hour. And the schedule will be good. It will have a start time and a name column and a relief column and a box for the person who did not come, and it will be better than two tired men turning up because it will be reliable, and the day it goes up the hour stops being an hour and becomes a post.**

---

He stood at the wall by the fire door and he worked out the thing that is the only thing he has got this month.

**The hour cannot be scheduled without somebody being required to be in it.**

Not a rota. Not a rule. **Any schedule at all contains the sentence *somebody will be there*, and a sentence with somebody in it is a requirement, and a requirement is the thing this borough has refused in every room it has ever sat in, and the registrar put it in a minute in June: a clause that cannot be challenged by a person cannot require them to, and a column with a name in it can be challenged by the person whose name is in it, and a person who is allowed to say no to a column is no longer a column, and then there is nothing on the wall at all.**

**So the only way to keep the hour is to keep it unscheduled. And the instrument that keeps it unscheduled is the same instrument that has been doing everything else for nine months, which is two people who are not on anything turning up at ten o'clock on a Saturday because nobody wrote anything down.**

Not a policy. Not a standard. Two men, a waiting area, four chairs, a machine for hot water, about nine minutes, and then they go home at about half past six.

**It is the cheapest thing in this borough. It is the only thing in this borough that works. And there is no instrument for it, and there cannot be one, and the attempt to make one is the exact moment this whole thing stops.**

---

**And then he did the arithmetic he had been avoiding for four months, and it is the reason he did not write this down until the Monday.**

**He has stood in that corridor on a Sunday morning exactly twice. The first was half past four on the fourth of January and it was a decision, and it was the last hour and a half of a term, and there was no hour in that morning in which this borough had nothing. The second is twenty past six this morning and it is the hour after a term has stopped, and he had arranged the whole Saturday to get here, and he had arranged it on purpose, and he was standing in the one hour of the week when nothing in this borough is in force, in a corridor, at a time he had chosen, and there was nothing whatever wrong with any of it.**

**A third one is a habit. A fourth is a rota with one name in it. And he has eight weeks of that coming, one Sunday at a time, and there is no form on which anybody could stop him and there is no form he would fill in.**

And the finding underneath the finding, and it is not a rule and it is not a promise and it goes in no book:

**The hour does not need him. It has not needed him for four months. The gate has gone up on every Sunday morning since the twenty-fifth of January because two men who are not on anything turn up, and it will go up on Sunday the nineteenth of April and on the twenty-sixth whether he is in that corridor or four hundred yards away or in a bed, and the only reason he was ever in it at all is that he was not required to be anywhere.**

And then, because he is a person who finishes things:

**And the reason four hundred yards is not the answer is not the distance. It is that a man who is regularly four hundred yards away is a man who is regularly four hundred yards away. And the reason standing in a corridor is not the answer is the same. And there is no third place, and he has looked, and the only instrument this borough has for a thing that must not become a rota is the absence of a rule, and an absence of a rule cannot be enforced on the one person who would break it, and the one person who would break it is him, and nobody else in this city is going to.**

So he is not going to be helped with it, and he is not going to be asked about it, and he is not going to be told about it, and on Wednesday the fifteenth of April, which is a Wednesday, nothing whatever is going to happen in this borough and there is no arrangement of any kind in that day and it is not on any list.

---

**And on the Monday morning, the thirteenth of April, about ten past ten, the standing of that building was exactly what it was and had been since the second of April and had not moved.**

**The rota has still not been rebuilt and there is no instrument in this city by which it could be. The eleven o'clock to half past one row is still empty and still does not say *vacant*. Bram Osei's Thursday is still refused in his own handwriting with *no reason I can give* under a *no reason given* in somebody else's hand, and he has asked twice that the second line not be looked at, and nobody has looked at it and nobody is going to. Priya Ranganathan's card is still in a plastic sleeve on the Friday and the Saturday rows because she put it there.**

**The thirteenth of them is on Saturday the eighteenth of April, at ten o'clock at night, and there is no date on it. The fourteenth is on Saturday the twenty-fifth. And there is a date on neither of them, and there is a date on none of them, and there is not going to be one, and the reason is that a person is not required to be told and there is nobody in this borough who is required to write it down.**

Nobody was helped and nobody was hurt and nothing was resolved and no door was opened and no term was read in a room with anybody else in it and no slip was written in a corridor and about eleven people a day went in and out of a vestibule eleven feet by nine and got a date.

**And the forty minutes is the time until a family knew, on the fifteenth of April of a year before this one, and it is a woman of eighty-four in bay six and it is not about a door and it is not about an hour and it is not the length of anything anybody in this borough does on a Sunday morning, and it is the only figure of minutes this borough has, and nobody is going to be told about it and it is not going in anything.**

---

**And on the evening of Monday the thirteenth of April, in a shop at the end of a street, a woman who has been in that street thirty-one years oiled a bench and did not read a page, which is the reason he writes there, and he said one thing.**

"**You have told me for four months that the expensive half is not being found, it is being asked.**"

"**I have.**"

"**Then I have got a Sunday morning every week for about eight weeks in front of me and two ways of standing in it and both of them turn into me being a man who is expected somewhere, and there is no third one, and I have not got anybody to give it to, and you said in December that a thing like that has to be said to somebody four times a day for a week before it holds, and I have said it to you once.**"

Beatrice Osunde did not stop oiling the bench.

"**Four times a day for a week is for a thing you have to survive,**" she said. "**Nobody survives standing in a corridor. Nobody has ever survived it. It is not a position. It is just a thing a man does until he is an old man who does it, and then it is a habit and then it is a rota, and there is no version of it where a man in his thirties has got a position at a bus turning head and a Sunday morning in it.**"

"**So I am going to have to work it out on my own.**"

"**You have been working it out on your own since the fourth of September and you are extremely good at it and it has taken you nine months and a bench.**" She put the ash down. "**And I am not going to be asked twice, Mr Mercer, and I have told you that, and if you come in here on Wednesday with the same thing again in a different coat I shall put the broom in front of you.**"
