# Chapter 0122 — The Man Who Was Asked And Did Not Know What He Was Signing

**Wednesday the fourth of February 2015, about twenty past eleven in the morning, the counter, the records office, second floor. And Friday the sixth of February, about half past ten in the morning, a ground-floor corridor, Saint Orra west.**

**He asked nothing in either room, and that is not an omission. It is the finding.**

---

The autumn compliance report went out in the last week of November and it is public, and anybody may have it at that counter for nothing, and Jonas Mercer had it in his hand at about twenty past eleven on the Wednesday and read the same page for the fourth time.

**Paragraph 4.11. Decontamination, west site, level zero. The historical half of the weekly verification record cannot be reconstructed and the non-conformance is carried forward.**

That is the whole of it. There is no name in paragraph 4.11 because there has never been a name field on the sheet the paragraphs are counted from, and there has not been one since a supplier's engineer put a grid on a clipboard in July and said, in front of nine people, that anybody in the building could sign it, once a week, twenty minutes, that it did not have to be clinical, that it was a person reading a strip and writing their name, and that it was nobody's job. **Four hundred and twenty women in this borough have instruments coming out of that room every weekday and every one of them is on the far side of a paragraph number, and no person is on the near side of it, and that is not an oversight either.**

A doctor refused it in her own words in May and has not been asked since and must not be asked again, and the reason she gave is correct and has not been argued out of her: if her name is against twenty minutes a week then twenty minutes a week has a person on it, that person is a doctor, she is already in that building at eleven at night and seven in the morning, and then it is a rota with one name on it. **A rota with one name on it is what this borough has spent eight months refusing, and Bram Osei's Thursday is a rota with one name on it, and he asked for it to be left in exactly those words, and both of those refusals are the failure, and neither of them is cowardice.**

And then there is the third one, and he is the one nobody in this borough talks about.

**A man of forty-eight did the weekly verification for about eleven weeks in the autumn, on his own time, with no name against it, and stopped in October, and said out loud in a corridor in August, to about four people, that a job in November has a department in it and a department is a rota. It was correct. Nobody has asked him about it since. Nobody has rung him. There is no log to check, because the log is a supplier's grid of fifty-two Sundays with a tick in a temperature box for four books and a signature box that is empty on every line, and there is no name field on that log and there never was, and the man who could be asked about any of it is a doctor who has already refused it, and the other man is a porter with a refused Thursday on a board in his own handwriting.**

---

**On the Friday morning he went to Saint Orra west, and he did not go to level zero, and he did not try.**

He went as a member of the public on a Friday, in daylight, and he stood in a ground-floor corridor for about half an hour with nothing in his hands, the way he has stood in corridors since the fourth of September, and a man came past with a mop and did not look at him, and the corridor smelled of the same thing it has smelled of all year, which is warm plastic and floor polish and a thing underneath both of them that four hundred and twenty women would recognise and he has never been able to name.

On the wall by the lift there was a sheet in a plastic wallet, printed, and it is the only Lattice notice he has seen in this borough since the middle of January, and it is the only one anybody in this borough has put up since the middle of January, and it says:

> **LEVEL ZERO DECONTAMINATION. BENCH STERILISER 2.**
> **THE MACHINE KEEPS ITS OWN CYCLE RECORD ON A STRIP. THE STRIP GOES IN THE DRAWER IN THE ROOM.**
> **THE QUARTERLY RETURN IS MADE BY THE SUPPLIER. NO PERSON SIGNS THIS SHEET.**

Nothing on it defines anything. It does not say who empties the drawer. It does not say what a strip is held to prove. It says the strip goes in the drawer in the room and it does not say that anybody has to, and that is the design, or it is the neglect, and there is no instrument in this city that can tell the difference between those two words about a wall.

**Bram Osei was on the desk by the lift. He is a porter. He is the day bearer and he has refused a Thursday in his own handwriting and asked twice that it not be looked at, and the second line under his name was written by somebody else and says in their hand that no reason was given, and he knows about the second line and has never asked for it to come off.**

They talked for about nine minutes. Jonas Mercer did not ask him anything. There was no question of his in that corridor and there was not going to be one.

And then he said the sentence, out loud, once, in a corridor, to a man with a mop and a desk, and it is the reason he said it out loud, and it is going to be expensive in a later part of this than it is today.

"**I could go and find the man of forty-eight. I want you to hear me say out loud that I am not going to, and I want you to hear the reason, because I have never said it out loud and it is the first time in eight months that the correct thing to do has been to leave a person in ignorance on purpose.**"

Bram Osei did not look up from the desk.

"**Reason.**"

"**Because if I tell him, then he is the person who knows.**"

"**Knows what.**"

"**That the verification lapsed in October. That it lapsed because he said a correct thing in a corridor in August and nobody wrote it down. That the paragraph number in the autumn is about the twelve weeks either side of him.**" Jonas Mercer kept his voice at the volume it had been at, which is the volume of a man talking in a corridor. "**And the moment he is the person who knows, he is a person somebody can ask. Not me. Somebody. In about four years there is going to be a piece of paper about a decontamination room at that hospital and on it there is going to be a number of weeks that are not signed, and somebody is going to want to know why, and the only address any of that has is the man who did it for eleven weeks and stopped.**"

"That is not nothing," said Bram Osei.

"**It is not nothing. That is the whole of it. It is one of the things a person can be put into that they cannot get out of, and three of them are mine, and I made this one in August by not asking him a question, and I am not going to unmake it by asking him two.**"

---

**And then Bram Osei said the thing that made the sentence survivable, and he said it to the desk, and it took about four seconds, and Jonas Mercer wrote it down on the Friday evening in a shop on the back page of a pad where he keeps the things that are his.**

"**Then the only instrument in this borough that keeps a man from being askable is a man who does not know, and there is no other one, and I have never said that out loud before.**" He looked up. "**You have understood that I have known it since June and I have said nothing about it, and the reason I have said nothing is that I have a Thursday on that board with a line under it in somebody else's hand, and if I knew about the forty-eight I would be two men knowing and there is no room on that board for that.**"

"**He is safer not knowing.**"

"**He is safer not knowing. That is the sentence and you may have it, and I am not going to say it twice, and it is also true about me.**"

---

**Nobody told the man of forty-eight anything, in February or afterwards, and the reason was given out loud in a corridor by a man who is not on a rota and is not on a board and is not a member of anything, and nobody argued with him about it, because there was nobody in that corridor to argue with him, and that is also the sentence.**

**The autumn compliance report stands. Paragraph 4.11 stands. Four hundred and twenty women stand behind a paragraph number and no person stands in front of it. The bench steriliser on level zero is four years old and running on four hundred and ten pounds of service that a doctor bought herself, and the room goes on working, and the instruments go on coming out of it, and not one person in this borough is going to be harmed by any of that this year.**

**And nobody in this borough has been helped by any of it either, and that is going to be said out loud here, in its own voice, and it is not going to be apologised for, because apologising would be a way of making the not-helping into a thing that somebody intends, and it is not a thing that anybody intends. It is a machine in a room on level zero with a strip that comes out of it about forty times a day, and nobody has been hurt, and nobody has been helped, and both of those are true at once and there is no third thing to say.**

---

**And here is the part that is going to be in the pad, and it is the only thing in any of it that Jonas Mercer worked out on his own, and he worked it out standing at the bottom of a fire stair on the Friday morning because it was raining again.**

**The machine is still running.**

The report says the cycle is recorded. The notice by the lift says the record is the machine's own strip and that the strip goes in the drawer in the room. Paragraph 4.11 says the historical half cannot be reconstructed, which is a sentence about weeks that are gone, and says nothing at all about this week, and this week the machine is on and the room is in use and four instruments an hour go through it.

**A strip is a piece of paper. A drawer is a place. And a piece of paper does not put itself in a drawer.**

So somebody has been putting the strips in the drawer — not the doctor, who has refused in her own words and must not be asked; not the man of forty-eight, who stopped in October; not a name, because there is no name field and there never was. Somebody is doing a small, correct, unremarkable act about forty times a day in a room on level zero at a hospital in this borough, and that person is not on a form, is not on a rota, is not in a paragraph, and has not been asked, and **has not been found either, and Jonas Mercer is not going to go and find him, and that is the second time in three days he has decided not to find somebody, and the reason in both cases is the same one, and it is going to be the sentence he is judged on.**

The slot in the counter of the vestibule is still empty. The register still has nineteen entries in it. The blank line under the nineteenth is blank and it is the only fixed point in the building and it will be blank on Friday morning and on Tuesday morning and on the morning of the seventh of March.
