# Chapter 0107 — The Second Time Somebody Is Asked

**Monday the seventeenth of November 2014, ten past eleven in the morning, the counter, the records office, second floor.**

Nina Calder had the September page in a plastic wallet on the counter in front of her, because it was the only thing on the counter that had been in a bin tray and been rescued, and she keeps it there on and off.

"I want a second one," said Jonas Mercer.

"You have read the first one."

"Nine times."

"Then you know that it is one page and it names no person and it has no name box on it and it is a record of shapes and it may not be matched against any list of addresses, and any document that attempts it is recorded as attempting it, and it establishes nothing, and it is a document and it is not a service."

"I know all of that. **I want a second one because the first one was a photograph of a thing and a photograph is the last thing anybody makes when what is needed is a drawing.**"

She put her pen down, which she does about twice a day.

"Mr Mercer, I have been designing lists since 1999 and I have refused to design four of them this year and one of them in front of about nine people, and Douglas Imre thinks I am a obstacle, and a chair has said in public that I am the only mechanism this city has. **Neither of those things is true and both of those things are my fault.**"

"I know."

"Do you, because you have been the man who came and asked for the fourth page's brother since March, and I have said no four times, and you have not once argued with me, and I would like to know what has changed in eleven weeks or whether this is the sixth time."

"It is the second time, and I have not come to argue, and I have come because the first one came out in September and it has been in a corridor for about nine weeks and **nobody has done anything with it and nobody can, and a record that cannot be argued with and cannot be acted on is a photograph.**"

He asked his one question, and it was eleven words, and a counter was required to answer it and a counter was not required to make one.

"Can a person be told that they are in a record."

"**No.** And there is no form of that which says they can, and there is no power in this city by which any officer may begin an enquiry, write a letter, hand anything to anybody, or tell a person that a thing has happened to them, and a person may not be required even to be told, and I have said that to you in this corridor in September and I am saying it in November because I am going to be saying it in a document in about four minutes." She took the plastic wallet off the counter and put it on the shelf behind her. "Now. **You are not getting a second page with a bigger photograph on it. Tell me what is on yours that is not on mine.**"

"Two things. **The first one has no number in it.** It says *a number* where the schedules are concerned, and *sixteen* where the register of narrowings is concerned, and *six water points* and a chamber and a spur and an outfall, and no total, and it is a record of shapes and shapes without a number are a list of kinds. **A list of kinds cannot be argued with, and it also cannot be checked.** Nobody can walk into a counter on a Tuesday and put a piece of paper next to a piece of paper and say *this is wrong*. You could only read it and feel something about it, and a feeling is not a thing anybody can be held to."

"And the second."

"**The first one cannot be wrong, so it cannot be found wrong. And in nine weeks nobody has found a single thing wrong with it, and that is not because it is right, it is because there is nothing in it to be wrong about.**"

Nina Calder looked at him for about four seconds and then she said the thing she had said since the thirtieth of April, for the fifth time, and she said it in the same order, because she is a person who believes in order.

"**A record with one name in it cannot be argued with.** I have watched three of those happen in this borough this year and I have been in the room for all three. One of them is a heat protocol with a number box in it and a chief officer's name under it that she did not put there. One of them was a night porter on a chalk board in Gantry Ward for six weeks before anybody asked him a single question, and the six weeks were in a room that the man was in every day, and he did not know that his name was up there, and when somebody finally asked him about it he said he had known since the Tuesday." She turned a page over and did not turn it back. "**And I have not decided which of the three was the worst and I am not going to.** I have been asked four times this year which was the worst and I have given the same answer four times and I am not going to change it in November, and you did not ask me, and I would have told you the same thing, and that is the difference between you and Douglas Imre and it is not a compliment, it is a measurement."

"Neither is it," said Jonas Mercer.

"No. **It is a measurement.**"

---

**Wednesday the nineteenth of November 2014, four o'clock in the afternoon, the records office, second floor, three people and a door that was shut.**

Frances Tolley was there because she had been asked to be there and she had said yes, and she had brought nothing, which was the first thing she said.

"You have not brought a pen."

"I have not brought a pen, and I am not going to, and if either of you thinks I am being delicate then one of you is wrong and it is not me." She sat down on the edge of the desk. "I have read a draft of it off Mr Mercer's pad and I have read the first page and the first page is right, and I want to say the thing that is wrong with it and then I want to go and open a room."

"Go on."

"**It is a photograph.**"

"You said that on Monday and you are right and that is the whole of the question."

"Then the answer is arithmetic, and it is the only arithmetic in this city that anybody can check, and here it is, and it took me about four minutes and it took me four minutes because I have never once in twenty-two years asked the schedule office for an extract and it turns out you can, in a minute, at a counter, by post, for nothing, as of a date." She put her hands in her pockets. "Fifty-one nodes on the continuity schedule as at six o'clock on Monday the sixth of October 2014. Thirty-eight parties answering them. **Thirteen shapes.** And anybody in this borough may go to that office and ask for that extract on that date and perform the subtraction and check it, and if it is wrong they have a piece of paper that says so with a date on it and a reference on it and a signature of a clerk on it, and they have never in the history of this city been able to do that with anything."

"That is the number."

"That is the number and it is a number of shapes and it is not a number of people and it may never be worked out into one, because then it would be a number of people, and a woman of about sixty has said that sentence in a flood forum in public twice and I have it in a minute and I am not going to improve on it."

"**Now the hard part, and it is the only hard part.**" Nina Calder had not said anything for about four minutes, and she had a pencil out and had not used it. "Nobody is required to be told they are in it. That is the design and it is not an oversight, and I am going to say it in the room where it is agreed and I am going to say it in those words, and if the two of you want me to soften it then this is the moment to say so and it is the last moment anybody in this city will get to say so."

Nobody said anything.

"**Right.**" She wrote for about eleven seconds and then she turned the sheet round, and it was on the city's own paper, and it had no crest on it and no form number, and there was no box on it for the person who had put it together, and the last line of it said that absence out loud and said that it was a decision.

> **THIS DOCUMENT DESCRIBES SHAPES. A DOCUMENT NOBODY CAN BE HELD TO IS NOT A SAFER DOCUMENT. IT IS ONLY A DOCUMENT THAT HAS NOT BEEN ARGUED WITH YET, AND THE DAY IT IS ARGUED WITH IS THE ONLY DAY THAT MATTERS.**

"You have not signed that," said Frances Tolley.

"**No.**

"Why not, when you initialled the card on the board in August."

"Because the card in August was a *condition*, and a condition nobody has claimed can be put in an edition, and a condition in an edition is a rule, and a rule is a thing this city can be held to and is a thing a person can be made to live under." She put the pencil down. "This is a sentence about a document. **The day I initial a sentence about a document it becomes a rule about documents, and in about four years there will be a form, and the form will have my initials in the corner of it, and somebody in a basement will say that it is Nina Calder's page, and it will be, and that will be the end of it.** So it goes out unsigned, and anybody may take it down and add their name to it, and about four hundred people will, and that is not worse, that is four hundred arguments instead of one document nobody can touch."

"That is a genuinely new sentence," said Frances Tolley.

"It is a genuinely new sentence and I have been keeping it since about the eleventh of September and I have not written it down, because writing it down makes it a scheme, and a scheme is a list with a future in it." She turned the sheet back. "**A form is the last thing a person writes and the first thing an institution keeps.** I said that at a counter on the first of September and I am not going to unsay it now that I have a piece of paper in my hand."

---

**The four.**

Jonas Mercer had worked the difference out on the Friday and he had it written on the fourth envelope, and he read it out, and Calder stopped him after the third.

"That one is a node at a private clinic's residential floor in Kestrel Hill. The trust put it back on a schedule on the twenty-ninth of October at Tier 2, four thousand two hundred pounds, term to the sixth of January, having let it lapse at six o'clock on the fourth of August and having done nothing about it for eleven weeks. **That is a shape because a trust re-priced a floor over a garden and nobody on that floor is a party and nobody was asked.**"

"That's one," said Frances Tolley.

"**Two and three are two survey nodes placed in October by a freeholder at a block built in 1938, a riser space and a roof void, at Tier 1, eighteen hundred and eighty pounds each, and they are on a schedule and there is no party answering either of them because there is no party for a block.**" He put the envelope down. "Those two have cost thirty-seven pounds sixty a year, which is the first money anybody in this city has ever spent on that block that was not paid by somebody in it, and neither of them will put a drop of water in the building, and both of them are correct."

"So that is three," said Frances Tolley, "and the difference is four, and you are not going to read me the fourth."

"**No.**"

"Will you tell me the fourth yourself, in this room, without writing it down, so that there are two of us."

"No."

"**Then why not.**"

"Because the fourth one is a shape that there is exactly one of in this borough," said Jonas Mercer, "and if I say it in this room then it is a sentence with three people in it, and in about four years it will be a sentence with a lot of people in it, and a sentence with a lot of people in it about a thing that has no address is a **direction**." He did not raise his voice. "**A record with a number in it and no name on it is the first thing anybody in this city has built that can be argued with, and I will not hand it over with a street in it, and I have spent seven months refusing to be the man who says a street out loud in a room and I am not going to stop on a Wednesday because it would have been useful to you.**"

Nina Calder did not look up from the sheet.

"**Good,**" she said. "**That is the first time in four months that you have been the right person in one of my rooms, and it is a miserable thing to be praised for, and I am going to the chair at four thirty with a piece of paper and it will not be adopted, because I am not asking her to adopt it and I have not suggested it and I will not.**"

"What will she do with it."

"She will receive it and she will refuse to adopt it and she will say so in a minute and she will ask for that refusal to be minuted, and then it will be published in November, because a document received by a Committee is a public thing whether anybody likes it or not." She squared the sheets. "**And you will not be on it. There is no field for you and there is not going to be one, and that is the fourth of my sentences of this year and I have said the other three to you in this corridor at different times and you have written all of them down.**"

"You said on the twenty-sixth of September that you wanted it recorded that a man with a register had not been told what was in a regulation, so that in four years it would not look as though a registrar had kept a thing back."

"Yes."

"**This is the same sentence with a number on it.**"

"Yes," said Nina Calder. "**And a number can be checked, which a sentence cannot, and that is the whole of the difference between September and November, and it is nine weeks, and it has taken me nine weeks and a man who does not know how to draw.**"

---

She took it up to the chair at half past four and the chair received it and declined to adopt it and asked for the declining to be minuted, and it was published in the third week of November with the number on it and the four on it and the difference between thirteen and nine on it and no name on it anywhere and no box for a name.

**And on the first morning it was on the counter, a man of about fifty stood in front of it for about nine minutes and worked out where he was, and everybody in that room let him, because the whole of the design of that page is that a person is allowed to find out that it is in a document, and there is no power in this city to make him do anything about it, and it is checkable, and being checkable is the only thing anybody in this borough has ever been able to do to anybody else.**

**And the thirteenth shape is in it. And the twelfth is the fourth of the four. And the fourth of the four is a meter room in a building of thirty-one rented rooms, Tideglass, on which the holder field is empty, and it is the only one of the thirteen that a person could walk to, and it is on a public document, and it names nobody, and the page says in its own last line that it may not be matched against any list of addresses.**

**And it can be walked to. That is what nobody in that room on the first morning thought about, and it is what the woman who wrote the page thought about in a corridor on the nineteenth of November, standing up, with her coat on, before she had said goodnight to anybody.**
