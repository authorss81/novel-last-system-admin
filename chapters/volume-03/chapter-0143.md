# Chapter 0143 — For Whom

Saturday the second of May 2015, from about ten past ten in the morning, the records office, second floor, the Old Ward. And a corridor outside it.

**He asked no question of anybody in that office or in that corridor, and he has not asked one since the twenty-fifth of February, and the counting has been right in every week of this year. There were five questions of a stranger's in that corridor at about half past ten, the shortest of them four words, he asked every one of them at the counter and not one of them at the wall, and not one of them was about anything, and he was given one thing and the one thing he was given was a sheet of paper that is public and requires nothing of anybody.**

---

He was thirty-three. He worked nights and he was on nothing and had been on nothing since he was twenty, and he had come in on a Saturday morning because a man at work had said there was a minute you could have, and that it was free, and that you did not have to ask anybody for it.

The building is open on a Saturday morning for that counter and it has been open for years, and nobody has ever written down why, and the answer is a car park and a canteen and a man who comes in at nine to do the photocopying and goes at twelve, and he is not paid for the Saturday and he comes anyway and has never been asked to and would not know what to do if he were.

**Jonas Mercer had come in on a Saturday for the first time in nine months, and he had not come for anything, and there was a man at the end of that corridor who had come for something, and neither of them knew the other was there for the first twenty minutes of that morning.**

The counter on that floor is about eight feet long and there is a window in it that has never had a grille on it, and the woman at it was about fifty and she had been at that counter for eleven years. **She is not the woman who comes down to the ground floor at about ten and at about three, and the two of them have never been the same person, and there is nothing anywhere in that building which says so and there is not going to be anything — and the woman who comes down to that counter, at about ten and about three, is the only person in this city who keeps the minutes, and that woman was two floors down at that moment, and she is not going to be told that any of this happened.**

She gave him the minute of the twenty-seventh of April. It went out on the Friday. It is public, it is unsigned, it has no crest on it, and it requires no person to do anything at all.

"**Is that all of it.**"

"**That is all of it.**"

"**Right.**"

---

He read it standing up in the corridor, on the side with the fire door on the wedge.

He is not a fast reader. He read the first two items and stopped on the fourth and then read the fourth again, and the fourth item is the only item on that sheet with a person in it, and the person in it has no name in it and is not on the agenda and is not a member and is not a departmental officer.

**And in the middle of that item there is a sentence about what this floor is for, and the sentence names a class of people, and the class has no members in it that anybody can point at, and the man reading it stood in a corridor on a Saturday morning and found that out.**

On the wall at the end of that corridor, at about eye height, there is a notice that has been there a long time and defines nothing and requires nothing of anybody.

> **THE RECORD OF THIS CITY IS AVAILABLE TO ANY PERSON, FREE, AND WITHOUT A FORM.**
> **ANYBODY MAY HAVE IT BY POST, OR CHECK IT AGAINST THE BOROUGH'S OWN PAPER IN PERSON.**
> **NO PERSON IS ASKED FOR ANYTHING IN ORDER TO HAVE IT, AND NOTHING ON IT REQUIRES ANY PERSON TO DO ANYTHING.**

Nobody has ever asked him whether the person he is thinking of is on the record, and nobody is going to, and whatever the record says about anybody it does not say anything about her.

---

"**It says what this floor is for.**"

The woman at the counter did not look up from what she was doing with a bundle of paper.

"**It does.**"

"**It says it's for a group of people. It doesn't say which ones.**"

"**No.**"

"**How would somebody find out whether they were one of them.**"

And she squared a bundle of paper with the edge of the counter and set it down and said:

"**You cannot. I could not do it for you this morning and I could not do it for you in a month and I am not going to try, and I want you to understand that the not-trying is the correct thing and not a brush-off.**"

"**Right.**"

"**Here is why, and then you can decide what you want to do on your own time. The only way I could put a person inside that sentence is by making a new sheet. Anything new I write has got a box on it. A box wants a name in it. And the day a name goes in a box in this building, that person is on something, and anybody in this city can then ask that person things, and that person cannot say no and mean it, because being on it will be a thing that person did to themselves, in a room, with me, in about eleven seconds, and they will have done it because they wanted help.**"

---

He stood in the corridor and looked at the sheet in his hand.

"**So the minute's got no name on it.**"

"**The minute has got no box on it either, and those are the same thing, and it is not an accident and it is not an oversight and I put it that way myself in March and I have not regretted it once.**"

"**Then what is the sheet for. If it can't say who it is for.**"

"**It says who it is for. It does it in the only way there is, which is by naming a kind of person instead of a person. That is a real thing and it is a useful thing and it is the only way a public document is allowed to do it, and it is why nobody in this city can be written down as being helped by reading it.**"

She turned a sheet over and squared it with the edge of the counter.

"**And here is the other half, and this is the half that took me eleven years at this counter and I am giving it to you for nothing on a Saturday morning. A sheet with no box on it is a sheet that a person has to produce about themselves. That is what it is. It is not a sheet anybody can produce about anybody else. So the one document in this city that could tell you whether a person can be helped is the one that person has to bring with them, and the document is them, and there is nowhere on that sheet where them would go.**"

"**So I cannot ask.**"

"**You cannot ask, and I cannot ask, and there is nobody in this building you can ask, and I am not being kind to you, I am telling you where the wall is.**"

---

**And then he said the thing he had come to say, and it is two questions, and it is the question the minute was never written to answer.**

He had got as far as saying what was wrong with her, in a corridor, on a day that was not this one, and had stopped, and had said *somebody I work with* instead, and had done it without appearing to notice that he had done it.

"**I am asking because of somebody I work with.**"

"**Then I am not going to ask you who, and you had better not tell me.**"

"**Right.**"

"**Who is it for. How do I get to be one.**"

"**No.**"

"**It is a public minute. It is free. It is on the wall of a corridor with a notice about it.**"

"**It is free and it is public and you may have it and keep it and put it in a drawer and show it to everybody in this city, and none of that is the question, and you know it is not the question, because you did not ask it.**"

"**No.**"

"**No. And that is the whole of it, and I am going to say the last of it in one go and then I am going to pick my bundle up again. A sentence about who a floor is for is not a route to a floor. It is not a list and it is not a queue and it is not a way in. It tells a man like you that you are the sort of person a thing is for, and it gives you nothing at all to take with you, and that is not a flaw in the minute. That is what a minute is.**"

---

**And Jonas Mercer was at the end of that corridor, at the wall, about nine feet from the counter, for the whole of it, and he did not say one word, and nobody at that counter asked him anything, and the man never knew he was there.**

Jonas Mercer had one question available to him in nine months and he has not spent it since the twenty-fifth of February, and this was not the morning to spend it, and he worked that out in about four seconds and it did not take him four seconds to work out the other thing and that is where the four seconds went.

**The man did not say who he was thinking of.**

He said *a group of people* and *somebody I work with* and he did not say a name, and he did not say where she works, and he did not say what is wrong with her, and Jonas Mercer has watched a great many people make a mistake in nine months and that is the first time he has watched somebody not make one.

**And a man who is not on a board and is not on a list and has not been asked anything by anybody either did not say a name, on a Saturday morning, in a corridor, and put it back: a person who is not in a record cannot be talked about in a place where the talking might be written down, because the writing down is the part that lasts, and the talking is the part that does not. Whether he worked that out in about four seconds or whether he has simply never once done the other thing is not knowable and is not going to be looked into.**

Nobody taught him that. Nobody in this borough is able to teach anybody that. **And Jonas Mercer is the person who made the first half of it cheap, in a room, in daylight, in nine seconds, on the third of December, and he has never met this man and is not going to and did not ask him one word.**

---

**And nobody was helped. Nobody was harmed. Nothing was put in any document, and there is no minute of this morning and there is not going to be one, because that body has not sat since the middle of January and is not required to and has no power to require anybody to sit in it, and because a corridor is the only place in this building where a conversation can happen without leaving anything behind, and a man in a corridor having a conversation is not an establishment.**

The man of thirty-three stood in the corridor for a while longer with the sheet in his hand and then he went down the stairs and out into the street with it, and it is a public document and it requires nothing of him and he may burn it, and he put it in the inside of his coat, which is what everybody does with a piece of paper in this borough.

**And that is the third and last of the three answers given in that room in nine months coming back at him from the outside of the building, and it has cost a Saturday morning and a piece of paper and nobody was helped, and it was correct, and it is not going to be improved, and it is not going in a minute, and it is not going on a wall.**

---

Nothing in that building had moved on that Saturday morning, and the three old things in it were where they had been on Thursday: a blank line a hundred and fifty-seven days old under a nineteenth entry, a card seventy-one days into a slot, and a letter seventy-three days into a tray on the floor above, under two other pieces of paper. **The register of narrowings is at nineteen entries and it was nineteen at ten o'clock that morning and it will be nineteen on Wednesday.**

**And the term in force in this borough at ten past ten on that Saturday morning was still the fourteenth, six days long, and it stops at six o'clock on the Sunday morning, and for the hour after that this city has nothing in it at all, and there is no date anywhere in this city on that hour, and he has stood in it three times, and he is not going to stand in it a fourth, and nobody has ever asked him about any of that either.**

**And the eleven of them are where they were on the thirteenth of March, and the count is still eleven and not twelve, and the reason it has not become twelve is that a correct refusal about a wallet of paper is not a new request, and nobody has been sent anywhere today and nobody is going to be.**
