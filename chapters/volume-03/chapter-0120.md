# Chapter 0120 — What Is On The Floor

**Wednesday the twenty-eighth of January 2015, about eleven in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward.**

They moved the meeting into the vestibule on purpose and nobody can say who decided it, and about nine people went into a room that is eleven feet by nine and had to stand sideways against the wall, and there is no chair, because a body of nine seats has one power and a chair is not one of the three parts of it, and the ninth seat has been going through the order of things since July because she is the only one of the nine anybody can write to, and she has said in a room that she is not one and nobody is to make her into one, and the woman who keeps the minutes stood at the end of the counter with her sheet because there is no table in the room.

**It was in daylight. That is the only procedural thing anybody did differently and it was done on purpose and nobody minuted that it was on purpose.**

There is a board on the wall at the end of the counter with **nineteen entries** on it and a **blank line** under the nineteenth, and the blank line is the only fixed point in the building. There is a slot in the counter for post and it has been empty since the eighteenth of July. Sixteen inches above the board, where nine people have to look up, there are the two conditions, and they are still sixteen inches apart and still do not agree, and nobody in that room said a word about either of them: one of them is a printed sheet the city put up on the fourteenth of August with two letters in the corner of it, and the other is a strip of enamel that has never had anything written on the front of it in this account and has two letters and one pencilled word on the back, and the word is *not mine*.

**Jonas Mercer was against the wall with nothing in his hands. He was not on the agenda, not a party, not a member and not a departmental officer, and the woman who keeps the minutes said so at the top of the morning in those words, and nobody in the room could make her say anything else, and nobody asked. He asked no question in that room, and there was no question in it that was his, and the counting has been right in every chapter of this year.**

---

**And before anything else was said, the room said the things that are true, because it does that now, and it does it in about nine minutes, and it is the only agenda in this borough that begins with a list of what has not been done.**

The lowland cannot be seated. It is not among the districts the layer draws, and a node with no party is not a party, and the registrar has said that in a corridor and will say it again, and it is correct, and it was not corrected this morning.

A man of forty-four in a building in Gantry Ward signed a line in about ninety seconds in August and **nobody in this borough is able to ask him one question about any of it, ever**, and two people have said so to Jonas Mercer and he has not asked.

A woman of thirty-four is in about eleven people and in no document, and there is no list of who has it and there is not going to be one, and nobody is to go down there.

A woman of seventy-two in room 14 has a sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet on her door about a condition survey of a riser space and a roof void, and she is no more findable than she was on the sixth of May and she is a great deal more frightened, and there is no reason anybody would recognise, and nobody has gone up those stairs since the fourteenth of November.

A woman of sixty-one is on no board, in no field and on no rota, and has a number of Wednesdays on a card under a rubber band in a biscuit tin, and it is a number of Wednesdays and it is not a number of people and nobody has ever asked her about it and nobody is going to.

**And the ninth seat of that body is the only person in a building of thirty-one rented rooms that anybody can write to, and nobody in that room has ever told her that, and nobody in that room is going to, and the reason is a card in a plastic sleeve on a wall in a Methodist hall and not anybody's kindness.**

**Four of the nine have said they will stop if it turns into a job, and one of them has been asked why she means it and has given a reason.**

"**Ines Tarrant said in November that she meant it,**" said the woman who keeps the minutes, reading it off the sheet, "**and she has not withdrawn it, and she has not stopped, and she asked on the twenty-sixth of November to be asked why, and nobody was able to ask her, because nobody in that room can require a person to answer a question.**"

"I am asking her," said the ninth seat, and turned round. "**Ines. You said you wanted to be asked. I am asking. You do not have to answer and I will not ask you twice and it will not go in the minute.**"

And Ines Tarrant, 44, car park supervisor, eleven years, said it in about nine seconds, and it was this: "**Because I have been the nearest person to a barrier for eleven years and nobody has ever asked me what it is for, and if this turns into a job then I am a woman with a job on a board and the nearest person to the barrier is a woman with a job on a board, and in about four years somebody will use that to tell me what the barrier is for. And I am still here. That is all I have got. I have not got an answer and I have got a reason.**"

Nobody argued. The ninth seat said, "Minutes," and the woman who keeps the minutes said she would not put it in, and both of those were correct.

---

**And then, at about a quarter past eleven, a man who is not a member and did not come as a member and has come to that body twice since November said it from the wall, and he is about fifty-six, and he is not on any list, and he asked the question.**

He did not introduce it. He did not say *may I ask*. He said it into a room of nine people in eleven feet by nine, in daylight, standing sideways, the way a person says a thing they have been carrying in a car.

"**What is a service floor made of.**"

Nobody answered for about nine seconds.

"**You have been in this borough for eight months,**" he said, "**and you have refused a chair, and you have refused a seat, and you have refused a form, and you have said six words out loud in a room in November and a woman's name out loud in a room in December, and I have followed all of it from a car park and I could not tell you for one of those days what any of it was for. So tell me what it is made of. **And I am not on this agenda and neither are you and that is the only reason I am asking and it is the only reason in this borough anybody can ask you anything.**"

And Jonas Mercer said, first, "**I am not going to answer that,**" and everybody in that room understood that he was going to, and the ninth seat did not press him and did not argue and said nothing, and after about four seconds he said:

"**A counter. A person who is at it. A state asked for instead of a list. A date. And a blank line at the bottom of a board that nobody is required to write on.**"

Nobody said anything at all.

"That is it," he said. "**That is the whole of it and I have not got a scheme and I am not going to give you one, and if you write any of that down it will be a scheme inside four months and you will all know what a scheme is, it is a list with a future in it, and I have watched seven of them refused in about eleven seconds at a counter in this borough in nineteen years and every single one of the seven was right.**"

---

**And then he said where it came from, because nobody was going to be told otherwise, and it is the sentence he has been carrying since the third of April without knowing it.**

"**The third of April was a Thursday and it was the worst night of my working life and it was the night two people said no.** Not to me — to a rota, to a board, to a form, to a clause; two of them, separately, in their own words, and both of them were right. And at six nineteen in the morning a gate went up anyway, and the reason it went up was that two people happened to be in a corridor and neither of them was required to be and neither of them was on anything. **I have spent eight months trying to say that in a sentence that would survive being written down, and it will not, and I have found that the only sentences that survive are the small ones with a date on them.**"

"That is not an answer," said somebody.

"**It is a description. A description is not an answer. I have been asked what it is made of and I have told you what it is made of and I have not told you what it is for, and I am not going to, and there are three people in this borough who know why and I am one of them and I am not going to say, and you are all going to have to live with that.**"

---

**And the roll-call, because a body that cannot require anybody to do anything cannot be told what it will do, and both of those sentences are on a card on a wall in a Methodist hall, and neither of them is going to be argued out of the other in this room or in any room.**

The register of narrowings is at **nineteen** and the blank line at the bottom of it is **blank**.

The September record is public and it is a record of shapes with sixteen of them in it and no total and no name box by decision, and it cannot be wrong, and that is exactly why it was a photograph. The determination of the twenty-eighth of August is public. The quarterly is public and it has a number of times in it and the source of that number in the same paragraph.

The second public record went out in the third week of November and it is **fifty-one nodes, thirty-eight parties, thirteen shapes**, checkable at a counter by anybody, for nothing, by post, as of a date. It is **unsigned**. It has no name box. **About four hundred people have added their names to it**, and about eleven have taken the extract away to check, and two of them have written back and been wrong, and the clerk had to write *enclosure received, no comment*, and that is in the file, and it is the best thing that counter has ever done.

The reading rule of the twenty-first of August is in the inside pocket of a clerk of sixty-one and it was read out once, in a corridor, in about eleven seconds, to a man of forty-four who does not know what any of the words mean, and **nobody is going to be told that it happened and nobody is going to be asked whether it worked.**

**The board stands at seven of nine.** Two chairs have been empty since the eighth of October and the evening of the twenty-sixth of November, and nobody can fill either and nobody can unfill either, and there is no power in this city that could, and two chairs is a Tuesday.

The letter worked. The four meters are not going into the Block C cupboard and they went to a block of 1974 and a condition survey is coming in the spring on a sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet with the word *feasibility* on it. **The price of that letter is a sheet of A4 on a door and a woman of seventy-two who is a great deal more frightened, and nobody was saved, and it was not supposed to save anybody.**

**Six words are his. `6-L/14. NON-CIVIC PLANT.` He said them out loud in a room twice, in October and in November, and in November he said a man of forty-nine's name with them, and the man of forty-nine can be asked one question about it tomorrow morning and has not been asked, and the man of forty-four cannot be asked at all, ever, and Jonas Mercer made both of them findable on purpose and cannot unmake either.**

**And a woman's name is in about eleven people in a Methodist hall in Tideglass and in no document at all, and there are now two pieces of paper in this city with her name on it, and one of them is in a drawer in a hall with no key and one of them is in a handbag in a building of thirty-one rented rooms, and neither of them is a document and neither can be used for anything, and she put them both there herself, in daylight, with nobody watching, and she was given nothing and she asked for nothing and she came on a bus she had planned herself.**

**And the winter has one Sunday in it, and the Sunday is the fourth of January.** At ten o'clock on the Saturday night before it, in a waiting area with four chairs and a machine for hot water that has not worked since August, two people who are not on anything read five lines out loud to each other, and there is no document about it, and there is not going to be one, and the gate opened at seven on the fourth of January because of it and for no other reason, and **nobody in this borough is going to be told that it worked.**

---

**And at about twenty to twelve, with about four people still talking in a corridor outside, Jonas Mercer said the thing he had been carrying since the twenty-third of April, and he said it out loud, once, in a room, in daylight, and it was the last door in this volume and it did not open all the way.**

"A borough safety panel chair of fifty-eight and eleven years made a finding on the twenty-third of April and it is in a minute and almost nobody in this borough has read it twice. And I have had it since April and I have not said it in a room, and I am going to say it now, once, and then I am going to stop saying it, because a thing said in a room cannot be un-said and I have been doing that all year and I know what it costs."

He said it to the floor and not to anybody. It took about eleven seconds.

"**A building has an address. It is the people who have no location of record. And a panel cannot weigh a claim from a person it cannot locate.**"

The room did not move.

"**That is Prue Calloway's finding of the twenty-third of April, and I am not asking anybody to do anything about it and nothing in it requires any person to do anything, and I am not going to say what I think it means, because I have written in a pad eleven times since March that I do not know and eleven is a number of times and it is not a number of people.**"

"And what do you think it means," said the man of about fifty-six.

"**That is not an answer to your question and I am not going to give you one, and here is why, and here is the thing I have wanted to say since August: I think I know what it means, and there is a fourth page in a document from 2008 that **three** people in this borough have read and told nobody, and **I am not one of the three and I am not going to say who the three are**, and I am not going to say what is on that page in a room with nine people in it, and I want that minuted as a decision and not as a virtue, because I have wanted to and I am not going to and that is the whole of what I have to say about myself this morning.**"

The woman who keeps the minutes said, and this is the whole of what is in it:

> **Item 4. A person present, not on the agenda, not a party, not a member and not a departmental officer, was asked a question in the room, in daylight, by a person who is also not on the agenda. He declined at first and then answered. His answer is recorded above and consists of five items and no scheme.**
>
> **Recorded: that a finding of the borough safety panel of 23 April 2014 was stated in the room, once, and is recorded, and that it was stated by a person and not by the panel, and that no person in the room was asked to do anything and no person in the room asked to be asked to do anything.**
>
> **Recorded: that this minute has no box for the name of a person, and that the sheet is the minute until about four o'clock and after that it is only a sheet, and that nobody has asked her about it in eight weeks and she was asked once and answered.**
>
> **Nothing in this item requires any person to do anything.**

She read it back standing up, in the room, which she does, and it went out on the Friday, and it is a public document, and it is in the borough's records, and it contains no names at all.

---

**The ninth seat is fifty-four and unpaid and findable and she is the only one of the nine anybody can write to, and nobody in that room said anything about that, and there is a fourth page of a regulation from 2008 in three people's heads in this borough, and one of the three wrote it out by hand in an afternoon in May because she did not trust herself not to improve it, and it is in none of what was said this morning, and it stays where it is, and nobody is going to be told.**

**And the Saint Orra west terms are six days. They have been six days since the first week of April. The rota has still not been rebuilt. The eleven o'clock to half past one gap is still published and still unfilled. Bram Osei's Thursday is still refused on a board in his own words with *no reason I can give* under a *no reason given* in somebody else's hand. Priya Ranganathan's card is still on that board because she put it there. The gate still runs on its local term and cannot be renewed by the department, by a contractor, by a manager or by a man with civic standing and no grade, and it can only be renewed by two people in a waiting area with four chairs at ten o'clock at night.**

**The term in force was read at ten o'clock on Saturday the twenty-fourth of January, in a waiting area with four chairs, by two people who are not on anything, and it began at seven o'clock on Sunday the twenty-fifth and it expires at six o'clock on Sunday the first of February. There is no date written down anywhere in this borough for that Saturday night either, and there is not going to be a date for any of them, because a term runs from seven on a Sunday to six on the Sunday after, and the handover is on the Saturday night in between, and a Saturday night is seven days, and there has been one of them every seven days since the first week of April: the thirty-first of January, the seventh of February, the fourteenth, the twenty-first, the twenty-eighth, and there will be one on the seventh of March, which is the seventh of them in six weeks, and there is no date on any piece of paper in this city for the seventh of them, or the first, or any of the five in between, because a person is not required to be told, and there is nobody in this borough who is required to write it.**
