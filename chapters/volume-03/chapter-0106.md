# Chapter 0106 — A Letter Came Back

**Wednesday the twelfth of November 2014, four o'clock in the afternoon, the rear of Carrow Lane.**

You can tell a great deal about a block from about ninety feet away in about four minutes, and Jonas Mercer stood across the service alley on the cobbles with his hands in his coat and did it.

**There was a skip outside the rear block that had not been there on the twenty-ninth of September.** There was a scaffold tower at the north end that had one lift and no boards on the top platform, which is how they stand when they are measuring. There was a folding barrier round the bottom of the light in the yard, which in this borough means a bulb is out and cannot be replaced, and the reason a bulb cannot be replaced in a yard behind a block of rented rooms is that there is no party for the yard.

**And there was no workman at the meter cupboard.**

He had known the cupboard was in a corner of the ground floor at the bottom of a flight. He had never been inside it and had no reason to and had said in a shop in September that he was not going to look at it.

There was a new padlock hasp on the frame of the cover plate. The cover plate had four slotted screws and a plastic lifting handle and it was the plate he had described from memory in a letter written in ink on a bench, and it had a padlock on it now, and the padlock was a good one, and it was not the cupboard that had been locked in a way that mattered, because the stopcock is under the cover plate and the cover plate is a screw-down plate and four screws do not stop anybody who has a screwdriver and thirty seconds.

**The four meters were not going into that cupboard.**

He stood there for about eleven minutes and worked it out from the outside, in order, the way he does everything.

**One: the four meters had not been installed.** The water in that block is what it was in April and it is what it was in July and there is a woman on the top landing who has carried it up four flights in containers on a Tuesday since the sixth of May, and the riser died in February 1998.

**Two: the freeholder's standing instruction of the fourteenth of April had been withdrawn.** He did not know that from any document. He knew it from the fact that a man had come out of a firm in Salter's Yard, who is not a woman he has spoken to, and told nobody.

**Three: something else was going somewhere.** The four meters had gone to a different block. Not a different cupboard in the same block — a *different block*, and one of the eight, and the one at the top of the lane where the bins are, which is 1974 and has a communal hot water main and about forty flats and a landlord who has been in correspondence with that firm since April.

**Which meant the freeholder had read its own records, and had found four meters going to a block that had asked for nothing, and had gone and asked for them somewhere it did not have to, and the somewhere it did not have to was a block of 1974 where everybody is at home in August.**

**Four: there was a notice on the door of the rear block, under a sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet, and it had been there for about a fortnight, and the plastic wallet was the same make as the one on the notice about the lift.**

> **BLOCK C, REAR. CONSTRUCTED 1938. INTERNAL RISER REMOVED 1998. INCOMING SUPPLY ISOLATED 6 MAY 2014. THE FREEHOLDER'S INTENTION IS TO CARRY OUT A CONDITION SURVEY OF THE RISER SPACE AND THE ROOF VOID AND TO REPORT ON THE FEASIBILITY OF RESTORED SUPPLY.**
>
> **THE OCCUPIER OF EACH DWELLING WILL BE NOTIFIED OF A DATE. NO OCCUPIER IS REQUIRED TO BE PRESENT.**

The block was constructed in 1938. The riser was removed in 1998. **Past tense, past tense, past tense, and then one future tense with a word in it — *feasibility* — that has never once in the history of this city produced a restored anything.**

Jonas Mercer read it three times, standing up, in the cold, about ninety feet from a door he had never knocked on.

**The letter worked.** That was the first thing. It worked completely, in eleven weeks, against a firm with four hundred and ten flats and a standing instruction and a man who reads his own records. It had stopped four meters going into a cupboard in a block with no water in it, and it had done it with a year and a stopcock and a plastic handle and one name at the bottom and no household in it at all, and nobody had been asked to confirm anything, and it is the cheapest thing anybody in this borough has produced this year.

**And the price of it is on a sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet, and the price is that there is now a document about that block, and the document is a freeholder's, and the freeholder has noticed that there is a document about that block, because the document is unusual, and he is going to be careful with it in the way that people are careful with things that are unusual.**

**And Ivy Marsden, seventy-two years old, in room 14, has carried water up four flights for thirty rooms since 1998 and has been asked twice for a letter and refused twice, and does not know that a letter exists.**

---

He went up on the Thursday.

**He went up on the Thursday the thirteenth of November, at about half past three in the afternoon, in daylight, in about nine minutes' notice to himself, and he did not telephone ahead because a telephone call would have turned it into an appointment and an appointment is a thing a person can decline, and he had come to give a person the right to decline a thing.**

Room 14 is on the fourth floor and there is no lift and there has not been a lift since before she moved in. The stairs have a handrail on two sides. On the second landing somebody has left a bicycle against the wall in the way that has been the way since about 2011.

**She opened the door before he knocked, which he took at the time to be a coincidence and did not take at the time to be a woman of seventy-two who has heard every stranger in this building go up those stairs for nineteen years and can tell the weight of them.**

"Yes?"

"My name is Jonas Mercer." He said it in the way a person says a thing they have not said in five months, which is badly. "I am the man who wrote the letter."

The woman on the other side of the door did not move at all for about four seconds, and then she said:

"**Then you will want to come in, and I will tell you now that I am going to be polite about it, and I am also going to tell you that I have not asked you here and I am going to hear you out and then I am going to shut the door.**"

The room is about eleven feet by nine, and it is a top-floor room over a landing with a bed, a chair, a table with a cloth on it, a photograph of a woman of about sixty with a cardigan, a television, a telephone that still has a dial on it, and a chair by the window with a blanket over the arm of it that is not for warmth.

There were two empty plastic containers by the door with the tops off. There was a third one in the corner that had a lid on it and water in it.

"You have come about the meters," she said.

"Yes."

"No."

"I know. They are not going in. That is why I have come. I wrote the letter that stopped them and you have not been told that a letter exists, and I decided in about four minutes on Monday that I would tell you, and I have thought about it for two days and I have come on a Thursday afternoon with about nine minutes' notice, which is the worst way there is of doing it, and I am aware of that."

"Then sit down, because you are not going to be worse than that and I am seventy-two and I have got a chair."

---

She would not have a copy. That took eleven seconds and it was not remotely difficult and it was the fastest thing in the chapter.

"**No,**" she said. "**Not because of you. Because a copy of it would be a thing in this flat, and I am seventy-two and I do not want a thing in this flat that came from anybody, because the day it is here it is a thing somebody can ask me about and I would have to be a person who has a letter.** I have been a woman with a stopcock and a bucket since 1998 and I would like to be that until I am not, and that is not a virtue and I am not being brave about it, it is just a preference, and it is the only thing in this room that is mine."

"I am not going to send you a copy."

"You are not, because I have told you not to, and you will not, and in about four years somebody is going to come into this block and ask whether this flat has had correspondence, and the answer will be no, and the answer will be true." She put her hands flat on the table cloth. "**Now. You have come to tell me a thing and you have not told me the thing yet. You have told me the meters. The meters are the easy half.**"

"There is a notice on the door downstairs."

"There is."

"It is about a condition survey of the riser space and the roof void and the feasibility of restored supply."

"**Feasibility.**" She said the word the way you say the name of a thing you have been sold. "I have been in this borough fifty-one years. There was a feasibility in this street in about 1994 and they did the feasibility and the feasibility was a report and the report went in a drawer and there has been a lift out of the top of this building for about nineteen years and nobody ever asked for a lift again."

"They are not going to do anything with it. I want to be straight with you. Feasibility has never once in this city produced a restored anything and I have no reason to think this one is different, and I did not write a notice and I cannot unwrite a notice."

"Then what did you write," said Ivy Marsden.

And Jonas Mercer had come up four flights with an answer to that and it went out of him on the landing, in a hallway, in the way that a thing goes out of you when you have rehearsed it on a hill.

"**A letter with a year on it. Saying the buckets are the block's and not yours, so that in about four years when the freeholder asks whose the water is, the answer is a building and not a person.**"

She looked at him for about four seconds.

"**Yes,**" she said. "**That is what it is for. And I have had two and a half days to work that out on my own and I got to it on about the second, and it took me two days and you took eleven months, and I am not being unkind, I am being accurate, and I would like to know what you want me to say about that.**"

"I do not want you to say anything about that."

"Then what do you want."

"**I want to tell you that it worked, and that the price of it is a sheet of A4 on a door, and that the four meters have gone to a different block where everybody is in in August, and that I did not know any of that until ninety feet away on Wednesday, and that you have not been told any of it and I have now told you and that is the whole of what I came to do.**"

She nodded for about four seconds and then she said the thing.

"**There is a third half of that and you have not got to it and you are not going to, so I will.**" She put her hand on the table. "**You have written me a letter so that the buckets would be the block's and not mine. It has not worked. It is not going to work. And here is exactly why, and then you can go down those stairs and I will not say anything else to you for as long as you live.**

"**The day a piece of paper says the buckets are the block's, the block is a thing that can be asked. And a thing that can be asked is a thing that has a landlord. And a thing that has a landlord is a thing that has a list. That is the whole of how this country works and it has worked that way since about 1994 and it is not going to be argued with by a man with a pen.**

"**I did not want a letter for me. I wanted a letter so that in about four years, when somebody comes into this block and asks whose the water is, there is one true thing already on paper and I am not standing in a corridor being asked the question.** And that is what I have got. And it is the best thing anybody has done for me since 1998 and it is the reason there is a survey coming in the spring."

"Then I have made a mistake."

"**You have made a Tuesday,**" said Ivy Marsden. "You have made a Tuesday and it was not your fault and it is not anybody's fault and that is what a Tuesday is."

---

She got up, which took a while and which she did not apologise for, and went to the corner and put the hand on the container with the lid on it.

"This is Tuesday's," she said. "The others go up on Tuesday. There is not enough coming in that door for thirty flats since the sixth of May and there has not been since February 1998 and before February 1998 it was not this building's problem because there was a riser."

"Mrs Marsden—"

"**And you have given me your name.**" She said it with her back to him. "**You have given me your name. That is the second thing that has happened to me in sixteen years and both of them were done by a man who thought he was being careful.**"

He did not answer that and there was nothing to answer it with.

"Your name is now in a thing in your head that has to do with me," she said, turning round, "and I am going to die before about a third of the things you are going to be asked about, and when that happens somebody is going to ring a council and say *there is a man in this borough who went to Ivy Marsden's door*, and they will be right, and it will be in a file, and there is going to be a piece of paper with my name at the top of it and your name under it and the year 1998 in the middle of it, and there is not going to be one line anywhere in that piece of paper that says I asked for it."

He stood up.

"That is true."

"**Yes,**" said Ivy Marsden. "**And you may write that down. That is the first thing anybody has said to me in sixteen years that I would let a piece of paper say.**"

And she opened the door, and the corridor on the fourth floor of the rear block of Carrow Lane smells of other people's cooking, and he went down four flights, and there is no lift, and there has not been a lift since before she moved in.

---

He did not write it down that night. He wrote it down on the Friday, at the bench, in four lines, and it is the first entry in the pad since the twenty-second of October that is longer than a sentence.

> *Friday 14 November. Ivy Marsden, 72, room 14, four flights and no lift, on Thursday afternoon, in daylight, about nine minutes' notice, without telephoning first because a telephone call makes it an appointment.*
> *She would not have a copy and the reason was not me. She will not have a thing in that flat that came from anybody. In about four years somebody is going to ask whether room 14 has had correspondence and the answer will be no and it will be true.*
> *She worked out what the letter was for in two days. I took eleven months. She is not being unkind about that and she is being accurate and I do not want it argued with.*
> *The letter worked. Four meters are not going in and they have gone to a block of 1974. A condition survey is coming in the spring and it is on a sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet and it says feasibility and feasibility has never in this city produced a restored anything. She said that and she is right and she is the reason I came up and not a notice.*
> *And the third half, which I did not have: a letter that makes the block a thing that can be asked has made the block a thing that has a landlord, and a thing that has a landlord has a list, and that is how it has worked since 1994. She said it on a landing with her back to me and then she said my name is now in a thing in her head that has to do with her, and that when she dies somebody is going to ring a council, and there is not going to be one line anywhere in it that says she asked.*
> *Nobody in this borough can make a person findable. I have been telling that to people since April as though it were a wall. It is not a wall. It is a thing that lasts about as long as one person, and she is the one who has just spent it, and she did not have to, and I did not ask her to, and she is seventy-two and I put my own name on her door.*
> *Nobody was saved. There is a woman with a bucket on a Tuesday and there is going to be a survey in the spring and she is no more findable than she was on Monday and she is a great deal more frightened, and she told me so in a kitchen, and she shut the door.*
