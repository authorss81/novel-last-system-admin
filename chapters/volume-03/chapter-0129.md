# Chapter 0129 — A Service Floor, In February

**Friday the sixth of March 2015, about ten past ten in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward. And Monday the ninth of March, about twenty past three in the afternoon, the same room.**

**There was one question of his in that vestibule on the Friday morning and he asked it at about ten past ten and it was five words, and the woman he asked it to did not answer it, and forty minutes later she answered it to a stranger for nothing. Both of those are correct and neither of them is an accident, and the difference between them is the whole of what is in it.**
---

It is eleven feet by nine. There is a counter along the left with a slot in it for post, a board at the end of it, two conditions on the wall sixteen inches apart, a corridor forty-one feet long with a public door on the end of it, and **about eleven people a day now, where it was about nine on the twenty-sixth of January.**

**That is the first thing that has changed in here and the only one anybody has noticed, and it has not been written down anywhere, and the woman who knows it has not written it down and is not going to, and it is on the back of a hand, and a number a person can be held to is a level of service, and the day there is a level of service somebody can cut it.**

**Everything else in that room is exactly as it was on the twenty-sixth of January, thirty-nine days ago, and it is worth doing the whole inventory again because the man who did it then is the one this borough is arguing with, including him, and apart from the card in the slot and the number of people coming through the door, nothing has moved.**

**The register has nineteen entries on it.** The meter room, the riser space, the yard light, and the bulb is out. **The blank line under the nineteenth is blank.** It has been blank since the twenty-sixth of November, which is a hundred days on Friday, and Nina Calder said out loud in a corridor on Monday the first of December that if anybody in this borough ever used that board against anybody they would not use the meter room, they would use the bulb, and **the bulb is still a bulb and it is still out and she is still right, and being right about that is the reason the line has stayed empty for longer than it has ever stayed.**

**The two conditions have never been reconciled.** The city's own paper, printed the fourteenth of August with two letters in the corner in a hand that is not a capital hand. And an enamel strip with nothing on its face, undated, unattributed, two letters and one word in pencil on the back of it, and the word is **not mine**, and the two letters are not going to be decided by anybody.

**And the slot in the counter has had a card in it since the Friday before last and nobody has taken it out and nobody has put it up.**

---

**And the drawer under the counter is a drawer and it is not locked, and there is a tray sitting on top of it, and from about four feet away the edges of a stack of paper are visible in it, and Jonas Mercer did not count them and did not look at them and did not ask.**

**There are nine, and that is the only count of them that exists in the world, and the person who could produce it has not been asked and is not going to tell, and it is in this account for the same reason everything else is in this account: because this has been counting things in this borough that somebody is supposed to be counting since the fourth of September.**

They are in one hand. They are about four inches by two and they are the slips she has been handing out since the twenty-sixth of January: **about a market and a trough, and about the six water points, and about a standpipe on a post at the bottom of a car park, and about a great many other things that about eleven people a day bring in off the street with them.** They have a date on each one and a hand on each one and **no reference, no number, no crest, no form, no box, and no name on any of them, including hers.** They are not a series. There is no form in this borough, on any counter, on any tray, in any office, on which anybody could write *these are not a series*, and the reason there is not is that a box that says *not a series* is a series.

**Nobody put them there on an instruction. Nobody told her to. There is no rota, no board, no establishment, no job description, no edition, no standing order and no handbook, and she is not on one and cannot be made to be on one, and if a second person in this borough wrote down the word *service* next to a description of what she is doing, the description would be accurate and the word would finish it.**

---

"**What is this counter for.**"

Five words, at about ten past ten, in a public room, on a Friday morning, and he had not spent his question that day.

Nina Calder put her pen down and looked at him.

"**I am not going to answer that,**" she said, "**and I am going to give you the three reasons, in order, and the order is the whole of it, and then I am not going to say anything else about it this morning.**"

"Reasons."

"**One: it is not a question about the records office and I am not going to pretend that it is. Two: I have not got a minute, there is no box on anything in this room that says who wrote a thing down, and you have been asking me questions since September and every single one of them has been about a document. Three:**" she put her hand flat on the counter, which she does about four times a year, "**a counter that can be asked what it is for is a counter that has been asked to be something, and the day it has been asked to be something it is a service, and I have told you what a service is and you do not need me to say it again.**"

"That is three reasons and not an answer."

"**That is correct and I would like it noticed that I have never once given you an answer and you have never once needed one, and that is the first nine months of this in a single morning.**"

---

**And at about ten to eleven a man came in, and he was about fifty, and he had a wet coat, and he was not on any list and had not been in the building before and did not know it was a records counter.**

He had been in about two minutes before he said it, and he said it to her and not to the room, and Jonas Mercer was four feet away and heard all of it.

"**Is this where you go if you want to know about a person?**"

"**No,**" said Nina Calder.

"**I have been to the other one, on the Thursday, and the other one is a different borough, and the man there told me to try here, and I have not come for the water. I have come because my sister went out on the ninth of February and she has a bag with her clothes in it and she is not at my mother's and she is not at work and she has a flat she is not at the keys to, and it is the sixth of March and I have been up since half past three.**"

"**I am not going to be able to tell you where she is.**"

"**I did not think you would. I have got a photograph of her and her name and I have been to three places and I have come here because the man in the other borough said you would tell me the date of something and I did not understand a word of it and I am asking you now whether I am in the right room.**"

"**You are in a records office. There is nothing in this borough that can tell you where your sister is, and there is no form on any counter in this city that would take your question, and if there were one, then in about four years there would be a list of sisters, and I would not be able to give it to you and neither would anybody else, and I am sorry and I am not going to improve that.**"

He did not argue. He stood there for about four seconds.

"**Then can I ask you one thing and will you not write it down.**"

"**I do not write anything down.**"

"**Then why do you do this at all.**"

---

**And she answered him in about eleven seconds, and it is the whole of what six weeks and a bit have been for, and nobody asked her to, and it was not a service, and it is the only thing in this account that anybody has ever said that was both free and a use.**

"**Because there are nine pieces of paper in the drawer under this counter and every one of them has a date on it and a hand on it and no name on it, and the reason they are in there is that a person stood here on a day and said what was true on that day, and I wrote it down, and I am not required to and there is nobody who can make me.**"

He looked at the drawer.

"**So the counter works because you are on it.**"

"**The counter works because a person is on it, and I am not findable, and I am not on anything, and I have got a job on the second floor and I come down twice a day because nobody has ever told me to and nobody could, and if I stopped coming down there would be a counter and there would not be a counter.**"

"And who knows that?"

"**You do. You have known it since the twenty-eighth of January and there are about eleven people a day who have not worked that out at all, and one of them has worked out the other half of it, which is that you can do it to anything with a date on it, and he has not worked out that the reason it works is that I am not on it.**""

"**That is the answer to my question and I did not ask it.**"

"**No. You asked me why I do this and I have told you about a drawer.**"

---

**And the asking was recorded by nobody, and the not-answering was correct, and neither of those was a service, and a man of about fifty went out into the rain on the sixth of March with nothing at all, and nobody in this borough can be blamed for that, and nobody was helped, and nothing was rescued, and the whole of what he got was a sentence about a drawer.**

**And a counter that is required to answer and is not required to make one has now answered the only question anybody has ever asked it about itself, and the answer is not a scheme, and it is not an item, and it is not going on a wall, and it is not in a minute, and it is the first thing anybody has added to the five things a man said out loud in a room in daylight on the twenty-eighth of January, and it is one sentence long, and nobody in this borough is going to improve it:**

**The floor can be asked about itself, and the only reason it can be asked about itself is that there is a person on it and the person is not findable.**

---

**And on the Monday afternoon, the ninth of March, at about twenty past three, Jonas Mercer came in and did not ask a question, and the counting has been right in every month of this year.**

He stood in a room eleven feet by nine for about eleven minutes and looked at a board with nineteen entries and a blank line under the nineteenth, and a card in a slot that has been there since the twentieth of February, and two pieces of paper sixteen inches apart on a wall, and a drawer he did not open and did not look into and has never been told what is in and will not ask.

**And a woman who is not on a rota stood at the other end of a counter and did not look up, and he did not ask her anything, and he is not going to ask her anything, and the reason is the reason he has had since the second of February and it is nine words long and it goes in a pad and nowhere else:**

**A number of what a counter does is a level of service, and the day a floor has a level of service somebody can cut it.**

**And on the Monday afternoon he asked her about nothing else, and there was one thing sitting in a tray on the second floor that had been sitting in a tray since Wednesday the eighteenth of February, and he has not been told what is in it and she has not told him, and it is not going to come up in this room.**

**It came into the records office on the second floor at about half past nine on the eighteenth of February in an envelope that was not on borough stock and had a departmental reference on it in the correct typeface, and it was about four lines long, and it was not a reason column, and it is the smallest request anybody in this borough has made in nine months.**

**Nina Calder has not answered it. She has told nobody that it is there. She has not opened it in front of a second person and she is not going to, and she has not put it in the tray on this floor, and the tray on this floor is where post goes and that is the whole of what it is for. A counter is required to answer and this is not a counter, and a records supervisor is not required to answer anything, and there is no form in this borough that says what happens to a letter that nobody has opened for nineteen days.**

**And nobody in this borough is going to open it in a hurry, and Jonas Mercer is not going to ask her to, and there is a version of him that would have asked her on the twenty-third of February and he did not, and the reason is on the back page of a pad and it is nine words long, and it has not changed since the second of February.**

**And the whole of what has happened in this borough since the twenty-sixth of January is that a thing which nobody designed and nobody decided and nobody put in a minute has started working, and it works at about eleven a day, and it is a counter and a person and it is eleven seconds, and there is no form on which anybody could write *this is a service*, and there is no arrangement behind it, and it does not scale, and it cannot be given to anybody, and nobody can be required to keep it going, and it is the only thing anybody in this city has produced this year that nobody can hold over anybody.**

---

**And the last movement in this borough is a Saturday night, and one of them fell on the seventh of March, which was the seventh in six weeks, and there is another one coming on the fourteenth, and the rota has still not been rebuilt and the eleven o'clock to half past one row is still empty and still does not say *vacant*, and Bram Osei's Thursday is still refused in his own handwriting, and Priya Ranganathan's card is still on the Friday and Saturday rows because she put it there.**

**And nobody has written a date on any piece of paper in this city for the twenty-fourth of January or the thirty-first of January or the seventh of February or the fourteenth or the twenty-first or the twenty-eighth or the seventh of March, and there is not going to be one, and the reason is that a person is not required to be told and there is nobody in this borough who is required to write it.**
