# Chapter 0133 — What A Man Of Thirty-Eight Did On A Sunday

Wednesday the twenty-fifth of March 2015, about two in the afternoon, a kitchen on the top floor of a building on a road he has never seen the name of on any paper. And about half past five the same evening, a corridor, ground floor, the Old Ward. And Friday the twenty-seventh, from about ten past ten in the morning, the same corridor.

**There was one question of his in that week and it was six words long and it was asked in a corridor on the Wednesday evening at about half past five, and a corridor is a corridor and he knew that when he asked it, and he has asked nothing else since the twenty-fifth of February, and the counting has been right in every week of this year.**

---

The kitchen had one window and the window was over a yard and the yard had a wall on it with nothing on the wall. There was a table with two chairs and one of the chairs had a coat over the back of it. There was a plate in the sink with the pattern worn through in the middle.

**EMMANUEL OTIENO IS THIRTY-EIGHT.** He has been on the books of four different agencies in nine years and the same two in the last four. He does nights, four to ten, at a depot. He does a second job on Fridays that he does not talk about, and nothing on that page is about the Fridays. He has a daughter of nine who walks to a bus stop at the end of that road on her own at twenty past seven and gets picked up and has never once been late.

She was at school. She gets out at half past three. He had about an hour and he had used about half of it and there was nothing else to do, so he did the thing he had been not doing.

---

The van was not his. It was in a yard two streets away because that is when it is there, and the glovebox was where it had been since the middle of February, and the plastic wallet had been in the glovebox since then, and he had not counted what was in it and he would not have been able to say what was in it if anybody had asked him, and that was not modesty.

**The thing he had worked out had two halves and the first half was the good one.**

The first half: a person can ask a question in this borough and get a date, and a date is a piece of paper that says what was true on a day, and the person who hands it over can be wrong, and if the person who handed it over is wrong then the person holding it can come back and find out, and a man who can be wrong in public is not a man who has to be right in a van.

He had worked the first half out on a Sunday in February at that sink, on his own, with nobody in the room, and nobody in this borough taught him that and nobody in this borough is able to tell him what to do with it.

The second half was the one that had kept him awake, and the second half was this: **he could do it to anything.**

Not to water. Not to a bus stop. To anything with a date on it. A rent book. A school letter. A note from a hospital. A card in a shop window with a date on it. A thing anybody had ever handed him.

He had sat on the edge of the bath at about two in the morning and understood that he had a thing that was not a power, and that it was going to be called a power by somebody else, at some point, by somebody who wanted it to be.

---

He had not shown the wallet to anybody. He had thought about showing it to a man at the depot for about nine days and had not done it, and the reason he had not done it was not modesty either.

**It was that the second half is only frightening if nobody else has it, and the first half is only useful if somebody else has it, and those two facts together mean a man who has worked out something on his own in a kitchen is standing in a room holding the only copy, and the only thing a person can do with the only copy is give it away.**

So he had given it away. He had done it three times and he had not told anybody that he had done it, and he was not going to, and there is no form in this borough on which anybody could record that a man of thirty-eight taught three people a thing he worked out himself.

**One.** A woman of about thirty-one at the depot, four years, who had been up two nights running with a mother in a block of flats and had asked him on a Tuesday in February how he knew a thing was true on a day and he had told her, and she had not written anything down either, and he had watched her not write anything down and had been prouder of that than of anything he has ever done.

**Two.** A man of about fifty-five on the second floor of that building, who does not work shifts and is not on any list anybody has ever seen, who had asked him in that kitchen on a Sunday in the middle of March why he was so calm about a landlord, and he had said it out loud, and the man had asked him to say it again in a different way, and he had, twice.

**Three.** A man about thirty that he had met once, in a queue, in the middle of February, outside a hospital, over a broken coffee machine, and it had turned out they had been at the same school in a different decade, and the man had a daughter too, and the man had written a date on the back of his hand and had asked him whether that counted.

**It counted.** That was the first time anybody had asked him that, and he had said yes, and he had not told him the half about a rent book, and he was not going to.

---

**And here is the thing that had taken him the time between two o'clock and twenty to three on the Wednesday afternoon, and it is not a discovery and it is not a method and nobody in this borough is going to hear it.**

He had done the arithmetic on the far end of it.

In about four years, somebody was going to write a form. Not soon. Formally, not this year, not because anybody in this borough is bad — because forms are how a thing becomes a thing a person can rely on, and there will be more of them through that door in four years than there are now, because it went up in seven weeks and it has not come down.

And the form will be a good form. It will have a box on it and a reference and a date and it will say what that counter does, because that is what a form is for, and the woman who hands it over will not have chosen to be in it and she will not be able to be un-in it, because a form with a name on it is a duty and a duty cannot be refused after it is printed.

**And the wall is not the counter. The wall is the day somebody can say *we can only do eight today* and be entirely correct, and the day that can be said is the day the room is a room with a service in it, and it will not be the man who said it. It will never be the man who said it. It will be the first person who is tired and honest.**

He had worked that out at that sink in February, with the tap running and the window open, and he had told nobody, and there was nobody to tell, and the woman at the counter does not know his name and does not know he comes and has never been told that anything about that room is knowable from outside, and he is one of about eleven people a day and he is not going to be one of the ones who has a slip, because he does not need one, and that is the only reason he has the wallet, and it is a stupid reason and it is his.

---

He put the plate in the sink and washed it and put it on the rack, and he got his daughter's shoes from under the table and put them by the door, because she is nine and she comes in on the left foot first and puts them on wrong and if they are not in the right place she does them on the pavement.

Then he went down and got the van and did not open the glovebox, and it is not a thing he is going to start doing, and the reason is that a man who opens a glovebox on a Wednesday afternoon is a man who is looking at it, and a man who is looking at it will open it again.

---

**And at about half past five on the Wednesday evening, on the ground floor of the Old Ward, in a corridor forty-one feet long, a man of about thirty-four was leaning against the wall by the fire door that is on a hold-open wedge, drinking a tea out of a plastic cup, waiting for somebody to sign a book.**

He works in a canteen. He is not on any list. He had been in that corridor on two previous occasions and had not been in a room and had not asked anybody anything.

Jonas Mercer had nodded at him twice. That was the whole of the acquaintance.

The man had been in the corridor about nine minutes when he said it, and he said it to the wall and then to Jonas Mercer, in the way a man says a thing he has been chewing on in a canteen all afternoon.

"**Is it true that there's a counter down there that gives you a date.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**Right. Well, it's not strange any more, that's all I wanted.**"

And then he said the rest of it, and Jonas Mercer felt the whole nine months of his own work arrive somewhere in the middle of his chest and sit down.

"**A fella asked me about it in January, worked nights, agency, I know the type of him, and he told a woman on our shift and she'd already been told by somebody, and one of the girls works over at the hospital and she's told the whole of that kitchen by now, and they're not making a thing of it, that's what I mean. They're just doing it. There's a girl on our till who's had two of them off a landlord and she's not even surprised, she just said *oh, they all do that now*.**"

---

"**And who else has got it.**"

Six words, in a corridor, on the Wednesday evening, and a corridor is a corridor, and he knew what that was when he asked it, and it is the only question he has asked anybody since the twenty-fifth of February and it is the fourth thing in nine months he has done that he cannot unmake afterwards, and he knew that on the Wednesday evening before he opened his mouth, and he did it anyway, and he has not decided whether that was right.

"**Three that I know of,**" said the man. "**And it'll be more than that by now, mate, because that's the thing about it, isn't it. There's no — **" he waved the cup "**— there's no *form* for it. You can't stop it doing it. You can only stop somebody putting it on paper, and nobody's going to put it on paper, are they.**"

"**No.**"

"**No. And I'm not going to be one of the ones who writes it down either, for what it's worth.**"

"**Nobody is going to be.**"

"**No. And that's the bit that's doing my head in, that. Because it used to be a thing, right? It used to be a thing somebody was doing.**" He put the cup on the windowsill. "**Now it's just what happens.**"

---

**And that is the finding, and Jonas Mercer did not put it in his pad until the Saturday morning, and when he put it in he wrote it twice because it is the whole of what the last four months have been.**

> *Twenty-seventh of March, the corridor, the wall by the fire door.*
> *The thing I was afraid of was a form. I have been afraid of a form since the fourth of September and I have been right to be and it is not going to happen.*
> *What has happened instead is that it stopped being a thing anybody is doing. A man of thirty-four in a canteen stood against a wall and told me that the girls on a till have stopped being surprised, and that is not a discovery any more. It is weather.*
> *I have been trying to protect a counter from being written down for six weeks and the way you protect a thing like that from being written down is to make sure nobody can describe it. And a man of thirty-eight in a kitchen on a Sunday in February made sure it could be described, on purpose, out loud, three times, to three people, and he worked that out on his own, and nobody in this borough taught him that and nobody in this borough can tell him what to do with it.*

He put the pen down, and picked it up again, and wrote the second line.

> *And I am not going to go and ask him. I am not going to go and ask a man of thirty-eight what he has done with a method that came out of a counter, and the reason is eight words and it goes in this pad and nowhere else: **I am the person who made it cheap.** The first time I ask him a question about it, he is a person somebody can ask about, and I have spent nine months refusing to make doors out of people, and I am not going to make one out of the only man in this borough who worked something out on his own and gave it away for nothing.*

---

**And on the Friday morning at about ten past ten he stood in the same corridor and worked out the last piece of it, and the last piece is the part he is not going to say out loud to anybody for about four years.**

**A discovery travels sideways.**

It does not travel upwards. It did not go to a manager, or a committee, or a records office, or a board of nine seats with two chairs empty. It went to a woman on a shift, and a man on a second floor, and a man in a queue, and from a woman on a shift to a girl on a till, and from a girl on a till to a kitchen on the other side of this borough, and every single step of that is a person telling a person at a kettle or in a queue or in a doorway, and not one of the steps is a document, and not one of the steps could be produced by anybody, and there is no instrument in this city that can see any of it.

**And the only instrument this borough has for anything that travels sideways is a form. A form is how a thing between people becomes a thing between organisations. And the moment a form is written about a man of thirty-eight and a woman at a counter and a girl on a till, the thing stops being sideways and becomes a programme, and a programme has an owner, and an owner can be asked questions, and the two people at the bottom of it cannot.**

So he did not write it down. He wrote the finding and the reason and he did not write the three, and he did not write the names, and there are no names anywhere in this, and he has not got a pad page about the woman at the depot or the man on the second floor of that building or the man in the queue outside a hospital, and there is not going to be one, because a pad page is where he keeps the things that are his, and the only thing in this that is his is the arithmetic.

**And one of the three works somewhere that has a name.**

The man in the corridor said a name on the Wednesday evening. It was not said slowly and it was not said twice and Jonas Mercer did not ask him to say it again, and it is a name that is on the drawing layer in this city with a filled square beside it and an expiry and a node number on it, because eight of the nine shapes on that layer have names and a filled square, and the ninth has not got one and never will.

**It is the first time in nine months that anything has come out of that counter and touched a district.**

Nothing has come out of that counter. A tray went out and a hand went out and about eleven people a day came in and went out carrying it in their heads for four months. And on a Wednesday afternoon in February a man in a kitchen worked out that the only way to keep a thing was to give it away, and on a Wednesday evening in March a man in a canteen said the name of a district in a corridor to a stranger without knowing he was doing anything at all.

**And Jonas Mercer does not know which one of the three it is, and he is not going to find out, and there is no form in this borough on which he could ask, and asking is how a discovery becomes a finding, and a finding goes in a report, and a report is a thing a person can be held to.**

And nobody was helped. Nobody was harmed. The register is at nineteen entries and the blank line under the nineteenth is blank and it has been blank for a hundred and twenty-one days, and a card has been in the slot in that counter since the twentieth of February, and about eleven people a day are walking into a vestibule eleven feet by nine and getting a date off a woman who is not on anything, and none of them is writing it down, and the man of thirty-eight went to work at four o'clock and washed about four instruments' worth of hands and went home at ten and did not open the glovebox.
