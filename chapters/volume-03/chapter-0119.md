# Chapter 0119 — A Service Floor

**Monday the twenty-sixth of January 2015, about ten past nine in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward.**

It is eleven feet by nine and there is a smaller room in this city, off a corridor on level two at Saint Orra west, with four chairs in it and no door, where two people have to be at ten o'clock at night and neither of them is on anything. This one is the smallest room in this city in which anybody can be said to work, and the reason it is small rather than large is that a person cannot stand in it comfortably with another person in it and has to turn sideways, the way nine people had to stand sideways in a hall on Carrow Lane earlier in January, and that has turned out in nine months to matter more than anything about the size.

**Here is what is on the floor of it, and it is the whole of the inventory, and the woman who comes down from the second floor every morning can say all of it without looking at anything.**

A counter, along the left, eleven feet of it, with a slot in it for post. **The slot has been empty since the eighteenth of July.** Not full, not emptied — empty: no post has been put in it since the eighteenth of July and nobody has asked why and there is no box on the building that says the slot is a service, and one day somebody will put a post in it and somebody else will call that a change.

A board on the wall at the end of the counter. The register on that board has **nineteen entries** on it, and under the nineteenth there is a **blank line**, and the blank line is the only fixed point in the building and it has been blank since the twenty-sixth of November, when the nineteenth entry went on the board, and it was blank before that, under eighteen entries, and it will be blank on any given morning.

Two conditions, sixteen inches apart on the wall above it, where anybody coming up the ground-floor corridor has to walk underneath. **The city's own paper, printed the fourteenth of August, initialled N.C.** And **an enamel strip with nothing on its face, undated, unattributed, with two initials and one word in pencil on the back of it, and the word is *not mine*.** They are not the same document, they have never been reconciled, and nobody in this borough is going to reconcile them, and there is a person in this borough who has been asked once and did not take it.

And about nine people a day, which is the last item on the list and the only one that is not a thing at all. **He asked about it, and it was the only question he asked in that room, and a vestibule with a public corridor running through it is a public room, and the counting has to be done in the open.**

"**Nine,**" said the clerk from the second floor. "**It is nine on a good day and it is four on a wet one and it has been between four and eleven since the fourth of September and I have been counting since about the middle of October because I did not have anything else to do at that end of the corridor, and that is on the back of my hand and it is in no book, and it is not going in a book, and I am not going to say it twice, and if you write it down I shall deny that I said it.**"

---

**Oren Pell came down at about a quarter to ten, because he does that, and stood in the corridor outside the vestibule with his coat on and did not come in, because a registrar who comes into a room of eleven feet by nine turns it into a room he was in.**

"Mr Mercer. Two things and then I am going."

"Two."

"**One. A thing that was going to happen anyway now has to be argued for, in public, repeatedly, by people who are not paid to argue.** That is Mr Rook's sentence about me. He wrote it in August in a report that cost him four hundred evenings of two people's time and about nine thousand pounds, and he was describing what my office had become, and he was right, and he made me expensive on purpose. **And a clause that cannot be challenged by a person cannot require them to.** That is the same sentence, and it is in my minute of the second of June, and neither of us took it off the other, and it is the only sentence in this borough that anybody can build with."

"And the second."

"**Two. The borough's own instrument for finding out what is needed is a list. And a list of who needs it is a list of who exists.** I have been registrar for nineteen years and I have signed about nine hundred things and I can tell you exactly what happens on the day somebody produces the list, and it is not that anybody is hurt, and it is not that the list is wrong. It is that in four years the list is in a drawer with a date on it, and the date is the day somebody asked, and the asking is the harm." He put his hands in his pockets. "**You have spent eight months arguing with that sentence and you have not refuted it and you are not going to, and I want to be recorded that I said that to you in a corridor in January and not in a minute.**"

"I have not refuted it."

"No. **And that is the correct position and it is the reason I have not gone to bed for two nights.**"

---

**And then a man came in at about ten past ten, and Jonas Mercer had not asked him to and did not know him, and the clerk at the counter did not know him either, and he had come in off the street in the rain.**

He was fifty-eight, and he was the man from the hall on Carrow Lane with the thermos, and he had a bowser on the road outside with a man in it, and he said: "Is this where you do the water."

"This is a records counter."

"Then I am in the wrong place."

"You are in the right place," said Jonas Mercer, from about four feet away, and then said nothing else, because he had used his one question in that room at ten past nine and he was not going to use a second one.

"**I want a state,**" said the man. "I do not want a list. There are six places on the lowland that I have to fill from and I have got a bowser and a man on the bowser and he is paid on Fridays out of my own pocket, and on a Tuesday I have to decide which five of the six I drive to, and on the fifteenth of this month I drove to all six because I did not know, and that is a day of my week I do not get back."

"Six water points," said the clerk from the second floor, who had gone the colour of the wall.

"**Six water points. And I have been to a hall on the thirteenth of January and I have said what I know about five of them in a room and I have not said where the sixth one is, and I am not going to say now either, and I would like it understood that I have not been asked.**"

"**You have not been asked,**" said the clerk from the second floor, "and you will not be, and I am the records office and there is nothing here about water and I am about to tell you that and I am going to be wrong to tell you that, and here is why.**"

---

**And this is the whole of it, and it took about eleven seconds, and she did it standing up, and she did not look anything up.**

"A counter is required to answer and a counter is not required to make one. I cannot give you a list of six, and if I could you would have a piece of paper with six lines on it and you would use it as a list by Friday, and in about four years somebody would hand it back and say *the records office told us there were six*."

"I do not want six."

"**You want a state.** A state is what a thing is doing. Name the two you care about and I shall tell you what I can be held to and what I cannot, and then I shall put a date on it and you shall take it, and that is the whole of the service and it takes eleven seconds and I have never done it before."

"There is only the one of those two I can be held to," he said. "I do not know which one yet."

"Say it then."

"**Two of them are running and three of them are not, and I can be held to all five of them and not to the sixth.** The one at the bus turning head takes money in a slot and a bucket comes off a chain and there is a board with forty slots on it, and you wash it and hang it back, and I have never seen a name anywhere near it, and that is the one I would sign. The school wall one is a timer and a quarter to eight and forty minutes and then it is a pipe, and the children get theirs before most people are up, and that is the other one I would sign. The market one has a tap on a post and it only runs when somebody has paid for the water, and nobody has paid for it since the Friday of the twenty-eighth of November, and the man who carries the water in a bowser is the man you are, and I know that because it is in a minute and the minute is public. The trough by the lock-ups has been frozen since about the twenty-second of December and a trough is not going to be anything else in February. **And the one by the yard has been a dry tap since the summer, and there is a person who is not a party and no answer has been received from anybody, and that is in a file, and the file is the whole of what the records office knows.**"

She wrote a date on the top of a slip and turned it round and put it on the counter.

"**And the sixth.**"

"And the sixth," said the clerk, "**is not in this. And you did not tell me and I did not ask you and there is no box on this for it and I did not write it down, and I would like that minuted if there were anybody here to minute it, and there is not.**"

"I would like that minuted as well," said the man.

"You may want it. It is not going to be minuted by anybody, and that is not because you are not important, and I have known you for ninety seconds."

---

**And Jonas Mercer, four feet away, understood the whole of what had just happened and it took about four seconds, and it is the whole of the answer to the question this volume has been asking since the first of October, and it is one sentence long and it does not scale.**

**A service floor is a counter, a person at it, a state asked for instead of a list, and a date.**

There is no scheme. There is no form and no form number and no box for his name and no box for anything, and the slip has a date on it and a hand on it and no reference, and it cannot be produced again, and in about four years it will be a piece of paper in a drawer in a building that somebody has cleared out, and it will establish nothing.

**And it cannot be argued with, because there is nothing in it to argue with.** That is the point and Calder has said it five times in the same order since April: a document nobody can be held to is not a safer document, it is only a document that has not been argued with yet, and the day it is argued with is the only day that matters. It will be argued with. Any person may come back to that counter on any morning and say *the state of the market standpipe is not that*, and be right, and be argued with, and get a second date.

**And the man does not get his Tuesday back. The water still costs him four pounds a bucket and his man on the bowser is still not on anything and is still paid out of a pocket on a Friday, and the trough is still frozen and the tap by the yard is still dry, and the man who turns the bucket on the board of forty slots is still not findable from any record in this city, and a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped, and nothing in nine months has argued with that sentence, and nothing in a vestibule eleven feet by nine is going to.**

He took the slip and put it in his bag and said, "That is the first time anybody has told me anything in this borough that I could put in a van."

"It is eleven seconds," said the clerk from the second floor. "**It is eleven seconds and a date. It is not a service and it is not going to become one, and if anybody comes back with a form I shall say the same eleven seconds and put another date on it, and that is not a policy, that is a counter.**"

---

**And the man went out to the bowser, and the two conditions on the wall stayed sixteen inches apart, and the blank line at the bottom of the register stayed blank, and about nine people came through that counter that day and four of them on the twenty-seventh, because it was a wet Tuesday.**

**And the volume turns on the floor, and the counter, and the blank line, and the next thing in it is not an event. It is a person being at a counter, and it costs eleven seconds, and it is the cheapest thing anybody in this borough has produced this year, and it is worth what it costs, which is eleven seconds.**
