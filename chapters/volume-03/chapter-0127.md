# Chapter 0127 — The Man Who Could Be Asked, Still

Wednesday the twenty-fifth of February 2015, about half past nine in the morning, the counter, the records office, second floor. And Friday the twenty-seventh of February, about two in the afternoon, the shop.

**There was one question in that counter and it was five words long and it was asked by Jonas Mercer, and the woman who answered it is not on any board and has been at that counter for eleven years, and she is not Nina Calder, and the two of them are the same age and work on the same floor and have never once been confused for each other.**

---

**He went up to the second floor on the Wednesday morning to do arithmetic.**

That is what he has done since the fourth of September: he goes and stands somewhere and works out a number, and the number is nearly always a count of routes, and the counting is what he does instead of doing the thing, and he has never once pretended to anybody, including himself, that it is the same.

The routes to Royden Achebe have not changed. They have not changed since the twenty-second of December and they will not change in February, and there are five of them and every one of them is a document and every one of them is a person who will remember it: a switchboard with a queue and a list in a room; a team in a building with a reception and a book; a rack that post arrives in and somebody signs for; a reporting officer number which is Jonas Mercer's own and is on a piece of paper a man of forty-four signed in about ninety seconds in August without reading it; and a duty at 6-L/16 that is described in two files in two buildings which do not know each other exists.

**And the fifth one, which is not a document and which is the reason he has not gone, which is that turning up at a man's door at half past six in the evening is what he did to a woman of seventy-two in November and it cost her the last thing she had left.**

So there is no way. He established that in December and it is still true.

**What had changed by the twenty-fifth of February is that there is now a way of putting a thing in front of a person that costs eleven seconds and a date.**

---

That is the whole of the change and it took three weeks for him to see what it actually was, and what it was is this:

**A counter in this borough now mints findability, and findability used to cost something.**

For nine months the only thing this borough had to make a person findable was a person going to a place and standing in a corridor, and a corridor costs a day and a raincoat and a decision, and a decision is expensive, and a man who has spent eight months refusing to be a person anybody can ring has understood that expense in his body and has been living on it.

And in the third week of January a woman stood up in a vestibule eleven feet by nine and said that a person could ask a question and get a date, and a date is a piece of paper, and a piece of paper saying what was true on a day is the cheapest handle in this city.

Not cheap to her. Cheap to him. He is the one who would be handing it over.

---

And so he did the arithmetic, and here it is, and it is four lines and it is the reason the second refusal of a man of forty-nine is harder than the first, and nobody in this borough has ever seen it because it is on the back page of a pad in a shop.

> *Twenty-fifth February.*
> *A date is a state with a day on it. That is all it is. A records supervisor can be held to one about a trough and cannot be held to one about a bus stop, and the difference between the two is the whole of what she can be held to.*
> *So: a date about a trough costs eleven seconds and a slip and is worth exactly that.*
> *A date about me saying a man's name in a room on the twenty-first of November would cost me about the same. One slip. One hand. One date. Twenty-first of November. A man said a man's name in a room and the man was not in it and the sentence is in two public minutes and neither minute contains a name.*
> *That is a state. That is true on that day. She could hold me to it. She could hand it to him in a corridor in about eleven seconds and neither of them would be asked to do anything and there is no form and no box and no reference.*
> *And it would be the cheapest thing anybody has offered that man in eight months, and that is the problem, and I have taken four days to be able to write that down.*

---

And the woman at the counter on the second floor answered the five words, and she has never asked him a question in three years and has never once thanked him for coming, and there is no box anywhere in that office on either of those two subjects.

"**Does a correction go anywhere.**"

"No."

"**Say the whole of it, please, because I am going to do something with this and I would like to do it correctly.**"

"**A correction is not a document, it is not a return, it is not a minute and it is not a file, and there is no box on anything in this building that would take it, and if you put it in the post it becomes a letter, and a letter is a thing that exists in a building that keeps post, and in about four years somebody can say that a man with civic standing and no grade wrote to a manager in the borough on a date, and they cannot say what was in it, and that is exactly why they will say it.**"

"That is the opposite of what I want."

"**It is both of the things you want, at once, and you have been in this borough eight months and that should not surprise you.**" She put her pen down. "**If I could make it go nowhere, it would be worthless. If I could make it go somewhere, it would be a record. Those are the same door and it only opens one way, and the way it opens is into a file.**"

---

**And he went down the stairs and out and did not go to Gantry Ward, and he did not telephone, and he did not use the switchboard, and he did not go to the team, and he did not ask anybody whether the man was in, and he did not stand on a landing.**

**Nobody told Royden Achebe anything, on the twenty-fifth of February or on any day in that month, and nobody is going to, and Jonas Mercer is the only person in this borough who could have gone and did not, and that is the second time and it is not going to be a pattern, and a pattern of two is a policy and he has been told that by a better person than him.**

He wrote it down on the Wednesday night. On the Thursday he did not write anything. On the Friday afternoon he went down to the shop at about two o'clock, and Bea Osunde was oiling a bench, and she had a length of ash on her shoulder, and she did not ask him a single question about a document, which is the only reason he can say any of this out loud to another human being.

He told her in about nine minutes, standing up, at the barrier end, with a customer trolley going past at some point that neither of them acknowledged.

And she did not argue with him and she did not improve it and she did not tell him he was brave, which is what she has refused to do since about the fourth of September, and she has been right about that refusal four times.

"**No,**" she said.

"No."

"**Say why not, and do not stand there being brave at me, and do not improve it. Give me the arithmetic and then I will tell you whether the arithmetic is right and I am not going to tell you whether it is right.**"

He gave her the arithmetic. A date is a state with a day on it. A state about a trough is worth eleven seconds. **A state about him saying a man's name in a room is worth the same eleven seconds, and that is the entire difficulty and there has never been a difficulty about it before the twenty-sixth of January, and the difficulty is that it has become cheap.**

Beatrice Osunde put the ash down, which is the thing she does when she is about to say something she has said before, and she said it once.

"**A man who can be asked one question about anything, for ever, by anybody, is not findable. He is available. And a handle is cheap, Mr Mercer, that is what a handle is, that is the whole of what a handle is and I have been in this street thirty-one years and I can tell you that every single handle anybody has ever put on anybody in this borough cost somebody about eleven seconds and a slip of paper, and the reason we do not talk about that is that it does not look like anything when it is done.**"

"Yes."

"**And you have worked out that if you gave him a date he would be able to say that a man came and said it out loud, and that is not a service to him. That is a receipt.**" She picked the ash up and put it down again. "**A receipt is not an apology. A receipt is a thing you hand to a person so that from that day they cannot say the bill was never presented. And the whole of what you have been protecting that man from for eight months is a bill.**"

"Say the rest of it."

"**There is no rest of it, and I am not going to supply one, and I have watched you do this twice now and both times you have gone away and been right.**"

---

And then she said the sentence, and she said it to the bench oiling, and she said it once and she did not say it twice, and she has never said anything twice in thirty-one years at the end of that street, and Jonas Mercer wrote it down on the Friday night in a shop in about four seconds because he knew he was not going to be able to hold it otherwise.

"**You have been finding people for eight months and you have not yet found out that the expensive half is not being found. It is being asked.**"

Nobody answered it. A trolley came past. She went back to the bench.

---

And here is what February established, and it is a finding and not a scheme, and it is the last thing anybody in this borough has to say about a man of forty-nine.

**Findability and askability are two different currencies, and this borough has been minting the first one very successfully since the third of April, and it has not spent one unit of the second one at all.**

He made a man of forty-four a wall in August and a man of forty-nine a door in November, and neither of them was in a room. **A wall is a person who cannot be asked. A door is a person who can be asked. Both of them are findable. Findability is what he has been producing, deliberately, in rooms, out loud, with his own mouth, and it is the only thing he has actually built this year, and it is in two public minutes and one of them contains no names at all.**

And the price of a door is that it opens. The price of being found is nothing. The argument of the last three months is that being findable is not the same as being useful, and a woman of about sixty said that to him in a shop in November and told him she was only going to say it once, and he wrote it down because he writes everything down, and she was right. And a woman of fifty-four said something else to him in a kitchen in December and told him not to write it down, and he did not, and she was also right. Put the two of them together and the second half is worse: being findable is not the same as being askable**, and a borough that has learned how to make people findable has not thereby learned anything at all about whether anybody can be asked anything.**

**And a man of forty-nine can be asked one question tomorrow morning, about anything, for ever, by anybody, and he is not untouchable, and he is not to be protected, and there is no argument in this borough in which protecting him is the right thing, and he is not going to be asked, and Jonas Mercer is not going to go, and the reason is eleven seconds long and it is on the back page of a pad and it is the only reason he has got.**

---

**And on the Friday afternoon, at about ten past two, on his way back from the shop, he thought about the other one, which he has not thought about in three weeks, and which is a thing he put a person into on purpose in August and cannot unmake.**

**PETER NAYLOR IS FORTY-FOUR. He is a level-three acceptance officer, field services, Gantry Ward, and he has been out of estates since 2011, and he signed an acceptance in about ninety seconds in August without knowing what any of the words mean, and in January a rule was read to him in a corridor in about eleven seconds and he was able to say no and did not and does not know he was in it. Nobody in this borough is able to ask him one question about any of it, ever. That is what makes him a wall and it is the correct arrangement and it is the only arrangement anybody has.**

And he has a fortnight. He has had one in this city since before Christmas and one in the middle of January and he has one again now, and it ends on the twenty-eighth of February, or the first of March, and he goes somewhere in this city with a bucket and a tap and he does whatever a man does with a fortnight and a bucket, and he is on his own, and he is not on a rota, and there is no instrument in this city that has ever recorded that he goes.

It has been four degrees all week. There is a frost warning out for the twenty-eighth that nobody in this borough is anybody's business to act on. And there is nobody in this borough who is able to ask him whether he has got a coat.
