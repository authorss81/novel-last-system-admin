# Chapter 0134 — A Woman Who Was Asked, Twice, And Did Not Come

Monday the thirtieth of March 2015, from about eleven in the morning, a hall in Tideglass. And Wednesday the first of April, about ten past ten in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward.

**There was no question of his in either room and there was no question of his in the hall and there has not been one since the twenty-fifth of February, and the counting has been right in every week of this year. There is no number in this borough that anybody can telephone, and that is not on a list, and it is not going on one, and the reason is four words long.**

> **NOBODY OWNS THAT NUMBER.**

---

The trestle table is against the wall to the left of the doors and it has been in that hall since before the board was formed, and it is used for folding up and for tea and, since the second of December, for a drawer.

The drawer has no key on it. There is no key in the building. It is a drawer in a trestle table and anybody in that hall could open it and on the afternoon of the thirtieth of March nobody did, and about nine people were in that hall and none of them went near it, and the reason none of them went near it is not a rule and there is no rule and there is nobody who could make one.

**There is a plastic wallet in it. There are two pieces of paper in the plastic wallet and neither of them is a document and neither of them has a crest, a form number, a reference or a box on it, and neither of them can be used to show anybody anything.**

**The four lines at the top of that sheet of A4 are the minute-taker's.** They are in biro. They were written on Wednesday the twenty-first of January, in one go, without stopping, on a second sheet that had nothing at the top of it when she took it.

And underneath them, in a different hand, are four lines that are not hers and are nobody else's, and nobody has said out loud in a room that those four lines came from the woman, and nobody in this borough is going to say it, and it is not said here.

The other piece of paper is a letter. It is in a handbag in a kitchen off a landing, and it is not on this table and it is not in this hall and nobody in this borough has been in that kitchen, and it has a name at the bottom of it in a hand, and it is not a document, and it is not going to be opened.

---

**And neither of those pieces of paper is his, and neither has ever been his, and he has never claimed either, and it is going to be said properly here because the wrong version has been in circulation, including in his own head, for four months.**

He did not write the four lines at the top. He did not write any of them.

**What he did was say a name out loud.**

On Wednesday the third of December, at about twenty to seven in the evening, in a room with about eleven people in it, in about nine seconds, he said a woman's name. Not her surname first. Not a title. A name, as a name, in a room, in daylight-ish, with people in it who had not come from the same street.

The minute of that evening says that a name was stated. It does not contain the name. It cannot be produced, argued with, minuted, published, attached to a claim, or found by anybody who was not one of the about eleven people in that room, and there is no list of who was in that room and there is not going to be one.

**A name said in a room cannot be un-said. That is the only thing about it that anybody in this borough has ever been able to rely on.**

He has not written it down. He has never written it down. In December, on a bench in a shop, a woman who has been in that street thirty-one years told him four things about a pad in one afternoon, and one of them was a rule, and he has kept it every day since.

> *You have been writing things down since April for the people in the room. That last one is yours. Do not lose it and do not show it to anybody, and there is no box on it anywhere and that is also the point.*

There is no box on it anywhere. That is the point.

---

**And she was asked twice, and both times in daylight in a room, and she did not come either time, and the two times are in two papers that say that a person was asked and do not say who.**

The first was in the middle of December and the second was in the third week of January, and the second one was not a repeat of the first one, it was a different asking with a different thing to be asked about, and both of them were reasonable and both of them were made by people who had no idea they were making an instrument.

**And a body of nine seats cannot require a person to do anything. Not a seat, not a form, not a signature, and not a telephone call. A body of nine seats cannot require a person to attend a sitting it is not required to hold, and it is not required to hold one, and nobody has ever made it hold one, and the seven people who are in those seats have not met since the middle of January and are not going to meet in April.**

Nobody can make her come. Nobody can even make anybody try to make her come, because a body cannot require a person to do anything, including the thing that would require the person to be required to do anything first, and that is not a paradox anybody in this borough has solved and it is not going to be solved.

**And there is no number. There is no number in this borough for anybody to telephone, and it is not on a list, and it is not going on one, and the reason is four words long and they are that nobody owns that number.**

A list of telephone numbers is a list of people. A list of people who cannot be reached is a list of people who are not in a room. And a room in this borough cannot require anybody to do anything, and so a room that held a list of the people it could not reach would be a room that had made a list of the people it could not ask, and that is a list, and a list is a thing somebody can hold over you, and the only person in this borough who has said that sentence out loud is a records supervisor of about fifty in a vestibule eleven feet by nine, and she said it about a bulb. **She is the woman who keeps the minutes and she is the woman who stands at that counter at about ten and at about three, and there is one of her and not two, and there is no box anywhere in this borough that could say so.**

---

**And the printed slip came back.**

It came into that hall in the third week of November, and it is the extract of the second public record, and it is unsigned and it has no name box on it, and about four hundred people have added their names to the record itself and about eleven have taken the extract away and two have written back and been wrong and the clerk wrote *enclosure received, no comment*, and that is in the file.

**It went back into a tray in that hall on the thirtieth of March at about eleven o'clock in the morning, and the putting back took a woman about four seconds, and the putting back is the only thing anybody in this borough has done about that person in four months.**

Her name is not on it. There is no name box on it. It has never had her name on it and it is not going to be put on it, and there is no instrument in this city by which anybody could put a name on a piece of paper that has no name box, and a person who was in a room in December is not a person who may be found on a form.

---

**And here is the finding, and it is the whole of it, and it is not a consolation and it is not a defence and it is not going to be improved by anybody.**

**Nothing happened.**

Four months. Two rooms, in daylight, with papers. A printed slip that went out in November and came back in March. A name said once, in nine seconds, to about eleven people, in a room, on the third of December. And the whole of the result, at ten past ten on the first of April, is a hole in the record where a person is, and **nobody in this borough is going to fill that hole, and nobody is going to try, and there is no form in this city that could be used to fill it and no box on any of them that would take what would go in it.**

**A hole in the record is the only honest thing anybody in this borough has produced about a person in nine months.**

Everything else that has happened in this borough in nine months is a sentence, a state, a date, a register with a blank line under it, a form with no name box, a chair that does not exist, a term read aloud in a room with four chairs in it at ten o'clock on a Saturday night. Every one of those is a thing. This is the only one that is an absence, and the absence is a person, and it is the only one that nobody can use.

---

**And the price of the absence is this, and it is exact, and nobody in this borough has worked it out before and he worked it out at about ten past ten on the first of April, standing at a counter he had not gone to.**

**The most expensive thing anybody in this city has produced about a person in nine months is a piece of A4 in a drawer with no key on it, and nobody is going to open it, and the price of that piece of paper is that a woman of thirty-four is a person this borough can say nothing about at all.**

Not nothing nearly. Nothing at all. There is no claim, no reference, no return, no field, no counter, no list, no form, no minute, no slip, no extract, no envelope with a departmental reference in the correct typeface, and no telephone number, and there is no route to her that is not a document, and every one of the five documents that exist is a document that cannot be produced by anybody she has not spoken to.

**And that is the correct outcome, and it is not a consolation, and there is no argument in this borough in which it could be made better by anybody doing more.**

Nine months of this account have been spent refusing to put a person on a thing. He has said a name out loud once and has not written it. Three men who said things in rooms are findable. A counter answers in eleven seconds and gives a date and no name. A board of nine seats has two chairs empty and no chair at all. **And the whole of what all of it has produced about one person of thirty-four at the bottom of a street nobody has been down since the nineteenth of January is that this city is unable to say anything whatever about her, and that is the only protection she has ever had, and it was not given to her, and she does not know she has it, and nobody is going to tell her.**

---

**And on the Monday morning, in that hall, Jonas Mercer stood about four feet from that trestle table a while and did not open the drawer and did not look at the wallet and has never looked at the wallet and is not going to, and the reason is not a promise and is not on a pad and is twelve words long:**

**A drawer with no key is the only honest thing about her.**

And about nine people were in that hall that Monday and the board was at seven of nine and two chairs had been empty since the eighth of October and the twenty-sixth of November, and the ninth seat is the only door in a building of thirty-one rented rooms and has been findable at a shopfront since the fourth of July and is unpaid and has not been told anything and is not going to be.

**And nobody has asked her whether she can afford that.**

Not the woman of fifty-four. Not the man of forty-nine. Not the man of forty-four. Not the man of forty-eight. Not the man of thirty-eight. Not the woman of thirty-four. Not the woman of seventy-two behind a door who is a great deal more frightened than she was in May. Not the woman of sixty-one with a number of Wednesdays on a card under a rubber band in a biscuit tin. Not the four hundred and twentieth woman behind a paragraph number.

**Nobody has been asked, and this borough has no instrument for asking, and the only instrument it has ever had is a form, and a form has a box on it, and the box is where the answer would go, and a person's answer to *can you afford to be found* does not fit in a box, and if it did it would not be true.**

And the register of narrowings is at nineteen entries and the blank line under the nineteenth is blank, and on Monday the thirtieth of March it was a hundred and twenty-four days, and the card in the slot in that counter had been in it since the twentieth of February and nobody had taken it out and nobody had put it up, and there is a tray on the second floor with post on top of something in it that nobody has dealt with, and about eleven people a day were going in and out of a vestibule eleven feet by nine and getting a date off a woman who is not on anything, and being findable, and not being asked about it.
