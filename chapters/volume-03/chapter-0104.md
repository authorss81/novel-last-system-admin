# Chapter 0104 — The Reading Rule

**Monday the thirty-first of October 2014, twenty past nine in the morning, the counter, the records office, second floor.**

The file with the two entries in it is nine pages long and four of them are the determination and the other five are the correspondence about it, and the two entries are on page one under a heading that says *determinations — second determination, Thursday 21 August 2014* and they have been on page one since the twenty-first of August, unamended, unmergeable, and correct.

He read them on the twenty-first of October and read them together on the thirty-first, and the whole of the difference between the two acts was about ninety seconds.

> **METER ROOM 3, SALTER'S YARD, TIDEGLASS. Party holding continuity: THE ALDBROOK TRUST, Kestrel Hill, as freeholder of the fee payer, one-half. Tier 3. Claim reference T3/11400/0304. Answer received 6 June 2014.**
>
> **METER ROOM 3, SALTER'S YARD, TIDEGLASS. Party holding continuity: THE NAMED STEWARD OF THE BUILDING, named in the form as received. Tier 3. Claim reference T3/11400/0304. Answer received 16 June 2014.**

And beneath them, in the same typing, in the same face, the thing the Committee did with them in the only way it had:

> **READING RULE. Where a party relies on either of the two entries above, the other is to be read with it. This is not an amendment of either entry and cures nothing and changes no word. It is the only procedural act available to this Committee in the present case and it is made under the general power to give directions as to the manner in which returns are to be read.**

Thirty-one days old. Nine words of substance, one page, a transcript, and a press list that was unchecked at the door.

**Read together, on the thirty-first of October, in a records office, by a man who is not a party, they say this: a party that relies on the second entry is also relying on the first, and the first entry names a trust that was served with a ninety-day notice on the third of July and has answered a machine once and a person once, and nobody has been required to tell it anything, and there is no instrument in this city that can require anybody to be aware of having been served.**

Which meant that every week since the third of October there had been a managing agent in Kestrel Hill, or in Tideglass, or four hundred yards from either, relying on an entry that names a description and reading the other one as a formality.

And nobody had read it to her.

**Not because anybody refused. Because the rule is a door and the door is in a room, and the room is committee room two on the second floor, and the Committee has no power to make anybody read anything, and a rule that requires a person to be in a room to work is not a rule; it is a sitting.**

He got his one question out of the counter clerk on the way past, and it was the only question he asked anybody that day.

"Has anybody ever read that rule to anybody."

The counter clerk did not need to look at anything.

"No," she said. "**It's on the page. People take the page.**"

---

**The same afternoon, twenty past two, a corridor, second floor, outside a room that was locked.**

Frances Tolley had a lever-arch file under her arm and about four minutes before something, and he asked her the question in the corridor because a corridor is not a public room and the rule about one question a room has never once been tested on a corridor and he was not going to be the man who tested it.

"Frances. Who can use that rule."

She stopped, and she did not say *what rule*, which she has never once said to anybody in twenty-two years.

"**You have just described the only instrument anybody in this city has produced this year that nobody can refuse,**" she said. "So I am going to answer it properly and it is going to take longer than four minutes, and I am going to be late for the Committee's room, and it is that room's fault."

"Take longer."

"The Committee can use it. The Committee is the only body that can use it, and it used it once, in a room, in front of nineteen people and a transcript, and it was the first thing that Committee has done in four months that is not a form, and I want that said in a corridor before anybody ruins it." She shifted the file. "**A person at a meter cupboard in Salter's Yard on a Saturday morning at ten o'clock cannot use it. A managing agent cannot use it. A woman in an apron cannot use it. A registrar can use it in a determination and nobody will let him.** So the answer to your question is that the rule works, and it works in a room, and the room is eleven feet by nine on the first floor and a very long way from Tideglass, and about nine people in this borough know it exists and all nine of them work here."

"Then it is a door in a wall."

"**It is a door in a wall with a light on above it, and that is the whole of it, and if you write *door* in a report and stop there you have wasted a year.**" She looked at him. "You cannot put it in a report. That is the second half of your question and you did not ask it out loud because you have got nine words a week and you spend them well. **A reading rule is an act. It is a person reading two sentences out loud to another person, in a room, with the second person able to say no. It is not a document.**"

"Then how does anybody do it."

"By somebody doing it." She said it as though it were nothing. "In work time. At a counter. To whoever turns up. For as long as anybody turns up. And I am the only person in this city who can do it, because I am the person who wrote it down, and because I am at a counter, and because I am not doing it for anybody — I am doing it because it is in the minutes of a Committee I clerk and I typed it and I made it up because the chair could not think of a third thing and I did not want the meeting to end."

"You wrote it."

"I typed it. **Which is the same as writing it and worse, because I did it in a font.**"

"What would it take."

She was quiet for about four seconds.

"**Not money.** That is the part nobody is going to like. It would take a person being allowed to do it, in work time, in a way that is not a favour and not a favour from anybody. Because if the managing agent's firm pays for it, it is a service. If a committee funds it, it is a service. **A service is a rota with one name on it and that is the whole reason there is a bank in this city with four tonnes of stone owed to nobody.**"

"So it cannot be written anywhere."

"It cannot be written anywhere, and it cannot be in a job description, and it cannot be in a rota, and it cannot be in an establishment, and it cannot be in a form." She put the file down on the sill. "**It can be a woman doing her job in a corridor because it is in a minute, and it can be nothing more than that, and if she does not do it on a day then nobody is to notice that she did not do it on that day, and there is no way at all to say that, and that is the price.**"

"That is the design."

"That is the design and I have been designing things since 1999." She picked the file up. "Now go away, Mr Mercer, because I have to open a room in four minutes and if I am late twice in one week somebody will write *punctuality* on a card."

---

**Wednesday the fifth of November 2014, twenty past ten in the morning, outside the post office on the corner.**

She gave him the sheet at about a quarter past eleven, on a bench, in a wind, and it was one sheet of A4 and it was in biro and it had no crest on it and no form number and no box of any kind on it.

> **READ TOGETHER, AND NOT AMENDED. Meter Room 3, Salter's Yard, Tideglass. The two entries, as they stand, both, unaltered, from the second determination of Thursday 21 August 2014.**
>
> **1.** *METER ROOM 3, SALTER'S YARD, TIDEGLASS. Party holding continuity: THE ALDBROOK TRUST, Kestrel Hill, as freeholder of the fee payer, one-half. Tier 3. Claim reference T3/11400/0304. Answer received 6 June 2014.*
>
> **2.** *METER ROOM 3, SALTER'S YARD, TIDEGLASS. Party holding continuity: THE NAMED STEWARD OF THE BUILDING, named in the form as received. Tier 3. Claim reference T3/11400/0304. Answer received 16 June 2014.*
>
> **The reading rule, in the words of the Committee: where a party relies on either of the two entries above, the other is to be read with it. This is not an amendment of either entry and cures nothing and changes no word.**
>
> **A person relying on entry 2 is relying on entry 1. A person relying on entry 1 is relying on entry 2. Neither may be read alone. There is no amendment and there is no correction and there is no version of this page in which either entry is wrong.**
>
> **Neither entry contains the name of a person.**

"It is the determination with the determination taken out of it," said Frances Tolley. "**It is the smallest useful thing in this city and it is smaller than that, and it has never left a room, and it is leaving this building now on foot because I am not going to type it, and I have not told the chair, and if she telephones me I shall say that I have misunderstood something.**"

"Who is it for?"

"**Everybody who relies on either of them, and there are two organisations in this borough that do, and one of them is four hundred yards from where this sheet is going and one of them is in Kestrel Hill, and the second one is the reason this sheet exists.**"

"You have made a copy."

"I have made **one**."

"Frances—"

"I have made one, and I am going to walk it there myself, and I am going to walk back, and it is forty-one minutes each way at my age and it is a Tuesday, and you are going to walk with me because I am sixty-one and it is windy and I am not going to argue about that either."

---

**Twenty past twelve, Salter's Yard.**

The shopfront has said SALTER'S on the glass since before anybody living in that building was born, and the glass has been broken since March, and the stairwell on the north side is open to the air because the door has been broken since March and nobody has had it fixed and the landlord is not the landlord.

Beverley Annis came out of the office door in a coat and read the sheet standing on the step, in the wind, with her hand on the rail, and she read all of it, and she read entry 1 again, and then she stood there for about eleven seconds.

"Yes," she said. "**All of that is right and I have known all of that since Monday week and I could not have told you how I knew it, and now I have it on a sheet a clerk typed in a font and then wrote out again in biro.**"

"Then you will want a copy."

"**No.**" She did not raise her voice and she did not go inside. "I want you to understand what I am about to say and I want you to understand that I am not being careful, I am being greedy, and I would like it minuted that I have said that."

"Go on."

"**A copy of that page goes to the Aldbrook Trust. It goes to a clerk of sixty-three with a lever-arch file and a lanyard from 2009 with nothing on it, and the moment she has it, the trust has a defence.**" She folded her arms. "Not a good one. A *defence*. Because the reading rule says the two entries are to be read together, and the two entries are both correct, and if she is holding a piece of paper that says so, then in about four years, or in about four months, there will be a letter in this city that says *the question was put twice and the second answer was read with the first and the party relied on the reading and did not know*, and that letter will be right, and it will be true, and and it will be in a file, and **it will be the reason a woman in this building finds out in 2030 that she was in a claim for sixteen years.**"

Frances Tolley said, "That is the first time anybody has said that to me and I have been thinking about it since Tuesday."

"I have been reading it for four days," said Beverley Annis. "I have read every word of the determination and I read the transcript and I read the two entries and I have not been able to make myself say one word of it to anybody, because **I am a party, and a party that has read the reading rule is a party that has been told what the other entry is, and I do not know which side of the node I am on, and neither does anybody else, and that is not a thing to put in a file.**"

"So what is it for," said Jonas Mercer.

"**It is for her. It is not for me.** It is for whoever has to be in that cupboard next, on a Saturday, in about four years, and it is for the person who has to decide whether to put a name in a box, and **the whole of what it does is make them have to look at the other one as well**, and there is no other mechanism in this city that makes anybody look at anything, and that is why I cannot give a copy of it to a trust and would not if you asked me on my knees."

They stood in the wind on a step outside an office with a broken glass door.

"Frances," said Jonas Mercer. "May I say the thing I have been carrying since Saturday."

"You may say one of them and you may not say both of them and you have known that since March."

"**A document is a thing that can be copied, and the moment it can be copied it is a record, and a record is a lever, and the reason this page is going to be any use at all is that there is exactly one of it and I argued for that in a shop in April.**"

"Yes," said Frances Tolley, and put her hand on the rail. "**And I have made exactly one, and I am going to walk home with it in my inside pocket, and I am going to read it to anybody who asks me at that counter for as long as I have a counter, and nobody is going to know on any given day whether I have done it or not, and neither will I.**"

And Jonas Mercer, who has spent seven months arguing in rooms and counters and car parks that a form should not be able to say who put it in, stood on a step in the wind and found that he had nothing to add to that, and that this was the first time in about four months that he had found that.

---

**The walk back was forty-one minutes and neither of them said much, and about halfway, at the top of the hill where you can see four of the seven river bridges, Frances Tolley said the thing she had been carrying since Tuesday and had not said in a corridor.**

"Mr Mercer. In eleven weeks' time there will be a fourteenth person in this city who knows that page exists, and he will be about forty-four, and he will be in a building in Gantry Ward, and he will have signed a piece of paper in August in about ninety seconds and he does not know what any of the words in it mean."

"I know who that is."

"**Then you know that nobody in this city is able to ask him one question about any of it and that two people have told you not to and that you have not.**" She was not looking at him. "And what I want to say is this, and it is not a request, and you are not to put it in a pad. **On a Tuesday in November, if I am at a counter and a man from field services comes in front of me, I am going to read him those two entries. I am not going to ask him anything. I am not going to know anything about him afterwards. And he is not going to have been told that he was in it, and that is going to be the only way it reaches him, and I am not going to be told that it worked, because there is nothing to work with.**"

"That is not the rule," said Jonas Mercer. "The rule is about a party relying on an entry."

"No," said Frances Tolley. "It isn't. **I am going to misuse a procedural act of a Committee in a corridor, on my own time, for a person nobody may be told about, and I have been a clerk for twenty-two years and I have never done anything like it and I would like somebody to have told me it was a bad idea so that I did it having been told.**"

"You have been told," he said.

"Good," said Frances Tolley. "**Then both of us can carry it, and neither of us will ever be asked about it, and that is a rota and I have just written one and I have written it with my own name on it and I have not asked anybody's permission, and I would like to walk the rest of this hill in silence.**"

They did.
