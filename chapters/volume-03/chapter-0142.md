# Chapter 0142 — Asked On A Landing

Friday the first of May 2015, from about ten past ten in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward. And about a quarter to eleven, a landing, a block of flats. And the Tuesday and the Thursday after that.

**He asked no question of anybody in any of the three places, and he has not asked one since the twenty-fifth of February, and the counting has been right in every week of this year. Nobody asked him anything either. There was a question asked on that landing on Friday morning, by a man of about thirty, to a woman who is not in any list in this borough and never has been, it was about eight words long, it was not about anything, and the person who would have known whether there was anybody in that building to give a card to was not there and is not going to be told.**

---

He was in the vestibule for about a quarter of an hour on the Friday morning because it was a Friday and he was in that room on Fridays, and he stood at the east end of the counter with his back to the wall, and the board at the end of the counter read nineteen entries and a blank line under the nineteenth, and the line had been blank since Wednesday the twenty-sixth of November, which was a hundred and fifty-six days.

Nobody asked him anything. Two people came in and went out and neither of them looked at him, and a woman of about fifty came down at about ten and stood at the far end of the counter with a tray, and he had not asked her anything since the eighth of April and he was not going to ask her anything this morning, and neither of them said one word to the other.

**And he worked out, standing there, the thing that room has never been asked and that he was not going to ask it either, and it is the plainest thing in the borough and it is the whole of what the floor is made of: the entire mechanism runs on people being in the same place at the same hour every morning. That is all. A counter, a board, a slot for post, a tray upstairs, a drawer with no key — none of it works because anybody built it, and all of it works because the same faces come past it in the same order on the same day. He has stood at that wall on Fridays since the twenty-eighth of January. He has never once asked who else comes at ten, and he is not going to now, and if he asked, it would be the first question anybody in nine months had asked in there about a person and it would be about all of them at once, and a question about all of them at once is a register.**

He left at about a quarter past ten and did not go up the stairs and did not go to the trough.

**And the thing that happened on that Friday morning, which is the only thing that happened in this borough that day, happened about half a mile away on a landing, and he was not in it, and he could not have been, and nobody told him about it and nobody is going to.**

---

She is about sixty. Nobody in this account knows her name, and she has been in that corridor every morning since about the beginning of last summer, which is about a year, and she has never once been asked about it.

The reason she goes is the mornings. She is on her own and the flat is warm and there is a bus she can get from the top of that street, and the corridor at Saint Orra west is on the way, and there is a board at the end of it about nine feet long and about four feet off the floor, and everybody who comes up that corridor reads it and everybody who comes up that corridor has read it every morning since the second of April a year ago.

The eleven o'clock to half past one row is empty. It is not shaded. It does not say *vacant*.

Further down there is a Thursday in a porter's own handwriting that says NO, and a second line under it in somebody else's hand that says nothing at all. She has read that every morning since the beginning of last summer. She has not worked out what a Thursday in a hospital is for, and she is not going to, and there is a card in a plastic sleeve on the Friday row and on the Saturday row because somebody put it there herself, which she thinks of as the same sort of thing as putting your own coat on a hook.

**She does not know what any of it means and she has never said a word about it to anybody, and there is nobody in this borough she could say it to who would be allowed to do anything about it afterwards, and that is not a comfort and she has not been treating it as one. It is just a board that has been there longer than she has.**

The woman got back to her building at about a quarter to eleven and there was a young man on the second landing with a plastic folder under his arm and a card in his hand.

---

"**Are you the one who does the board.**"

It was not said unkindly. It was said the way a person says a thing when they have been told something and are checking it, and he had a folder and a card and he was about thirty and he was not impatient with her.

"**No.**"

"**Right.**" He looked at the card and then at her. "**I have been told to leave this somewhere in this building. That's all it is. There's a man coming Monday to take a floor up and I have to be honest about the noise, and my mate said ask at the clinic, because he said there was a woman in that corridor every morning who would know who to give it to. He didn't say what your name was. He just said there was a woman.**"

"**There is a woman. I am not her.**"

"**Right. Sorry.**"

---

**And here is the whole of what happened on that landing, and it took about four seconds, and it is the correct answer, and it is correct because of a rule that was put in front of a room in this city in June and has never been argued out by anybody.**

"**Nobody has asked me whether that board is mine. I go and read it. That is all I do and I have been doing it since about last summer and I could not tell you what is on it and I am not going to start.**"

"**I only wanted somebody to give it to.**"

"**I know. And I cannot give it to anybody, because I am not anything, and there is no list anywhere that says I am anything, and there is nowhere in this borough I could be put on one, and that is not an oversight either. If somebody had asked me in April whether I was the woman who did that board, and had written down that I said no, then I would be a person who had been asked about a job, and a person who has been asked about a job is on something, and you cannot get off it again, and I am not going to be on something because a man on a landing handed me a card.**"

He said, "**Do you know who is on in the mornings?**"

"**No. I don't know that either.**"

"**Right.**"

And then he stood there for a second the way people do when they have realised they have taken the wrong turning, and then he said sorry, and he went down the stairs with the card still in his hand, and he did not leave it on the mat of the flat on the left, and he did not put it under the door of the flat on the right, and nobody in that building was given a card about a floor being taken up on the Monday.

---

**And there was a second person on that landing, and she saw the whole of it, and she is not in any document in this borough and nobody has asked her what she saw.**

She was going down to the shops with a bag over her wrist and she came out of her door at the moment the young man was saying *are you the one who does the board*, and she stopped, and she did not say anything, and she went on down the stairs, and she was not asked and she was not asked to say anything and she has not said anything to anybody.

That is all being seen to be asked is. It is a woman with a bag on a landing at a quarter to eleven on a Friday morning, and it goes nowhere, and it is the only record of it, and there is not going to be another one.

---

**And this is the finding, and it is the price, and Jonas Mercer has been carrying it since the thirteenth of March without being told by anybody, and he was not told it on the first of May by anybody either.**

The whole of what a price does is that somebody else pays it, and it is not the person who asked.

He came to a building on a Friday morning with a card and a folder and he was told the truth in about four seconds by a woman who is nobody, and he was not angry and he did not go away feeling clever, and he carried the card back down the stairs with him, and he has not thought about it since, and he is going to be all right.

**And the person who is left holding it is a woman of about sixty who goes out at ten in the morning because the mornings are the only part of the day she goes out, and who was seen to be asked, and who cannot be released from it, and the reason she cannot be released from it is the reason everything in this borough cannot be released from anything: nobody may be required to be told anything, and a person who is asked is a person who is on something, and there is no form in this city on which a person could be recorded as not being on something, because a form with a name on it is a form that has asked.**

The only way to stop being asked about the door is to stop being at the door, and the door is the only part of her day she has got, and nobody in this borough is entitled to ask her about the mornings, and she is not going to explain them to anybody.

---

**On the Tuesday a man at the bins asked her whether she knew who lived at number four B. On the Thursday a girl on the second landing asked her whether the water was going off at the weekend. Both of them were thirty seconds long. Both of them were ordinary. She said no to both of them and she was not thanked for it and she was not asked anything else and she was not helped with either of them, and afterwards she stood at her own window for a bit.**

The rest of the week was a week. She did the shopping and she did not go to the clinic on the Wednesday because it was raining and there was no need to, and the corridor will be there on Monday and it has been there every morning for about a year, and she is not going to stop going, and the going is not the problem and was never the problem.

**The problem is that a thing happened at a quarter to eleven on Friday in a corridor and there is no part of her, now, that is the same as it was at a quarter to twelve. Nothing was taken from her. Nobody told her anything. A man asked her a question and she gave him the correct answer and he went away with his card. And the whole of what she has instead is that in a building of about thirty flats a person has been established as the one who is asked, and she did not agree to it, and she cannot be released from it, and the only instrument anybody in this borough has for releasing anybody from anything is a piece of paper with a name on it, and the one instrument she is not getting is a piece of paper with a name on it, because the absence of her name is the only thing keeping her out of it.**

That is the price, and it is a very small price, and it is exact, and nobody in this borough can do anything about it, including her.

The sentence that was given out loud in that room in March was about four people. **It was correct. It is still correct. There are more than four, and nobody in this borough has an instrument for saying how many more, and the number is not going to be arrived at by adding them up, because the first of them is not on a list either and neither is the fifth and there is nowhere to put the answer.**

---

Nobody died and nobody was rescued and nothing was lucky and nobody was helped, and the term in force in this borough on that Friday morning is the fourteenth of them and it runs six days and it stops at six o'clock on Sunday morning, and there is an hour between six and seven on that Sunday morning when nothing in this city is in force at all, and there is no date in this city for that hour or for any of the others, and Jonas Mercer is not going to be in it, and he has not said why, and he is not going to.

The register of narrowings is at nineteen entries and the blank line is blank and it will be blank on Saturday. The card in the slot has been in it since the twentieth of February and it is seventy days. The letter in the tray went in on the eighteenth of February and it is seventy-two days and somebody has started to leave post on top of it. The drawer under the counter has no key on it and no list of what is in it and nobody in this borough can say how many are in it, including the woman who keeps them and including the man who was standing at the wall on Friday morning.

**And all eleven of the refusals in this borough still stand, and a request refused twice is one request and not two, and nobody in this borough has been helped by any of them, and nobody in this borough has been hurt by any of them either.**
