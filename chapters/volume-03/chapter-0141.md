# Chapter 0141 — What Becomes Of A Thursday

Thursday the thirtieth of April 2015, from about ten past ten in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward. And about half past eleven, the trough by the lock-ups.

**He asked no question of anybody in either place, and he has not asked one since the twenty-fifth of February, and the counting has been right in every week of this year. Nobody asked him anything either, and the last question anybody has asked him in this borough was put to him in that room on the twenty-seventh of April, and he answered that one, and nobody has put another since. There were four questions of a stranger's at that counter at about a quarter past eleven, the shortest of them two words, not one of them was about anything, and he did not get what he asked for in answer to any of the four, and the counter was right about all four, and nobody has ever been told no there about a piece of paper before and nobody had understood the why at the same time as the no until that morning.**

---

He came in off the street at about ten past ten and stood at the east end of the counter with his back to the wall where he stands, and the board at the end of the counter read nineteen entries and a blank line under the nineteenth, and it had been blank since Wednesday the twenty-sixth of November, which was a hundred and fifty-five days.

He had not come for a state. He had come for an arithmetic, and he had been carrying it since the middle of March, and what it came to was four pieces of paper and a day of the week, and it added up to nothing, and there is no total for it and there is not going to be one.

**Four pieces of paper in this borough mention the trough by the lock-ups.**

The oldest is dated the twenty-sixth of January and mentions it in a line between four others, and it went into the inside pocket of a man with a bowser in the rain, and he has not looked at it once. The second is dated the second of February and mentions nothing else. The third is dated the thirteenth of February and mentions nothing else. **All three of those are in the same inside pocket, in the same coat, and the man has not stood in that room since the middle of February, and there is no reason he would come back, and he is right not to come back.** The fourth is dated the sixteenth of March, is about the second of February, and is in the inside pocket of a man with a galvanised can who has never been in that vestibule in his life and who came once more on the twentieth of April to ask whether a piece of paper was a receipt, and was told no, and put it back in the coat where it had been, and has not been back.

**So: a single trough by a lock-ups, on a single morning in February, is the subject of four pieces of paper in two inside pockets, and every one of them is true, and not one of them can be produced by anybody, and there is no way in this city to ask which of them is the state of the trough, because a state is a person's account of a thing at ten past ten on a day, and there are two men, and they are both right, and neither of them may be required to answer anything, including a correction.**

---

**And here is what is left of the man with the bowser, and it is the only thing about him in this account that is not in a minute.**

He came on the second of February and on the thirteenth of February and he has not come since, and one of those two was a Friday, and Jonas Mercer has been in that room on Fridays for four months, and nobody in that room knows what day anybody comes, and he is not going to tell them, and he is not going to be asked.

**A piece of paper in an inside pocket has become a thing with a routine. A routine with one man in it is a rota. Nobody wrote the rota.**

He worked out in April, in a corridor, with a porter, that he could not stop it, and he went through three ways of stopping it and all three of them were closed, and he has not gone back to it. He cannot ask the man to stop coming, because a man may not be required to answer anything. He cannot put it in writing, because a paragraph in a corridor is a form, and the man who gives it is the man who wrote it. And a man who is regularly four hundred yards away is a man who is regularly four hundred yards away.

**So the man with the bowser will come on a Friday or he will not, and there is no instrument anywhere in this city that can tell anybody the difference between those two things, and Jonas Mercer would rather it stayed a coincidence, and he knows exactly what a coincidence is worth, and he knows exactly what it costs to write one down.**

---

At about a quarter past eleven a man of about forty-five came in off the street and stood in the middle of that room for a moment as though he had been told to wait, and he was not told to wait, and he had never been in it in his life.

He had a driver's coat on and a cardboard wallet in his hand and there was grease on the side of the second finger of his right hand, which is what a steering wheel does to a hand in time, and which no amount of washing at a sink in a back yard ever gets out.

He opened the wallet and there were three pieces of paper in it, and they were about four inches by two, and they had been folded once, and they had a date on each of them and a hand on each of them and nothing else on any of them — no reference, no number, no crest, no form, no box, and no name.

"**Are these yours.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**Are those three pieces of paper yours. That is all I am asking and then I will go.**"

"**I have never seen them before this morning, and I could not tell you whose they are if I had, and there is nothing on them to tell me, and I am not going to guess and I am not going to be asked to.**"

"**That is not an answer.**"

"**It is the whole of the answer I have got, and I am going to give you the rest of it and then you will go, because I have not got time and you have not got a bus to catch that I know of.**"

---

**And what she said then was not quick and it is the correct answer, and it is the only thing she has ever been entitled to do with a piece of paper in that room, and she has been doing this for nineteen years and she has never once been asked whether she had said it right.**

"**I cannot tell you whose they are. I could not tell you if I wanted to, because I have got no way of knowing, and I am not going to work it out, and there is nothing on them to work it out from, because there is no name on them and no number and no box, and no box is going to be on them.**"

"**Right. So there is a list.**"

"**There is no list. There is not a list of them and there is not a list of people, and I am not able to write one, and there is nowhere in this borough to write one, and that is not an oversight, and if there were somewhere to write one then in about four years somebody would be able to look one up, and the first thing anybody would do with a list like that is find out whether a person is on it, and the second thing anybody would do is ask that person how they feel about being on it, and neither of those has anything to do with me or with you or with three pieces of paper in a wallet.**"

"**So I have to keep them.**"

"**Nobody is going to make you keep them. Nobody is going to make you throw them away either, and those are the same sentence, and I have said it out loud and I am not going to say it again today.**"

---

The man of forty-five stood in the middle of that room and understood it, and Jonas Mercer watched him understand it, and it took about four seconds, and it is the only time in nine months that he has watched a person at that counter be told no and be told why at the same time and come out of the room holding a different idea of what is on the other side of the counter.

"**So they are not mine.**"

"**They are not yours. And they are not not-yours, and that is the half I cannot help you with, and I have never been able to help anybody with that half, and it is not going to change this year.**"

"**I have had these since about Christmas.**"

"**Then you have been carrying three pieces of paper since about Christmas, and you will go on carrying them, and they will go on being no use to you, and I would rather that than the other thing.**"

"**What is the other thing.**"

"**That is the question you did not ask, and I am not going to answer it, and you are not going to be able to go up the street and tell anybody that I answered it.**"

He put the three of them back into the cardboard wallet and put the wallet inside his coat, on the inside, in a pocket, and he did it without hurrying, the way a man does a thing he has been doing for years, and then he went out into the street.

**And here is what happened, and it is the finding of this morning and it is not a warning about anything: about eleven people a day walk in and out of that room and a good many of them walk out with a piece of paper they cannot use, and nobody decides to do it, and the only mechanism this borough has for acting on the fact that a floor exists is a man turning up with a can under his arm, and this morning it was a man turning up with a wallet.**

---

**Nobody was harmed. Nobody was helped. The register did not move, and the line under the nineteenth is blank, and it is the only fixed point in the building, and it has been blank a hundred and fifty-five days.**

And Jonas Mercer stood at that wall and did not say one word, and the reason he did not say one word is not that he had nothing to say and it is not that he was being careful.

**The three pieces of paper in that van have no owner and no person attached to them. That is the whole of the difference between them and every other thing in this borough, and it is a difference nobody has made and nobody could. The oldest of them is about three months old. The two people they came from are not going to be asked, and the man who is carrying them is not going to be able to say whose they are, and there is nothing in this city that can find out, and in about four years somebody is going to hand a piece of paper to a frightened person in a kitchen and it is not going to have a bulb on it.**

**And the answer to what that is going to become is nothing, because there is no instrument, and that is correct, and it is the same answer the trough gave him in January, and it is the answer the corridor gave him in February, and he has now been given it four times and he has not improved it once.**

---

At about half past eleven he walked to the trough by the lock-ups and stood over it, and nobody went with him and nobody knew he had gone.

It is a concrete trough about four feet long under a downpipe at the end of a row of lock-ups, and there is a bar across the top of it, and the bar is there so that nobody can put a bucket in it, and it has been there longer than the yard.

It was not frozen. It has not been frozen since the Friday night before the second of February. There is something in the bottom of it, and it is not water, and it is not what was in it in February, and the smell of it in the cold is a sweetish smell that nobody in this borough is going to be asked about.

**A thing can be looked at again.**

That is the whole of it. A thing is out there and anybody may look at it, and that is the closest this borough gets to a correction, and it has no form in it, and it costs nothing, and it binds nobody, and there is no instrument anywhere that could go and record that a man stood over a trough on a Thursday morning and saw a thing that was not there in February.

**And he could have written it down. He had a pad in his coat and a pen, and a state is a date and a person's account of a thing, and he had been in a room on the Friday before the thirteenth of March and been handed one in eleven seconds, and he knew exactly what the paper would have said, and he could have taken it to a counter on a Friday and asked for it and got it.**

And he did not, and the reason is three sentences long and it is the whole of what is wrong with nine months.

**The two men with the two pieces of paper in their coats are not going to be asked. If he wrote a third and the third did not agree with the first two, there would be a contradiction, and a contradiction can only be moved by going back to the men, and the men may not be required to answer anything, including a correction. So the third piece of paper would have been a thing in this borough that could never be moved by anybody, ever, and he would have been the one who put it there. And the only thing he is actually able to do about a wrong statement in this city is look at the thing it is about, and he has done that, and there is no way on earth of writing down that he did it.**

---

He got back to the vestibule at about ten to twelve and she was at the far end of the counter where she had been, with the tray in front of her, and neither of them said one word, and the card was in the slot where it has been since the twentieth of February, which is sixty-nine days, and nobody had taken it out and nobody had put it up.

On the second floor there is a tray with post on top of something in it, and the thing went in on the eighteenth of February at about half past nine, and it has been there seventy-one days, and nobody has opened it in front of a second person and nobody is going to.

**All eleven of the refusals in this borough stand exactly as they stood on the thirteenth of March, and none of them has been argued out, and a request refused twice is one request and not two, and a sentence a woman of about fifty said about herself in that room on the eighth of April is not a refusal and is not a twelfth and is in about eleven people and in no document.**

**And the two conditions on that wall are sixteen inches apart and have never been reconciled, and nobody reconciled them this morning, and a person who walks under two things that disagree goes faster, and there is no way of asking them to go slower.**

Nothing was resolved either, and no piece of paper was written on a Thursday morning by anybody, and about eleven people a day went in and out of a room eleven feet by nine and got a date, and the board at the end of the counter did not move, and the line under the nineteenth is blank, and it will be blank tomorrow.
