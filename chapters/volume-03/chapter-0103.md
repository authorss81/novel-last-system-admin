# Chapter 0103 — Six Water Points And No Seat

**Friday the twenty-fourth of October 2014, eleven minutes past ten in the morning, the counter, the records office, second floor.**

He had one question and he asked it at the counter because a counter is required to answer and a counter is not required to make one, and it was seven words, and then he stopped asking, and the rest of it happened because counters are like that.

"Who is the party for the lowland."

The woman at the counter was about fifty and had been there a long time and did not go and look anything up, and she answered in the flat voice of somebody who has answered a version of it about nine times this year.

"**There isn't one.** There has not been one since the sixth of April. The September record says so in a line anybody can read standing up, and the quarterly says the same thing in a different line, and if you want both of them it is two pieces of paper and about four minutes."

He wrote the date of the meeting on the back of the fourth envelope and slid it under the glass, and he did not ask anything else, and the woman at the counter read it upside down, which is a thing counters are for, and then she said:

"**The nineteenth of May. No, it would not say the same thing, because the nineteenth of May was a determinations meeting on fourteen returns and the lowland was not at it.** And then, without being asked: **the lowland's absence from those fourteen is compliance and not failure, because a node with no party is not a party and was never required to answer.** That is in the minute of the nineteenth of June, word for word, and it was read out and nobody wrote it down, and it is written down anyway."

He asked for the minute of the nineteenth of May. It came out of a box in about nine seconds, because he is a name on four documents in three buildings in two cities of a borough and every one of them is correct.

**The figure is on page eleven, in a numbered paragraph, in a document recorded as *received* and not as *evidence*, read out in full at 11:11 in the morning with no comment.**

> **6.1** *The following is stated for the record and is not a determination: that a schedule of costs was placed before the chair on 28 April 2014 and communicated to her in writing at 07:50 on 19 May 2014, comprising six water points on the lowland at Tier 1 and a chamber, a spur and an outfall; that the sum at Tier 1 is £1,880 per node per year and the sum of six is £11,280 per year; that the source of the figure is the arithmetic of the officer named in the covering note; that no party has been asked for any part of it; and that the Committee does not consider itself to have power to require a person at a water point, which it has twice asked to be minuted.*

And then, in the margin, in the hand of the clerk of twenty-two years:

> *Chair: I will not require a person at six water points. Noted. The reason nobody has asked is the reason this Committee cannot answer.*

---

**The rest of the day was arithmetic and it was his, done on a bench in a shop with a pencil and the backs of four wage envelopes, and Bea Osunde left him alone, and at about half past three he had it and it was this.**

A body of nine seats has one power: that a party holding continuity may be required to answer, the answer may be received, and the body may be asked to consider a matter. It has no money. It has no chair. It has no way to lose itself and no way to lose a member. It is not a committee and not a party, and none of its members may require a person to do anything, and none of them has ever been paid, and none of them can be.

There is no party for the lowland. There has not been one since the sixth of April. So the first half of the one power is available in name only, because there is nobody to serve the paper on, and the second half — *be asked to consider a matter* — is the whole of what a body with no money and no party has.

**And the third thing is the seat, and the third thing is the wall.**

The layer draws nine service districts. Eight are named. The ninth is not drawn. The drawing layer has no delete operation and it has never had an add operation that anybody in this city has been able to find, and the layer is a thing CivicCore keeps for the service floor at Gantry Ward and is not a record of the city in any sense anybody can require it to be.

**The lowland is not drawn. Therefore the lowland is not in the eight districts the nine seats are for. Therefore there is no seat for it. And there is no instrument in this city by which there could be one: a cooperative steward board *may be constituted* for the districts the layer draws, and a permissive power to constitute a body for eight things cannot be stretched in a Methodist hall by anybody in a bad mood to constitute it for a ninth. The registrar has said that in those words and will say them again.**

**And the reason there is no seat is not that the lowland cannot pay. It is that a node with no party is not a party, and a place is not a person, and the difference between a district that cannot pay and a district that is not drawn is a difference nobody in this city has ever been asked to establish.**

He wrote that last sentence on the fourth envelope and read it back and did not cross it out, and then put the four envelopes in the pad and went down to the shop.

At about four o'clock Ibrahim Sesay telephoned the shop, which he had done once in eleven weeks and had never had a reason for before.

"Mr Mercer. I am caretaker of a school in Tarn Street and I am the second seat of a board of nine, and I am not ringing you as a board, because a board cannot require anybody to do anything, and I would like it said that I know that."

"Say the thing you want."

"**We have had a chair empty since the eighth of October and we have spent three weeks trying to fill it, and we have not asked anybody, because we are not allowed to ask anybody, and the three weeks have not gone well.**" He let that sit. "And on Tuesday we are taking the lowland. Not the people on it. The thing. The six water points and the number, off the nineteenth of May, because the number is the only figure in this borough anybody can read and the six are the only figures in it that are not written down anywhere. And I have watched that room get hold of a phrase in about three weeks, and I do not think the phrase is a good phrase, and I would like the man who has read that minute in the room when it is said, so that somebody in the room has read it."

"You want me to bring a number."

"I want you to bring a number **with its source on it**, and I want you to sit against a wall and say nothing. And I am telling you now that I know what the second half of that is, and I cannot promise you nobody in that room will ask you something, and if somebody does then you can say no, and I will not mind, and I will not argue with you afterwards."

"That is the first time anybody has offered me that in seven months."

"Then it is about time, and I am sorry about it."

---

**Tuesday the twenty-eighth of October 2014, twenty-five minutes past five in the evening, the Methodist hall, Carrow Lane, Tideglass.**

About eleven people. Nine chairs in a horseshoe, **and the seventh one had been empty for twenty days**, and two others were on the windowsill because there was nothing else. The hall has a piano in it that has not been tuned since about 2011, and a trestle table at the back with the registrar's one-page statement on it in a plastic wallet, and about nine folding chairs stacked against the wall that nobody brought out.

**Jonas Mercer stood against the left-hand wall with his hands behind his back and nothing in them, and the woman who keeps the minutes said at the top of the evening, unasked, that he was not on the agenda, not a party, not a member and not a departmental officer, and that he was present because seat two had asked him to be in the room, and that no person had been required to do anything by his being there.**

He gave the four envelopes to Ibrahim Sesay at the door and did not say a word to anybody all evening.

**The number went on the table at twenty to six and it took about four minutes.**

Winifred Crabbe, 62, linen supervisor, nineteen years, had the minute extract in both hands and her glasses on, and she read the figure out of it in the voice of a woman who has read a laundry list aloud to a room of about nine people for nineteen years.

"**Six water points at eighteen hundred and eighty pounds. Eleven thousand two hundred and eighty pounds a year.** And a chamber and a spur and an outfall, and that is the whole of the figure, and it has been in a public document since the nineteenth of May, and there are about four people in this borough who know it, and one of them is in this room."

The room did what rooms do with a figure that is nearly twelve thousand pounds.

"Whose money is that."

"That is the wrong question and I know it is the wrong question and I have not got a better one," said Gloria Nye, 44, self-employed heating engineer, twenty-one years, three shifts a week, who had been in the room four minutes. "**It is nobody's money. That is what the minute says. No party has been asked for any part of it, and it is on the paper in those words, and it is the only reason this number has survived in public for five months.**"

"It is going to be somebody's money," said a voice from the windowsill. "It is always somebody's money. Somebody has to put it in a pot and somebody has to be the pot."

"Yes," said Gloria Nye. "And there is no instrument in this city by which the pot can be made, and that is not a sad thing to say at half past five on a Tuesday evening, it is a thing four of us have been reading about since March."

---

**And then somebody said the phrase, and Jonas Mercer had heard it three times in three weeks in two corridors and a car park and had never once been able to do anything about it, and the third time was at twenty-five to seven on a Friday evening in a Methodist hall with about eleven people in it.**

"We need somebody who can say what a thing is."

"Somebody who can say what a thing *is*," said a man of fifty-six who was not a member and had come along. "That is what this board has been stuck on for a month. There is a man in this city who looked at a piece of ground and decided in eleven minutes that it was not civic, and the whole of the last four months has followed off the back of those six words, and we have a chair empty and a figure we cannot do anything with, and those are the same problem."

"You mean *non-civic plant*," said Dermot Fahey, 33, facilities supervisor, Saint Orra west, six years, without looking up. "6-L/14. Written on the fourteenth of March in a records room and accepted on the first of April. It is the reason there is a bank with no stone on it and a stand nobody can find to look at, and it is the only instance in this city of anybody deciding what a thing is."

"Nobody knows who wrote it," said the man from the windowsill.

"**No,**" said Dermot Fahey. "That has been in the papers and on a radio programme and in a report that went to the Assembly. Nobody knows who wrote it. Oyelaran stood up in a room in August and said she has never been able to find out and is not going to."

"**Then find out.**" The man put his hands out flat. "That is the whole ask. We have a chair and a number and no person who can stand in the chair and say whether the sixth of those water points is a service or a piece of ground, and there is a man in this city who did it to a bank, and if he would stand in that chair for four minutes this room could put something in a minute that it is going to have to put in a year anyway."

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. A bus went past outside. The piano lid was shut.

**And Jonas Mercer stood against the left-hand wall of a Methodist hall on Carrow Lane with his hands behind his back, and he had written *non-civic plant* in a records room on the fourteenth of March in eleven minutes, and initialled it, and lodged it on the twenty-second under a reporting officer number that was not his, and it had been eight months, and he had said so out loud to nobody in this city except four people, all four of whom had found out from a document.**

**And he did not open his mouth.**

---

**And the six were named, which is the thing that happened, and it happened in about eleven minutes, in a room in daylight, with about eleven people in it, and no list was made.**

The rule the room worked out by itself, inside a minute, without anybody proposing it: **a seat may name a thing it answers for, and a thing it answers for is a thing a person responsible for it can describe.** Nobody in that room was responsible for the lowland. So nobody in that room could name the lowland. What they could do was name the six, one at a time, out loud, out of their own lives — and the room's minute says *six shapes were identified in discussion* and does not name them, and the naming is not in any document at all.

Winifred Crabbe named two. Dermot Fahey named one. Gloria Nye named two, both out of plant rooms, both with the model number in them, and one of them had a tide in it. Ibrahim Sesay named one, and named it as a gate, and said: *I have never been able to find out what it is for and I have not got the time, and if somebody tells me it is a service I will put a sign up, and if somebody tells me it is a piece of ground I will stop sweeping it, and I have been doing both for thirty-one years.*

**The sixth was named by the ninth seat.**

Yolanda Fofana had been in the room the whole of it and had said almost nothing, and she named the sixth in about four seconds in the flat voice of a woman who has read a chain out of a deed register onto four sheets in her own hand.

"**A green pillar with a bucket on a chain and a forty-slot board on it, at the top of a street three hundred metres long with a canal on one side and boat sheds on the other, whose trap is gone, and whose bank is gone, and which is not on the layer, and which has no number, and which is on nobody's list.**"

"And where is that street," said the man from the windowsill.

"That is the whole of what I am going to say about it and I am not going to say where it is, and neither is the minute going to say where it is, and I have never said where it is to anybody in this city including the man against that wall, and I am not going to start in a hall." She did not raise her voice. "**I am the only one of the nine who can be written to. That is the whole of my price. I have just spent a bit of it, and I would like the minute to say that I spent a bit of it, and that I did not have to, and that nobody asked me to.**"

The woman who keeps the minutes said, "It will say exactly that."

"Thank you."

**And Gloria Nye said the thing the room needed, and said it to the wall rather than to the room.** "Then that is five, and one that is a street nobody can find, and the minute will say six shapes and no names, and in about four years somebody is going to ask this board for the list, and there is not going to be one. And that is not a failure. **That is the only design in this city that has ever worked and it is working tonight.**"

---

**And at seven minutes past seven the woman who keeps the minutes read out what the body had done, which is what it always does, because that is the second half of the one power and it is the whole of the one power.**

> **Item 2. Matter considered: six water points on the lowland, a chamber, a spur and an outfall, being the figures at 6.1 of the minute of 19 May 2014, together with the sum of £11,280 per year at Tier 1. Noted, and recorded that no party answers for any of the six and that none has been asked.
>
> Recorded further: that the layer draws nine service districts and eight are named, and that the ninth is not drawn, and that this board has no seat for it and no power to create one, and that the board has not been asked whether the absence of a seat is a consequence of the drawing or of the cost.
>
> Recorded further: that six shapes were identified in discussion and are not named in this minute, and that the person who identified the sixth did so of her own motion, and was not asked, and that no person in this room asked her anything about it.
>
> Nothing in this item requires any person to do anything.**

"It establishes nothing," said Gloria Nye, standing up. "It is a number on a table. There is no money in this body and there is no party to answer and there is no seat for the thing the number is about. I have read it and it is correct and it is the most useful thing anybody has produced in this room since September, and I would like it noted that those two sentences have both been said and that I have said them both in one breath on purpose."

"Minutes," said the woman at the back.

**And then the man from the windowsill said the sentence that ended the evening, across a Methodist hall, in front of about eleven people, in about eleven words.**

"You wrote the six words. You wrote *non-civic plant*. You have been standing against that wall for ninety minutes and I have watched you not say one word, and I am sixty-one and I do not think there is anybody left in this city who is going to be able to help us, and I would like to say it out loud in a room, once, before I go home."

Nobody corrected him and nobody supported him and nobody asked him to say it again.

Jonas Mercer took his hands from behind his back. He looked at the man for about four seconds and then he said, "Yes," and it came out of him before he had decided whether it was going to, and it was one word, and it was in a room, and it was on the twenty-eighth of October 2014, and about ten people heard it.

He did not say anything else. There was nothing else to say that would not have been worse, and he had spent seven months learning that the second half of any sentence he starts in a room is the half that costs somebody something.

The minute recorded it. That is the part he has not been able to put down since. **The minute of the twenty-eighth of October 2014, item 2, fourth paragraph, in the words of the woman who keeps it, reads: *"A person present, not on the agenda, stated in the room that the classification of 6-L/14 as non-civic plant, made on 14 March 2014, was written by him. No person was asked to make that statement. He was not asked anything else and made no other statement."*** And that minute is a public document, and it went out on Wednesday, and **Jonas Mercer's name is now in the same public record as the six words, and the two of them are eleven lines apart in a file in a building in Gantry Ward, and the file does not know that the man who wrote the classification is the man who has been standing in the room.**

Nobody rang him about it. He had expected three people to ring him and none of them did, and the one who rang on the Monday was not one of those three.

---

**The walk home was fifty minutes and he did it on foot and he did not go anywhere near a road that leads to a lowland.**

At about half past eight he was at the top of a hill where you can see four of the seven river bridges and he stopped, and did the thing he does, which is to take the pad out and put his elbow on somebody's brick wall.

> *Tuesday 28 October, Methodist hall, Carrow Lane. Item 2, fourth paragraph. It is a public document. It went out on Wednesday. That is me and those six words in the same record and I did not get asked and I did not decide, and I have said the thing I have refused to say for eight months in a room with ten people in it, and the reason is that a man of sixty-one who is not a member of anything asked me in front of everybody, and I have spent seven months refusing to be asked, and I have never once been able to name the two halves of what I do to a room.*
> *What it has cost, and I am putting it down because I have to: the bank has four tonnes of stone owed to nobody and a man of forty-four in that building has signed a line about it and cannot be asked one question, and my name is now in the record of the sentence that made him signable, and I have made a man who has never been asked anything into a man somebody can now connect to a classification. That is the first thing I have done in seven months that has made anybody findable, and it was not me doing it, and it is still me.*
> *Four of the nine heard it as a liability, one of them heard it as a correction, and one of them asked me whether I would stand in a chair. I have not answered that and I am not going to on a bench. It goes in the minute on Friday week or it does not go in at all.*
> *The sixth water point is named in a room and is not in a document and I was in the room and I did not say it. That is the second time in a year that the right thing has been done by somebody else while I stood in a corridor, and it is the second time that has cost me.*

He put the pad away and went down the hill.
