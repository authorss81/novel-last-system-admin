# Chapter 0126 — The Two Conditions, Sixteen Inches Apart

Friday the twentieth of February 2015, from about half past nine in the morning, the ground-floor corridor, the Old Ward. And Monday the twenty-third of February, about ten past ten.

**There was one question of his in that vestibule on the Friday morning and it was eight words long. He has not asked one in that corridor at all this year and he is not going to, and the counting has been right in every month of it.**

---

He had started timing them. That is the whole of the method and it took him a fortnight to work out that it was a method and about four minutes to accept that it was not a rescue.

The ground-floor corridor of that building is forty-one feet long and everybody who comes to the counter at the end of it has to walk the whole of it, and on the wall about nine feet in from the counter, above head height, in the strip of wall that nine feet of corridor has to put somewhere, there are **two things, sixteen inches apart, and everybody who comes to that counter has to walk underneath them**, and they do not agree.

**The city's own paper.** Printed, on borough stock, with two letters in the corner of it in a hand that is not a capital hand — the letters of a person, not the letters of an office, which is the first thing anybody ever noticed about it and is still the first thing anybody notices. It is a condition. It has a number of words on it and it is legible and it is the only piece of printed paper in this borough that has ever been initialled by the person who designs the absence of boxes rather than by a manager.

**And, sixteen inches away from it, an enamel strip with nothing on its face.** Undated. Unattributed. Nothing has ever been written on the front of it. It has two letters and one word in pencil on the back of it, and the word is **not mine**, and the two letters are not going to be decided by anybody in this borough, including him, and Jonas Mercer has looked at the back of that strip four times since the fourth of December and has never once written the two letters down and has never once said them out loud.

**They are not the same document. They have never been reconciled. There is no procedure anywhere in this city for reconciling them and there is no person in this borough who is entitled to reconcile them and nobody has tried.**

---

On the Friday morning he stood at the east end of that corridor with a watch in his hand and nothing else, and he did four things in about fifty minutes, and none of them were a question, and he wrote none of it down.

One. A man of about thirty with a folder went the full length at a normal walk and did not look up.

Two. A woman of about sixty went the full length, stopped for about two seconds about four feet short of the two things, looked at the printed one, went on. **Two seconds is not hesitation.** Two seconds is a person recognising a piece of paper that is about a building they live in and deciding there is nothing they can do about it before the next step.

Three. A boy of about fourteen, on his own, going the other way, went the full length at a faster walk and did not look up.

Four. A woman in a blue uniform with a lorry docket went the full length and **put her hand up in front of her face as she passed under them**, which is the thing a person does with a low branch.

---

And the finding is physical and it is not moral, and Jonas Mercer worked it out on the Friday morning and did not say it to anybody until the twenty-third, and it is the whole of that morning.

**A person walking under two conditions that do not agree does not slow down. A person walking under two conditions that do not agree goes faster.**

He did it four more times over two days with a watch, and it held, and the reason is not psychology and it is not fear and it is not anything anybody in this borough would want to write in a minute.

It is this. If there were one condition on that wall, a person would have to do one of three things with it: obey it, or refuse it, or stop. Those are all slow. All three of them take time in a corridor and all three of them are visible to the person behind you. Give that person a second condition that says something else and you have taken all three of those off the table, because whichever one they choose, the other piece of paper is still on the wall contradicting it, and there is no version of stopping that is not being wrong in front of a clerk.

What is left is the fourth thing, and the fourth thing is walking.

**A contradiction is not a pause. A contradiction is a floor that is not level, and everybody crosses a floor that is not level faster than a floor that is.** He timed a normal walk down that corridor at about three miles an hour and the same person going through the sixteen inches at about four, and the difference is about a second and a half, and a second and a half is nothing, and on the two days he watched it it happened about four times a day, and four times a day for two days is not a rate and I know it is not a rate, and it is still the only measurement anybody has ever taken of that wall and nobody will ever take another one.

And the registrar said it in June, in a minute, and it is the only sentence in this borough anybody can build with:

**A clause that cannot be challenged by a person cannot require them to.**

Put that beside a woman with a hand up in front of her face and you get this: **a person who goes faster under a wall of two contradictory conditions has been required to do something by neither of them.** Not obeyed. Not refused. Not stopped. **Walked past at four miles an hour with a hand in front of a face, and there is no instrument in this city that can tell her she was required to do anything, and that is not because she is free. It is because nobody has been able to say what the requirement was, and a requirement nobody can say out loud is not a requirement, it is a floor that is not level.**

---

And on the Friday morning, at about ten past ten, he asked his one question, and it was in a public room, and it was eight words, and she answered it before he had finished it because she had been waiting for it since the twenty-eighth of January.

"**Do either of those apply to this counter.**"

"**No.**"

"**No,**" said Nina Calder. "**Neither of them applies to this counter, and one of them does not say anything about anything, and I am going to give you both halves of that straight because you are the only person in this borough who has asked me that question in nine months and I would rather it were asked than not.**"

"Say both halves."

"**Half one: nothing on that wall governs this counter. Not the condition and not the strip. I am not appointed under either of them, I do not keep a register under either of them, and I do not report to anybody who does. If somebody walks in here and asks me for a date, the fact that there are two pieces of paper sixteen inches apart on the wall behind him has no bearing on it whatever, and if it did have a bearing on it then this counter would be a service, and a service is a rota with one name on it, and I am not on a rota and I cannot be made to be on one.**"

"And half two."

"**Half two: one of them would be right if anybody said what I am about to tell you is the most dangerous sentence anybody in this borough could say this month.**"

He waited.

"**Somebody is going to walk in here in the next six weeks and say *that is the rules*. And they are not going to be lying, and they are going to believe it, and they are going to point up at one of the two, and one of the two will be right, and the other one will still be on the wall.**"

"**And I am not going to tell you which one, and I am not going to be asked, and you have not asked me a second question, and that is not luck, it is the arrangement.**" She squared the tray. "**And I want you to hear the reason, because it is the same reason you gave in a room in January and I was in that room. You did not tell nine people what is on a page of a regulation from 2008 that three people in this borough have read. You said you were not one of the three and you were not going to say who they were and you were not going to say what was on it in a room. I am not going to say which of those two things on that wall is the operative document in a corridor, and it is not a manoeuvre, and if you ask me on the second of June I shall tell you the same thing in the same words.**"

"**I will tell you why it matters, because you were going to ask me and I would rather you did not have to. It is a lever, that is all. A condition that governs this counter is a lever that could be pointed at me in about four years by anybody who has read it, and the only thing that is standing between that and a records supervisor of about fifty is that there are two pieces of paper up there and they contradict each other and no two people in this borough can agree about which one means what.**" She put her pen down. "**I would not give that up. I would rather stand under a contradiction for another four years than be the woman who settled it.**"

---

He did not ask a second question in that room on the Friday, and there was nothing in that corridor on the Friday except the one he had already spent, and on the Monday morning he asked two words and could not have helped it, and there was no more of him in either room after that.

And what he took away from the Friday, and wrote on the back page of a pad in a shop on the Saturday morning, in about four minutes, with Bea Osunde cutting a piece of ash and not reading it, which is the reason he writes there, is this:

> *Twenty February, the corridor, about ten minutes to eleven.*
> *Eight people in two days, four on the Friday and four over the Saturday, and not one of them stopped, and one of them put a hand up in front of her face, and that is a floor that is not level and everybody crosses it faster.*
> *And there is now a counter at the end of that corridor that answers a question in about eleven seconds, and I have worked out this week, and it took me a fortnight, and it is the only thing I have worked out in nine months that I am going to keep: **the floor of a service is a place where a person can stand under a contradiction and be answered.***
> *That is all it is. It is eleven feet by nine. It is not an institution and it is not a scheme and it has no rule on the wall that applies to it and no form and no chair and no money and no person on it that anybody is required to be, and it is the only floor in this city where a person is allowed to be in a hurry and be answered anyway.*
> *I am not going to write that on a wall. If it goes on a wall it is a scheme inside four months, and I said that in a room in January and I was right.*

---

And on Monday the twenty-third of February, at about ten past ten in the morning, he came in off the street and stopped four feet short of the counter, which he has not done in nine months, and Nina Calder, who was at the counter, saw him do it and did not say anything about it.

**There was something in the slot in the counter.**

The slot is in the counter along the left, about four inches by two, set into the wood at about the height of a person's hand, and it has been there longer than any of this. It has been **empty since the eighteenth of July.** Not full, not emptied. Empty.

There was a card in it. It was about four inches by two and it was folded once and it was not a form and there is no crest on it and no reference and no box and nothing on the envelope it came in, because there was no envelope.

Nobody had taken it out. Nobody had put it up. The card was still in the slot and the slot was still a slot.

"**How long,**" said Jonas Mercer.

"**Since Friday. Somebody put it in on Friday afternoon or Saturday, and it is Monday, and it has been there for two nights.**"

"**You are going to ask me whether I have read it, and the answer is no, and I am not going to, and I would like to tell you why before you ask, because it took me the whole weekend and there is no reason you should have to stand here while I find it.**"

"**Because a slot in a counter is not mine. There is no box anywhere in this building that says who empties that slot, and it was last emptied on the eighteenth of July and I do not know who emptied it then and neither does anybody else on either floor.**" She put her hand flat on the counter. "**A slot that gets emptied is a rule. And a rule is a service, and a service is a rota with one name on it, and mine is the name it would have, because I am the person at the counter.**"

"So somebody has put something in a service and you are leaving it in it."

"**I am not leaving it in it. I am not taking it out of it. Those are different, and the difference is the whole of what this room is, and if you can hold it in your head for four years without saying it in a room, then you are the only person in this borough who can.**"

The board at the end of the counter read nineteen entries and a blank line under the nineteenth. The line had now been blank for eighty-nine days, and the longest it had ever been still since the fourth of August, and Nina Calder had been right about it since the first of December and was going to be right about it for a good while yet, and nobody was going to be told.

**Sixteen inches above it, the two conditions went on not agreeing with each other, and one of them would have been right if anybody had said the three words that nobody in this borough said.**
