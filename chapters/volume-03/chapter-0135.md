# Chapter 0135 — The Counter Is Asked About The Gate

Friday the third of April 2015, from about eleven in the morning, a ground-floor corridor, Saint Orra west. And Sunday the fifth, at about twenty past six in the morning, four hundred yards from that building. And Monday the sixth, about ten past ten in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward.

**There was no question of his in the corridor on the Friday morning, and none on the Monday morning, and there has not been one since the twenty-fifth of February, and the counting has been right in every week of this year. There was no question of anybody's in either corridor. Nobody asked the counter anything, and the reason nobody asked it anything is that a counter that answers about a gate is a service, and the sentence that says so was given out loud on the fourth of March, and it is the only rule in this borough that anybody has ever been able to enforce on anybody else, and it works by nobody enforcing it.**

---

He went in off the street at about eleven on the Friday morning, on foot, in daylight, the way anybody walks into a hospital.

He is not on the rota. He is not on a board. He is not a member of anything and had not come as a member of anything. He is a person with civic standing and no grade and no department and no probation, and there is no box anywhere in that building that says he may not stand in a public corridor of it at eleven on a Friday morning.

He wanted to look at one thing, and the thing is nine feet long and is about four feet off the floor, and anybody who comes up that corridor reads it every morning and has read it every morning since the second of April a year ago and has not been asked about it.

**The eleven o'clock to half past one row is empty. It is not shaded. It does not say *vacant*.**

It has been that way since the second of April a year ago. The rota has not been rebuilt and there is no instrument in this city by which it could be, and a person coming up that corridor has been reading an empty row every morning since and nobody has been asked what they make of it and nobody is going to be.

On it, further down, in Bram Osei's own handwriting, with a second line under it in somebody else's:

> **B. OSEI. NO.**
> *no reason I can give*
> *(no reason given)*

He has asked twice that the second line not be looked at. Nobody has looked at it. It was not looked at on the Friday morning and it is not going to be looked at this year.

Priya Ranganathan's card is in a plastic sleeve on the Friday and Saturday rows because she put it there.

---

Bram Osei had a trolley at the end of that corridor and he was putting a stack of linen back the way he does, and he stopped when he saw who it was, and he did not put the last one down.

Neither of them said anything for about four seconds.

"**Mr Osei. I am not going to ask you a question and I have not got one and I did not come to ask you one, and there is no counter in this building that I have asked anything about a gate, and there is not going to be one today.**"

"**I know there has not.**"

"**Then I am going to say one thing and it is going to be the wrong thing to say and I am going to say it anyway, and I would rather you heard it from me on a Friday morning than worked it out.**"

Bram Osei finished putting the trolley down.

"**You have not been down.**"

---

**That is all he said. Twenty-seven days after the seventh of March, and twenty days after the fourteenth, and it is a true sentence, and it was said out loud, in a corridor, in daylight, on purpose, by a man who was not going to be asked a question and had not been asked one.**

Jonas Mercer did not reply for about four seconds and then said the only thing he said in that corridor that day.

"**No. I have not.**"

And then he worked out, standing there, what a porter is.

---

**A porter is a person who can tell the difference between a man who is there and a man who is not.**

Not today. Today it is a thing said to a man with a trolley and it is worth nothing and it is in nobody's ear but Bram Osei's. But it is in Bram Osei's ear, and Bram Osei works every day of that week at the end of that corridor, and on the second of March he was given, on purpose, by a man with no grade, the information that a man of thirty-two was not going to be in a room on a Saturday night, and he said the whole of what he would do with it in one go, and it was: *then I shall not say that you were there, and I shall not say that you were not there, and a thing said by two people in a corridor is a thing that two people knows, and I do not want to be two.*

**And that arrangement had one price and the price was that nobody could ever look.**

And now a porter has looked, on his own, without being given anything, because a man has now missed four and the fourth one was never explained to anybody, and a man who is regularly somewhere and then is regularly not somewhere is a pattern, and a pattern is the only thing a building has ever been able to run on.

**So the thing that was about to happen out loud, in a corridor, in daylight, on purpose, was that a true sentence about a man's absences existed in that building, and it was going to keep existing, and it was going to be free, and nobody was going to be paid to say it, and it was going to be the cheapest accurate thing in the whole of Saint Orra west.**

He had about nine hours to stop it.

---

**And he worked out at that wall, in about nine minutes, that he could not stop it, and he went through the three ways in order, and all three of them were closed, and it is the only arithmetic of its kind anybody in this borough has done.**

**One: he could ask Bram Osei not to say it.** He had told a porter in a corridor on the second of March that he was not going to ask him a question, in a room with no minutes and no second person and no record, and he said it in advance on purpose. **A request is a question with better manners. If he now asked a porter for one thing, then the whole of the two of March was a thing he does when it suits him, and the next thing will be too, and there will be a man in that corridor who weighs what he is asked against what he was told, and there is no room in this borough for a porter who has to weigh anything.**

**Two: he could explain it.** He could stand there and say: the gate opens at seven on Sunday because two people who are not on anything turn up at ten o'clock on a Saturday, and it has opened on every Sunday morning since the twenty-fifth of January, and I have never once opened it, and my being away from it is not a thing and there is nothing to explain. And that is all true, and it is a paragraph, and **a paragraph in a corridor is a form. It is a box. It has a man in it, and it is the first of a series, and if a porter can be given an account of a man's absences then a porter can be asked for them, and a person who can be asked for an account of a Saturday night is a person on a board with one row on it.** And the man giving the account would be the man who wrote it.

**Three, and this is the one he had to sit down for, and he did not sit down:**

**He could not be there.** That was the other half. If he stood in that corridor at ten o'clock on a Saturday night four times running, then the thing that is about to become a rota with one name in it would be a duty with one name in it, and a duty is a rota, and a rota is the exact object this borough has spent nine months refusing to be, and it has already cost four tonnes of stone and about eleven hundred and eighty pounds off somebody's schedule and it has bought nobody anything at all.

---

**And that is the cost, and it is the whole of it, and nobody in that building paid any of it, and it was paid in one place and in one currency.**

**A correct thing was going to happen in a building, and he was the only person he knew of who could see it happening, and he was going to stand in a corridor and watch a porter be right about a man, and there is no box on that, and he was not going to be able to put it in a minute, and nobody was going to be asked about it, and it was going to be correct.**

The gate went up at seven o'clock on Sunday the fifth of April.

It went up because two people who are not on anything were in a room with four chairs at ten o'clock the night before. **That is the same answer it has given every Sunday since the twenty-fifth of January, and it is correct, and there is a version of this week in which a man notices a thing that is correct and says nothing, and this is that week.**

---

At about twenty past six on the Sunday morning he was four hundred yards away.

He has been on that pavement twice, in the rain in January, and once in the dark in December, and he did not go into the building and he did not stand in the corridor and he was four hundred yards off, on purpose, in the dark, with his hands in his pockets, and he had decided that on the Friday afternoon, and the decision took him about as long as the walk from that counter to the door.

**The term that had been in force until twenty to six that morning was the tenth of them. It had begun at seven o'clock on Sunday the twenty-ninth of March and it ran six days and it expired at six o'clock on Sunday the fifth of April, and the eleventh was read at ten o'clock on the Saturday night before, and it began at seven o'clock on the Sunday, and so there was an hour on that Sunday morning between six and seven when no term was in force at all in the whole of this city.**

He stood in the middle of it and looked at nothing, and what he found out was this.

**From four hundred yards you cannot tell whether a door is open or shut.**

Not at night, not in rain, not at half past six in April. There is a light on in that corridor and there is a light on in the car park and there is a shape of a building and there is no way on God's earth to tell a man standing on a piece of wet pavement four hundred yards away whether a door on the far side of it is open, and that is not a failure of eyesight and it is not a tragedy. It is the correct amount of evidence.

That hour is his, once a week, and until this morning he had never once been in it. The only other Sunday morning he has spent in that corridor was half past four on the fourth of January, which is the last hour and a half of a term, and there is no gap in this city in that hour at all, and it was a decision, and he had not planned it for a week. **And the gate has never once been opened by anything he did in it, and the only reason he was ever there is that he was not required to be anywhere.**

**And from four hundred yards he could not be a rota, could not be expected, could not be noticed, could not be seen to be there, could not be asked whether he had been, and could not be a reason that a porter began to look at a corridor.**

That was the whole of what four hundred yards buys, and it is the only thing in this borough that has ever bought it, and it cost a decision every Friday, and the decision is about nothing, and he has been making it since the second of March.

---

**And on the Monday morning, the sixth of April, about ten past ten, the standing of that building was what it has been, and none of it was his doing and none of it was undone.**

**The rota has still not been rebuilt. The eleven o'clock to half past one row is still empty and still does not say *vacant*. Bram Osei's Thursday is still refused in his own handwriting and the second line is still in somebody else's and nobody has looked at it and nobody is going to. Priya Ranganathan's card is still in a plastic sleeve on the Friday and the Saturday rows because she put it there.**

**Eleven of them since the twenty-fourth of January.** Twenty-fourth of January, the thirty-first, the seventh of February, the fourteenth, the twenty-first, the twenty-eighth, the seventh of March, the fourteenth, the twenty-first, the twenty-eighth, the fourth of April. All of them at ten o'clock at night. All of them read by two men who are not on anything, and nothing was written down for any of them, and there is no form in this borough on which anybody could record that a handover happened, and **the next one is Saturday the eleventh of April, and the one after that is the eighteenth, and there is no date on any of the three, and there is not going to be one.**

Nobody was helped and nobody was hurt.

**And the only thing anybody in that borough got out of the whole of it was a sentence about a rota with one name in it, in a man's own handwriting, on a board at the end of a corridor, that nobody has looked at.**

---

**And the machine on level zero is still running.**

It is a bench steriliser, four years old, running on four hundred and ten pounds of service a doctor bought herself after the supplier's contract expired at midnight on the thirtieth of April a year ago. About four instruments an hour come out of it. Four hundred and twenty women in this borough have instruments out of that room every weekday and every single one of them is on the far side of a paragraph number and there is no person on the near side of it, and that is not an oversight.

**The machine keeps its own cycle record on a strip, and the strip goes in a drawer in the room, and a piece of paper does not put itself in a drawer, and about forty times a day somebody puts it there, and that person is not on a form and is not on a rota and has not been asked and has not been found either, and Jonas Mercer has said out loud in a corridor that he is not going to look for him, and he is not going to.**

The frost warning that was out for the twenty-eighth of February came off on the second of April. It is eight degrees in that room.

**And there is nothing at all in this borough that is going to happen in April.**
