# Chapter 0123 — What Comes Back To A Counter

Friday the sixth of February 2015, about ten past ten in the morning and about half past three in the afternoon, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward. And the corridor outside it.

---

**On the Friday morning there was a man in that vestibule who had never been in it before, and he had not been sent, and he had worked out something on his own that Jonas Mercer had been trying to teach a room of nine people since the fourth of April, and he did it in about four minutes with a plastic wallet in a glovebox.**

The thing he had worked out is a method. It is worth setting down how a method travels, because it does not travel through minutes and it does not travel through anybody with civic standing and no grade, and nobody in this borough has an instrument for it.

**It travels out of a room in somebody's shoes.**

---

**And the counting is the reason Jonas Mercer was in the corridor that afternoon and not in the room.**

Nina Calder comes down at about ten and again at about three. She had a number and she would not give it to him and he was not going to ask for it, and the reason she would not give it is the reason she will not write it down, and both of those are the same sentence and he has heard it from her three times since the middle of October. It is on the back of a hand. It goes in no book.

**And a number of nine in a record is a capacity. And a capacity is a promise.**

He worked that out on the Friday morning in about four minutes and it is the sentence everything in that room turns on, and it is his and not hers, and nobody has said it to him.

A counter that answers nine people a day is a counter that has been asked to answer nine people a day. Write the nine down and it is not an observation any more, it is a level of service, and a level of service is a thing a person may be given **more** of, and — this is the half that matters, and it is the half every office in this city has understood since March and none of them has ever had to say out loud — **a level of service is a thing a person may also be given less of.** The day there is a number on that counter there is a morning somebody in this city can point at the number and say *we can only do eight today*, and be entirely correct, and not be lying, and the eight will not be chosen by the man with the trough.

**Nobody has to cut it. The number does the cutting, by existing.** That is the whole of what a number of nine in a record is, and it is why it is going to stay on the back of a hand in a room eleven feet by nine, and it is the reason that a person who is good at this has spent nineteen years designing the absence of boxes instead of the boxes.

**And the nine is people, and the nine is not slips.** That is the thing that has to be understood about every number in that room and nobody has ever said it out loud, because it is not the sort of thing anybody says out loud: nine people a day come through that door and almost none of them ask what was true on a day, and a piece of paper only gets written for the ones who do, and there are three of those and all three have come more than once, and the two numbers have nothing to do with each other and must never be added together. **A counter that writes one slip a week and sees nine people a day is not under-performing and is not over-performing. It is a counter.**

There was no question of his in that vestibule on the Friday morning, and the counting has been right in every month of this year.

---

**At about ten past ten a man of thirty-eight came in and asked for a state, and he was not angry before or after, which Jonas Mercer noted, and he had a rain hood down and a van key in his fist.**

**EMMANUEL OTIENO. Thirty-eight. Agency, ten years, on the books of four different agencies in nine years and the same two in the last four. Nights at a depot, four to ten, and a second job on Fridays that he does not talk about. Rents the top floor of a building on a road he has never seen the name of on any paper, and there is a bus stop at the end of it.** He is not on any list. He is not a member of anything. He has no claim, no reference, no party, and nothing in any file in this city, and he has been to a counter five times in his life, and **four of the five have been in that vestibule in the last week and the fifth was in another town in January, took eleven days, and had a reference on it.**

He wanted two things and he asked for both in one go, and Jonas Mercer was four feet away and heard all of it and did not intervene.

He wanted a state about the water at the tap on the wall of the yard where he parks the van, and he wanted to know whether a bus stop was being moved.

"**The yard is on the council side and the tap is a tap and it runs when somebody has paid for the water, and it has not run since before Christmas, and I have been buying bottled water for a van of ten men and I would like to know what is true about it on a date.**"

Nina Calder gave him a state and a date in about eleven seconds, and it was the seventh time she had written one for anybody since the twenty-sixth of January and the fourth time she had written one for the same man, and she did it standing up and she did not look anything up.

Then the bus stop.

"**And I want to know whether the stop at the bottom of my road is being moved. My daughter is nine and she walks to it and she has been told a new place and I would like to know what is true about it on a date.**"

"**No.**"

He did not argue. He did not say *why not*. That is the part that Jonas Mercer wrote down afterwards, because a man who does not say *why not* at a counter in his thirty-eighth year is a man who has been told no by a lot of counters and has worked out that the ones that explain are the ones that are about to change their minds.

"**I am required to answer and I am not required to make one,**" said Nina Calder, "**and the reason is the same for you as it is for everybody: I can be held to what is true on a date, and that is a thing about a time, and you have asked me for a thing about a place, and a place does not hold still, and I have got nothing in front of me about a bus stop because nobody has ever written down anything about a bus stop, in this borough, by anybody, on any date, and I am not going to go and find out, because the day I go and find out is the day this counter starts having a department.**"

"And you can't just... ask somebody."

"**No, I cannot ask somebody. A counter is required to answer. A counter is not required to go and look. Those are two different jobs and this borough has money for neither of them and a person for one of them.**"

---

**And he said the thing, and it took eleven words, and he said it to the door and not to her, and Jonas Mercer was four feet away and has been asked nine questions since September and has not asked one, and this is what eleven seconds of a records counter sounds like when it is heard by somebody who has never heard one before.**

"**A person can ask a question here and get a date.**"

Then he thanked her, which is a thing people do at a counter and which nobody in this borough is going to write down, and he went out into the rain.

---

**And here is the inside of it, and no minute of it exists.**

**Emmanuel Otieno had a plastic wallet in the glovebox of a van he does not own, with more than one piece of paper in it, and he had had it since about the middle of January, and nobody in this city can say how many, and he had not shown it to anybody.**

The first one was from the middle of January, before this counter was a counter to him, and it had come out of a different office in a different town and it had taken eleven days and it had been a form with a reference on it, and the reference had been on it because the reference was what made it findable, and he had understood nothing that had happened to him in those eleven days and had been given a date at the end of it.

**That is what a date is, and he had had one, and he had not known what it was.**

The second was on the Monday, and the third was on the Wednesday, and the fourth was on the Thursday of this week, and the fifth went into the wallet about ten past ten this morning, and every one of the four had taken eleven seconds and had been given by a person standing up.

He had worked out the difference on the Sunday, in a kitchen, on his own, and he had not told anybody and was not going to.

**The difference is not the answer.** He knew a lot about answers. He had been given answers in four towns and every one of the answers had been correct and none of the answers had ever once been true on the day he needed it to be true. An answer is a thing about a state of the world. A date is a thing about a piece of paper.

**The difference is that a date can be wrong, and if it is wrong you can come back.** That is the whole of it. That is the entire mechanism, and it is eleven words long and nobody in this borough has said it to him and nobody in this borough is going to.

And the second thing he worked out, in the same kitchen, on the same Sunday, and it is the thing that nobody in this borough has thought about, and it is the thing that Jonas Mercer spends the rest of the morning being frightened of:

**He can now do this to anything.**

Not to water. Not to a bus stop. To *anything with a date on it.* His landlord's notice. The agency's text about a shift. The letter about his daughter's school in September. The form he got a copy of in October. Anything. He had worked out, by himself, in a kitchen, on a Sunday, with no help from anybody, that **a person can put a thing in front of a counter and ask what was true about it on a day, and get a piece of paper with a day on it, and carry it, and put it next to another piece of paper.** He had a wallet with five in it and there was no box on the wallet and no reference on the wallet and nothing in the world that could be produced about it.

**And he felt, standing at the sink on the Sunday, something that he did not have a word for, and the word he did not have a word for was *powerful*, and it is worth saying that it was not power, and that he did not know that, and that it is going to be called power later by somebody else.**

---

**At about half past three on the Friday afternoon a letter came into the records office on the second floor and it went into a tray, and Jonas Mercer saw it because the tray is in his eyeline every time he comes up those stairs, and it is the second question anybody in this borough has put to that counter in a month, and neither of them has been asked for a list.**

> **TO THE RECORDS OFFICE.**
>
> *I would like to know whether the counter on the ground floor at the Old Ward can be put on the borough's website.*
>
> *I am asking because I have been there twice and I did not know it existed for eleven months, and I have a friend in the lowland who has been without water for a fortnight, and there is no website, and there is no page anywhere, and if it were on a website with the hours on it then a person who did not know could find it in about four minutes instead of asking.*
>
> *I am not asking for anything else. I am not asking for a list of people who use it and I would like it recorded that I have not asked for that.*

Nina Calder read it at about ten past four and put it down and picked it up again and read the last line a third time, and Jonas Mercer did not ask her a single question about it then or afterwards.

**There is no website. The borough has no website. It has a counter, a board, a set of minutes that go out on a Friday, and about nine other things that are all of them rooms.**

And the second question in that letter is worse than the first, and the letter's writer is entirely right that they have not asked it, and Nina Calder said out loud, to a tray, on the second floor, at about ten past four on a Friday in February, and there was nobody in the corridor and she was not saying it to anybody:

"**In four years somebody will ask for the second one, and it will not be a person who is ashamed of asking.**"

---

**And a service floor has been discovered, from the outside, by an ordinary person, in eleven words, in a week, with a plastic wallet in a glovebox and no form and no name and no reference and nobody in this borough to tell.**

**There is no instrument in this borough for nine people a day doing that. The instrument this borough has is a form, and a form has a box on it, and the box is where a person goes, and the reason nobody in this borough has ever put a name on any of the good things in it is the reason a form cannot be the answer to this, and by the time somebody writes the form, the eleven words will be on it, and a sentence on a form is a decision, and a decision has a date and an author, and the author will be a records supervisor of about fifty who is not on a rota and cannot be made to be on one.**

The board at the end of the counter still reads nineteen entries and a blank line. The two conditions are still sixteen inches apart and nobody has looked at either of them.
