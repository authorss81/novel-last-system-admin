# Chapter 0118 — The Price Of The Rule

**Thursday the twenty-second of January 2015, about four in the afternoon, a corridor, second floor, the civic offices.**

**He has asked Frances Tolley nine questions about documents since March and got the same no every time, and on the twenty-sixth of November she said no before he had said what the question was, and gave the reason, and the reason was a pattern and not a fault, and he has written it down twice.**

She was on her way to open a room and she had a lever-arch file under her arm and she stopped about four feet away when she saw who it was, and she did not put the file down.

"Frances."

"No."

"You have not said what the question is."

"**I have not, and I am not going to, and it is the twenty-second of January and I have had eight weeks to think about the fact that I do not know what your question is and I am still going to say no, and I would like that written down as a thing that happened in January and not in November.**" She shifted the file. "That is five."

"Five."

"**Four about a document and one in a corridor in November before you had said what it was, and I want to correct something you have got wrong in your own pad, which is that you have written down twice that I gave you a reason, and I did not give you a reason the second time. A reason is a thing you give once. The second time it is a policy and I am not a policy.**"

"I wrote that you gave me a reason and that it was a pattern and not a fault. That is what you said."

"Yes. And you wrote it down twice, which means it is in two places, which means in about four years there is a piece of paper about a clerk and a man and a pattern, and I said at the time that in about four years that will be the only thing anybody in this borough knows about you, and it has been eight weeks and it is already the truest thing anybody has said to you this year, and I have not said it to you again because I do not say things twice." She looked at him for about a second. "**What is the question.**"

"Whether the sheet is still in your pocket."

She stopped walking.

"**That is a question about a document.**"

"**It is.**"

"**No.**"

"Then I am not going to ask anything else about it."

"You are not going to ask anything else about it because you have been told not to and not because you have run out, and I know the difference, and it is the whole of what is left of you as a person that I can still work with, Mr Mercer, and it is about four inches. **And before you do anything at all with the next nine seconds: I have said no, and I am also going to tell you the truth, and both of those are allowed, and you are not going to enjoy the second one.**" She went on down the corridor. "**It is in my inside pocket. It has been in my inside pocket since the fifth of November. It goes to Salter's Yard on a Tuesday and it comes back on a Tuesday and I do it on my own time and it is forty-one minutes each way and I am sixty-one and it is January and I have not told my husband what is in my coat and I am not going to start, and nobody knows, and nobody is to know, and that is the price.**"

---

**And she said the price out loud, in a corridor, to a man she has said no to five times, in about nine seconds, and she did not stop, and she did not wait to be asked for any of it, and that is the first time in this account that anybody has given the price of that sheet out loud and it is given to the one person in the city who cannot use it.**

"There is no way at all to say that I have not done it on a given day. That is the price and it is the whole of it. **I could put it in a rota and then there would be a rota. I could put it in a form and then there would be a form. I could put it in an establishment, in a job description, in an edition, in a standing order, in a handbook — and every one of those is the obvious solution and every single one of them makes it a service, and a service is a rota with one name on it, and the whole reason there is a bank in this city with four tonnes of stone owed to nobody is that somebody eventually put a name in a box.**"

"And so nobody can check."

"**Nobody can check and nobody can say I have not, and I would not want them to be able to, and the price of that is that on any given Tuesday in this borough nobody in the world can tell whether a reading rule was read in a corridor at a counter in Kestrel Hill or in Tideglass, including me.**" She stopped at the door. "**That is what a corridor is for, by the way. You have been asking me that for five months. A corridor is the only place in this borough that has no minute in it, no agenda, no box, no name, no gallery and no publication, and a rule is a door, and a door in a corridor is a door that can be opened by a person on her own time for a person who turns up, and there is no other room in this city where that sentence is true.**"

---

**And that is what happened on the Tuesday, or the Thursday, or a Tuesday in January, and nobody knows which, and nobody is going to be told, and there is no date on it anywhere.**

It is in a corridor at the records counter, second floor, at about eleven in the morning on a day in January, and it took about eleven seconds, and it is the only time in this account that a rule of a Committee has been used, and it was used wrongly, deliberately, by a clerk of sixty-one who announced it on a hill in November and told nobody she was going to and told a man in a shop in June that she had never done anything like it.

She does not know whether it worked.

**She is not going to say whether it worked. Nobody is going to ask her. That was the arrangement on the hill and it has not been broken and nobody has come near breaking it, and the man who made it has not asked her once in eight weeks and has said so out loud in two other places.**

**And the man it was read to is not going to be told that it happened, and he has not been told, and Jonas Mercer did not tell him and is not going to, and there is no instrument in this borough by which anybody could tell him, and that is the last of the things in this account that a person has been put into that they cannot get out of, and he has done it twice on purpose, and he is not going to do it a third time, and that is the whole of what he has left to give on that account.**

---

**Jonas Mercer found out on Thursday the twenty-second of January at about eleven in the morning, because a man of forty-four said four words at a counter to somebody else.**

He had been at the counter with nothing in his hands and had not spent a question, because it was a Thursday and the extract had come on Monday and he had come for something else, and Peter Naylor came in at about a quarter past eleven, which is not a time anybody at that counter is expecting a man of forty-four who is not on the rota to be, and he put a hand on the counter to ask a question about a different thing entirely and the woman at the counter answered him, and he said:

"**I was read to.**"

Four words. He said them to the counter, in the way a man says a thing that has come back to him about nine times and has nowhere else to go, and then he said thank you and went, and he was gone in about eleven seconds.

**The woman at the counter looked at the back of a door for about four seconds and then said, to Jonas Mercer, in the flat voice she says everything in:**

"Mr Mercer, I am not going to tell you anything about that and I have not told you anything about that, and if you ask me a question about it I shall answer it, because a counter is required to answer, and I shall answer it in a way that is a counter's answer, and I would like you to go away now, and you have not spent your question and I would rather you spent it on something else this morning."

He did not ask it.

**That was the fifth time in eight months he has had a thing put in front of him about a person and not asked, and it is the first time a person has been read two sentences and been able to say no and had said no, and the only thing anybody in this borough is permitted to say about it afterwards is that, and that is not nothing, and it is not evidence, and it will not be in any file, and being arguable is the point.**

---

**He wrote it down on the Friday, the twenty-third, on the bench, in the shop, in about four minutes, and Bea Osunde was cutting a piece of ash and did not read it over his shoulder, which is the reason he writes there.**

> *Thursday 22 January. A corridor, then a counter, then the shop on Friday.*
> *I did not ask. That is the fifth time and I have now stopped keeping a list of the times, which is the only progress I have made in eight months.*
> *The sheet is in her inside pocket. It has been there since the fifth of November. Forty-one minutes each way on a Tuesday, on her own time, in the wind, at sixty-one, and nobody knows and nobody is to know, and she has now said the price out loud to me in a corridor, which she had not done before, and she did it because I asked her whether it was still there and the answer to that is yes and the answer costs forty-one minutes a week forever.*
> *She did it. Once. In a corridor, at a counter, in about eleven seconds, to a man of forty-four who does not know what any of the words mean, who has never known, and who was not told he was in them, and who said four words this morning at that counter that I am not going to write down in this pad because four words said at a counter by a man who came in for something else is the only thing in this year that has arrived without a document.*
> *He is not going to be told. She is not going to be asked whether it worked. I am not going to be told. Three people, three instruments, and every one of them is a refusal, and this is the first refusal in eight months that I have been able to use, and I have not used it, and I do not know what I have got, and I think what I have got is that a person was read two sentences and was able to say no and did not.*
> *The reading rule has reached the only person it was designed for and it cost a procedure being misused, and it established nothing, and a registrar can use it in a determination and nobody will let him, and a managing agent has refused a copy for an entirely correct reason, and about four hundred people have added their names to a page that says a thing is arguable.*
> *I do not know if that is the answer to Tomas Renn's question. I do not know that it is even the same question. I have written in this pad eleven times since March that I do not know, and eleven is a number of times and it is not a number of people, and it is not a number of anything anybody can be helped by, and it is the only column I have got.*

---

**And on the Friday afternoon he walked down to the bottom of a service road at about half past two where there is no pavement and the road turns, and he did not go up the path, and he stood there for about ten minutes with a pad in his hand and did not write anything, because there is no reason she would recognise, and in November he worked out on a wall of somebody's kitchen that going up there without one is a man who has made a street findable and then turned up at the end of it.**

**There is a sixth water point at the top of a three-hundred-metre street with a canal on one side and boat sheds on the other, and its trap is packed and its bank is gone and it cannot be cleared by anybody, ever, and the three positions on that kitchen wall with a name against each of them are still on that wall where they have been since the spring, and the fourth line has a ring of pencil round a blank space and nothing in the ring, and Margo Doré has not been asked anything since the first of June and is not angry and has not forgiven him and both of those are correct, and the reason nobody has gone up that path since June is that nobody has been asked to, and nobody is able to ask, and that is the only door in this borough that has never once been opened by anybody being helpful.**

**It is not resolved. It is not going to be resolved this month, and the winter is not the season for it, and there are pumps for sale in four shops in this borough at about two hundred and forty pounds, and not one of them has ever been in a van on that street, and not one of them is going to be, and it is not a matter of money and it never was.**
