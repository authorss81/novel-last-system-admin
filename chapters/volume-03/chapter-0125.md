# Chapter 0125 — A Reason Column, Again, And Why There Is No Eighth

Monday the sixteenth of February 2015, about twenty past ten in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward. And Wednesday the eighteenth of February, about a quarter past eleven in the morning, the same room.

**There was one question of Jonas Mercer's in that room on the Monday morning and it was nine words. The other question in that room was asked by a man of sixty-one who is not on any list, and it took him about four minutes to get to, and it is the best argument anybody has made in this borough since the first of March.**

---

**It is not M. Furneaux.**

He wrote once, on the second of January, and was refused at that counter in the right order in about eleven seconds, and the letter went back with nothing in it on the fifth, and a records supervisor said out loud at that counter that he would write again in about four years and she would say no a second time in the same words, and he has not written. He is not going to write before about 2018 and he is sixty-one and he lives at the back of a block of rented rooms on Carrow Lane and nothing has happened to him since the second of January except that a board in a vestibule has not moved.

This is a different man, and he is also sixty-one, and he is not a pattern, and there is a card on a wall in a Methodist hall that says a pattern is a thing two people can see and one of them can write down, and nobody has written this one down and nobody is going to.

**HOWARD BRAMBLE. Sixty-one. Fourth floor of a block of forty flats in Tideglass, on the estate that has a parade of shops on the top of it. Nineteen months. A wife who cannot carry anything heavier than a kettle. He is not on any list and has never been on any list and has asked for nothing from this borough in nineteen months.**

He came in on the Monday morning with a plastic wallet and he had it out before he had got to the counter, which is the second time in a fortnight that a man has done that in this room, and Jonas Mercer understood all of it in about four seconds.

Inside the wallet: a printed extract, three pages, unsigned, no name box, fifty-one nodes, thirty-eight parties, thirteen shapes, as at 18:00 on Monday the sixth of October 2014, checkable at a counter by anybody, for nothing, by post, as of a date. He had gone and asked for that himself, in the third week of January, and a clerk had given it to him, and it has about four hundred other people's names written on the top sheet in about four hundred different hands, and he has read it.

And in the other pocket: a slip of paper with a date on it and a hand on it and no reference and no name, which said what was true about a standpipe on a post at the bottom of his car park at about ten past ten on the morning of Thursday the fifth of February, in eleven seconds, by a person standing up.

A date is a thing you can attach a question to. That is the whole mechanism and it is nine months old and it has been sitting in this borough the entire time waiting for somebody to work out that it works on other things.

---

"**You gave me a date,**" said Howard Bramble.

"You did not get it from me. I was at the counter on the Thursday. There is only one counter."

"**I do not care which counter. I want you to understand what has happened to me and then I am going to ask you for something and I am not going to be angry when you say no, and I want that said first, because I think you will find it unusual, and I found it unusual when I wrote the other letter in January and somebody told me you would.**"

"**Mr Furneaux said almost exactly that, in January, and I refused him, and the two of you are not a pattern, and I am not going to put anybody's next to anybody's.**"

"**Then I will be quick.**" He put the wallet on the counter and squared it. "**You will give me a date about a standpipe and you will not tell me your name and you will not write a reference and you will not put me on anything. That is a state with a date on it and no person in it. It is the most useful piece of paper I have been given in nineteen months and I have carried it in my wallet against a photograph of my wife.**"

"It is a piece of paper."

"**It is a piece of paper that says a thing was true on a day, and my wife and I decided on the sixth of February, off that piece of paper, whether to carry four gallons up four flights, and we decided wrong, because it ran on the Thursday and it did not run on the Saturday, and being wrong cost us nothing, and if I had asked you on the Friday you would have told me so in eleven seconds, and I did not ask you on the Friday because there was no reason in the world for me to think of you on the Friday.**" He stopped. "**And I have thought about that all weekend. That there was no reason in the world for me to think of you. And I have read the other thing — the three pages, the one that is unsigned, the one about four hundred people — and I have read that and I have thought: there is no reason in the world for anybody ever to think of that either, and about four hundred people thought of it anyway, and about eleven of them took the extract away to check it, and two of those wrote back and were wrong, and a clerk had to write *enclosure received, no comment*, and that is in the file.**"

---

"**So here is the question, and I have brought it to the right counter, and it is not the question the other man asked.**"

He put two fingers on the board at the end of the counter without looking away from her.

"**That board has nineteen things on it. I have read every one of them and I have read it twice. It says what was done. I know what was done: a meter room, a riser space, a yard light. The light is a bulb and the bulb is out, which I can see from my car park, so that one is honest, and I want to say that first and I want it noticed.**"

"It is noticed."

"**And there is nowhere on that page, or on any copy of it I have asked for, for anybody to have written down in one line what the reason was. I am not asking you for a name. I have read your page about names. I have been careful about that for two months.**"

"**Mr Furneaux was careful about it for a month.**"

"**I am careful about it for two and I have a better reason for being careful about it than he had, and here it is, and it is the reason I am not going to give you a bad argument.**" He put his hand flat on the counter. "**You will give me a date. You will not give me a reason. And I want to know why the same counter can do one of those and not the other.**"

---

And that is the argument, and it is a good one, and it is the best argument anybody has made in this borough since the first of March, and Jonas Mercer, who was four feet away with nothing in his hands, felt it land.

**Because she can.**

That is the whole of it and it took a man of sixty-one twenty minutes on a Monday morning. The counter gives a state and a date. The counter declines to give a reason. And the two are the same shape — a line of free text, in a hand, on a date — and **the only reason the counter can do the first and not the second is that the first one is about a thing and the second one is about a person, and everybody in this borough has known that since March and nobody had ever put the two next to each other in a room before this morning.**

Howard Bramble did it in a queue with a wallet.

He looked at her and waited, and he was not being clever, and Jonas Mercer had the distinct and unpleasant experience of watching a man who has no standing whatsoever make an argument that four people with standing had not made.

---

And she refused in the right order, at a counter, in a public room, in about eleven seconds, and it is the same refusal, and it is not the eighth of anything, and here is the sentence she said about that, out loud, in daylight, in a room, in February.

"No."

And then, in the order, which is the whole of the argument:

"**Mr Bramble. I have refused this once and I am going to refuse it in the same words, and you are not the second time this has happened, you are the first time it has come back, and I am going to tell you what the difference is and I am going to say it out loud so that it is in the air in a room, and then somebody can do whatever they like with it.**"

Nobody in the vestibule said anything for about four seconds. There were three people in it and the room is eleven feet by nine, and the whole of the rest of the borough was going up and down a corridor outside it, and none of that was in the room.

"**You have not been refused twice. You have been asked once, by two men, and the reason column has been refused twice, and that is not a list. A list is nineteen entries with something against each of them and you can count it. This is a thing somebody has asked for twice and been told no twice by a person who is standing in front of you, and if I write a number against it then I have made a list of people who asked for a reason, and a list of people who asked for a reason is a list of people who think the board is dangerous, and in about four years that list is a drawer.**"

"And if I write a third?"

"**Then there are three, and it is still not a list, and you are still not the eighth of anything, and I shall say the same eleven words, and I have said them twice now and I am not going to improve them for a third.**"

And then Jonas Mercer, four feet away with nothing in his hands, asked his one question in a public room, and it was nine words, and she answered it before he had finished it.

"**Is that the best argument anybody has made here.**"

"**It is the best one anybody has made in this borough since the first of March, and I have been listening to them make arguments for nineteen years and I could not have made it in a year. That is not a compliment, Mr Mercer, it is a measurement, and I told you in December that it is a measurement and not a compliment, and you have written it down twice.**"

"Twice."

"**You have written it down twice, which means it is in two places, and we had that conversation already.**" She turned the tray round. "**Now the answer to your man.**"

---

"**And now the part of it that is actually an answer, and I am giving it to you because you brought me a photograph of your wife and I am not going to pretend that was nothing.**"

She squared the tray.

"**You want to know how the counter can do one and not the other. Here it is. A reason is a sentence. A sentence has a person in it. A state is a thing and a thing has nobody in it, and that is not a moral position, that is the only reason a piece of paper with a date on it has been worth anything to you for two weeks, and you worked it out in a queue and I have had it since the twenty-sixth of January.**"

"**And if the reasons were written down with dates on them and nobody's name?**"

"**Then they would be states about decisions, and there would be nineteen of them, and every one of every one of them would be a fact, and not one of them would help you explain that board to a frightened person in a kitchen, and in about four years somebody would use one of them to say *well, at least somebody wrote down that it was for the maintenance*, and it would be true, and it would be the end of the argument, and nobody would ever know whether it had been for the maintenance or whether somebody had wanted the argument to be over.**"

She put her hand flat on the counter, which she does about four times a year, and it was the second time in two days that anybody in this borough had seen it.

"**So if the reason is going to exist anywhere, it is going to exist on a slip from a counter, with a date on it, and nobody's name on it, in a hand, on a day, and it is going to be about a thing and not about a decision. And that is a very poor document and it will settle nothing. And I would rather it did not exist at all.**"

---

He stood there for about four seconds and then he said the thing that Jonas Mercer wrote down afterwards and did not show to anybody.

"**That is the first time in nineteen months that anybody in this borough has told me the price of a thing instead of telling me I cannot have it, and I am not going to argue with it, and I would like to say one thing back and then I am going to leave.**"

"Say it."

"**If the reasons are never going to be written down, then in about four years somebody is going to come to a frightened person in a kitchen and there is going to be nothing at all to put on the table. Not the meter room. Not the bulb. Nothing. And that person is going to be me, and I am sixty-one, and I will be older, and I will not have a copy of anything with a reason in it because I asked for one in February and a woman at a counter said no in eleven words and she was right.**"

"Yes," said Nina Calder. "**That is the cost of it. I have been designing the absence of that box since March and I have been right about it since March and I have just said the price out loud in a room with three people in it, and that is the only notice anybody in this borough is going to get, and I would like it recorded that I said it in about four minutes and I would have liked to have said it in eleven seconds.**"

Nobody in that room said anything about it, and nobody in that room thanked her, and there is no minute anywhere in this borough that records whether a person has been thanked for anything, and that is not a design and it is not a decision. It never was.

He shook her hand, which is a thing people do, and went out, and he did not write a third time, and Jonas Mercer is not going to know for about three years whether he wrote a third time and is not going to ask, because a man who asks whether a person wrote a third time is a man who has made a third time his business.

**And the finding of the chapter is not the refusal. The refusal is the seventh and it stands and it is not the eighth. The finding is this:**

**A request can be refused twice and the second refusal is cheaper.**

Not easier. Cheaper. **By the second time, both of them know what it is for.** The first letter in January was a man of sixty-one being careful. The second was a man of sixty-one with a wallet and a photograph in it and an argument, and the argument was better than anything she had been asked to refuse, and the reason it could be better is that the first refusal was public enough to be found, in the sense that a person who has been told no at a counter talks about it, and that is the only mechanism this borough has for improving a refusal, and it is not a mechanism anybody designed and it is not a mechanism anybody can close.

That is what the second one cost her: about nine minutes instead of eleven seconds, and she is going to be judged on the seconds and the nine minutes are the price of doing it properly.

---

And in the corridor outside, the man of about fifty-six, who is not a member and did not come as a member and is not on any list, was standing at the wall, and he had a pen, and he wrote five words on the back of his left hand in about four seconds, and nobody saw him do it and nobody has ever asked him what is on his hand and nobody is going to.

**IT IS NOT A LIST.**

That is all it says. It is not a document, it is not a minute, it is not in a file, it cannot be produced, it is in biro on the back of the left hand of a man who has been sitting in a car park since December, and it is the truest sentence anybody has said in this borough in nine months and there is nowhere in this city to put it.

---

And on the Wednesday morning, the eighteenth of February, a letter came into the records office on the second floor at about half past nine and went into a tray, and Jonas Mercer saw the envelope because the tray is in his eyeline every time he comes up those stairs.

The envelope was not on borough stock and it had no crest on it. It had a departmental reference on it, a real one, in the correct typeface, and it had a date on it, and it was about four lines long.

Neither of them wrote a third time. Somebody else did, and he is a records officer, and it is not a reason column, and it is not a list, and it is the only thing in this borough anybody has wanted since the first of March that is smaller than a list and it is one word long, and it is the word the whole of this year has been walking towards without anybody saying it.

Nobody opened it in front of anybody. It went into the tray with the rest of the correspondence, and the tray is on the second floor, and the ground-floor counter has a tray of its own, and the two trays are eleven feet and one floor apart, and in about four years somebody is going to be able to say which one a thing was in.

And the board at the end of the counter has nineteen entries on it and a blank line under the nineteenth, and nobody has written on the line and nobody is going to, and it is the only fixed point in the building, and the two conditions are sixteen inches apart above it and nobody has reconciled them and nobody is going to.
