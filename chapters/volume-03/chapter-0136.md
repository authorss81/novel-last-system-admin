# Chapter 0136 — A Reason, Given To Somebody With A Pen

Wednesday the eighth of April 2015, from about ten past ten in the morning, the vestibule, ground floor, the Old Ward. And Friday the tenth of April, about half past eleven in the morning, the records office, second floor.

**There was no question of his in either room, and there was no question of anybody's in the vestibule on the Wednesday morning, and nobody asked her anything about it afterwards, and he did not ask her whether she was going to put it in the minute, and he has not asked her anything since the twenty-fifth of February. The counting has been right in every week of this year.**

---

He worked out at about a quarter past ten on the Wednesday morning that the woman who keeps the minutes is the woman who stands at that counter, and that he had known it since the twenty-eighth of January, and that it had taken him ten weeks because he had been assuming there was a box somewhere that said so.

There is no box.

There is a sheet that says *minute taken by* and there is a name in it and that is the only name-box on the whole of the ground floor. **The sheet is the minute until about four o'clock and after that it is only a sheet**, and she is the only person in this city who can end a minute by picking one up and putting it down, and on the thirteenth of March she stood at the end of that counter and read back what a man had said in about eleven seconds of answer and it went out on the Friday with no names in it.

And she is at that counter at about ten and at about three, twice a day, because nobody has ever told her to and nobody could.

**If there were a box that said *minute taken by* and a name in it tied to *counter, ground floor*, that would be a rota with one name in it, and the name would be hers, and the rota would be the establishment of a service floor in a building, by a person, in about eleven seconds, and it would be the only thing anybody in this borough has actually wanted since the fourth of September.**

---

There were about eleven people in that room by about half past ten, all of them standing sideways, because it is eleven feet by nine and there is no queue for it because there is nothing to queue for.

He stood at the wall by the end of the counter. She was at the other end of it with a tray in front of her and about four sheets on it and nothing written on any of them.

The board at the end of the counter read nineteen entries and a blank line under the nineteenth. It had been blank since Wednesday the twenty-sixth of November, which was a hundred and thirty-three days, and it is the only fixed point in the building.

Sixteen inches above it, on the wall, two things that do not agree with each other were still not agreeing with each other, and one of them would have been right if anybody had said the three words nobody in this borough said.

---

**And at about ten past eleven, in daylight, in a room with about eleven people in it, she spoke, and nobody had asked her, and she had not been asked for anything all day.**

"**Right. I am going to say one sentence and it is about eleven seconds and it is not about the board and it is not about a slip and it is not going on anything.**"

Somebody at the back said, "**Sorry?**"

"**I said I am not going to say it again.**"

And she said it, to the middle of the room, at a volume that carried to the back wall and no further.

"**I decide it, and there is nobody I am allowed to ask whether I have decided it right, and that is the whole of it, and I have had that for nineteen years.**"

---

Nobody said anything. Eleven feet by nine and about eleven people standing sideways in it, and a sentence that was not a question, and no form anywhere in this city that a sentence like that could be entered on.

He wrote it down. Not in a minute — there was no minute, and it was not a sitting of anything, and a body of nine seats was not sitting and was not required to and has not met since the middle of January. He wrote it on the left-hand page of a pad, at the end of the counter, in about four seconds, because a woman had told him in December that there is no box on the last page of that pad anywhere and that is also the point.

She saw him do it. She put her pen down, which is the thing she does about four times a year.

"**Nobody in this borough has ever written down one of mine,**" she said. "**You are going to. And it is going in a book that goes in a shop at the end of a street and it is not going anywhere, and I know that, and I am letting it, and do not come back and tell me what you did with it.**"

"**I am not going to.**"

"**I did not say you were. I said do not come back and tell me. Those are different and the difference is the whole of what this room is, and I have explained that to you before.**"

---

**And here is what she had done, and he worked it out about nine seconds after she had stopped talking, standing at a wall, and it is the whole of what happened in that room on the eighth of April.**

She did not say it to him.

She said it to the room.

**A sentence said to one person in a corridor is a thing two people know, and it can be used, and it belongs to the two of them.** A porter said that to a man with a trolley on the second of March and he was exactly right, and a man of thirty-four in a canteen in March said the other side of it — a thing that is said in a room is a thing a room knows.

**There is no box on a room.**

There is no form in this borough that has a room on it, no return, no field, no reference, no index and no rota, and eleven people in a vestibule eleven feet by nine on a Wednesday morning in April is not an establishment and not a record and cannot be interrogated, and it is the only container in this city with nothing on it.

**She chose the eleven people because a room cannot be asked what happened in it, and a corridor can, and a corridor is where a man asks a porter a question.**

And the reason she said it in front of eleven people and not to him at that counter is that a person who has been refusing things for nineteen years has had nobody ask her for a reason, and a person who has never been asked for a reason does not know whether the reasons are any good, and the only way to find out whether a sentence is true is to say it out loud where it can be overheard, and there is no such thing in this borough as a thing said in a room.

Then she gave the other half of it, to him, and it took about nine seconds and it is the reason and it is not a rule and it is not going in a minute.

"**And I am saying it to you and not to the wall because you have had a pen since the fourth of September and you have been writing down why other people say no for nine months and nobody in this city has ever once asked you for one of yours, and I have got one, and it took me four minutes to find out that I had got one, and I have never told anybody that I did not have one until you handed it to me this morning by not asking.**"

---

**Nobody may ask her a question about it afterwards and nobody did.** He did not ask her whether it was going in the minute. She did not offer. Four people in that room heard it and two of them looked at her and one of them said "**what was that**" about four minutes later to somebody else and was told it was nothing, and nothing was the correct answer, and it was not a lie, because a sentence in a room is a thing a room knows and that is a different thing from a thing anybody has been told.

**That sentence is now in about eleven people and in no document. And that is precisely the thing she refused on the second of January, and it has happened anyway, and it was not anybody's fault, and nobody in this borough did it to her, and she went into that room on purpose and she had worked out on the way in what she was going to do and she did it because the room has no box on it.**

The minute of the thirteenth of March is a public document and it is in a file and it has no names in it, and there is no item on it about the eighth of April, and there is not going to be one, because there was no sitting, and because a body of nine seats cannot require any person to do anything, including the writing of a record about a sentence that was said in a room by a person who was not asked for it.

**And that is the first time anything she has ever said has gone anywhere at all, and it has gone into one hand in a book that goes nowhere, and she knew that when she let him do it, and she let it, and she has not asked about it since and nobody is going to ask her and nobody is going to open the pad.**

---

Sixteen inches above the board, on the wall of that vestibule, there is a notice. It has been there since the middle of January and it says four lines and it requires nothing of anybody and it does not define anything, and it is the only piece of paper in this borough that says a true thing about a register without mentioning a person.

> **THE REGISTER OF NARROWINGS IS AN ACCOUNT OF NODES THAT ARE NARROWED.**
> **A NODE IS NOT A PERSON.**
> **A PERSON MAY BE AFFECTED BY EVERY ENTRY ON THIS BOARD AND IS NAMED ON NONE OF THEM.**
> **NO PERSON SIGNS THIS SHEET.**

There is nothing on it about who holds anything, and nothing on it in three years is going to define who holds anything, and nobody in this borough is going to define it, and it does not say whose board it is.

---

**And the drawer under that counter is a drawer and it is not locked, and there is no key on it, and there is no list of what is in it, and a tray is sitting on top of it.**

There are more of them in it than there were in February, and there is no way in this borough to tell anybody how many more. They have a date on each one and a hand on each one and no reference, no number, no crest, no form, no box, and no name on any of them, including hers. She keeps a copy of each one and the person who is handed it carries it away in a pocket and that is the end of it.

**And there is no form in this borough, on any counter, on any tray, in any office, on which anybody could write *THESE ARE NOT A SERIES*. A box that says *not a series* is a series. Nobody put them there on an instruction and there is no standing order and no handbook and she is not on one and cannot be made to be on one.**

And about eleven people a day come through that door, up from about nine on the twenty-sixth of January, and it is on the back of a hand and in no book. A number of what a counter does is a level of service, and the day a floor has a level of service somebody can cut it.

**Those are two numbers about two different things and they may never be added together, and the reason is hers, given out loud on the second of February, and nobody has improved on it. A slip is only written when a person stands in front of her and asks what was true on a day. A form that added the two together would be a form that said *this counter sees eleven people and produces one document*, and that is a level of service, and there is no level of service in that room and there is not going to be one.**

---

**And on the Friday morning, the tenth of April, at about half past eleven, Jonas Mercer went up to the second floor to look at a board he had not looked at for a fortnight, and he went past a desk, and there was a tray on the desk.**

There is post in the tray. There are two things on top of the post, and one of them is a bundle with an elastic band round it, and one of them is a single envelope, and the envelope is not on the stock of this borough and has a departmental reference on it in the correct typeface.

**There is something under the post.**

He did not move the post. He did not lift the bundle. He did not look at the reference and he did not look at the envelope and he does not know what is under it and nobody in this borough is going to tell him, and there is no form in this city that would require anybody to.

And he stood there for about nine seconds and worked out the only thing about that desk that is a fact and not a story, and it is this:

**A piece of paper under two other pieces of paper is a piece of paper that has stopped being dealt with, and that is the oldest way in the world of saying so, and anybody who works in an office knows it, and nobody in this borough has to be told it, and there is no instrument anywhere that could go and find a thing like that on a tray and ask what it is.**

A register with nineteen entries and a blank line under the nineteenth says that nothing was added. **A tray with post on top of something says that something was asked for and not dealt with, and it says it in a way that cannot be answered, because answering it would mean taking the post off.**

---

**And on the Friday morning he worked out the second thing, and he wrote it in the shop at about two o'clock in the afternoon, and it is the last paragraph of that pad's page, and it is the reason he is not going to say anything to a records supervisor of about fifty this month or any month.**

> *Tenth of April, half past eleven, the second floor.*
> *She said on the first of December that if anybody in this borough ever uses that board against anybody they will not use the meter room. They will use the bulb. And she has been right about that for four months and being right about it is the reason the line under the nineteenth has been blank for longer than it has ever been blank.*
> *And she said something else on the first of December, and I have not written it down because I was not asked to and because it is hers.*
> *A register that grows when nobody is hurt is a register somebody can hold over you. That is the second half. That is the half I have been carrying since December without knowing I was carrying it.*
> *It is not going to be the bulb. It is going to be the shelf, because the shelf is the only thing in this building that grows, and it grows out of correct things done by careful people, and nobody is hurt by it, and in about four years somebody is going to hand a piece of paper to a frightened person and it is not going to have a bulb on it, it is going to have nine dates and one hand on it.*
> *And I know which side of that she is on. She said it on the first of December and she was right. And it is going to happen, and it is not going to happen to her, and she does not know that yet, and I am not going to tell her, and there is no instrument in this borough by which I could tell her, and if I told her she would be the woman who was warned.*

He read it back once and did not improve it and did not tear it out.

**A register that grows when nobody is hurt is a register somebody can hold over you. And it was not the bulb, and she was right, and she is going to be right, and the piece of paper that will do it is about four inches by two and it has no name on it and no reference and no box, and there are more of them in that drawer than there were in March, and about eleven people a day walk in and out of that room, and there are more pieces of paper in the drawer than there were in March, and nobody has been asked whether any of it is worth anything, and it is not going on a wall.**
