# Chapter 0036 — The Assembly Sits

The Reclamation Season maintenance review sat in committee room two of the Old Ward at half past nine in the morning with six members and a minute book and a public gallery of forty-one, and the first item on the schedule was the two lowland nodes and the six words in the box.

Jonas Mercer stood up to present the series 6 response, which was the last thing anybody in the city had asked him to do, and read all two hundred and forty words of it, and the chair, Councillor Fenella Marsh, let him finish without interrupting, which he had clearly been told to expect and had braced for.

“Thank you. Reporting officer, series 6 plate 41, grade three, present for the classification item only and not otherwise cleared. And before we start: the six words at the top of that recommendation are mine, and there are six of them, and two of them are a node number and four of them are two words apiece, and I would like that on the record so that nobody in this room has to guess which six I mean.” He put the sheet face down. “That is the response. I am not going to summarise it and I am not going to explain it, because everything in it is either in the file or on a wall in a place with no address, and both of those are better sources than me.”

“Then we go to the recommendation,” said Councillor Marsh. “Non-civic plant, both nodes, authored on the fourteenth of March at 12:41, lodged on the twenty-second, on your reporting officer number. Mr Mercer, you have three options and I am going to read them because I would like them on the record in that order. You may maintain it, you may withdraw it, or you may decline to speak to it. Do you maintain it?”

“No.”

“You do not maintain it.”

“No, ma’am.”

“Will you withdraw it?”

“I will not, and I am going to say why in one sentence and then I am going to sit down, because the reason is the same reason it exists. There is a cure available to me and I cannot write it. The cure is a refusal to classify, on the ground that the local custodians of the node were asked for a statement of function and declined, and that the reporting officer therefore has no basis for a finding. I cannot file that, because a refusal to classify is a statement about what those custodians said, and they have told me they will not say it, and I am not permitted to put words in the mouths of people who have refused to speak. So it stays as it is, and I am not withdrawing it, and I am not maintaining it, and I would like the panel to understand that the man who wrote it is not the man who is arguing for it.”

He sat down.

Royden Achebe was in the room as field services and he was the author of the lodging, and he stood up, and he said, “The department maintains the recommendation as lodged. The plate is a flap gate and a standpipe. Neither carries a civic function. If the panel wants a positive finding, the department can supply it, and the department does supply it, and the department has been supplying it since the twenty-second of March.”

Oren Pell stood up second, and he stood up without a clipboard, which Jonas noticed.

“I want it minuted that I am the deputy registrar and I am not here in support of a closure and I am not here in support of a plant finding, and that the registrar’s office objects to the classification on a filing ground and one filing ground only. The recommendation states that neither node carries a responsible holder. A node at 6-L/19 carried a holder at 13:30 on the seventeenth of March and that holder was a rota, and there is a stamped and witnessed term on the public board outside this building with a line on it that says the term expired on the twenty-first at half past one, and it has not been renewed since, and the reason it has not been renewed is a clause that is binding on the officer who wrote it, which is Mr Mercer, and he cannot renew it and has not tried to.” He looked at the panel. “I am not asking this panel to decide anything. I am asking it to record that a node with a lapsed holder is not the same instrument as a node with no holder, and that the difference is the entire dispute.”

“And you are aware, Mr Pell,” said Councillor Marsh, “that the department is not a holder of a plate term and that the term in question was issued by the rota and not by the department.”

“I am aware of it and I have written four thousand words about it this month,” said Oren Pell, “and I have not managed to put one word of it into a file that anybody can cite, which is why the panel is hearing it from me and not from a document, and I would ask the panel to forgive me that and to weight it accordingly.”

The chair then did the thing that Jonas had been waiting for since the eighth of March and had not wanted.

“Mr Mercer. You attended the node at 6-L/14 on the fifteenth, the seventeenth, the twenty-first and the twenty-second of March. You have told this panel and the registrar that a civic plate is present in the chamber and that it is silted and that one line of it is legible. I would like the line read into the record.”

There were forty-one people in the gallery and Jonas could feel every one of them.

“No, ma’am.”

The chair waited.

“There is a condition line on that plate,” said Jonas, “and it is legible, and I have read it, and I am not going to read it into a public record.”

Councillor Marsh put her pen down. “Mr Mercer, that is not an option available to a reporting officer at this panel.”

“I know it is not, and I am doing it anyway, and I would like the minute to say that I was asked and declined and was not permitted to decline.” He kept his voice level and it took more out of him than the refusal in Marsh Lane had. “The reason is that a minute is a list. Every document that this panel has ever produced is a list, and a list is the only shape this city knows how to ask a place to take, and the one thing that has gone wrong on that street in thirty-two years is not the absence of a service. It is the arrival of a list. If I read that line, then there is a document in a city file that says a plate on a street with no address answers for something, and after that document the panel will want to see the plate, and to see the plate somebody has to write down where it is, and there is no address, and the only way there is ever going to be an address is if somebody who lives there is made findable in order to produce one. I am not going to be the instrument of that. It is a bad decision to withhold evidence from a statutory panel and I am making it deliberately, in a room, at nine forty in the morning, and I would like it recorded as a decision and not as an oversight.”

“Your reasons are minuted,” said Councillor Marsh, “in those words, at nine forty-two.”

They took forty minutes and the decision, when it came, was the decision nobody in the room had asked for.

**SERIES 6 — RECLAMATION SEASON MAINTENANCE REVIEW. 1 APRIL.**
**6-L/14: NON-CIVIC PLANT. ACCEPTED. NODE LEAVES THE REVIEW. NO CIVIC CLAIM ATTACHES TO THE CONTENTS OF THE CHAMBER OR THE BANK.**
**6-L/19: NON-CIVIC PLANT. NOT ACCEPTED. NODE REMAINS IN SCOPE PENDING PHYSICAL INSPECTION. NO INSPECTION SCHEDULED — NO LOCATION OF RECORD.**

Jonas sat in the gallery for the second half of the morning and did not move, and the second line went into the file over his head, and it is the single worst sentence in this whole business, and it is four lines long, and nobody wrote it who was cruel.

The barrier had cost four working mornings inside a tide table and six people and seven hundred and sixty pounds out of a church hall tin and thirty-nine pounds out of a woman’s own pocket, and it held a four-hour flood on the night of the twenty-second and it was set to shut on the flood by a painted instruction that had been under a lead seal for a hundred years, and by a quarter past ten on the first of April it was plant. Plant does not have a claim. The next time the lowland fills, the flap will shut because a man in a department ironworks set a stop in 2011 that was hidden under a sill until a boy of seventeen found two sets of bolt holes, and nobody in this city has any duty to it whatever, and there is no name on the line, and the ring round the blank space on the loft wall is no longer a gap that somebody might fill. It is a gap that the city has now formally confirmed it cannot see.

---

He caught Royden Achebe in the corridor at ten past twelve with a panel member’s answer in his hand and nowhere to be for two hours.

“You are going to let them take it.”

“I am going to sit in a room for six weeks and watch six people decide whether the two words I put in a file on the twenty-second of March are correct,” said Achebe, “and you are going to sit next to me, and neither of us is going to enjoy the afternoon. So let us not do the thing where you tell me what I did.”

“I did not write your words.”

“You wrote the six words the four lines were built out of, and you initialled them at 12:41 on a Friday, and Royden Achebe is a man with a mortgage and a department of thirty-four technicians who could not put a finding on a plate without a signature from you.” He put the tie away. “I withdrew a draft on the fourteenth because a department cannot be the holder of a plate. That was the most useful thing anybody has done for this department in a decade and I have been living inside the consequence of it ever since. If a department cannot hold it, the nearest person has to, and the nearest person is you, and you are thirty-two and probationary and you have a cabinet you may not open. So do not stand in a corridor and be shocked at me. I have been the answer on this file since March. The only reason it is not in my name is that you would not sign it.”

He went back into the committee room and left Jonas in a corridor holding a panel answer whose fourth line said that no inspection would be scheduled in the absence of a location of record, which meant that the one thing in this building that could have put a name on two plates and one tap was not going to be scheduled by anybody, ever, in any room.

---

The Civic Assembly sat at two o’clock with a full gallery and one hundred and nine members and an item on the paper that had been on no agenda in this city since the Quiet Cut, which Jonas knew about and the nineteen people in the morning gallery had mostly not.

The continuity contract went first and it was delayed in the ordinary way.

A councillor moved a Standing Committee amendment to refer it back for further report, on the ground that the committee had not yet heard the contractor’s operational figures. That was true. It was carried on a show of hands without a division, with a reporting date of the second of June, and not one clause of the contract was struck and not one was approved.

“That is not a defeat,” Ceri Vaughan said afterwards, in a corridor, to nobody in particular. “That is a delay with a date on it, and I have a date on everything, so I will see you on the second of June.”

The Brine Wharf closure was ratified on a procedural motion in ninety seconds, the ratifying words including *subject to the appellant’s panel on the twenty-third of April*. Oren Pell stood and said nothing at all when it was passed, and Jonas understood that a man who has been right about a building twice does not get any pleasure from the third.

Councillor Iven Sore got the Weir into the record.

She did not have a motion. She had a procedural submission, which is a different thing, and she made it under a standing order that allows a member to place a document before the Assembly, and the document was one side of a sheet of ruled net-loft paper, pinned with four paperclips and a piece of tape over the seam, and it had been on a public board under a glass canopy since the twenty-second of March.

Jonas was on his feet before it was read.

“Objection. That document contains no signature, no address, no reference, and no author, and it describes a work on a node that this Assembly has just classified as plant, and I ask that it not be received.”

“Received,” said the Speaker, Councillor Adaeze Farrant. “The standing order is that a member may place a document and it is received. Mr Mercer, if you have an objection to its contents, the Assembly will hear it after the document is read. You may sit down or you may stand there.”

He sat down.

It took ninety seconds to read and it said the work and the cost and the refusal, and the middle of it was the sentence in the slightly larger hand.

**WE ARE NOT A DISTRICT. WE HAVE NEVER BEEN A DISTRICT. WE ARE A STREET WITH WATER ON IT AND A FLAP GATE AT THE BOTTOM OF IT. WE ARE NOT GIVING A LIST OF HOUSES AND WE ARE NOT GOING TO.**

“Nobody is named,” said Councillor Sore. “I have checked, because I would not have moved it otherwise. Not one personal name, not one door number, and a ring drawn round a blank space on a wall somewhere in this city with nothing inside it. I want that document on the record, because in four weeks it will be a *file*, and a file can be produced, and in six months somebody in an office will produce it in a hearing about a plant asset.”

“Councillor, I have a motion,” said Sore, “and it is the only motion in this Assembly tonight on this matter and I am going to move it, and I am telling you now that I expect to lose it, and I am moving it anyway, because in six years I will be able to say I moved it.

“That this Assembly require a named service holder for node 6-L/19, Lowland Works weir stand, and direct that the department appoint one.”

The Speaker looked at her for a second.

“Cannot be moved,” she said, and there was a half-second in which she looked as though she was going to enjoy it. “The standing orders do not permit this Assembly to require a person to accept a duty. A duty is accepted or it is not held, and this House may not put a name in a holder field.”

“And the motion needs a service. Whether there is a service at 6-L/19 is the question this morning’s review declined to answer, and I am not going to answer it from this chair in the afternoon when a committee in this building is opening the file on it in about an hour.”

Councillor Sore said, “Point taken,” and sat down, and Jonas wrote in his pad, in the margin, in his own hand, in a book that stayed in his own pocket: *she noticed it. she cannot do anything with it. that is the machine, and I am inside it.*

And then the Speaker read the interim motion, and Jonas understood, about two seconds before it was read, what it said, and what it was for, and who it was for.

“That the Assembly appoint an interim lowland maintenance review, pending the physical inspection of 6-L/19 and any further classification, with the duty of maintaining continuity of service at those nodes; the post to be filled by the reporting officer of record for series 6 plate 6-41, issuer of the west-clinic care entrance, and for the lowland chain hanging off it.”

“Fenella,” said Councillor Sore, from across the floor, mildly.

“I am aware,” said Councillor Marsh.

It costs nothing and it takes no money and it discharges nothing, and it solves one specific problem, which is that a series 6 file has an open item with a name in it and an Assembly does not like open items with names in them on the eve of a season. Nobody in the gallery had understood what they were being offered. If Jonas took it, the Weir’s water would become a municipal service held by the man who classified it as plant, the file would close, and everyone in the room would go home.

He stood up.

“Ma’am, I decline.”

The Speaker said, “Declined in the room, minuted. The Assembly will fill the post.”

“The department will fill the post,” said Royden Achebe, standing, before the Speaker had finished, and Jonas turned round in the aisle and looked at the man who had written the six words into the lodging and put his own name under them on the twenty-second of March, and who had said *a department cannot be a holder of a plate* and had been right, and who was now offering to be the nearest responsible maintenance route for a street with fifty-nine doors and no address.

“Mine,” said Achebe. “Field services. It is my file, it is my classification, and if the Assembly wants somebody to hold it, it is not going to be a grade three who has been reviewed twice this month and declined, and I will say the thing that makes people uncomfortable, which is that the man who wrote *non-civic plant* is the man who ought to be standing next to it, and if I am standing next to it then somebody will come and ask me what a civic plant is, and I will have to have an answer, and that is the entire mechanism and I am not going to pretend to be too pure for it.”

“Moved and carried,” said the Speaker.

Jonas was on his feet before it had finished echoing, and the Speaker let him get two words out and then stopped him.

“On the fourteenth of March this department withdrew a draft in this room because a department cannot be the holder of a plate. I was in the room. The point is noted, Mr Mercer, and the motion is before the House and you may not reopen it. The House will hear anything else at the item.”

“Point noted,” said Jonas.

“Noted, and not answered,” said Royden Achebe, without turning round. “I have read that minute, Mr Mercer, I wrote most of it, and you are right, and it is also the reason I have just put my name on a list instead of the department’s. I am not standing here as a department. There is no department in that field. There is a man called Royden Achebe who has held a field service for six years and who will be served, and if the department had gone in that field the panel would have thrown it out inside a week, and the file would have been open again on Thursday.”

He said it to the Speaker and not to Jonas, and Jonas sat down, and the city was exactly as safe as it had been on Wednesday.

Councillor Iven Sore caught him at the doors of the chamber on his way out and did not congratulate him on anything, which he had not expected, and did not commiserate, which he had.

“Mr Mercer,” she said. “You have had an impossible afternoon and I am not going to add to it. One thing, and then I am going to a room, and I am going to say it to you because you are the only man I have spoken to today who did not put one of my sentences into a document.

“You are going to hold that piece of paper in your hand for the rest of your life and you are going to keep it out of files, and that is a real thing and I have watched two men do it. And it is also the only power that street has, and in about four years a man with a budget is going to come and offer to buy it, and he will be polite, and he will be right about the trap, and you are going to have to work out how to refuse him without putting their names in a list, and I do not know how anybody does that twice.”

“And the other thing, which is not advice.” She took her coat off the back of the chair. “All of this city’s machinery for the next flood — the order your registrar made this month, the closures, the emergency powers — works by naming a body and giving it the power to act when nobody can be reached. And the moment a person on a street can be named, that machinery reaches for them. It does not ask. It has no procedure for asking. It reaches, and in nine years I have watched every one of the hundred and forty people it reached in this city say yes, because saying no to an emergency body in Nacre is not a thing anybody has ever been able to do.

“You have said no twice today, in two rooms, and I would like this House to have noticed, because it is not supposed to be available. I moved that motion and lost it and I would move it again. And I am telling you, as the only man in this building who has just been made to be the answer and has said no, that the reason I want that contract passed in June is not that I want one person holding this city. It is that I do not believe it can keep naming people one at a time until the water comes.”

“You will not believe a word of that,” she said, “and you should not, and I have said it anyway.” And she went.

---

It was nine past four when he got out into the yard under the glass canopy. The board had two new lines on it in the flat register and a piece of ruled net-loft paper clipped to the frame, and a laminated card from a company in Marsh Lane.

Bea Osunde was standing in front of the board with her coat on, looking at the two new lines, and she had been there long enough to have read them twice.

“There is the answer,” she said.

“There is the answer.”

“I have told you twice that you have stopped being the answer on that street and have not noticed, and I have told you the third time has to come from you or it does not count, and I have been standing here for twenty minutes working out how to make it count anyway, which I am not going to do.” She turned round. “So say it. Say the thing yourself, in words, to a person, out loud, and then I will go home.”

“I have stopped being the answer on that street,” said Jonas Mercer.

Bea Osunde did not move.

“I noticed it on the seventeenth of March at about half past four in the afternoon in a kitchen, when you told me I had put a name in a holder line and a plate would not take a role without one, and I went out to the towpath and I sat on the wall for forty minutes. I have been carrying that round for a fortnight and I have used it as a reason not to ask anybody for anything, and it has been the most comfortable thing I have owned since the fourteenth of March. And this afternoon an Act of the Assembly put a chair in front of me with my name on it, and I said no in a room in about a second and a half, and Royden Achebe took it standing up before the Speaker had finished the sentence, and the reason I said no is that you told me to and not because I had worked it out, and that is the third thing and I am saying it to you because you said it had to come from me.”

“That is true,” said Bea Osunde. “It is also not the thing you need, and I have been waiting twenty minutes to tell you that as well.

“What you need is to be able to say it to somebody who does not care whether you have said it. Not to me — I have been telling you since the fourteenth of March and I have a stake and a yellow board. Not to a woman of seventy-eight with a tin. Not to a nineteen-year-old who has already told you the shape of it. Every single person who has said that sentence to you in this month has been paid to say it or has a reason to, and you have started to be able to say it only to people who already agreed with you, which is the same trick the department does with a form.”

She got her bag off the wall.

“So here is the fourth thing, and it is mine and not yours, and then I am going home. The bond stops you from doing work you have not named. It does not stop you from walking into rooms and asking people for things. You have been confusing the two for a fortnight and the second one is the actual job and there is no instrument for it and no instrument is coming, and if you want one you will have to build it out of strangers, one at a time, without me, because I am a grade two line technician and I have nineteen years and I am not your conscience and I am not going to be anybody’s conscience, and I have four people to go and see about a gate on a Wednesday.”

She went. Jonas stood under the glass canopy for another few minutes.

Under the glass, in the flat register, the city had two new sentences and a piece of paper it could not use and a card it had not answered, and the man who had read all three of them walked away from the board for the first time in a month without writing anything down.
