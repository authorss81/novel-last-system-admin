# Chapter 0047 — The Map Redraws Itself

The map came out of a printer in the Old Ward basement on the Friday night and Jonas Mercer had it in his hands at about eleven o'clock and did not put it down until Saturday afternoon.

It is a service diagram, four sheets, stapled at the corner, printed off the Lattice's own drawing layer, which is the only drawing layer anybody in the city has, and it is not a street map. It shows the civic nodes and the districts that hold terms, in a flat grey key, and each district is a closed shape with a name in it and a small square for its holder, and a line for its term expiry, and a number for the node in the middle of it.

It shows nine service districts.

Nine ordinary ones, with a name in each and a filled square for the holder, and a line for the expiry, and a node number in the middle: a borough, a trust, a company, a hospital, a school, a transit operator, a housing association, a market, and a utilities district. Those are not the city's eight administrative districts, and they have never been; a service district is a shape the drawing layer makes out of the terms in force inside it, and a shape with a term in it is a service district whatever anybody at the education office calls the ward. Every one of the nine has a name. Every one of the nine has a holder. Every one of the nine has a date on it, and five of the nine have a claim against the holder, and two of the nine have a small printed mark in the corner that Jonas Mercer did not ask about and has read twice.

And in the middle of the four sheets, in the position where a city has a centre, there is nothing.

Not a name and not a number and not a node and not a district. A blank square the size of a thumbnail, in the middle of the middle, and the key has no line for it, because there is no line in the city for it.

“Two things,” said the man from the basement, who was thirty-one and had a print cardigan on and who had printed a hundred and forty of these in March and had never once been asked about a ninth sheet. “The eighth sheet got wet, sorry. And the middle of that is not a printing fault. That is what it is. It has been that since I was on this desk and I did not know anybody else in the building knew.”

“How long has that square been blank.”

“I asked that once,” said the printer, “in about 2011, and the woman I asked said it is a root field and it has no holder, and root fields are not drawn, and can I go back to my work.”

Jonas Mercer stood in a basement in the Old Ward at eleven o'clock on Friday night, a probationary grade three technician with no appointment, no cabinet, no gate-initiation rights, and a chain of written claims with his name at the end of it, and a hand that would not close, and he looked at the blank square for a long time without touching the print, because there is a rule about not touching a drawing layer and about not touching a map, and both of them are about the same thing.

Then he noticed that there was a shape inside the blank square.

It was very faint, in grey, and it was not a district: it was a closed outline, about forty millimetres across, with a small empty square in the middle of it where the other nine have filled ones, and a dot where the other nine have a node number, and under it, in the smallest type the printer has, one line.

**DRAWN FROM A TERM IN FORCE 16:40, 17 APRIL TO 06:00, 18 APRIL. NO HOLDER FIELD. NO NAME. NO CLASSIFICATION.**

The term that drew it was issued at twenty to five on Thursday afternoon in a net-loft kitchen, out loud, in about four minutes, by a woman of sixty-one who would not give her name to a plate and would not stand up to read her own paper to a hospital board on the ninth of April, which was eight days earlier, and Jonas Mercer had been in that doorway with his coat on, holding a plate that says no term answers to a forecast, and had not read it out loud. It had not been issued for a service. It had been issued for a door, a stove, a floor and a woman of seventy-seven with a chest, and it was the shortest-lived term in the history of the chain, and it is the only reason anything at all is drawn inside the blank square, and it expired at six o'clock on Friday morning, and it is still drawn there — and this print came out of a machine at about eleven o'clock on the Friday night, which is seventeen hours after the term ended, and the five words underneath this paragraph were on the wall of that basement while the machine was breaking them.

**THE MAP IS DRAWN FROM THE TERMS IN FORCE. A DISTRICT WITH NO TERM IS NOT DRAWN.**

He read those five words twice with a finger on them, and they are true, and he has spent five weeks learning that everything anybody prints in this city is true. All nine districts on that print were on it because there was a term in force inside each of them at eleven o'clock on a Friday night. There was no term in force inside the blank square, because there had not been one since six o'clock that morning, and a term that has ended is not a term, and if the rule is true then the shape in the middle of that square cannot be there.

It is there. It is faint, and it is grey, and it has an empty holder square in it and a dot where a node number should be, and the layer has annotated its own error underneath in the smallest type it has, the way a man who has been wrong will write down exactly what he was wrong about and where.

He carried the four sheets up the stairs and out under the canopy, and the board was lit at the timer, and nobody in that building printed a second one, and nobody in that building thought there was anything to print, and he did not ask, because the man at the desk had said *that is what it is* about the middle of it and had not said anything about the mark, and the man at the desk printed a hundred and forty of these in March and has not had a conversation about one of them since, and would not thank a technician at eleven o'clock on a Friday night for a theory. The print is in his coat.

The Old Ward committee room two holds six at a table and forty-one in the gallery and the gallery was full at five to eleven on Saturday the nineteenth of April, which was four days' notice, and which had happened because Fenella Marsh is a chair who will convene on four days' notice if the notice is going to be about water.

Present: Marsh in the chair. Sore. Four other members, two of whom had never been in the room. Oren Pell, in attendance as registrar, not a member. Nina Calder, records. Royden Achebe, in attendance in his own name as the holder of the interim lowland post and of an unclaimed burden attached on the afternoon of the eighteenth. Jonas Mercer, in attendance as a witness to the determination of the seventeenth. **Alden Rook**, for CivicCore, and Ceri Vaughan, who had come from a hearing in Juniper Flats and had not said why she had come and did not need to.

The first item was four minutes and it was the one everybody came for.

“Item one,” said Marsh. “Determination of emergency continuity, Saint Orra west care entrance, made at 11:20 on the seventeenth of April by the CivicCore Consortium under paragraph eleven of the Saint Orra service agreement of 2019. Registrar’s finding, delivered in the car on the night and entered this morning: the term in question had expired at 06:00 on the fifteenth and had been renewed at 22:00 on the sixteenth, with two holders and a witness present, and a term which ends on the end written into it has not failed within the meaning of paragraph eleven. The determination was not within the power of the party that made it. It is suspended pending determination of this committee. The suspension is not a dissolution. CivicCore Consortium continues to hold and operate the physical network under the existing agreement, and the agreement is not before this committee today, and if anybody in the gallery has come to dissolve a contractor I would like them to go home and come back on the second of June.”

Somebody in the gallery said something. Rook did not turn round.

“Second item,” said Marsh, “and this is the difficult one and I am going to take it in the order it is written.”

Councillor Sore moved that the committee adjourn, that the hearing on the continuity contract scheduled for the second of June be adjourned to a later date, and that the committee be directed to report on no date until it had established a definition of the word *holder* for the purposes of any node in the chain.

“That motion is not in the interest of my own party,” said Councillor Sore, before anybody had asked her, “and I am moving it, and I am not going to explain it in this room, and I would like the minute to record that I said that.”

“Seconded,” said a member.

“Any objections to the *form* of the question,” said Marsh.

There were none. It is a rare morning in that room when the form of a question is uncontroversial, and the reason it was uncontroversial is that between half past four and twenty to five on Friday afternoon a registrar attached an unclaimed burden to a piece of lowland plant, and no member of that committee had known before that afternoon that the word *holder* was not a thing anybody could define, and all of them had spent the morning finding out.

**Adjourned. Re-convened 19 May. The committee is unable to report on a contract which turns on the concept of a holder until it has determined what a holder is.**

---

The third item was the weir.

“Item three. Unclaimed burden, Lowland Wards 6-L/14, chamber and bank, attached on the eighteenth of April to the interim lowland maintenance post, series 6, holder Royden Achebe. The chair will note that the committee is unable to take any step in relation to the node, for the reason given on the first of April, which the chair is going to read out again because it is the single most useful sentence that has ever been said in this room.” Marsh read from the sheet in front of her. *“This House may not require a person to accept a duty, and the motion needs a service whose existence was not established that morning.”* “That is unchanged. Is the holder able to give the committee a date by which a report could be produced.”

“Twenty-ninth of May,” said Royden Achebe, without looking up.

“Will that date require an inspection.”

“It will require somebody to know where it is,” said Royden Achebe, “and the honest answer from this table is that nobody does, and I have written a piece of paper about that and it is a very short piece of paper and it is not ready, and I am not going to stand here and pretend to twenty people that the seventeenth of April solved anything for me, because the seventeenth of April made my problem bigger and gave it a name, which is what a public record is for.”

Nobody spoke for a moment. The gallery was the quietest it had been in five weeks.

“Item four,” said Marsh. “Councillor Sore’s paper of the first of April, received as a document, together with a registrar’s record of the eighteenth of April, together with a temporary term issued in a net-loft kitchen on the seventeenth. The chair is going to read the resolution and then the chair is going to stop, because the chair has been advised by the registrar that everything after the word *provided* is outside the competence of this committee and the chair is not going to pretend otherwise in public.”

The resolution read:

> **THAT THE SHAPE SHOWN UNNAMED INSIDE THE BLANK CENTRE OF THE SERVICE DIAGRAM OF 19 APRIL IS RECORDED AS APPEARING ON THAT DIAGRAM;**
> **THAT NO HOLDER FIELD IS CREATED FOR IT AND NO NAME IS ENTERED AGAINST IT;**
> **THAT FOR NINETY DAYS A CLAIM MAY BE STATED IN THE BOX AT THE OLD WARD BOARD, AND A CLAIM SO STATED DOES NOT ESTABLISH A SERVICE, DOES NOT REQUIRE A NAME, AND MAY NOT BE USED AS EVIDENCE OF OCCUPANCY, POPULATION, HOUSEHOLDS OR PERSONS;**
> **AND THAT THE COMMITTEE IS UNABLE TO ACT ON THE MATTER AND RESERVES ITS POSITION.**

“Thank you,” said Oren Pell, from the side of the room.

Then item five, and it is the one that the Old Ward public notice board carried for the rest of that day and the next, and that Jonas Mercer did not know about until the Monday, because he was not in the room when it was done, and had not been asked, and would not have been asked.

Nina Calder had a piece of paper in front of her and it was not minutes, because it was not minutes, and it had been drafted twice by a records supervisor who had been told to draft it twice.

“The registrar’s determination of the eighteenth of April attaches an unclaimed burden to a named holder,” said Nina Calder. “That is the first time in the life of this department that a burden has been attached to a person who agreed to it in a telephone call. There is no register of where that person can be found, because he is not a post, and a person who is findable is the entire mechanism by which the Lattice assigns a claim. The chair has asked me to raise whether the city should maintain a register of the person to whom an unclaimed burden attaches, so that a burden is never attached to a person who cannot be reached.”

“Objection?” said Marsh.

There was none, and Oren Pell's objection, when he made it, was on filing grounds and was correct, and is in the minute.

“Proceed.”

“The entry will be in these terms,” said Nina Calder, and read it out, and Jonas Mercer was standing at the back of the gallery of the Old Ward committee room two in a jacket with a map under his arm and had not been told that any of it was going to happen, because nothing in five weeks had told him that anything in this city was going to happen to him before it happened.

> **REGISTER OF ATTACHMENT. Name: MERCER, J. Position: LAST WORKING SYSTEM ADMINISTRATOR, CITY OF NACRE. Appointing resolution: none. Status: civic standing, not departmental. Not subject to departmental review, grade, or probation. Liability: bears unclaimed burdens and maintenance claims attached to the civic chain in his own name, in the city, findable by the city, until released by no person.**

“That last line,” said Councillor Sore, “is remarkable.”

“That last line,” said Oren Pell, “is the entire point and it is mine, and Nina Calder drafted it twice and I have read it back, and the chair is going to minute the reason, because I am the one who has to live in the room this register creates.”

“Will the chair minute the reason.”

“I will minute the reason,” said Oren Pell, “and the reason is this. On the eighteenth of April a piece of lowland plant with no holder was recorded, and an unclaimed burden attached, and the nearest responsible maintenance route is a man with a name. On the first of May, or in June, or in three years, the same thing will happen to another node, and the Lattice will attach it to the nearest person who can be reached, and I am the officer who must send somebody to that node, and I have spent nineteen years sending people.

“This register does not give that man power. It gives him no root access, no precedence, no office, no staff, no vote, no key, and no right to refuse an attachment, and it does not make him a department, and a department cannot hold a plate and I withdrew a draft to that effect in March and I was right. What it does is put his name in a place where the city can find it, permanently, without a review, and it makes the claims land on him in the open instead of in a file in a series 6 drawer, and it makes the next person who wants to attach a burden to somebody in this city have to look at a register first and see that he is already the one.

“That is not a promotion. Nobody in this room should leave here thinking it is a promotion. It is a bucket under a tap, and the tap is going to run for a long time, and I would like the minute to say that I said it.”

“Minuted,” said Fenella Marsh.

And then Jonas Mercer was asked whether he wished to make any observation, which is the ordinary thing at the end of an item in that room, and he had about four seconds and he used them.

“Can it be taken off,” he said.

“No,” said Oren Pell. “Not by the department, not by a grade, not by a review, and not by you. It is released by no person. That is a phrase Nina Calder invented and I have read it four times since Tuesday and I have not been able to improve it and I have not been able to decide whether it is a protection or a trap.”

“All right,” said Jonas Mercer.

“All right,” said Oren Pell.

He did not thank anybody, and he did not say it was a honour, and he asked one more question because he is a technician and it was the only one that mattered.

“Would it have been better if a person had taken it.”

“Yes,” said Oren Pell. “It would have been better, and the person it would have gone to is a person on a street with no address, and there are three of them it would have gone to, and I did not offer it to them and neither did anybody else in this room, and if you had refused this today then the next attachment would have gone to the next reachable man, and Mr Achebe can tell you who that is, and he does not need to, because he has been it for seventeen days and it is in the minute of the first of April in a different building.”

“I will minute that,” said Fenella Marsh, and had the pen down.

“I am obliged to object,” said Oren Pell. “The candidates are not before this committee, they are not named in this room, and they are not described here.”

“Objection sustained in part and overruled in part,” said Marsh, and wrote it down, and Jonas Mercer understood, standing at the back of that gallery, that she had done it on purpose, and that the registrar had known she would, and that this was the arrangement the two of them had for the twenty years before he got here.

Nobody said anything else about it and the item closed and the room began to move, and Alden Rook, who had sat through three hours of it without once taking a hand out of his pocket, said his piece in the corridor at ten past two, to four people, in about forty seconds, and then went to find his car.

“Two things,” he said, to Jonas, and to Sore, and to Oren Pell, and to Ceri Vaughan. “One, and it is the only thing I have come for: on the second of June the clause comes back. It comes back because you have all discovered in one week that the word *holder* is undefinable, and the first person who can define it for you controls every node in this city, and the answer is not going to be written by a committee, it is going to be written by whoever puts a schedule in front of you.

“And two, to you,” he said to Jonas. “On the fourteenth of March I offered you forty-four thousand pounds and root notices and every term you have ever issued with no expiry, and you turned it down, and I went away and I have not come back, and I was wrong about one thing only, which was that I thought you would come back. You will not. You are not going to be the man who comes back. You are going to be the man who is in the register, and in about two years somebody is going to use that register to say that the city has an administrator, and I am going to have been right about that in a corridor at half past two in the afternoon and there is going to be nobody in the world who is on my side when I say it.”

He looked at the four of them for a second.

“Do not lose the map,” said Alden Rook. “Maps are the only thing a contractor cannot re-draw without somebody in a room saying so.”

And Jonas Mercer, standing in a corridor in the Old Ward on Saturday the nineteenth of April at twenty past two in the afternoon, with four sheets of paper stapled at the corner under his arm, a tool bag over his other shoulder, and a line of his own handwriting in a clerk's margin on the ninth of April that had started all of this, said, “I have never lost anything in my life,” and then went to find a phone box, because there was one number he had to ring and it was a shed in River Stacks and the man in it would not have heard anything, and there was a number in Bellow Market that he was not going to ring, and there was a doctor in Salt Row who had been refused four consultants and one education office since the twenty-fourth of March, and who had said no to line nine in a corridor on the seventh, and who had not told him one word of any of it, and he was not going to ring her either, and there was a red diary in a bag in a corridor that he was not going to ask about.
