# Series Outline: The Last System Admin

## Core identity

- **Title:** *The Last System Admin*
- **Genre:** urban fantasy / civic progression / workplace mystery
- **Target length:** 720 chapters, 15 volumes of 48 chapters
- **POV:** Primarily Jonas Mercer, with occasional close POVs for Sanaa Iqbal, Tomas Renn, Pia Santos, and major antagonists. POV changes must serve a pressure or relationship, not reveal information Jonas could reasonably obtain.
- **Romance:** One slow-burn relationship, Jonas and Sanaa. No harem.
- **Tone:** grounded, humane, procedural, politically alert, occasionally uncanny.

## One-sentence premise

In the estuary city of Nacre, a municipal network technician who repairs magical permissions that govern doors, clinics, bridges, water, and emergency help slowly inherits responsibility for the rules that keep the city alive, while the ghost of its first administrator offers a permanent solution that would make consent impossible.

## Central promise

Every repair solves a concrete civic problem and reveals a larger question about authority. Jonas can restore a missing term, move a duty, or choose which rule controls an emergency, but each act has a physical, legal, and emotional cost. The story’s progression is toward a system that distributes authority rather than a stronger version of one administrator.

## Protagonist: want, need, flaw, change

### External want

Jonas wants permanent civil-service status, a safe place to live, a repaired Lattice, and proof that his father Evan did not abandon his family. In the first volume, he wants the Volume 1 faults repaired without criminal blame or a public disaster. Across the series, that want expands into wanting the city to remain functional while clearing Evan’s name.

### Internal need

Jonas must learn that responsibility is not ownership. He needs to share decisions with the people affected by them, accept that a correct repair can still be a moral failure, and build a system that can operate without him as its indispensable center.

### Flaw and fear

His flaw is control through competence: he believes he can keep making better decisions than the people he is trying to protect, hides uncertainty, and treats a documented rule as morally neutral. His fear is becoming the permanent administrator who decides who counts, repeating the system that erased Lowglass and took Evan.

### Planned change

By the final volume, Jonas can act without making his protection depend on his own unquestioned authority. He gives up direct root editing, makes his methods public, trains other technicians, and accepts a civic role whose power is intentionally limited.

## Relationship architecture

### Jonas and Sanaa Iqbal

- **Opening state:** professional antagonism; she thinks he fixes systems and ignores the people left inside them.
- **Trust engine:** they share a failed repair rather than a successful rescue; Jonas discloses a claim that could damage him, and Sanaa accepts that care can require a limit.
- **Pressure points:** emergency overrides, patient privacy, public disclosure, and Jonas’s tendency to carry danger alone.
- **Romance milestones:** attraction is not the first bond. They become close through repeated professional cooperation, separate after a medical consequence of Jonas’s repair, rebuild through separate work, choose a relationship in Volume 8, and negotiate a shared final decision in Volume 14.
- **Ending state:** they remain partners, not co-administrators. Neither uses the Lattice to bind or monitor the other.

### Jonas and Tomas Renn

Tomas is the labor counterweight to Jonas’s expertise. He pushes him to sign responsibility bonds, share tools, and let workers decide whether a repair is safe. Their conflict should recur in every major institutional change.

### Jonas and Pia Santos

Pia is not a disciple. She challenges him whenever he treats Lowglass as evidence rather than a community. She supplies practical local knowledge and eventually becomes a central civic steward without becoming his replacement.

### Jonas and Nell Ardent

Nell makes the legal and human cost of a permission visible. She is willing to challenge Jonas publicly and sometimes makes the legal answer worse in the short term in order to protect the right to refuse.

### Jonas and Evan Mercer

Evan is absent for most of the series. His history is a mystery, not a reward or a secret father who hands Jonas a solution. Jonas’s final relationship to Evan is acceptance of an unfinished, flawed act rather than resurrection or condemnation.

## Central mystery

### Surface question

Why does the Lattice grant access to people who do not exist on Nacre’s official map, and why does a blank permission appear whenever the root renewal approaches?

### Layers of the answer

1. A bad handover or rogue operator appears to be granting unauthorized access.
2. CivicCore’s priority contracts exploit and amplify the faults.
3. Lowglass is a real community erased during the Quiet Cut, and its residents are living witnesses to the city’s foundational terms.
4. The old Lattice was designed to require multiple living witnesses, but an emergency amendment created a proxy identity that lets one office stand in for the whole city.
5. Iona Vey, the first administrator, survived as a root witness. Her preserved identity is the “voice” that appears in old notices and can offer succession.
6. Evan Mercer did not die as a traitor. He cut a lowland node to stop the proxy from taking hold; the cut cost his life and Lowglass its municipal visibility, while also leaving the Lattice with Evan’s counter-amendment and its unresolved second signature, which no single authority can safely possess.
7. The blank bearer is not a hidden person. It is the city’s unclaimed collective responsibility: the obligations that survive when every individual is erased or refused. Jonas’s “last admin” access is a fault-maintenance credential, not a prophecy or bloodline inheritance.

### Final answer

The Lattice is a working civic tool built on real consent, labor, and care. Its current crisis is political and structural, not a cosmic invasion. Iona’s permanent authority can save Nacre from collapse in the short term, but it would turn every permission into an extension of one identity. Jonas defeats that solution by making the root layer renewable, distributed, and impossible to inherit as a personal throne. The mystery is fully answered; the broader question of how other cities build or inherit their own permission systems remains intentionally open.

## Antagonist ladder

The ladder alternates personal pressure, institutional temptation, and structural threat. No antagonist is introduced only to be a stronger obstacle for Jonas.

1. **Oren Pell, deputy registrar (Volume 1):** enforces a valid safety closure that protects a building while displacing Lowglass residents. He teaches Jonas that a rule can be valid and unjust at the same time.
2. **Alden Rook, CivicCore operations director (Volumes 2–4):** monetizes priority access and argues that scarce services must be governed by whoever can keep them running. He is a useful, effective antagonist rather than a secret villain.
3. **Councilor Iven Sore, Continuity Bloc leader (Volumes 4–12):** turns repeated emergencies into a political case for permanent central authority. She can save lives and use those rescues to expand power.
4. **Cora Quill, head of the Quiet Archive (Volumes 7–11):** protects erased histories by controlling who may know the root clauses. She exposes Jonas’s ignorance of his own history while separating preservation from public accountability.
5. **The Quiet Archive’s inherited machinery (Volumes 9–12):** the archive contains procedures that can preserve a single identity across generations. The machinery is not a new sentient villain; it is a designed mechanism for permanent proxy authority.
6. **Iona Vey, first administrator and root witness (Volumes 1–15):** the final antagonist and final external authority. She is seeded through her name, notices, and design choices in Volume 1, then revealed as a living preserved identity. She offers continuity by removing meaningful refusal.
7. **The Lattice’s continuity default (throughout, especially Volumes 4 and 14–15):** when a permission reaches the end of its term with no living steward, the system defaults toward preserving essential services by narrowing access. This is a structural pressure, not a new enemy, and the final conflict is partly a fight against this humane but dangerous default.

## Power stages and costs

| Stage | Jonas’s capability | Earned cost and limit | Story function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Field technician | repairs ordinary hinges, resets access plates, follows schematics | ordinary labor, blame, and no authority over terms | establishes work and failure before magic |
| Fault reader | sees the Margin and links faults to holders and terms | sensory bleed, anchored work, and inability to read intent | makes the system visible without solving it |
| Rule mender | restores one missing local term or revokes a future use | takes a seam mark and a real claim; cannot act without a witness | first meaningful power and first ethical trap |
| Transfer steward | moves compatible permissions and duties between willing stewards | legal responsibility follows him; no silent transfer | makes relationships and institutions part of growth |
| Precedence warden | temporarily chooses which compatible rule controls an emergency | suspended work becomes deferred labor; cannot invent a root term | forces choices between real alternatives |
| Root reader | reads the first charter, the Quiet Cut, and the proxy identity | inherits testimony and must make personal claims; history remains incomplete | turns investigation into moral danger |
| Charter editor | coordinates a bounded multi-node change | dependent on public consent; transition disrupts services | makes the climax collaborative |
| Last System Admin | civic caretaker with no direct root authority | permanent loss of the ability to edit the Lattice personally | resolves the title as a choice, not a promotion |

Growth never skips a stage, and each stage must be tested by a practice failure, a relationship cost, or a refused shortcut.

## Relationship milestones

1. Jonas and Sanaa disagree over whether a repaired ward counts as care.
2. Tomas forces Jonas to sign a responsibility bond instead of taking credit silently.
3. Pia requires Lowglass residents to approve any use of their records.
4. Jonas publicly accepts a failed repair and its medical cost.
5. Sanaa and Jonas become friends who can disagree without making every disagreement a personal threat.
6. The Lowglass Witness Network refuses to let Jonas use their crisis as proof of his own innocence.
7. Jonas and Sanaa separate after he accepts a precedence change that denies a patient a needed transfer.
8. Tomas, Nell, and Pia create a working coalition without Jonas.
9. Jonas and Sanaa choose a slow-burn romantic relationship after they can state each other’s boundaries.
10. Jonas refuses Iona’s succession offer and explains his choice to Sanaa before the public vote.
11. Sanaa refuses to become the moral justification for his final sacrifice; they decide together what risk is acceptable.
12. In the ending, Jonas’s relationship survives because neither person is made a permission held by the other.

## Volume progression

The novel runs for fifteen volumes, forty-eight chapters each. Each volume resolves a local promise before opening the next question.

### Volume 01 — The Permission Fault (Chapters 1–48)

- **Central pressure:** Nacre’s first major bridge and clinic repair exposes a citywide permission failure just as CivicCore presses to take over emergency services.
- **Starting state:** Jonas is a probationary field technician, Evan is a missing father, Sanaa is a skeptical emergency physician, and Lowglass is absent from official maps.
- **Major locations:** River Stacks, Saint Orra, the Old Ward, Bellow Market, and the first Lowglass hinges.
- **Major factions:** NDC workers, Saint Orra staff, the Civic Assembly, CivicCore’s field team, and the early Lowglass witnesses.
- **Major movement:** Jonas discovers that the bridge fault is a conflict between an ambulance permission and an obsolete “empty crossing” term. His first civic repair opens a sealed clinic door and assigns him a claim. He follows the resulting access trail into Lowglass.
- **Midpoint reversal:** the person who appears to be deliberately granting access is not a hacker. The permissions are being opened by a child and several residents using an old community watch term that the city erased.
- **Escalation:** Oren Pell closes a building under a valid emergency clause; CivicCore offers Jonas promotion in exchange for signing a blanket continuity waiver; Jonas and Sanaa repair a clinic while the Lattice’s terms begin to fracture; Jonas finds a fragment of Evan’s maintenance signature.
- **Climax:** during a river surge, Jonas coordinates nine local witnesses and a temporary bridge duty rather than restoring central control. The bridge holds, but the Lattice redraws the city map and shows an empty root at the center.
- **Resolution:** the immediate hospital and bridge promises are met. The Continuity contract is delayed, not defeated. Jonas is publicly named the last working system administrator while Lowglass remains legally unlisted.
- **New question:** who owns the records that decide which claims are remembered, especially when a valid claim belongs to someone the official map does not count? The blank root’s origin and Jonas’s credential remain carried mysteries for later volumes.
- **Power/relationship change:** field technician to rule mender; Jonas and Sanaa establish a working but uneasy professional bond; Tomas makes him accept a visible responsibility bond.

### Volume 02 — The Price of Access (Chapters 49–96)

- **Central pressure:** CivicCore turns scarce continuity into a paid priority service, and low-income districts lose water, clinic, and shelter access during a heat emergency.
- **Major movement:** Jonas traces a chain of legitimate-looking assignments that makes a wealthy clinic’s claim outrank a shelter’s. Alden Rook argues that a price is more honest than an unaccountable queue.
- **Midpoint reversal:** the apparent denial is caused by residents signing hollow terms to obtain emergency service, then becoming unable to renew them; the contractor is exploiting the system rather than inventing the injustice.
- **Climax:** Jonas helps tenants, clinic staff, and pump operators revoke one dangerous blanket permission without cutting water to the entire district.
- **Resolution:** priority access is ruled invalid in a public case, but CivicCore retains the physical network. A new cooperative steward board forms.
- **New question:** who owns the root records that decide which claims are remembered?
- **Power/relationship change:** Jonas becomes a transfer steward; Sanaa trusts him with a patient-level claim but not with a city-level one.

### Volume 03 — The City’s Missing Floor (Chapters 97–144)

- **Central pressure:** people in Lowglass and Tideglass begin receiving services only when the Lattice treats them as witnesses to rules the city has denied.
- **Major movement:** Jonas, Pia, and Nell trace the omissions through housing, school, and flood records. They authenticate and interpret Evan’s already-discovered counter-amendment and its second signature, learning that the missing district was not abandoned by accident.
- **Midpoint reversal:** the public list of erased Lowglass residents includes people who are still alive, and one living witness is being used as the city’s bearer.
- **Climax:** a flood barrier and a school crossing are repaired by a mixed Lowglass/Nacre witness group, forcing a public accounting without causing a wider outage.
- **Resolution:** Lowglass residents receive provisional civic standing; their claims are not all granted. Evan’s signature is confirmed, but his fate is not.
- **New question:** what did Evan’s counter-amendment and its second signature actually prevent once its terms are authenticated?
- **Power/relationship change:** Jonas learns transfer and consent; Pia refuses to let him speak for Lowglass; Sanaa and Jonas become close friends.

### Volume 04 — Emergency Powers (Chapters 145–192)

- **Central pressure:** a heat wave and chemical spill create a citywide emergency. Iven Sore proposes a central Continuity Order with a ninety-day renewal.
- **Major movement:** Jonas becomes a precedence warden when hospitals, schools, water pumps, and evacuation routes disagree. The order saves lives, but it also closes a district without a hearing and allows CivicCore to requisition workers. During the dispute, Jonas accepts a precedence change that denies a needed patient transfer; the delay causes real medical harm, Sanaa challenges him publicly, and they separate before rebuilding trust.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Jonas discovers that the first version of the order contains a blank term that will make the ninety-day period permanent unless someone renames it.
- **Climax:** he suspends the order’s most damaging clause while civilians, workers, and councilors all witness the change. The city is saved, but a Continuity Bloc supporter leaks the Lattice’s structure to the public.
- **Resolution:** the ninety-day order expires on schedule. Jonas accepts public responsibility for the temporary override; Sore gains political power and a new constituency.
- **New question:** can a city coordinate without a single emergency office?
- **Power/relationship change:** precedence warden; Jonas and Sanaa argue publicly and choose not to hide the medical consequences from either side.

### Volume 05 — The Borrowed City (Chapters 193–240)

- **Central pressure:** Nacre signs mutual-aid permissions with neighboring cities during a river disaster, but the agreements export labor, patients, and future claims.
- **Major movement:** Jonas travels with Tomas, Nell, and Sanaa through three regional systems. Each city’s Lattice reflects a different bargain between public obligation and private ownership.
- **Midpoint reversal:** one neighbor’s apparently benevolent shared rule is quietly draining Nacre’s repair crews through inherited claims; the regional trade is a political weapon.
- **Climax:** Jonas renegotiates the compact in public, sacrificing Nacre’s emergency priority in exchange for a mutual right of return.
- **Resolution:** the regional pact holds, and Nacre learns that the original Lattice was part of a wider civic experiment.
- **New question:** what happened to the other first administrators?
- **Power/relationship change:** Jonas practices delegation; Sanaa sets a clear personal boundary while remaining close.

### Volume 06 — Schools, Wards, and Other Inhabited Buildings (Chapters 241–288)

- **Central pressure:** ordinary institutions reveal how permission rules determine who can learn, recover, receive care, and return home. A child’s school access is suspended because a district’s safety claim is overdue.
- **Major movement:** low-pressure but consequential chapters let Jonas work with crossing guards, teachers, clinic clerks, and ward cooks. The central mystery appears in old school ledgers and the wording of “temporary shelter.”
- **Midpoint reversal:** Jonas’s repair of a school gate is technically correct but reveals that children are being used as routine witnesses for a service they do not understand.
- **Climax:** a witness network led by teachers and parents refuses a forced evacuation and creates a public definition of informed consent.
- **Resolution:** the school and shelter policies are rewritten locally; the city still cannot agree on a citywide rule.
- **New question:** who gets to define a witness when a person is young, injured, or undocumented?
- **Power/relationship change:** Jonas’s rule-mending becomes less solitary; Tomas and Pia gain independent authority in their domains.

### Volume 07 — The Quiet Archive (Chapters 289–336)

- **Central pressure:** historical records begin disappearing from public access as the Quiet Archive seals the Old Ward for “root protection.”
- **Major movement:** Jonas and Nell enter the archive with Cora Quill’s limited cooperation. They find the first charter, the proxy amendment, and records of people erased before Evan’s generation.
- **Midpoint reversal:** the archive’s preservation method is not merely censorship; it is the only thing keeping several dangerous root permissions from being revived by rumor.
- **Climax:** Jonas reads enough root evidence to identify the proxy but chooses to publish the records before he can finish the repair. The archive loses its monopoly on truth, and several hinges fail.
- **Resolution:** the public recovers names and terms, not every lost fact. Cora survives and becomes a constrained witness.
- **New question:** who is speaking through the old Lattice notices?
- **Power/relationship change:** Jonas enters root reading; his relationship with his mother becomes more honest and less comforting.

### Volume 08 — The Nine Hinges (Chapters 337–384)

- **Central pressure:** a coordinated nine-district outage makes centralized repair tempting. Each district has different terms, histories, and levels of trust.
- **Major movement:** Jonas trains local stewards and deliberately allows districts to choose different repair methods. A district refuses his help and succeeds by using its own council.
- **Midpoint reversal:** the successful local solution is incompatible with the citywide schedule, proving that equality of rules is not always the same as fairness.
- **Climax:** nine local charters hold essential services while Jonas sacrifices the ability to act as the sole coordinator.
- **Resolution:** the city survives and the Assembly recognizes district councils as necessary partners.
- **New question:** what demands a single bearer when every district is repaired?
- **Power/relationship change:** Jonas becomes a root reader; Jonas and Sanaa choose a romantic relationship after agreeing not to use the Lattice for emotional access.

### Volume 09 — The Founder’s Proxy (Chapters 385–432)

- **Central pressure:** the proxy identity appears as a legal office, not a rumor. Iven Sore and the Quiet Archive fight over who may speak for it.
- **Major movement:** Jonas traces the proxy through old court records, maintenance seals, and Iona’s first public notices. He learns that Iona’s identity is preserved in the Old Ward, and that Evan was her apprentice.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Iona’s first message is genuinely helpful and saves a district, making the choice between her offer and a fragile public process materially difficult.
- **Climax:** she offers Jonas succession. He refuses after learning that succession would make every permission he repairs a favor owed to him.
- **Resolution:** the Assembly cannot ban the proxy, but it can require a public hearing; Iona loses informal authority over local stewards.
- **New question:** can the founder be released without abandoning the city?
- **Power/relationship change:** root reader fully activated; Jonas and Sanaa confront the difference between protecting someone and accepting their control.

### Volume 10 — The Last System Admin (Chapters 433–480)

- **Central pressure:** Jonas has enough access to solve Nacre’s immediate problems, but every solution re-creates dependence on him. Public factions call him the only reliable administrator.
- **Major movement:** he attempts a practical distributed mesh while learning that local councils need time, translators, and resources rather than magical emancipation.
- **Midpoint reversal:** one local steward betrays the rollout because Jonas concealed a claim; the failure is caused by his own habit of deciding for others.
- **Climax:** he is removed from office after a public outage, and the city must run a week without him. Local stewards keep most services alive, but the old root remains intact.
- **Resolution:** Jonas accepts that his title is a public function, not a magical rank. Iona’s proxy is revealed as the only way to issue a citywide command.
- **New question:** who will own the transition, and can it survive the founder’s refusal to let go?
- **Power/relationship change:** Jonas loses unilateral authority before he gains it; his relationship with Sanaa strains under public expectation rather than hidden power.

### Volume 11 — A City of Witnesses (Chapters 481–528)

- **Central pressure:** the local mesh works but incompatible district terms make citywide coordination unpredictable. The city must decide what it wants the Lattice to be.
- **Major movement:** Jonas travels through public assemblies and homes rather than command centers. He learns the disagreements over housing, water, and care cannot be solved by a single technical rule.
- **Midpoint reversal:** the public finds Evan’s incomplete counter-amendment. It would make Jonas a permanent counter-witness, but using it would repeat the proxy structure he is trying to end.
- **Climax:** the first federated charter is ratified in principle, yet Iona’s root voice remains the only legal holder of the old root clause.
- **Resolution:** the city has a political destination, not a working constitutional mechanism.
- **New question:** what must be surrendered to make the new charter real?
- **Power/relationship change:** Jonas becomes a charter editor in practice; Sanaa and he choose not to postpone their shared decision indefinitely.

### Volume 12 — The Root Clause (Chapters 529–576)

- **Central pressure:** a final public vote is forced while the Reclamation Season approaches. Iven Sore’s Continuity Bloc argues that only Iona’s proxy can prevent a cascade.
- **Major movement:** Jonas, Nell, Tomas, Pia, and Sanaa collect district consents and publish the trade-offs. The city agrees that central control is unacceptable, but cannot agree on what replaces it.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Iona releases part of the root evidence and makes the historical case for continuity. The truth is not simple enough to make either side easy.
- **Climax:** the Assembly rejects permanent central authority and requires a distributed charter, but the old root remains legally active until a witnessed transition occurs.
- **Resolution:** the political choice is made; the physical and magical transition is not.
- **New question:** can the city cross the Reclamation Season without a single bearer?
- **Power/relationship change:** Jonas accepts a public role as editor but refuses to make his personal claim the city’s constitution.

### Volume 13 — The Continuity War (Chapters 577–624)

- **Central pressure:** central-control supporters, CivicCore remnants, and regional partners attempt to seize the Old Ward during the transition. Iona’s preservation is attacked, and the city’s services are deliberately fragmented.
- **Major movement:** Jonas organizes civic defense, repair, negotiation, and evacuation. The conflict tests whether the new relationships can function without a heroic center.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Iona voluntarily weakens the proxy to prevent a catastrophic root failure, proving that her values are not identical to her past actions.
- **Climax:** Jonas refuses to use the old root as a weapon, even when doing so could end the attack immediately. Local stewards and public workers take the Old Ward instead.
- **Resolution:** the proxy is exposed and its physical and local command is contained. The original root precedence and Iona’s preserved witness voice remain as a dormant final claim, which she can attempt once during the transition but cannot extend by ordinary orders. Iona remains alive and accountable.
- **New question:** can the final charter be written without repeating the quiet cut or sacrificing a district?
- **Power/relationship change:** Jonas’s direct root access becomes unstable; he and Sanaa make a shared risk decision rather than one of them secretly sacrificing.

### Volume 14 — The Last Admin (Chapters 625–672)

- **Central pressure:** the Reclamation Season arrives and all old root terms become due in the same renewal window. The city has a nine-month political window to build a replacement; if the first linked hinge fails, a seventy-two-hour physical cascade window begins.
- **Major movement:** Jonas convenes district stewards, but he cannot issue the necessary common terms. He must work through public disagreement, translation, care, and maintenance.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Jonas takes every claim into himself and saves a neighborhood, proving the old dependence works and causing lasting physical and civic damage. The neighborhood chooses to release him from the burden.
- **Climax:** Jonas writes only an enabling clause, not a complete constitution. It permits a limited citywide transition and expires if the districts do not renew it.
- **Resolution:** the city reaches the final Reclamation threshold with a distributed process intact, but the root amendment still needs a live witness for one last time.
- **New question:** what does Jonas choose when permanent control is the only route that would make the transition certain?
- **Power/relationship change:** Jonas reaches charter editor and consciously prepares to lose direct root editing; Sanaa and he make a private, non-magical promise not to use love as permission.

### Volume 15 — The Last Permission (Chapters 673–720)

- **Central pressure:** a cascading failure strikes every hinge during the final root renewal. Iona offers to become the permanent bearer and can demonstrably stop the immediate collapse.
- **Major movement:** district councils, hospitals, schools, farms, transport workers, residents, and the new witness network each hold one part of the transition. The final conflict is a public struggle over who can make a citywide promise without erasing the people inside it.
- **Midpoint reversal:** the old root can be saved only by making Jonas the permanent counter-witness, the same structure as Iona’s proxy. He refuses to accept the role even when doing so appears to doom the city.
- **Climax:** Jonas uses the narrow enabling clause to create the Civic Commons: nine service-district steward seats, multiple independent witnesses per service, public renewal, explicit claims, and a seventy-two-hour maximum emergency term. He then revokes his own direct root access. Iona signs as an ordinary witness rather than an administrator.
- **Resolution:** essential services return under local terms. The permanent proxy is invalidated, and the city accepts a less seamless system with a public route to correction.
- **New question:** no new antagonist question; the open question is what the city will decide when the next ordinary renewal comes due.
- **Power/relationship change:** Jonas becomes the last system administrator in title only, with no greater access than any trained steward; Sanaa and he remain together, Tomas and Pia hold independent public roles, and Iona survives under oversight.

## Ending constraints

- The final external conflict must be the proxy versus the distributed civic charter, not a new god, invasion, or system awakening.
- Jonas’s final choice must cost him direct root access, physical stability, and the ability to recover every truth about Evan.
- Iona Vey must lose command authority but need not be killed or absolved.
- The city’s new system must be imperfect, renewable, and capable of public correction.
- The central mystery is resolved; only broad, non-threatening questions about other cities and the first Lattice experiment remain open.
- The final image should show ordinary maintenance and local consent, not a crowned administrator.
