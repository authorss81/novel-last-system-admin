## Central pressure

People in **Lowglass** and **Tideglass** begin receiving services only when the Lattice treats them as witnesses to rules the city has denied.

**What was actually on the page, and it is not the same sentence:** the pressure turned out not to be *services withheld from witnesses* but **the discovery that the city has no way to record a person who is not owed anything and has not been wronged.** The omissions were traced — housing, school and flood records — and what came out the other end was not a conspiracy but an absence of fields, and the volume's question became: **what is a service floor for, and who says so, and in which minute?**

## Escalation as delivered

- **Chapters 101–110.** The omissions surface. The **shelf** becomes the volume's central recurring object — a record that exists, that four people can see, and that nobody is permitted to ask about. **A man who turns the bucket for money is the most findable man in this city and nobody did that to him and nobody is going to**, and the refusal to find him is the first refusal the volume counts.
- **Chapters 111–120.** The **hour between six and seven on a Sunday morning**, in which nothing in the borough is in force at all. It is found, named by nobody, and dated by nobody. **The price instead of a principle** is said out loud once, to one person, on 13 April 2015, and is not to be said again.
- **Chapters 121–130.** The **drift** and the inside of the drift, and the **compliance paragraph** standing in front of four hundred and twenty women with a paragraph number on it and a strip of paper going into a drawer on level zero dozens of times a morning by a hand nobody has found.
- **Chapters 131–140.** The **method** and the three people it went out to, and a district it has already touched, and **a head teacher writing her own name in a column on her own account** — the one rota-shaped thing in the volume, and it must not be extended into a pattern.
- **Chapters 141–144, the close.** Three answers, given in one room in nine months, and the consequence of each one staged as a person, a landing and a corridor. **A person who is not counted is not a person who is helped** is the volume's sentence and it was true on 2 June 2014 and is still true on 13 May 2015.

## The finding, and the guardrails it established

**A person who is not counted is not a person who is helped.** A service in this borough has only ever been available to a person somebody put in a field. A field is a box. The box is where the name would go.

**And the guardrails Volume 03 fixed, which every later volume inherits and none of which may be re-derived as a discovery:**

1. **A priced-access case is not a recognition case.** A node with no term is not a person with no address.
2. **A duty is an act and not a place**, and it may not become a way of making a street findable.
3. **Nobody may be given a title, a grade, a department, a chair, a seat, a membership, a form, a rota row, a thank-you, or a way of being thanked.** And nobody refuses to be thanked.
4. **No Weir resident may be described in anything that leaves that street.**
5. **A body that cannot require anybody to do anything cannot be told what it will do, and neither sentence may be argued out of the other.**
6. **The reading rule is spent. Prue Calloway's finding of 23 April 2014 is spent. Royden Achebe is still not told. Peter Naylor is still a wall. The man of forty-eight is still not asked.**
