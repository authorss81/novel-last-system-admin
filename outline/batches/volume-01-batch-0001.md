# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline

Chapter range: 1–10

Batch goal: Establish Jonas Mercer’s ordinary work, carry the Saint Orra bridge-and-clinic failure through its immediate service consequences, introduce a limited Fault-reader view without skipping the later rule-mending milestone, and leave the reader with a real unlisted service route rather than a solved civic mystery.

Starting condition: Jonas is a probationary municipal network technician in River Stacks. He has no reliable supernatural sight, no personal authority, a pending permanent civil-service appointment, and no formal CivicCore offer yet. Evan Mercer has been missing since the Quiet Cut. Sanaa Iqbal is an emergency physician who needs a reliable repair, not another technical promise. The twelve-year Reclamation Season is three months away.

Starting relationships: Jonas and Tomas Renn are former mentor and apprentice with a dispute over undocumented repairs. Jonas and Sanaa are professionally wary of one another. Jonas has no relationship with Pia Santos or the unlisted residents. Nadia Mercer is anxious about reopening the subject of Evan.

Batch midpoint (local reversal, not the volume midpoint): The apparent unauthorized opening is produced by an obsolete crossing clause and a live, unlisted service route, not by a rogue operator. The people behind the route are real, but the batch does not yet explain how the neighborhood was erased or who maintains its permissions.

Batch climax: During a scheduled attended-service test, Jonas and the existing crews keep one bridge lane, the clinic route, and the shelter handoff open for a stated period without pretending to have repaired the underlying terms. The local service test ends safely, the unresolved seam is entered into the record, and a public Old Ward notice shows one empty central root field. Tomas blocks Jonas from touching a shared node again until he signs a visible responsibility bond.

Required character changes: Jonas moves from “fix it and move on” to recording what a repair cannot settle and who must answer for it. Sanaa remains wary but trusts his disclosure of the night-transfer cost. Tomas refuses an undocumented shared-node repair and puts the formal responsibility bond on the next agenda. Pia first speaks for an unlisted passenger without becoming Jonas’s apprentice or Lowglass’s spokesperson. Jonas learns that Evan’s absence is tied to incomplete records, without receiving the counter-amendment or its second signature.

Required power changes: Chapters 1–2 remain Stage 0: ordinary tools, schematics, attended work orders, and temporary routes only. The first major bridge repair in Chapter 3 activates a limited Fault-reader view of the Margin, with sensory bleed and a physical anchor required. Chapters 4–10 can diagnose a bounded conflict but cannot mend a civic term, create a bearer, transfer a duty, or leave Jonas with an active supernatural claim or seam mark. Stage 2 rule mending begins only in Chapters 11–18 after Tomas’s responsibility bond.

Required world reveals: A permission needs a holder, scope, witness, term, claim, and precedence; a valid permission can still cause harm when its terms are incomplete. The Lattice notices are incomplete maintenance messages, not an all-knowing voice. A route can be present in the Lattice while missing from human maps, but the batch does not yet identify its full civic history. An old I. Vey seal marks the bridge hardware. A public Old Ward maintenance notice shows an empty central root field; this is a field, not a hidden person or a request to find one.

Open questions after the batch: Why is a live service route absent from the city’s maps? What does the obsolete crossing clause protect, and who maintains the unlisted route? What does the empty central root field mean during an uneven renewal? Why does Jonas’s ordinary maintenance credential reach a fault without making him its holder? The direct CivicCore offer, the full Lowglass history, Evan’s counter-amendment and second signature, the nine-witness coalition, the public “last working system administrator” title, and the blank-bearer request remain reserved for later Volume 1 sections.

## Claim and service ledger

- No supernatural claim or seam mark exists at the start of the batch.
- Any temporary bridge, clinic, or shelter route made in Chapters 1–9 has a named custodian, a stated end or renewal point, and an on-page closure or handover. It is not Jonas’s personal bearer status.
- Chapter 10 ends with the underlying terms still unresolved, the attended test safely closed, and no Stage 2 claim carried forward. The next civic repair must wait for Tomas’s visible bond and a named witness.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0001 — The Door That Owes Nothing

POV: Jonas Mercer.

Location/time: River Stacks access cabinet before dawn, moving into Saint Orra bridge control room.

Immediate goal: Replace a worn relay and clear the probationary work order before the morning rush.

Resistance: The bridge begins rejecting an ambulance because an old “empty crossing” permission conflicts with a service claim. A conventional bypass could clear the hardware but make the conflict invisible.

Information revealed: A physical access plate carries a faint silver seam that Jonas has dismissed as residue. The bridge is not mechanically broken; the permission attached to the crossing is. The plate also bears an old Continuity Project seal with the initials I. Vey, which Jonas treats as a manufacturer mark.

Action or decision: Jonas documents the fault, then uses an attended maintenance bypass: a dispatcher, crossing guard, and technician remain on the bridge while he resets the route for the current service window. The bypass follows the bridge’s physical service chain and expires when the attended window ends. It does not create a permission, name Jonas bearer, or open a distant door by itself.

Emotional change: Routine pride becomes unease when the bridge accepts his maintenance credential but refuses to explain the conflict, and a downstream Saint Orra service plate requests help at the same local chain.

Power or resource change: Jonas remains Stage 0. No supernatural ability, seam mark, or personal claim is confirmed. He loses the clean, low-risk work order and has to file an incident report that the schematics cannot explain.

Continuity fact to preserve: Jonas is a probationary NDC technician with ordinary tools, limited clearance, and no root access. Evan’s missing-file reference remains private.

Ending type: discovery.

Next-chapter pull: The Saint Orra west clinic gate at the end of the same service corridor has gone quiet, and its night watch needs help before the patient transfer arrives.

### Chapter 0002 — A Borrowed Key

POV: Jonas Mercer.

Location/time: Saint Orra west clinic and service corridor, late morning.

Immediate goal: Find the clinic’s night watch and complete the patient transfer without allowing an old access route to become an unauthorized shortcut.

Resistance: Sanaa Iqbal needs the corridor open for a patient whose care claim is valid. The clinic plate rejects Jonas’s credential as a sole authority because the night-watch duty is missing from the local record. The night-watch worker understands the duty and is present, but the existing service route has no current witness entry.

Information revealed: A permission can be true for one holder and false for another. The clinic’s night-watch duty was removed from the local record even though people are still performing it. A valid patient claim does not automatically repair the route that carries it.

Action or decision: Jonas checks the physical chain and uses the existing emergency maintenance procedure with the night-watch worker and Sanaa present. The worker keeps the named duty; Jonas does not name himself bearer or create a new claim. The patient is admitted for observation, given a ward bed and a named next shift, and the temporary route is closed when the escort ends.

Emotional change: Jonas is relieved that saving a person in time did not make him responsible for the whole corridor. Sanaa refuses his first attempt to call the limit harmless and makes him write down what remains unverified.

Power or resource change: Jonas remains Stage 0 and performs no civic edit. A short Lattice notice identifies a missing term but gives no repair instruction, no bearer, and no personal claim. The route has an expiry and a responsible watch; the incident becomes a documented fault rather than a supernatural burden.

Continuity fact to preserve: Sanaa is a physician, not a magical authority. She records the transfer’s exact limits and keeps the patient’s disposition independent of Jonas’s clearance.

Ending type: cost.

Next-chapter pull: The bridge incident report points to an obsolete route that the official map says is closed, while a registrar orders Jonas to stop using the clinic gate.

### Chapter 0003 — The Clause in the Wall

POV: Jonas Mercer.

Location/time: Saint Orra bridge maintenance bay, afternoon into evening.

Immediate goal: Restore the bridge’s ambulance lane and document the conflict before the next inspection.

Resistance: The physical reset can hold only during an attended service window. Deputy Registrar Oren Pell invokes an emergency rule and says the bridge must be closed because its permission record is incomplete. The crew cannot stay on the hinge indefinitely.

Information revealed: The bridge’s “empty crossing” term was written for a different traffic pattern and now overrides the ambulance claim. After the first major repair, Jonas sees the Margin as a thin physical connection between the relay fault and the conflicting term. It shows relationships, not intent or a solution.

Action or decision: Jonas asks the crossing guard, dispatcher, and maintenance crew to witness an attended test, not a civic rewrite. They keep the ambulance lane open for a stated ninety-minute window, log every use, and close the lane at the end of the window so the next inspection can decide its future. The repair is safe for the current service need and plainly incomplete.

Emotional change: Jonas’s pride in solving the hardware gives way to the first uncomfortable recognition that a witness can be part of safe operation rather than an obstacle to it.

Power or resource change: Stage 1 Fault-reader view begins after the repair. Jonas must anchor his attention to the access plate and a trusted person; crowds make the competing claims difficult to separate. He cannot alter the term, create a seam mark, or carry a personal claim.

Continuity fact to preserve: Oren Pell enforces a valid but harmful emergency rule. He is not secretly the source of the fault.

Ending type: reversal.

Next-chapter pull: As the attended window is closing, an unlisted shuttle arrives with a child, a courier, and a clinic patient whose care route has no matching transit record.

### Chapter 0004 — The Unlisted Stop

POV: Jonas Mercer.

Location/time: Saint Orra transit platform and an unmarked River Stacks route, evening.

Immediate goal: Keep the unlisted vehicle from becoming a public safety incident and get every passenger to a safe, known destination.

Resistance: The route exists in the Lattice but not in the transit authority’s maps. The driver will not explain the route because doing so could expose the passengers, while the station dispatcher wants the vehicle impounded. The clinic patient is too unstable to wait through an argument.

Information revealed: A route can be physically present and operationally real while the human map treats it as empty. The vehicle carries a child, Pia Santos, and an older patient with a valid clinic appointment; the route’s local custodians are not rogue operators, but their practice and history remain unexplained.

Action or decision: Jonas lets Pia speak for the passengers instead of questioning them as a threat. Under the incident’s attended escort procedure, he keeps the platform clear long enough for the patient to be admitted, the child to be handed to a waiting relative, and the courier to record the route without being detained. The vehicle is released to its driver with a written safety check, not entered into the city map.

Emotional change: Jonas’s assumption that an unregistered route is a rogue exception gives way to the less convenient recognition that a person can be real without a record.

Power or resource change: The Fault-reader view shows a local chain with several holders and no single obvious missing term. It is useful only near a witness and a physical route; Jonas does not repair, transfer, or interpret the custodians’ permission.

Continuity fact to preserve: The passengers have completed dispositions. The route is a seed, not the full Lowglass reveal; do not explain the Quiet Cut, civic erasure, or the neighborhood’s entire witness network yet.

Ending type: discovery.

Next-chapter pull: The patient’s return-of-care route ends at an old-canal shelter, where staff will not let Jonas open the door without naming what the shelter owes the people inside.

### Chapter 0005 — The Price of a Door

POV: Jonas Mercer.

Location/time: Old-canal shelter, night.

Immediate goal: Open a short return-of-care route for the patient after the clinic confirms the patient is stable, without exposing the shelter’s residents to a city inspection.

Resistance: The shelter’s service record has no current return-of-care term. Jonas can make the door open by promising to take the missing duty himself, but he has no authority to create that claim and the residents will not let him speak for them.

Information revealed: A permission can be true for a working local practice even when the municipal index omits its custodian. A temporary route is not a loophole: someone must say who will escort the patient, when the route ends, and what happens if the promise fails.

Action or decision: Jonas refuses the convenient promise. The shelter’s existing watch worker, the clinic clerk, and the patient’s relative state the short escort duty together. Under the existing service procedure, they open the physical route, bring the patient back to the shelter, record a follow-up appointment, and close the route when the handoff is complete. Jonas remains an attended technician, not the bearer.

Emotional change: Jonas feels the urge to take responsibility for strangers’ safety and recognizes that accepting a claim can become another way of taking control. He chooses to listen instead.

Power or resource change: No Stage 2 mending is available. The Margin shows where the record is missing, but Jonas cannot repair it; he can only document the bounded route and its responsible custodians. The patient and shelter residents are safe for the night, while the underlying omission remains active.

Continuity fact to preserve: The shelter is an unlisted service address, not yet a full explanation of Lowglass. The patient’s return and follow-up are completed on-page; do not leave a clinic patient or transfer as an off-page promise.

Ending type: cost.

Next-chapter pull: Oren Pell arrives with an order that the shelter be closed, and the residents ask Jonas whether a valid closure can be challenged without exposing everyone inside.

### Chapter 0006 — The Registrar’s Rule

POV: Jonas Mercer.

Location/time: Old-canal shelter, dawn.

Immediate goal: Respond to the closure and get the patient and shelter residents to a safer, recognized location without pretending Jonas can overrule a valid safety order.

Resistance: Oren Pell’s order is valid under the public-safety clause. The building is old, the water line is unstable, and Jonas cannot dismiss the danger merely because the rule harms the people inside. The residents have one night’s worth of belongings and no recognized address to claim.

Information revealed: A valid permission can be unjust. Oren is protecting a building and his own liability, not acting as a hidden magical villain. The closure’s authority comes from the Assembly’s emergency terms, not from Oren’s personal invention.

Action or decision: Jonas refuses the tempting offer to take over the building-safety inspection without qualifications or authority. He helps the residents choose the safer public shelter in Bellow Market, records an itemized handoff with the shelter steward, and escorts the patient and medication bag there. Oren closes the old building after the last resident leaves; the residents keep their own keys and a written record of what was removed.

Emotional change: Jonas stops seeing the registrar as an obstacle to be defeated and accepts that his own repair has made a safe challenge harder, not easier.

Power or resource change: Jonas can read the local precedence in the Margin, but he cannot override Oren, change the closure, or take its inspection duty. No seam mark or personal claim appears. Tomas arrives and refuses to let Jonas turn the relocation into an undocumented personal guarantee.

Continuity fact to preserve: Oren remains a pressured local official. His order is the first concrete example of the story’s central distinction between validity and justice. The relocation is complete before the next chapter.

Ending type: decision.

Next-chapter pull: A public inspection will be issued at noon for the old water line; reopening depends on evidence, not on Jonas’s promise.

### Chapter 0007 — No One Signed the Map

POV: Jonas Mercer.

Location/time: Old-canal pump house and culvert, late morning.

Immediate goal: Attend the noon inspection, document the water condition honestly, and protect residents from a count disguised as a safety check.

Resistance: The pump has a valid right to draw water but no current term for shared maintenance. The residents can show how the line is used, but they will not surrender a roster or let an NDC technician quietly turn a local practice into a city record. The water line is unstable in more than one place.

Information revealed: The Lattice can retain a service claim after the human index loses the custodian’s name, but it cannot explain why the omission happened. Jonas sees a missing term and a live local duty; he cannot make the missing term true by writing it down.

Action or decision: Jonas performs an ordinary pressure and physical-line check with the pump operator and a tenant representative, then steps back while the residents show the inspector their existing maintenance practice. The inspection closes the pump and denies reopening because the line is unsafe. The residents accept bottled water and a clinic-supplied tank for the next shift. No civic term is mended, and no headcount enters the report.

Emotional change: Jonas wants an old record that might clear Evan’s name, but the residents’ immediate safety claim outranks his private need for proof. He chooses to report the failure plainly.

Power or resource change: Stage 1 Fault-reader view lets Jonas separate the physical leak from the missing term, with the pump plate as his anchor. Sensory bleed leaves him nauseated and unable to keep reading in the crowd. He gains no seam mark, personal claim, or authority over the pump.

Continuity fact to preserve: The water inspection has a completed result: unsafe line, reopening denied, temporary water and clinic access arranged. Do not turn this into a Lowglass recognition or an Evan-signature discovery.

Ending type: discovery.

Next-chapter pull: NDC management orders Jonas to close the incident as a hardware failure before the afternoon records deadline, but the bridge log still shows a permission conflict.

### Chapter 0009 — The First Night Shift

POV: Jonas Mercer.

Location/time: Saint Orra west clinic and the neighboring shelter route, night.

Immediate goal: Keep an urgent patient transfer and the clinic’s existing night access open through a pressure fault, while making no claim that Jonas can reconcile separate services.

Resistance: The clinic and shelter use incompatible definitions of who counts as a witness. The night watch is real work, but its record is incomplete. Jonas can diagnose the physical hinge and the local fault; he cannot issue a one-person override or move a duty between bearers.

Information revealed: A night watch is not just labor. It is the living proof that a promise to protect people remains active. Removing the watch from the record can make a working permission hollow even while the people continue to show up.

Action or decision: Jonas declines to write a cross-node shared term. He resets the clinic’s local hardware under the existing work order, then helps the crossing guard and shelter worker make a manual, witnessed handover using the bearers already responsible for each route. The handover holds for the shift, but a nonurgent transfer is delayed while the urgent patient crosses. Once the pressure fault clears, the delayed transfer is completed and its waiting time is recorded.

Emotional change: Jonas tells Sanaa the exact cost of the delay instead of presenting the night as a clean success. Sanaa remains professionally independent, but she stops treating his silence as agreement.

Power or resource change: Stage 1 lets Jonas read the local Margin, not alter it. The symptoms that worsen after the transfer are sensory bleed and exhaustion from reading near an active hinge; the Lattice is not reading his intention. He gains no seam mark, personal claim, or transfer authority.

Continuity fact to preserve: Existing bearers complete the handover. Jonas cannot reconcile permissions across nodes, prevent every harm, or turn a technically safe night into a moral success.

Ending type: cost.

Next-chapter pull: The Old Ward records office schedules an attended-service test for the next morning; Tomas orders Jonas to bring every witness log and explains that no shared-node civic edit will be authorized without a visible responsibility bond.

Immediate goal: Complete the scheduled attended-service test, keep the bridge and clinic routes usable for their stated period, and file an honest report that names the unresolved conflict.

The crew has enough existing authority to isolate the side route and keep one lane open for a stated period, not enough to promise a permanent citywide solution. Management still wants the incident recorded as a hardware-only failure.

It is a missing holder, not a hidden person, and the notice does not ask Jonas to find anyone. The I. Vey seal is still the only old name attached to the route; no root clause, counter-amendment, or succession claim is available to him.

Action or decision: Jonas anchors himself to the bridge plate and directs the diagnosis while the crossing guard, dispatcher, clinic clerk, and shelter worker operate their existing duties. Jonas records the delay, the witness names, the expiry, and the unresolved seam. The Old Ward board posts the empty field as a maintenance fact, not a solution.

Power or resource change: Jonas ends the batch as Stage 1, not a rule mender. No supernatural seam mark or personal claim remains active after the routes close.

The public “last working system administrator” title and the “find the blank bearer” notice are reserved for Chapters 45–48.

Next-chapter pull: Tomas refuses to authorize another shared-node attempt until Jonas signs a visible responsibility bond.
