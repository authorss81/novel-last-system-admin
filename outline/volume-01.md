# Volume 01 Outline: The Permission Fault

## Central pressure

Nacre’s first large permission failure begins at Saint Orra’s pedestrian bridge and spreads through clinics, shelters, transit, and flood gates. The public needs repair now, but the city’s largest infrastructure contractor, CivicCore, is offering a blanket continuity contract in exchange for permanent emergency authority. Jonas Mercer must choose between a quick central fix that will make him useful and a slower local repair that may leave some doors unopened.

## Starting state

- **Jonas:** 32, probationary NDC field technician, competent with physical hinges but not yet able to see or change the Lattice’s civic terms. He wants permanent civil-service status and an answer about his missing father.
- **Power:** ordinary tools, municipal schematics, temporary bypasses, and limited institutional clearance.
- **Relationships:** Tomas Renn is a protective but distrustful shop steward; Sanaa Iqbal treats him as a technician who ignores consequences; Pia Santos is a Lowglass courier Jonas has not yet met; Nadia Mercer is his mother and the only family member who has not entirely retreated from the subject of Evan.
- **World state:** the twelve-year Reclamation Season is three months away. Old permissions are expiring unevenly. The city publicly believes the Lattice is ordinary infrastructure; the central root is hidden in the Old Ward.
- **Immediate material goal:** repair the Saint Orra bridge and the west clinic without causing a public outage.
- **Central mystery promise:** the bridge fault is not a failed machine. It is a conflict between old terms that point toward Lowglass, a neighborhood missing from the official map.

## Starting relationships

- Jonas and Tomas: mentor/student history with a conflict over taking personal responsibility instead of asking for a proper work order.
- Jonas and Sanaa: professional friction; she needs a clinic gate opened for a patient, while he needs a valid witness and a bounded claim.
- Jonas and Pia: no relationship yet; she will become the first person to explain Lowglass’s witness practice.
- Jonas and Evan: absence framed as abandonment. Jonas’s private goal is to find proof that Evan’s disappearance was not cowardice.
- Jonas and the city: the city sees him as replaceable labor. His first repair will make him visible in a way he does not control.

## Starting power level

Jonas can work with ordinary fiber, relays, access plates, and bridge controls. He can read the city’s conventional signs and follow a work order. He cannot see a missing permission, distinguish a consent term from a clerical error, or repair a rule without physical tools and a human witness. The first volume moves him from field technician to rule mender, not to a higher rank or an administrator.

## Major locations

- **River Stacks:** Jonas’s home, repair shops, rental rooms, and the first failed access plate.
- **Saint Orra:** hospital, pedestrian bridge, clinic gate, medical staff, and the first consequential civic repair.
- **Bellow Market:** workshops and informal repair economy where Local witnesses first help Jonas without trusting the NDC.
- **Old Ward:** the Assembly, administrative archive, buried root chamber, and the source of the blank map line.
- **Lowglass:** old canal streets, warehouses, worker housing, and the missing district’s informal witness network.
- **Gantry Ward:** CivicCore staging yard and the first evidence that the contractor is buying priority rather than merely maintaining nodes.

## Major factions

- **NDC field crews:** Jonas, Tomas, other technicians, dispatchers, and a management layer that wants quiet repairs.
- **Saint Orra care staff:** nurses, clinic clerks, crossing guards, and patients whose routines show what a permission actually protects.
- **CivicCore Consortium:** a private maintenance company offering a continuity contract and demanding blanket emergency access.
- **Civic Assembly:** officials deciding whether to reopen the bridge, outsource the network, or investigate the old root.
- **Lowglass witnesses:** residents and workers who have kept a parallel service network alive for eighteen years.

## Escalation sequence

### Chapters 1–10 — The first fault

Jonas finds that the bridge’s “empty crossing” permission contradicts an ambulance service claim. He uses an attended hardware bypass and follows the same physical service chain to the west clinic, where an existing night watch completes a patient transfer. After the first major repair, he sees the Margin but cannot edit the civic term. The clinic route and a later shelter handoff expose a live service address absent from human maps, without yet explaining Lowglass’s civic erasure or the neighborhood’s full witness practice. Oren Pell invokes a valid safety closure, the water inspection fails, and Jonas files an unresolved conflict instead of accepting a hardware-only account. An old I. Vey Continuity Project seal appears on the bridge hardware, and a public Old Ward maintenance notice shows one empty central root field. No direct CivicCore offer, full Evan record, public administrator title, or blank-bearer request occurs in this section.

### Chapters 11–18 — The cost of a quick fix

The repaired gate works, but the service schedule now follows Jonas’s personal availability. A patient is denied a transfer because the claim has no available bearer. Jonas learns that a fix can be technically correct and socially harmful. Before he is allowed to make a civic edit at a shared node, Tomas makes him sign a visible responsibility bond, and Sanaa refuses to certify the repair until the missing term is disclosed.

### Chapters 19–27 — Lowglass by another name

Pia leads Jonas through routes that exist in the Lattice but not on official maps. They meet residents who keep shelters, childcare, and water claims alive through informal watches. Jonas repairs a flood barrier with them and learns that Lowglass was erased during the Quiet Cut. A fragment of Evan’s handwriting appears beneath a hinge seal.

### Chapters 28–36 — The contractor’s bargain

CivicCore offers Jonas permanent employment and access to root-level notices if he signs a blanket continuity waiver. Rook’s engineers demonstrate that private maintenance can keep more hinges running than the underfunded NDC. Jonas refuses the waiver, but the Assembly schedules a vote while Oren closes a Lowglass building under an emergency rule. A second district fails because its hospital and shelter terms are incompatible.

### Chapters 37–44 — Nine witnesses

Jonas stops trying to repair the city from the Old Ward. He recruits one willing witness from each affected service: a crossing guard, a clinic clerk, a market cook, a pump operator, a mechanic, a teacher, a Lowglass resident, a transit dispatcher, and Sanaa. Each witness must understand the scope and the claim. The team builds a temporary bridge and shelter permission by recording a witnessed handover among the existing bearers, without granting one person permanent control.

### Chapters 45–48 — The map with a blank center

CivicCore attempts to seize the central node during the surge, claiming the public has granted it emergency authority. Jonas and his witnesses preserve the local terms, but the Lattice redraws itself as the storm passes. The bridge and clinic are safe; Lowglass is visible for one night; the Assembly delays the contract; Jonas is named the last working system administrator. The map leaves a blank center and a short notice: **Permission: find the blank bearer.**

## Midpoint reversal

The apparent attack on the clinic is not a rogue operator. Lowglass residents are using an old watch term to bring essential services into a neighborhood that the city’s human administration insists is empty. Their method is dangerous and nonstandard, but the residents are not simply exploiting the Lattice; they are trying to keep people alive without a recognized civic identity. The mystery changes from “who is hacking the system?” to “why did the city make its own residents invisible?”

## Volume climax

A river surge combines with the bridge, clinic, shelter, and flood-gate faults. The city’s easiest solution is to grant CivicCore a single emergency command. Instead, the nine independent witnesses record a bounded handover among the existing bearers, and Jonas mends the bridge hinge. The local terms open the bridge for an ambulance, keep the clinic gate open, redirect a pump, and preserve a shelter route without making Jonas the permanent holder. The repair is imperfect: one neighborhood loses power, a Lowglass block must evacuate, and several residents bear visible claims.

The climax is a practical victory, not a victory over all corruption. CivicCore’s authority is suspended only until the next vote, and the Assembly’s refusal to recognize Lowglass remains unresolved.

## Concrete resolution

- The Saint Orra bridge and west clinic receive valid, renewable local permissions.
- Jonas receives permanent civil-service status but loses his old clearance to work without a public responsibility bond.
- Tomas establishes a worker-led inspection requirement for major repairs.
- Sanaa’s clinic adopts a consent and appeal process that cannot be overridden invisibly.
- CivicCore is temporarily barred from taking the central node, not dissolved.
- Lowglass residents receive a provisional place on the emergency map and a right to state claims, but not full civic recognition.
- Jonas can now see a Margin and mend one bounded local term. He can help witnesses name a shared claim and record the existing bearers, but he cannot read the root or issue a citywide command.

## New question for Volume 02

The first repair exposed a citywide market for access: CivicCore can sell priority to whoever can afford a reliable claim. Who owns the records that decide which claims are remembered, and why are people outside the official map still valid witnesses?

## Final chapter image

Jonas leaves the Old Ward carrying a tool roll that is too heavy for one person. Behind him, a city map printed from the repaired Lattice shows nine ordinary service districts and one unmarked center. The bridge is open, the clinic is quiet, and a Lowglass child’s name appears in a hand-written margin. Jonas’s name appears nowhere on the permission that kept the door open.
