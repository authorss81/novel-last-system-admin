## Central pressure

A heat wave and a chemical spill make a citywide emergency out of a borough that has spent nine months discovering it cannot name the word *holder*. Into that emergency a central Continuity Order is proposed, with a ninety-day renewal, and it is proposed by **Iven Sore**, who has a constituency and a reason and is right about the need.

The order's pressure is not that it is evil. It is that **it is the only thing anybody has ever written down that would work**, and it works by putting a person in a field.

**The volume's question, stated by the mechanism and not by a villain: what does it cost to be the only person in the room who can make a district function, and who pays for it?**

**THE CALENDAR QUESTION, ANSWERED ON 26 SEPTEMBER 2026, AND IT IS BINDING ON CHAPTERS 185-192. THE OPEN PLANNING QUESTION THAT STOOD AT THE END OF THIS SECTION IS NOW CLOSED AND THE SECOND OF THE TWO AVAILABLE RESOLUTIONS HAS BEEN TAKEN.**

**THE QUESTION WAS THIS: the order's making date and its expiry are the only two dates in this volume that have to be fixed, and they cannot both be what `outline/series.md` implied. A ninety-day order made after 31 July 2015 cannot expire inside a volume that ends in September. Either the last eight chapters cross the autumn and close on the expiry, which costs pacing, or the order is made in the second heat and the ninety days is a *renewal* clause whose first expiry falls in the next volume, which moves the resolution out of Volume 04 and amends `outline/series.md`.**

**THE ANSWER IS THE SECOND ONE, FOR THREE REASONS, AND THE REASONS ARE THE ARGUMENT.**

1. **The autumn option spends the whole tail of the volume on waiting.** Eight chapters stretched from August to November is a volume that ends by standing still, and this account's method has never once been improved by standing still, and the whole argument of Volumes 02 to 04 is that a thing which happens slowly is a thing which does not happen. A climax that arrives in November because a clause expires in November is a calendar, not a decision.
2. **The renewal clause is the better instrument for this book, and it is worse for the city, which is the volume's signature.** A ninety-day order that *expires* is a thing with an end. A ninety-day order that must be *renewed* is a thing with a **blank at the end of it**, and this borough has spent four volumes learning what a blank does: the blank line under the nineteenth entry of the register, the empty signature block on the page of 29 May, the column headed *date seen again* with three words under it and nothing in it, the row from eleven o'clock to half past one that does not say *vacant*. **A renewal clause is a blank with a date on it. Nobody has to do anything to it and nothing changes until somebody does, and that is the volume's argument arriving inside the mechanism.**
3. **It is the only option that does not require a chapter to be improved.** The expiry line in `outline/series.md` said the order expires on schedule and that Jonas accepts public responsibility for the temporary override. Under the first option that acceptance lands in November and the volume is nine months of a man holding a thing. Under this one the acceptance lands in **August, in daylight, without a form, in the same fortnight as the thing he suspended**, and there is nowhere to put it and he is not going to find somewhere.
