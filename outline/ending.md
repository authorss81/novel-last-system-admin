# Ending Outline: *The Last Permission*

This is the author-facing destination for the final volume. It should be planned, not improvised, while the first volume remains grounded in repair work and local consequences.

## Final volume premise

During the Reclamation Season, every old root term in Nacre expires in a different place. Flood defenses, hospital gates, school access, transit permissions, water claims, shelter wards, and the old proxy all fail or demand renewal. The city is not invaded by a new force. Its existing system is forced to choose what it will do when no office can be trusted with every permission.

Iona Vey can restore a permanent root charter. Her preserved identity still carries the authority that held the city together during the Quiet Cut. She can probably stop the immediate collapse, but the price is that every person’s access, duty, and refusal become extensions of one living administrator. She is not lying when she says that the alternative may kill people.

Jonas Mercer can prevent the proxy from taking hold. He cannot guarantee that the city will remain orderly, nor can he guarantee that every service will reopen at the same minute. The final conflict is therefore not a battle between safety and freedom in the abstract. It is a contest over who has the right to make an unavoidable promise, and what happens when the people inside that promise can still say no.

## Starting state of Volume 15

- Nacre has completed the political rejection of permanent central authority, but the old root clause remains active.
- The nine Lattice service districts, including Lowglass, have functioning steward groups with different local terms and uneven resources. The Old Ward is the root chamber, not a separate steward seat.
- Jonas has charter-editor access, but his body carries accumulated seam marks and he can no longer work safely through the full city alarm field.
- Sanaa leads a distributed public-health ring and has explicitly refused to make medical decisions on Jonas’s behalf.
- Tomas Renn coordinates continuity workers; Pia Santos represents the Lowglass Witness Network; Nell Ardent maintains the public charter record.
- Iona Vey is physically alive in the Old Ward’s root chamber, partially conscious and no longer able to issue local orders without a proxy act. The earlier containment limited her physical and local command but did not erase the dormant root precedence or her one final preserved witness claim.
- Evan Mercer’s counter-amendment and its second signature are known but incomplete. The second signature could give Jonas permanent counter-witness authority, which would repeat the structure of the proxy.
- The city has two clocks: a nine-month political window to replace the old root, and a seventy-two-hour physical cascade window that begins if the first linked hinge fails. The political deadline can be met in advance; the physical deadline cannot be postponed without making the city gamble on central command.

## Final external conflict

### The continuity collapse

The old root terms are not a single switch. Each hinge rejects a different promise, and the failures cascade through the services that depend on them. The city can respond in three ways:

1. **Permanent central charter:** Iona assumes one bearer for all permissions. Immediate services stabilize, but local councils lose renewal authority and refusal becomes technically unenforceable.
2. **Fragmented local control:** the old root is abandoned in stages. Some districts continue, but uncoordinated failures can kill people before the slower civic process catches up.
3. **Civic Commons transition:** a narrow enabling clause links local stewards, renewals, and public witnesses. It is slower, more difficult to enforce, and can be refused or corrected by the people it governs.

The final plot must make the first option attractive through real successes and the third option desirable through real work, not through a villain explaining that it is evil.

## Final internal choice

Jonas can use the second signature on Evan’s counter-amendment to become the city’s permanent counter-witness. That would let him stop the cascade, coordinate every hinge, and become the opposite of Iona: a kind, accountable administrator who says he will leave eventually but cannot be removed. The offer works because he has spent his life wanting to repair the system his father damaged.

He refuses permanent counter-witness authority. The decision costs him:

- direct root editing for the rest of his life;
- the ability to read the complete Evan record and hear any final message;
- months of physical recovery from concentrating too many claims;
- the chance to guarantee that the transition succeeds on the first attempt;
- his public reputation as the person Nacre can call in any emergency.

He does not choose helplessness. He chooses a smaller, renewable power that requires other people to remain capable and willing.

## The Civic Commons charter

The final rule is not a spell that controls minds and not a new central computer. It is a bounded civic permission with the following terms:

1. **Nine service-district steward seats:** one rotating seat for each Lattice service district, including Lowglass. The Old Ward is the root chamber and has no separate district seat. A seat carries a duty, not permanent authority, and can be removed by a public process if the steward fails the claim.
2. **Service-level witnesses:** every essential service requires at least two independent witnesses from different institutions or neighborhoods. A witness must understand the scope, expiry, and claim.
3. **Public renewal:** citywide precedence can last no more than seventy-two hours in an emergency. Renewal requires a public notice, a recorded reason, and a vote or assembly of at least six of the nine service-district stewards. The six authorize only a non-coercive common link for the consenting districts; dissenting districts keep their local terms and a public right of refusal, so the vote cannot be used to bind them.
4. **Local refusal:** a district may refuse a citywide rule if doing so does not knowingly transfer an immediate lethal danger to another district. Refusal is reviewed through a public claim process rather than erased by a central override.
5. **Explicit claim:** every override names who will perform the work, who may refuse, who receives the result, and what repair is owed afterward.
6. **No inheritable office:** no bloodline, family, individual, or company can hold the root position permanently. Access is split into steward keys and expires.
7. **Public ledger:** the terms, witnesses, claims, dissent, and renewals are visible to anyone affected by them. The ledger is a civic record, not a private control panel.
8. **Repair limit:** Jonas’s final clause can open the transition, but it cannot dictate the permanent rules of each district. The people who live under the system must write those terms.

The final transition is won when the city can enforce essential services without giving any one identity the right to rewrite every other life.

## Final climax sequence

### 1. The offer and the first successes

Iona invokes one final preserved root claim. The invocation restores the bridge, hospital, and water permissions, and because the proxy amendment carries the highest precedence introduced during the Quiet Cut, it also begins appending a permanent bearer. People survive because she is genuinely competent. The city sees what a permanent bearer can do. Jonas’s allies cannot dismiss the offer without admitting that it may be the safest immediate choice.

### 2. The distributed failure

The Civic Commons is tested district by district. Saint Orra succeeds because its witnesses are organized; Tideglass fails because its newest residents are not represented; Morrow Fields refuses a citywide water precedence that would transfer drought risk to the coast; Lowglass activates a claim the city thought it had erased. Each difficulty is a consequence of earlier choices, not a new disaster invented for the finale.

### 3. Jonas’s last public repair

Jonas writes a bounded enabling clause at the Old Ward. The rule does not solve the city. It permits each district to connect its own terms to a common emergency, subject to witnesses and renewal. The transfer causes ordinary failures: a school gate flickers, a pump changes pressure, a bus line misses a stop. The city experiences the cost of transition instead of being told that the cost will happen later.

### 4. The proxy attempt

Iona’s preserved identity does not launch a separate attack. Her one invocation reaches the highest precedence of the proxy amendment introduced during the Quiet Cut and continues the attempt to complete the clause by adding a permanent bearer. Jonas could stop it by accepting the counter-witness role. Instead, he revokes his own root access at the moment of transfer. The public stewards, not one person, must decide whether the charter becomes valid.

### 5. The public choice

Each district acts. Some choose the citywide clause; some choose a local alternative; some refuse for a stated reason. Six districts renew the non-coercive common link within the seventy-two-hour limit, enough to keep the connected services from cascading while the dissenting districts retain their own terms. The claim records every dissent.

### 6. The cost

Jonas survives, but the seam marks across his body no longer settle. He cannot hear the city’s alarms and can no longer make a direct repair in the Lattice. He can still work with ordinary tools, teach other technicians, and serve as a public steward. Iona survives as an ordinary person under oversight; she is not restored to a hidden root role.

The city’s services return unevenly. That unevenness is the proof that no single office is secretly controlling the result.

## Relationship resolutions

### Jonas and Sanaa

They do not use the transition as a romantic proof of permanence. Sanaa refuses to stay inside the Old Ward or bind herself to Jonas through a permission. She remains a physician and a public-health steward. Jonas remains a repairer and civic witness.

They choose to live together later because they want to, not because the city has certified that the choice is safe. Their relationship survives the loss of magical access, the public blame, and the knowledge that Jonas cannot guarantee the next emergency. Sanaa’s final advice is not “save the city”; it is “tell people what you cannot promise.”

### Jonas and Tomas

Tomas hands Jonas a maintenance worker’s toolkit rather than a ceremonial key. Their final disagreement is over who gets credit for the transition. Tomas insists the city remember the crews who kept local services running, and Jonas agrees.

### Jonas and Pia

Pia becomes one of the first Lowglass stewards and rejects any attempt to make her a symbolic spokesperson for all unlisted residents. She is loyal to Jonas only where their choices agree.

### Jonas and Evan

Jonas learns the full available record: Evan died during the Quiet Cut after cutting the node. He understands Evan’s choice and its terrible price, but cannot recover an apology or a private final message. He releases the desire to use Evan as proof of his own innocence.

### Iona and Jonas

They do not become friends or lovers. Iona is held to public questioning and loses root authority. Jonas recognizes that her fear of mass death was not false; he rejects her conclusion that fear justifies permanent obedience.

## World after the ending

Nacre is not instantly harmonious. The new system creates new arguments:

- district councils disagree about which citywide rules deserve renewal;
- people without formal status must navigate a more visible appeal process;
- essential services can be slower during a renewal;
- private contractors may still exist, but cannot hold a permanent public claim;
- a new generation of technicians can specialize in local terms without becoming a new ruling class;
- the Lattice’s notices are public, bounded, and easier to challenge, but still incomplete.

The city survives its founder because the founder is no longer the only person who can make a promise on behalf of the city. Authority is distributed, renewed, and revocable. The cost is coordination work, political disagreement, and the loss of effortless control.

## Mysteries resolved

- Why do people absent from the official map still receive permissions? Because Lowglass and other residents remain living witnesses even after civic erasure.
- What is the blank bearer? It is the root’s empty holder field, which represents the unclaimed collective responsibility left by every erased or refused promise; it is not a secret person.
- Why does Jonas have admin access? His maintenance credential contains Evan’s counter-amendment and its unresolved second signature, which make him a fault-temporary maintenance route rather than an heir or the root holder.
- What happened to Evan? He was killed in the Quiet Cut after cutting the lowland node; his choice saved Lowglass and cost him his life, his municipal record, and the ability to complete the counter-amendment.
- Who is Iona Vey? She is the first administrator, preserved as a root witness and the bearer of the original proxy authority.
- Are the System notices her? They are partly her preserved voice, partly the Lattice’s maintenance language, and partly a legacy interface created by the first project. The distinction matters, but the System is not an independent antagonist.

## Mysteries intentionally left open

- Why did the first charter stone respond to civic promises in Nacre rather than remaining inert?
- Do other cities have comparable Lattices, and have they chosen different forms of authority?
- What becomes of permissions that cannot be separated from a single person or place?
- Can the city’s public appeal process handle a future disaster without repeating the Quiet Cut?

These questions open future reflection without introducing a new final enemy or undercutting the resolution.

## Final image

At dawn after the first seventy-two-hour emergency, Jonas walks through River Stacks with an ordinary tool roll. A clinic door opens because its local steward and two witnesses have renewed the care permission; nobody calls Jonas to authorize it. He checks the hinge, explains the missing term to a junior technician, and steps aside when the district crew takes over the next repair. The city is not quiet, perfect, or centralized. It is asking people to keep promises to one another—and leaving them free to ask who is holding the door open.

## Ending guardrails

- Do not make Jonas’s sacrifice erase his relationships or make him a godlike civic savior.
- Do not let Iona return to a hidden root role after the transfer.
- Do not have every district agree; unanimity would make the public consent meaningless.
- Do not solve the city with a new System panel, a hidden command, or a one-person rewrite.
- Do not erase the lasting cost of the Quiet Cut. The ending changes the structure around the wound, not the wound itself.
