# Volume 02 Outline: The Price of Access

## Central pressure

Nacre has learned that it cannot name the word *holder*. What it learns next is that a word you cannot name can be supplied by whoever hands you a schedule — and that a schedule can make a price into a permission without anybody in the room deciding to do that.

**CivicCore's Schedule 4 to the Saint Orra service agreement of 2019 — continuity pricing.** It has been valid since 1 March and nobody in this city has read it until 22 April. It takes the *contractual* failure definition in paragraph 11 — a node in failure where its civic term has not been continuously in force for more than twenty-four consecutive hours — and converts it into a market:

- a node in failure is an **unfunded node**;
- an unfunded node may be placed on a **continuity schedule** by the district it stands in, on payment of a **continuity fee**, at a **tier**;
- **the order of tiers is set by what is paid.** A Tier 3 node outranks a Tier 1 node in any precedence dispute. A node that is unfunded is outranked by every node that is not;
- **a deferred node does not disconnect. It narrows** to its **last verified essential use**, and if none is recorded, to the node's maintenance;
- **a tier may be challenged by a service district or by a node in the same tier. It may not be challenged by a person.**

The volume's question, stated by the mechanism and not by a villain: **what happens to a service when payment becomes a prerequisite for urgent care?**

## Section D — Chapters 81–90: Revocation

**Climax.** Jonas, tenants, clinic staff and a pump operator revoke one dangerous blanket permission — a Tier 3 continuity claim bought by a Kestrel Hill trust and passed, through a legal chain of perfectly valid assignments, into a service that thirty-one people live inside — **without cutting water to the district.** The method is the volume's method: find the chain, find the node with a *person* in its holder field, get that person to instruct the mending, and refuse to do it himself.

The cost is that revocation must be paid for somewhere, and it is paid for at **Wren Court**, where the fee stops, and where Yolanda Fofana's third condition — *the fee does not stop* — has to be renegotiated by the people it protects, in a room, without Jonas in it.

## Section E — Chapters 91–96: The Floor

Priority access is ruled invalid in a public case. **CivicCore keeps the physical network** and is not dissolved. A **cooperative steward board** forms — nine seats, one per service district, none of them permanent, one of them held by a man who classifies streets as plant. Jonas becomes a **transfer steward** in practice, not in title, and **Sanaa trusts him with a patient-level claim and not with a city-level one.**

## Midpoint reversal (Chapters 66–70)

The apparent denial is not caused by CivicCore inventing an injustice. It is caused by residents signing hollow terms to obtain emergency service and then being unable to renew them. **The contractor exploits a real mechanism; it does not create the wrong.** And the first hollow term of the volume was not written by a contractor — it was written in nine seconds on the back of a laundry delivery note by a twenty-nine-year-old night orderly who was trying to stop a mortuary gate narrowing to its maintenance, and Jonas told the hospital to write one.

## New mechanism canon this volume establishes (binding on later volumes)

1. **Continuity tiering and its order.** The order of tiers is set by what is paid, not by what is needed. A paid claim outranks a need.
2. **Deferral, not disconnection.** A deferred node narrows to its last verified essential use. If none is recorded, the use is the node's maintenance.
3. **Last verified essential use.** A recordable fact about a node that determines who it serves when money stops. **It can be written in nine seconds by an uninformed person, it is then unchallengeable for a year, and it is the volume's central instrument.**
4. **Rule 4(c).** A participation-agreement signature is a consent mark and may not be withdrawn except on the same date next year. Consent is graded by date.
5. **Challenge restriction.** A tier may be challenged by a service district or by a node in the same tier, and **not by a person.** Nobody can appeal their own bill.
6. **The register of narrowings / Return of a deferred node.** Nina Calder's form, adopted 30 April. Published on the city's paper, not the Lattice's, because **the Lattice cannot publish a narrowing and the city can.** Eight nodes on the first return, a ninth with no holder, and a blank line at the bottom of the form that is not a design feature.
