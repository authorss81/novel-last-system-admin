# Power System: Civic Permissions

## Core principle

The Lattice does not grant power. It makes a promise enforceable inside a piece of shared infrastructure. A technician’s growth consists of learning how to diagnose broken promises, negotiate their terms, and carry the consequences without pretending that a convenient edit is a just outcome.

## The six parts of every permission

A permission is valid when all six parts are present and compatible:

1. **Holder:** the person, office, institution, or bounded place that receives the rule.
2. **Scope:** the exact action or boundary allowed or denied.
3. **Witness:** a living person, deliberately maintained group record, or community practice that can recognize the rule.
4. **Term:** the trigger, duration, renewal condition, and end point.
5. **Claim:** the real duty, care, repayment, repair, or return that follows if the promise fails.
6. **Precedence:** the public rule that tells the Lattice which permission controls a conflict.

A missing term is a **seam**. A false witness is a **hollow term**. A claim without a real counterparty becomes an **unclaimed burden**. A permission may still function while one of these faults exists, but failure will spread into neighboring services.

## How repair works

A technician needs four things:

- a physical anchor or hinge he can reach;
- a clear description of the desired service or boundary;
- at least one witness who understands the change;
- a named claim if the change is more than a routine reset.

Routine repair can be done with tools and a work order. Civic repair changes how people and institutions are related, so it cannot be completed by a tool alone.

### Ordinary repair

Examples include replacing a relay, reseating a fiber coupler, renewing a hospital access plate, resetting a floodgate’s schedule, and correcting a clerical error. Ordinary repair changes the next use of a service but not the underlying terms.

### Civic repair

Examples include assigning a night watch to a shelter, giving a clinic a temporary care claim, allowing a water pump to serve two blocks, restoring a district’s right of return, or changing which rule wins during an emergency. Civic repair changes the people and obligations inside the rule.

Jonas must perform both. The second kind cannot be solved by technical skill alone.

## What the Lattice can and cannot affect

### Can

- make an established rite, ward, machine, or boundary valid under a stated civic term;
- route access, water, power, transit, shelter, care, or safety through a permission;
- bind a role to a duty while the role exists;
- transfer a holder to an existing, willing steward;
- revoke a permission for a future use while preserving its history;
- set precedence between compatible local rules;
- return a defined benefit or create a repair duty when a claim fails.

### Cannot

- force a person to act, think, confess, or love;
- make a person consent by increasing the danger of refusal;
- create matter, energy, water, or usable power;
- grant a person a skill they have not learned or a social status with no supporting institution;
- rewrite a memory, erase the Lattice’s own historical record, or bring back a dead person;
- bypass distance, physical anchors, witnesses, or expiry;
- issue a citywide rule from one hinge without a chain of compatible local terms;
- remain valid forever without recurring acts of maintenance, witnessing, and consent.

People can still destroy, seal, or falsify the Lattice’s paper maps, indexes, and municipal reports. Those human records may disagree with the Lattice’s retained history; the Lattice cannot quietly repair the lie for them.

A coercive rule can function. That does not make its coercion invisible to the story. The Lattice enforces the stated relationship; the human consequences of designing a coercive relationship remain the story’s subject.

## Failure modes

### Seam

Two permissions overlap without a clear precedence. A seam may open a forbidden door, close a lawful route, or make an emergency rule flicker on and off. Repairing the visible symptom can worsen the underlying conflict.

### Hollow term

A witness signs or repeats a promise they do not understand or are forced to accept. The rule works, but the person or institution loses the trust required to maintain it. Hollow terms spread as denial, evasion, and people quietly disabling the Lattice by hand.

### Unclaimed burden

The holder or issuer cannot name anyone who owes or receives the claim. The burden attaches to the nearest responsible person or place. This is how a seemingly harmless repair can follow Jonas into his home, sleep, relationships, and legal record.

### Root lock

A foundational clause cannot be amended by a local technician. Root locks can be read by a sufficiently trained admin, altered only through a witnessed civic process, or temporarily suspended with severe collateral costs. Root locks are the reason a single technician cannot simply decree a new city.

### Proxy authority

A foundational clause can name a single office, bloodline, or identity as the permanent bearer of the city’s authority. The original Lattice required multiple witnesses and renewal. The Quiet Cut introduced a proxy term that makes a single living identity stand in for the whole city. A proxy is valid in the Lattice’s narrow sense and oppressive in practice.

### Continuity default

If a permission reaches the end of its term with no living steward, the Lattice narrows it to the last verified essential service and places an unclaimed burden on the nearest responsible maintenance route. This default is a bounded continuity behavior, not a mind or a new antagonist: it cannot create a new service, choose a permanent ruler, or override a valid refusal without witnesses. Its cost is predictable but severe—people may be denied access while a new steward is found. Jonas’s distributed-civic solution exists because this safe-looking default is still capable of turning an absent steward into a gatekeeper.

## Growth stages

Growth is a change in responsibility and option, not a rise in raw destructive power. Each stage requires practice, failure, relationships, and a decision about who will carry the next cost.

### Stage 0: Field technician

Jonas can repair ordinary hinges, read conventional schematics, use physical bypasses, and diagnose common civic faults. He has no reliable supernatural sight and can be overruled by a supervisor, a contractor, or a district steward.

- **Cost:** ordinary injury, blame, unpaid labor, and the knowledge that a temporary bypass can become a new policy.
- **Limit:** he can change the route of a service but cannot change the terms that route serves.

### Stage 1: Fault reader

After the first major repair, Jonas can see the Margin: the relationship between a physical fault and a missing or conflicting permission. He sees holders, terms, and claims only when the hinge is active or a living witness is close.

- **Cost:** sensory bleed. Nearby claims arrive as pressure, sound, taste, or involuntary emotional residue; crowds make it difficult to distinguish his own feelings from the network’s. He must be anchored by a trusted person and physical tool before attempting a major read.
- **Limit:** he can diagnose but cannot edit. Misreading a claim can make him blame a victim for an institutional failure.
- **Required growth:** learns to ask for another person’s account rather than treating the visible rule as the whole truth.

### Stage 2: Rule mender

Jonas can restore one missing term in a local permission, revoke a future use, or create a temporary claim with a named bearer and a real witness.

- **Cost:** he takes a visible **seam mark** on his body and a proportionate claim. The claim cannot be paid by intention or apology; it must be discharged through action, care, work, or a negotiated transfer. Until it is paid, he feels its pressure at the boundaries most associated with the rule.
- **Limit:** one hinge and one local rule at a time. He cannot repair a rule whose holder is absent, and he cannot create a permission for a service that has no existing physical or civic function.
- **Failure:** a technically correct repair can transfer harm to an uninformed group. Jonas must learn to disclose that possibility and accept a decision not to use his power.

#### Stage 2 sub-rule: the locally held term (established Chapter 25)

A stage-2 mending may be performed **at a hinge the technician does not issue**, provided a person or group with local standing instructs it, the witness understands the claim, and a named holder can be established. Three consequences are canonical and must not be quietly reversed:

1. **The issuer of the mended term is the local holder, not the technician.** The maintenance claim that follows the term belongs to the holder. The technician's own claim on the hinge, if he has one, is unaffected and does not transfer.
2. **A holder may be a role rather than a person** — for example, "the holder on the rota" — but a plate will not record a bare role. A name must accompany it, and the person named must consent to being findable. Bea Osunde's rule: a rota means *these people, today, and they can put it down*; the moment a single name is written into it, it means *the person who must not leave.*
3. **The issuer cannot renew the term.** Renewal is a fresh act by the holders, read aloud to each other at a handover. A term with a renewal clause the issuer wrote binds him; a term that expires does not wait for him to notice.

**The failure mode of this sub-rule is the Chapter 28–30 sequence:** a well-made, correctly witnessed, correctly bounded local term can fail because there is no person in the required place at the required hour, and its expiry can remove the very protection it created. A term is not a person and does not keep itself.

### Stage 3: Transfer steward

Jonas can move a permission between compatible nodes, assign a duty to a willing office or witness group, and reconcile two district terms through a shared handover.

- **Cost:** legal responsibility follows every transfer. Revoked holders know his name, and disputes come to him for explanation. He cannot move a burden silently.
- **Limit:** a receiving steward must understand and accept the duty. The Lattice will not treat a signature from an uninformed proxy as a valid handover.
- **Required growth:** learns that delegating is not the same as abandoning, and that a person who refuses a duty can still deserve protection.

### Stage 4: Precedence warden

Jonas can temporarily choose which compatible permission controls a local emergency and suspend a lower rule for a stated period. The rule must be witnessed by at least two independent local actors and a civic record.

- **Cost:** the suspended work does not disappear. It becomes deferred labor, maintenance debt, or an unclaimed burden that attaches to the issuer. During a major suspension, Jonas also loses the ability to ignore citywide alarm signals.
- **Limit:** he can choose among existing terms; he cannot invent a root clause. The duration cannot exceed the shortest real consent among the witnesses.
- **Failure:** saving a district by denying another can be politically defensible and morally monstrous. The story never treats an emergency as a blank cheque.

### Stage 5: Root reader

Jonas can read historical root clauses, identify the proxy identity, and distinguish an old rule from a later administrative interpretation. He can see the promises that founders made to one another.

- **Cost:** root evidence carries testimony and emotional residue. He temporarily experiences the limited viewpoints of historical witnesses and risks mistaking one person’s memory for the whole record. Each deep reading forces a specific personal claim: the reader must name what he is willing to lose or repair.
- **Limit:** he can read and explain a root clause but cannot amend it alone. The more complete the history he sees, the less certain he is about who is telling the truth.
- **Required growth:** learns that Evan’s choices and Iona’s intentions cannot be recovered by one privileged viewpoint.

### Stage 6: Charter editor

Jonas can compose a multi-node civic change only when the affected district stewards, witnesses, and physical anchors agree to the same bounded terms. This is the highest practical power stage in the series.

- **Cost:** the change re-evaluates every dependent permission, so ordinary services can fail during the handover. Jonas must spend months learning, delegating, and taking a share of the failures. The final cost is permanent loss of his direct root access.
- **Limit:** no unilateral edit, no permanent office, and no mind control. The editor can create a condition for public action but cannot force the public to act.
- **Final use:** Jonas uses this stage to write the narrow enabling clause of the Civic Commons, then gives up the role that made him uniquely powerful.

## Volume 01 close — the rules the volume established

These are now canon and are inherited by every later volume.

- **Three bounded civic edits were performed by Jonas Mercer, all of them at hinges he did not own and none of them issued by him**: the Saint Orra west-clinic care entrance (Ch 13, precedence; Ch 17, one fallback clause), the Bellow Market cold-store hatch (Ch 39, precedence, issuer the named holder of the collectors' self-collect term by rule eleven), and the Saint Orra bridge west-lane hinge (Ch 46, precedence, issuer the bridge dispatcher, claim the barrier keeper's, witness a crossing guard, second bearer an ambulance duty officer, **two handovers in the log in fifty minutes**).
- **A witnessed handover grants nobody an office.** That is the plate's own line and it is the volume's mechanism. Renewal remains a fresh act of the holders, spoken, in daylight. The issuer cannot renew, and the maintenance claim belongs to the holder, not the mender.
- **The register of attachment** (Ch 47) is an institutional instrument, not a Stage. It grants no power and no protection from power. It changes where a claim is *recorded*, and that is the whole of it. **It must never be written as a promotion, and Jonas must never acquire root access, precedence, staff, or a vote through it.**
- **Continuity failure, contractual** (Ch 45): a contractor's schedule may define failure for the contractor's own guarantee. It may not thereby determine a node's civic state. **This is the mechanism Volume 02 turns on, and it is a clause, not a villain.**
- **The drawing layer draws from the terms in force** (Ch 47). A district with no term in force is not drawn. A term of thirteen hours and twenty minutes put a shape inside a blank centre, **and the shape was still on the layer seventeen hours after the term expired, because the layer has no delete operation: it drew a shape out of a term that had already lapsed, annotated its own error underneath it in the smallest type it has, and nobody in the building printed a second copy. The number thirteen hours and twenty minutes is the duration of the term and never the duration of the shape.** **Canon: a shape on the drawing layer is evidence that a term once existed, not evidence of a population or a present service, and no later volume may read a name, a number or a count off a map, nor treat an outline left behind as a live district.**
- **Nobody died in Volume 01 and nobody was saved by luck.** The only serious injury is Perdita Sow, who is alive. **This register — no death, no rescue by chance, no lucky timing — is a hard rule for the series.**

## The System notices

The maintenance interface may state a fault, a missing term, a warning, or a choice. It is not a character with a plan and should not narrate a solution.

### Rules for notices

- plain language;
- at most one notice in an ordinary chapter;
- no experience totals, rarity labels, skill menus, or prediction;
- the notice can be incomplete or biased;
- Jonas must still gather evidence, persuade witnesses, and perform the repair;
- a notice never proves who is morally right.

### Example

> **Permission repair requested.**  
> **Rule:** People may enter Saint Orra’s west shelter after 22:00.  
> **Missing term:** A staffed watch must remain until the last person leaves.  
> **Choose a bearer.**  
> **Warning:** The issuer will answer for every breach.

The notice identifies the immediate fault. It does not choose the watch, solve the staffing shortage, or decide whether the shelter should be open at all.

## Power progression rules for drafting

- Every new ability must first appear as a small, bounded problem.
- Every stage must include a practice failure, a relationship cost, or a refused shortcut before the next stage.
- Larger repairs must require more witnesses, not merely more strength.
- A victory that leaves one office controlling all city permissions is a temporary victory, not a final one.
- The final system should make ordinary technicians more capable and make central authority less necessary.
