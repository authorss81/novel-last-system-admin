# Terminology and Naming Guide

Use these terms consistently. They are fictional civic and magical terms, not a software interface.

## Core terms

### Lattice

The citywide layer of enforceable civic permissions woven into Nacre’s physical infrastructure. It includes the hinges, records, witnesses, and human technicians who maintain it. The word does not mean an abstract computer simulation.

### Permission

A rule with a holder, scope, witness, term, claim, and precedence. A permission can allow or deny an action, assign a duty, or return a defined benefit. It is not a spell, a talent, or a personality.

### Margin

The thin visible connection between a physical fault and the permission that is failing around it. A fault reader sees a Margin when a hinge is active or a witness is close. A notice in the Margin is incomplete evidence, not an answer.

### Hinge

A physical node where ordinary infrastructure meets the Lattice: a cabinet, gate, pump, clinic plate, bridge relay, transit signal, or civic record case. Hinges are maintained by people and machines.

### Thread

The relationship between a holder, a service, and a claim. A thread can be intact, frayed, or cut. It is not a strand of hair and not a data line.

### Term

A stated condition, duration, trigger, and boundary of a permission. Missing terms create seams. A term can be technical, civic, or both.

### Witness

A living person or deliberately maintained group record that recognizes a permission and can be held to its terms. A signature alone is not necessarily informed consent.

### Claim

The real duty, care, repair, repayment, or return that follows a promise. Claims are discharged by action, not by good intentions.

### Seam

A fault where incompatible permissions overlap or a term is missing. Seams can spread from a hinge into streets, wards, clinics, or homes.

### Hollow term

A permission that functions through a witness who did not understand, agree to, or have power over the consequence. Hollow terms create quiet sabotage and institutional distrust.

### Unclaimed burden

A claim with no real counterparty that attaches to the nearest responsible person or place. Jonas experiences these as pressure, debt, and legal obligation.

### Blank bearer

The holder field of a root permission when no living person, office, or institution has accepted the responsibility. It is not a hidden person waiting to be found. When a blank root needs service, the Lattice assigns an unclaimed burden to the nearest valid maintenance route. Jonas can use that route temporarily, but it does not make him the root holder.

### Register of attachment

**Established Chapter 47.** A civic register kept by the registrar's office, entered under the registrar's duty, naming the person to whom unclaimed burdens and maintenance claims on a chain are attached. Its founding entry is **Mercer, J., Last Working System Administrator, City of Nacre** — civic standing, not departmental; no grade, no probation, not subject to review; findable by the city; **released by no person**. A register entry grants no root access, no precedence, no office, no staff, no vote, no key, and no right to refuse an attachment. It exists so that a claim lands on a man in a public record instead of in a file, and so that the next person who wants to attach a burden must look at the register first. **A title created by an institution for its own convenience is not a promotion, and the Lattice has no opinion about it.**

### Continuity failure (contractual)

**Established Chapter 45, from the Saint Orra service agreement of 2019, paragraph 11.** *A node is in failure where its civic term has not been continuously in force for a period exceeding twenty-four consecutive hours.* This is a **contractual maintenance guarantee**, not a civic determination. It measures whether a term is continuous; it cannot decide what a term is, and a contract between a city and a contractor does not get to define a word the city uses everywhere else in the same sentence. A term that is broken and then renewed does not cure the break. This is the instrument the priced-access market is built on.

### Registrar's record

A public record made under the registrar's duty that describes **the state of a node** at a time. It is not a minute. A minute is a list, may be read in a gallery, names persons, and can be produced in a hearing; a record describes a node and names a person only if the person is the state. Reading a condition line into a record is lawful. **It is also how a civic work with no holder becomes an unclaimed burden**, because a recorded work with no holder and no issuer is exactly what a burden attaches to.

### Service district

A shape made by the Lattice's drawing layer out of the terms in force inside an area. **It is not an administrative ward.** A service district with a term in force is drawn; a district with no term in force is not drawn at all. Nacre's map therefore shows nine ordinary service districts and, in the middle of the middle, a blank square the size of a thumbnail with no name, no number and no line in the key.

### Claims box

**Established Chapter 47.** A box on the Old Ward public board in which a claim may be stated for ninety days. **A claim so stated does not establish a service, does not require a name, and may not be used as evidence of occupancy, population, households or persons.** It is the only civic surface in Volume 01 that Lowglass was ever offered, and it is empty.

### Root clause

A foundational permission that defines the Lattice’s limits and precedence. Root clauses require multiple witnesses and recurring renewal. They cannot be changed by a local technician acting alone.

### Proxy authority

A root term that allows one identity, office, or family to stand in for the whole city. The Quiet Cut introduced the proxy that Nacre later treats as normal governance.

### Issuer

The person, office, or group that makes or repairs a permission and therefore answers for its stated terms and claim. An issuer is not automatically the current bearer. A transferred or revoked permission keeps the original issuer in the record.

### Bearer

The person, office, institution, or place currently named in a permission’s holder field. A bearer can accept a duty or service permission without becoming its issuer. The blank root field has no named bearer; Jonas’s credential is only a temporary maintenance route.

### Maintenance route

A temporary maintenance credential that can carry a bounded repair claim when a root permission’s holder field is blank. A route is not the root bearer and cannot issue permanent authority. It is the path by which an issuer can reach a fault without pretending to own the whole system.

### Counter-amendment and counter-signature

A counter-amendment is a foundational change proposed to limit or replace a root clause. Evan Mercer authored Nacre’s unfinished counter-amendment. Its **counter-signature** is the second signature on that document, belonging to a third witness who is neither Evan nor the mayor. If completed, it could create a counter-witness that opposes a proxy; if forced into permanent succession, it would reproduce the same problem.

### Steward

A person, office, or group that accepts a duty, a term, or a claim on behalf of a community. A steward has obligations and can be challenged, transferred, or revoked.

### Revocation

Ending a permission for future use while preserving the record of who held it and why. Revocation is not erasure and is not proof that the consequences disappear.

### Transfer

Moving a permission or duty from one willing holder to another through a witnessed handover. Transfer does not erase the original responsibility.

### Override

A temporary precedence change made during an emergency. An override has a stated duration, a witness, a claim, and a cost. It is not an unlimited command.

### Consent mark

A clear, informed, revocable acceptance of a permission’s scope and claim. It can be spoken, witnessed, signed, or maintained through a community practice; the Lattice cannot infer its quality.

## City and faction terms

### Nacre

The estuary city where the story takes place.

### Civic Lattice

The full citywide permission network. Usually shortened to “the Lattice.”

### NDC

The Nacre Department of Civic Continuity, the public department that maintains physical nodes and employs field technicians.

### CivicCore Consortium

The private contractor that maintains nodes and sells priority continuity. Its commercial promises are legally valid permissions and politically dangerous products.

### Civic Assembly

Nacre’s elected municipal government.

### District Witness Council

A local body that maintains the people, records, and terms behind a permission. Councils are imperfect, elected in different ways, and not automatically democratic.

### Quiet Archive

A custodial group that preserves dangerous historical records and controls access to the Old Ward’s root chamber.

### Lowglass

The real but officially erased district around the buried canal and old civic core. “Unlisted” means excluded from formal civic maps and registers, not invisible to the physical world.

### Quiet Cut

The flood-and-heat crisis eighteen years before the opening in which the proxy amendment was imposed, Lowglass was erased from human maps and municipal indexes, and Evan Mercer disappeared. It was an off-cycle emergency renewal, not a scheduled Reclamation Season.

### Reclamation Season

The twelve-year period in which root clauses must be renewed. The series begins three months before the current Reclamation Season.

### Old Ward

The island and administrative center at the heart of Nacre. It contains the Civic Assembly, the root chamber, and the oldest hinges.

## Language rules

- Use “permission” when a rule grants, denies, assigns, or returns something.
- Use “service” for an ordinary public function such as water, care, shelter, transit, or education.
- Use “holder” for the person or institution named by a permission, not as a synonym for “villain.”
- Use “witness” for someone or something that recognizes a term, not merely an observer.
- Use “claim” for the consequence of a promise, not for a legal case unless characters are in court.
- Use “hinge” for a physical node, not a metaphysical gateway.
- Use “root clause” for a foundational rule, not a computer command.
- Use “the Margin” for the interface between a fault and its terms; do not call it a screen, HUD, status bar, or game interface.
- Use “the Lattice notices” or a short **Lattice notice** when the System displays a rule. Do not use API, protocol, backend, runtime, token, latency, deployment, or similar technical terms.
- Numbers should matter because they alter access, duration, precedence, or responsibility—not because a panel reports them.
