# Character Bible

## Relationship policy

There is one slow-burn romantic relationship: Jonas Mercer and Dr. Sanaa Iqbal. It develops through professional respect, disagreement, shared consequence, and deliberate choices. There is no harem structure, no instant attraction, and no romantic resolution that erases either character’s independence. Other relationships are family, friendship, mentorship, professional rivalry, or civic alliance.

## Protagonist: Jonas Mercer

### Basic facts

- **Age:** 32 at the start of the story.
- **Occupation:** Municipal network technician, Nacre Department of Civic Continuity, grade III.
- **Home:** A small rented room above a repair shop in River Stacks; he keeps a second cot in the department locker room during emergency rotations.
- **Family:** Mother Nadia Mercer, a school crossing supervisor now working part-time at a childcare exchange; father Evan Mercer, a maintenance engineer missing since the Quiet Cut.
- **Immediate external want:** Earn permanent civil-service status, keep his clearance, repair the Lattice without causing a public disaster, and clear his father’s name.
- **Immediate internal want:** Remain the person who fixes the problem so no one else has to pay for it.
- **Deep need:** Accept that competence is not ownership, that consent cannot be optimized away, and that a healthy system must be able to function without him.
- **Central fear:** Becoming a permanent administrator who decides who counts, repeating the fate of the person he believes destroyed Lowglass.
- **Flaw:** Jonas treats a documented permission as morally neutral. He can see the rule, its effects, and its beneficiaries, but he is slow to ask whether the rule should exist. He fixes first and explains later, then mistakes the gratitude of people he saved for permission to make larger decisions for them.
- **Personal lie:** “If I can repair it correctly, I do not need to burden anyone else with the explanation.”
- **Strengths:** Patient observation, excellent hands, memory for physical routes, calm under interruption, willingness to do unglamorous work.
- **Weaknesses:** Overconfidence in technical fixes, reluctance to ask for help, poor political instinct, tendency to hide his family history, and a dangerous belief that a burden he chooses is automatically more honest than a burden imposed by the city.
- **Physical habits:** Carries a battered canvas tool roll; keeps spare screws in his shirt pocket; bites the side of his thumbnail when a claim is due; cannot sleep when a hinge is open.
- **Moral center:** He wants people to be able to refuse a promise and still be helped when they are in danger. He is not naturally anti-authority; he is against unaccountable authority.

### Arc

Jonas begins as a repairman who believes a well-made rule is neutral. He ends as a public steward who knows no rule is neutral because someone must answer for its cost. His growth is practical and relational: better diagnosis, harder self-restraint, more willingness to delegate, and the ability to leave a system running without his name on it.

He does not become emotionally invulnerable. He can make a technically correct repair that is morally disastrous, lose access to a service he is trying to protect, and be wrong about a person he has judged. His final power is not a larger command; it is the deliberate surrender of the largest command available to him.

## Primary relationships

### Dr. Sanaa Iqbal — slow-burn romantic partner

- **Age:** 35.
- **Occupation:** Emergency physician at Saint Orra Civic Hospital; later helps create the city’s distributed public-health ring.
- **Want:** Keep patients from being turned into administrative categories at the moment they are most vulnerable.
- **Fear:** That care will become a transaction and that people in power will call triage an excuse for cruelty.
- **Flaw:** Sanaa can mistake moral clarity for certainty and withhold trust from anyone who hides consequences from her.
- **Beginning friction:** She sees Jonas as a technician who fixes the lock and walks away before the patient, family, or doctor has to live with the repaired rule. He sees her as someone who asks impossible questions at the worst time.
- **Trust progression:** They cooperate during the Volume 1 bridge and clinic crisis; she forces him to disclose the claims he takes; he refuses to conceal a patient’s access failure to protect his clearance; they disagree publicly over emergency powers; they separate after he accepts a precedence change that denies a needed patient transfer and causes harm; they rebuild trust by sharing decisions and by letting one another say no.
- **Romance progression:** Recognition of care, not a rescue fantasy. Their first personal conversation happens after a failed repair, not during a confession. They become partners in Volume 8, when both can state what the relationship costs and what remains private. In the ending they live together by choice, keep professional boundaries, and do not use the Lattice to monitor or bind one another.
- **Final resolution:** Sanaa becomes a steward of public-health permissions, not Jonas’s moral reward. She remains the person who can tell him that a technically valid outcome may still be a failure.

### Tomas Renn — friend, mentor, and institutional skeptic

- **Age:** 44.
- **Occupation:** Shop steward for the Civic Continuity Workers’ Union; former NDC field supervisor.
- **Want:** Keep the people who maintain the city from becoming disposable service labor.
- **Flaw:** He assumes every institution will repeat its oldest mistake and can be tempted to sabotage a useful system rather than reform it.
- **Function:** He teaches Jonas that a repair is also a bargaining position and that workers, not only administrators, deserve a voice in the Lattice. He argues against Jonas becoming a benevolent expert.

### Pia Santos — Lowglass courier and witness

- **Age:** 19.
- **Occupation:** Bicycle courier, part-time caregiver, and informal keeper of Lowglass route markers.
- **Want:** Have Lowglass recognized without exposing residents to a government that has already erased them once.
- **Flaw:** Pia distrusts institutions so completely that she can create new exclusions while protecting the neighborhood.
- **Function:** She is not Jonas’s apprentice or chosen successor. She insists that “being in the system” is not the same as being heard, and she becomes one of the most important independent witnesses in the final charter.

### Nell Ardent — civic lawyer and public-interest advocate

- **Age:** 39.
- **Occupation:** Founder of the Open Covenants Legal Clinic, which challenges revoked permissions and coerced signatures.
- **Want:** Make the city’s promises legible to the people who suffer from them.
- **Flaw:** She can reduce complicated relationships to a binary case and underestimate the need for an imperfect system that works tonight.
- **Function:** She provides legal consequences and teaches Jonas that a locally valid rule can still be a public abuse.

### Nadia Mercer — mother and family anchor

- **Age:** 61.
- **Occupation:** School crossing supervisor and part-time childcare coordinator.
- **Want:** Keep the family’s remaining history private without lying about the danger Evan entered.
- **Function:** She remembers ordinary details of the Quiet Cut and refuses to let Jonas turn his father into a martyr. Her trust returns when he stops using family history as leverage.

### Evan Mercer — missing father and central mystery subject

- **Age when last seen:** 52.
- **Occupation:** Maintenance engineer and Iona Vey’s former apprentice.
- **Known history:** Evan argued that the Lattice required distributed witnesses. During the Quiet Cut he cut a lowland node, saving Lowglass but losing the district from human maps and municipal indexes and apparently his own life.
- **Mystery function:** His name, signature, and incomplete counter-amendment link the family to the first administrator. The amendment’s second signature is the unresolved counter-signature that can either oppose or inherit the proxy. He is not secretly alive, not a remote helper, and not the protagonist’s secret inheritance of power. The final answer explains what happened to him without restoring him.
- **Emotional truth:** Jonas must accept that his father made a consequential choice without becoming either a hero to worship or a villain to punish.

## Antagonists and pressure figures

### Oren Pell — Volume 1 local antagonist

A deputy registrar who obeys an emergency clause that lets the city close an unsafe building. He is not corrupt in the simple sense: he believes a damaged permission must be closed before the next inspection, and he has a frightened family behind him. Jonas exposes the cost of the clause but cannot pretend the building was safe. Oren later becomes a reluctant witness in Volume 3.

### Alden Rook — Volumes 2–4 corporate antagonist

Operations director of CivicCore Consortium. Rook can keep a district supplied and pays workers fairly compared with the old department. His mistake is selling priority: a wealthy hospital receives a continuity claim before a lowland shelter, and the lost access becomes a legal debt. He is a mirror of Jonas’s early belief that the best technical operator should make the final decision.

### Councilor Iven Sore — Volumes 4–12 political antagonist

A former emergency planner who uses fear, public trust, and incomplete information to build the Continuity Bloc. Sore does not want magic for its own sake; she wants a state that can act without negotiation. She can save lives, which makes her dangerous. Her arc ends in public accountability rather than unexplained redemption or death.

### Cora Quill — Volumes 7–11 Archive antagonist

Head of the Quiet Archive. She preserves the history of people erased by the city and uses that preservation to justify suppressing the public. She knows more about Evan than anyone else and can make a strong case for keeping the Lattice sealed. Her methods damage the relationships that might have made a civic solution possible.

### Iona Vey — long-term antagonist and final authority

- **Age:** 68 at the start of the story.
- **Role:** First Administrator, lead architect of the Continuity Project, and holder of the original proxy authority.
- **Public history:** She vanished during the Quiet Cut and is remembered as a heroic pioneer.
- **Hidden state:** Her identity and partial consciousness are preserved as a root witness in the Old Ward. She can advise, approve, or reject certain root actions while the Lattice has enough human connection to carry her voice.
- **Want:** Prevent another citywide death by ensuring that one accountable authority can command every permission.
- **Flaw:** She treats individual consent as a luxury that becomes dangerous when resources are scarce, and she has confused being responsible with being entitled to rule.
- **Antagonistic logic:** If people can refuse, essential services may fail. If only one person can act, the person will be alone but effective. She is willing to preserve lives by removing meaningful choice.
- **Ending status:** Her root authority is irrevocably revoked. She survives as an ordinary person under public oversight, not a hidden power source. She is not absolved of responsibility for the Quiet Cut.

## Relationship milestones across the series

1. Jonas and Sanaa establish professional trust through a repair that fails visibly and is corrected.
2. Tomas makes Jonas sign a visible responsibility bond before allowing him to make a civic edit at a shared node; ordinary hardware work and Stage 1 diagnosis remain possible before the bond.
3. Pia refuses to let Jonas use Lowglass as evidence without giving residents a vote.
4. Jonas and Sanaa disagree publicly over an emergency override; they do not resolve the disagreement by simple moral certainty.
5. Jonas accepts a claim that causes medical harm, loses Sanaa’s trust, and learns that good intentions do not cancel consequences.
6. Tomas, Nell, and Pia build a coalition that can work without Jonas’s personal access.
7. Jonas and Sanaa choose a relationship while keeping professional independence.
8. Iona offers Jonas the role of First Administrator; he refuses succession.
9. Jonas and Sanaa disagree over whether to risk the final transition; they choose a shared decision rather than a private sacrifice.
10. In the ending, Jonas relinquishes root access and the relationship survives because neither person is made into the other’s system.
