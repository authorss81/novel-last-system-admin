## What did not pass, and what was done about it

### The blocking one: a pivotal fact was attributed to the wrong person in the prose

`chapter-0193.md:125` gave the Friday clause-stopping sentence to "the man of about thirty-eight" — Wendell Pryce, the continuity field manager, who is not the man who stopped clause six. But `0193:67` itself said "a man of thirty-two said a sentence here on Friday", and `0192:43` puts the sentence in Jonas Mercer's own mouth, `0192:71` names him as a man of thirty-two, and `0194:97` has him claim it in person. **The chapter contradicted itself fifty-eight lines apart, and the outline and every state block carried the correct version, so the prose was the sole outlier and nothing anywhere recorded the discrepancy. This is the one fact the volume turns on.** Fixed in the prose: the sentence is Jonas Mercer's, and the paragraph now also states that the man of about thirty-eight has spent the two days since telling four people on a telephone that there is nothing to put it in, which is his own business and not the sentence. He is still named by age and occupation and is still not named.

### 0193 could not decide who made the calls, and gave the wrong reason for the empty parade

`0193:87` said the thirty-eight "called nine people in a doorway on the twenty-second of August and thirty on this parade yesterday", which reads as one man placing thirty people on a pavement and, worse, cannot be reconciled with `0192:63`, where the only calls he made on the Friday were **four**, between about twenty past eleven and about half past eleven. `0193:65` compounded it: it attributed the drop from thirty to five to "the second call did not go off", when `0192:63` states the second call *went off* and that nothing is ever cancelled because there is no instrument in this city that cancels anything. The chapter stated the real mechanism — clause six not applied, the field closed, a closed field cannot take anybody not already in it — four paragraphs later, and contradicted itself. Both fixed. The chapter now gives the whole sequence in order: nine people put together in a doorway at seven in the morning on 22 August, thirty on the parade on the Friday, and inside the half hour after the sentence four of them rung back and told not this time.

### A date slip neither the review nor the writing phase's own measuring pass caught

While checking finding 2 the repair found a third: `0193:5` said the hole was dug on the Wednesday and `0193:19` said the main came off "yesterday morning", which from Saturday the nineteenth is the Friday. `0192:49` puts the dig on the Wednesday and `0194:19` has a crew in the trench on Monday and Tuesday, backfilled Tuesday night, made good Wednesday morning — which only works if the main came off on Wednesday the sixteenth. **The main now came off on Wednesday the sixteenth at about ten to nine in the morning and the trench has been open since.** Nothing else moves and no earlier chapter is touched.

### The prose had degenerated into editorial assertion

0193 carried 26 standalone all-caps paragraphs and 14 `---` breaks across 143 lines; 0194 carried 18 and 7 across 155. The same drift is measurable backwards through the book — Volume 01 chapters 0001 and 0020 carry none, 0044 carries three, Volume 03 chapter 0140 carries twenty — so it is a standing regression and not a close-only one. Against `AGENTS.md`: *"Use natural paragraphs… a one-line paragraph only when it is a deliberate dramatic beat," "Never replace a scene with a list of actions, states, or beats," "Do not deliver lore lectures."* The worst instance was `chapter-0194.md:143–155`, a thirteen-line state dump that restated the new block of `state/current.md` almost item for item: the reviewer's own recorded pattern — *"the state files described it more cleanly than it was"* — running in reverse, with the chapter file become the state file.

**Repaired, and the shout was reduced and not removed.** 0193 now carries five narrative all-caps paragraphs plus the diegetic notice and no `---` at all; 0194 carries one plus the notice and no `---` at all. The thirteen-line tail of 0194 was cut, and what was kept in it is named in §20.2: the permitted gate sentence, the order not renewed and not ended, the clause holding and not settled, and the closing pairing of the empty square with the empty line. What is now only in the state files is the register and its day-counts, the card, the letter, the paraphrase of the four questions, the nine who said yes in a doorway, the empty column, and the eleven refusals — every one of them unchanged and none of them lost. Words: 3,497 → 3,487 and 2,847 → 2,306, and the volume's own sentence — *a person who is not counted is not a person who is helped* — was cut with the tail and then put back at the end of the chapter, because the last chapter of a volume is where the volume's sentence belongs. A repair that loses one is a repair that has taken something out. **This overturns a decision recorded twice before — the Batch 0005 review declined to repair the refrain as "the sound of the book" and the close repeated the decision — and §18 says so rather than quietly replacing it.**
