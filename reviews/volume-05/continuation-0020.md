## The second pass, and what it was

**The six runs in the block did not reproduce.** The detector was rebuilt at the §12A method, all three controls were asked for before any figure was believed, and three more defects fell out of the rebuilding. **All three were over-dropping, which is the failure that hides a finding rather than the one that adds one to a list.**

- **The closed set of head nouns still had a place noun in it.** §12A records that this was fixed and that the set holds figures only. It did not: it carried *hall* and *ladder*, and a span containing either was being called a figure's own by-name description and excluded.
- **The dateline test was applied to the target and not to the set.** The method drops a dateline **on both sides**; this detector dropped it on one, so a dateline in a comparison chapter stayed in the index and could be matched against a chapter's body.
- **A dateline is structural.** It is a paragraph that **begins** with a day of the week or with a number and a month name. Testing a whole paragraph for a weekday name anywhere inside it drops every state-file summary that mentions a day in passing, and `state/chapter-summaries.md` is mostly summaries that mention days in passing. **That false drop was hiding a real finding** — 0387's own closing block, found inside the 0142 entry, which is a faithful record of a chapter that said it first.

**After the three were fixed the control still read 326, and the runs then found sixteen reuses of prose that the recorded pass had marked as repaired: seven in chapters and nine in state files.** All sixteen were repaired at source — the seven in the chapters, the nine in the files and not in the chapters, which is the rule §13A item 4 sets out.
