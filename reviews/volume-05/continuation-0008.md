## Findings

### 1. 0274 put a summer hasp on an October room, and the error was in five state files

`chapter-0274.md:31` said the second room "has a door that shuts because somebody put a
hasp on it **in the summer**", in the same sentence that puts her in a room she took in
the middle of October. 0271 stages the hasp, the staple and the screw eye as stock in a
bag in the back of a shop **on 26 October**, with the young woman paying for her own
post-fix. A summer hasp on an October room is impossible, and the wrong fact had been
copied into `state/character-state.md`, `state/open-threads.md`,
`state/continuity.md`, `state/current.md` and `state/chapter-summaries.md`.

**Repaired.** The fix went on at the end of October, in the chapter and in all five state
files. The review also named `state/live-canon.md` as a fourth place; that file was
checked and has never carried the sentence, and `state/live-canon.md` and the
Continuation 0008 entry in `state/batch-summaries.md` were both checked for finding 2
and are clean.

### 2. The state files reported a gift that was refused

`state/continuity.md` §7, `state/character-state.md` and `state/open-threads.md` §7 all
said the woman who runs the shop that shuts early "gave a torch off a shelf and a hasp, a
staple and a screw eye out of a bag" and "has asked for nothing for them". 0271 has her
explicitly refuse and tell the young woman to take them off the shelf and pay: *"You'll
take them off a shelf in a shop that shuts at one and you'll pay for a bag of post-fix
with your own money."* The torch also conflicts with 0260, where the man behind the till
counted four lamps out of a box and said "they'd want the money".

**Repaired, and the correction is better than the error.** Nothing was given. What went
round is the man behind the counter on the parade's account, told in a queue on the same
afternoon and repeated in that kitchen by both the young woman and the man of about
eighteen. Something did go off a shelf in that shop on the twenty-fifth of August; nobody
was given anything for nothing. The account is not corrected by anybody in any chapter
and is not to be corrected by a later one. It is what went round.

### 3. The batch's verbatim-span check did not cover the batch's own chapters

§12 item 17 reports "four spans of nine words or more and none of ten or more". That
figure was scoped to the chapters against every other file in the repository and not
against each other. Run chapter-against-chapter inside 0265–0274 it returned **30
cross-chapter 9-grams, 13 of them ten words or longer, longest 13 words** — a verbatim
reuse of 0268's description of the key-holder, eleven words of 0269's dateline phrase for
a room over a shop, ten words of 0268's attribution of the woman of about forty-two, ten
words of 0267, and 0268's Corrance Lane roof re-told. This broke the batch's own §12
item 7, *"A chapter may not reuse another chapter's sentence, even a true one."*

**Repaired and the figure restated as scoped.** Every reuse of prose was rewritten, seven
places across 0273 and 0274. The ten chapters now return **six cross-chapter 9-grams, one
of ten words and none of eleven**, and every one of the six is a dateline, a recurring
location of this borough, or a figure's own by-name description. The rule in item 7
stands; what was corrected is the figure, which was scoped and read as though it were not.

### 4. Two weekday errors in the state files, both about one scene

`chapter-0274.md` is Tuesday 15 November. The register scene opened *on the Monday* and
printed 720, which is the fifteenth's figure; on the fourteenth it is 719.
`state/character-state.md` said "On **Monday** the fifteenth of November" and
`state/current.md` said she "opened it on **Monday the ninth**" — the ninth was a
Wednesday and was not the day it was opened.

**Repaired.** The register is opened on the Tuesday, which is its own chapter's own date,
and the one day-count in the prose is still *seven hundred and twenty* at 0274, in the
body. Both state files now say Tuesday the fifteenth. The same chapter also put a woman
carrying a bundle of post *on the Wednesday morning*, a day that had not happened yet;
that is now the Tuesday morning.

### 5. Two halls, one key-holder, never established

0264, 0268 and 0272 make the hall *off the parade* hers on her meter. 0273 made her "the
woman of about forty-two with the key to **that** hall" where *that hall* is the Methodist
hall on Carrow Lane, shutting *its* gas at *its* meter. 0240's "because it is her hall" is
the hall off the parade.

**Repaired with one clause.** She has both keys, which is what 0273 has always shown by
padlocking that meter, and 0273 now says so.

### 6. The Thursday room's ownership was tangled across three chapters

0269 puts the first-floor room over a shop in the hands of a man in his thirties, with
the woman of about fifty-five in it as a visitor. 0271 had her saying "the door of **that
room** does not shut", which left the man's door in question.

**Repaired with one clause.** The door is her own — the room she took in the middle of
October — and 0271 now says so, and it also names the Thursday, which is the Thursday
before last, the thirteenth of October, which is when she was in that room. The man's room
and its door are not mentioned again and have no hasp on them.

### 7. The 28 June claim is a deliberate drift — confirm intent

0269 gives the twenty-eighth of June as the only thing the borough has ever agreed on,
credited to one man in a street with five people turning up. On the page, 28 June is one
man carrying a bath out of a landing in Marsh Lane (0252, 0257), and the five in the rain
is 7 September (0262), which the same young woman puts on the page herself in 0265: *"On
the seventh, there was five of us stood in the rain."* 0274 propagates the re-dating.

**Confirmed as intent and recorded as one.** This is the unreliable-folk-memory theme and
the chapters mark it: 0269 says the answer is not better "because it's more true", and
0274 has it come out of a table as "that's what they're all saying". The batch's
measuring pass caught a canon-count contradiction in this same paragraph and fixed the
number, which is why the date looked like an oversight. The count of five is canon; the
date is wrong on purpose and is not to be corrected, explained or improved on. The count
and the drift are now recorded in §12B and in §5 of the open-threads block so no later
batch "fixes" it.

### 8. The boundary has been deferred three times — escalated, not decided

`outline/volume-05.md` dates Volume 05 as 195–242, complete. Chapters 243–274 now belong
to no volume. The phase prompt correctly forbids a writer from closing, so the volume
cannot close and the self-dispatch loop will keep issuing continuations.

**Escalated, and the decision is still not taken.** Three answers exist and a human may
pick any of them in one line: (a) the boundary is where the page puts it, the two
outlines are corrected to 195–274, and 275 is a volume close — the only one of the three
that ends the loop; (b) Volume 06 is defined from 243, giving it thirty-two chapters and
leaving Volume 05 at forty-eight; (c) a fifth block is named and 243–274 are given to it,
at the cost of an outline nobody has written. Until a human rules, every phase is a
continuation. The escalation is written into §12B, into §11 of the open-threads block and
into the next prompt, and it replaces a seventh repetition. No outline was rewritten and
no volume-close prompt was written.

### 9. AGENTS.md's chapter gate is met by eight of ten, not by 0273

0273 is eleven people, a boy, two mechanical fixes and one line — "Somebody did." No
resistance, no cost, and a kindness nobody acts on. The batch defends the length in §12
and the defence is fair, but it is a scene and not a chapter. Related: 0268 and 0274 both
open on the same parade inventory — shutter, window card, the woman of about sixty-five
behind a counter who is not told anything — and that inventory has now run for four
consecutive batches.

**Carried, not fixed.** It is recorded in §12B as a standing observation, together with
the larger risk it belongs to: the last seventy chapters have contained no Lattice, no
permission and no panel, and `bible/premise.md` is still the premise. A writer who wants
to restore any part of it may not do it by resumption — a Lattice needs a card, and a
card is a human's.
